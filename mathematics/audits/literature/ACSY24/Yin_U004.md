## Point-spectrum and Brown-measure weights

The source defines the point-spectrum weight as the trace of the kernel projection.
For a bounded nonnormal operator, its point-spectrum distribution is bounded by its Brown measure, and an atom can have strictly larger Brown mass.
For a normal operator the two weights agree.
An epsilon-squared regularized inverse trace therefore recovers a kernel dimension, and cannot generally determine the Brown atom weight.
A nonzero nilpotent matrix illustrates strict inequality: its Brown measure is concentrated at zero, while its normalized kernel dimension is smaller than one.

This selected passage supplies the distinction between Brown atoms and kernel dimensions, not an atom-location theorem for arbitrary matrix coefficients.
