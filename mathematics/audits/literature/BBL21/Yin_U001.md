## BBL21-U001

The intended consumer is [ACSY24](../../../literature/pdf/ACSY24.pdf), Theorem 4.7 and its proof, pp.43--44.
That source cites “Corollary 3.3” for an arbitrary matrix rank in the subgroup generated by marginal atomic weights.
The available preprint's item 3.3 is a **Remark**.
Its relevant Corollary 4.3 has additional hypotheses.
This unit uses the [selected arXiv v2 edition](readme.md#yin-edition); the final journal Corollary 3.3 was not inspected, so a preprint-number mismatch alone establishes no error in the published numbering.

Precisely, let X_1,X_2 be free selfadjoint affiliated operators, let a_1,a_2,b be selfadjoint scalar n by n matrices, and set
\[
 \xi=E_n\bigl(p_{\ker(b\otimes1-a_1\otimes X_1-a_2\otimes X_2)}\bigr).
\]
Corollary 4.3 assumes xi is **invertible positive**, not merely nonzero positive.
It expresses n(1+tr_n(xi)) as an integer plus a finite integer combination of marginal atomic masses.
Its proof uses the additive kernel-trace identity of Theorem 3.5 and the one-variable pencil calculation of Lemma 4.2.
In that latter calculation the scalar kernel dimension is constant at its minimum outside finitely many real points, so integrating it gives the asserted finite atomic correction.
Those deductions and the trace normalization were checked.
Theorem 3.5 itself is accepted only within its explicit invertibility hypothesis; its original analytic proof is outside this scoped inspection.

### Exact subordination interface used by the local reconstruction

Let \(Y_1,Y_2\) be selfadjoint operators in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\), free over a finite-dimensional matrix subalgebra \(B\) for the trace-preserving conditional expectation \(E_B\), and set \(Y:=Y_1+Y_2\).
Theorem 3.5 assumes that \(E_B(p_{Y-b})\) is invertible, for \(b=b^*\in B\).
Its construction uses the selfadjoint norm limits \(b_j:=\lim_{y\downarrow0}\omega_j(b+\mathrm iy1)\) from Theorem 3.2.
Equation (3.7) states
\[
 E_{B\langle Y_j\rangle}((z-Y)^{-1})=(\omega_j(z)-Y_j)^{-1},
 \qquad z\in\mathbb H^+(B),
\]
where the conditional expectation is trace preserving.
Theorem 3.5(vii) then gives \(\tau(p_{Y_1-b_1})+\tau(p_{Y_2-b_2})=1+\tau(p_{Y-b})\), for the ambient tracial state.
These exact analytic statements are accepted external inputs at their stated hypotheses; reading their displayed identities and use in the source proof does not amount to an independent audit of their analytic foundations.

### Limits of the source's stated doubling conclusion

Lemma 4.7 cuts a kernel by scalar-matrix support projections to make its expected kernel invertible, with an integer change in the unnormalized nullity.
The resulting pencil may cease to be selfadjoint.
Proposition 4.8 uses selfadjoint doubling, which controls **twice** the original nullity modulo integers.
Its stated conclusion excludes input atomlessness only when an output kernel mass lies outside {0,1/2,1}.
This available statement is not the arbitrary-Gamma Atiyah statement asserted in ACSY24.
One cannot silently divide a subgroup inclusion by two.

The ACSY24 display also linearizes a general polynomial matrix without explicitly obtaining a selfadjoint pencil.
For the selected v2 edition, Corollary 4.3 alone does not justify that use; the complete reduction without changing Gamma is supplied by the local proof below.
These are limitations of that invocation, not a counterexample to the Atiyah conclusion.
