## Exact source interfaces

For a C*-algebra A, III.5.2.5--8 identify its bidual A** with the von Neumann algebra of its universal representation, whose predual restricts isometrically onto A*.
Section III.5.2.9 extends any nondegenerate representation of A uniquely to a normal representation of A**, with its range isomorphic to a direct summand.

For a von Neumann algebra M, III.5.2.14 considers the universal normal representation.
Section III.5.2.15 gives a normal star homomorphism theta:M** -> M that restricts to the identity on the canonical copy of M.
It explicitly identifies theta as the Banach adjoint of the inclusion M_* -> M*.

Section III.1.1.13 states that a weakly closed two-sided ideal of a von Neumann algebra is generated by a central projection and has the complementary central ideal as a quotient summand.
The text's short argument uses the projection onto the essential subspace of the ideal.

## Direct conversion to the normal summand

Apply the ideal description to ker theta.
Write ker theta=(1-e)M** with e central.
Restriction of theta identifies eM** normally with M.
Its inverse j is a normal isometric star homomorphism and, for the canonical inclusion iota:M -> M**, satisfies

\[
j(a)=e\,\iota(a),\qquad \theta(j(a))=a.
\]

A normal functional on M extends to M** by composing with theta, because theta is the adjoint of the predual inclusion.
Consequently evaluation on j(a) is its original value at a.
Centrality of e makes this conversion compatible with left and right multiplication by the canonical image of M.
These are direct consequences of the inspected source interfaces, not a new claim about Hilbert-space adjoint domains.

For a reflexive Banach target H, the subsequent construction T -> T** j for a bounded map T:M -> H is a project-level use of this summand.
