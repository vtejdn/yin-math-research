## Actual-domain interface

The source allows a nonselfadjoint tuple provided its list is closed under taking adjoints.
It defines adjoints of the formal evaluated gradient and Jacobian as operators by their Hilbert pairing identities, even when their domains are not dense.
Lemma 3.1 identifies derivations on the represented star algebra with actual vacuum-adjoint membership with the **actual** gradient-adjoint domain: the image consists of the componentwise tensor adjoints of the generator values.
Its proof is the word-cut pairing identity and tensor-adjoint module action.
This established interface is accepted at its exact scope.

Theorem 2.10 instead computes a von Neumann dimension of the **closure** of that domain.
These are different objects; closure membership does not supply an actual vacuum.
The local notation conversion and prescribed-coordinate affine slice are developed in [D_F0005](../../../drafts/Yin_D_F0005.md#an-exact-target-only-functional-reduction).

## Restriction is not extension

Proposition 3.4 restricts derivations from the algebra generated by a tuple X to the smaller polynomial algebra generated by Y=F(X), and expresses their generator coefficients using the Jacobian.
Its surrounding text explicitly says restriction need be neither injective nor surjective.
Neither its closed-module inequality nor Theorem 3.2's equality for equal polynomial algebras extends every small-core witness to a larger reference core.

There is a literal normalization correction in the displayed adjoint-value equality of Proposition 3.4.
Under Section 1.1's declared codomain L2(B<Y>), the restricted vacuum must be the Hilbert projection of the original vacuum to that subspace.
It cannot in general equal the unchanged original vector.
For example, take X=S a standard semicircle, B=C, Y=0 and the reference derivative partial_S: its original vacuum is S, but its restriction to constants is zero, with vacuum zero.
The restricted coefficient tuple is zero as well.
The pairing proof gives the corrected conditional-expectation value immediately.
Only the corrected restriction statement is accepted here.

There is also an ambient-space convention to keep explicit.
Restricting the input algebra retains the original ambient coarse codomain unless one additionally projects both tensor legs to the smaller generated algebra.
If one wants intrinsic smaller-algebra coarse values, apply that tensor conditional expectation; its vacuum is the corresponding target Hilbert projection.
No unqualified identification of these two coefficient modules is used here.

This source therefore does not reverse C_F0147's failed reference-lift result.
No full-Fisher hypothesis for the four target symbols, ambient-dimension upper bound for the target, or actual-domain extension is inferred.
