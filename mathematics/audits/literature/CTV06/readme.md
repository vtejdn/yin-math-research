# CTV06: singular-border rank growth and its symmetric source scope

## Yin

The selected original is [arXiv:math/0505156v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0505156v1), stamped 9 May 2005 and retained as [CTV06--arxiv-v1.pdf](../../../literature/pdf/CTV06--arxiv-v1.pdf).
It has 16 pages, with printed and one-based PDF labels agreeing at the selected passages.
[Costello's institutional publication page](https://mathdept.ucr.edu/costello) identifies the Duke Mathematical Journal 135 (2006), 395--413 publication used for the canonical year.
The source numbering here belongs to the preprint; no full comparison with the journal layout is asserted.
No supplied contents list was identified, so no reconstructed contents transcription is introduced.

The selected reading covers the Bernoulli model and the source's probability convention on pp. 1--2, Section 2's rank-chain explanation and equation (1), Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.4 on p. 3, the distinction from the invertible-border Lemma 2.8 on pp. 4--5, and the complete proof of Lemma 2.4 on pp. 11--12.
The source's phrase almost surely is explicitly defined on p. 1 to mean probability tending to one, rather than one-event convergence for an arbitrary coupling of dimensions.
Lemma 2.4 concerns a singular symmetric normal matrix, where normal means no nonzero row dependence supported on fewer than N=n^(1-epsilon) rows.
It adds an independent Bernoulli zero-one vector and its transpose and gives rank increase two outside probability O(N^(-1/2)).
The proof on p. 12 explicitly invokes symmetry to pass from a column rank increase to a rank increase of two for the full border.
The source's complete nonsingularity argument also uses a separate quadratic estimate for borders of invertible matrices.
Its full proof and other generalizations were not independently audited here.

[The fixed nonsymmetric-offset deduction](../../../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v2.md#rank-growth-attribution) uses this as attribution for the recognized rank-growth mechanism, not as a theorem with the present shift, law or summable-probability hypotheses.
That deduction supplies its own complex row/column-space calculation and separately controls right-kernel and transpose-left-kernel linear forms through the already reviewed nonsymmetric-offset counting module.
No direct transfer of the symmetric source's one-form argument, full nonsingularity conclusion or coupling convention is asserted.
