### Definition and complex-algebra specialization

For a ring homomorphism \(f:R\to S\), Cohn defines the unit-closure as the least subring of \(S\) containing \(\operatorname{im}f\) and closed under inverses of its elements whenever those inverses exist in \(S\).
His conventions require rings to have an identity, subrings to share that identity, and homomorphisms to preserve it.

For an inclusion \(A\hookrightarrow B\) of unital complex algebras, this is the least unital subalgebra containing \(A\) and closed under inverses available in \(B\).
The ring formulation automatically gives a complex subalgebra because \(A\) already contains every scalar multiple of the identity.

[Lin00, Section 1](../Lin00/readme.md), retained preprint p.2, directly uses the name division closure for the same construction.
The independent reviewer reread that passage together with both Cohn pages and confirmed the applicability and the distinction in names.
The earlier, directly checked [Coh85 Exercise 7.1.4](../Coh85/Yin_U001_v1.md#coh85-u001) already uses the name division closure; this unit records the same definition under the later name.
