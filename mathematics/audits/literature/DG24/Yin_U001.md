Theorem 2.9(b) characterizes B-valued R-diagonal elements by the vanishing of all cumulants except the even alternating ones.
Definition 2.10 calls such an element B-valued circular when only the two alternating second cumulants may remain.
Theorem 2.9 is cited there from earlier work; its equivalence is accepted as a standard external statement at this limited scope.
No other characterization, bipolar decomposition, or polar decomposition theorem is used.

Take B=M_m(C) and E=E_m=id tensor tau.
For a centered linear combination T of free semicircular elements, only second B-valued cumulants may be nonzero.
Zero pseudo-covariance rho eliminates E_m(TxT) and E_m(T*xT*), leaving E_m(TxT*)=eta(x) and E_m(T*xT)=eta*(x).
Thus the cited definition applies, and these same cumulants immediately give invariance under scalar rotations.
These explanatory calculations do not import a Brown-measure theorem from DG24; radial potential, distribution and spectral conclusions require their own proof inputs.
