## Selected scalar statement and attribution

For a Laurent polynomial q with complex coefficients, support in [-m,m] and nonnegative values on the unit circle, the selected statement gives an ordinary polynomial p of degree at most m with q=|p|^2 on that circle.
One may choose p with no zeros in the open disk.
The following paragraph describes pairing roots by z_j and 1/conjugate(z_j), then selecting the outside members and adjusting a scalar factor.

This verifies the classical scalar Fejer--Riesz name and degree/root-pair convention only.
It does NOT promise rational coefficients for p when q has rational coefficients, a rational prescribed quotient interpolant, a strict rational positive definite Gram with a specified size, or any arithmetic denominator/window bound.
A rational-Gram argument must prove its real/rational approximation and affine constraints itself.
This U adds no project theorem.
