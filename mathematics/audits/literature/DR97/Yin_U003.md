### Statement and Hypotheses

Keep finite $X$, $\Gamma=F(X)$, and let $\mathcal A=C_r^*(\Gamma)$.
For each integer $n\geq1$, let $R_n$ be the smallest unital complex subalgebra of $\mathcal A$ containing $\mathbb C\Gamma$ such that whenever $M\in M_n(R_n)$ is invertible in $M_n(\mathcal A)$, every entry of $M^{-1}$ belongs to $R_n$.
Set $R_\infty=\bigcup_{n\geq1}R_n$.
In particular, $R_1$ is the scalar inverse closure called "rational closure" by DR97.
Modern uses of "rational closure" may refer to all matrix sizes; this audit avoids conflating the two definitions before their equality is proved.

Let $\Gamma^{(1)}$ be the unoriented edges of the Cayley tree, and

\[
H^+=\ell^2(\Gamma),\qquad
H^-=\ell^2(\Gamma^{(1)})\oplus\mathbb C,
\qquad H=H^+\oplus H^-.
\]

The tree unitary $P:H^+\to H^-$ and the involution

\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&P^{-1}\\P&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

are those of [DR97-U002](Yin_U002.md#dr97-u002).
For $a\in\mathcal A$ use the representation

\[
\pi(a)=\lambda(a)\oplus(\lambda_E(a)\oplus0),
\]

where the edge representation is a direct sum of $|X|$ copies of the regular representation: an edge is uniquely $\{g,gx\}$ with $g\in\Gamma$, $x\in X$.
Thus $\lambda_E$ extends boundedly to $\mathcal A$.
The auxiliary scalar summand has the **zero** action, as specified in the original p. 617 construction.
Define

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm fin}=\{a\in\mathcal A:[F,\pi(a)]\text{ has finite rank on }H\}.
\]

Theorem 7 asserts

\[
R_1=R_2=\cdots=R_n=\cdots=R_\infty=\mathcal A_{\rm fin}.
\]

Together with U004 this also identifies these sets with the bounded operators whose reduced-word Fourier coefficient series is recognizable.

### Lemma 8 and the Correct Ambient Algebra

The printed abstract lemma is valid for a unital algebra $\mathcal D$, a derivation $d:\mathcal D\to\mathcal D$, and a two-sided ideal $J$: $\mathcal C=\{a:d(a)\in J\}$ is an algebra closed under adjoining entries of inverses of matrices invertible in $\mathcal D$.
Entrywise extension to matrices gives

\[
d_n(M^{-1})=-M^{-1}d_n(M)M^{-1}.
\]

The same proof works when the derivation takes values in a unital $\mathcal D$-bimodule and $J$ is a sub-bimodule.
To see the generated closure exactly, start with the algebra generated by $Z\subset\mathcal C$ and at each integer stage adjoin inverse entries of the permitted size.
The increasing union has the required closure property because a matrix has finitely many entries, all appearing in one stage.
Minimality of the union follows by induction.
This supplies the stage argument compressed on p. 619.

The printed application "$d=\operatorname{ad}F$, $\mathcal D=C_r^*(\Gamma)$, $J$ the finite-rank ideal of $\mathcal D$" is not literally type-correct: $F$ and its commutators live on the graded space $H$, and $[F,\pi(a)]$ need not belong to $\pi(\mathcal A)$.
There is a second issue: $\pi$ is not unital because its scalar action is zero.
These issues are resolved algebraically as follows.

Let $\mathcal F(H)\subset B(H)$ be the ideal of finite-rank operators and let $q:B(H)\to B(H)/\mathcal F(H)$ be the algebra quotient.
No norm completion is used: replacing finite rank by compactness would weaken the criterion.
The defect $I-\pi(1)$ is the rank-one projection onto the auxiliary scalar summand.
Therefore

\[
\bar\pi=q\circ\pi:\mathcal A\to B(H)/\mathcal F(H)
\]

is a **unital** homomorphism.
With $\bar F=q(F)$, the map

\[
\delta(a)=\bar F\bar\pi(a)-\bar\pi(a)\bar F
\]

is a derivation into the unital $\mathcal A$-bimodule $B(H)/\mathcal F(H)$.
Its kernel is exactly $\mathcal A_{\rm fin}$.
For an invertible matrix $M\in M_n(\mathcal A)$, $\bar\pi_n(M^{-1})$ is the inverse of $\bar\pi_n(M)$ and the displayed inverse-derivation identity holds.
Hence a matrix with entries in $\mathcal A_{\rm fin}$ has inverse entries in that algebra.
This proves the intended Lemma 8 application for every matrix size.

Adding an augmentation action on the scalar summand is unnecessary and would require a bounded extension to $C_r^*(\Gamma)$, which is unavailable for a nonabelian free group.
The quotient argument uses precisely the original representation and its finite-dimensional unit defect.

### Theorem 7 Assembly

1. **Nested closures.**
   Stability under $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ inverses implies stability under $n\times n$ inverses by embedding $M$ as $\operatorname{diag}(M,1)$.
   Thus $R_n\subseteq R_{n+1}$.
   The union $R_\infty$ is an algebra; any finite matrix over it lies in some $M_N(R_N)$ after increasing $N$ and padding when needed.
   This is the all-matrix inverse closure.
2. **Forward finite-rank inclusion.**
   The tree calculation in U002 gives $[F,\pi(g)]$ finite rank for each group word $g$, and therefore for each group polynomial.
   The repaired Lemma 8 application gives $R_n\subseteq\mathcal A_{\rm fin}$ for every $n$, and hence $R_\infty\subseteq\mathcal A_{\rm fin}$.
3. **Reverse inclusion.**
   If $a\in\mathcal A_{\rm fin}$, write its coefficient vector as $a\delta_e\in\ell^2(\Gamma)$.
   The algebraic finite-rank criterion of U002 applies to the restrictions of the bounded operators to finitely supported vectors, so $m(a\delta_e)$ is rational.
   Proposition 9 in U004 supplies $b\in R_1$ with $b\delta_e=a\delta_e$.
   Operators in $C_r^*(\Gamma)$ commute with right translations.
   Since the right translates of $\delta_e$ span a dense subspace, equality on $\delta_e$ implies $a=b$.
   Therefore $\mathcal A_{\rm fin}\subseteq R_1$.

The inclusions close the asserted equality chain.
There is no circular dependency: U002 supplies a necessary algebraic condition, U004 independently constructs a bounded scalar-inverse realization from that condition, and Lemma 8 supplies the closure-to-finite-rank direction.

### Dependencies and Scope Limits

- U002 supplies the tree construction, finite rank for group polynomials, and Proposition 5 with its restricted-support injectivity repair.
  Its finite-generator hypothesis is retained here.
- U004 supplies the square-summable rational realization; in particular boundedness is established before applying Banach matrix inversion.
  The homogeneous Haagerup input and formal-series interfaces retain their recorded review boundaries.
- The conclusion is about finite rank and algebraic inverse closures **inside** $C_r^*(\Gamma)$.
  It does not assert that these algebras are closed in operator norm, or identify them with the complete reduced C*-algebra.
- Arbitrary group coefficient functions do not have unrestricted convolution products, and arbitrary $\ell^2$ coefficient vectors do not already define bounded operators.
  These distinctions are essential to both the statement and its proof.
- The ambient representation and quotient repair are a reconstruction of the source argument, not a new project claim or an asserted erratum endorsed by the authors.
