# Mixed identities with constants from a group

## Mixed identities and simultaneous substitution

For a group \(G\) and a free group \(F_n\) on \(n\ge1\) variables, a word \(w\in G*F_n\) gives a mixed identity if every homomorphism \(G*F_n\to G\) fixing \(G\) sends \(w\) to the identity.
It is nontrivial if \(w\ne1\) in the free product.
Hull and Osin call a group *mixed identity free* (MIF) when it satisfies no such nontrivial identity, in any number of variables. [Definition 5.2 and Remark 5.1, arXiv v1, p. 26](../../../literature/pdf/HO16--arxiv-v1.pdf#page=26)

Proposition 5.3 identifies this condition for countable \(G\) with universal equivalence of \(G\) and \(G*F_n\) in the language with constants from \(G\), for every \(n\).
For a finite set of nonidentity words, the disjunction that some word equals one fails at the free variables.
Universal equivalence therefore gives one substitution in \(G\) keeping every word nonidentity.
The proof in the source combines the words into an iterated commutator using a further free variable. [Proposition 5.3, arXiv v1, pp. 26–27](../../../literature/pdf/HO16--arxiv-v1.pdf#page=26)

Corollary 5.10 gives the MIF property for the quotient of an acylindrically hyperbolic group by its largest finite normal subgroup.
A torsion-free group has trivial finite radical, so the result applies to the group itself under the stated acylindrical hyperbolicity hypothesis.
The inspected argument invokes highly transitive actions and its preceding dichotomy. [Corollary 5.10, arXiv v1, p. 30](../../../literature/pdf/HO16--arxiv-v1.pdf#page=30)

The [Schur-kernel application](../../../claims/Yin_C_A0238_v1.md#transferring-the-moment-by-finitely-many-word-tests) uses the one-variable finite substitution consequence.
The present inspection covers these statements and the indicated proof steps, without a new verification of the paper's earlier action theorems.
