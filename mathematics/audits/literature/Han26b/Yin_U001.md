# Least singular values of tensor pencils with adjoints and Wigner symmetry

In Theorems 1.2 and 1.5, Yi Han bounds the smallest singular value of an affine tensor pencil in terms of the dimension of its fixed coefficient module.
This reading of [arXiv v1](readme.md#yin-v1) covers the introduction and the arguments in Sections 2–6, pp. 1–30, including the changes of probability space in the covering argument and the diagonal terms in the determinant gradient.
The bounds follow under the stated density and second-moment assumptions.
The quantitative estimate following Theorem 1.5 has a separate moment qualification on p. 29.
The [Brown measure argument](Yin_U002.md#han26b-u002) uses only the qualitative part of that estimate.

<a id="entry-models"></a>
## Entry laws and tail estimates

In Section 1.2, \(X_\ell=N^{-1/2}(\xi_{\ell,ij})\) has independent entries across all directed indices and all matrices, drawn from one fixed centered law with \(\mathbb E|\xi|^2=1\).
The law has either a bounded density on \(\mathbb R\) or a bounded joint density on \(\mathbb R^2\).
In Section 1.3, a Hermitian input \(H_\ell\) uses independent upper off-diagonal atoms \(\zeta_{\ell,ij}/\sqrt N\), their conjugates below the diagonal, and independent real diagonal atoms \(d_{\ell,i}/\sqrt N\).
All matrices are independent and all laws are fixed in \(N\).
Within each matrix the off-diagonal atoms have the same centered, unit-absolute-variance law from one of the two density classes, and the diagonal atoms have the same centered law with bounded real density and finite second moment.
Different Wigner matrices may have different laws, including real and planar laws in the same tuple.
The planar density permits dependence between the coordinates and nonzero pseudovariance \(\mathbb E\zeta^2\).

For fixed \(m\times m\) coefficients the two pencils are \(T=I+\sum_\ell(A_\ell\otimes X_\ell+B_\ell\otimes X_\ell^*)\) and \(T=I+\sum_\ell C_\ell\otimes H_\ell\).
Write \(r\le m\) for the composition length of \(\mathbb C^m\) over the unital complex algebra generated by its coefficients.
Let \(\Omega_N=\{\max_\ell N^{-1}\|Z_\ell\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le2\}\) for the respective inputs \(Z_\ell\), and put \(q_N=\mathbb P(\Omega_N^c)\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\) and all \(t>0\), the two theorems give
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(T)\le t\}
 \le C N^K t^{\omega/r}+C e^{-cN}+q_N,\qquad q_N\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Within the stated density classes, \(\omega=1\) when all input atoms are real and \(\omega=1/2\) when at least one input law is planar.
For directed iid entries with a finite \(2+\varepsilon\) moment, the localization error is \(O(N^{-\min(\varepsilon,2)})\).
For Gaussian inputs in the stated classes, whitening yields \(\omega=1\) and an exponentially small total additive error.
Corollaries 1.3 and 6.3 give \(\mathbb P\{s_{\min}(P(Z)-zI)<N^{-B_z}\}\to0\) for every fixed \(z\ne P(0)\), with constants allowed to depend on \(z\).

## Linearization and the trace observations

Lemma 2.1, pp. 9–10, represents each word by the upper-right entry of \((I-S_w)^{-1}\) for a strictly upper triangular word chain.
With \(Q=\bigoplus_w(I-S_w)\), its boundary row and column satisfy \(uQ^{-1}v=P(0)-P\).
The Schur complement is therefore \(P-zI\).
At zero input \(Q=I\) and \(uv=0\), so the constant matrix has determinant \(P(0)-z\).
The upper-left inverse block supplies the least-value comparison using only the inverse of this fixed constant matrix.
The two elimination factors and their inverses are polynomials, a stronger fact used for counting many singular values in Section 7.
In Remark 2.2 the real iid polynomial \(X-X^T\) has a zero eigenvalue in every odd dimension at the excluded shift.

For a nonzero irreducible coefficient family, write \(A_{\ell h},B_{\ell h}\) for the coefficients after expressing every atom in real coordinates.
The generators \(C_j\) in (2.1) are the two matrices \(\operatorname{diag}(A_{\ell h},B_{\ell h}^{T})\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(B_{\ell h},A_{\ell h}^{T})\) for each coordinate.
Their unital algebra \(\mathcal B\) acts faithfully on a direct sum of two irreducible modules, the second verified using the annihilator for the bilinear transpose pairing.
Its radical thus vanishes, and the ambient trace on each simple matrix summand is the ordinary trace times a positive multiplicity.
This proves nondegeneracy of \((b,c)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(bc)\) on \(\mathcal B\).
The common kernel of its coefficient generators is zero in the nonzero irreducible case.
Cayley–Hamilton applied to the doubled pencil \(T\oplus T^T\) puts each doubled inverse block \(\operatorname{diag}(R_{ij},R_{ji}^T)\) in \(\mathcal B\), where \(R=T^{-1}\).

On \(V=\{b^*:b\in\mathcal B\}\) with its real Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, put \(\mathcal O(\Phi)=(\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi^*C_j))_j\) and \(\mathcal D=\ker\mathcal O\).
If a nonzero real \(E\subset\mathcal D\) were invariant under all left multiplications by \(C_j^*\), then \((E+iE)^*\) would be a nonzero right ideal of \(\mathcal B\).
For an element \(W\) of this ideal, \(\operatorname{Tr}(C_j Wb)=0\) for every \(b\in\mathcal B\).
Nondegeneracy would give \(C_jW=0\) for every \(j\), contradicting the common-kernel calculation.
This checks Lemmas 2.3–2.5 with real Hilbert adjoints on the specified space, including orthogonal compression where required.

## Independent outputs in the profile estimate

In Theorem 3.1, \(p=\dim_{\mathbb R}V\) is fixed and the profile operator \(\mathfrak D_a\) acts on \(\mathcal D^N\) with output \(a\) omitted.
For \(0\ne E\subset\mathcal D\), list the coefficient maps as \(H_j\) and set \(\Psi_E((v_j)_j)=\sum_j H_jv_j\) on \(E^{\#\{j\}}\).
The active image \(Y_\sigma(E)\) is the left singular subspace of \(\Psi_E\) for singular values at least \(\sigma\).
The subspace \(C_{\tau,\sigma}(E)\) is the right singular subspace of \(P_{Y_\sigma(E)^\perp}|_E\) for singular values at most \(\tau\).
Compactness and the absence of an invariant \(E\) give a uniform surplus \(\dim Y_\sigma(E)\ge1+\dim C_{\tau,\sigma}(E)\) for fixed small \(\tau\) and all sufficiently small \(\sigma\).
Equality of the dimensions would force the entire active image close to \(E\) and make the leakage \(\sum_j\|P_{E^\perp}H_jP_E\|\) arbitrarily small, contrary to its positive minimum.

Given a deterministic profile \(v\in E^N\) of bounded norm with \(\sum_i v_i\otimes_{\mathbb R} v_i\succeq\gamma P_E\), Lemma 3.4 uses Cauchy–Binet to select \(d=\dim E\) anchor indices.
Their Gram operator dominates \(c\gamma^dN^{-d}P_E\).
Keep only outputs outside those indices and the omitted output \(a\), exposing all atoms except the edges between anchors and retained outputs in both directions.
A second occurrence of such an atom belongs to a discarded output.
The remaining output sums consequently have disjoint random inputs after exposure.
On the active image, the Euclidean Gram operator of each remaining map dominates \(c\sigma^2\gamma^dN^{-d-1}I\).
This calculation uses the map on real coordinates without any assumption on independence inside a planar atom.

To use a bounded joint density on a projection, Lemmas 3.5–3.6 first restrict each participating whole atom to a fixed ball.
For each anchor set, a Chernoff bound leaves at most \(\kappa N\) bad outputs, \(\kappa=1/(8p)\), apart from an exponentially small event even after the polynomial union over anchor sets.
Conditioning on the membership pattern for this particular set preserves independence of the retained good outputs.
The simultaneous good event enters through inclusion when multiplying the conditional estimates.
Conditioning directly on that event or on \(\Omega_N\) would require a new justification.

In Lemma 3.7 the profile coordinates lie in a tube around the active image.
The mesh \(c\delta/N\) per coordinate gives a total profile error \(O(\delta/\sqrt N)\), consistent with the \(O(\sqrt N)\) operator bound on \(\Omega_N\).
The exponent of \(\delta\) from the good outputs, after covering those coordinates, is at least \(7N/8-O_p(1)\) before the fixed-dimensional Grassmannian cost.
Lemmas 3.7–3.8 use the gap between successive scales to choose the active space at a net centre, allowing its dimension to vary between strata.
Section 3.5 projects a general profile onto successively smaller Gram subspaces, choosing the Gram thresholds so that the image error after multiplication by \(O(\sqrt N)\) is below the preceding polynomial floor.
There are at most \(p\) ranks and \(N\) omitted outputs.
This yields the simultaneous exponentially small probability in (3.4) on \(\Omega_N\).

## Determinant differentiation and reconstruction

The adjoint of a doubled inverse row solves the punctured homogeneous equations.
Project it onto \(\mathcal D\) and its orthogonal complement.
Theorem 3.1 bounds the first component by a polynomial in \(N\) times the second, and \(\mathcal O\) is bounded below on the fixed complement.
Summing the resulting inequality (4.2) over rows and using Jacobi's formula, each derivative with respect to a directed atom observes the two traces in (2.4).
Accounting for both ordered copies gives \(\|T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le N^{\beta/2}\|\nabla F\|_2/|F|\) on the good event, where \(F=\det T\).
Here \(F(0)=1\), its null set is Lebesgue null, and every individual real coordinate has degree at most \(2m\) because its perturbation has rank at most \(2m\).

Lemma 4.3 uses \(|q|\le v|q'|\) on a real slice of a nonzero polynomial of degree \(b\) to localize within \(bv\) of one of its complex roots.
The length bound and a union over \(k\) independent real coordinates cost \(O(k^{3/2}t)\).
For a planar block, integrating the real slices in a disk of radius \(R\) uses the joint density, and the second moment bounds the probability outside the disk.
The terms \(O(k^{3/2}Rt+kR^{-2})\) with \(R=t^{-1/2}\) give (4.6).
After whitening Gaussian blocks, the determinant still has bounded degree in each transformed direction, and the chain rule costs only a fixed factor.
For general coefficients Section 5 puts \(T\) in block upper triangular form with \(r\) simple diagonal factors.
On \(\Omega_N\), if all these factors have least singular value at least \(\tau\le1\), the finite inverse expansion gives \(\|T^{-1}\|\le C N^{(r-1)/2}\tau^{-r}\).
This accounts for the division by \(r\) in the tail exponent.
For iid entries, the localization estimate follows by centering the \(N^2\) squared moduli and bounding their sum in moment \(1+\min(\varepsilon,2)/2\).

## Wigner edges and the diagonal derivatives

In Section 6 the real coordinate of an off-diagonal atom enters symmetrically, and its imaginary coordinate enters antisymmetrically.
The doubled generators are \(\operatorname{diag}(C_\ell,C_\ell^T)\) and, for planar laws, \(\operatorname{diag}(iC_\ell,-iC_\ell^T)\).
For the anchor-to-complement exposure a retained output uses each unordered edge once.
The endpoint sign in the imaginary direction cancels in the Euclidean Gram of the map.
Lemma 6.1 consequently admits the same dimension count, and Lemma 6.2 handles independent real and planar blocks together.
The diagonals can be exposed at this stage.

For \(i<j\), the off-diagonal derivatives in (6.10)–(6.11) give the sum and the imaginary multiple of the difference of the two endpoint traces.
For \(i=j\), the diagonal derivative has a single trace, although the doubled observation has twice that trace.
Thus the sum of squared observations in (6.13) is exactly \(2N\|\nabla_{\rm off}F/F\|^2+4N\|\nabla_{\rm diag}F/F\|^2\), and the squared norm of the doubled inverse is \(2\|T^{-1}\|_{\rm HS}^2\).
The factor \(\sqrt2\) in (6.14) follows.
The proof of relative anti-concentration uses the bounded real diagonal densities on the final, unconditioned product law.

## Which moments give the rate on page 29

Section 6.4 estimates \(q_N^W\) from \(N^{-2}(2\sum_{i<j}|\zeta_{\ell,ij}|^2+\sum_i d_{\ell,i}^2)\).
The off-diagonal weak law and the \(O(N^{-1})\) expectation of the diagonal part suffice for \(q_N^W=o(1)\) in Theorem 1.5.
The further \(O(N^{-\min(\varepsilon,2)})\) estimate on p. 29 follows by applying the centered-sum moment inequality to both sums when both have the requisite moments.
If only the off-diagonal atoms have a \(2+\varepsilon\) moment, the same computation with Markov's inequality on the diagonal gives \(O(N^{-\min(\varepsilon,1)})\).

For example, take Gaussian off-diagonal atoms and a symmetric real diagonal law with bounded density whose tail is \(\mathbb P(|d|>u)\asymp u^{-2}(\log u)^{-2}\) for \(u\ge e\).
Such a density is obtained by taking a small constant multiple of \(|u|^{-3}(\log|u|)^{-2}\) for \(|u|\ge e\) and distributing the remaining mass uniformly on \([-e,e]\).
Its variance is finite.
A single \(|d_i|>\sqrt2 N\) forces \((\Omega_N^W)^c\), so independence gives \(q_N^W\gtrsim N^{-1}(\log N)^{-2}\).
This rules out the faster exponent for \(\varepsilon>1\) under just the off-diagonal extra moment.
Theorem 1.5 states \(q_N^W\to0\), and the proof of Theorem 1.4 adds this probability once when removing the logarithmic cutoff.
Both conclusions therefore hold with precisely the diagonal hypothesis in their statements.
