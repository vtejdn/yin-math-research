## Definitions and Conventions

**Definition IV.1.1, p. 169.**
A left R-module is an abelian group A with an action satisfying r(a+b)=ra+rb, (r+s)a=ra+sa and (rs)a=r(sa).
The source does not assume that R has an identity, or that an existing identity acts as the identity.
When R has an identity, a **unitary module** satisfies 1a=a.
A unitary module over a division ring is a vector space over that division ring.
The chapter defaults to left modules; right modules have the corresponding right-action axioms.
Over a commutative ring the induced symmetric right action is ar=ra.

**Definitions IV.1.2–1.4, pp. 170–171.**
An R-module homomorphism is additive and preserves the action.
A submodule is an additive subgroup closed under the action.
The submodule generated by a subset is the intersection of all submodules containing it.
A cyclic module has a single generator; a finitely generated module has a finite generating set.
A sum of submodules is the submodule generated by their union.
Empty sums and the submodule generated by the empty set are zero.

For general modules the cyclic submodule generated by a is **Ra+Za**, and a generated submodule includes finite integer as well as R-linear combinations (IV.1.5).
The simpler Ra and finite R-linear spans require the unitary setting used in the project.
A left ideal I yields the left module R/I; a quotient ring additionally requires I to be two-sided.

**Products and direct sums, IV.1.11–1.15, pp. 173–175.**
The external product consists of all coordinate families; the external direct sum consists of the families of finite support.
Actions are coordinatewise.
An internal direct sum is a sum in which every element has a unique finite decomposition into the specified submodules.
No topological closure is involved.

<a id="exact-sequences"></a>
**Definition IV.1.16 and following text, pp. 175–176.**
A sequence is exact at a module when the image of its incoming map equals the kernel of its outgoing map.
A short exact sequence has the form

\[
0\longrightarrow A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}C\longrightarrow0.
\]

Its splitting means a section of g or, equivalently by IV.1.18, a retraction of f.
Kernel, image, coimage A/ker(f), and cokernel B/Im(f) have their usual algebraic meanings.
The equivalence of splitting formulations is a theorem, not a definition imposed without justification.

## Result and Proof Audit

| Result and locator | Exact content and checked mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| IV.1.5, pp. 171–172 | Generated-submodule formulas above. Finite sums of scalar and integer multiples form a submodule and lie in every submodule containing the generators; this completes the source's exercise proof. |
| IV.1.6–1.7, p. 172 | Quotient action and factorization through A/C when C is contained in the kernel. Changing a representative by c changes its scalar multiple by rc in C; factorization is additive, R-linear and unique. In particular A/ker(f) is isomorphic to Im(f). |
| IV.1.8, p. 172 | If f(A') is contained in B', f induces A/A' to B/B'. It is surjective iff Im(f)+B'=B and injective iff f^{-1}(B') is contained in A' (the reverse containment is already assumed). These image/kernel computations check the source's cross-reference to group theory. |
| IV.1.9–1.10, p. 173 | Second and third isomorphism theorems and submodule correspondence for A/B. The maps a to a+C and a+B to a+C have precisely the displayed kernels; inverse image and quotient give inverse inclusion-preserving correspondences. |
| IV.1.11–1.13, pp. 173–174 | Products and coproducts exist with the stated coordinate actions. A map to a product is determined by every projection; a map from a direct sum is the finite sum of the specified component maps. Finite support is essential in the latter formula. |
| IV.1.14–1.15, pp. 174–175 | Finite biproducts are characterized by projection/injection identities and the sum of their composites being the identity. The resulting block maps are mutual inverses. For internal sums, intersection of any summand with the sum of the others being zero is exactly uniqueness of finite decompositions. |
| IV.1.17, pp. 176–177 | Short Five Lemma: in a commutative diagram of short exact sequences, injective outer vertical maps imply the middle map injective; surjective outer maps imply it surjective. In the first chase move a kernel element into the left image; in the second lift its right image and correct the difference using the left surjection. |
| IV.1.18, pp. 177–178 | The short exact sequence splits iff f has a retraction iff g has a section iff it is isomorphic, with fixed endpoints, to the standard sequence for A direct-sum C. Given a section h, (a,c) maps to f(a)+h(c); subtracting h(g(b)) puts b in Im(f) and gives its unique inverse decomposition. A retraction gives the complementary kernel in the same way. |

## Dependencies and Limits

Underlying abelian-group quotients, finite-support constructions and elementary set/category language are retained foundations; earlier chapters are not audited in full.
The quotient and universal-map checks above verify the R-linear interfaces where the source refers to group results.
Opposite rings (Exercise IV.1.16, p. 180) convert a right R-module into a left R-opposite module; reversing multiplication verifies the action axiom directly.
This conversion will justify right-sided uses of the chapter's left-sided basis theorem.

These are algebraic statements.
An invariant dense subspace of a Hilbert space is a module only for the specified action; neither completeness nor existence of bounded projections follows from algebraic splitting.
No source Claim about affiliated operators, dual systems or finite operator rank is imported by this unit.
