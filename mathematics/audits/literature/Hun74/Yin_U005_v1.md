## Definition and Universal Property

Let A be a right R-module and B a left R-module.
The text on **p. 207** calls a map beta from A times B into an abelian group G **middle linear** when it is additive in both variables and

\[
\beta(ar,b)=\beta(a,rb)\qquad(a\in A,\ b\in B,\ r\in R).
\]

The term **balanced biadditive map** records precisely the same conditions here.
It is not an assertion of separate R-linearity into an R-module over a noncommutative R.

**Definition IV.5.1, p. 208.**
Form the free abelian group on A times B and quotient by the subgroup generated by the two additivity relations and (ar,b)-(a,rb).
The resulting group is A tensor_R B; write a tensor b for the class of (a,b).
Every element is a finite sum of such classes.
A general element need not itself be a single pure tensor.
Over a noncommutative R the output initially has only the abelian-group structure; extra module actions require the data below.

**Theorem IV.5.2, p. 209.**
The canonical map from A times B to A tensor_R B is universal for balanced biadditive maps to abelian groups.
Thus each beta as above induces a unique additive map beta-tilde with beta-tilde(a tensor b)=beta(a,b).
Proof: the free-abelian-group map induced by beta kills exactly the required defining relations; uniqueness follows because pure tensors generate.
The usual universal-property uniqueness isomorphism is obtained by composing the two induced maps and checking generators.
This verifies existence and uniqueness rather than treating them as additional definition clauses.

**Corollary IV.5.3, p. 209.**
Right/left R-linear maps f and g induce f tensor g.
Its defining map on pairs is balanced, and functoriality follows on pure tensors; tensor products of isomorphisms are isomorphisms.

This definition and universal property are accepted for [Yin_C_F0035-P1](../../../claims/Yin_C_F0035_v1.md#yin_c_f0035-hun74-source-supplement).
In that application the map (x,y) to phi(x)y is balanced by right F-linearity of phi and associativity of multiplication.
No centrality of F is needed.

## Exactness, Actions and Composition

**Proposition IV.5.4, pp. 209–210.**
Tensoring is right exact in either variable.
For an exact sequence A to B to C to 0 of left modules and a right module D, the sequence D tensor_R A to D tensor_R B to D tensor_R C to 0 is exact.
It need not preserve a preceding injection.

Proof check: let T be (D tensor_R B) modulo the image of D tensor_R A.
For c in C choose b mapping to c, and send (d,c) to the class of d tensor b in T.
Changing the lift changes b by an image from A, hence does not change its class.
Additivity and balancing follow in the quotient.
The induced map is the inverse of the natural map T to D tensor_R C, checked on pure tensors.
This checks the only representative-dependent step of the source proof; the opposite-side argument is identical with actions reversed.

**Theorem IV.5.5, pp. 210–211.**
An S–R bimodule A and a left R-module B give a left S-action on A tensor_R B by s(a tensor b)=(sa) tensor b.
A right R-module A and an R–S bimodule B give a right S-action by (a tensor b)s=a tensor(bs).
Both are well-defined by commuting actions and the universal property.
If both outer actions are supplied, they commute.
Induced tensor maps preserve the supplied outer actions when their factors do.

**Theorem IV.5.6, pp. 211–212.**
For commutative R and its symmetric module actions, A tensor_R B has the universal property for separately R-linear, biadditive maps into R-modules.
The map furnished by IV.5.2 is R-linear since this can be checked on pure tensors.
The general balanced-group construction remains the starting point; the qualification below concerns a different presentation.

**Theorem IV.5.7, p. 212.**
Over a ring with identity, for unitary modules there are natural identifications A tensor_R R with A and R tensor_R B with B.
Multiplication is inverse to a to a tensor 1 or b to 1 tensor b; balancing verifies the composites.
Unitarity is explicitly assumed in the source theorem.

**Theorem IV.5.8, pp. 212–213.**
For A_R, an R–S bimodule B, and a left S-module C, the associativity map

\[
(A\otimes_R B)\otimes_S C\ \cong\ A\otimes_R(B\otimes_S C),
\qquad (a\otimes b)\otimes c\longmapsto a\otimes(b\otimes c)
\]

is an additive isomorphism.
The proof factors the trilinear formula successively through the two balancing relations.
Both inverse formulas descend, and their composites fix all elementary triples, which generate.
Associativity without the middle bimodule structure would not be a well-typed assertion.

**Theorem IV.5.9, pp. 213–214.**
Tensoring commutes with arbitrary direct sums in either variable.
Injections induce a map from the sum of tensors, and coordinate projections give its inverse; the image has finite support because every tensor has a finite expression and every sum-factor element has finite support.
The source does not assert commutation with arbitrary products.

**Theorem IV.5.10, p. 214 (Adjoint Associativity).**
For A_R, an R–S bimodule B, and C_S, there is an isomorphism of abelian groups

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_S(A\otimes_R B,C)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}_R\bigl(A,\operatorname{Hom}_S(B,C)\bigr).
\]

Here both displayed Hom conventions are for **right modules**, and Hom_S(B,C) has right R-action (g r)(b)=g(rb).
Currying sends f to the map a to (b to f(a tensor b)); uncurrying sends h to the map induced by (a,b) to h(a)(b).
Right R-linearity makes that last map balanced; right S-linearity makes its induced map S-linear.
These formulas are mutually inverse.
The word adjoint in this theorem refers to the tensor–Hom adjunction, not a Hilbert-space adjoint.

<a id="tensor-basis-expansion"></a>
## Basis Expansion and Extension of Scalars

**Theorem IV.5.11, p. 215.**
Suppose R has an identity, A is a unitary right R-module and F is a free left R-module with basis Y.
Then

\[
A\otimes_R F\cong\bigoplus_{y\in Y} A,
\qquad \sum_y a_y\otimes y\longleftrightarrow(a_y)_{y\in Y}.
\]

The coefficient family has finite support and is unique.
This is the precise meaning of uniqueness in the source: ordering terms differently or inserting zero terms does not give a different coefficient family.
Proof: F is the direct sum of Ry; each Ry is isomorphic to R, so IV.5.9 and IV.5.7 identify the tensor with the displayed sum.
The composite takes a tensor y to the y-coordinate a, verifying the asserted coefficient map.
The empty basis and zero tensor cause no exception.

**Corollary IV.5.12, pp. 215–216.**
If A_R and the left module B are free with bases X and Y, their tensor is free as a right R-module with basis consisting of x tensor y, **using the extra right action on B induced by its chosen left basis**.
Explicitly,

\[
\left(\sum_y r_y y\right)s=\sum_y(r_y s)y.
\]

This commutes with the left action.
Under IV.5.11 the induced right action on the tensor becomes the componentwise right action on copies of A, and its basis is as stated.
The source's preceding remark supplies this otherwise easily missed extra structure.
If B already has a natural right R-action, it need not be this basis-induced action.
The corollary must not be used to compute the rank for a different specified bimodule structure.

**Corollary IV.5.13, p. 216.**
If R is a subring of S containing 1_S and F is free as a left R-module with basis X, S tensor_R F is free as a left S-module with basis 1 tensor x.
The coefficient isomorphism of IV.5.11 respects the natural outer left S-action.
In particular this is extension of scalars, not an identification S tensor_R S with S in an arbitrary extension.

## Local Qualification of the Alternate Presentation on p. 212

After IV.5.6, the source gives an alternate construction for commutative R with identity: take a free R-module on A times B and impose additivity and the two scalar-linearity relations.
It does not restate that A and B are unitary.
Under the chapter's general module convention and its unitary free-module convention, the unrestricted equivalence with Definition IV.5.1 fails.

Direct countercheck: let R=Z and let both underlying additive groups A=B=Z have **trivial R-action**.
Definition IV.5.1 imposes ordinary biadditivity; its balancing relations are automatic, so its group is Z, generated by 1 tensor 1.
The map (m,n) to mn verifies that this generator is nonzero.
In the alternative unitary free-module quotient, the relation for r=1 gives

\[
[(0,b)]-1[(a,b)]=0.
\]

Additivity gives [(0,b)]=0, and the unitary free-module identity then kills every [(a,b)].
That quotient is zero.
Thus the two constructions are not equivalent in this nonunitary example.

Accepted restriction: use this alternate free-R-module presentation **for unitary A and B**.
Then A tensor_R B is unitary, the free-R-module map descends with the same bilinear universal property, and the standard uniqueness argument applies.
Definition IV.5.1, IV.5.2 and the explicitly unitary IV.5.7 are unaffected.
This is a locally checked hypothesis qualification, not a claim of author-confirmed errata.
The [bounded search record](readme.md) states the external-search limit.

Exercise IV.5.9(a), p. 217, similarly needs a unitary B when importing (R/I) tensor_R B isomorphic to B/IB: with I=0 and a nonzero trivial-action B the claimed unrestricted form fails.
In the unitary setting the maps are (r+I) tensor b to rb+IB and b+IB to (1+I) tensor b; balancing and I annihilation prove they are inverse.
No existing project consumer uses a nonunitary version, so this qualification does not change a current Claim conclusion.

## Selected Exercise Checks and Available Exactness Input

Exercise IV.5.7, p. 217, demonstrates failure of left exactness: Z/2 injects into Z/4 by 1 to 2, but tensoring with Z/2 sends its generator to 2 tensor 1 = 1 tensor 2 = 0.
Both tensor groups are Z/2 under their usual module structures, so the resulting map is not injective.

For **unitary modules over a ring with identity**, the cases of Exercise IV.5.8, p. 217, are checked as follows.
Tensoring a split short exact sequence preserves its section/retraction and therefore preserves exactness.
A free tensor factor gives a direct sum of copies of the original sequence by IV.5.7 and IV.5.9, hence preserves exactness.
A projective factor is a direct summand of a free factor by IV.3.4; its tensor sequence is a direct summand of that exact sequence and is exact.
These are selected elementary source-exercise verifications, not an audit of every exercise.

In particular, over a division ring every unitary vector space is free, so tensoring preserves short exact sequences in either variable.
Equivalently, use algebraic basis complements to split the original sequence.
This accepted standard consequence is available for future arguments; it is not evidence that a completed analytic tensor functor preserves an injection, or that a bounded scalar rational value has a bounded complete inverse pencil.
