# Dicks' Ore obstruction for a periodic lamp

<a id="lls03-u001"></a>
## A lamp and the shift

In Section 3, Theorem 11, take the restricted wreath product \(W=C_d\wr\mathbb Z\), \(d\ge2\), with lamp generator \(a\) at height zero and shift \(x\).
For any nonzero commutative unital coefficient ring \(k\), the equality \(u(a-1)=v(x-1)\) in \(kW\) implies that some nonzero \(w\in kW\) satisfies \(uw=0\).
The infinite-order element \(x\) gives a regular denominator \(x-1\), so the equality rules out the corresponding Ore fraction.
The authors attribute the proof of the theorem to Warren Dicks. [Theorem 11, preprint pp. 5–6](../../../literature/pdf/LLS03--arxiv-v2.pdf#page=5)

I checked the presentation, the orientation of the equality, and the use of finitely many shifted lamps to annihilate the possible multiplier in the proof.
For \(k=\mathbb C\) and \(a^2=1\), taking adjoints converts the equation \((a-1)c=(x^{-1}-1)b\) to the theorem's equation with \(u=c^*\) and \(v=b^*\).
Thus a putative right denominator \(c\) in this equation is a zero divisor.
The source proves the group-ring Ore obstruction with a finite lamp.
The rate of approximation by height-limited kernels and the case of an arbitrary lamp group require separate arguments.

The convention for the right Ore condition in Definition 1, preprint p. 1, agrees with the [recorded classical fraction convention](../Lin06/Yin_U001.md#crossed-product-and-ore-conventions).
This inspection covered the indicated passages of the [second preprint](readme.md#preprint-and-proceedings-editions).
