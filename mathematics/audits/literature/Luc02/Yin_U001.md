### Terminology and affiliated fractions

The source calls an expression of the form \(s^{-1}r\) a left fraction and an expression of the form \(r'(s')^{-1}\) a right fraction.
The side therefore describes the position of the inverse denominator.
The surrounding definition constructs the right Ore localization from pairs consisting of a numerator and a denominator; its addition formula chooses a common right multiple of the two denominators.

For a finite von Neumann algebra \(\mathcal M\), an expression \(u=s^{-1}a=bt^{-1}\), with \(s,a,b,t\in\mathcal M\) and \(s,t\) invertible in \(L^0(\mathcal M)\), consists of a left fraction and a right fraction in this terminology.
The four-entry name fraction pair is not attributed to Luc02.
This terminology check supplies no existence or common-denominator theorem for affiliated operators and imports no localization theorem as a proof premise.
