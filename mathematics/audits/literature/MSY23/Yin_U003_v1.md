### Conventions and Dependency Boundary

Put \(R=\mathbb C\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\rangle\) and let \(F\) be its universal free field.
An affine linear matrix has entries of degree at most one; it need not have invertible constant term.
A full square matrix has inner rank equal to its size.
A linear representation in Definition 4.2 is

\[
r=uA^{-1}v,\qquad A\in M_N(R)\text{ affine linear and full},\quad
u\in M_{1,N}(\mathbb C),\ v\in M_{N,1}(\mathbb C).
\]

Neither minimality nor regularity at zero is imposed.
The source's Theorem 4.1, p. 21, supplies existence of \(F\), preservation of polynomial matrix inner rank in \(F\), and inversion of full square matrices.
These are the same type of foundational interfaces retained in [Reu96](../../../literature/pdf/Reu96.pdf), Sections 1–2, pp.93–98.
They are not consequences of a bounded operator realization.

The source attributes this construction to its Cohn book reference [14], registered as Coh06, *Free Ideal Rings and Localization in General Rings*.
The selected primary book interfaces are identified in [MSY23-U001](Yin_U001_v1.md#msy23-u001); this is distinct from the repository's Coh06b historical survey.
The inspected [CR94](../../../literature/pdf/CR94.pdf) supplies selected representation statements, with source-local numbers converted to section-prefixed numbers: Proposition 2.10, Theorem 3.2, Theorem 4.1, and Corollary 4.2.
Its stronger free-field comparison hypotheses include \(k=Z(D)\), infinite \(k\), and \([D:k]=\infty\).
Those results are not directly invoked here with \(D=k=\mathbb C\), and their minimality conclusions are unnecessary for Section 4's proof chain.

All numbered locators below use the 50-page published version.
In the earlier arXiv v2 interface checked for this project, Proposition 4.9 corresponds to published Proposition 4.11.
No complete cross-version numbering map is asserted.

### Theorem 4.3: Constructive Existence of Representations

Once the free-field foundation above is accepted, existence can be verified by finite arithmetic.
Constants have one-dimensional representations; \(x_j\) is the upper-right entry of

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&-x_j\\0&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.
\]

For representations \((u,A,v)\) and \((s,B,t)\), sums use the pencil \(\operatorname{diag}(A,B)\), row \((u,s)\), and column \((v,t)^T\).
Products use

\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}A&-vs\\0&B\end{pmatrix},\quad
(u,0)C^{-1}\binom{0}{t}=(uA^{-1}v)(sB^{-1}t).
\]

For nonzero \(r=uA^{-1}v\), the affine pencil

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-v&A\end{pmatrix}
\]

has Schur complement \(r\), is invertible over \(F\), and satisfies \((H^{-1})_{11}=r^{-1}\).
Each pencil is full by Theorem 4.1, and these operations produce every element of the division ring generated by \(R\).
The argument also checks the order of noncommuting factors.
Using only constants, generators, sums, and products gives polynomial representations whose pencils are unit upper triangular, hence invertible after evaluation in every unital complex algebra.
This verifies the polynomial-consistency construction used in Remark 4.6.

### Theorem 4.4 and Definition 4.5: Consistency on the Chosen Domain

The ambient algebra \(\mathcal A\) is unital and **stably finite**: one-sided inverses in every \(M_N(\mathcal A)\) are two-sided inverses.
For a representation \(\rho=(u,A,v)\),

\[
\operatorname{dom}_{\mathcal A}(\rho)
=\{X\in\mathcal A^n:A(X)\in GL_N(\mathcal A)\},\qquad
\operatorname{dom}_{\mathcal A}(r)
=\bigcup_{\rho\text{ representing }r}\operatorname{dom}_{\mathcal A}(\rho).
\]

Theorem 4.4 proves that the values agree on overlaps.
It does not assert that a specified tuple belongs to this union.

User-requested Schur-complement clarification, 2026-09-09: the matrix calculation at the center of the proof is precisely

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&-uA^{-1}\\0&I_N\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\v&A\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-A^{-1}v&I_N\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}-uA^{-1}v&0\\0&A\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{SC}
\]

The two triangular factors are invertible in the same algebra in which A is invertible.
The proof applies (SC) first in the free field and then in the chosen evaluation algebra.
The bridge between those applications is polynomial inner-rank preservation and evaluation of a polynomial rank factorization; stable finiteness detects the vanishing scalar Schur complement after evaluation.
Thus Schur elimination is the core calculation, while these two rank facts justify transporting the zero identity.
With the ambient algebra U(M), (SC) is an affiliated identity and the triangular factors need not be bounded even when the scalar compression is bounded.
It does not by itself construct a different bounded inverse pencil.

Here is the source's proof with the rank step made explicit.
Subtract two representations by using \(\operatorname{diag}(A_1,-A_2)\), thereby reducing to a representation \(uA^{-1}v=0\) in \(F\).
Its bordered polynomial matrix

\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\v&A\end{pmatrix}
\]

is equivalent over \(F\) to \(\operatorname{diag}(0,I_N)\), so it has inner rank \(N\) over \(F\), and therefore over \(R\) by Theorem 4.1.
A factorization of \(L\) through \(N\) columns evaluates in every ambient algebra.

If \(A(X)\) is invertible but \(s=uA(X)^{-1}v\ne0\), block elimination makes \(L(X)\) equivalent over \(\mathcal A\) to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_N,-s)\).
This matrix cannot factor through \(N\) columns.
Indeed, a proposed factorization yields, on its first \(N\) rows and columns, square matrices \(E,G\) with \(EG=I_N\).
Stable finiteness gives \(GE=I_N\).
The last-row/first-column-block equation is \(dG=0\), so \(d=0\), forcing the last diagonal entry to vanish.
Factorizations through fewer columns can be padded to this size.
This contradicts the evaluated polynomial factorization.
Thus \(s=0\), proving consistency.

The nonfinite example on p. 22 correctly explains the hypothesis: a backward unilateral shift \(l\) has \(ll^*=1\), \(l^*l\ne1\), so the free-field identity \(y(xy)^{-1}x=1\) fails under the expression evaluation \((x,y)=(l,l^*)\).
The source's displayed intermediate inverse is suppressed because \(ll^*=1\); the example's conclusion is unchanged.

### Definitions 4.7 and 4.10: Two Closures in One Ambient Algebra

For a unital subalgebra \(B\subseteq\mathcal A\), write

\[
\operatorname{Rat}_{\mathcal A}(B)
=\{\text{entries of }C^{-1}:C\in M_N(B),\ C\in GL_N(\mathcal A),\ N\ge1\},
\]

and let \(\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal A}(B)\) be the smallest subalgebra containing \(B\) and closed under inverses of its elements that are units **in \(\mathcal A\)**.
A division-closed subalgebra need not be a division ring.
In particular, the phrase “division algebra” in Definition 4.10(ii) is to be read through the preceding closure definition, not as an additional assertion that all its nonzero elements are units.

Lemma 4.9 is checked directly by the sum/product block formulas above, which apply to invertible matrices over \(B\).
An arbitrary element of \(B\) is an entry of the inverse of a two-by-two unit triangular matrix over \(B\).
Hence the rational closure is a subalgebra containing \(B\).

### Lemma 4.8: Linearization Preserves an Existing Inverse Domain

No stable-finiteness hypothesis is needed for this lemma.
Suppose \(P\in M_m(R)\), \(P(X)\in GL_m(\mathcal A)\), and the desired element is a constant compression of \(P(X)^{-1}\).
The polynomial constructions already given, applied to each entry and assembled with constant coordinate inclusions, give

\[
P=UQ^{-1}V,
\]

where \(Q\) is affine linear and unit upper triangular, and \(U,V\) are constant rectangular matrices.
For every tuple in every unital algebra, \(Q(X)\) is invertible.
Schur elimination for

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}0&U\\-V&Q\end{pmatrix}
\]

shows that \(H(X)\) is invertible exactly when \(P(X)\) is, and the upper-left block of \(H(X)^{-1}\) equals \(P(X)^{-1}\).
This proves Lemma 4.8 without importing an unchecked domain implication from Helton-Mai-Speicher [25, Theorem 4.12].
It also verifies the sign adaptation noted by MSY23.
In an arbitrary ambient algebra the lemma does not claim that the formal pencil is full; that adjective is correctly absent from its statement.
In a stably finite ambient algebra, evaluated invertibility forces formal fullness, since a nonfull polynomial factorization would evaluate to a forbidden factorization of an invertible square matrix through fewer columns.

Thus the lemma starts with membership in \(\operatorname{Rat}_{\mathcal A}(\mathbb C\langle X\rangle)\).
It does not start merely with a bounded value of an expression evaluated in a larger affiliated algebra.

### Complete Division Closure and Proposition 4.11

The p. 26 scalar inverse argument has a printed formula error: for \(r=uA^{-1}v\), it asserts that \(r^{-1}\) is an entry of \(\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\v&A\end{pmatrix}\).
The displayed matrix lacks an inverse, and the upper-left entry of its inverse is \(-r^{-1}\).
Replacing it by the inverse of \(\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-v&A\end{pmatrix}\) gives the claimed entry with the correct sign.
Both omissions were confirmed visually.

The same argument proves matrix closure.
For any matrix \(T\) over \(\operatorname{Rat}_{\mathcal A}(B)\), assemble representations of its finitely many entries into \(T=UQ^{-1}V\), with \(Q\) an invertible matrix over \(B\) and \(U,V\) constant.
If \(T\) is invertible in the ambient matrix algebra, the corrected bordered matrix is invertible, and its inverse contains \(T^{-1}\).
Every entry of \(T^{-1}\) therefore lies in the rational closure.
Conversely, any completely division-closed subalgebra containing \(B\) must contain the inverses used in its definition.
Thus rational closure equals complete division closure, and

\[
\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal A}(B)\subseteq\operatorname{Rat}_{\mathcal A}(B).
\]

Proposition 4.11 states equality if either closure is a division ring.
To prove Lemma 4.12 locally, let \(D=\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal A}(B)\) be a division ring.
Gaussian elimination over \(D\) sends every square matrix \(T\) over \(D\) to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_k,0)\) by invertible matrices over \(D\).
If \(T\) is invertible over \(\mathcal A\), then \(k\) must be its full size: a zero diagonal block cannot have a two-sided inverse in a nonzero unital algebra.
Hence \(T^{-1}\) is already over \(D\).
This proves complete division closedness without the source's deferred proof reference [56].
If instead the rational closure is a division ring, every nonzero element of the division closure has an inverse in that larger ring and therefore in \(\mathcal A\); the definition puts the inverse back in the division closure.
This reduces to the preceding case.

### Lemma 4.13: Elimination and the Second Formula Repair

Over a division ring, choose a nonzero pivot \(a\) and write

\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\d&B\end{pmatrix},\quad
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-da^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix},\quad
V=\begin{pmatrix}1&-a^{-1}c\\0&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The product is \(UTV=\operatorname{diag}(a,B-da^{-1}c)\), whereas p. 27 prints \(1\) in place of \(a\).
Multiplying on the left by \(\operatorname{diag}(a^{-1},I)\) supplies the omitted normalization.
Iteration gives \(\operatorname{diag}(I_k,0)\).
Its inner rank is \(k\): it factors through \(k\) columns, and a smaller factorization would express the identity map of a \(k\)-dimensional right vector space over the division ring through a smaller-dimensional space.
This proves both the rectangular version and the stated square invertibility consequence.
The repaired argument has no analytic boundedness hypothesis or conclusion.

### Exact Bounded-Realization Consequences

For \(B=\mathbb C\langle X\rangle\subseteq M\subseteq U(M)\), the definitions always distinguish

\[
\operatorname{Div}_{M}(B)\subseteq\operatorname{Rat}_{M}(B)
\subseteq\operatorname{Rat}_{U(M)}(B)\cap M.
\]

Under the free-field realization of [MSY23-U004](Yin_U004_v1.md#msy23-u004), the last rational closure is the affiliated free field \(F_X\).
Lemma 4.8 says that membership in the middle ring is equivalent to existence of a linear pencil representation invertible over \(M\).
It does not prove \(F_X\cap M\subseteq\operatorname{Rat}_M(B)\).
Even that additional bounded-realization implication would initially yield a matrix rational-closure statement; the passage from \(\operatorname{Rat}_M(B)\) to scalar \(\operatorname{Div}_M(B)\) would need its own justification.
Proposition 4.11 cannot be applied by replacing its closure with the different closure computed in \(U(M)\).

Boundedness of one compression does not force the inverse of an arbitrary representing pencil to be bounded: for a semicircular \(S\), \(r=1\) has the representation \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,x)\), \(u=(1,0)\), \(v=(1,0)^T\), while \(A(S)^{-1}\) contains the unbounded affiliated operator \(S^{-1}\).
This deliberately nonminimal representation does not refute a minimal-representation or existence assertion.
No such stronger assertion, or resolution of the project's bounded scalar descent, is claimed here.

The separately inspected HMS18 source (MSY23 reference [25]; arXiv:1511.05330v2, Section 5.4, Theorem 5.7, printed p. 40) does not erase this distinction.
For a matrix of rational expressions regular at zero over a stably finite ambient algebra, its minimal-descriptor result includes the existing expression domain in the descriptor domain.
With ambient \(M\), it starts from an expression already evaluable in \(M\); with ambient \(U(M)\), it yields affiliated invertibility.
Lemmas 5.5-5.6 on pp. 38-40 compare full realization and minimal realization domains under those hypotheses, rather than infer boundedness of an entire inverse from one bounded compression.
This is a selected primary-statement interface, not a full audit of HMS18 or a claim about every nonregular free-field minimal representation.
