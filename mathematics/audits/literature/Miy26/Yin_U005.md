### Statement and hypotheses

Let `(M,tau)` be the finite tracial von Neumann algebra generated by a standard free circular family `c_1,...,c_d`, with faithful normal trace.
Put

\[
c_i=(s_i+\mathrm i s_{d+i})/\sqrt2,
\quad u_i=(e_i+\mathrm i e_{d+i})/\sqrt2,
\quad u_{\bar i}=(e_i-\mathrm i e_{d+i})/\sqrt2.
\]

On the full Fock space over `C^(2d)`,

\[
c_i=l(u_i)+l(u_{\bar i})^*,\qquad
c_i^*=l(u_{\bar i})+l(u_i)^*.
\]

The variance convention is `tau(c_i^*c_j)=delta_ij`.
The complex conjugation in the annihilation label is essential.
The adjoint operation on words is `w^*=bar(w_n)...bar(w_1)`, where `bar(bar(i))=i`.

Distinguish the full GNS space `L2(M,tau)=L2(c,c*,tau)` from

\[
H^2_c=\overline{\mathbb C\langle c\rangle}^{\|\cdot\|_2}
=L^2(c,\tau).
\]

The latter is a closed Hilbert subspace, not the full GNS space of a self-adjoint algebra.
The map `c_w -> e_w` identifies it with `F(C^d)`; the words `c_w` are orthonormal there.
Left multiplication by `c_i` restricts on this subspace to the left creation operator `l_i`.

Lemma 4.1 states that for a holomorphic polynomial `f`, if the affiliated inverse `(lambda-f)^(-1)` exists and belongs to `L2(M,tau)`, then it belongs to `H2_c`.
Corollary 4.2 states, for every scalar noncommutative polynomial `P` including constants,

\[
\operatorname{spec}_{M}(P(c))
=\operatorname{spec}_{B(\mathcal F(\mathbb C^d))}(P(l)).
\]

No adjoints of the indeterminates are allowed in this polynomial.
No joint-spectrum, arbitrary rational-evaluation, matrix-valued, or free-ball statement is asserted by this corollary.

### Lemma 4.1: repaired proof and exact coefficient identity

Let `q=(lambda-f)^(-1) in L2(M,tau)`.
By [Miy26-U003](Yin_U003.md#miy26-u003), Corollary 1.2 first upgrades this affiliated inverse to a bounded inverse in `M`.
This order matters: the ordinary bounded inverse commutator formula must not be used on an unbounded inverse without a domain argument.

Put `D_i=r(u_bar(i))^*`.
The creation and annihilation relations give `[D_i,c_j]=0`, and thus `[D_i,q]=0` as bounded operators.
For a mixed word `w` let `U_w` be its Wick polynomial, characterized by `U_w Omega=u_w`.
Its Wick expansion gives the exact relation

\[
[D_i,U_w]u_v=
\begin{cases}
u_a,&w=a\bar i v^*,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]

For `q Omega=sum_w alpha_w u_w`, the resulting identity is

\[
[D_i,q]u_{v^*}
=\sum_{a\in\{1,\ldots,d,\bar1,\ldots,\bar d\}^*}
\alpha_{a\bar i v}u_a.
\tag{2}
\]

To justify the passage from finite Wick sums to (2), take those sums converging to `q` in `L2`.
Their action on any fixed vector `U_v Omega` converges because right multiplication by `U_v` is bounded on `L2(M,tau)`.
The same applies to `D_i u_v`, which is zero or a polynomial vector.
Applying bounded `D_i` then passes the commutator identity to the limit.
This uses `L2` convergence on each polynomial vector, not an unsupported operator-norm convergence of Wick truncations.

Every coefficient of a word containing a barred letter occurs on the right of (2), and `[D_i,q]=0` forces it to vanish.
Hence `q in H2_c`.

The displayed sum on source p. 13 prints an inadequate index `w in [d]`; it must run over the entire mixed-word monoid as in (2).
Equation (1) and the following coefficient-extraction argument fix the intended labels in (2).
The phrase `i in [d]^*` before the inverse formula should be `i in [d]`.
These are local transcription corrections, not changes to the lemma.

### Corollary 4.2: both inclusions with explicit inverse construction

Write `A=lambda-P(c)` and `B=lambda-P(l)`.
The unitary identification `Phi:F(Cd)->H2_c` intertwines their left actions.

If `B` has a bounded inverse, set `X=Phi(B^(-1)Omega)`.
Then `X in L2(M,tau)` and `AX=1`.
This one-sided relation is sufficient in a finite von Neumann algebra: `AX=1` makes the range of `A` dense, so `ker(A*)=0`; equality of the trace dimensions of `ker(A)` and `ker(A*)` gives `ker(A)=0`.
Thus `A` has its affiliated inverse and `X=A^(-1)`.
Equivalently one may use direct finiteness of the affiliated algebra.
Corollary 1.2 now makes the inverse bounded, proving this resolvent inclusion.
Merely asserting existence of a square-summable formal coefficient sequence would not discharge this step.

Conversely, let `q=A^(-1)` be bounded.
Lemma 4.1 puts `q` in `H2_c`.
For every positive word `w`, choose holomorphic polynomial approximants `q_n -> q` in `L2`; then `q_n c_w -> q c_w` in `L2`, so `q c_w in H2_c`.
By density and boundedness, `q` preserves `H2_c`.
Both `A` and `q` therefore restrict to this subspace, where their two inverse identities remain valid.
Under `Phi`, the restriction of `q` is a bounded two-sided inverse of `B`.

This restriction argument is an elementary reconstruction of the source corollary.
It removes the need to fill in the source's abbreviated exponential-coefficient-decay step on p. 14.
The source homogeneous creation estimate itself is exact at `q=0`:

\[
\Big\|\sum_{|w|=n}\beta_w l_w\Big\|^2
=\sum_{|w|=n}|\beta_w|^2,
\]

because the ranges of equal-length creations are orthogonal.
The `c` in the printed final decay bound should be the previously chosen number `A<1`.
The full external q-Fock estimate is unnecessary for this local proof.
