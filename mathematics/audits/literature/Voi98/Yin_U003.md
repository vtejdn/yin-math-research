## Proposition 3.6: Freely Adjoining Coefficients

Let $B,C\subseteq M$ be unital star-subalgebras and suppose $A=B[X]$ is freely independent of $C$.
For every integer $p\geq1$, the source states

$$
J_p(X:B)=J_p(X:W^*(B\cup C)),
\tag{1}
$$

with existence on either side equivalent to existence on the other.
The moment-identity meaning of coefficient closure and the well-definedness condition for evaluated derivations are those in U002.
First prove the algebraic extension to $B\vee C$, the unital star-algebra generated by $B\cup C$, and then use bounded strong-star coefficient approximation.

If an enlarged conjugate exists, its conditional expectation onto $W^*(A)$ solves the original identities.
Conversely let $\xi=J_p(X:B)\in L^1(W^*(A))$.
By centering and multilinearity, it suffices to test words

$$
w=c_0P_1c_1\cdots P_nc_n,
\quad P_j\in A,\quad c_j\in C,
$$

where all $P_j$ and all interior $c_1,\ldots,c_{n-1}$ have zero trace; $c_0,c_n$ need not.
Centering a factor reduces to words of shorter alternating length, so this spanning reduction is an induction, not an additional hypothesis on the original word.

For $n\geq2$, $\tau(\xi w)=0$.
To apply freeness when $\xi$ is unbounded, first use centered bounded truncations of $\xi$ converging in $L^1$.
Alternating centered-word identities apply to those truncations, and the bounded remaining product permits passage in the trace pairing.
The mean-zero condition $\tau(\xi)=0$ is supplied by its conjugate relation at $1$.

In $\partial^{(p)}w$, all differentiations fall on the $P_j$ factors.
A term can be indexed by nonnegative integers $k_1,\ldots,k_n$ with total $p$, giving the number of cuts in each $P_j$.
If an interior $P_j$ is uncut, the corresponding tensor trace contains a centered alternating segment and vanishes.
The same reasoning at the two ends forces the first and last $P_j$ to be cut.
Thus only terms with every $k_j\geq1$ can survive.
Each such term still has, between the derivatives of $P_1$ and $P_2$, a tensor component $a c_1 b$ with $a,b\in A$.
Its trace is $\tau(c_1)\tau(ba)=0$.
Hence the product trace is zero when $n\geq2$ as well.
This describes the source's formula $(*)$ while retaining every coefficient separator.

For $n=1$,

$$
\tau(\xi c_0P_1c_1)
=\tau(c_0)\tau(c_1)\tau(\xi P_1).
$$

Expanding the two endpoint coefficients into centered and scalar parts proves this identity: the possible contraction of the two endpoint centered factors also contains $\tau(\xi)\tau(P_1)=0$.
On the derivative side, $\partial^{(p)}P_1$ is a sum of pure $A$ tensors, so the endpoint trace factors give the same $\tau(c_0)\tau(c_1)$ times $\tau^{\otimes(p+1)}(\partial^{(p)}P_1)$.
For $n=0$, both sides vanish since $\xi$ is centered and free from $C$.
This completes the moment identity in all cases and then uniqueness proves (1).

**Printed separator error.**
In the simplified formula $(**)$ on p. 198, the factor

$$
1^{\otimes k_1}\otimes c_1\otimes1^{\otimes(p-k_1)}
$$

between the derivatives of $P_1$ and $P_2$ is missing.
The following page explicitly relies on that factor, so the correct $(*)$ and the argument above repair the proof.
For an exact failure of the printed expression, take $p=n=2$, $P_1=P_2=S$ standard semicircular, $c_0=c_2=1$, and $c_1=T$ centered and free from $S$.
The correct derivative is $\partial_S^{(2)}(STS)=1\otimes T\otimes1$, with product trace zero.
Dropping $T$ gives product trace one.
Later stray $s,j_s$ indices in $(**)$ must be specialized to $n,n$; its last tensor exponent as printed is not a valid nonnegative count.
The ordered-cut definition removes these transcription ambiguities without changing the statement of Proposition 3.6.

## Proposition 3.7: Projection for a Free Sum

Let $X_j=X_j^*$, $Y_j=Y_j^*\in M$ for $1\leq j\leq n$, with

$$
B[X_1,\ldots,X_n]\quad\text{free from}\quad C[Y_1,\ldots,Y_n].
$$

There is no assumption of freeness among the coordinates of either tuple.
Let $Z_j=X_j+Y_j$ and $D=W^*((B\vee C)[Z_1,\ldots,Z_n])$.
If $J_p(X_1:B[X_2,\ldots,X_n])$ exists, then

$$
J_p(Z_1:(B\vee C)[Z_2,\ldots,Z_n])
=E_D J_p(X_1:B[X_2,\ldots,X_n]).
\tag{2}
$$

If additionally $X_1$ is free from $B[X_2,\ldots,X_n]$ and the scalar $J_p(X_1:\mathbb C)$ exists, the right side can be replaced by $E_DJ_p(X_1:\mathbb C)$.

To check the proof, first adjoin $C[Y_1,\ldots,Y_n]$ using Proposition 3.6.
The resulting $\partial_{X_1}$ kills every $Y_j$, $B,C$, and $X_j$ for $j\ne1$.
On the algebra of the $Z_j$ it therefore agrees with $\partial_{Z_1}$, including its iterates: it sends $Z_1$ to $1\otimes1$ and the other generators to zero, and the derivative outputs stay in the smaller tensor algebra.
Equality of product-trace pairings and the conditional expectation property give (2).
When original algebraic freeness is used to define these evaluated derivations, the enlarged algebra is an algebraic free product by faithfulness and free independence.
The substitution $X_1\mapsto X_1+Y_1$ is an invertible triangular algebraic substitution over the enlarged coefficient algebra, so the required algebraic freeness persists.
This does not assert that mere freeness of the two blocks makes the coordinates within a block algebraically free.

Conditional expectation is contractive on $L^q$ for $1\leq q\leq\infty$, so (2) transfers the same $L^q$ bound whenever the original conjugate belongs to that space.
In the $q=2$ applications this is precisely the orthogonal-projection mechanism used for Fisher information.

## Proposition 3.8: The Semicircular Higher Conjugates

For a standard semicircular $S$, define the **monic, orthonormal** polynomials

$$
P_0=1,\quad P_1=S,\quad P_{k+1}=SP_k-P_{k-1}.
$$

Then, for every $k\geq1$,

$$
J_k(S:\mathbb C)=P_k=U_k(S/2),
\tag{3}
$$

where $U_k$ denotes the classical Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.
There is no $k!$ factor.
The source's word "orthogonalization" in Proposition 3.8 must be understood with this normalization, fixed explicitly in its proof and in Corollary 3.9.

The local proof can be checked without an external orthogonal-polynomial theorem.
On $\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)$ put $S=\ell+\ell^*$, with vacuum $e_0$.
The recurrence gives $P_k(S)e_0=e_k$, proving orthonormality and the stated leading coefficient.
The scalar moment $\tau(P_kS^m)$ counts paths of $m$ up/down steps which start at zero, remain nonnegative, and end at height $k$.
Every such path splits uniquely, at its $k$ unmatched up steps, into $k+1$ Dyck excursions.
Consequently

$$
\tau(P_kS^m)=
\sum_{n_0+\cdots+n_k=m-k\atop n_i\geq0}
\tau(S^{n_0})\cdots\tau(S^{n_k}),
\tag{4}
$$

the empty sum being zero if $m<k$.
The right side is exactly the product trace of the ordered-cut formula for $\partial^{(k)}S^m$, proving (3).

The source instead proves (4) inductively: subtract the $n_{p+1}=0$ terms using $P_{p+1}=SP_p-P_{p-1}$, then split the remaining last moment by the semicircular Catalan recurrence.
Its generating-function argument for that recurrence uses $G^2-zG+1=0$; coefficient comparison agrees with the path count above.
Both the induction indices and the boundary $m<k$ agree after treating negative-total-degree sums as empty.
In particular $J_2=S^2-1$ and $J_3=S^3-2S$.

## Corollary 3.9: Regularization and the Correct Powers

Let $S$ be standard semicircular and free from $B[X]$.
For every $t>0$ and $k\geq1$,

$$
J_k(X+\sqrt tS:B)
=t^{-k/2}E_{W^*(B[X+\sqrt tS])}P_k(S).
\tag{5}
$$

To obtain this, apply (2) with the semicircular variable as the first summand and the original $X$ as the other block.
Formula (3) and the scaling relation

$$
J_k(\lambda Y:B)=\lambda^{-k}J_k(Y:B),
\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\},
$$

give (5).
The scaling relation follows by counting the $k$ removed occurrences in each word; negative real $\lambda$ is allowed, while zero is not.
Freeness of the semicircle from the full original algebra supplies algebraic freeness and the triangular-substitution argument above, even when the original $X$ has relations with $B$.

All conjugates in (5) are bounded.
Specifically the correct first-order estimates are

$$
\|J(X+\sqrt tS:B)\|\leq2t^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|J(X+\sqrt tS:B)\|_2\leq t^{-1/2}.
\tag{6}
$$

They use $\|S\|=2$, $\|S\|_2=1$ and contractivity of conditional expectation.
Directly, (5) also gives $\|J_k\|_2\leq t^{-k/2}$ and $\|J_k\|\leq(k+1)t^{-k/2}$, since $\|P_k\|_2=1$ and $\sup_{[-2,2]}|U_k(x/2)|=k+1$.

**Printed bounds are false as written.**
The first bound on p. 202 has $X+t^{-1/2}S$ instead of $X+t^{1/2}S$ inside $J$; the second has $t^{1/2}$ on the right instead of $t^{-1/2}$.
For $X=0$, $B=\mathbb C$, the exact identity $J(\lambda S)=\lambda^{-1}S$ refutes the first printed bound whenever $t>1$ and the second whenever $0<t<1$.
Equations (5)-(6), and the source's proof itself, fix both exponents.
No assertion of uniform boundedness as $t\downarrow0$ survives: the correct estimate diverges.

## Proposition 3.10: Infinitesimal Microstates-Entropy Variation

For a bounded selfadjoint tuple $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$, assume the **microstates** free entropy $\chi(X_1,\ldots,X_n)>-\infty$ and that all first conjugates

$$
J_j=J(X_j:\mathbb C[X_1,\ldots,\widehat X_j,\ldots,X_n])
$$

exist in $L^1$.
For selfadjoint scalar noncommutative polynomials $P_j$ evaluated at the tuple, the result is

$$
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\chi(X_1+\varepsilon P_1,\ldots,X_n+\varepsilon P_n)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}
=\sum_{j=1}^n\tau(J_jP_j).
\tag{7}
$$

The imported change-of-variables input supplies the derivative as $\sum_j(\tau\otimes\tau^{\rm op})(\widetilde\partial_jP_j)$.
In the notation cited from Part IV, formal differentiation $D_j$ followed by evaluation in the original and opposite algebra is exactly this $\widetilde\partial_j$.
Remark 3.2(d), checked in U002, converts each trace to $\tau(J_jP_j)$.
This last conversion is local and only needs $L^1$ because $P_j$ is bounded.
There is no relative-$B$ entropy change of variables or nonmicrostates-$\chi^*$ change-of-variables theorem in this proposition.
