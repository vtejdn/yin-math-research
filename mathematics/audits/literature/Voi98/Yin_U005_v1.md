## Definitions and scope: 5.1-5.4

Let $(M,\tau)$ be finite with faithful normal tracial state, $H=L^2(M,\tau)$, $\Omega=1$, and $P=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$.
The algebra acts by left multiplication, $B\subseteq M$ is a unital star algebra, and $X_1,\ldots,X_n\in M$ are bounded selfadjoint.
A dual system means **bounded selfadjoint** operators $Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\in B(H)$ satisfying

$$
[b,Y_j]=0\ (b\in B),\qquad [X_j,Y_k]=i\delta_{jk}P.
\tag{1}
$$

This source does not define a general unbounded dual system.
Later conventions allowing nonsymmetric duals, removing the factor $i$, or prescribing an invariant domain require explicit conversion and are not automatic restatements of 5.1.
The surrounding discussion concerns the unbounded difference quotient; that does not make its dual operators unbounded.

Commutation with $B$ is equivalent to commutation with $W^*(B)$ by strong closure and boundedness of $Y_j$.
Coordinatewise duality is equivalent to duality of $X_j$ relative to the algebra generated by $B$ and all other coordinates.
Two systems differ coordinatewise by a selfadjoint element of the commutant of $B[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$; conversely such perturbations preserve (1).

If $Q=Q^*=Q^2$ commutes with $B$ and the $X_j$ and satisfies $Q\Omega=\Omega$, then on $QH$

$$
\widehat Y_j=QY_j|_{QH},\qquad
[X_k|_{QH},\widehat Y_j]=i\delta_{jk}P|_{QH}.
\tag{2}
$$

Compression is selfadjoint because $Y_j$ is bounded selfadjoint.
Equation (2) follows by putting $Q$ on both sides of (1); it does **not** require $Q$ to commute with $Y_j$.
For $N\subseteq M$ containing the generators, $Q$ is the conditional-expectation projection onto $L^2(N)$.
The statement of 5.3 misprints the restricted domain as $L^2(M)$; its proof has the correct $L^2(N)$ domain.

## Free products: 5.5-5.8

For pointed Hilbert spaces $(H_i,\Omega_i)$, the free-product Hilbert space is

$$
\mathbb C\Omega\oplus
\bigoplus_{m\geq1,\ i_1\ne i_2\ne\cdots\ne i_m}
H_{i_1}^{\circ}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{i_m}^{\circ},
$$

where the displayed condition means consecutive indices differ.
The left representation $\lambda_i(T)$ acts at the left end through the decomposition $H=H_i\otimes H(i,\mathrm{left})$; the right representation $\rho_i(S)$ acts at the right end.
On reduced words of length at least two these end actions commute, including their scalar-vacuum components.
On the vacuum and one-letter summand, direct multiplication gives

$$
[\lambda_i(T),\rho_j(S)]
=0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
[\lambda_i(T),\rho_i(S)]
=V_i[T,S]V_i^*,
\tag{3}
$$

where $V_i:H_i\to H$ is the vacuum-plus-one-factor isometry, and the right side is zero on its orthogonal complement.
This explicitly reconstructs the commutation identity imported in 5.5.
In particular $[T,S]=iP_{\Omega_i}$ becomes $iP_\Omega$, not the projection onto an entire free-product tensor tail.

Thus for $(M,\tau)=(M_1,\tau_1)*(M_2,\tau_2)$, right lifting the separate duals $Y_j^{(i)}$ gives a joint dual system for the left copies of all $X_j^{(i)}$, relative to the generated algebra $B_1*B_2$.
Cross commutators vanish by (3), and same-factor commutators are exactly (1).
If only the first factor has a dual system, the same right lifts are dual to $\lambda_1(X_j^{(1)})+\lambda_2(X_j^{(2)})$, relative to $\lambda_1(B)$, for arbitrary bounded selfadjoint $X_j^{(2)}$.

For 5.7 in an ambient algebra $M$, first compress the given duals to their generated factor algebras, identify their generated free product with $N=W^*(B_1[X^{(1)}],B_2[X^{(2)}])$, and apply the construction on $L^2(N)$.
Extend the resulting operators by zero on $L^2(N)^\perp$.
This complement reduces the entire algebra $N$, so all required commutators remain zero there, while $P$ is supported on $L^2(N)$.
This supplies the ambient-space step compressed in the source.

The pointed free-product model itself follows by sending a centered reduced tensor $a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m$ to $a_1\cdots a_m\Omega$: freeness gives the same inner products and the words span densely.
No full original proof of the source's references [12] or [15] is imported for (3); its needed identity is reconstructed here.

## Corollary 5.9: the printed compression is not a dual to the sum

The source states that, if $B[X_1]$ and $X_2$ are free and $Y_2$ is dual to $(X_2;\mathbb C)$ on $L^2(M)$, then $Q_2Y_2Q_2$ is dual to $(X_1+X_2;B)$ on that same space, with $Q_2$ projecting onto $L^2(W^*(X_2))$.
This literal operator assertion is false.

For a concrete counterexample, take standard free semicircular variables $X_j=\ell_j+\ell_j^*$ on full Fock space over two orthonormal vectors, $B=\mathbb C[X_1]$, and

$$
Y_2=\tfrac i2(r_2-r_2^*).
$$

The creation/annihilation identities give $[X_2,Y_2]=iP$ and $[X_1,Y_2]=0$, so the source assumptions hold.
The projection $Q_2$ keeps precisely the vacuum and words consisting entirely of the letter 2.
Put $Z=Q_2Y_2Q_2$.
Then

$$
[X_1,Z]\Omega=\tfrac i2e_1\otimes e_2\ne0.
$$

Therefore $Z$ fails even the required commutation with $B$.
Also $[X_1+X_2,Z]\Omega=i\Omega+\tfrac i2e_1\otimes e_2$, not $i\Omega$.

The valid conclusion is **existence** of a dual to the sum.
Compress $Y_2$ to $L^2(W^*(X_2))$, right-lift that compression in the free product with $W^*(B[X_1])$ as in 5.8, then extend by zero to any larger ambient $L^2(M)$.
The right lift is essential; it is absent from the printed formula.
Later uses of existence can use this repair; the displayed compressed operator cannot be reused.

## Conjugates and Hilbert-Schmidt realization: 5.10-5.12

Assume first $M=W^*(B[X])$.
Write $J:H\to H$ for the tracial antiunitary $J(a\Omega)=a^*\Omega$, to distinguish it from $\mathcal J$.
For a dual $Y$, the vector

$$
\xi=i(JYJ-Y)\Omega=i(JY\Omega-Y\Omega)
\tag{4}
$$

lies in $L^2(M)$, is fixed by $J$, and satisfies $\|\xi\|_2\leq2\|Y\|$.
To check the conjugate relation, expand $[Y,b_0Xb_1\cdots Xb_m]$ into one commutator per occurrence of $X$.
Each is $-iP$, and taking its vacuum expectation factors the prefix and suffix traces.
Moving the two terms of (4) through the tracial pairing gives

$$
\tau(\xi q)=(\tau\otimes\tau)(\partial q),\qquad q\in B[X].
$$

At this point the right side is first interpreted on formal polynomial expressions: the definition of a dual system itself does not assume algebraic freeness.
The first-order formal-conjugate relation argument in [U002](Yin_U002_v1.md#voi98-u002) then proves algebraic freeness by derivative descent and a minimal-degree contradiction.
Therefore the evaluated $\partial$ is well-defined, and Section 3.3 identifies (4) with $\mathcal J(X:B)$.
If the ambient algebra is larger, first compress to the generated algebra; without that step, (4) need not belong to its $L^2$ subspace.

With the first-variable-linear inner product, define

$$
\Psi(u\otimes v)=\langle\,\cdot,Jv\rangle u.
$$

The anti-linearity of $J$ cancels the conjugate-linearity in the second argument, so $\Psi$ is linear in both tensor inputs.
Direct multiplication of rank-one operators gives

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\Psi(u\otimes v)\Psi(u'\otimes v')^*)
=\langle u,u'\rangle\langle v,v'\rangle.
$$

Hence it extends isometrically from $H\otimes_2H$ to Hilbert-Schmidt operators.
On bounded elementary inputs, $\Psi(a\otimes b)=aPb$, so the wordwise commutator expansion gives all of 5.11-5.12:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i[Y,T]&=\Psi(\partial T),\\
\langle\partial T_1,\partial T_2\rangle
 &=\operatorname{Tr}([T_1,Y][T_2,Y]^*),\\
\operatorname{Tr}([T,Y])&=i\tau(m_1(\partial T)),\\
\operatorname{Tr}(P[T,Y])&=i(\tau\otimes\tau)(\partial T).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

Here $T,T_1,T_2\in B[X]$, so the commutators are finite rank and all traces displayed exist.
The last two expressions differ: $\operatorname{Tr}(aPb)=\tau(ab)$, whereas $\operatorname{Tr}(PaPb)=\tau(a)\tau(b)$.
A trace-class commutator of bounded operators need not have zero trace; applying cyclicity to two non-trace-class factors would incorrectly contradict (1).

These identities realize polynomial difference quotients through bounded-operator commutators.
They do not identify an arbitrary extension defined through Hilbert-Schmidt commutators with the minimal closed operator $\bar\partial$ in [U004](Yin_U004_v1.md#voi98-u004).

## Semicircular model and sign correction: 5.13

Let $\mathcal H$ be a real Hilbert space, $\mathcal H_{\mathbb C}$ its complexification, and use the source normalization

$$
s(h)=\tfrac12(\ell(h)+\ell(h)^*),\qquad
d(k)=\tfrac12(r(k)+r(k)^*).
$$

Thus real $h$ has semicircular variance $\|h\|^2/4$.
Left and right creation commute, as do their adjoints, while direct vacuum/word action gives

$$
[\ell(h)^*,r(k)]=\langle k,h\rangle P,\qquad
[\ell(h),r(k)^*]=-\langle h,k\rangle P.
$$

Consequently

$$
[s(h),d(k)]
=\tfrac14(\langle k,h\rangle-\langle h,k\rangle)P
=-\tfrac i2\operatorname{Im}\langle h,k\rangle P.
\tag{6}
$$

The source p. 213 prints a **plus** sign in the last expression, inconsistent with its own basic commutators and the inner-product convention fixed by 5.11.
The corrected condition for duality is

$$
\operatorname{Im}\langle\mathcal H_0,k_j\rangle=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Im}\langle h_p,k_q\rangle=-2\delta_{pq}.
\tag{7}
$$

If the real vectors $h_1,\ldots,h_n$ are linearly independent modulo the closed real subspace $\mathcal H_0$, their projections to $\mathcal H_0^\perp$ admit real biorthogonal vectors $g_q$ there.
Taking $k_q=2ig_q$ satisfies (7) for the first-variable-linear inner product.
Thus $Y_q=d(k_q)$ is dual to $s(h_q)$ relative to $W^*(s(\mathcal H_0))$.
For one unit vector, $Y=i(r-r^*)$ and $[s,Y]=iP$, providing an immediate sign and scale check.

The tracial standard representation of the real semicircular algebra can be seen directly: Wick polynomials applied to the vacuum span the full Fock space; the right real fields commute with the left ones and have the same cyclic vacuum, making the vacuum separating.
Pairings give the tracial state.
This is the normalization conversion needed when comparing the source with later variance-one Fock models, not a new claim about arbitrary circular or rational variables.

## Generalized duals: 5.14 needs compression, not restriction

The definition allows an arbitrary Hilbert space $K$, a unit vector $v$ tracial for the represented star algebra $A=B[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$, and bounded selfadjoint $Y_j$ satisfying (1) with $P_v$.
Let $K_0=\overline{Av}$.
This space reduces $A$, and its projection $Q$ satisfies $Qv=v$.
The source additionally claims $K_0$ reduces each $Y_j$; its displayed proof expands $Y_jav$ as $[Y_j,a]v$ and omits $aY_jv$.

That extra claim is false.
Start with any genuine one-variable dual $(X,Y)$ on $L^2(N)$, and put

$$
K=L^2(N)\oplus L^2(N),\quad v=(\Omega,0),\quad
\widehat X=\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\0&X\end{pmatrix},\quad
\widehat Y=\begin{pmatrix}Y&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Take $B=\mathbb C I$ and a cyclic $X$ model, such as the one-variable semicircular example.
Then $\widehat Y$ is bounded selfadjoint and

$$
[\widehat X,\widehat Y]=\begin{pmatrix}iP_\Omega&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=iP_v,
$$

so the generalized dual assumptions hold.
However $K_0=L^2(N)\oplus0$ and $\widehat Yv=(Y\Omega,\Omega)\notin K_0$.

The useful conclusion still follows from (2): **compress** to $QY_j|_{K_0}$.
The natural GNS isometry identifies $K_0$ with the $L^2$ space of the tracial representation, after the usual null-space quotient if the initial trace is not faithful.
The compressed operators form a dual system there.
Restriction alone is invalid without an additional invariance hypothesis.
No assumption $Y_jv=0$ is present in the source, and it must not be silently inserted.
