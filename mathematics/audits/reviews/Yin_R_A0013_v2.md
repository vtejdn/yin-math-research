## Whole-artifact finding

No unresolved mathematical defect was found in the stated factorizations, rank conclusions, examples or limitations.
The common element is central in the subgroup generated by the labels, and subgroup restriction preserves the canonical kernel dimensions used throughout.
Nonidentity of the common power certifies the individual denominators; distinct actual labels separately certify every initial binomial difference.
The ordered difference-of-inverses identity gives (3)--(4) without commuting row labels.
The additive matrix factors through two coordinates, and its displayed two-square Schur residual is a product of already known units, giving exactly the asserted rank two when both sizes are at least two.
Initial equalities lie outside the distinct-label premises: a cross equality makes the corresponding reciprocal undefined, while the displayed certificates do not invert a zero same-side difference.

The first inverse-difference Schur identity was rederived by multiplying on the left by \(A_i\) and on the right by \(B_j\).
This gives the ordered product in (7), whose inverse at \((2,2)\) is exactly (8).
Substitution into the second Schur residual cancels only adjacent inverse pairs and gives (9).
Multiplying the first term's inverse by the second gives the precise ordered \(\Omega\) in (10), so (11) and both rank and kernel identities in (12) follow.
These are three-square identities; the rectangular discussion correctly retains the rank of the residual block instead of declaring it determined by its dimensions.
The fact that \(\Omega\) is a unit supplies no cyclic certificate for \(1-\Omega\).

With central columns, (13) has the correct sign and order.
The similarity in (14) transports every cross-difference unit and preserves the actual common-power identities, without turning a rational conjugate into an original group element.
Formula (18) is the last scalar of the transformed two-square inverse-difference array after removal of the known first-Schur row and column units in (15), as specified by the cited v1 shape interface.
All its factors except \(a_2'-a_3'\) are independently units, establishing (17).
For (18a), equality \(b_j^d=w\) would make the commuting quotient \(a_i b_j^{-1}\) a finite-order group element, contradicting distinctness and torsion-freeness.
The resulting geometric expansion factors through the powers \(a_i^{d-1},\ldots,1\), giving rank at most \(d\) for every rectangular size under those stronger hypotheses.
It supplies no additional zero-or-unit conclusion below that bound.

In (19), the action on the free abelian group has order three, and projection to the infinite cyclic coordinate proves torsion-freeness of the semidirect product.
The norm products give exactly the common powers and exponents in (21); normal forms distinguish every row and column label.
The ordered similarity formula (22) gives the two displayed coefficients and their difference (23), with \(t\) remaining on the right.
The factors \(1-h\), \(1-k\), \(hk-1\) and \(t\) are separately certified actual units, so the third pivot and rank three do not rely on a whole-group Atiyah theorem.
The independent commuting parameters give the known coefficient field \(\mathbb C(h,k)\), normalized by \(t\), and the index-three abelian subgroup confirms the stated limitation on group coverage.

For the Klein bottle example, the semidirect-product normal forms establish torsion-freeness, distinct labels and the common-square relation.
The displayed identity \(u_i(a_i'+a_1)=a_1^2-a_i^2\) has the correct noncommutative order.
It forces both transformed rows to equal \(-a_1\), and the first two certified pivots give rank two and kernel dimension one.
Thus both examples attain their respective degree bounds, and the merger defeats only the proposed persistence of nonzero pivots, not a possible integer-rank induction that controls mergers.

For the C_A0006 comparison, the character degree forces equal positive exponents for any common power of its three rows.
Equality of the first two powers forces that exponent to be even, and comparison with the third then requires \(q^N=I_m\).
The arbitrary Cayley coefficient has no such constraint in general, and the third row is generally a rational unitary rather than an actual group element.
The complete all-size equivalence in the larger class uses block arrays and the reduction \(a=A^*A\); it does not become an equivalence for the scalar group-label family in (2).
The swap semidirect product in (27) is torsion-free, has central \(t^2\), and its displayed distinct roots square to that element exactly.
