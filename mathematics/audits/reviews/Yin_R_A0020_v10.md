# Independent review of the constant field of a recovered cyclic boundary

## Whole finding

At 2026-11-21T04:36 UTC, a reviewer outside the development of this proof checked the complete [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v10.md#statement) and [argument](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v10.md#argument).
The reviewer received the fixed claim and its scope, and read its mathematical dependencies directly, without a development conversation, an author explanation or an earlier review of v10.
The argument establishes all three assertions at their stated hypotheses.

For the fraction ring, I checked the finite-slice injection in [v8](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#the-fraction-ring-in-the-regular-representation).
After a common bounded left clearing, each end coefficient is injective, so a vector in the kernel of a polynomial of degree \(d>0\) is determined by \(d\) consecutive slices.
The resulting upper bound \(d\) on its \(N\)-dimension forces zero kernel, since each cyclic slice contributes the same group trace of the kernel projection.
This argument applies to the given \(D\), and the nonzero skew Laurent polynomials are affiliated units.
For \(Qb=P\), applying a complex linear functional to the bounded coefficient equations detects a nonzero denominator and gives \(b\in\mathbb C(z)\).
With the scalar denominator cleared on the right, the top coefficient of \(Q\) is a unit in \(D\), and downward recursion puts every scalar Laurent coefficient of \(b\) in \(D\cap\mathbb C1\).
The denominator equations over this intersection have finitely many unknowns, and their augmented rows have a finite basis over the intersection.
Consistency over \(\mathbb C\) therefore gives a denominator over \(k_0\) with constant coefficient one after passage to \(t=z^{-1}\).
The numerator then lies over \(k_0\) as well, and the opposite inclusion follows inside the embedded fraction ring.

For the sofic assertion, [Jaikin-Zapirain, Theorem 1.3 and Corollaries 1.6–1.7, pp. 470–471](../../literature/pdf/JZ19.pdf#page=7) give the precise source comparison.
On a finitely generated subgroup containing the support, the same word lifts and finite permutation matrices can be used before and after the coefficient automorphism.
Their ordinary ranks agree by minors, and Theorem 1.3 passes this equality to the regular ranks.
Group inversion on the standard basis changes left convolution on columns to right multiplication on rows after transposition and inversion of each group label, operations compatible with the coefficient automorphism.
Induction on cosets gives the claimed invariance also for an arbitrary sofic group.
Corollary 1.7 states this rank invariance directly for two embeddings of a field into \(\mathbb C\).
For a countable sofic group and a conjugation-stable \(k\), Corollary 1.6 already gives scalar intersection \(k\) for the larger rank-metric closure of the \(*\)-regular ring generated by \(k[N]\).
The finite-expression proof here covers ordinary division closure for every subfield \(k\).
I checked the diagonal, triangular and bordered block representations of sums, products and inverses of actual units.
Schur elimination of the scalar test matrix yields \(\operatorname{diag}(A,\lambda-uA^{-1}v)\), and applying an automorphism fixing \(k\) changes only the lower right scalar in the test matrix.
The unchanged rank forces the new Schur complement to vanish.
Separability moves any algebraic scalar outside \(k\) to a distinct conjugate, and translation in a transcendence basis moves any transcendental scalar outside \(k\).
Both embeddings extend to automorphisms of \(\mathbb C\), so the fixed scalar lies in \(k\).

For finite recovery, I checked that closure under adjoints allows the Gram projection over the fraction division ring and identifies its algebraic image with the analytic kernel.
The finite joins of its conjugates have integer unnormalized trace, and a plateau gives a projection fixed by conjugation.
The orbit hypothesis identifies such a projection as an edge projection, so the first plateau equals the least edge projection \(q\) containing the kernel.
The [corner formula](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#deficient-expected-support) therefore recovers \(b_jq\) over \(F\), including \(q=0\) and \(q=I\).
Applying the scalar intersection entrywise gives the stated coefficient field.
For the free amalgam, its retraction kernel is free and sofic, and [Linnell, Theorem 1.3, p. 562](../../literature/pdf/Lin93.pdf#page=3) applies to that free group with trivial quotient.
Its intermediate division ring in \(U(N)\) guarantees that every nonzero element of the division closure of \(k[N]\) has an affiliated inverse there.
Conjugation by \(z\) preserves the closure, and conjugation stability of \(k\) gives closure under adjoints.
Reduced words give the centralizer of every nonzero power of \(z\) and freeness of the vertex algebras over the edge, which persists under matrix amplification.
At \(b=0\), [BBL21, Theorems 3.2 and 3.5(vii), arXiv v2, pp. 6–8](../../literature/pdf/BBL21--arxiv-v2.pdf#page=6) give both the norm limits and the kernel inclusion required by the formula when \(E_H(p)\) is boundedly invertible.

This review checked these external theorem statements and their applications and accepted their original proofs.
For deficient expected support the result determines only \(b_jq\) for a bounded edge solution.
The analytic existence assertion retains its freeness and bounded-inverse assumptions.
