Left multiplication by the unit \(z^{-\ell}I_n\) preserves the right kernel and shifts all exponents into \([0,w]\).

The pivot rows span all rows on the left, and the additional coordinates certify right independence over \(F\).
Thus the resulting \(n-a\) polynomial columns span the entire right kernel over \(F\).
The coordinate basis at \(a=0\), the zero kernel at \(a=n\), and the case \(w=0\) all agree with this count.
The [affiliated kernel projection](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#kernel-projections-and-the-translate-sum) has this same right range over \(F\), and [v10](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v10.md#applying-the-finite-recovery-formula) supplies rationality over \(k(z)\) for both \(q\) and \(b_jq\) under the given orbit and adjoint hypotheses.

For \(c\in k\), the coefficient of \(cz^t z^h d_h\) on the right of \(z^{t+h}\) is \(c d_h\).
This proves left \(k[z,z^{-1}]\)-linearity of \(\Phi_\phi\) without moving \(z\) past a coefficient of \(D\).
Left multiplication clears a common denominator in each rational matrix equation before \(\Phi_\phi\) is applied.
In particular \(qv=v\) puts every scalarized column in the range of \(q\).
If a polynomial row annihilates every scalarized column, all \(k\)-linear functionals vanish on each coefficient of each corresponding skew row-column product.
These functionals separate even a single nonzero coefficient by extending it to a vector-space basis.
The row therefore kills \(V\), hence \(p\), and its bounded edge coefficients allow one to take the conditional expectation.
Annihilation of \(E_H(p)\) forces annihilation of its closed range projection \(q\).
Clearing a denominator in any rational annihilating row now gives equality of the two annihilators, hence of the two column spaces by finite-dimensional duality.
Choosing \(U\) in this common space depends only on \(Y\) for the fixed interval and coefficient ring.
The same functionals applied to \(Y_jV\) give (3) for each action, and multiplication adds exponents within \([\ell,u+L]\).

On the circle outside the zeros of a nonzero maximal minor of \(U\), its pointwise Gram matrix is invertible.

Multiplying \(T_j\) by \(z^{-\ell}\) gives degree at most \(w+L\).

The projection numerator has degree at most \(2sL-\ell\le 2sL+w\).
For \(q=I_n\), \(U\) is square and its adjugate has degree at most \((n-1)L\), giving the two sharper bounds \(nL-\ell\) and \(nL+w\).
