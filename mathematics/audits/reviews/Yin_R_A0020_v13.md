# Independent review of the two vertex rank formulas

## Whole finding

At 2026-11-21T07:07 UTC I independently reviewed the complete [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v13.md#statement) and [argument](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v13.md#argument) for Yin.
I received the fixed claim and the review assignment and had no part in the proof development or access to its development discussion.
I consulted the preceding v11 and v12 findings and checked the source theorem and the v11 construction used here.
Both rank formulas hold for the stated rectangular inputs, with the unnormalized matrix trace, and the expected graph support attains the maximum in (1).

For \(X_j=1-e_j\), positivity gives \(\ker(X_1+X_2)=e_1\wedge e_2=e\), identifying a closed subspace with its projection.
When \(E_0(e)\) has a bounded inverse, normalization of the trace gives exactly the tracial state required by the source theorem for an arbitrary unital von Neumann base.
Its meet assertion puts \(e\) below each shifted kernel, so \(b_je=X_je=0\).
Bimodularity and invertibility then force \(b_j=0\), and the trace assertion with these two shifts gives (3) after undoing normalization.
I checked the norm limits in the proof of Theorem 3.2 and both assertions of Theorem 3.5(vii) against the [BBL21 arXiv v2 text, pp. 6–8](../../literature/pdf/BBL21--arxiv-v2.pdf#page=6), accepting its analytic theorems as established inputs.

For the spectral cut \(h_t\), the two joins remain projections of the respective free vertex algebras because both contain the base.
Their meet dominates both \(e\) and \(h_t\), hence its expectation dominates half the sum \(E_0(e)+h_t\).
On the spectral interval \([0,t)\) the added projection contributes \(1\), and on its complement \(E_0(e)\ge t\).
Thus each application of the bounded-inverse case has the required lower bound \(t/2\).
Writing \(J=e_1\vee e_2\), the resulting equality \(J\vee h_t=1\) gives \(\operatorname{tr}(1-J)=\operatorname{tr}(h_t)-\operatorname{tr}(J\wedge h_t)\) by the two-projection dimension identity.
The polar decomposition of \((1-J)h_t\) gives that identity even for nonclosed range.
Since \(s(E_0(e))=1\), the decreasing cuts tend to zero and normality gives \(\operatorname{tr}(1-J)=0\).
Faithfulness yields \(J=1\), which proves (3) also for this case.

For the general pair of vertex rows, faithfulness applied to \((1-Q_0)P_0(1-Q_0)\) gives \(P_0(1-Q_0)=0\).
Decomposing each input along \(Q_0\) shows that the joint kernel after right multiplication by \(Q_0\) has projection \(P_0+1-Q_0\).
The expectation of this projection has full support, including when \(Q_0=0\).
The individual kernel projections are in the input-size vertex amplifications by spectral calculus.
Freeness there follows by expanding each entry of an alternating centered matrix product and using subgroup freeness in each summand.
In (3) each individual kernel dimension is \(N-\operatorname{rk}(L_jQ_0)\) by polar decomposition, and the common kernel has dimension \(\operatorname{tr}(P_0)+N-\operatorname{tr}(Q_0)\).
Substitution gives (4), with subgroup inclusions preserving the range projections and traces.
For any edge projection \(U\), the rank of the stacked operator restricted to \(U\) is \(\operatorname{tr}(U)-\operatorname{tr}(P_0\wedge U)\).
Its closed range lies in the direct sum of the two closed component ranges, giving the upper bound \(\operatorname{tr}(P_0\wedge U)\le\operatorname{tr}(P_0)\) on the maximized expression.
For \(R_1,R_2\), the map \(x\mapsto(x,Y_1x)\) on \(\ker Y\) has norm at least \(\|x\|\) on each vector and its range is precisely the joint kernel.
Its partial isometry identifies the kernel projection of \(Y\) with the graph projection in trace, which proves (1).

For (2), bimodularity gives \((d_1+d_2)E(p)=0\), and density of the range of \(E(p)\) in \(q\) gives \((d_1+d_2)q=0\).
The support assumption \(d_jq=d_j\) therefore gives \(d_2=-d_1\), and this choice always works if only \(d_1\) is supplied since \(Y_2p=-Y_1p\).
Each \(V_j\) kills both \(p\) and \(1-q\).
Conversely, adding the two equations \(V_jx=0\) gives \(Yqx=0\), so \(qx\) belongs to the range of \(p\).
This proves the joint kernel identity \(p+1-q\), and its expected full support gives (2) with all \(m\) output coordinates.

For cyclic \(H\), a nonzero maximal minor over \(k(z)\) vanishes at only finitely many points on the circle, and all larger minors vanish identically.
Consequently the fiber rank is the field rank almost everywhere, giving integral unnormalized trace for a rational projection, also in rank zero.
In a reduced entry, Bézout's identity prevents numerator and denominator from sharing a complex root.
Any denominator root on the circle would force unbounded values on sets of positive Haar measure, contradicting boundedness.
Thus the common polynomial denominator is bounded away from zero on the circle, and multiplying on the right by its diagonal matrix preserves each range exactly.
The resulting factors \(Q\Delta\) or \(q\Delta,d_j\Delta\) have polynomial entries, in the displayed order relative to each vertex coefficient.
This verifies the conditional reduction to two finite group-ring ranks.
The unresolved rationality question concerns finite group-ring input.
For arbitrary bounded inputs already over the edge, taking \(m=n=1\), \(Y_1=1_I\), \(Y_2=0\) with \(I\) a proper arc of positive measure and positive-measure complement gives \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1-1_I,0)\), whose rank varies on sets of positive measure.

In the [v11 example](../../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v11.md#an-arbitrary-finite-matrix-rank-on-the-same-coordinates), its kernel description and support estimate (5)–(6) give the quoted constant \(q\) for every \(P_\Gamma\).
I used the [independent v11 finding](Yin_R_A0020_v11.md#whole-finding) for the expected-kernel lower bound in that construction.
Substituting its two opposite determined actions into the block matrices eliminates the shifts and gives exactly the \(V_a,V_b\) in v13.
Since \(R_j\) is onto and \(M\) is positive invertible, \(R_j^*MR_j\) has kernel \(\ker R_j\) and rank \(r\).
For any fixed \(v\) and prescribed \(t\), \(w=u^*(Sv-t)\) realizes \(Sv-uw=t\).
It follows that the top and bottom output ranges of \(V_a\) form a direct product even before closure, so closure adds precisely the closed range of \(P_\Gamma\) to the rank-\(r\) top block.
Subgroup trace preservation identifies this contribution with \(\operatorname{rk}_\Gamma(P_\Gamma)\).
As \(\operatorname{tr}(q)=4r\), formula (2) gives \(2r-\operatorname{rk}_\Gamma(P_\Gamma)\), in agreement with the v11 defect and its compressed nullity \(2r\).
The conclusion leaves the integrality of an arbitrary such vertex rank unresolved.
