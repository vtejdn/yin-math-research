## Obligations and decisive checks

### Actual group, denominator and trace

The four-cycle on the central generators defines an actual automorphism of \(N=H\times\mathbb Z^4\), fixing \(H\), with fourth power the identity.
The normal form \(nt^k\) identifies the quotient as an infinite cyclic group.
A finite-order element has zero height and then lies in torsion-free \(N\), proving torsion-freeness of \(G\) without using any kernel arithmetic.
The same normal form proves that the subgroup generated by \(H,t\) is \(H\times\mathbb Z\).
The direct-product description \(G=H\times(\mathbb Z^4\rtimes\mathbb Z)\) and the index-four abelian subgroup of the auxiliary factor are valid.
Neither description supplies an Atiyah assertion for arbitrary \(H\).

For fixed \(z_1,z_2,z_3\), the equation \(f(z)=0\) determines at most one \(z_0\) on its circle.
Fubini therefore gives a Haar-null zero set, and the conjugate multiplier has the same property.
The corresponding bounded operators are injective.
Restriction to subgroup right cosets transports this injectivity into the actual regular representations of \(N\) and \(G\).
The same decomposition preserves canonical traces of coefficient spectral projections, including the unknown kernel projection of \(A\).
For \(H\times\langle t\rangle\), the tensor-product trace is consequently the actual subgroup trace, rather than an imposed law.

The finite-trace affiliated inverse criterion applies to \(f,f^*\) independently of \(A\).
The closed quotient \(w=f(f^*)^{-1}\) is the multiplier \(f/\overline f\), which has modulus one off the null set and hence is a bounded unitary.
Its inverse and adjoint are \(f^*f^{-1}\).
The coefficient calculation gives \(\sigma(f)=if\), \(\sigma(f^*)=-if^*\), and therefore \(\sigma(w)=-w\).
The construction uses actual inverses in the fixed affiliated algebra, so its rational-closure claim needs no formal free-field evaluation.
No boundedness of either scalar denominator inverse is required.

### Rational frame and complete finite kernel

For the bounded positive matrix \(C=A^*A\), both \(d=I+C\) and \(g=I+AA^*\) have bounded inverses from their lower bounds by the identity.
The ordered relation \(gA=Ad\) yields \(g^{-1}A=Ad^{-1}\).
Thus the rectangular sizes and all multiplication orders in the definitions of \(R,B\) are correct.
The identity
\[
R^2+B^*B=((I-C)^2+4C)(I+C)^{-2}=I
\]
proves boundedness and the normalization of \(F\) independently of \(\ker A\).
The Cayley-column comparison is correct: the first column of \((I+iS)(I-iS)^{-1}\), for \(S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A^*\\A&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), is \(\binom{R}{iB}\).
It uses the bounded selfadjoint resolvent interface only.

The identity \(F^*F=I_m\) makes \(p=FF^*\) a complete range projection of trace \(m\).
All entries of \(F\) and \(p\) are in the actual rational closure, because their only denominator inputs have already been certified.
The centrality and sign change of \(w\) give
\[
F^*\sigma(F)=\tfrac12(I-R^2+B^*B)=B^*B
=4C(I+C)^{-2}=Q.
\]
The spectral function \(4s/(1+s)^2\) is nonnegative, at most one, and vanishes on \([0,\infty)\) exactly at zero.
Therefore \(Q\) is a positive contraction with exactly the kernel projection of \(A\), including when that projection is neither known rational nor known to have integer trace.

The displayed \(M\) has \(m+r\) rows and \(2m+r\) columns, with finite group-ring entries.
Its second equation uniquely determines \(z=Bx\) through the bounded inverse \(g^{-1}\).
The bounded identity \(f^*d\,wR=f(I-C)\) rewrites its first equation as \(f^*d(y-wRx)=0\).
The bounded operator \(f^*d\) is injective, so \(y=wRx\).
The converse follows from the same two identities.
Thus the complete Hilbert-space kernel is exactly the bounded graph in (15), whose normalized isometric frame is \(F\).
This cancellation does not apply an unbounded inverse to an arbitrary vector.
It establishes finite origin and completeness of \(p\), rather than merely exhibiting an included subspace.

### Normal compression and all scalar parameters

The covariance relation \(tF=\sigma(F)tI_m\) gives \(F^*KF=Qt\).
Since \(\sigma(Q)=Q\), \(Q\) commutes with \(t\), and both products with the adjoint equal \(Q^2\).
Normality of \(K\) follows on its actual \(p\)-corner.
Its kernel and adjoint kernel both transport under \(F\) from \(\ker Q=\ker A\), with projection \(\delta=FeF^*\).
Cyclic traciality and the subgroup trace comparison give \(\tau_n(\delta)=\dim_H\ker A\), with no normalization factor.

The hypotheses of the normal homogeneous theorem in C_A0008 are all present: \(p\) is a coefficient projection, the height action fixes it, \(K\) has height one, and \(K\) is normal.
That theorem requires no finite support of \(p\) and no coefficient division property.
Its conclusion gives precisely (19).
The candidate's second proof also works directly with the spectral measure of the positive matrix \(Q\) and Haar height phase.
For every \(s>0\), the equation \(2s\cos\theta+\varepsilon=0\) has a null phase set, including the endpoint cases.
For \(s=0\), the entire phase circle contributes exactly when \(\varepsilon=0\).
Faithfulness of the trace upgrades the resulting zero trace or equal trace to the asserted kernel-projection identity.
This verifies all real potentials without a smallness assumption and preserves the possible mass at \(e\).

### Singular Gram support and saddle elimination

The equality \(\ker(M^*M)=\ker M\) identifies the entire zero support of \(B_0\) as \(p\).
On \(q=I-p\), the positive restriction \(b\) is injective and has its two-sided affiliated corner inverse.
No integer-rank premise or residual injectivity enters this inverse certificate.

The absence of a bounded inverse is genuinely possible.
For \(A=0\), direct multiplication gives \(MM^*=\operatorname{diag}(2|f|^2I_m,I_r)\).
The continuous function \(f\) vanishes at \((1,1,1,1)\), and every sufficiently small neighborhood has positive Haar measure, so the first summand has no positive lower bound.
The independent pivots give full row support of \(M\).
Polar decomposition therefore identifies \(MM^*\) with the supported part of \(M^*M\), proving the failure of a bounded inverse for \(b\) in this example.
The bounded Gram hypothesis from the earlier star construction is unavailable in this family.

In the decomposition of (20), the lower \(p\)-coordinate is exactly a zero direct summand.
For the remaining \(q\oplus q\) block, multiplication of (21) on either side cancels the mixed terms in their displayed order and gives the corner identity.
Its upper-left entry is zero, so the Schur correction in (22) vanishes.
The eliminating triangular matrices are affiliated units regardless of the rank of \(R_\varepsilon\).
Affiliated rank invariance then gives the support contribution \(2\tau_n(q)\) plus the residual rank.
Since \(\tau_n(p)=m\), subtraction from the full size \(2n\) gives \(m+\dim_p\ker R_\varepsilon\), exactly (23).

This reasoning establishes analytic kernel dimensions of the original bounded matrices through closed affiliated ranks.
It does not use a pointwise application of \(b^{-1}\) to all residual kernel vectors.
For nonzero \(\varepsilon\), the separate bounded equations do establish the entire projection: the lower equation forces \(x=px\), residual injectivity forces \(x=0\), and the upper equation then forces \(y=py\).
The converse is immediate.
The probability-normalized atoms are therefore the stated dimensions divided by \(2n\), while every preceding rank calculation uses the unnormalized trace.

### Strengthened restrictions, equivalence and limits

The sign rule gives \(\sigma^2(F)=F\), hence orbit period at most two for \(p\).
If \(p\) commutes with \(\sigma(p)\), their compressed product is a projection, and its expression under \(F\) is \(Q^2\).
Positivity then forces \(Q\) itself to be a projection.
This is a sufficient noncommutation test and does not require its converse.

For the rectangular stabilization \(\widetilde A=\operatorname{diag}(A,2)\), the new positive scalar block is \(4\), with \(\widetilde Q\)-value \(16/25\).
That value is nonzero and nonidempotent.
Thus the stabilized hopping is nonzero and normal, its projections do not commute, and its orbit has exactly two elements.
The added scalar is injective, so the unknown kernel is unchanged and the known graph trace increases from \(m\) to \(m+1\).
This proves (26), including when \(A=0\) or \(A\) is injective.

The forward equivalence applies the unchanged all-size A0001 assertion to the actual torsion-free \(G\) and finite square matrix \(\mathcal H_0\).
The reverse implication starts with arbitrary torsion-free \(H\), arbitrary \(r,m\ge1\) and arbitrary \(A\in M_{r,m}(\mathbb CH)\), then subtracts the known integer \(m\) from (2).
The restricted family uses the same argument with \(m+1\).
No square-input restriction on \(A\), bounded Gram restriction, coefficient-field hypothesis or zero-or-unit assertion survives this reduction.
The construction consequently proves the stated equivalence, while resolving neither side's integer-rank assertion.

On the reducing defect, \(R_\varepsilon=\varepsilon\delta\), so its nonzero-parameter affiliated inverse restricts to \(\varepsilon^{-1}\delta\).
A nonzero defect is therefore fully consistent with every nonzero-potential conclusion and with an inverse singularity at zero.
The candidate makes no unjustified uniform inverse estimate or limiting injectivity inference.
