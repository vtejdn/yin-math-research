## Exact field and Albert identification

The transformation from the independent Cayley variables in L_A0028_v2 gives a finite degree-four extension of the field generated by U, V and T, so those three invariants are algebraically independent.
Thus the field in L_A0028_v3 is exactly the original invariant field, not a specialization or completion.
In this field, \(U,V,T,1-U,1-V,1-W,V-W\) are nonzero; for example, \(V-W=(UV^2-T^2)/(UV)\), with distinct monomials in the independent variables.
Consequently all quaternion parameters and all diagonal coefficients of the six-dimensional form are nonzero.
The parameterization of the three eigenspaces and its substitution into the Plucker equation in L_A0028_v2 give the stated coefficients d, -Ud, -U, UV, e, -eW with the indicated signs.

The reviewer inspected [Lewis, publisher edition, pp. 42--43 and 47](../../literature/pdf/Lew06.pdf#page=2).
Pages 42--43 stipulate characteristic different from two and the quaternion generator convention used in the candidate.
Section 1.2 on p. 47 defines the Albert form with diagonal entries a1, b1, -a1b1, -a2, -b2, a2b2 and states Theorem 6(1).
The base field has characteristic zero, and both quaternion factors have nonzero parameters, so this statement applies to the displayed tensor product.
The theorem is accepted at its checked statement and applicability scope; its original proof, referred elsewhere by Lewis, was not re-audited.

Substituting (U,d) and (UV,e) into that source convention gives exactly the displayed Albert form.
The map (21) permutes coordinates and multiplies them by nonzero elements of L, hence is invertible over L.

## Henselian valuation argument

For a henselian valuation with residue characteristic different from two, a unit \(a\) with nonzero square residue has a square root: a chosen residue root of the monic polynomial \(X^2-a\) is simple because twice that root is nonzero in the residue field.
This argument uses neither a rank-one value group nor a discrete valuation.
The signs of v(U) are exhaustive in every ordered value group.

If v(U) is positive, 1-U is a unit with residue one.
A lifted root \(r\) satisfies \(r^2=1-U\), and the first proposed vector gives \(-4U+e(1-U)=0\).
Its R1-coordinate is 2, so it is nonzero.
If \(v(U)<0\), then \(1-U^{-1}\) is a unit with residue one.
For its nonzero lifted root \(r\), the element \(E_0=2\iota/r\) satisfies \(E_0^2=-4/(1-U^{-1})=e\).
The second proposed vector gives \(UV\,T^2e/(U^2V^2)-eW=0\), because \(W=T^2/(UV)\).
Its S2-coordinate is 1.
If \(v(U)=0\) and the residue of U is a square, the lifted root \(r\) satisfies \(r^2=U\), and the third vector gives \(d(r^2-U)=0\) with H2-coordinate 1.
No value or residue hypothesis on V, T or d enters any of these verifications.

The square-residue assumption supplies the last case for every unit U and is unnecessary for the first two cases.
Hence the whole arbitrary-rank henselian statement is proved, together with its more general necessary condition on U for anisotropy over a henselian valued extension of residue characteristic different from two.
That necessary condition supplies no sufficient anisotropy criterion.
The qualification on a trivial valuation is correct: its residue field is E itself, so its use of the square-residue assumption does not make the original rational field quadratically closed.

## Non-descent and completion scope

The U=0 and U=1 polynomial valuations are discrete valuations of L viewed as the rational function field in U over C(V,T).
Their values on any nonzero square are even.
The value of U at U=0 is one, the value of 1-U at U=1 is one, and the value of e at U=0 is one.
Thus all three claimed nonsquare statements hold in L.
The displayed nonzero vectors use a coordinate equal to 1 or 2, so a projective rescaling cannot remove their required nonrational root: the corresponding coordinate ratio still recovers a square root of U, 1-U or e.
For the second vector, its R2-coordinate recovers E0 after multiplying by the nonzero rational factor UV/T.
Therefore the assertion that these particular vectors do not descend is justified and says nothing about other potential rational isotropic vectors.

The U-adic completion is \(\mathbb C(V,T)((U))\), and the inverse-adic completion is \(\mathbb C(V,T)((U^{-1}))\).
Both contain L and supply the indicated binomial series root with residue one.
The candidate correctly treats their isotropic vectors as a failure of those anisotropy tests, not as a rational solution to the original problem.
No henselian or completed field is substituted for L in the claimed original equivalence.
