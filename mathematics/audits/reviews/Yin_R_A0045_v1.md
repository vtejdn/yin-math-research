### Actual local resolvents and the obstruction boundary

For an actual inverse in \(\ell^1(G)\), faithfulness of its regular representation identifies \(t(H+t)^{-1}\) with the positive regular resolvent.
The scalar functions \(t/(x+t)\) are bounded by one on the regular spectrum and converge pointwise to the indicator of zero.
Consequently the asserted \(L^2(\tau)\) limit follows by dominated convergence, and the C14 v2 coefficient argument applies exactly to the bounded subsequence in (1).
That argument needs neither an operator-norm limit nor a gap at zero.

The Neumann estimate uses \(t\|H^{-1}\|_A<1/2\), so its bound and its local domain of validity are correct.
A fixed negative Banach spectral point gives \(t/(t-c)\), which vanishes as \(t\) decreases to zero and supplies no contradiction to the proposed local condition.
For the integral in (2), the character of the closed unital algebra generated by \(H\) evaluates the integrand to \(t e^{(c-t)s}\).
Its divergent integral for \(0<t<c\) precludes Bochner integrability in \(A\), without deciding whether a separately defined algebraic inverse exists at that parameter.
The global heat obstruction and this local inverse question are therefore kept logically distinct.

### Principal annihilators and finite projection constants

Finite-support left convolution is weak-star continuous on \(c_0(G)^*=\ell^1(G)\).
The right translations preserve its kernel, and the scalar pairing in (3) defines an element of that dual with the stated norm bound.
With the convention \(R_gf(x)=f(xg)\), one has \(R_hR_g=R_{hg}\); left invariance of the mean is therefore the correct invariance for the conjugation calculation.
Range containment and fixation of the kernel pass through the mean.
An operator commuting with all right translations is left convolution by its value on \(\delta_e\), first on the coordinate basis and then on all of \(\ell^1(G)\).
The resulting scalar idempotent cannot be one because \(a\ne0\).
The scalar-idempotent input gives the zero kernel in \(\ell^1\), and the exact C1 v2 density interface then gives the zero Hilbert kernel.

For (5), finite liminf of the infima in (4) supplies projections with a common finite bound after allowing a fixed additive error in choosing approximate minimizers.
Zero extension and a subnet in the product of weak-star compact image balls produce a bounded linear map with range in \(K\).
Every finite-support kernel vector is eventually fixed because the exhaustion is increasing.
This map need not be a projection onto all of \(K\), and the proof correctly does not assume \(\ell^1\)-density of those vectors.

The average fixes each finite-support kernel vector because all its right translates are again such vectors.
As convolution by an \(\ell^1\) element, it acts boundedly on \(\ell^2(G)\).
Complete Hilbert-kernel density gives \(qp=p\), while \(aq=0\) gives \(pq=q\).

Thus both the equivalence for every increasing exhaustion and the asserted divergence under a hypothetical atom are justified.

### Følner multiplier, norm residual and exact defect

The overlap function in (8) is the coefficient of the normalized indicator of \(F\) in the left regular representation.
It is real, positive definite, supported in \(FF^{-1}\), normalized at the identity and converges pointwise to one along a left Følner sequence.

\[
\|(\lambda_s\otimes I)V-V\lambda_s\|

Define \(D:=(H\otimes I)V-VH\).
