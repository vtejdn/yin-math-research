## Whole-argument finding

The normalized row has norm one and the column of four group unitaries has norm two, giving \(0\le X\le4\).
The trace is one because different occupied cosets give orthogonal terms.
At rank at most one half, removing a mass of \(1/2\) at zero preserves a positive measure.
Taking the degree-two moment matrix and its Schur complement at the residual constant moment \(1/2\) yields exactly the stated matrix.
Projection of the range support onto the span of \(X,X^2\) uses the Gram matrix with diagonal \(s,\mu_4\), off-diagonal \(\mu_3\), and the inner-product vector \((1,s)\).
If the determinant vanishes then \(X^2=sX\) by linear dependence and the unit first moment.
Otherwise the stated inverse formula follows, and equality \(\mu_3=s^2\) would contradict independence by trace Cauchy–Schwarz on \(X^{1/2},X^{3/2}\).
The identity \(\|X-2P\|_2^2=s-4+4r(A)\) and \(\|X\|\le4\) give the residual estimate.

In the free example the differences without \(v\) are \(u^k\) for \(-2\le k\le2\).
Products in \(YY\) involving \(v\) can reduce to a pure \(u\)-power only by canceling the two occurrences of \(v^{\pm1}\), producing \(1\) or \(u^{i-j}\) for \(0\le i,j\le2\).
Thus \(u^4\) arises only from \(u^2u^2\), and each repeated off-diagonal difference \(u^{\pm1}\) contributes two extra cross terms in the squared norm.
This gives \(s=2+(1-t^4)/(3+t^2)^2\), with \(\widehat {X^2-2X}(u^4)=(3+t^2)^{-2}\).
At \(t=9999/10000\), \(1-t^4<4/10000\) and \(3+t^2<4\), so \(16(1-t^4)(3+t^2)^2<0.1024<1\).
The denominator of the normalization stays uniformly positive near these coefficients, giving the asserted strict open neighborhood by operator norm continuity of the normalized operator, its moments and that coefficient.

With distinct off-diagonal differences the fourth moment of a normalized sum on \(m\le4\) points is at most \(2-1/m\le7/4\).
A repeated difference \(d\) gives two distinct edges of the map \(g\mapsto dg\).
Each edge lies in one right coset of the infinite cyclic subgroup generated by \(d\).
If they share an endpoint their union has three points in one coset, since reversed edges would force \(d^2=e\).
If they are disjoint they cover all four points in at most two cosets.
The adjoint has the requisite left-coset cover in either case.
At two occupied cosets, the cyclic-coset estimate gives \(t=1/8\) and \(c\ge 2^{-31}\), hence an excess of at least \(2^{-69}\) above one half.
The one-coset case has rank one.

For the uniform spectral threshold one can pass to a fixed number of distinct marked group elements and convergent normalized coefficients, then stabilize each word relation by diagonal extraction.
Eventual relations form a normal subgroup of the free group, and torsion-freeness passes to the quotient since any eventually trivial power gives an eventually trivial word.
The marked elements remain distinct.
Even if some limiting coefficients vanish, their squared moduli sum to one, so the four-point estimate applies to the resulting element.
Word expansion gives convergence of every fixed moment on the common compact spectral interval, hence convergence of continuous spectral tests.
A continuous test supported away from zero captures more than \(1/2+2^{-70}\) of the limiting mass by the four-point gap, contradicting failure of the proposed uniform bound along thresholds \(1/n\).
