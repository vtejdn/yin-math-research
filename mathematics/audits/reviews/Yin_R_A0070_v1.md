## Small quotient tests

Merging equal quotient products gives necessary coefficient equations for an original lift, because summing products on quotient fibers sums the original zero coefficient equations.
For order at most four, assigning opposite signs to disjoint pairs of occurrences leaves one occurrence in each odd-multiplicity class and removes the other classes from the signed sums.
If either parity support is empty this construction already gives one zero factor.
In order two, nonempty proper supports are singletons and hence units.
In order three, factorization into the augmentation field and the quadratic field forces one nonzero zero divisor to be the whole-group sum and its partner to have even augmentation.
When one support is the whole group in these cases or the order-four cases, signs of opposite total sum on its even partner produce an integral zero product.
In cyclic order four, absent an order-three factor in \(1+X\), both orders must be two.
The supports are unions of pairs under translation by \(x^2\), making the opposing factors divisible integrally by \(1+x^2\) and \(1-x^2\) with suitable signs.
In the Klein group every odd indicator squares to one in characteristic two, and two proper even supports are translated pairs.
Two different pair differences yield four distinct products, so the differences must coincide and opposite signs on the corresponding involution factors suffice.

For the \(C_7\) example the three rows of sums are \((0,1,2,4),(1,2,3,5),(3,4,5,0)\).

Extra occupied classes of even multiplicity could introduce additional lifted terms, so the parity sets alone do not suffice for that conclusion.
