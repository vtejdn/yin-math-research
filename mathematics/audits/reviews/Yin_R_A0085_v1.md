## Whole-argument finding

All changes are bounded independently of \(n\), restore the exact original four-line coarse counts on summing the extra coordinates, and leave each original full type total unchanged.

The translated pairs of intersection stubs therefore match with the prescribed coarse increment and zero fine increment.
The mixed inverse pairs carrying nonzero fine increments have uniform counts at both ends since their ordinary partners are exterior points and none of the modified types carries extras.
These checks suffice for bijections in every block, and the increments generate the specified entire quotient in both constructions.
The closed-walk relations hence preserve surjectivity before choosing any particular good tuple.

With a zero-color root on a connected side, the two nonzero-coordinate rows add precisely the indicators of the translates \(D+1,D+2\) to even uniform counts.
Their union hits all three even coarse classes, and inversion on the second side negates the coarse coordinate and permutes the fine coordinates.

In the order-28 example the unused binary row is even in every coarse class, so the projected first-bit test detects the claimed two colors.

The matching block sizes are \(n\rho/|P|+O(1)\), with fixed \(|P|=28\) or \(21\).
