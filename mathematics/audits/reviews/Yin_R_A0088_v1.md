## Whole-argument finding

The two factors map to the group algebra of \(C_7\times P\) for each fixed finite two-group image \(P\).
The four odd pieces on either side retain augmentation one under this map.
The induction using a central involution shows that the augmentation ideal is nilpotent, since the kernel ideal of passage to the quotient group is square-zero.
Consequently the augmentation-one units form a group with power-of-two order.
Left normalization of every first-factor coefficient and right normalization of every second-factor coefficient preserve the product equations even in a noncommutative group algebra.

Under simultaneous erasure, difference \(1\) receives precisely index pairs \((1,0),(2,1)\) and difference \(2\) receives \((2,0),(4,2)\).
Difference \(4\) receives \((4,0),(1,4)\), and the negative differences have the reversed pairs.
These yield all six equations with the indicated right multiplication, without using any equation in the identity grade.
In an additive abelian quotient, the first three equations give \(u=x-y,\ v=y-z,\ w=z-x\).
Substitution in the other three gives \(z=2y,\ x=2z,\ y=2x\), whence \(7x=0\).
Thus in any abelian two-group quotient all generator images vanish.
If the subgroup generated by the six normalized units were nontrivial it would have a quotient of order two, contradicting this calculation.
Undoing the two normalizations gives the separate four equalities on both factors.

On the finite set of original support labels, a finite product of separating homomorphisms distinguishes all distinct classes modulo the intersection \(R_2\) of the kernels.
The product target is again a finite two-group, so its coefficient equalities imply the equalities in the group algebra of \(G/R_2\) on precisely those finite supports.
The common image on each side has augmentation one, hence contains a coset with coefficient one in every odd piece on that side.
This gives a choice of four representatives that agree under all finite two-group homomorphisms simultaneously.
If \(R_2\) is trivial, the original four support sets on a side are disjoint and nonempty, precluding equality of these four group-algebra elements.

The test is a necessary condition for erasure on both sides in the original group.
The four representatives and their common coset are selected separately on the two sides.
The existence of a detecting homomorphism on a given tuple, erasure of an individual even class, and equations for a nontrivial semidirect-product action remain outside this conclusion.
