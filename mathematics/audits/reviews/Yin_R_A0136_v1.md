## Whole finding

The scalar-adjunction conclusion holds for every prescribed tuple under its rational matrix integrality hypothesis.
For the example the tuple is chosen in the construction.
This qualification matters even when \(n=1\), since a rational choice would give a rational Laurent polynomial on the first power, where fractional kernel mass is excluded by the example's own rank condition.
The corrected statement gives the quantifiers established by the argument.

For the algebraic input, rational integrality descends to every coordinate subgroup by the equality of the canonical traces on induced kernel projections.
For each resulting torsion-free group the rational division closure in its affiliated algebra is a division ring, and the spatial coordinate inclusions carry the smaller division closures into the larger one.
I used the existing inspection of the Lück–Linnell criterion at rational coefficients and its coordinate inclusion argument. [C_A0096_v1](../../claims/Yin_C_A0096_v1.md#alternation-in-actual-affiliated-rings) · [R_A0132_v1](Yin_R_A0132_v1.md#whole-finding)
The coordinate images of the specified \(F\) commute and belong to a common abelian affiliated algebra.
Every nonzero element of the ring they generate has an affiliated inverse in the larger division ring, hence vanishes on a null set in the abelian representation.
Taking its measurable reciprocal realizes its inverse there, so their commutative fraction field consists of actual affiliated operators.

For a fixed monic polynomial of degree \(e>0\), the matrix of \((a,b)\mapsto ap_X+bp_Y\) between the two \(2e\)-dimensional spaces has almost surely constant rank.
A nonzero minor of maximal order is a unit of the preceding field, whereas each larger minor is zero.
If \(g=\gcd(p_X,p_Y)\), the kernel consists of the pairs \((cp_Y/g,-cp_X/g)\) with \(\deg c<\deg g\), since the two quotients are coprime.
Thus the total common-root multiplicity \(\deg g\) is constant almost surely.
Independence and conditioning on the finite first root multiset show that only atoms of the expected root-counting measure contribute to \(\deg g\).
These atoms form a countable set.
The indicators of multiplicity at least \(i\) at those atoms give a countably valued vector because its support has size at most \(e\).
Every pair of positive-probability values \(u,w\) consequently satisfies \(\|u\|^2=u\cdot w=\|w\|^2\), by applying the common-degree identity on three positive-probability product events.
Hence \(u=w\), and the multiplicity at each fixed atom is deterministic.
Every fixed point outside the atoms already has root probability zero.
This proves that evaluating at each specified \(\lambda\) gives either zero or a measurable unit, even when some sample-dependent roots have diffuse distributions.
Normalization by the leading coefficient and the case of constants use only units of the initial field.

After \(k\) adjunctions, the field on any block of \(j\le 2^{l-k}\) coordinates is generated by its coordinate copies of \(F\) and the previously specified constants.
Whenever \(2j\le 2^{l-k}\), each of the two block inclusions takes this field into the \(2j\)-coordinate field, since it preserves both the generators and their actual inverses.
The product trace makes the two blocks independent, so the preceding proof applies at the next scalar.
After \(l\) steps it gives the assertion on one coordinate.
For a finite matrix over the resulting field, elimination gives an identity and zero block with integer rank under the unnormalized matrix trace.
Equivalently, the finitely many almost-everywhere invertible operations preserve its fiber rank pointwise on a set of full measure.
The number-field case uses the primitive element theorem in characteristic zero and imposes no bound on degree.
The separate number-field estimate for general group-ring coefficients uses a different coefficient hypothesis. [C_A0097_v1](../../claims/Yin_C_A0097_v1.md#statement)

In the example, the positive-density ambient component gives full support on the torus, and the hyperplane component gives precisely the mass \(p\) to its defining equation.
The Cayley chart has \(it_h=(u_h-1)/(u_h+1)\), so clearing the nonvanishing denominators gives coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(i a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})\).
For \(b\le j\le n\) constrained samples, the evaluation rows \((1,t_1,\ldots,t_{n-1})\) can have rank \(b\) on a nonempty open set.
One can interpolate arbitrary last coordinates there by solving the \(b\) equations for the common \(n\) hyperplane coefficients.
Thus formal substitution keeps a nonzero numerator nonzero, and specialization at the algebraically independent \(a_h\) preserves a nonzero coefficient in the remaining sample variables.
The zero set is null on each mixture component by absolute continuity in its free coordinates, including the constant case when \(n=j=b=1\).
Passing to the largest nonzero minor gives constant almost-sure rank for every rational Laurent matrix through power \(n\).

For \(n+1\) samples the affine determinant vanishes on the all-hyperplane component.
On the other components the number of hyperplane points is at most \(n\), and these points are affinely independent almost surely because each successively forbidden proper affine span has zero measure in the hyperplane.
One then adjoins each ambient point outside the span of its predecessors almost surely.
This also covers a single hyperplane atom when \(n=1\).
The probability that the determinant vanishes is therefore exactly \(p^{n+1}\).
Replacing the \(t_h\) columns by \(it_h\) multiplies the determinant by \(i^n\) and produces integer coefficients after denominator clearing.

Finally, the uniform coordinatewise \(m\)-th-root lift preserves full support because the power map is a finite covering and the base law has full support.

Cyclotomic Galois invariance makes it a rational Laurent polynomial of the base variables, whose null set pulls back to a null set through power \(n\).
