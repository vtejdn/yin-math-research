On 2026-11-21 at 05:50 UTC a reviewer for Yin outside the proof development completed a whole-proof inspection of C_A0103_v2.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and review scope, consulted the previous finding on the v1 dependency, and received no development dialogue, author explanation or prior verdict on the v2 argument.
The length bounds (2)–(5), the arithmetic consequences and the torsion-free bounds (9)–(11) and (23) hold under the stated geometric minimality assumption in each indicated characteristic.

Geometric minimality puts all geometric solutions in the coefficient torus.
A positive-dimensional projective component would meet a coordinate hyperplane of the Segre embedding, so the projective base scheme is finite.
Over the algebraic closure, the \(c\) forms are independent because each product fiber has a distinct, nonempty monomial support.
Their ideal has height \(d\), and at each of the first \(d\) cuts an associated component of positive projective dimension cannot lie in the base locus.
The diagonal section ring is Cohen–Macaulay by the projective-space cohomology computation in (12), so avoiding these components gives a regular sequence.
Its one-dimensional quotient \(T\) has depth one, and the leading coefficient of the original Hilbert polynomial gives its projective section length \(N\).
The \(r=c-d\) remaining independent linear forms lie in the ideal \(J\) of \(Z\) in this section.
The sheaf of ideals twisted by one has a space of sections of dimension \(N-\ell\) by exactness on the finite scheme, and the map from \(J_1\) is injective by depth one.

A linear form avoiding the section is regular on both \(T\) and \(T/J\).
Consequently \(J\cap\lambda T=\lambda J\), and \(J_0=0\) identifies the degree-one piece of its image \(I\) in the Artinian reduction with \(J_1\).
For \(a\le b\), the polynomial factors have dimensions \(a,b\) and canonical shifts \(-a,-b\).
The canonical module formula in the proof of HHS19, Theorem 4.15, then gives the diagonal module in (18).
It is generated in degree \(b\) since each monomial of bidegree \((n-a,n-b)\) contains a left-variable monomial of degree \(b-a\).
The regular linear sequence shifts its generating degree to \(b-(d+1)=-(a-1)\).
Dualizing the quotient by the maximal homogeneous ideal thus puts the entire socle of \(\mathcal A\) in degree \(a-1\).
Interchanging the factors gives the same calculation with \(s=\min(a-1,b-1)\).
For \(r>0\), extend a nonzero element of the residual span in degree one by nonzero successive products with linear forms until degree \(s\).
This accounts for \(s-1\) dimensions beyond the at least \(r\) in degree one, including the endpoint case \(s=1\).
Equality forces the graded dimensions in (20).

The Hilbert numerator in (15) can be checked by applying \((1/p!)({\rm d}/{\rm d}t)^p\) to \(t^p/(1-t)^{q+1}\), with \(p=a-1\) and \(q=b-1\).
After taking the common denominator, the coefficient of \(t^i\) is \(\binom pi\) times the \(i\)-th forward difference at \(j=0\) of \(\binom{q+j}{q}\), giving \(\binom pi\binom qi\).
The quotient algebra has at most \(h_1-r=ab-c-1\) degree-one generators, which proves the Hilbert-function bound degree by degree through \(s\), including \(m=0\).
The rank in (21) measures the degree-\(i\) image of the ideal generated by the residual forms, which is contained in \(I_i\).
This proves the lower bound (22) on \(N-\ell\).
Here \(I\) is the image of the saturated ideal \(J\), and the quadratic contribution in the three-row illustration uses \(b\ge3\).

For finite sets in an arbitrary torsion-free group, BPS12, page 2, states the lower bound \(a+b-1\), its progression equality case when both sizes are at least two, and the bound \(a+b+1\) when \(b\ge4\) and \(A\) avoids all left cyclic cosets.
For a left factor in \(u\langle t\rangle\), remove the unit \(u\) and decompose the right factor over \(\langle t\rangle v\).
Multiplication by the nonzero Laurent polynomial on each summand is injective, with the trivial subgroup giving a monomial unit.
The right-factor argument on \(v\langle t\rangle\) excludes right cyclic cosets for \(B\).
Inversion therefore makes the bound for \(b\ge4\) available whenever \(\max(a,b)\ge4\).
The three resulting lower bounds on \(r\) are \(1,2,3\).
For an integer \(k\ge1\), Theorem 2 and its remark on page 3 allow \(c(k)\le32(k+3)^6\) and require \(b>c(k)\).
Thus \(\max(a,b)>32(k+3)^6\) gives \(c\ge a+b+k+1\), so \(r\ge k+3\) and \(\ell\le N-k-s-2\).
