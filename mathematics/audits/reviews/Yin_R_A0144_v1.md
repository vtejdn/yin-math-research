### Ranks, spectrum and mass

For arbitrary complex \(x,y\), the three roots of \(T^3-(x/2)T^2+(y/2)T-1\) have product one and realize \((x,y)\) under the two Laurent substitutions.
Hence each nonzero polynomial minor remains a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
After clearing negative exponents, a nonzero coefficient in the second variable excludes only finitely many exceptional first coordinates, and all the remaining slices have finite zero sets.
Fubini gives a null set for each chosen minor and hence an almost-everywhere rank equal to the field rank for each fixed matrix, including rank zero.
For a nonzero scalar polynomial the zero spectral projection vanishes by faithfulness of the trace, and normality gives dense range.
Its affiliated inverse exists, excluding annihilators on either side even in the ambient affiliated algebra.
For a rectangular matrix the kernel projection is the zero spectral projection of \(P^*P\), computed in \(M_n(W^*(H))\).
Integrating its ordinary matrix trace therefore gives \(n-r\) also for the ambient von Neumann dimension.

### Arbitrary affiliated matrices

For selfadjoint bounded \(A\) and \(VA=-AV\) in the affiliated algebra, taking adjoints gives \(AV^*=-V^*A\), whence \(A\) commutes with \(d=V^*V\).
It then commutes with the bounded inverse \((1+d)^{-1}\) and its spectral projections, hence with \((1+d)^{-1/2}\).
The bounded transform \(T=V(1+d)^{-1/2}\) anticommutes with \(A\) and satisfies \(T^*T=d/(1+d)\), so \(\ker T=\ker V\).
For \(A=S_0 I_k\), spectral intertwining sends \(1_{(6,12]}(A)\) into \(1_{[-12,-6)}(A)=0\), with the endpoint \(-6\) excluded.
