# Independent reviews of the metabelian quotient at noncyclic order twenty

## The central commutator and dihedral quotients

At 2026-11-26T16:11 UTC the fixed proof received the following inspection.
The independent reviewer verified the four square types, two-site conservation after reversing edges, the twenty-state return matrix, and the ten-state reduction in the mixed case. The reviewer also checked the class-two quotient and both types of dihedral generating pairs, including composite orders and the infinite dihedral case.

## The marked metabelian quotient

At 2026-11-26T16:13 UTC the fixed proof received the following inspection.
The independent reviewer checked the simple sign modules in both characteristics, the dihedral quotient obtained from a negative sign, the automorphism of the five-step module and finiteness. The centralizer splitting and the conjugation placing the actual first generator in a complement were also verified.

## Odd primes in the binary coinvariants

At 2026-11-26T16:19 UTC the fixed proof received the following inspection.
The reviewer checked the applicability of the cyclotomic cutoff and of the sign action in the coupled five-channel representation. Independently enumerating the 120 row permutations gave 24 distinct edge sets, then the exact extrema for all seventeen residual markings. The widths 25, 110, 315, 395 and 720 and the power-of-two possibilities for the coinvariants were verified. The reviewer had developed parts of the earlier cyclotomic estimates and did not develop the present seventeen certificates.

## The sixteen-point affine action

At 2026-11-26T16:21 UTC the fixed proof received the following inspection.
The independent reviewer reconstructed both permutations by finite-field multiplication and all 32 lifted edge costs. The 12 reversed edges conserve the number of inputs at each site; the two zero-cost circuits read the group elements stated in the proof. The reviewer checked the ten genuine twists, the bound of 26 on total column width, the quotient supplied by the binary module, and the ensuing sign decomposition. A second reviewer independently checked the affine bound and had contributed to the last module deduction.
