## Automatic continuity: all hypotheses checked

The nonatomicity argument is valid before any continuity is known.
If p is the spectral projection for an atom lambda, the full-domain identity on p gives `(x-lambda)E(p)=-tau(p)Omega`; left multiplication by p contradicts faithfulness.
Thus lambda(x) on H has no eigenvalues.
Left multiplication T on the Banach space M has no eigenvalues either, since the faithful inclusion M into H intertwines these operators.

On `Y=H direct_sum M`, the block operator `R=[[N,P|M],[0,T]]` is bounded.
The restriction P|M is bounded because the trace is a state.
The algebraic graph map `J(a)=(E(a),a)` obeys RJ=JT with the displayed sign.
Continuity of J is not used while checking R.

For smooth phi on C, the divided-difference kernel is bounded on the compact spectral square by a Lipschitz constant of phi.
The spectral realization of P therefore gives an actual HS operator Gamma_phi, even if the spectral representation of N on all of H has multiplicity.
This uses only the cyclic vectors generated by the spectral projections of Omega.
Its restriction to M is bounded M-to-H because `||a||2<=||a||`.
The off-diagonal kernel gives Gamma_z=P because the law is nonatomic.
The elementary product identity verifies the upper-right block of `Phi(phi psi)=Phi(phi)Phi(psi)`; the lower-right multiplication agrees with restriction of the Hilbert-space spectral calculus.
Thus this is a legitimate bounded triangular smooth calculus on H direct_sum M, without assuming a norm-countably-additive spectral measure on the Banach space M.

The localization proof supplies exactly super-decomposability.
For an arbitrary two-set cover of C, choose compactly supported smooth cutoffs inside the cover adding to one near the compact spectrum of x.
The associated closed ranges Y_j are invariant; their sum contains Y because the cutoff operators sum to the identity.
If lambda lies outside the support K_j, a smooth inverse g near K_j satisfies `Phi(g)(R-lambda)Phi(chi_j)=Phi(chi_j)` and the reverse product identity.
Commutation also shows Phi(g) preserves Y_j.
Both identities extend to the closed range, placing the restricted spectrum in K_j.
The decomposition operator commutes with R, which is the source's stronger super-decomposable case.
For T on M, a finite Borel partition subordinate to compact subsets of the open cover gives the required closed summands `p_j M`; multiplication by `(x-lambda)^{-1}p_j` checks their restricted spectra.
No assertion about an unbounded inverse is needed.

The no-divisible-subspace argument is correct.
For a normal N, divisibility puts v in Ran(N-lambda)^2 for every lambda, hence its vector spectral measure satisfies `integral |z-lambda|^-4 dnu_v(z)<infinity`.
If nu_v is nonzero, some measurable level set A_n of this integral has positive nu_v measure.
For centers in A_n, every radius-r disk has nu_v mass at most n r^4.
A bounded planar set can be covered by O(r^-2) grid disks with centers chosen in A_n and radius O(r), so nu_v(A_n)<=C n r^2 for all r, a contradiction.
At a point where the denominator vanishes, the range inclusion already makes the corresponding spectral mass zero.
This covers both atomic and nonatomic vector spectral measures.
Projecting an R-divisible subspace onto M, then including M in H, gives an N-divisible subspace; it must vanish.
The original subspace then lies in the first H coordinate and vanishes by the same argument.

Consequently Lau88 Proposition 3.7 applies to R.
Separately, the triangular eigenvector equations and the absence of eigenvalues for T and N show that R has no eigenvalues.
Thus the pair (R,T) has no critical eigenvalues, irrespective of algebraic codimensions.
Lau88 Theorem 4.1 applies with exponent n=1 to the algebraic J and gives its continuity.
The first coordinate yields the claimed bound for E.
Every premise has been checked independently of closability and of any adjoint domain.

## Scalar Fourier argument and actual adjoint

Normalization `E(a)=-D(a)+aD(1)` is bounded M-to-H by part A and the bounded multiplication estimate for the fixed L2 vector D(1); it annihilates 1 and has `[E,x]=P`.
Polynomial induction therefore identifies E with the canonical divided-difference contraction.
For exp(itx), Taylor polynomials and their first derivatives converge uniformly on a compact real interval containing the spectrum, so their divided differences converge uniformly.
Norm continuity of E then proves the same formula on these unitaries.

The integrand `u_t* E(u_t)` is continuous as an H-valued function and has norm at most C.
Hence the finite-interval Bochner integral exists and its average has norm at most C.
The scalar divided-difference bound by |t| is integrable on each fixed averaging interval, justifying Fubini.
The computed average kernel `(1-sinc(T(s-r)))/(s-r)` has the correct sign and is antisymmetric.
Swapping s and r gives the factor 1/2 multiplying the polynomial divided difference.

For every fixed polynomial, that divided difference is bounded on the compact real square.
The averaging factor is uniformly bounded and tends to one away from the diagonal; diffuseness makes the diagonal null.
Dominated convergence therefore gives the displayed functional limit.
Cauchy-Schwarz with `||A_T||2<=C` yields `|(tau tensor tau)(partial p)|<=2C||p||2`, including complex polynomials.
No principal-value limit, pointwise Hilbert transform, or Hilbert-space closability is assumed.

A diffuse compactly supported real measure has infinite support, so polynomial evaluation is injective; the represented divided-difference map is a well-defined derivation into the outer tensor bimodule.
Polynomials are dense in L2 by the compact real functional calculus.
The last bound is therefore precisely the definition of membership of `1 tensor 1` in the actual Hilbert adjoint domain of partial, with norm at most 2C.
Part B proves the ACSY24 premise directly and does not reverse an implication in the source.

For the marginal corollary, let A=W*(X_i) and let Q be the orthogonal projection onto L2(A).
This projection commutes with left multiplication by A.
Restrict the bare dual to A and compress its values by Q: the commutator with the selfadjoint X_i is still P restricted to L2(A), because Q Omega=Omega.
The scalar proof now applies in A.
The argument needs only this coordinate selfadjoint; it does not require the other normal coordinates to be selfadjoint or freely independent.
It gives the marginal vacuum, without constructing any joint vacuum.
