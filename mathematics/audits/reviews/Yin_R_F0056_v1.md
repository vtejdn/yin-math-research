### Standard representation and full original setting

The measure dmu(t)=4t^3 dt is a probability, h is bounded selfadjoint with spectrum [0,1], its zero spectral projection is zero, and W*(h)=A.
The inverse q=h^{-1} is unbounded but is an L2 vector with squared norm 2.
On the stated orthogonal tensor sum, prefix creation is an isometry and its adjoint is the first-factor trace, zero on grade zero.
The constant tensor subspace gives the semicircular Jacobi model, so b is selfadjoint with norm 2, zero mean and second moment one.

The W_m recurrence verifies freeness directly: each centered A slot kills first-factor contraction, and a centered b polynomial prepends a positive string of vacuum factors to such a slot.
The highest-grade term of a_0 b a_1 ... b a_n Omega is the corresponding elementary tensor, so induction gives a dense left vacuum orbit.
The mirrored calculation gives a dense right orbit.
The exceptional grade-zero commutators of prefix and suffix creation or contraction are P and -P and cancel for the two b actions.
Thus the right action commutes with the left algebra and its cyclicity makes Omega separating.

The tensor-reversing conjugation gives JxOmega=x*Omega on the polynomial star algebra.
Equality of the two norms, polarization and normal extension give a faithful normal tracial state with tau(1)=1.
Consequently the constructed representation is the required L2(M,tau), with the indicated bounded right multiplications.
No change of tensor norm or unverified free-product lift is needed.
The tuple is selfadjoint, hence normal; no mutual commutativity, freeness of (h,Y), or vacuum normalization of the duals is added to the original contract.

### Bounded merge operator and exceptional grades

The row integral of |F(s,t)|^2 is exactly 2, so the fiber Cauchy-Schwarz estimate gives a bounded T:K tensor K to K of norm at most sqrt(2).
Different input grades of R have orthogonal output grades, giving the same uniform bound.
Its commutation with left A uses the first-factor module identity in grade one and the untouched first factor in all higher grades; taking adjoints preserves it.

On grade zero, R ell is K_F and ell R is zero.
On positive grades, prefix creation and the last-two-factor merge commute.
Both compositions in [R,ell*] vanish on grades zero and one.
On grade two they give the same first-factor trace times T on the other two factors, and all higher grades have the same calculation.
Hence [R,b] is K_F on grade zero and zero elsewhere.

The kernel F is Hilbert-Schmidt, with squared norm 2.
The diagonal is null and F(s,t)+overline(F(t,s))=q(s)q(t), so K_F+K_F* is |q><q|.
The adjoint sign [R*,b]=-[R,b]* therefore gives

\[
[K_0,b]=\Theta(q\otimes q),\qquad K_0=R-R^*.
\]

This is an actual bounded rank-one operator on the whole space.
Since K_0 commutes with h and hq=1 in L2, multiplication of this rank-one operator on both sides by bounded h gives [K_0,hbh]=P.
No use of q as a bounded right multiplier occurs.

### Hilbert dual, last-factor lift and correction

The accepted analytic input is the normalized Hilbert transform on L2(R), with kernel 1/(pi(s-t)), Fourier multiplier -i sgn and norm one.
Its normalization and L2 extension were checked directly in [Tao06, page 1, Proposition 1.2 on page 3 and the following discussion on page 4](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~tao/247a.1.06f/notes4.pdf).
The symmetric truncation statement on page 4 was read as an accepted classical input, not as a proof supplied there.
The complete original Hilbert-transform or Calderon-Zygmund proof was not audited.

For zero extension E, the interval operator with kernel 1/(t-s) is -pi E* H E and has norm at most pi.
The weighted formula uses a unitary W:K to unweighted L2 and two bounded multipliers sqrt(w), giving norm at most 4pi.
Although the formula for W^{-1} contains an unbounded function on unweighted L2, W^{-1} is a unitary between the two different Hilbert spaces; no bounded inverse multiplier on one space is assumed.
Principal-value cancellation on compactly supported smooth functions gives [D_0,h]=P_A with the positive sign, and boundedness extends it to K.

The last-factor extension is bounded with the same norm.
It has commutator P with first-factor h only in grade zero, and commutes with b in every grade.
In particular, both orders in the grade-one contraction give tau_A(xi_0)D_0 xi_1.
The correction uses only bounded right multipliers by hb and bh.
The order identities rho(hb)P=hbP and P rho(bh)=Pbh, the second by traciality, cancel the two terms in [Dhat_h,hbh].
This gives every displayed dual relation and the stated finite norm bound for E_h.
No condition D_0 Omega_A=0, E_i Omega=0 or E_i in M is required.

### Actual ranks and bounded generation

The correction formula and commutation of right multiplication with left b give

\[
[E_Y,b]=\Theta(q\otimes q),\qquad
[E_h,b]=-\Theta(b\otimes q+q\otimes b).
\]

For the second formula, rho(hb)q=qhb=b and the other functional is tau(q xi bh)=tau(b xi).
The latter identity is first valid on bounded xi and extends by continuous L2 pairings.
The nonzero vectors q and bOmega lie in grades zero and one and are orthogonal.
The two-term operator therefore has rank exactly two, and the other operator has rank one.
All these commutators are bounded and defined on all of H.

For \(z_\epsilon=1_{[\epsilon,1]}(h)\) and \(a_\epsilon=h^{-1}z_\epsilon\), both cutoffs are bounded Borel functions in N=W*(h,Y).
The products a_epsilon Y a_epsilon=z_epsilon b z_epsilon have norm at most two and converge strongly to b because h has zero kernel.
Strong closure gives N=M.
Thus the duals act on precisely the standard space for the generated algebra of the tuple.
Affiliated multiplication and the earlier rationality Claims are not inputs to these dual and generation calculations.

### Full scalar-closure exclusion

The low-spectrum unit vectors xi_epsilon satisfy ||h xi_epsilon||<=epsilon and ||Y xi_epsilon||<=2epsilon.
Every nonconstant fixed word in h,Y therefore tends to zero on these vectors by its rightmost factor.
For each polynomial p with constant c, (p-c)xi_epsilon tends to zero.
Freeness and traciality give <xi_epsilon,b xi_epsilon>=0 and ||b xi_epsilon||=1.
It follows that

\[
\|b-p(h,Y)\|\ge\sqrt{1+|c|^2}\ge1.
\]

The selfadjointness of both generators makes the tested polynomial algebra a star algebra, so b lies at norm distance at least one from C*(h,Y).
For any a in that unital C*-algebra invertible in M, the lower bound on a*a and its rescaled Neumann series give (a*a)^{-1} in the same algebra and then a^{-1}=(a*a)^{-1}a* there.
Thus C*(h,Y) contains every expression allowed in the original scalar division closure.
The distance bound excludes the whole closure, not merely the displayed pencil or a chosen expression.

### Monic chart and original scope

Replacing h by X=1-h preserves selfadjointness, generation and the scalar polynomial and division closures, and changing E_h to -E_h gives the correct dual sign for X.
The affiliated inverse of the displayed monic pencil is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}q&b\\0&q\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Reachability follows from e_2 and E_12 e_2=e_1, and observability from e_1^T and e_1^T E_12=e_2^T.
These are the explicit minimality conditions used in Yin_C_F0007; no external realization theorem is needed for the stated check.
The left and right states are (q,b) and (b,q)^T, all in L2, and the coefficient ranks two and one match the directly calculated commutator ranks.
The diagonal q remains unbounded.

The direct identity b=qYq places b in the affiliated division closure, consistently with Yin_C_F0005's stated conclusion.
The displayed inverse and states also satisfy the L2 and L1 conclusions discussed in Yin_C_F0007 and Yin_C_F0010.
These are consistency checks, not reliance on the proofs of those Claims.
A single admissible d=2 setting and dual system disproves the universally quantified original implication, and no common rank bound is part of that contract.
