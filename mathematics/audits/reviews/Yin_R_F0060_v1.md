## MS23-U001: Independent foundation and domain finding

For every fixed finite \(d\ge1\) and \(-1<q<1\), the local conclusions follow under the stated q-Fock, symmetrizer and homogeneous-norm inputs.
Strict finite-degree positivity gives linear independence of raw word tensors.
The triangular top-degree part of Wick evaluation then gives algebraic freeness without requiring raw-word q-orthonormality.
The corrected recursion retains \(\delta_{j_{n+1},j_k}\), as forced by \(W(e_{ij})=A_iA_j-\delta_{ij}\).
Distinct-color and equal-color degree-two cases check the contraction.
This verifies the correction mathematically; the printed-page omission remains the retained primary-reading evidence.

Free deletion uses the undeformed tensor inner product:
\[
\|r_i\eta\|_q^2
=\langle\eta,(P^{(m)}\otimes Q_i)\eta\rangle_0
\le w(q)^{-1}\langle\eta,P^{(m+1)}\eta\rangle_0.
\]
Here \(0\le Q_i\le I\), and lowering degree by one preserves orthogonality between outputs from distinct input grades.
Summing squared bounds gives \(\|r_i\|\le w(q)^{-1/2}\) globally.
For fixed \(|q|<1\), logarithmic product tails are controlled by \(\sum_k|q|^k\), so \(w(q)>0\); at zero, \(w(0)=1\).
No q-orthonormality or identification of \(r_i^*\) with plain right concatenation is used.

Let \(\mathcal A=\mathbb C\langle X\rangle\) and let \(D_i^\circ\) be the polynomial-domain restriction.
With first-variable-linear inner products, the flip-star identity makes the conjugate functional Hermitian and its representing vector selfadjoint.
The formula
\[
D_i^\circ(p\Omega)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_i p)\Omega
\]
has zero vacuum value and satisfies \([D_i^\circ,X_j]p\Omega=\delta_{ij}\tau(p)\Omega\).
Conversely, expanding the normalized commutator on a word and applying it to the vacuum forces that formula.
The adjoint-vacuum functional has the conjugate identity's stated orientation; no hidden star or word reversal is dropped.
Starting from formal conjugates, Voi98-U002's no-relations argument ensures well-defined evaluated quotients.
For canonical q-Gaussians, triangular Wick evaluation already supplies that requirement.

For polynomial p, the finite Leibniz expansion of \([D_i^\circ,p^*]\) is a finite sum of bounded operators \(aP_\Omega b\).
For polynomial a,
\[
\langle D_i^\circ a,p\rangle
=\langle p^*a,\xi_i\rangle
-\langle[D_i^\circ,p^*]a,\Omega\rangle.
\]
Both terms are bounded by a constant depending on p times \(\|a\|_2\).
Consequently,
\[
p\in\operatorname{Dom}(D_i^\circ)^*,\qquad
(D_i^\circ)^*p=p\xi_i-[D_i^\circ,p^*]^*\Omega.
\]
Polynomial density proves closability of that restriction.
An arbitrary larger extension needs the functional bound on its entire domain; the current unit expressly keeps this qualification.
Every elementary commutator in the expansion carries its Kronecker delta, agreeing with the corrected p. 10 formula.
The undeformed-word terminology and one-variable norm \(\|e_n\|_q^2=[n]_q!\) are compatible.

The distinct difference-quotient argument is complete.
For \(a,b\in\mathcal A\), Voi98-U004 gives
\[
a\otimes b\in\operatorname{Dom}\partial_i^*,\qquad
\partial_i^*(a\otimes b)
=a\xi_i b-(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_i a)b
-a(\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\partial_i b).
\]
The output is in L2, and \(\mathcal A\odot\mathcal A\) is dense in the Hilbert tensor target.
Thus \(\partial_i\) is closable.
This is a different map and adjoint-domain conclusion from closability of \(D_i^\circ\), and supplies MS23-U004's graph step.

## MS23-U004: Independent complete partition and analytic finding

The family \(C(n+1)\) and rectangular drawing give the derivative formula at all lengths, including the empty-word zero and degree-one delta.

\[
N(\pi)=\operatorname{cross}(\pi)-|s_r(\pi)|
\ge k-1-|s_r(\pi)|=|p(\pi)|-1\ge0.
\]

\[
n-h-(|s_l|-r).
\]
