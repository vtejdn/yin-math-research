## Analytic Embedded-Arc Application

The proof requires holomorphic \(r\) near a compact real interval, injectivity on that whole interval and nonvanishing derivative there.
Its inverse on the image is continuous, so the functional-calculus identity \(W^*(S)=W^*(r(S))\) is justified, and the interval-indicator coefficients belong to the required algebra.

The jointly holomorphic divided difference of \(r^\#(z)=\overline{r(\bar z)}\) is nonzero on the real square: injectivity handles distinct points and the derivative condition handles the diagonal.
Compactness then supplies an open complex neighborhood of the entire square with no zero.
Hence the quotient \(F=G_r/G_{r^\#}\) is holomorphic there.
A compact square has positive distance from the complement of a smaller such neighborhood, which justifies one common polydisk radius and one finite bound for all pairs of partition cells.

On each rectangle, the radius-\(R\) Cauchy bounds and cells of diameter at most \(R/2\) give
\[
\|a_{jkmn}\|_\infty\|b_{jkmn}\|_\infty
\le M_F2^{-m-n}.
\]
Summing first in \(m,n\) and then in the finitely many pairs \(j,k\) yields the displayed \(4M_FN^2\) bound.
The finite grid is shared by all rectangles, so there is no unproved requirement of a partition adapted separately to infinitely many analytic expansions.
The reconstructed function agrees with the conjugate phase off the diagonal; Yin_C_F0037 makes that diagonal null for the relevant distribution.
Its different continuous diagonal value causes no mismatch in (47.2).

For \(r(t)=t+\mathrm i\varepsilon t^2\), real-part injectivity and \(r'(t)=1+2\mathrm i\varepsilon t\ne0\) prove the example for every real \(\varepsilon\).
This establishes the phase condition.
