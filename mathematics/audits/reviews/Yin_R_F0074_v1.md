## 2. Restriction of the Algebra and of the Adjoints

The real graph assumption gives
\[
S_i=(X_i+X_i^*)/2\in\mathcal P_X,
\qquad X_i=S_i+\mathrm i g_i(S_i)\in W^*(S).
\]
Hence \(\mathcal P_S\subseteq\mathcal P_X\) and \(W^*(S)=M\).
The first inclusion need not be equality for nonpolynomial g.
This suffices to make \(\mathcal P_S\Omega\) dense in H and the restricted operators and derivations densely defined.

If \(\partial_X\) is the coarse derivation on \(\mathcal P_X\) and \(\partial=\partial_X|_{\mathcal P_S}\), then \(\partial_X^*\subseteq\partial^*\).
Thus the vacuum tensor remains in the restricted adjoint domain, and its representing vector is unchanged: the two vectors agree on the dense smaller algebra.
Similarly \(D_{i0}^*\subseteq F_{i0}^*\) initially.
The proof correctly uses the latter restricted adjoint for the new estimates; equality of these two adjoints is only established at the end.

The graph parametrization \(r_i(s)=s+\mathrm i g_i(s)\) is injective on the real interval.
Yin_C_F0025's coordinate atomlessness therefore transfers to the law of \(S_i\).
On the off-diagonal spectral square,
\[
\frac{1+k_{X_i}(r_i(s),r_i(t))}{2}
=\frac12\left(1+\frac{1-\mathrm i g_i^{[1]}(s,t)}{1+\mathrm i g_i^{[1]}(s,t)}\right)
=K_i(s,t).
\]
This is exactly the derivative of \(S_i=(X_i+X_i^*)/2\).
The spectral diagonal is null, so its continuous convention introduces no extra atom or normalization term.
Other coordinates give zero as stated.

## 3. The Analytic Inner Multiplier and the New Adjoint-Domain Step

The divided difference \(g_i^{[1]}\) extends holomorphically across the complex diagonal.
It is real on the real square, hence \(|1+\mathrm i g_i^{[1]}|\ge1\) there.
Compactness gives a complex neighborhood of the square on which this denominator stays nonzero.
Both J and its reciprocal K are consequently holomorphic on a sufficiently small product of Bernstein ellipses after affine rescaling.
No smallness of the derivatives or coefficients of g is required.

The inner multiplier is formed from right multiplication on the first tensor factor and left multiplication on the second.
These commute with both outer module actions.
Thus \(I_J\partial\) remains a derivation, and \(I_JI_K=I\) holds as an operator identity on the whole coarse Hilbert space by their commuting inner spectral calculus.
Its value on each \(S_j\) is \(\delta_{ij}1\otimes1\).
Merely multiplying the derivative by an outer coefficient would not provide this argument; the target uses the correct inner action.

For the rescaled Chebyshev polynomial \(t_n\), the explicit derivative formula gives
\[
\|t_n\|_{\infty,I}\le1,
\qquad \|t_n'\|_{\infty,I}\le\frac{2n^2}{b-a}.
\]
The endpoint values are obtained by limits in \(U_{n-1}(\cos\theta)=\sin(n\theta)/\sin\theta\), and n=0 has derivative zero.
By the product rule for one-variable polynomials,
\[
\partial v(S_i)=v^{[1]}(S_i\otimes1,1\otimes S_i)K_i.
\]
For real s,t, the integral of v' along the segment from s to t bounds \(|v^{[1]}(s,t)|\) by \(\|v'\|_\infty\), even for complex polynomial v.
This proves the target's quadratic derivative estimate in Hilbert tensor norm.
It is an estimate of commuting spectral multipliers, not an unstated operator-Lipschitz theorem.

The polynomial tensor adjoint bound is valid without a reality condition on \(\partial\).
Expanding \(\partial(u^*pv^*)\) produces the functional paired against \(u\otimes v\) and two error terms.
Their bounds are respectively
\[
\|\partial(u^*)\|_2\|v\|\|p\|_2,
\qquad \|u\|\|\partial(v^*)\|_2\|p\|_2.
\]
The vacuum-adjoint term has bound \(\|u\|\|v\|\|\partial^*(1\otimes1)\|_2\|p\|_2\).
This proves both membership in the restricted adjoint domain and inequality (10) of the target.
In particular its use does not assume that the adjoint exists on arbitrary bounded tensor factors.

Chebyshev tensor coefficients of J decay geometrically, so \(\sum_{m,n}|c_{mn}|(1+m^2+n^2)<\infty\).
The polynomial tensors representing \(I_J^*(1\otimes1)\) use \(\overline{c_{mn}}\), because the \(t_n(S_i)\) are selfadjoint.
Their Hilbert tensor values converge by the unweighted absolute sum; their restricted \(\partial^*\)-images converge absolutely by the weighted estimate.
Closedness of that actual Hilbert adjoint yields the required domain membership.
This simultaneously justifies the conjugation of complex coefficients and the interchange with the adjoint limit.

Therefore \(\delta_i=I_{J_i}\partial_i|_{\mathcal P_S}\) has an L2 conjugate variable for each i.
The ordinary selfadjoint finite-Fisher interface applies to S.
Algebraic freeness and selfadjointness of its conjugates are the established consequences in that interface, not new assumptions about the original normal tuple.

## 4. Two Separate Estimates for the Restricted Original Dual

Dab10-U002 gives the closure E of the normalized contraction of \(\delta_i\), and its actual Hilbert adjoint, on every bounded element of M.
Its sufficient constants 4 and 5 times the L2 conjugate norm are consistent with the inner product linear in the second argument.
The source record also covers the one-variable case, so no hidden requirement of two or more generators is imposed here.

The Chebyshev expansion of K supplies \(\sum_r\|a_r\|\|b_r\|<\infty\).
With \(\rho(a)\eta=\eta a\), the Hilbert-Schmidt identification obeys
\[
\Theta(I_{a\otimes b}v)=\rho(a)\Theta(v)\rho(b).
\]
Apply this to the polynomial commutator \([E,p]\) and then to the vacuum.
It gives precisely
\[
F_{i0}p=\sum_r(E(pb_r)-pE(b_r))a_r.
\]
All inputs are bounded, hence belong to the actual domain of E.
The polynomial commutator extends to its closed domain because p and p* preserve the core and have bounded commutators.
The projective coefficient sum gives Hilbert-Schmidt convergence and an absolutely convergent L2 series.
This proves the first infinity-to-2 estimate.

For the second, the restricted polynomial adjoint identity is
\[
F_{i0}^*p=pF_{i0}^*\Omega-\Theta(\partial_i(p^*))^*\Omega.
\]
Taking adjoints of the multiplier formula reverses the two right factors.
The sign comes from \([E,p^*]^*=-[E^*,p]\).
The resulting term is
\[
\sum_r(pE^*(a_r^*)-E^*(pa_r^*))b_r^*,
\]
exactly as in the target.
Its bound uses the independently available E* estimate.
Neither the sign nor the tensor order is obtained by treating \(\partial_i\) as a real derivation.
This establishes the separate bound for \(F_{i0}^*\).

If the original dual was not normalized, \(p\mapsto p\eta_i\) has polynomial adjoint \(p\mapsto p\eta_i^*\), and both have infinity-to-2 norm at most \(\|\eta_i\|_2\).
Adding these terms restores both core bounds.
This does not identify a closure of a sum with a sum of closures.
Closability of each original operator has already been proved directly by Yin_C_F0025's polynomial-adjoint argument.

## 5. Weak Graph Limits and Equality with the Original Closure

Let F0 now be the restriction of the original operator to \(\mathcal P_S\), and T its closure.
Kaplansky approximation supplies a uniformly operator-norm-bounded net in that algebra converging to any x in M in L2.
The first estimate makes its F0-images bounded in H, so a weakly convergent subnet exists.
A norm-closed linear graph is weakly closed; therefore the limiting pair belongs to the graph of T.
This places x in the actual domain of T.

For the adjoint, the same approximating polynomials already belong to \(\operatorname{Dom}F0^*\), and the separate adjoint bound gives a weak graph limit in that closed operator.
Thus \(M\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}F0^*\) as well.
There is no assumption that the polynomial algebra is a core for F0*.
The norm bounds pass to the limit by weak lower semicontinuity, with the approximation tolerance tending to zero.

Now write \(D=\overline{D_{i0}}\) for the original larger-core closure.
Because \(F0\subseteq D_{i0}\) and D is closed,
\[
T\subseteq D.
\]
Every \(p\in\mathcal P_X\) also belongs to \(M\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}T\).
The inclusion just proved therefore gives
\[
Tp=Dp=D_{i0}p.
\]
Consequently \(D_{i0}\subseteq T\); taking its closure gives \(D\subseteq T\).
Both graph inclusions together imply
\[
\overline{D_{i0}}=\overline{F0},
\qquad D_{i0}^*=F0^*.
\]
The adjoint equality is now justified, rather than assumed when restricting the algebra.
This is the decisive repair of the unavailable identity \(\mathcal P_X=\mathcal P_S\).
It requires no approximation of \(g_i(S_i)\) in an unspecified graph norm.

The original generator relations consequently hold on the entire bounded algebra.
They can be extended by the stated bounded-set weak continuity; alternatively their bounded commutators already extend through the original graph closure.
The hypotheses of Yin_C_F0024 are thus met by these actual closures, and Yin_C_F0025 provides the stated reverse implication for bounded finite-rank polynomial weak forms.
