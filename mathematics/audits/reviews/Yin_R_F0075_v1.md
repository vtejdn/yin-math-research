## 2. The real unitary derivative and its actual adjoint

For the fixed unitary \(U\), the inner multiplier convention gives
\[
 \delta=\mathrm i I_{U\otimes1}\theta,\qquad
 \delta(U)=\mathrm iU\otimes1,\qquad
 \delta(U^*)=-\mathrm i1\otimes U^*.
\]
The tensor flip followed by adjoint converts the first formula into the second, so the claimed reality identity holds on the generators.
The outer Leibniz rule then proves it on the evaluated Laurent polynomial algebra; no algebraic independence assumption is required at this step.

The adjoint of the bounded multiplier satisfies
\[
 (\mathrm i I_{U\otimes1})^*(1\otimes1)
             =-\mathrm i U^*\otimes1.

## 3. Coassociativity defect and the norm identity

The difference
\[
 (\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta
       -(\mathrm{id}\otimes\delta)\delta
\]
is an outer derivation into the triple algebraic tensor product: the two middle product terms cancel.
On \(U\) it is \(-U\otimes1\otimes1\), and on \(U^*\) it is \(1\otimes1\otimes U^*\).
These are precisely \(\mathrm i\kappa\delta\), with \(\kappa(a\otimes b)=a\otimes1\otimes b\).
The candidate's sign in (11) is correct.

Contracting the last two tensor legs gives
\[
 E_0^2p=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)((\delta p)\cdot\zeta)
                                  +\mathrm iE_0p.
\]
All terms are defined on the polynomial algebra: \(E_0p\) is polynomial and \(\delta p\) is a finite tensor of bounded coefficients.
Its multiplication by \(\zeta\) only requires \(L^2\).

The review checked (13) with the declared inner product linear in the second variable.
The two key identities, for polynomial \(p\), are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \langle(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)((\delta p)\cdot\zeta),p\rangle
     &=\langle\delta p,p\otimes\zeta\rangle,\\
 \langle\delta(p^*)\cdot p,1\otimes\zeta\rangle
     &=\langle p\zeta,E_0p\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
Trace cyclicity and \(\zeta=\zeta^*\) verify both.
They give exactly the displayed real-part term and the additional \(-\langle\mathrm iE_0p,p\rangle\).
