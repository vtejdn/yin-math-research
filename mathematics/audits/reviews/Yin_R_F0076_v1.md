## 2. Scalar derivative and the distributional vacuum statement

For a starred scalar polynomial, the quotient
\[
 K_p(z,w)=\frac{p(z)-p(w)}{z-w}
\]
has the outer Leibniz rule and the required values on z and bar z.
It is therefore the actual Yin_C_F0025 marginal derivative.
The kernel is bounded on the compact spectrum squared off its null diagonal, because p is Lipschitz on a containing disk.
Its integral is an ordinary absolutely convergent integral; no principal value is needed at this stage.

With the candidate's kernel 1/(z-w), swapping the two variables changes the sign of the Cauchy term.
Consequently
\[
 \int E_\varepsilon p\,d\mu=2\int p\,C_\varepsilon\mu\,d\mu.
\]
The divided-difference bound and atomlessness justify dominated convergence of E_epsilon p in L2.
Under the inner product linear in the second argument, the density in the resulting bilinear polynomial pairing is
\[
 h=\tfrac12\overline{\mathbb E_{B_j}\xi_j}.
\]
Both the factor 2 and conjugation are correct.

The candidate properly stops at convergence of polynomial pairings.
It does not apply the uniform boundedness principle to a merely dense test subspace or declare L2 weak convergence without a uniform norm estimate.
It also does not claim that the stronger implication has been disproved.

## 3. Curvature, positive weights and constants

The source identity applies to a finite positive measure with uniform linear growth.
Curvature is nonnegative, and its truncated integrals increase to total curvature; hence under that growth hypothesis finite total curvature is equivalent to a uniform bound for the global truncated transforms of 1.

For a real f with sup norm at most 1, the measure nu=(1+f/2)mu satisfies
\[
 \tfrac12\mu\le\nu\le\tfrac32\mu.
\]
Thus its growth constant and mass are at most 3/2 times the corresponding bounds for mu, while its curvature integral is at most 27/8 times that of mu.
The source error and curvature term give, with the candidate's A,
\[
 \|C_\varepsilon\nu\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
 \le \frac{27}{8}A^2,\qquad
 \|C_\varepsilon\nu\|_{L^2(\mu)}
 \le \frac{\sqrt{27}}2 A.
\]
The second inequality uses the lower positive bound on the weight.
Subtracting the transform of mu and multiplying by 2 gives the real-f constant sqrt(27)+2.
Splitting real and imaginary parts gives the claimed complex constant 2(sqrt(27)+2).
All estimates are uniform in epsilon.

In particular the argument does not apply curvature positivity to a complex measure, nor does it infer an L2-to-L2 operator bound from a global test on 1.
The conclusion is exactly the needed L-infinity-to-L2 bound.
Combining it with the truncated divided-difference identity and its L2 limit proves the normalized scalar dual estimate.

## 4. The original adjoints and graph domains

The symmetric divided-difference kernel gives
\[
 \Theta(\partial_j(p^*))^*1
    =\overline{E_{j,0}(\bar p)}
\]
as a vector in the scalar coordinate subspace.
Yin_C_F0025's formula consequently yields
\[
 E_{j,0}^*p=p\xi_j-\overline{E_{j,0}(\bar p)}.
\]
This uses the full xi_j in H.
Replacing it by its conditional expectation would identify only a compressed/restricted adjoint and would not justify the stated conclusion.
The candidate makes the correct choice and tests the identity against the full original polynomial core.

For i different from j, both scalar-coordinate commutators vanish, giving E_i0 p=0 and E_i0* p=p xi_i.
Restoring right multiplication by eta_i gives exactly the two norm bounds stated in the candidate; the adjoint correction is multiplication by eta_i* and has the same L2 norm.

Uniformly bounded scalar starred polynomials can approximate any f in B_j in L2.
This follows from the bounded C*(X_j) approximation and the scalar Stone-Weierstrass density, with arbitrarily small norm slack.
Their original operator and adjoint images are bounded by the proved estimates.
Weak subsequential limits of graph pairs belong to the weakly closed graphs of the actual closure D_i and actual adjoint D_i0*.
The vector limits in the first component are f.
This proves the stated marginal domain inclusion and norm bounds without assuming an adjoint core.

Only for d=1 is this the all-M conclusion.
No graph convergence for products of arbitrary coordinate functions follows from these marginal bounds, and the candidate does not assert it.

The additional scalar construction from the geometric measure hypotheses is also valid.
Its commutators are the vacuum rank-one operator and the Hilbert-Schmidt phase kernel.
Uniform truncated vacuum bounds have an L2 weak cluster point; the dense polynomial pairings identify an adjoint-vacuum vector, with the conjugation prescribed above.
The full scalar polynomial adjoint formula then proves closability.
Thus the stated geometric examples meet the exact core assumptions.

## 5. The power-density boundary example

For dmu=(1-alpha)x^(-alpha) dx on (0,1), subtracting the constant x^(-alpha) from the kernel's density leaves an absolutely integrable remainder bounded by
\[
 C_\alpha x^{-\alpha}(1+|\log x|).
\]
After rescaling y=xs, the integrand is integrable at s=0 for alpha<1, has a removable singularity at s=1, and has an O(1/s) tail.

The truncated constant-density integral has the claimed endpoint-log bound in each of its four cases: both tails present, only the left tail, only the right tail, or neither.
Therefore the displayed bound is uniform in epsilon.
Its square is integrable against mu because 3 alpha<1; the logarithmic endpoint factors do not change integrability.
Local smoothness of the density gives pointwise principal values at interior points, and the common square-integrable bound gives strong L2 convergence.

The vacuum adjoint is 2h because here the kernel and limiting h are real.
The polynomial adjoint formula is valid and gives closability.
Both generator commutators are the rank-one P, so this example satisfies Yin_C_F0025, including compactness.
However mu((0,r))/r=r^(-alpha) diverges.
This is a valid counterexample to automatic linear growth even when global truncated vacuum transforms are uniformly bounded.
It is not a counterexample to the all-M domain conclusion; the existing selfadjoint result already covers it.

## 6. The sole correction and final scope

\[
 E_{j,0}(aX_jb)
 =(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)((\partial_j a)\cdot X_jb)
   +a\tau(b)
   +(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)(aX_j\cdot\partial_j b),

The middle term is the contraction of a tensor b.
The outer bimodule actions in the other terms are the original coarse-derivative actions.
