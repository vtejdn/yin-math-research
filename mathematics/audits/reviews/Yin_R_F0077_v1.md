## 2. Angular coefficients and vacuum orientation

The starred polynomial divided difference is bounded on the compact support squared off the null diagonal.
Its original integral is absolutely convergent.
For unequal radii the two geometric series converge uniformly in the angles.
Radius nonatomicity removes the equal-radius set from the product measure.
The resulting radial integrals are individually finite: their kernels are bounded by 1/r on s<r and by 1/s on s>r.

Recomputing each angular coefficient gives the candidate's equation (8), with output index n-1.
Input n<=0 has the positive inner term; input n>=1 has the negative outer term.
Thus the identity \(e_0p=e^{-i\theta}A(r)p-Tp\) has the correct sign.
This does not assert absolute integrability of the separated two-dimensional Cauchy kernels.

Only input mode 1 contributes to the vacuum functional:
\[
 \tau(e_0p)=2\int A(r)p_1(r)\,d\nu(r).
\]
The calculation does not need A in L2 beforehand: p_1(r)/r is a bounded polynomial in r squared.
For the inner product linear in its second variable, the representing vector is \(2e^{i\theta}A(r)\).
The check p=z gives \(2\int F\,d\nu=1\), agreeing with the rank-one commutator.

Necessity in equation (4) is also established.
Test the Fourier coefficients of a proposed L2 adjoint vector against \(r^{|n|}e^{in\theta}Q(r^2)\).
The weighted coefficient densities are L1.
Density of polynomials in r squared in C([0,R]) identifies their finite complex measures, giving zero for n different from 1 and \(r\overline{\xi_1}=2F\).
Since r>0 almost everywhere, cancellation is legitimate.
The result is precisely \(1\in\operatorname{Dom}e_0^*\) if and only if A belongs to L2(nu).

## 3. Hardy estimate and the constants

On [epsilon,R], F and B are continuous functions of bounded variation, even for singular radial measures.
Stieltjes integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_{(\varepsilon,R]}B^2\,d\nu
 =-F(\varepsilon)B(\varepsilon)^2
   +2\int_{(\varepsilon,R]}AB\,d\nu.
\]
The lower boundary term is nonpositive.
Cauchy--Schwarz and monotone convergence therefore yield \(\|B\|_2\le2\|A\|_2\).
There is no assumed vanishing boundary limit at zero and no hidden absolute-continuity hypothesis.

For the finite vector of angular coefficients, the triangle inequality and angular Parseval bound give pointwise bounds A(r)||p||_infinity and B(r)||p||_infinity for the inner and outer groups.
Their output frequencies are disjoint.
Consequently
\[
 \|Tp\|_2^2\le5\|A\|_2^2\|p\|_\infty^2,\qquad
 \|e_0p\|_2\le(1+\sqrt5)\|A\|_2\|p\|_\infty.
\]
This checks equation (5) for complex polynomials without an extra real/imaginary splitting factor.
No bound for isotropic truncations or from L2 to L2 is used.

## 4. Closability and original domains

The two generator commutators are P and the phase kernel \((\bar z-\bar w)/(z-w)\).

 e_0^*p=p\xi-J e_0Jp.

## 5. Power-density example and limits

For \(d\nu=\beta r^{\beta-1}dr\), one obtains F=r^beta and
\[
 \|A\|_2^2=\frac{\beta}{3\beta-2}.
\]
Thus 2/3<beta<1 satisfies the vacuum premise and the exact compact-core hypotheses.
Meanwhile \(\mu(B(0,r))/r=r^{\beta-1}\) tends to infinity, proving failure of uniform linear growth.

If the canonical dual had a bounded Hilbert extension E, its commutator with X would extend to P.
Compression by \(e=1_{\{|X|<r\}}\) gives
\[
 r^\beta=\|ePe\|
 =\|e[E,X]e\|\le2r\|E\|,
\]
since e commutes with X and \(\|Xe\|\le r\).
This contradicts beta<1.
The operator is therefore unbounded, despite both domains containing all bounded functions.
For 0<beta<=2/3 the vacuum premise fails.

No automatic theorem for arbitrary measures follows from these calculations.
The angular decomposition and continuous radial Stieltjes identity use the stated radius assumptions.
