## Mathematical checks

The hypothesis uses the coordinate algebras \(A_i=W^*(X_i)\), not the single algebra \(N\) relative to scalars.
The finiteness assumption therefore supplies every \(j_i\) needed by the construction.
A formal relation \(r\) evaluates to zero, and applying its moment identity to \(urv\) removes the other two Leibniz terms.
The resulting product-trace tests against arbitrary two factors force the evaluated tensor derivative of \(r\) to vanish.
This establishes an actual derivation on the quotient rather than presuming that a free-word formula defines an operator.

The signs were checked with the declared inner product.
The source convention has \(\gamma_i^*1=-j_i\), so \((\mathrm i\gamma_i)^*1=\mathrm i j_i\).
Skewadjointness of \(j_i\) makes this vector selfadjoint.
The adjoint of the bounded partial trace sends \(p\) to \(p\otimes1\); the real tensor-adjoint formula therefore proves actual membership in \(\operatorname{Dom}(C_i^\circ)^*\), with value \(p\eta_i-C_i^\circ p\).
Closability follows from its dense adjoint domain, not from a formal integration-by-parts notation.

The defect identity was checked on every coordinate algebra and then by the outer Leibniz rule.
It is exactly the nonzero defect used in C_F0063 Appendix A.
The contraction square and energy calculation take place on an algebraic core whose contraction values remain in that core.
The analytic-unitary step needs only \(\|a\|<1\), algebraic derivatives, reality and the actual L2 vacuum; the differentiated binomial coefficients are summable after multiplication by \(n\|a\|^{n-1}\).
Thus the proof does not use normality or existence of the ordinary conjugate-variable derivations in establishing the two norm bounds.
The adjoint bound is also checked directly from its core formula.
Mai15's coassociative norm theorem is not invoked.

For each bounded \(x\), a uniformly bounded Kaplansky net converges on the vacuum.
The two output nets are bounded simultaneously, so weak compactness in the product Hilbert space provides a common subnet.
The graphs of the actual closed operator and actual adjoint are weakly closed linear subspaces.
Both domain inclusions follow separately from these graphs.
This step requires both norm bounds and cannot be replaced by closability alone.

The core commutator is \([C_i^\circ,X_j]=\mathrm i\delta_{ij}[X_i,P_\Omega]\).
Multiplication of the closed operator by \(\mathrm i\) and reversal to the generator-first commutator give the required positive sign.
The bounded core commutator extends by graph-core approximation and proves domain invariance.
The coordinate sum was checked as an identity of derivations on each generating algebra.
A common weak subnet for the finite family extends the sum identity to all bounded vectors; no unproved identity between a sum of closures and a closure of a sum is used.
Adding \(I_{H_N}/d\) preserves closedness and gives the stated adjoint and normalization.

For the ambient extension, \(E_N(x)\) is bounded and its vacuum vector is the orthogonal projection of \(x\Omega\) to \(H_N\).
Both left multiplication by \(X_j\) and its adjoint reduce \(H_N\).
The direct-sum operator has exactly the stated adjoint domain, so both full-\(M\Omega\) inclusions, the commutators and normalization pass to the ambient algebra.
The one-coordinate projection test gives \(L=P_\Omega\), consistently with zero liberation information and atomic spectral law.
