## The New Domain Arguments

The claim about countable intersections concerns dense **left von Neumann algebra modules**, not arbitrary dense Hilbert subspaces.
For any positive epsilon, Yin_C_F0032 supplies one projection in each module with a summable sequence of complement traces.
Their meet belongs to every module by bounded left multiplication.
Its complement trace is at most the sum of the deficits.
Taking epsilon to zero therefore produces projections in the intersection converging to one in L2; multiplying them on the left by bounded elements proves density of the intersection.
This argument applies in a nonseparable finite tracial algebra as well.

For each right unitary word \(w\), the equality \(E_w=Ew^*\) has the stated order, and right multiplication is a Hilbert-space unitary commuting with the left algebra.
Thus each \(E_w\) is a dense left module.
The group of words is countable because there are finitely many designated unitary coordinates.
Its inclusion of the identity ensures \(F\subset E\), and closure under word multiplication ensures \(Fu=F\) for each group element \(u\).

## Paired Derivatives and the Non-Hermitian Form

For a unitary coordinate \(U\), both occurrences of \(\rho(U)\) in \(Q=-\rho(U)D\rho(U)\) are intentional.
Since the restricted domain is invariant under the unitary and its inverse, bounded unitary input and output transport preserves closability and gives the displayed transported closure.
No assertion that this is conjugation by an inverse is needed.

On the common domain, differentiating \(UU^*=1\) gives \([D,U^*]=-U^*PU^*\).
In the tracial representation, \(\rho(U)P\rho(U)=UPU\), because both map a vector \(v\) to \(\tau(Uv)U\).
It follows that

\[
[Q,U]=-UPU=[D,U^*]^*,\qquad [Q,U^*]=P=[D,U]^*.
\]

For other coordinates both sides vanish.
For a designated selfadjoint coordinate, \(Q=D\), and the same paired identities follow directly.
The Leibniz rule propagates them to all star-polynomials with the correct reversed order under adjoint: the adjoint of \([D,b^*a^*]\) is \(a[D,b^*]^*+[D,a^*]^*b\).
Thus (51.11) is valid also for complex coefficients.

The cross form uses \(\langle Qp,q\rangle\), not an assumed adjoint of \(D\).
