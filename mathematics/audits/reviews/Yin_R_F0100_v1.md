## Local identity and its exact limitation

1. Regard M_n(C) as n copies of the natural left A-module C^n by reading columns.
   Every A-module endomorphism is a block matrix `(c_rs)` with `c_rs in A'`.
   In matrix notation this has a finite expression `d(X)=sum_k c_k X b_k`, with `c_k in A'` and arbitrary `b_k in M_n(C)`.
   For `q in A''`, each c_k commutes with q, so `d(q)=q d(1)=0`.
   This proves that A'' is contained in the joint kernel, even before imposing `tau d=0`.

2. Conversely, for each `b in A'`, the map `d_b(X)=Xb-bX` is complex linear, kills 1, and has trace zero by traciality.
   Its left A-linearity is literal: `d_b(aX)=aXb-baX=a(Xb-bX)`, since ba=ab.
   Their joint kernel is `(A')'=A''`.
   Hence restricting to trace-zero-output maps has not made the joint kernel larger than A''.
   Both inclusions in (1) hold without assuming A is semisimple or star closed.

3. For UT2, commutation with e11 and e22 forces a commutant matrix to be diagonal, and commutation with e12 equates the two diagonal entries.
   Thus `A'=C1` and `A''=M2`.
   The column-module calculation then makes every A-module endomorphism right multiplication by a single matrix b.
   The condition d(1)=0 gives b=0.
   Thus **every** map of the specified class vanishes, not just the inner-commutator subfamily.
   The missing e21 direction cannot be detected by this method.
   This is an exact failure of the local detection condition, not a counterexample to the desired division-closure equality.

## Full free-product and domain argument

4. A finite vector-space basis of each A_i' suffices: commuting with its basis is equivalent to commuting with the whole commutant.
   The maps `d_(i,r)(c)=c b_(i,r)-b_(i,r)c` are bounded on the finite-dimensional Hilbert space, have no scalar output, kill A_i and 1, and commute with the left action of A_i.
   Their joint kernel on the centered factor space is `A_i'' intersect ker tau=A_i^circ` by the assumption A_i=A_i''.
   The trace-centered permitted space has an orthonormal complex basis extending to an orthonormal basis of the entire centered factor space, whether or not A_i is star closed.

5. Fix i,r.
   The reduced free-product Hilbert space splits orthogonally into the terminal-i fibers `K_prefix tensor M_i^circ`, for prefixes empty or ending outside i, and the remaining terminal types plus the vacuum.
   Acting by d_(i,r) on the last factor of each such fiber, and by zero elsewhere, defines a bounded operator T_(i,r), with norm at most the local norm.
   The absence of scalar output prevents a terminal deletion and prevents mixing of distinct prefix fibers.
   Accordingly the common kernel over r on each terminal-i fiber is precisely the permitted space A_i^circ.

6. The commutation proof with the left A_j action covers every boundary.
   Scalars are immediate, so take a centered a in A_j.
   On the vacuum the terminal image of a is zero.
   On one block c from factor i with j=i, the two compositions agree by `d(ac)=a d(c)`; removing the scalar component of ac changes neither d(ac) nor the terminal operation because d(1)=0.
   Moreover `tau(a d(c))=tau(d(ac))=0`, so there is no hidden scalar term on the other side.
   On a single block from another factor both terminal actions vanish, or a new first block is prefixed without altering the terminal operation.
   For a word of length at least two, changing the first block commutes with changing the last.
   If multiplication deletes the first block, the last block remains; even at length two its d-image is centered, so no further terminal deletion arises.
   These cases prove `[T_(i,r),L_a]=0` on the reduced-word span and then on all H by boundedness.
   No square-zero relation is needed.

7. C_F0116 Section 4 supplies the required statement for **any bounded T**: the class of actual closed affiliated operators a with `T Dom(a) subset Dom(a)` and `aT=Ta` on Dom(a) is a unital scalar-division-closed algebra.
   The proof was checked at its actual domains.
   Initial sum and composition domains are T-invariant, and the bounded operator `diag(T,T)` preserves their graph closures, giving the same property for affiliated sums and products.
   For an affiliated invertible a, `Dom(a^(-1))=Ran(a)`; from `T a xi=a T xi` one obtains range invariance and `a^(-1)T=T a^(-1)` there.
   No selfadjointness of T, commutation with T*, or boundedness of a is required.
   Applying this lemma to the bounded input algebra gives the commutation property for every affiliated rational element.
   For a bounded output f its actual closed domain is all H, hence `[T_(i,r),L_f]=0` in the bounded-operator sense needed next.

8. Let a reduced basis word be `w=v t s`, where t is a complementary centered letter in factor i.
   If s is nonempty, its first factor differs from i; consequently s* is a finite linear combination of reduced words ending outside i, even though the chosen factor bases need not be selfadjoint.
   If s is empty it is the vacuum.
   In either case every T_(i,r) kills `s* Omega`, and commutation implies that every T_(i,r) kills `f s* Omega`.
   The exact joint-kernel description on the terminal-i fiber then makes its coefficient at v t zero.
   Since f, v, t and s are bounded, tracial cyclicity is legitimate and preserves the required factor order:

   `0=tau((v t)* f s*)=tau(s* (v t)* f)=tau((v t s)* f)`.

   Any forbidden letter in any position can be chosen as t.
   Thus all coefficients of f containing a complementary letter vanish.
   This step uses full commutation, not merely that T kills f Omega.
   It proves support on reduced products of the permitted spaces A_i^circ.

## Reconstruction in the prescribed non-star algebra

9. Put B equal to the full factor-generated star algebra.

10. In each factor write the new permitted-plus-complement basis as `z_t=sum_s U_ts c_s`, with U unitary, and set `mu'_t=sum_s conjugate(U_ts) mu_s`.
    This is the coefficient transformation with the correct conjugation.
    The old and new transition spans agree factor by factor, preserving reachability and observability.
    Same-factor zero products are preserved as linear combinations of the old zero products.
    Also

    `sum_t mu'_t tensor z_t = sum_(t,s,r) conjugate(U_ts) U_tr mu_s tensor c_r = V`.

    Consequently the actual pencil and its power decay are unchanged.
    The argument does not require the new letters to be selfadjoint.

11. For a complementary letter t and arbitrary words p,q in the full monoid, the quantity `lambda mu'(p) mu'_t mu'(q) gamma` is zero.
    For reduced p t q this is the newly proved support assertion; for nonreduced concatenations a same-factor state product is already zero.
    The reachable columns span the whole state space and the observable rows separate it, so the middle matrix mu'_t itself vanishes.
    This works for any dimension of the permitted subspace, including zero or the whole centered factor space.
    The surviving letters all belong to the prescribed A_i, hence `V in M_N(A)`, while retaining the original norm decay.
    Constants and zero series are immediate and do not require a nontrivial minimal state space.

12. The DR97-U004 small-power procedure now applies in the unital Banach algebra M.
    A sufficiently large q makes each entry of V^q smaller than `1/(2N)`.
    Schur elimination of `I-V^q` uses scalar Neumann-invertible pivots; the iterative entry bounds `r_k=1/(2N-k)<1` justify every bounded pivot inverse.
    Every expression is formed from entries of V and scalar inverses available in M.
    Therefore all entries of `(I-V^q)^(-1)`, and then of

    `(I-V)^(-1)=(I-V^q)^(-1)(I+V+...+V^(q-1))`,

    No adjoints are adjoined, and no unbounded inverse is used in this final reconstruction.
    Multiplication by the scalar state row and column gives `f in Div_M(A)`.
    Conversely every bounded scalar inverse is its affiliated inverse, so the reverse inclusion is immediate.
    This proves the full equality (2).
