## 2. Weighted orbit norms and both commutators

For a fixed v in E, closability makes the map a to D_i(av), from the operator-norm Banach space M to H, bounded: a convergent graph in these two norms is a graph in H for the closure of D_i, and its limiting input remains in E.
Testing against the dense adjoint domain proves weak continuity on uniformly bounded strongly convergent nets of a.
These statements remain valid for v=e_k b_in once that vector is explicitly required to lie in E.

If v is a finite sum of c_k e_k, target (2) gives
\[
 \sum_n\|D_i(avb_{in})a_{in}\|_2
 \le \|a\|\sum_k\|c_k\|L_{i,k}.
\]
Thus target (4) is an absolutely convergent H-series and defines a linear map on F.
Its value depends on the vector v because every summand does; no uniqueness of the finite-sum presentation is needed.
The same estimate controls the tail uniformly along bounded left orbits, so termwise weak convergence passes to Q_i.
In particular no global Hilbert-norm bound for D_i or Q_i is being inferred.
Here F is already contained in M, since it is the algebraic span of the M e_k; the notation F intersect M in the target is harmless.

With the target's right-multiplication convention, the commutator sums are
\[
 [Q_i,X_j]
 =\sum_n\rho(a_{in})[D_i,X_j]\rho(b_{in}),
 \qquad
 [Q_i,X_j^*]
 =\sum_n\rho(a_{in})[D_i,X_j^*]\rho(b_{in}).
\]
Left invariance of F and of E supplies every domain composition.
For either bounded Hilbert-Schmidt representative T in these formulas,
\[
 \sum_n\|\rho(a_{in})T\rho(b_{in})\|_{\rm HS}
 \le \|T\|_{\rm HS}\sum_n\|a_{in}\|\|b_{in}\|<\infty.
\]
Consequently the algebraic calculation on finite partial sums has a justified limit both on the input vector and in Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

Under the spectral tensor identification, right multiplication on the output and input multiplies the kernel by a_in(z)b_in(w), in this order.
If K_i=Theta(k_i), symmetry k_i(z,w)=k_i(w,z) gives K_i^*=Theta(conjugate k_i).
The first commutator is therefore delta_ij K_i^*.
The second has kernel k_i times its conjugate, which is one almost everywhere by nonatomicity, and is delta_ij P.
The zero off-coordinate terms remain zero.
This proves both identities in target (5); it does not use an inverse of K_i.

## 3. Why closability of Q is not needed

This is the principal changed interface from Yin_C_F0086, whose original finite unitary transport happened to make Q closable.
I checked the subsequent argument without retaining that extra property.

First, the two generator commutator identities propagate to star-polynomials and balance the cross form.
Orbit bounds give the norm-continuous extension to C*(X), and uniformly bounded strong-star Kaplansky approximants give balance for all M.
Strong-star approximation is available here; mere strong convergence of the starred approximants is not assumed without it.

Second, for a projection e in F the only difficult normality term in a to b_i(e,ae) is a to <e,D_i(ae)>.
Approximation of e by vectors in Dom D_i^*, together with the D_i orbit bound, gives a norm limit of normal functionals.
The other term <Q_i e,ae> is already a normal vector functional.
Thus no vector in Dom Q_i^* is needed.
The non-Hermitian corner densities and their measure gluing use the same compatibility and support bounds as Yin_C_F0086; they do not require a selfadjoint density or a globally L1 glued operator.

Third, V_i consists of bounded p in F such that p xi_i^* is in H.
Left cutoffs of p xi_i^*, preceded by the bounded-vector cutoff when needed, yield weak convergence of triples
\[
 (p_n,D_i p_n,Q_i p_n)\longrightarrow(p,D_i p,Q_i p).
\]
The norm closure of the linear set of such triples is a weakly closed linear subspace of H cubed.
It need not be the graph of an operator.
This elementary fact suffices for density of the restricted triples and hence for the required D_i graph-core conclusion.
Closability of Q_i would be stronger than the property used.

Finally, for p,q in V_i the column polar decomposition gives
\[
 h=(p^*p+q^*q)^{1/2},\quad p=ah,\quad q=ch,
 \quad h=a^*p+c^*q\in F,
 \quad h\xi_i^*\in H.
\]
The spectral projections g_l=1_[1/l,infinity)(h) belong to F because they are bounded left multiples of h.
The corner identity applies to p g_l and q g_l.
The orbit at h gives the weak D_i and Q_i limits; the remaining factors converge strongly.
On its right side,
\[
 p g_l\xi_i^*=a g_l(h\xi_i^*)\longrightarrow p\xi_i^*
 \quad\text{in }H.
\]
The final right cutoff converges to multiplication by the support s of h, which is harmless in pairing with q=qs.
This verifies the adjoint and the order of xi_i^* in the formula.
The D_i graph core first extends q from V_i to F, then Yin_C_F0086's restriction core extends q to all of E.
The resulting adjoint is exactly that of the originally specified D_i, as claimed.

## 4. Coordinate-commuting projections

If e_i,k commutes with W*(X_i), then e_i,k b_in=b_in e_i,k.
It follows that this right product lies in E by left invariance, and
\[
 \|a\mapsto D_i(a e_{i,k}b_{in})\|_{\infty\to2}
 \le c_{i,k}\|b_{in}\|.
\]
The weighted sum is therefore bounded by c_i,k times the projective coefficient sum.
Only the family for this i is needed to construct its Q_i and V_i.
The common-domain E remains unchanged for the invocation of Yin_C_F0029.

## 5. The cancelling-block example

The bounded Haar dual input uses the corrected project sign in [MSY23-U005, Proposition 6.3](../literature/MSY23/Yin_U005.md#msy23-u005): terminal deletion B_i satisfies [B_i,X_j]=delta_ij P.
The source's opposite-sign convention was already corrected in that audit.
The example uses the corrected formula.

For positive eta finite almost everywhere, right multiplication rho(eta) is a closed densely defined operator with a dense left M-module domain.
Positivity and non-L1 integrability are compatible.
Bounded perturbation makes D_1=B_1+rho(eta) closed on E, with D_1^*=B_1^*+rho(eta) on the same E.
The bounded restriction D_2 is closable, and the added right multiplier commutes with every bounded left multiplier.
Thus the example satisfies the actual dual identities and already has the desired adjoint test modules.

Each translated domain {v:v U^r in E}=E U^(-r) is a dense left module.
Yin_C_F0086's countable-intersection argument applies to all integers r and yields the stated F; its restriction-core argument applies to the original D_1,D_2.
This supplies a fixed common domain before any divergent expansion is considered.

The free-algebra assertion was checked directly and against the retained [NS06 book](../../literature/pdf/NS06.pdf), Definition 5.6, Example 5.7 and Proposition 5.11, printed pp. 66-68, PDF pp. 83-85.
A product of nonidentity powers from successively distinct conjugate cyclic subgroups expands as
\[
 U^{-n_1}V^{k_1}U^{n_1-n_2}V^{k_2}\cdots
 U^{n_{l-1}-n_l}V^{k_l}U^{n_l},
\]
where the interior U powers and all V powers are nonzero.
This is nontrivial in the free group.
The cited proposition gives freeness of the group algebras; bounded L2 approximation extends it to the von Neumann algebras needed for z_K.
Their centered copies are orthogonal, which proves target (8) by expanding the squared norm.
No free law of large numbers or unchecked moment formula is imported.

The choices of K_m and N_m are possible because tau(min(eta,K)) increases to infinity, whereas every bounded truncation has a finite L2 norm.
For e a projection in F, each e U^(-n) eta U^n belongs to H, so e A_m and e A_m e belong to H.
The finite affiliated sum A_m is positive, and comparison with its bounded positive truncation gives
\[
 \tau(eA_me)
 \ge c_m\mu_{K_m}\tau(e)-\|e\|_2/m.
\]
This uses only the trace of the compressed L2 operator, which is finite in the probability-trace setting.
Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to eA_m and e yields exactly target (9).
It does not apply a finite trace to eta itself.
Finite sums, products and positivity here have the accepted finite-tracial affiliated-algebra meaning; no unclosed sum of unbounded operators is substituted for that product.

The positive block has projective norm at most c_m and the negative block has the same bound.
Thus their ordered concatenation is absolutely summable in the Banach projective tensor product, sums to zero, and also sums uniformly as a function on the two-torus.
Appending it to the finite tensor -U tensor U leaves the conjugate unitary phase unchanged.

On any fixed nonzero projection e in F, the positive-block paired value is eA_m plus a vector of norm at most c_m times ||B_1|| times ||e||_2.
Its norm tends to infinity by (9), whereas the subsequent negative block cancels it exactly.
Hence the partial sums of the paired series cannot be Cauchy.
Every dense left submodule of F contains a nonzero projection by Yin_C_F0032, so there is no such submodule on which this ordered series converges at every vector.
Failure of (2) for these coefficients follows as well, since (2) would imply absolute convergence on the corresponding large projections.

The quantifier boundary is decisive: this is a bad infinite representation of a phase which also has a good finite representation, for an example whose adjoint domains are already suitable.
It disproves the claimed automatic action of arbitrary projective expansions on arbitrary unbounded duals.
It neither disproves existence of a suitable paired operator nor provides a counterexample to automatic adjoint modules or to Yin_T_F0005 Q1.
