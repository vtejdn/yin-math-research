## Square sums and strong convergence

Fix one coordinate.
For \(p\in M\), the map \(T_p(b)=E(pb)\) on the commutative marginal \(N\) is bounded by \(C\|p\|\).
The finite-family theorem in Pis12 applies to any unital commutative C*-algebra; \(N\) is one, and restriction to the algebra generated by the coefficient family is also sufficient.
The application introduces no normality assumption on \(E\), no complete-boundedness assumption and no unproved identification of a domination measure with the trace.

Applying the finite-family inequality to the first \(N\) input coefficients and taking an increasing limit gives
\[
 \sum_n\|E(pb_n)\|_2^2\le(4/\pi)C^2\|p\|^2 B^2.
\]
The row-operator identity has the correct order for right multiplication:
\[
 \rho(a_n)\rho(a_n)^*=\rho(a_n^*a_n).
\]
The square-sum assumption therefore bounds every finite row and every finite tail row by \(A\).
Since the input vectors are square summable, the output series is Cauchy in \(H\) for every fixed \(p\), with bound \(2CAB\|p\|/\sqrt\pi\).
It is linear by the pointwise norm limit and is a bounded map on all \(M\).

This is pointwise strong convergence of the maps on each vector of the Banach domain.
The proof neither needs nor establishes convergence of their operator norms or of the coefficient square-sum tails in \(L^\infty\).
Using the fixed bound \(A\) together with the convergent input square sum is exactly what validates the tails.

## Phase order and actual core identification

\[
 \rho(a)\Theta(g\otimes h)\rho(b)=\Theta(ga\otimes bh)

was checked directly from \(\Theta(g\otimes h)=gPh\) and the tracial input pairing.
Thus the chosen series \(Q(p)=\sum_nE(pb_n)a_n\) has unstarred commutator kernel \(\sum_n a_n\otimes b_n\), approaching \(\overline{k_i}\), with the coefficient order claimed.

For the starred commutator, all coefficients and both legs of the marginal phase lie in \(N_i\), so the kernel is \(k_i(z,w)\sum_n a_n(z)b_n(w)\).
This does not commute unrelated generators through the coefficients.
The off-coordinate starred commutators vanish by the precise C_F0070 statement.

The same bound holds after multiplication by \(k_i\).
Dominated convergence in the marginal product probability space gives convergence in the coarse Hilbert norm and hence in Hilbert-Schmidt norm after \(\Theta\).

## Fourier sign, input norm and trace normalization

For \(m(t)=\mathrm i(\pi-t)\) on \(0<t<2\pi\), integration against \(e^{-ikt}\), with normalized circle measure, gives \(\widehat m(0)=0\) and \(\widehat m(k)=1/k\) for every nonzero integer \(k\).
In particular the sign in the candidate is positive.
Multiplication by \(m\) has norm at most \(\pi\); compression to the Fourier modes \(1,\ldots,n\) produces exactly the matrix with entries \(1/(i-j)\).
Parseval and the orthogonal projection prove its \(\ell^2\) bound \(\pi\).

The domain block has at most \(n\) coordinates of modulus one, whereas \(L^2(\mathbb C^{2n})\) uses normalized counting trace on all \(2n\) coordinates.
Consequently the input-map norm is at most \(\pi\sqrt n/\sqrt{2n}=\pi/\sqrt2\), exactly as stated.
No convention for an unnormalized matrix trace is silently used.

The normal coordinate has \(2n\) distinct nonzero values on the positive real and positive imaginary axes, all of modulus at most one.
Equal cross-block indices remain different spectral values, but their matrix entries are zero, so omitting them is correct.

## All-row lower bound and both normalizations

For the nonzero cross-block entries,
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\frac{k_{ij}}{i-j}
 =\frac{1-4^{-|i-j|}}{1+4^{-|i-j|}}\frac1{|i-j|}
 \ge\frac3{5|i-j|}.
\]
Both signs of \(i-j\) were checked.
For the indicator of the input block, every output row therefore has real part at least
\[
 \frac35(H_{i-1}+H_{n-i})\ge\frac35H_{n-1}.
\]
The last inequality holds by termwise comparison of the relevant harmonic tails, including the boundary rows.
Summing over all \(n\) output rows and using the \(2n\)-point normalized trace gives \(3H_{n-1}/(5\sqrt2)\).
The ratio to the uniform input bound tends to infinity.
Complex conjugation of the phase leaves the real-part calculation unchanged.

For the normalized input, \(v_n=E_n(1)\) has \(L^2\) norm at most \(\pi/\sqrt2\), and \(B_n(f)=\tau(f)v_n\) has the same upper bound.
Hence \(E_n^0=E_n-B_n\) vanishes at \(1\) and has norm at most \(\sqrt2\pi\).
The matrix entries of \(B_n\) are \(v_n(r)/(2n)\), so entrywise multiplication by a phase of modulus at most one gives the pointwise bound
\[
 |(Q_kB_n)f(r)|\le |v_n(r)|\|f\|_\infty.
\]
This verifies the needed correction bound without a general Schur-multiplier theorem.

For the same block indicator, the subsequent output-normalization term \(f(Q_kE_n^0)(1)\) vanishes on the output block because \(f\) is zero there.
It also vanishes on the input block because both original maps have zero rows on that block.
The normalized lifted value on this test is therefore unchanged.
The triangle inequality gives
\[
 \|R_n\|_{\infty\to2}\ge
 \frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\frac35H_{n-1}-\pi\right),
\]
while the normalized input norms stay bounded.
This proves the strengthened obstruction, including normalization at both stages.
