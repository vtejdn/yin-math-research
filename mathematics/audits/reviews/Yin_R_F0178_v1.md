### Strict domain, quantifiers and the undefined boundary inverse

The trial domain is strict positivity of the selfadjoint linear operator \(\mathcal C_b-\eta\) on the entire complex Hilbert--Schmidt space, with \(b\) a positive definite matrix.
It is not positivity preservation of that difference.
Taking \(b=tI\) with sufficiently large \(t\) proves nonemptiness for every covariance, including zero covariance.

For \(\Psi=\mathcal C_{b^{-1/2}}\eta\mathcal C_{b^{-1/2}}\), congruence gives \(I-\Psi>0\).
The map is completely positive and Hilbert--Schmidt selfadjoint.
For a Hermitian eigenmatrix, expanding its positive and negative parts gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Tr}(X\Psi(X))|
 \leq \operatorname{Tr}(|X|\Psi(|X|))
 \leq \lambda_{\max}(\Psi)\operatorname{Tr}(X^2).
\]
A nonzero Hermitian eigenmatrix can be chosen because the spectrum is real and the map preserves adjoints.
Consequently every eigenvalue has absolute value at most \(\lambda_{\max}(\Psi)<1\), including the zero-map case.
It follows that \(I+\Psi>0\) and \(\mathcal C_b+\eta>0\).
The inverse in the objective therefore exists as an inverse of a finite-dimensional linear map and maps Hermitian matrices to Hermitian matrices.

The nonstrict source expression has a real definition defect.
For \(u=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), \(\eta(X)=uXu\) and \(b=I_2\), the diagonal matrix subspace has eigenvalue \(+1\) and the off-diagonal subspace has eigenvalue \(-1\) for \(\eta\).
Thus \(I-\eta\geq0\), whereas \(I+\eta\) vanishes on the off-diagonal subspace.
The strict-domain theorem neither supplies that missing ordinary inverse nor proves a pseudoinverse extension.

### Global comparison, support and kernel

For fixed positive regularization, the penalized mean functional
\[
 F_\varepsilon(a)=\ell_\varepsilon(a)
       -\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}_m(a(I+\Psi)^{-1}(a))
\]
is differentiable on the full real vector space of Hermitian means.
The logarithmic term grows at most logarithmically in the matrix norm, while the subtracted quadratic form is bounded below by \(\|a\|_2^2/(2(1+\rho))\).
This proves attainment of a global maximum.
The traced logarithmic derivative is \(\operatorname{tr}_m(Hh)\); stationarity is used only at that maximum and gives \(a=(I+\Psi)H\).

With the actual expected resolvent \(R=H+iQ\), \(Q>0\), C_F0119's Dyson identity gives
\[
 R^{-1}=H-iB,\qquad B=\Psi(Q)+\varepsilon I>0.
\]
C_F0119's primitive has the required sign under \(R=-G\), and subtraction of the mean penalty gives
\[
 F_\varepsilon(a)=-\tfrac12-\tfrac1m\log|\det R|
                         +\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2m}\operatorname{Tr}Q.
\]
Its additive constant comes from the large-imaginary-parameter normalization of the primitive.

The product split is valid in the order actually written.
Subtracting \(RR^{-1}\) and \(R^{-1}R\) first gives \([Q,B]+i[Q+B,H]=0\).
Its first summand is skewadjoint and its second selfadjoint, so both vanish.
Only then is \(QH-HB\) selfadjoint, allowing the subsequent selfadjoint/skewadjoint separation.
This yields
\[
 QB=BQ=I-H^2,\qquad QH=HB,\qquad HQ=BH.
\]
No premature real/imaginary split of a non-Hermitian coefficient is used.

The support \(e\) and kernel \(f\) of \(H\) reduce \(Q,B,R\).
On \(e\), \(U=\operatorname{sign}(H_e)\) satisfies \(B_e=UQ_eU\) and \(R_e^{-1}=UR_e^*U\), whence \(|\det R_e|=1\).
On \(f\), \(R_f=iQ_f\) and \(B_f=Q_f^{-1}\).
Thus the remaining determinant is \(\det Q_f\); no inverse of \(H\) on its kernel is required, and both signs of the nonzero spectrum of \(H\) are allowed.

For \(A=A^*\), \(\|A\|\leq\rho<1\), and a selfadjoint involution \(J\), the geometric series proves \(\|(J-A)^{-1}\|\leq(1-\rho)^{-1}\).
Writing \(J=I-2P\), the finite-rank inverse formula gives a negative semidefinite correction to \((I-A)^{-1}\), because \(PD_0^{-1}P>I_P/2\), where \(D_0=I-A\).
Hence \(L=(J-A)^{-1}\leq(I-A)^{-1}\).
For the actual \(J(X)=UXU\) and completely positive \(A\), every term of \(\sum_{k\geq0}(JA)^kJ\) is completely positive.
The map \(L\) is completely positive and Hilbert--Schmidt selfadjoint; it need not be positive as a Hilbert--Schmidt operator.
The proof uses the separately established order comparison at exactly that point.

All corner pairings use unnormalized traces.
Compression to \(M_e\oplus M_f\) preserves Hilbert--Schmidt selfadjointness and the norm bound.

 D=I_f+\Psi_{fe}(L(I_e))\geq I_f.

The Schur complement of \(I-\Psi\), together with \(L\leq(I-A)^{-1}\), proves \(I_f-\Phi>0\).
In particular \(\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(I_f)<r\) when \(r>0\).

A completely positive Hilbert--Schmidt selfadjoint map admits Hermitian Kraus matrices by averaging a Kraus representation with its adjoint and taking Hermitian real and imaginary parts.
C_F0120's finite positive-cone argument therefore applies to the effective covariance and drift \(\varepsilon D>0\).

                       +\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}L(I_e)
