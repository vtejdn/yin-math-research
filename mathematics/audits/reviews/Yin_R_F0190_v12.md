### Pair model, normalization and conditional floor

For every unordered off-diagonal position, the active Wigner entries produce exactly the displayed pair \((U,V)\).
Independence of all original families within a dimension gives independent, identically distributed pairs and independent, identically distributed diagonal entries.
The diagonal sum is a permitted fixed centered complex law with finite second moment; it need not be real after multiplication by the coefficients.
Centering and independence give
\[
 \mathbb E\left|\sum_{j\in I}a_j\xi_j\right|^2
 =\sum_{j\in I}|a_j|^2,
\]
so the asserted normalization of \(\kappa\) follows from the unit-variance hypothesis.
The complete real-coordinate covariance pattern, rather than a scalar correlation condition alone, is explicitly assumed.

The background uses only inputs outside the active index set and is therefore independent of the full raw pair matrix.
The entry-counting identity for the squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm gives a failure bound of order \(N^{1-2K}\) for an input operator norm exceeding \(N^K\).
For any chosen \(b>0\), taking \(2K\ge b+3\) makes this at most a constant times \(N^{-b-2}\).
On that event's complement, fixed degree and coefficients bound every entry of the raw shift by \(N^\alpha\), for a fixed sufficiently large \(\alpha\) and all sufficiently large dimensions.
This covers constant backgrounds and the case of no background variables.

The (NO-floor) constants are uniform over the admitted deterministic shifts, so integration over the independent background is legitimate.
Scaling back multiplies singular values by \(|\kappa|/\sqrt N\); enlarging the fixed exponent absorbs this factor and the use of a strict inequality in the candidate floor.
