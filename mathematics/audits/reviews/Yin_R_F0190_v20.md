### Exact model and finite free target

Both adopted inputs concern the same two independent original real laws, original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization, independent finite-second-moment diagonal laws, slopes \(0,1,\sqrt2\), identity path and unshifted pencil.
Their probability estimates impose no independence across sizes.
The middle proof supplies one simultaneous event for all the needed indices; its qualitative failure probability is used once.
Choosing a single fixed \(D_*>0\) is enough, and no summability of that qualitative error is needed.

For increasing index \(r=j+1\), the middle range gives
\[
 a_{r,N}\ge c_1(r/(3N))^{7/2}\qquad(r\ge r_N=6m_N+2).
\]
Consequently the empirical mass of \([0,t]\) is bounded by \(r_N/(3N)+Ct^{2/7}\) on the middle event.
The accepted weak spectral theorem applies to this fixed selfadjoint matrix polynomial.
Continuous absolute value therefore gives the singular-law limit \(\nu_{|\ell|}\) under every prescribed coupling.
At every positive continuity point \(t\), the empirical distribution function converges to the deterministic limit in probability.
Since the middle event has probability tending to one and \(r_N/N\to0\), the limit obeys \(\nu([0,t])\le Ct^{2/7}\).
Approximating any positive \(t\) from above by continuity points proves the same bound at all sufficiently small \(t\).
This also proves \(\nu(\{0\})=0\), rather than assuming it in the weak-limit step.

Integration against \(dx/x\), including the endpoint term \((-\log t)\nu((0,t))\), gives the stated \(Ct^{2/7}(1+|\log t|)\) negative-tail bound.
The positive free logarithm is bounded because the free semicircular inputs and hence the pencil are bounded.
Thus \(v=\log\Delta_{\tau_3}(\ell)\) is finite before invoking the cubic identity.
There is no circular use of a pre-assumed finite cubic logarithm.

### Original logarithmic tails and probability convergence

The deterministic middle profile permits the logarithmic sum only up to \(r\le CNt^{2/7}\), giving the claimed middle modulus.

On the adopted pencil-floor event, every omitted bottom logarithm is bounded above by \((17/2)\log N+|\log a|\).

\[
 O(N^{-1/6}(\log N)^{7/6})\longrightarrow0.

Zero singular values contribute infinite negative logarithm and are excluded only by the floor good event; they have not been silently removed from a determinant or empirical measure.
These bounds prove exactly the displayed iterated probability uniform-integrability assertion.

The parent Frobenius calculation gives \(\|L_N\|_F^2/N\to10\) in probability, with \(1+2+3\) from the diagonal auxiliary blocks and four from the path blocks.

 \frac1{3N}\sum_{a_{r,N}>T}\log a_{r,N}

### Finite and bounded free determinant algebra

The two-pivot calculation uses identity blocks only, so it is valid without inverses of \(A,B,E\).
Its finite block-column swaps each have sign \((-1)^{N^2}\), which cancel.
The block row subtractions have determinant one and leave the exact final block \(EBA-E-A\).
With \(A=C_N\), \(B=C_N+D_N\) and \(E=C_N+\sqrt2D_N\), this is precisely
\[
 (C_N+\sqrt2D_N)(C_N+D_N)C_N-2C_N-\sqrt2D_N,
\]
with no reversal, transpose or parameter change.
Hence \(\det L_N=\det P_N\), including singular matrices.

The same finite algebra takes place in \(M_3(\mathcal M)\) for the bounded free inputs.
Each row factor is a bounded elementary unitriangular element, and each column permutation is unitary.
The accepted unitriangular proof gives determinant one for elementary factors; finite factorization gives it for the final upper unitriangular factor as well.
The final displayed factorization has \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1,p)\) on the left, so it introduces no inverse of \(p\).
Bounded FK multiplicativity applies in the faithful finite tracial algebra, including when a factor has zero determinant.
The normalized block trace then gives
\[
 \log\Delta_{\tau_3}(\operatorname{diag}(1,1,p))
 =\tfrac13\log\Delta_\tau(p)
\]
in the extended-real sense.
It follows that \(\Delta_{\tau_3}(\ell)^3=\Delta_\tau(p)\).
The already finite \(v\) proves \(u=3v\in\mathbb R\), and the finite identity gives the pointwise equality \(Y_N=3X_N\).
Thus the scalar probability limit has exactly the same factor three as the free identity.
No logarithmic perturbation from a shifted pencil to a cubic is invoked.
