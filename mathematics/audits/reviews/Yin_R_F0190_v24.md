### Original finite-variance controls and the probability-one event

The input model requires fixed laws, finite second moments and independence of upper-triangular row blocks within each dimension.
These match C_F0186_v4 exactly, including dependence between the real and imaginary parts of a complex entry and arbitrary dependence between different dimensions.
The earlier direct inspection checked the finite-variance truncation comparison, rank concentration, raw-entry clipping, logarithmic-growth concentration and eigenvalue transfer used here.
In particular, raw clipping at modulus \(N^2\) has disagreement probability \(O(N^{-2})\), and the logarithmic-growth bounded-differences probabilities are summable.
Only first Borel--Cantelli enters these pathwise conclusions, so no independent-size assumption is hidden in their use.

Fixed-regularizer convergence on a countable dense planar set extends to local uniform convergence by the deterministic singular-value Lipschitz bound.
Intersecting over regularizers \(1/k\) and integer-radius disks gives the event needed by the candidate.
The logarithmic second-moment bound follows from the singular-value logarithmic-growth bound through Schur triangularization and Cauchy--Binet, independently of any desired Brown convergence.
Thus tightness and the large-eigenvalue control do not use the conclusion of this theorem.

On that event, the inequality between the unregularized and regularized potentials gives the positive-part bound for every disk.

Integer-radius disks dominate the compact sets subsequently used, avoiding an uncountable intersection of probability-one events.

### Repeated variables and the two moments

For the stated example, the standard semicircular spectrum of \(x\) lies in \([-2,2]\), so \(5-x^2\ge1\) in the free algebra.
Both factors may share \(x\) and \(y\), since neither the support theorem nor the bounded cyclic identity requires independence between those factors.
The free conditional second-moment identity for the two appearances of \(y\) follows from freeness and its centered variance-one law.
In the expansion of \(\tau(abab)\), the two terms with \(\tau(xb)\) vanish, while the other two each equal \(\tau(b)\tau(x^2b)\).
This gives \(2\cdot4\cdot3=24\), the Brown radial second moment.

The cyclic expression for \(\tau((ab)^*ab)\) is \(\tau(a^2b^2)\).
Its nonzero terms are \(\tau(x^2b^2)\) and \(\tau(b^2)\), which equal 10 and 17 using the semicircular moments 1, 2 and 5.
Thus its singular second moment is 27.
A normal element would have equal Brown radial and singular second moments, so this difference proves nonnormality in the free algebra.
The displayed two-by-two witness also multiplies to the claimed matrix with off-diagonal entries 4 and 5, whose two products with its adjoint differ.

The operator bounds for \(a\) and \(b\) are 8 and 5, yielding 40 for their product.
For any fixed \(R>40\), the clipped radial-square test is bounded and continuous and equals the radial square on the limiting Brown support.
Its almost-sure limit is therefore 24 without any claim about untruncated high finite-matrix trace moments.

### Eventual finite-dimensional indefiniteness

The Pareto law has second moment 3 and finite first moment; independent symmetric multiplication and division by \(\sqrt3\) give the stated centered variance-one off-diagonal law.
Zero diagonal entries are allowed by F0012.
For a normalized off-diagonal entry, exceeding \(\sqrt5\) is exactly the event that its Pareto radius exceeds \(\sqrt{15N}\).
Its probability is \((15N)^{-3/2}\).
Independence within the dimension gives the exact product probability for the maximum, and \(N(N-1)/2\) trials yield a bound \(\exp(-c\sqrt N)\).
This bound is summable.

First Borel--Cantelli makes an off-diagonal entry exceed the threshold eventually under every cross-size coupling.
The Hermitian operator norm is at least the modulus of that entry, giving a negative eigenvalue of \(5I-W^2\).
The accepted semicircle limit gives an eigenvalue of \(W\) in \((-1,1)\) eventually because that interval has positive semicircle mass, providing a positive eigenvalue of the same factor.
Thus eventual indefiniteness is proved under a single fixed finite-variance law, while the corresponding free factor remains strictly positive.
This is a valid example separating the theorem's free sign premise from finite-dimensional sign control; it is not a counterexample to Brown convergence.
