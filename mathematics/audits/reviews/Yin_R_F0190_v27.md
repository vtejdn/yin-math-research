### Conditional influence and the exponential estimate

For a fixed background, the two original-law copies of \(X_i\) give the logarithmic copy estimate divided by \(N^k\).

If \(\|g\|_1=0\), the statistic vanishes and concentration is interpreted directly.

The martingale identity \(H_i=\mathbb E[Z-Z^{(i)}\mid\mathcal F_i]\) is correct because replacing \(X_i\) and integrating the future leaves \(\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\) for the replaced term.

### Integrated cap concentration and the actual defect

The upper-and-lower clipped scalar logarithm has variation \(L_N+5\log N\) for \(N\ge2\).
A grouped replacement changes each input by rank at most two; the four-term polynomial telescoping identity bounds the output rank change by eight.
The difference of its squared-singular matrices then has rank at most sixteen.
The empirical-distribution rank inequality consequently bounds the clipped potential change by \(16(L_N+5\log N)/N\), uniformly in \(z\) and in the matrix values.
Integration over the fixed disk multiplies this bound by its area.
Applying independent-block bounded differences to these \(N\) groups gives the stated integrated cap exponent of order \(N/(L_N+\log N)^2\).

For each input, \(\mathbb E\|W_N\|_{\mathrm F}^2=N-1+\mathbb E\zeta^2\) is the correct normalization.
The operator norm is at most the Frobenius norm, so Markov at \(N^2\) gives probability at most \(CN^{-3}\); a union bound covers both colors.
On the resulting event, \(\|AB+2BA-zI\|\le3N^4+R\), whose square is at most \(N^{10}\) for sufficiently large \(N\) uniformly on the disk.
Thus the upper cap is inactive there, and J_N equals the capped planar statistic minus the actual planar potential integral.
This identity is unaffected by the finite eigenvalue set.

The C186v4 compact-uniform expected squared-positive-log bound controls the removed cap by \(C_R/\log N\), with the factor one half and threshold \(10\log N\) correctly retained.
This is expectation removal and is logically separate from the \(N^{-3}\) probability of cap disagreement.
C186v27 therefore identifies the limiting mean of the capped integral, while C186v26 identifies the same mean limit for U_N(R).
The deterministic difference of these means tends to zero without a required rate.

For that difference bounded in absolute value by epsilon/3, a defect larger than epsilon forces either cap disagreement, an upper deviation of the capped statistic larger than epsilon/3, or a lower deviation of U_N(R) larger than epsilon/3.
The two concentration estimates and the cap-disagreement bound give the claimed three-term probability bound.
Every subpolynomial positive \(L_N\) tending to infinity satisfies \(L_N+\log N\le N^{1/4}\) eventually, so the middle exponential is bounded by an exponential in \(-c\sqrt N\).
All three terms are summable.
This establishes complete convergence of the exact defect, rather than only convergence of its expectation or of an auxiliary statistic.
