## Integral on a narrow strip

With one input changed on an upper row block, the input difference has rank at most two and the product difference is a sum of two matrices of rank at most two.
On the kernel of their sum, the original and perturbed singular maps agree.
Intersecting a low-singular-value subspace with that kernel proves the rank-four bound on singular count changes by min-max in both directions.
The monotone function \(\min\{\tfrac12\log(1+s^2),6\log N\}\) has total variation \(6\log N\), so the normalized statistic changes by at most \(24\log N/N\) under one block change, for all values of those entries.
With \(2N\) independent blocks this gives the fixed-margin deviation \(2\exp[-cN/(\log N)^2]\).
The ordinary bounded-differences inequality and both tails were checked at Theorem 1 on page 2 of [Warnke, arXiv:1212.5796v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.5796v1).
The underlying concentration inequality is used at statement and applicability depth.

On \(\max(\|A\|,\|B\|)\le N^2\), all singular values in the disk are at most \(3N^4+R\), so the cap is inactive for large \(N\).
The complement probability \(O(N^{-3})\) follows from \(\mathbb E\|W\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=O(N)\) and Markov's inequality.
The uncapped statistic is nonnegative and has bounded expectation at each point of a fixed finite mesh by C186v4.
Ordered singular values change by at most the displacement of the shift, and the derivative of the uncapped test function is at most \(1/2\).
Together these facts give one constant \(C_R\) controlling the supremum of \(U_N\) over the disk outside the stated polynomial and exponential exceptions.
The qualitative shortcut in the proof also works by applying the independent second Borel--Cantelli lemma to exceedances of a deterministic constant above the locally uniform limit on that coupling.

Outside the finite eigenvalue set, \(D_N\le U_N-u_N\).
The exact determinant identity writes \(-u_N\) as the average of \(-\log|z-\lambda_j|\), each bounded above by \(\log^-|z-\lambda_j|\), with algebraic multiplicities.
For a set of area \(m\le\pi\), the level sets above \(t\ge0\) of the translated negative logarithm are disks of area \(\pi e^{-2t}\).
Integrating the smaller of that area and \(m\), with crossover \(t=\tfrac12\log(\pi/m)\), gives exactly \(\tfrac m2(1+\log(\pi/m))\), also by continuity at \(m=0\).
This estimate is uniform in every eigenvalue and every measurable choice of the set.
It bounds the full logarithmic loss, including contributions of singular vectors outside the structured class.
