## Obligations and decisive checks

### Comparison laws and weighted expectation limits

The normalized cutoff variables are bounded, centered and variance one for every sufficiently large fixed cutoff, and converge to the original off-diagonal variables in \(L^2\).
The original diagonal contribution has expected squared Frobenius norm of constant order, while a scalar shift contributes order \(N\theta_N^2\).
Summing the normalized off-diagonal differences gives the stated \(CN e_R\) contribution.
Principal deletion cannot increase this Frobenius difference, and \(n=N-|S|\) is comparable to \(N\) uniformly for \(|S|\le2\).

Writing \(R_A:=(A-z)^{-1}\), cyclicity of the finite trace and Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 |\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_a R_A(A-B)R_B|
 \le\|R_B\Pi_aR_A\|_{\rm F}\|A-B\|_{\rm F}
 \le\sqrt n\, (\operatorname{Im}z)^{-2}\|A-B\|_{\rm F}.
\]
Thus the normalized weighted resolvent comparison is valid without commutation of the weight and either matrix, and without a norm bound on the matrices.
Taking expectation before the limits \(N\to\infty\) and then \(R\to\infty\) makes the error vanish.
There are only three deletion sizes, and exchangeability within each size gives the claimed uniform expectation convergence.

The fixed bounded weighted input was checked against the [colored moment argument](../../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#bounded-moments).
Inserting the auxiliary projection changes only the coefficient trace, while the centered index count still leaves exactly the paired colored trees.
The normalization correction \(n/N\to1\), zero comparison diagonals and higher bounded moments satisfy that input's hypotheses.
Moment convergence of the positive expected weighted probability measures gives their expected weak limit because the limiting free measure is compactly supported.
No varying-cutoff moment estimate or almost-sure coupling of cutoff errors is assumed.

### Rank replacement and one-event weak convergence

The raw upper-triangular groups are independent even though entries are mirrored below the diagonal.
Replacing one group changes only one physical block row and column, hence has rank at most four, also after any selected deletion.
The difference of its two resolvents has rank at most four and norm at most \(2/\operatorname{Im}z\).
Consequently its trace norm is at most \(8/\operatorname{Im}z\), and the normalized weighted replacement bound is exactly the stated \(8/(n\operatorname{Im}z)\).
The ordinary bounded-differences theorem requires this bound on the function, not bounded support of the input law.
Applying it to the real and imaginary parts and taking the finite union gives the displayed \(CN^2e^{-cN(\operatorname{Im}z)^2t^2}\) failure probability.

For each fixed point and positive accuracy this is summable in \(N\).
First Borel--Cantelli on a countable dense set of points and countably many accuracies gives simultaneous uniform transform convergence with no independence across sizes.
The transform convention is \((t-z)^{-1}\), so its imaginary part is positive and \(T\operatorname{Im}F(iT)=\int T^2/(t^2+T^2)\,d\nu\).
The stated tail inequality therefore gives uniform tightness from the integer imaginary-axis points.
Any offending subsequence for a bounded continuous test has a further weakly convergent subsequence; the dense-set transform limit and the displayed resolvent Lipschitz estimate identify its transform everywhere.
The Poisson approximate-identity argument then identifies the limit measure.
This is a pathwise implication from the countable transform event, and proves the same-event assertion for every bounded continuous real test without an uncountable probability intersection.

### Positive mass of the fixed free pencil

The [C_F0119_v1 resolvent argument](../../claims/Yin_C_F0119_v1.md#resolvent-existence-and-pointwise-uniqueness) was checked at its noncrossing recursion and strict imaginary-part contraction.
That reasoning applies to the selfadjoint coefficients \(I_2,T,J_\circ\); it is not restricted to the Hermitization shape used elsewhere in that Claim.
The [L_F0066_v5 density argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v5.md#identity-direction-free-density) gives \(V\succeq G^*VG\) from the identity covariance direction, hence the scalar Poisson bound and density bound \(1/\pi\).
Each positive channel measure is dominated by twice the scalar measure, so its density bound is \(2/\pi\).

The transpose and signed-conjugation symmetries of the unique Dyson solution give diagonal entries \(-ia,-ib\) and a real off-diagonal entry.
The direct inversion formulas force \(D_\eta>\sqrt6\), and the trace Poisson bound gives \(a+b\le2\).
A vanishing subsequential diagonal limit would force both to vanish and contradict \(D_0\ge\sqrt6\).
The positive limits therefore satisfy \(D_0=\sqrt6\), \(a_0/b_0=\sqrt{3/2}\) and
\[
 a_0b_0=\frac1{\sqrt6}-\frac1{(\sqrt6+1+\sqrt2)^2}>0.
\]
These determine a unique positive pair.
The density upper bound makes the Poisson contribution outside \([-K\eta,K\eta]\) at most \(4/(\pi K)\); choosing fixed large \(K\) converts the positive Poisson limit to the required linear channel-mass lower bound.
This step does not assume continuity of the densities at zero.
A continuous cutoff supported inside each desired window now transfers that mass through the pathwise weighted weak limit, with a constant independent of the fixed small window.

### Weighted truncation of the actual fresh row

Deleting the physical row and column removes every mirrored occurrence of its off-diagonal variables, so the actual fresh rows are independent of the retained minor.
For complex unit \(u\), the covariance block is \(\operatorname{diag}(u)\Sigma\operatorname{diag}(\overline u)\), and \(\Sigma\succeq\gamma I_2\) because its determinant is \((\sqrt2-1)^2>0\).
Tracing against the positive projector proves the stated lower bound \(H_u\ge\gamma c_0\eta n'\).
The weights are between zero and two, uniformly in the conditioning and direction.

One fixed cutoff has probability and conditional variance at least \(1/2\) for each of the two laws.
For the independent retain indicators, the exponential-product calculation gives
\[
 \mathbb P\Bigl\{\sum I_{aj}w_{aj}<p_*H_u/2\Bigr\}
 \le\exp[-p_*(1-e^{-1}-1/2)H_u/2].
\]
This bound uses the projected weights; it does not require the unweighted number of deleted entries to be small compared with \(\eta N\).

After revealing all retain indicators and discarded values, the remaining variables are independent conditional truncated laws.
Centering them changes only a fixed offset, so the conditional second moment of the projected norm is the sum of its variance contributions plus the squared norm of that offset.
It is therefore at least one half of the retained weighted sum, regardless of the sizes of discarded values.
For the raw linear map, each physical block has squared Frobenius norm \(2|u_1|^2+3|u_2|^2\le3\); the block direct sum and projection give a uniform Lipschitz bound \(\sqrt3\).
The accepted bounded-product concentration estimate applies to this convex norm function with the fixed offset.
Its integrated squared deviation from a median is bounded by a constant depending on the fixed cutoff only.
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality gives the median lower bound of order \(\sqrt{\eta N}\), and the lower tail yields \(Ce^{-c\eta N}\).
Only the eventual dimension depends on the fixed window; the cutoff and positive constants do not.

### Summable nets and physical Schur compression

The raw square sum has expectation of order \(N^2\), so the threshold \(N^4\) has failure probability at most \(CN^{-2}\).
On that single event every required raw border has norm at most \(C_\alpha N^2\).
A mesh proportional to \(\sqrt\eta N^{-3/2}\) on the unit sphere in \(\mathbb C^2\) has at most \(C\eta^{-2}N^6\) points and loses at most half the lower norm bound under extension from the net.
There are exactly \(N+N(N-1)=N^2\) possible pairs \((S,i)\) with \(|S|\le1\).
The finite-direction conditional estimate can be integrated on the local channel-mass event and unioned over these pairs and the deterministic net.
The global channel-mass event is a subset of each required local event.
The norm guard is used only for the deterministic extension and is not added to the conditioning of a fresh row.
This gives \(CN^{-2}+C\eta^{-2}N^8e^{-c\eta N}\), summable for every fixed positive \(\eta\).

Dividing the squared raw-row lower bound by \(N\) gives \(B_i^TPB_i\succeq c_1\eta I_2\) with the original normalization.
The retained resolvent has imaginary part at least \(P/(2\eta)\), so the negative imaginary part of its physical Schur matrix is at least \(c_1I_2/2\).
Its inverse norm is at most \(2/c_1\), independently of the exposed real diagonal values and of \(\eta\).
The exact Schur identity identifies that inverse with the physical resolvent block.
The scalar spectral inequality then gives the claimed physical projector bound.

First Borel--Cantelli combines the summable failures with channel mass holding almost surely eventually.
Intersecting over a decreasing countable set of windows is sufficient for the stated leverage and residual limits.
Every closest eigenvalue is eventually in each such window, while every vector in its eigenspace is controlled by the same projector compression.
This includes zero eigenvalues, arbitrary multiplicity and every choice of real unit eigenvector; simplicity is not used.

### Every removed mode, full inverses and deterministic shifts

On an invertible branch, the mass count gives at least \(chn\) nonzero eigenvalues in each fixed window, and removing any one mode loses at most one of them.
Thus \(\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2\ge(chn-1)/h^2\) uniformly in the removed mode and the minor.
For every desired energy threshold, one first chooses a sufficiently small fixed window and then its eventual dimension.
This establishes uniform divergence of \(\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2/n\), rather than just a lower bound at one fixed window.

Even inside a repeated eigenspace, \(ww^T\) commutes with the actual matrix, so the low/high residual pieces are spectrally orthogonal.
The low physical projector bound gives the full-row estimate \(Ch\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2\), and the high part contributes at most \(2h^{-2}\) per rank-two physical row.
For a physical diagonal block,
\[
 \|P_iE_<P_i\|_{\rm F}^2
 \le\|P_iP_<\|^2\|E_<P_i\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
Summing and using the two-term square inequality gives the stated \(Ch\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2+4nh^{-2}\) bound.
At each fixed splitting window, the already established normalized energy divergence removes the high contribution; then decreasing that window removes the low contribution.
All estimates are uniform in every removed mode, and no bounded operator-norm event or visible-block comparison enters.
The empty-invertible-family conventions in the statement make these uniform assertions well defined.

For a closest mode, \(E_ww=0\) gives exactly
\[
 \|Z\|_{\rm F}^2=q^2+\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2,
 \qquad d_i(Z)=q^2\|P_iw\|^2+d_i(E_w).
\]
Also \(\mathcal D(qww^T)=q^2\sum_i\|P_iw\|^4\le q^2\max_i\|P_iw\|^2\).
These identities, the two-term bound for \(\mathcal D(Z)\), and closest-mode leverage prove both full-inverse spread ratios.
Energy divergence and \(\|Z\|^2\ge\|Z\|_{\rm F}^2/(2n)\) prove the remaining full-inverse conclusions.

At each size the excluded negative eigenvalue atoms form a countable deterministic set, because only finitely many minors and ordered eigenvalue laws occur.
A positive shift below \(N^{-10}\) can be chosen outside it.
A finite union of null events at each size followed by a countable union over sizes gives simultaneous invertibility under any prescribed cross-size coupling.
This shift depends only on marginal laws and is among the deterministic vanishing sequences already covered by the proof.
Only the atom-avoidance subsection of C_F0186_v17 is used; no later least-value or determinant conclusion from that Claim is a premise here.
