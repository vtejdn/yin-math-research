## Complete finite-variance middle finding

The complete C187v11 statement is supported under its explicitly accepted concentration and weak-convergence inputs.
No mathematical gap was found in the new proof or the checked interfaces.
In particular, its polynomial least-value floor remains an additional hypothesis rather than a conclusion of this review.

### Frobenius control and the actual kernel traces

In [the Frobenius calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v11.md#finite-variance-frobenius), the off-diagonal random variable \(H\) is integrable with mean \(2+3=5\).
The \(N(N-1)/2\) independent coordinate pairs at each size give the displayed normalization \(2N^{-2}\sum H_{rs}\to5\) in probability.
Truncation at a fixed level followed by the bounded-variable variance estimate and a Markov bound on the discarded mean uses only integrability.
The diagonal contribution has expectation of order \(N^{-1}\), so \(C_0>5\) gives \(p_N\to0\) without a fourth moment or a relation between different sizes.
Restriction to a principal submatrix decreases the squared Frobenius norms and therefore supplies every minor guard on the one parent event.

For [the kernel construction](../../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v11.md#kernel-graph-trace), the ambient space has dimension \(2(N-1)\) and at most \(2(N-1-k)\) retained spanning rows.
Thus \(\dim\mathcal K\ge2k\), and imposing \(v_T=0\) leaves dimension at least \(k\).
On the selected \(k\)-dimensional subspace, the actual second kernel equation gives \(v=-Au\), with \(A\) equal to \(X[U,V]\) followed by zero extension.
The first-channel projection is injective because \(u=0\) then forces both \(v_U=0\) and \(v_T=0\).
For the isometry \(C\) onto its image, the displayed matrix \(Q\) satisfies \(Q^TQ=I_k\), and its range is precisely the selected graph subspace.
Consequently \(QQ^T\preceq P\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(P\Pi_1)
 \ge\operatorname{Tr}(I_k+C^TA^TAC)^{-1}
 \ge\frac{k^2}{k+\|AC\|_{\rm F}^2}
 \ge\frac{k^2}{k+\|X[V,V]\|_{\rm F}^2}.
\]
The middle inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz for the \(k\) positive eigenvalues of \(I_k+C^TA^TAC\).
Interchanging the channels and imposing \(u_T=0\) gives the stated second-channel bound with \(Y\).
These arguments need no full row rank and permit the two traces to use different graph subspaces.
Since \(k\le N/2\), each denominator is at most \((C_0+1)N\) on the minor guard, giving exactly the \(k^2/N\) energy scale.

### Fixed cutoff and conditional concentration

In [the fresh-row step](../../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v11.md#finite-variance-kernel-row), deleting both physical columns removes all mirrored appearances of the current off-diagonal variables from the other rows.
It also removes the current diagonal variables and the identity coupling from the current block.
Conditioning on the two retained input principal minors therefore fixes \(P\) while leaving the \(2(N-1)\) original raw off-diagonal coordinates independent with their original laws.

The per-coordinate covariance is exactly \(\operatorname{diag}(a_1,a_2)\Sigma\operatorname{diag}(a_1,a_2)\).
Its lower bound by \(\gamma\operatorname{diag}(a_1^2,a_2^2)\), together with the two graph traces, gives \(H_a\ge\gamma c_1 k^2/N\) even when one component of \(a\) vanishes.
The individual weights obey \(0\le w_{bj}\le2\).
The finite-second-moment hypotheses allow one fixed cutoff with both retained probabilities and conditional variances at least one half.
The exponential transform calculation gives the displayed weighted-survival exponent because \(1-e^{-1}-1/2>0\).
It controls the retained projected energy directly and does not require the number of discarded coordinates to be small compared with \(k^2/N\).

After revealing all indicators and discarded values, the retained coordinates remain independent and lie in intervals of diameter at most \(2R\).
Their conditional means and all discarded values form an arbitrary fixed offset.
That offset cannot decrease the conditional second moment below the sum of the projected conditional variances.
The unprojected raw map is a direct sum over physical coordinates of two-column maps with squared Frobenius norm at most \(2a_1^2+3a_2^2\le3\).
Its operator norm, and hence the Lipschitz constant after projection and taking the norm, is at most \(\sqrt3\), uniformly in the dimension.
The accepted concentration inequality (9) therefore applies with fixed constants depending on the cutoff.
Its integrated squared median deviation is bounded independently of \(N,k,a\) and the conditioned minors.
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality gives a median of order \(k/\sqrt N\) once \(k^2/N\) exceeds a fixed threshold.
The lower concentration tail and the weighted-survival failure then yield \(C\exp(-ck^2/N)\), with the uniformity claimed in the candidate.

The [C187v10 calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v10.md#finite-variance-projected-row) was also read to check the stated extraction provenance.
The candidate restates the needed calculation with a kernel projection and energy \(k^2/N\); it does not invoke that source's spectral-window mass hypothesis.

### The common event and all auxiliary directions

In [the net argument](../../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v11.md#finite-variance-kernel-net), the deterministic \(N^{-2}\)-net can be chosen with at most \(CN^4\) points.
On the parent Frobenius event, the raw row block has norm at most \(\sqrt{C_0}N\), so extension from the net costs at most \(\sqrt{C_0}/N\).
For \(k\ge k_N\), this is smaller than half the fixed-direction lower bound eventually.
The full Frobenius event is used only for this deterministic extension and to imply the minor guards.
The conditional fresh-row estimates are integrated on their own minor-measurable guards; no conditioning on the full Frobenius event changes the fresh-coordinate laws.

Deleting columns is contractive, so distance to the projected other-row span is at most the original row distance.
This gives the required lower bound for the full row-distance Gram \(G_i\).
There are at most \(N^2\) pairs \((i,k)\), so the net and pair union has cost at most \(CN^6\exp(-ck_N^2/N)\).
The choice \(k_N:=\lceil A_D\sqrt{N\log N}\rceil\), with \(A_D\) sufficiently large for the prescribed \(D\), reduces this to \(C_DN^{-D}\).
The lower singular-value constant is independent of \(D\); only the cutoff constant and exceptional-probability constant need to vary with \(D\).
Adding \(p_N\) once is sufficient for the simultaneous Gram event.

### Full row rank, Schur identity and exact indices

In [the index calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v11.md#finite-variance-middle-indices), a nonzero row relation would contain a nonzero coefficient vector in one physical block and put that block combination in the span of the other blocks.
The positive Gram lower bound excludes this, so the retained raw matrix has full row rank before its Gram inverse is used.
The block Schur identity is therefore valid and gives
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^{2(N-k)}s_r(\mathsf B)^{-2}
 =\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\operatorname{Tr}G_i^{-1}
 \le C N^2/k^2.
\]
For \(4k_N\le j\le2N-1\) and \(k:=\lfloor j/4\rfloor\), the target index \(2N-j\) is in the singular list of length \(2(N-k)\).
The number of entries from that index to the end, including the endpoint, is exactly \(j-2k+1\ge j/2\).
Also \(k\ge j/8\) eventually and \(k_N\le k\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor\).
Consequently \(s_{2N-j}(\mathsf B)\ge c k\sqrt j/N\).
Row-deletion interlacing and the original raw factor \(\sqrt N\) yield
\[
 s_{2N-j}(L_N)\ge c k\sqrt j/N^{3/2}
 \ge c'(j/N)^{3/2}.
\]
There is no extra dimension-dependent factor, no use of the earlier positive coefficient frame, and no inherited \(N^{3/4}\) cutoff.

### Logarithmic ranges and the separate least-value hypothesis

In [the logarithmic subsection](../../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v11.md#finite-variance-middle-log), increasing index \(r\) corresponds to decreasing index \(2N-r+1\), hence to \(j=r-1\).
The choice \(r_N:=4k_N+2\) is within the established range and gives \(a_r\ge c_5(r/(2N))^{3/2}\) after changing a fixed constant.
If \(a_r<t\), then \(r\le2N(t/c_5)^{2/3}\).
The decreasing logarithmic majorant can be summed by integrating from zero to this upper bound; its integral is finite and bounded by \(Ct^{2/3}(1+|\log t|)\) after normalization.
The estimate is simultaneous for all sufficiently small positive \(t\) on the same event, including cutoffs for which the index set is empty.
No additional additive error or cutoff-dependent probability event is required.

The resulting counting bound has a discarded proportion at most \(r_N/(2N)\to0\).
The accepted C186v4 theorem applies to the exact fixed self-adjoint matrix polynomial
\[
 (x_1,x_2)\longmapsto
 \begin{pmatrix}x_1+x_2&1\\1&x_1+\sqrt2x_2\end{pmatrix}
\]
with normalized trace \(\operatorname{tr}_2\otimes\tau\) and the stated finite-second-moment input laws.
The absolute-value map passes its weak spectral convergence to the singular laws.
At each positive continuity point \(t\), the singular count converges to the limiting mass up to \(t\); all measures are supported on the nonnegative half-line, so a possible zero atom does not obstruct this passage.
The high-probability counting estimate forces that limit to be at most \(Ct^{2/3}\).
Decreasing continuity points establish the bound for all small \(t>0\), and then also show that the zero atom vanishes.
Integrating this power bound proves integrability of the negative logarithm.
The free operator is bounded, so its positive logarithm is integrable and its logarithmic determinant is finite.
This part does not use the additional finite-matrix polynomial floor.

When the separate floor is assumed, the omitted bottom indices contribute at most
\[
 \frac{r_N}{2N}(A\log N+|\log c_*|)
 =O\bigl(N^{-1/2}(\log N)^{3/2}\bigr)\longrightarrow0
\]
to the negative logarithmic tail on its good event.
The common Frobenius event also bounds the average squared singular value by \((C_0+2)/2\).
Thus the upper logarithmic tail tends to zero uniformly as its fixed cutoff tends to infinity, using \(\sup_{x>T}(\log x)/x^2\to0\).
For fixed lower and upper cutoffs the clipped logarithm is bounded and continuous, so weak convergence supplies convergence of its integrals.
Removing the two cutoffs with these estimates proves the claimed full-sequence convergence in probability.
The finite-dimensional identity \(\log|\det L_N|=\sum_r\log a_r\) has the required normalization \(1/(2N)\), with singular matrices confined to the additional floor's vanishing bad event for this deduction.
