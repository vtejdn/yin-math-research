## Finding

The complete argument establishes the exact finite-variance fixed-center joint gap, with no unresolved mathematical gap found at its stated external-input boundary.
The proof covers both full matrices and every single-coordinate principal deletion on one event whose failure probability is at most \(p_{C,N}+p_{T,N}+Ce^{-cN}\), with both common errors tending to zero.
This supports acceptance of the exact C187v12 statement; it does not itself select a Claim assessment or complete a Task.

### Conditional split and shared exceptional events

The decomposition is exact on both kinds of off-diagonal edges: a good edge has \(T_{ij}=m_L/\sqrt N\) and \(W_{ij}=(\xi_{2,ij}-m_L)/\sqrt N\), while a bad edge has \(T_{ij}=\xi_{2,ij}/\sqrt N\) and \(W_{ij}=0\).
All original diagonal entries are in \(T_N\).
The unconditional identity \(\mathbb E\eta_L=0\) follows from centering of \(\xi_2\), and its second moment is exactly the displayed discarded second moment plus \(p_Lm_L^2\).
Finite second moments imply all three cutoff requirements simultaneously for a sufficiently large fixed \(L\).

Conditioning on the cutoff indicators, bad-edge values and diagonals reveals no remaining good-edge values.
The remaining coordinates therefore have the independent conditional truncated laws, zero means after subtracting \(m_L\), variance at least \(1/2\) and support diameter at most \(2L\).
Conditioning additionally on the full independent first input preserves these facts.
In particular, although \(T_N\), the mask and the later projection are dependent, none depends on the unrevealed bounded coordinates after this conditioning.

The off-diagonal Frobenius expression has normalization \(2/N^2\) and converges in probability to \(e_L<\delta/2\).
For clarity, the weak law here needs only the integrable nonnegative law of \(\eta_L^2\): truncate that variable at a fixed height, apply its bounded variance estimate to the \(N(N-1)/2\) independent copies, and bound the discarded average in probability by its mean before sending the height to infinity.
The diagonal term has expectation \(\mathbb E\zeta_2^2/N\), so it vanishes in probability without a fourth moment.
This justifies the single common error \(p_{T,N}\to0\); it does not give or require a summable rate.

For each vertex, the bad-degree moment generating function is bounded by \(\exp((e-1)Nq_L)\), where \(q_L<\delta/(2e)\).
The stated exponential Markov bound and the union over vertices consequently give an exponentially small common mask exception.
Both the degree bound and the Frobenius bound pass to every principal minor without new probability losses.

### Conditional norm and projection geometry

For a fixed real unit vector, replacing one raw good edge changes its quadratic form by at most \(4L|v_iv_j|/\sqrt N\).
The squared bounds sum to at most \(8L^2/N\), since \(\sum_{i<j}v_i^2v_j^2\le1/2\).
Ordinary bounded differences therefore gives precisely the conditional exponent used in the candidate.
The Euclidean \(1/4\)-net has cardinality at most \(9^N\), and comparison of a quadratic form at a maximizing unit vector and its nearest net point gives the factor two in the symmetric operator-norm bound.
A sufficiently large fixed \(B_L\) absorbs this entropy uniformly over every revealed mask and tail realization.
Missing coordinates contribute zero, so no identical-distribution or variance normalization is needed for that estimate.

The spectral projection \(P_0\) discards at most \(\delta N\) directions because each discarded eigenvalue contributes more than one to \(\|T\|_{\rm F}^2\).
Intersecting its range with \((TV+V)^\perp\) loses at most another \(2\dim V\) dimensions.
As an orthogonal subprojection, \(P=PP_0\), whence \(\|PT\|\le1\); commutation of \(P\) with \(T\) is not required.
The same definition gives \(PV=PTV=0\), establishing the center and tail annihilation for every real \(b\) simultaneously.
The projection depends only on quantities already fixed by the conditioning, as required for both probabilistic estimates that follow.

### Uniform projected variance, concentration and the net

The edge-vector covariance identity has diagonal entries \(1-z_i^2\) and off-diagonal entries \(z_iz_j\), so it is exactly \(I+zz^T-2\operatorname{diag}(z_i^2)\).
Taking its trace against \(P\) gives at least \(\operatorname{rank}P-2\).
The discarded-edge energy is bounded by the maximum bad degree, because \(\sum_{\rm bad}\|g_{ij}\|^2=\sum_i\deg_{\rm bad}(i)z_i^2\).
Conditional independence and centering remove every cross term, while each surviving coordinate contributes variance at least \(1/2\).
Thus the lower bound is
\[
 \mathbb E(\|PWz\|^2\mid C_N,\mathcal A_N)
 \ge\frac{n-2\kappa n-2\delta N-2}{2N}\ge\frac14
\]
eventually, uniformly for \(n=N\) or \(N-1\), all admissible subspaces and all real unit vectors.
The retained factor \(N^{-1/2}\), rather than \(n^{-1/2}\), is used correctly throughout.

The map from the remaining raw coordinates to \(PWz\) is linear and has norm at most \(\sqrt{2/N}\), by the Frobenius bound for the symmetric matrix difference.
Taking its Euclidean norm produces a convex function with that Lipschitz constant.
Inequality (9) consequently has scale \(L/\sqrt N\), including the conditional second-moment bound about a median.
A median of this nonnegative function is nonnegative, and the \(L^2\) triangle inequality gives the stated median lower bound from the second-moment lower bound.
The resulting fixed-vector failure probability at \(\gamma=1/4\) is \(Ce^{-c_LN}\).
No lower bound on a coordinate density or small-ball regularity has entered this step.

The net is chosen in \(V\), which is fixed after conditioning on the first input, and the preceding estimate is uniform in the subsequently fixed tail-adapted projection.
Its entropy is at most \(\kappa n\log(3/\theta)\), dominated by the chosen exponential rate.
On the conditional norm event, the net approximation costs at most \(B_L\theta\le\gamma/8\), leaving the asserted weaker bound \(\gamma/2\).
For the zero subspace the infimum convention is harmless, and the final approximation argument cannot select that subspace when its first residual is small.
The ambient compressed transfer estimate uses \(\|PT\|+\|PW\|+|b|\le1+B_L+|b|\), so it does not assume a bounded norm for the heavy-tailed original input.

### Spectral windows, quantifiers and the final transfer

The scalar specialization of C186v4 applies to the exact first-input law and independent diagonal law stated here.
Its proof uses fixed entry truncations for the bounded-moment calculation and a finite-second-moment comparison before row-block concentration; it imports no higher untruncated moment hypothesis.
The earlier independent finding covers that same interface.
Weak convergence implies convergence of the counts in each fixed interval because the semicircle law assigns zero mass to every endpoint.
Its bounded density permits the prescribed finite grid with limiting window mass at most \(\kappa/4\), leaving room for the event threshold \(\kappa N/2\).
Thus \(p_{C,N}\to0\), with no rate required.

Principal interlacing changes each interval count by at most two, which is absorbed by the fixed positive dimension margin for all \(N-1\) minors at once.
Conditional on the full first input and the revealed tail data, there are only \(J(N+1)\) selected subspaces.
Their uniform exponential conditional estimates survive this union.
The events defining \(p_{C,N}\) and \(p_{T,N}\) are imposed before that union and are each added only once after integration.
Neither their independence nor independence across different minors is needed.

If the first residual is at most \(\epsilon\) and \(|a-a_j|\le h\), every eigenvalue outside the selected window is at distance greater than \(h\) from \(a\).
Spectral calculus therefore gives \(\|(I-Q)z\|\le\epsilon/h\).
For \(\epsilon<h/4\), normalization of \(Qz\) is legitimate and changes \(z\) by at most \(2\epsilon/h\).
The compressed transfer estimate then bounds the second projected residual by
\[
 \epsilon+2(1+B_L+R_0)\epsilon/h.
\]
The final fixed choice of \(\epsilon_{R_0}>0\) makes this strictly smaller than the verified lower bound \(\gamma/2\), a contradiction.
Every choice of center and unit vector is covered by this deterministic implication; a net for \(b\) is unnecessary.
The stated parameter order avoids any dependence of the cutoff, subspace fraction, grid or final gap on \(N\).
