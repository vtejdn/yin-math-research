## Finding

The complete proof supports both stated conclusions under the exact real finite-second-moment hypotheses.
No unresolved mathematical gap was found in the new arguments or their dependency interfaces.
In particular, the compressed joint gap and the exclusion of component collapse together justify a fixed positive auxiliary Gram bound for all unit eigenvectors in each fixed energy range.
The result is uniform over every full or single-deletion input pair, with the stated common \(o(1)\) error and an exponentially small remainder.
This finding supplies review evidence for the exact statement without selecting a Claim assessment or completing any wider Task.

### Conditional estimates and the dimension budget

The reused decomposition exposes every bad value and all diagonals before the bounded coordinates are sampled.
Conditional centering, variance at least \(1/2\), support diameter at most \(2L\) and independence remain valid after conditioning on the independent first input.
The Frobenius law needs only the finite mean of the squared raw tail; any fixed \(C_T\) strictly above its limiting density suffices.
The diagonal contribution tends to zero in probability by its finite second moment.
The full Frobenius event and full mask-degree event are inherited by every principal minor.

The conditional quadratic-form replacement sum \(8L^2/N\), ordinary bounded differences and a \(1/4\)-net give the stated fixed \(B_L\) uniformly in the revealed data.
The use of missing coordinates is harmless because their replacement bounds are zero.
For any projection fixed by the conditioning, the raw covariance identity and bad-degree subtraction give
\[
 \mathbb E(\|PWz\|^2\mid\text{data})
 \ge\frac{n-\operatorname{codim}P-2-\delta N}{2N}.
\]
Every projection later used has codimension at most \(7\kappa n/4\le7n/256\), since \(\kappa\le1/64\).
With \(\delta=1/32\) and \(n=N\) or \(N-1\), this lower bound is strictly above \(1/4\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Thus the initially unspecified small-codimension condition is satisfied by the actual numerical choices with a uniform margin.

Convexity and the Euclidean Lipschitz constant \(\sqrt{2/N}\) give median error of order \(N^{-1/2}\) with constants depending on the fixed \(L\).
The second-moment lower bound therefore yields the fixed-vector threshold \(\gamma=1/4\) and exponent \(c_LN\).
The choice of \(\kappa\) is made after these constants are fixed.
For all later subspaces, \(\dim V\le\kappa n\), so the entropy is at most \(\kappa n\log(3/\theta)\).
The stated inequality for that entropy and \(B_L\theta\le\gamma/8\) leave the uniform subspace bound \(\gamma/2\).
Conditioned subspaces may depend on the revealed tail as well as the first input; they remain deterministic for the remaining product law, which is all this argument requires.

### Compression of both inputs

For fixed \(K>2\), the first-input limiting semicircle has no mass outside \([-K,K]\).
Weak convergence and interlacing consequently allow the prescribed small high-energy count for all minors on one event of probability tending to one.
The tail Frobenius guard bounds the number of tail eigenvalues outside \([-H,H]\) by \(C_TN/H^2\).
The intersection defining \(Q\) therefore loses at most \(\kappa N/8\) dimensions, which is at most \(\kappa n/4\) eventually for both allowed minor sizes.

Although the two spectral projections need not commute, containment of \(\operatorname{Ran}Q\) gives \(Q=Q\mathbf1_{[-K,K]}(C)\) and \(Q=Q\mathbf1_{[-H,H]}(T)\).
These identities prove \(\|QC\|\le K\) and \(\|QT\|\le H\).
The bounds on \(QD,QX,QY\) then follow from the conditional \(W_N\) norm event, which bounds every minor.
No bound on the uncompressed original inputs is assumed.
Choosing the separate fixed \(H\) after the entropy budget is established is valid for every finite \(C_T\); no unproved comparison between a tail-decay rate and \(\kappa\) is needed.

### Excluding either small component

The space \(V_0=\ker Q\) has dimension \(q\le\kappa n/4\).
Intersecting \(\operatorname{Ran}Q\) with the orthogonal complement of \(V_0+CV_0+TV_0\) gives codimension at most \(4q\le\kappa n\).
The projection thus kills the scalar term and the \(C,T\) terms on \(V_0\).
The two restrictions become exactly \(PW\) and \(\alpha PW\), respectively.
The conditional subspace estimate applies without any independence assumption on the subsequently selected eigenmode.

If \(\|u\|\le b\), the first block equation gives \(\|Qv\|\le A_0b\).
Since \(\|v\|\ge1/2\) for \(b\le1/2\), the normalized \(v\) has distance at most \(2A_0b\) from \(V_0\).
The assumed smallness of \(b\) makes its projection nonzero, and normalization changes the vector by at most \(4A_0b\).
The second block equation gives projected residual at most \(2b\) before this replacement.
Because \(P\) is a subprojection of \(Q\), its ambient transfer norm is at most \(A_0\), leaving the upper bound \(2b+4A_0^2b\).
The lower bound is \(\alpha\gamma/2\).
Interchanging the components instead gives lower bound \(\gamma/2\), which is the smaller one because \(\alpha=\sqrt2>1\).
The stated \(b_*\) makes the upper bound at most \(\gamma/8\), so it works for both components with a strict margin.
If \(V_0\) is zero, the distance estimate itself is impossible for that choice of \(b\).
Thus no zero-subspace or zero-component case is omitted.

### Compressed spectral approximation and joint gap

The small windows have length \(2h\) and limiting mass at most \(\kappa/8\).
For every fixed finite grid, weak convergence at their continuity endpoints and interlacing give dimension at most \(\kappa n/2\) simultaneously for all selected minors.
Adding \(A_j(\ker Q)\) costs at most \(q\), so \(\dim V_j\le3\kappa n/4\).
The inverse notation is explicitly a bounded spectral function and remains defined when \(a_j\) is an eigenvalue.

The key decomposition is exact because \(A_j(C-a_jI)=E_j\) and \(I=(I-Q)+Q\).
It uses these identities in the written order and does not commute \(Q\) through \(C\).
The term \(A_j(I-Q)(C-a_jI)z\) belongs to \(A_j(\ker Q)\), so the only approximation error is \(A_jQ(C-a_jI)z\), of norm at most the compressed residual divided by \(h\).
Moving a center by at most \(\epsilon\) increases that residual by at most \(\epsilon\).
The normalized projection estimate \(4\epsilon/h\) follows once \(\epsilon<h/8\).

The new projection \(P_j\) has codimension at most \(q+2\dim V_j\le7\kappa n/4\), within the verified variance budget.
It is a subprojection of \(Q\), kills \(V_j\) and \(TV_j\), and satisfies \(P_j(D-bI)|_{V_j}=P_jW|_{V_j}\).
Its ambient transfer norm is bounded by \(H+B_L+|b|\).
The original compressed second residual controls the \(P_j\) residual since \(P_j=P_jQ\).
Therefore the claimed upper bound
\[
 \epsilon+4(H+B_L+R_*)\epsilon/h
\]
contradicts the uniform lower bound \(\gamma/2\) for a sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon\).
This choice precedes fixing the finite grid, so the grid's possibly large but fixed cardinality creates no circular parameter dependence.
The bound holds for all \(b\) in the selected interval because the projection annihilates the scalar term on \(V_j\); no second-center net is needed.

### Gram conversion and full eigenvector scope

The component bounds ensure that \(t=\langle u,v\rangle/\|v\|^2\) is well defined.
Orthogonality of \(e=u-tv\) to \(v\) gives \(\|e\|^2=\Delta/\|v\|^2\).
When \(\Delta\le b_*^4/4\), one has \(\|e\|\le b_*/2\) and
\[
 |t|\|v\|=\sqrt{\|u\|^2-\|e\|^2}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt3}{2}b_*.
\]
Together with \(\|v\|\le1\) and \(\|v\|\ge b_*\), this justifies the candidate's bounds \(b_*/2\le|t|\le1/b_*\).
Negative \(t\) is allowed, while zero \(t\) is excluded precisely in this small-determinant case.

Substituting \(u=tv+e\) in the two block equations gives exactly the displayed residual identities with \(x_0=\lambda-t^{-1}\) and \(y_0=\lambda-t\).
After left compression, the first residual is bounded using \(\|QX\|\), not \(\|X\|\); its bound is \(2A_0\|e\|/b_*\).
The second residual is bounded by \(\|e\|\).
The inverse linear transformation from \(X,Y\) to \(C,D\) is valid because \(\alpha-1\ne0\).
Both resulting scalar centers lie in a fixed bounded interval: \(|x_0|\le K_0+2/b_*\) and \(|y_0|\le K_0+1/b_*\) already suffice.
Division by \(\|v\|\ge b_*\) and \(\|e\|\le\sqrt\Delta/b_*\) gives a fixed \(C_*\sqrt\Delta\) bound for both compressed residuals.

The center range is therefore fixed before the window width, residual tolerance and grid are selected.
The compressed joint gap excludes every sufficiently small \(\Delta\), including zero.
A threshold smaller than both \(b_*^4/4\) and the resulting positive residual threshold supplies a uniform positive determinant bound.
For a positive semidefinite two-by-two Gram matrix with trace one, its larger eigenvalue is at most one, so \(\lambda_{\min}\ge\det=\Delta\).
This proves the desired least-eigenvalue bound.
The deterministic implications hold for every unit eigenvector on the same good event, including all vectors in a repeated eigenspace.
No eigenvalue spacing, eigenvector selection, matrix invertibility or net over eigenmodes is required.

### Shared errors and scope

The tail Frobenius failure and the first-input high-energy and finite-window count failures form one \(p_N=o(1)\).
They are imposed as common full-input events before the union over minors.
All other failures are exponential conditional estimates or the common exponential mask exception.
There are a fixed number of selected subspaces per minor and \(N+1\) minors, so their union remains \(Ce^{-cN}\) after adjusting constants.
This reasoning requires no independence among minors or among the common events.
Arbitrary coupling across sizes is harmless because the asserted conclusions concern probabilities tending to one.

The proof separately fixes the bounded-coordinate constants, dimension budget, tail spectral threshold and bounded first-input threshold before component separation and the final window choices.
The order of the independent choices \(K>2\) and \(H\) has no effect on any dependence.
For each prescribed finite \(K_0\) or \(R_*\), every constant is fixed before \(N\) tends to infinity.
The case \(K_0=0\) and absence of eigenmodes in the selected range introduce no exception.
