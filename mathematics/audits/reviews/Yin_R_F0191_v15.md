### Signed comparable weights: exact zero and compactness

The determinant hypothesis and the isometry imply
\[
 \|(aU+bV)h\|^2\ge g(a^2+b^2)\|h\|^2.
\]
Thus \(U\) has closed range, is bounded below, and has surjective adjoint.
For injective selfadjoint \(A\) of rank at least two, \(G=UAU^*\) has rank at least two.
The finite quadratic-product lemma therefore applies on a fixed finite subspace of rank at least two and on each of its finite enlargements.
Uniqueness of the scalar on that first subspace gives one real \(c\), and polarization yields \(S=cG\) and \(J=c^2G\) on the whole limit Hilbert space.
This verifies the infinite-dimensional use without an unproved global factorization assertion.

For \(x\perp\operatorname{ran}U\), the identity \(H+H^*=2cG\) gives \(Hx=0\), and injectivity of \(U,A\) gives \(V^*x=0\).
Closedness of \(\operatorname{ran}U\) then justifies \(\operatorname{ran}V\subset\operatorname{ran}U\) and the bounded factorization \(V=UB\).
Canceling \(U,U^*\) gives
\[
 AB^*+BA=2cA,\qquad BAB^*=c^2A.
\]
For \(D:=B-cI\), these imply \(DAD^*=0\).
The whole-subspace inequality gives \(\|Dh\|\ge\sqrt{g(1+c^2)}\|h\|\), so \(D\) is injective.
Consequently \(DAD^*=0\) implies \(AD^*=0\), and injectivity of \(A\) implies \(D^*=0\), a contradiction.
No bounded inverse of \(A\), positive sign of its weights or selfadjointness of \(D\) is needed.

In the compactness argument, the ordered eigenvalue bound gives the uniform operator-norm tail
\[
 \|A_m-A_m^{(J)}\|\le\sqrt{K/(J+1)}.
\]
The proof correctly refrains from asserting a uniform Frobenius tail.
Diagonal subsequences of the countably many eigenvalues and Gram entries produce the limit isometry and transfer the whole-subspace determinant inequality first to finitely supported vectors and then by continuity to all vectors.
The two largest absolute eigenvalues survive with moduli \(1\) and at least \(d\), while finite partial sums give squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm at most \(K\).

Each block and its spectral truncation has operator norm at most one.
Telescoping the two quadratic products therefore bounds the quartic truncation error by \(4\sqrt{K/(J+1)}\|x\|^4\).
For a fixed finite combination of Gram labels, its norm and truncated quadratic forms converge.
The assumed vanishing sphere supremum then passes to the limit after sending \(J\) to infinity, yielding the zero polynomial on the dense label span and hence everywhere.
Restricting the weight space to labels with nonzero limiting eigenvalue preserves the blocks and whole-subspace geometry and retains two eigenvalues.
The resulting injective weight contradicts the exact zero classification.
