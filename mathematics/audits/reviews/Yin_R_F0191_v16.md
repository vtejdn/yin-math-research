## Statement obligations and verification

The obligations are exact nonvanishing under support injectivity in arbitrary real Hilbert spaces, and a strictly positive lower bound uniform over both finite dimensions at fixed \(p,g,K,d\).
The quantitative assertion additionally requires the isometric stack, a Gram lower bound on every domain vector, bounded Frobenius energy relative to operator norm, and a nonvanishing \(p\)-th singular value.
None of these quantitative hypotheses is silently used in the first assertion.
The conclusion concerns the raw determinant polynomial, so a separate obligation is to preserve its distinction from coordinate coefficient or probabilistic estimates.

### Real transitivity and the infinite-dimensional support reduction

For a real transitive square matrix space, maximal rank is an attained integer.
Invertible left and right coordinate changes preserve transitivity.
After reducing a maximal-rank element to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)\), the Schur complement for small real \(t\) gives \(T_{22}=0\) for every element of the space.
A nonzero vector in the last \(p-r\) coordinates then has all its images in the first \(r\) coordinates, contradicting transitivity.
The argument covers \(r=0\) separately and does not require complex scalars or an algebraically closed field.

For bounded selfadjoint \(A\), the space \((\ker A)^\perp=\overline{\operatorname{ran}A}\) is reducing and its restriction of \(A\) is injective.
Restricting the \(U_i\) to that space replaces their adjoints by the corresponding orthogonal projections and leaves \(B_A\) unchanged.
Injectivity of every nonzero channel combination survives this restriction.
No closed-range assertion, bounded inverse, compactness or spectral basis for this general \(A\) is used.
The dimension assumption supplies a finite \(p\)-dimensional subspace even when the ambient Hilbert spaces are infinite-dimensional or nonseparable.
For each nonzero vector in this subspace, injectivity of \(U(a)\) makes its \(p\) channel images linearly independent.
The inner-product map onto their coordinates is therefore surjective, giving exactly the square transitivity required above and a point where \(S(x)\) has row rank \(p\).

### Simultaneous ranks and arbitrary signs

A nonzero maximal-rank minor of \(B_A\) and a nonzero determinant of \(S|_{\mathcal H_0}\) can be made nonzero simultaneously.
On the line joining their witnessing points, each remains a nonzero one-variable polynomial because it is nonzero at its own endpoint.
Avoiding their finitely many roots gives the stated \(x_0\); the case of maximal rank zero needs only the full-row-rank point of \(S\).
This step does not invoke finite-dimensional compactness or an unsupported open-dense intersection in the ambient Hilbert space.

At a maximal-rank symmetric \(B_A(x_0)\), restriction to its range is invertible even for an indefinite matrix.
For each direction \(y\), the zero Schur complement of \(B_A(x_0+ty)\) has zero first derivative on the kernel block, since the off-diagonal blocks vanish at \(t=0\).
For nonzero \(a\) in that kernel, differentiation gives precisely
\[
 0=2\langle U(a)A S(x_0)^*a,y\rangle
 \quad\text{for every }y.
\]
Injectivity of \(U(a)\), then of the restricted \(A\), contradicts injectivity of \(S(x_0)^*\).
The rank-zero case has the same consequence directly.
Thus every required step works with both signs of the weight and without inverting \(A\).
The argument also covers \(p=1\).

### Dimension-independent quantitative passage

Failure of a positive infimum gives admissible tuples with unit-sphere suprema tending to zero.
Restricting to each nonzero spectral support preserves the stack, the whole-subspace Gram hypothesis and the polynomial.
Passing to a subsequence with fixed support dimension or support dimension tending to infinity exhausts the possibilities.
The bounded signed eigenvalue labels and all pairwise channel-label Gram entries admit a common diagonal subsequence.
Their positive semidefinite Gram limit defines the required real Hilbert space after quotient and completion.

The stack identities first give bounded operators on finitely supported domain vectors and then on the completed domain.
The Gram lower bound passes on each such vector and extends by continuity to every domain vector.
Partial sums of the squared eigenvalues give \(\sum_j\lambda_j^2\le K\), while the first and \(p\)-th absolute eigenvalue limits retain \(1\) and at least \(d\), respectively.
Thus signs and possible disappearing later eigenvalues do not destroy the rank needed for the final exact theorem.

Ordering absolute eigenvalues gives the uniform tail bound \(\sqrt{K/(J+1)}\) in operator norm, both before and after the limit.
No uniformly small Frobenius tail is needed or asserted.
Each determinant entry is bounded by \(\|x\|^2\), and each entry changes by at most \(t_J\|x\|^2\) after truncation.
The permutation expansion with a telescoping product difference gives the stated constant \(p\,p!\) and degree \(2p\).
For any finite combination of limiting labels, identical coefficients define sequence vectors with convergent, hence bounded, norms and convergent fixed-truncation entries.
Homogeneity makes the untruncated determinants tend to zero on these sequence vectors.
The two uniform tail bounds then allow the finite-label limit followed by \(J\to\infty\); density and continuity extend the zero determinant to the entire limiting space.
In the fixed-support case the tails vanish once all labels are kept.

The limiting Gram inequality gives \(\|U(a)h\|^2\ge g\|a\|^2\|h\|^2\), so every nonzero channel combination is injective.
The limiting weight has support dimension at least \(p\), contradicting the exact theorem already checked.
This proves a positive existence constant depending only on \(p,g,K,d\), independent of both finite dimensions and all eigenvalue signs.
The empty admissible class is correctly treated as vacuous.
For a nonzero unnormalized weight, division by \(\|A\|\) preserves the normalized hypotheses, and the determinant scales by \(\|A\|^p\), as stated.
