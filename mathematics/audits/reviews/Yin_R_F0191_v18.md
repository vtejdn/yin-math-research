## Finding on the complete deterministic theorem

No unresolved mathematical gap was found in the complete deterministic statement and proof of C187v18.
The finding supports its stated conditional conclusions at fixed channel number and fixed separation parameters, with constants independent of the physical dimension.
The decisive checks are given below.

### Symmetric-space nonvanishing and quantitative compactness

The maximal-rank step requires symmetry of the matrix space, not positivity of a maximal-rank element.
An orthogonal auxiliary change of basis puts that element into an invertible symmetric block and a zero block.
For every other element, rank maximality makes the Schur complement of its small affine perturbation zero.
Its first-order term forces the entire kernel-to-kernel block to vanish.
Thus every vector in that nonzero kernel has zero quadratic pairing with every element of the matrix space, contradicting the specified separation.
The rank-zero case is explicitly excluded by the same separation assumption.
This proves the nonvanishing of the determinant polynomial without an unproved transitivity implication.

After division by \(F\), the Gram matrices of the \(q:=p(p+1)/2\) unordered-pair vectors lie in a compact finite-dimensional positive semidefinite set.
The lower inequality for every auxiliary vector is closed, and it survives a convergent Gram sequence.
The reduced determinant tensor's squared norm is a continuous polynomial in those Gram entries.
A zero limiting norm gives a zero symmetric tensor, hence a determinant polynomial that vanishes identically, contradicting the preceding argument.
Consequently the positive lower constant depends only on \(p,g\), even when the original operator-space dimension varies.
An empty feasible set causes no problem because the statement explicitly allows a vacuous implication.

### Contraction factor and high-rank error

The arithmetic-average convention gives one average over \((2p)!\) slot permutations in the inner product of two symmetrized products.
Every resulting bipartite contraction graph has degree two at every matrix vertex.
The terms consisting entirely of double edges have \(p!\) vertex matchings and \(2^p\) slot matchings per vertex matching.
Their determinant sum is therefore exactly
\[
 b_p\|\mathscr D\|_F^2,\qquad b_p:=\frac{2^p p!}{(2p)!}.
\]
This factor agrees with the separate order-\(p\) arithmetic symmetrization in the reduced tensor.

A longer cycle containing \(r\ge4\) matrix factors has contraction bounded in absolute value by \(Q^{r-2}F^2\), using two Frobenius factors and operator norms for the others.
The remaining cycles, together with \(Q\le F\), leave each non-double-edge term bounded by \(Q^2F^{2p-2}\).
The average of the slot weights is at most one, and the two determinant expansions contribute \((p!)^2\) terms.
Thus the displayed comparison error in C187v18 has the stated constant and sign-independent bound.
Combining it with the reduced-tensor lower bound gives the claimed choice of \(R\) and a positive \(c\).
For \(p=1\), the longer-cycle contribution is empty and the same conclusion remains valid.

### Signed channel-map corollary

The identity \(\sum_iU_i^*U_i=I\) gives \(\|U_i\|\le1\), so the required Frobenius and operator upper bounds on every symmetrized \(K_{ij}\) hold with \(F=\|A\|_F\) and \(Q=\|A\|\).
The channel Gram condition gives \(U(a)^*U(a)\succeq g\|a\|^2I\).
For nonzero \(a\), its positive square root is invertible, and the two-sided sandwich inequality gives
\[
 \|U(a)AU(a)^*\|_F
 =\|(U(a)^*U(a))^{1/2}A(U(a)^*U(a))^{1/2}\|_F
 \ge g\|a\|^2\|A\|_F.
\]
This uses no positivity of \(A\), and it supplies exactly the quadratic separation required by the first assertion.
The conclusion concerns the Frobenius norm of the coefficient tensor; it does not convert that norm into a dimension-independent unit-sphere value.

### Local factors and physical collisions

The physical matrices \(K_{ab}\) are symmetric both in the auxiliary labels and as operators on the \(2n\) row variables.
Their quadratic forms are exactly the entries of \(R(z)^TZR(z)\).
For a fixed physical position, the row norm of \(V_a^TZ_{ab}V_b\) uses the corresponding row of \(Z_{ab}\), while its transpose uses the row of \(Z_{ba}\).
Both are included in \(d_i=\|P_iZ\|_F^2\).
The diagonal two-by-two block uses the single scalar \((Z_{ab})_{ii}\), whose squared sum is bounded by the physical diagonal-block energy \(\mathcal D\).
These facts verify all four local-factor estimates with the stated powers of \(\Lambda\).
The substitutions \(F=\Lambda^2F_Z\), \(Q=\Lambda^2Q_Z\), and \(g=\eta/\Lambda^2\) therefore preserve every hypothesis of the abstract bound.

For two slots of the same quadratic factor, collision contributes at most \(\Lambda^{4p}\mathcal D F_Z^{2p-2}\).
For slots from two different factors, the contribution is bounded by
\[
 \Lambda^{4p}F_Z^{2p-4}\sum_i d_i^2
 \le \Lambda^{4p}d_*F_Z^{2p-2},
\]
because \(\sum_i d_i=F_Z^2\).
The latter case occurs only when \(p\ge2\), so it introduces no invalid negative-power argument at \(p=1\).
The collision projection is invariant under slot permutations and hence commutes with full symmetrization.
The union bound over slot pairs and the finite determinant sum give precisely a constant depending only on \(p,t\).
Choosing the spread tolerance after the raw tensor lower constant leaves at least half that lower energy on physically distinct tuples.

### Complete Walsh layer and loads

The determinant is homogeneous of degree \(2p\) in the separate variables \(x_i,y_i\).
A monomial with a repeated individual variable has Walsh degree strictly less than \(2p\) after sign substitution.
Consequently each complete degree-\(2p\) coefficient on distinct individual variables is exactly \((2p)!\mathscr T_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_{2p}}/N^p\).
There are \((2p)!\) ordered representations of each such set, giving the energy identity with factor \((2p)!/N^{2p}\).
Restricting additionally to distinct physical positions gives the claimed lower bound without merging the two independent variables at one position.

For the load estimate, every one-slot restriction of every unsymmetrized product is bounded using the corresponding local-factor row estimate.
Jensen's inequality remains valid after that restriction and after slot permutations.
The determinant sum contributes at most \((p!)^2\), and the union over \(2p\) slots controls all tuples meeting the physical group.
Restriction to distinct individual variables and the same factorial count then bound the complete top-layer load, including sets that contain both \(x_i\) and \(y_i\).
The complete-layer energy is at least its physically distinct subenergy, so the normalized maximum load is at most a fixed multiple of \(d_*/F_Z^2\) once the stated spread tolerance holds.
This proves the sequence conclusion at the given fixed high-rank threshold; no limit \(F_Z/Q_Z\to\infty\) is additionally required.
