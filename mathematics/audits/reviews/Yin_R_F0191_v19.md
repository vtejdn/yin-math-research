## Quadratic endpoint and its uniformity

The three residual equations are exactly the three columns of \(CV+DVT+VK-\lambda V\).
Under the first-column hypothesis, their compressed use gives \(\|u_2-z\|\le Bb+\rho\), then the full-vector approximation to \(u_3\), and finally
\[
 \|\Psi_\lambda(z,w)\|
 \le(B^2+B+2)\rho+(B^3+2B)b.
\]
Every error is acted on by a left-compressed operator whose norm is bounded by \(B\).
The rank-one approximation retaining only the third column gives the stated positive lower bound for \(\|z\|\); no bound for an uncompressed original matrix enters this reduction.

For a fixed nonzero \(z\), the three-block partition always has an \(I\)-block with \(\|z_I\|\ge\|z\|/\sqrt3\).
The two disjoint unordered edge families and the remaining edges are conditionally independent, and taking two independent copies of each preserves independence of all raw difference coordinates.
Each retained difference coordinate has variance at least one and support diameter at most \(4L\).
The projections onto the coordinate blocks intersected with \(\operatorname{Ran}Q\) lose at most \(q\) dimensions, remain fixed under the conditional law, and satisfy \(\Pi_JQ=\Pi_J\) and \(\Pi_KQ=\Pi_K\).

Expansion of the four-corner difference cancels its constant and linear terms.
The quadratic part is
\[
 -\alpha\Pi_KQ(\Delta A Q\Delta B+\Delta B Q\Delta A)z
 =-\alpha\Pi_K\Delta B Q\Delta A z,
\]
because \(\Pi_K\Delta A=0\).
This equality removes the background, energy, \(w\), and the common remaining edges exactly.
Writing \(v=(Q\Delta A z)_J\), one has \(\Pi_Jv=\Pi_J\Delta A_{JI}z_I\).
The first rectangular covariance lower bound is \((\operatorname{rank}\Pi_J-N/32)\|z_I\|^2/N\ge\|z_I\|^2/4\), eventually, and the analogous bound holds in the second rectangle.
The claimed Lipschitz constant \(\|a\|/\sqrt N\) follows from the rectangular Frobenius bound, without the extra symmetric-copy factor needed for a full symmetric matrix.
Integration of concentration gives the displayed median lower bound and a scale-invariant exponential lower tail at \(a_*=1/8\).
After the first rectangle, conditioning on \(\Delta A\) fixes \(v\) and leaves the second rectangle independent, so the two lower tails yield the asserted positive product threshold.
This reasoning uses no favorable norm event for any copy.

The four-cycle probability inequality has the required direction.
Indeed its right side equals
\[
 \mathbb E_{A_0,A_1}
 \bigl(\mathbb E_B f(A_0,B)f(A_1,B)\bigr)^2
 \ge\bigl(\mathbb E_B(\mathbb E_Af(A,B))^2\bigr)^2
 \ge p(U)^4.
\]
If all four indicators are one, the projected alternating difference has norm at most \(4t\).
The stated \(c_0=\alpha a_*^2/(8\sqrt3)\) places \(4c_0\|z\|\) strictly below the established product threshold.
The exponential bound is uniform in the remaining edges, and Jensen's inequality followed by a fourth root gives the claimed original-vector lower tail with a reduced positive exponential rate.

The parameter order is consistent.
The cutoff, bounded remainder norm, tail density constant and fixed-parameter exponential rate are chosen before the dimension budget.
The threshold \(H(\kappa)\) leaves at most \(\kappa N/16\) tail eigenvalues outside its window, and the scalar weak law gives the corresponding first-input count on one common event.
The intersection projection has \(q\le\kappa n/4\), and its left-compressed bounds hold despite noncommutation of the two spectral projections.
Moreover \(B(\kappa)=O(1+\kappa^{-1/2})\), so the two vector nets have logarithmic cardinality at most \((\kappa n/2)\log(1+2/\delta(\kappa))\), whose coefficient tends to zero with \(\kappa\).
The energy grid has a fixed size after this choice and contributes no exponential-in-\(N\) entropy.

For \(z,w\in\ker Q\), direct subtraction gives
\[
 \Psi_\lambda(z,w)-\Psi_\mu(z,w)
 = (\lambda-\mu)QB_\mu z,
\]
so the energy approximation requires no uncompressed bound.
The stated two vector meshes and energy mesh produce error at most \(c_0d/16\), while annular net centers retain norm at least \(d/2\).
The strict contradiction at the net points and the entropy choice therefore prove the uniform quadratic gap.
The displayed \(\eta_1\) then gives \(\|z\|>d/2\) and residual at most \(c_0d/8\), contradicting that gap with margin.
If the annulus is empty, its required positive-norm vector is already impossible.

## Remaining columns, Gram geometry and final quantifiers

The spectral-window spaces use a deterministic finite grid chosen after their required mesh is fixed.
The scalar weak law and principal interlacing control the entire grid and every deletion through one full first-input event.
The spectral inverse is zero on the small window, so no invertibility is assumed.
The decomposition into the low-window component, \(A_j(I-Q)(C-a_jI)u\), and \(A_jQ(C-a_jI)u\) is exact and never commutes \(Q\) with \(C\).

The dimensions and annihilations in both column tests match the linear noise estimate.
For the third-column test, \(\dim E_j\le3\kappa n/4\) and \(\operatorname{codim}P_j^{(3)}\le7\kappa n/4\); the projection removes the fixed background on \(V_0\) and the approximated first column.
For the second-column test, \(\dim F_j\le\kappa n\) and \(\operatorname{codim}P_j^{(2)}\le13\kappa n/4\); the projection removes the background on \(F_j\).
The residual equations give precisely the two displayed upper bounds, and the rank-one approximations give the required lower bounds for the tested vectors.
The common \(\eta_2\) bounds make these vector norms strictly larger than \(d/2\) and the upper bounds at most half the resulting linear-noise lower bounds.
Thus all three column exclusions hold with fixed positive constants, including the zero-tested-space case.

The deterministic Gram conversion also holds for rank-two component matrices.
For \(q_G<d/2\), the minimizing vector and its image under \(T\) are within \(q_G/d\) of the smallest right-singular direction, and their common direction consequently has a small \(T\)-variance.
The exact diagonal variance identity, together with the separation \(\sqrt2-1\) of distinct diagonal values of \(T\), forces this direction close to a coordinate axis.
The corresponding physical column then has norm at most \(13q_G/d\), contradicting its lower bound unless \(q_G\ge dm/13\).
The complementary case \(q_G\ge d/2\) gives the other term of the stated minimum.
No positive lower bound on \(s_3(V)\) or on the single-input Gram is required.

The final union bound combines two separately proved original-law events, not conditionally independent events.
All scalar and tail weak-law failures remain a single \(o(1)\) contribution before the minor union, while the finite-grid and \(N+1\)-minor union of conditional exponential errors remains exponential.
The final residual tolerance is the minimum of three fixed positive tolerances, and the column and Gram constants are independent of \(N\).
No summability or cross-size independence is used.

For every unit vector in the indicated low spectral subspace, the spectral theorem bounds its residual at the center by \(r\le\rho\), even when the interval extends outside the range of allowed centers.
The approximate-mode event is already simultaneous over every vector and center, so this step needs neither an eigenbasis choice nor a new union bound over embeddings or subspaces.
The displayed auxiliary Gram is exactly \(V_h^TV_h+TV_h^TV_hT\), and homogeneity gives the factor \(\|h\|^2\).
The zero vector, zero subspace, zero window radius and repeated eigenspaces cause no exception.
