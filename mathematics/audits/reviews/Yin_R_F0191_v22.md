### The bordered coefficient identity and its degree

For full-column-rank \(F\), an orthogonal change of row coordinates writes \(F=(R,0)^T\) with \(R\) invertible.
The coefficient of \(t^k\) in \(\det(B+tFF^T)\) is
\[
 \det(R)^2\det B_{22},
\]
where \(B_{22}\) is the compression of \(B\) to \((\operatorname{ran}F)^\perp\).
Expanding the bordered symmetric determinant along its last \(k\) rows and matching columns gives this expression multiplied by \((-1)^k\).
This verifies both the sign and the absence of an extra factorial in the identity.
Full-column-rank matrices are dense, so the polynomial identity also covers deficient \(F\).
Replacing \(FF^T\) by \(FDF^T\) multiplies the top coefficient by \(\det D\), including negative or zero diagonal entries.

The update has rank at most \(k\), so its determinant has degree at most \(k\) in \(t\).
The top coefficient uses \(k\) rank-one updates and \(p-k\) residual columns, giving degree \(2p\) in \(x\) and degree \(p-k\) in \(A\).
These degrees are also consistent with the bordered determinant, which uses \(2k\) linear entries and \(p-k\) quadratic entries.

### Exact nonvanishing, including infinite-dimensional residuals

Selfadjointness and \(AJ=0\) give the orthogonal decomposition
\[
 \mathcal H'=\operatorname{ran}J\oplus
              \overline{\operatorname{ran}A}.
\]
The restriction of \(A\) to its second summand is injective even when its range is not closed and its inverse is unbounded.
Restricting the domain to \(\mathcal H'\) leaves both \(SAS^T\) and \(SJ\) unchanged.
In this restricted domain, the kernel of \(A\) is precisely the head.

The residual dimension assumption permits a \(p\)-dimensional subspace containing all \(k\) head directions.
Injectivity of every nonzero \(U(a)\) makes the \(p\) vectors \(U_i h\) independent for each nonzero vector in that subspace.
The resulting square evaluation space is therefore transitive over the real field.
The accepted real lemma supplies an invertible restriction of \(S(x)\), so both full row rank of \(S(x)\) and full column rank of \(F(x)\) hold at one point.

If the mixed coefficient vanished identically, the bordered matrix would have maximal rank below \(p+k\).
A nonzero maximal-minor polynomial and the restriction-determinant polynomial remain nonzero on the finite-dimensional span of their witnesses.
Their product is a nonzero real polynomial there, which gives the required simultaneous point \(x_0\).
This argument does not assume finite dimension of the ambient codomain.

For a nonzero kernel vector \((a,c)\), full column rank of \(F(x_0)\) forces \(a\ne0\).
The symmetric range block of the bordered matrix at \(x_0\) is invertible, even when it is indefinite.
Maximal rank makes its nearby Schur complement zero; because the off-diagonal blocks vanish at zero, differentiation gives exactly
\[
 0=2\langle U(a)(AS(x_0)^Ta+Jc),y\rangle
 \quad\text{for every }y.
\]
The vector inside \(U(a)\) belongs to \(\mathcal H'\), so the stated injectivity applies.
Orthogonality of the two summands gives \(c=0\) and \(AS(x_0)^Ta=0\).
The lower kernel equation is \(J^TS(x_0)^Ta=0\).
Together these equations force \(S(x_0)^Ta=0\), contradicting full row rank.
No positivity, compactness or bounded inverse was used in this part.

### Uniform compactness with a persistent head

In a putative counterexample sequence, the first \(k\) domain labels remain the isometric head, and only the nonzero residual eigenvectors are ordered by decreasing absolute eigenvalue.
Restriction to this domain preserves both map inequalities, the coefficient and all residual spectral hypotheses.
Bounded label Gram entries and eigenvalues permit the stated diagonal subsequence.
The Gram quotient and completion produce bounded limiting maps because the stack upper bound passes to every finitely supported vector and then extends by continuity.
The whole-space lower Gram inequality passes by the same argument.

The limiting domain keeps its head as an actual orthonormal coordinate summand.
The residual retains norm one, at least \(p-k\) singular values of magnitude at least \(d\), and squared eigenvalue sum at most \(K\).
Some other residual labels may limit to zero without affecting these facts.
The exact theorem may subsequently restrict away those zero labels, as the proof explicitly allows.

For the truncation after \(l\) residual labels, ordering and the squared-sum bound give
\[
 \|A-A^{[l]}\|\le \sqrt{K/(l+1)}
\]
uniformly before and after the limit.
The finite-support case has zero tail after its last label.
The bounds \(\|S(x)\|\le\sqrt{p\Lambda}\|x\|\), \(\|B_A(x)\|\le p\Lambda\|x\|^2\) and \(\|F(x)F(x)^T\|\le p\Lambda\|x\|^2\) are valid consequences of the stack hypothesis.
They are deliberately nonsharp, which does not affect uniformity.

At the fixed points \(t=0,\ldots,k\), determinant telescoping bounds the effect of the residual truncation by a constant depending only on \(p,k,\Lambda\), times \(\sqrt{K/(l+1)}\|x\|^{2p}\).
Interpolation is legitimate because the polynomial degree in \(t\) is at most \(k\); in particular its leading coefficient is the \(k\)-th forward difference divided by \(k!\).
Thus the coefficient-tail estimate has no dimension or residual-sign dependence.

For a finite linear combination of limiting labels, its same-coefficient prelimit vectors have convergent norms and convergent fixed-truncation coefficients.
The assumed vanishing suprema control their full coefficients by homogeneity.
Passing first to the sequence limit and then letting \(l\) increase therefore gives zero limiting mixed coefficient on the dense label span, and hence everywhere by continuity.
The limiting lower Gram inequality implies injectivity of every nonzero \(U(a)\), contradicting the exact result.
This proves the claimed positive constant in the stated finite-dimensional quantitative theorem.

### Separated magnitudes and all proper head subsets

Rank-one updates make the determinant separately affine in each head eigenvalue.
Consequently the displayed expansion has one term for each subset of head labels, and its full-subset coefficient is exactly \(M_{A,J}\).
Normalization of the residual by \(r\) preserves the map and head hypotheses and gives the lower bound \(br^{p-k}\).

For a subset \(I\), there are at most \(p!\) assignments of its distinct head labels to distinct determinant columns.
Each update column has norm at most \(p\Lambda\), and every residual column has norm at most \(p\Lambda r\).
The permutation expansion therefore gives the stated safe bound
\[
 |c_I(x)|\le(p!)^2(p\Lambda)^p r^{p-|I|}.
\]
This count does not omit an ordering factor.
Dividing a proper-subset term by \((\prod_{j\le k}q_j)r^{p-k}\) leaves precisely
\[
 \prod_{j\notin I}\frac r{q_j}.
\]
When \(r/q_*\le1\), each nonempty such product is at most \(r/q_*\).
There are \(2^k-1\) proper subsets, giving the displayed \(C_*\) and the threshold \(\delta=\min\{1,b/(2C_*)\}\).
The reverse triangle inequality at a point witnessing the leading coefficient proves the half-\(b\) conclusion.
Taking absolute values only after retaining the full product verifies arbitrary head signs and arbitrarily separated head magnitudes.

### Three-channel branches and the essential support restriction

For the physical combined maps, the slope weights are \(1,2,3\), so orthonormality of the physical modes gives the stack bound with \(\Lambda=3\).
Their quadratic evaluation matrix is the actual border compression for the selected spectral support.
The determinant is therefore the raw sixth homogeneous part up to the overall minus sign, which does not affect the lower bound.

For \(k=1\), the residual norm is \(q_2\), its second singular value is \(q_3\), and the stated effective-rank and gap conditions match the theorem exactly.
The scale \(q_1q_2^2\) dominates \(q_1q_2q_3\) because \(q_3\le q_2\).
For \(k=2\), the residual norm and first singular value are both \(q_3\), so \(d=1\) is valid and the head minimum is \(q_2\).
Neither argument imposes a third-to-fourth gap.

The support qualification is essential for a nonvacuous physical application.
On the complete \(3n\)-dimensional mode space of an invertible physical matrix, the first combined map has image in \(\mathbb R^n\oplus\{0\}\) and rank at most \(n\).
It cannot satisfy the positive whole-space Gram lower bound, which would in particular make that map injective.
The accepted C187v19 theorem instead supplies the required geometry on its specified low spectral subspace.
The candidate correctly requires a separate tail comparison before a result for that restricted support becomes a result for the complete inverse.

For the example comparison, write the full physical Schur matrix as retained part plus discarded part.
The discarded norm bound gives \(\|E\|\le3/\rho\), while the retained singular values satisfy \(s_1(B_0)\le3q_1\) and \(s_2(B_0)\le3q_2\).

When the ordered third retained pole tends to infinity, \(q_2\) and \(q_1\) do also, so this is eventually at most \(C_\rho q_1q_2\).
Its ratio to \(q_1q_2q_3\) tends to zero as claimed.
