### Uniform random joint geometry

The original model gives principal blocks the law of \(\sqrt{n_U/N}\) times the normalized fixed-law Wigner model of size \(n_U\).
Thus \(n_U\ge N/3\) keeps the limiting semicircle densities bounded by \(\sqrt3/\pi\).
The expected distribution functions converge uniformly because their weak limit is continuous; taking the supremum over all integer sizes at least \(N/3\) preserves this convergence.
For a closed window, the same uniform convergence controls its left endpoint by the left limit of the distribution function.
The expected window and tail masses can consequently be made less than \(\kappa/4\), uniformly over the specified blocks, by a fixed small window and eventual dimension.

Replacing one independent upper-triangular row block changes the Hermitian matrix by rank at most two.
An interval indicator or the union of two tails has total variation two, so its normalized count changes by at most \(4/n_U\).
Ordinary bounded differences therefore gives an exponential bound for a fixed count deviation.
A union over the fixed center grid, the one tail space and at most \(N^2\) deterministic sets remains \(Ce^{-cN}\).
The unquantified scalar weak-convergence error is used only to establish eventual expectation bounds, and is not multiplied by \(N^2\).

The C187v12 tail split was checked with the new fixed \(\delta=1/64\).
Conditional on the exact tail data, good second-input coordinates remain independent, centered, bounded and of variance at least \(1/2\).
The full tail Frobenius event and maximum bad-degree event are inherited by all principal restrictions.
The conditional full-matrix bounded-remainder norm estimate is uniform over missing edges and the realized tail data.
Only the full tail Frobenius failure contributes the qualitative error \(p_{T,N}\).

For the adapted projection associated with a space of dimension at most \(\kappa n_U\), the covariance calculation gives

 \mathbb E[\|P_VW_Uz\|^2\mid C_N,\text{tail data}]

### Deterministic compression and all-direction covariance

The spectral-domain intersection \(H_0\) has codimension at most \(m/12\).
Its componentwise restriction removes at most \(m/4\) dimensions from a kernel of dimension at least \(3m\), without assuming that either matrix preserves \(H_0\).
