### The existing inverse and scalar spectral inputs

The matrix pencil L is a bounded selfadjoint polynomial in the finite semicircular family.
It belongs to the full-corner matrix rational model in C95.
Taking the generated algebra \(W^*(I_m,L)\) does not change its spectral measure or its faithful normalized trace.

Finiteness of \(\int x^{-2}\,d\mu\) first forces zero kernel trace.
Faithfulness removes the kernel projection itself.
Spectral calculus then supplies an actual densely defined affiliated inverse, whose squared L2 norm is exactly that integral.
This establishes the existing-inverse premise before C95 is invoked.
Equation (3) makes this inverse bounded and everywhere defined.
Conversely, a bounded inverse has finite squared trace.
Faithfulness also identifies scalar spectral support with the spectrum, so the equivalence with an open gap is exact.
Neither a formal inverse expression nor mere nonzeroness of L is substituted for the existing-inverse premise.
This part does not need the positive frame.

The accepted C186v4 theorem applies to the fixed real selfadjoint affine matrix polynomial.
A deterministic vanishing real shift preserves its weak limit.
Deleting at most \(r+1\) physical positions removes at most \(m(r+1)\) scalar coordinates, and scalar interlacing plus normalization gives the stated uniform CDF bound.
This is a deterministic bound over all the deletion sets, not a union of merely marginal weak-limit probabilities.
The minors retain the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.

For fixed h, \(h^2/(x^2+h^2)\) is bounded continuous with finite total variation.
Thus the weak law and deletion bound imply uniformly
\[
 N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}Q_h\longrightarrow m q(h).
\]
The factor m and the replacement of minor size by N are correct.
The limit \(q(h)>0\) for each fixed h need not stay positive as h decreases.
Monotone convergence supplies \(J(h)\to\infty\) on the asserted spectral branch without any density or window-mass hypothesis.

### Positive-contraction covariance and conditional truncation

Deleting the exposed physical index removes every mirrored coordinate of its border.
Conditioning only on the original further-deleted input minors therefore leaves all d original raw rows independent, with their unchanged real laws.
The exposed diagonal is irrelevant to this conditioning calculation and will contribute a real symmetric block later.

For a complex unit auxiliary direction a, the covariance trace is
\[
 H_a=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
 Q_h\left(\sum_j(A_ja)(A_ja)^*\otimes I\right)\right].
\]
Testing the inner matrix against any complex unit b yields the exact original frame sum.
Hence \(H_a\ge\chi\operatorname{Tr}Q_h\), uniformly over a.
This calculation is valid for a positive contraction Q_h; it does not require Q_h to be a projector.
The individual weights lie in \([0,W]\), with \(W>0\) because the frame is positive.

One fixed raw cutoff works for all finitely many laws and is independent of h.
The exponential-moment estimate for the retained weighted sum is correct:
\((1-e^{-1})/2-1/4>0\) gives a strictly positive exponential rate at threshold \(H_a/4\).
The calculation preserves weighted covariance rather than imposing a small discarded-coordinate fraction that would depend on h.

After revealing the indicators and discarded values, the retained variables are independent conditional truncated laws.
Their variances are at least 1/2 and their support diameters at most \(2L_0\).
Their means and the discarded part give a fixed offset.
The conditional second moment of the norm is at least \(H_a/8\); the mean term is nonnegative and is not assumed zero.
The raw map has Lipschitz constant at most \(\sqrt\Lambda\), and composition with \(Q_h^{1/2}\) is contractive.
Thus inequality (9) gives a bounded squared median deviation uniformly in the offset, h and N.
The L2 triangle inequality gives a median of order \(\sqrt{H_a}\), and the lower tail gives \(Ce^{-cq(h)N}\).
Its constants are independent of h; the sufficiently large N threshold is allowed to depend on the fixed positive \(q(h)\).
No parent norm condition has entered that conditional law.

### Summable nets, actual Schur blocks and fixed-h quantifiers

The total raw-coordinate guard has complement at most \(CN^{-2}\) by the original second moments.
On it, every relevant raw border has operator norm at most \(C_A N^2\).
The guard is used only for deterministic extension from a complex sphere net, with mesh proportional to \(\sqrt{q(h)}N^{-3/2}\).
Its cardinality is at most \(Cq(h)^{-m}N^{3m}\).

For each pair, the conditional tail is integrated only on that pair's local trace condition.
The event that all trace conditions hold but some pair fails is then bounded by the union of these legitimate local events.
A union over \(O_r(N^{r+1})\) pairs and the single guard exception gives precisely the displayed summable bound for fixed h.
No independence between pair events or across dimensions is required.
The trace conditions themselves hold almost surely eventually, so first Borel--Cantelli supplies the simultaneous row quadratic-form lower bound.

The actual physical Schur block satisfies
\[
 -\operatorname{Im}S_i(h)
 =hI_m+B_i^T\operatorname{Im}(\widetilde M-ih)^{-1}B_i

\]
All realized diagonal entries and the common shift remain real symmetric.
The imaginary quadratic-form lower bound implies the stated lower singular-value bound for \(S_i(h)\), and its inverse is exactly the physical block of the resolvent.

\[
 \mathbf1_{[-h,h]}(M)\preceq2h\,\operatorname{Im}(M-ih)^{-1}

For each fixed h these conclusions are eventually almost sure.
Intersecting over a prescribed countable family, including the dyadics, is legitimate.
This establishes the claimed uniformity over all fixed-deletion pairs and complex auxiliary directions.

### Capped residual energy and separate physical spread

The function \(\phi_B(x)=\min(x^{-2},B)\), with value B at zero, is bounded continuous and has finite total variation.
The scalar weak limit applies uniformly over the deletion sets, simultaneously for integer B.
Removing at most \(k_0\) orthonormal real eigenmodes loses at most \(k_0B\) from its capped trace.
Every residual squared Frobenius norm therefore dominates that capped trace minus \(k_0B\).

Taking N to infinity at fixed B and only then increasing B proves
\(F_E^2/N\to\infty\), uniformly over all allowed invertible minors and mode choices.
The finite-dimensional bound \(Q_E^2\ge F_E^2/(mn)\) then gives \(Q_E\to\infty\).
No convergence of uncapped random inverse moments is asserted or needed.
No probability is divided by the probability of invertibility; empty invertible collections are harmless.

Each removed eigenmode projection commutes with M, including an arbitrary orthonormal selection inside a repeated eigenspace.
Consequently \(E_<\) and \(E_>\) are selfadjoint and spectrally orthogonal, with \(\|E_>\|\le h^{-1}\).
The projector bound gives
\[
 \|P_iE_<\|_F^2\le\rho(h)F_E^2.
\]
The cross trace of the two row pieces vanishes, so their row energies add.
The high row has rank at most m and squared Frobenius norm at most \(mh^{-2}\), proving the displayed maximum-row estimate.

For the physical diagonal sum, the stronger individual compression calculation supplies a separate factor \(\rho(h)\).
Summing and using the two-term square inequality gives
\[
 \mathcal D(E)\le2\rho(h)F_E^2+2mn h^{-2}.
\]
This estimate is not inferred from the maximum-row ratio.
Uniform divergence relative to N removes the high terms at fixed h.
Then dyadic \(h\downarrow0\) makes \(\rho(h)\to0\), proving both ratios at each residual's own energy scale.
