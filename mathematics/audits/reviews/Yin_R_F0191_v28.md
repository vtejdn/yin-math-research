### Complex extension and exact covariance

For real centers, splitting a complex vector into real and imaginary parts preserves the sum of squared residuals.
Homogeneity extends the real unit-vector gap to each part, including a zero part.
For a complex center, Hermitian symmetry eliminates the cross term and adds the nonnegative squared imaginary part times the vector mass.
Thus the complex sum inequality follows on the same real-input event, without any assertion for complex-entry Wigner inputs.

Direct expansion of \(\mathbb E[XX^*]\) gives the displayed Hermitian matrix, with off-diagonal entries \(2u^*t\) and \(u^*v\) in its first row.
Independence between \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) removes the \((2,3)\) covariance, and centering makes this second-moment matrix the covariance.
For \(s=\|u\|^2>0\), its Schur complement is exactly \(\Delta=\|v_\perp\|^2+4\|t_\perp\|^2\), and its determinant is \(s^2\Delta\).
The complex-center sum gap applies to the minimizing Rayleigh quotients of the exact companions.
Projection contraction and the weighted direct-sum triangle inequality then give \(\sqrt\Delta\ge\rho\|u\|-2\delta\) for approximate companions.
This inequality is squared only after the positive lower-bound regime has been established.
