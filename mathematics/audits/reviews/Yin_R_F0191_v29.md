## Decisive checks

### Fourth moments and sparse two-image separation

For C196's affine input, the fourth-moment expansion only retains index patterns with no singleton; the pairing terms are controlled by the absolute second moments even for phase-line laws.
Symmetrization has second moment twice the coefficient norm squared and bounded fourth moment, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives a fixed probability of leaving a disk at the coefficient scale.
The independent row indicators therefore yield the stated rectangular lower norm with exponentially small failure when there are at least N/5 rows.
Neither identical coordinate laws nor real-imaginary independence is needed.

For fixed disjoint D and S in C187v29, the combined coefficient vector for the two matrices has squared norm one when the sparse vector and coefficient pair are unit.
Every rectangle entry corresponds to a distinct unordered off-diagonal pair within its input, and the two matrices are independent.
Conjugating a coordinate preserves centering, absolute variance and the fourth-moment bound.
Thus the claimed independence across output rows and the use of the mixed-law affine estimate are valid.

On an individual minor norm guard, the two successive net approximations cost at most the displayed sum of two terms, each bounded by the square root of two times K times the net radius.
The complex sphere dimensions give the exponents twice the support size and four for the coefficient pair.
The support entropy tends to zero with the erased fraction, while the bounded-deletion count contributes only a fixed-degree polynomial.
Choosing the fraction sufficiently small therefore leaves an exponential probability bound.
Enlarging a smaller support removes output coordinates and extends its vector by zero, so the size-s estimate implies every smaller-support estimate in the correct direction.
Zero vectors, zero coefficient pairs and empty supports are covered by homogeneity or are vacuous.
The auxiliary statement's omission of a separate large-size threshold is harmless: increasing its prefactor can cover the finitely many small sizes where the large-size construction is not used.

### Cancellation and bounded centers

The cancellation identity is valid over the complex numbers because the scalar products of the two centers commute.
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the combined residual by the coefficient-pair norm times the joint residual, while the guarded matrix term costs at most the square root of two times K times the retained mass.
The coefficient pair supplied to the sparse lemma has exactly the norm divided out in the displayed inequality.
For both centers zero, the first residual and the first matrix alone give the stated weaker inequality.

If retained mass were less than the chosen tau, the erased mass would be at least the square root of three divided by two, whereas the right side of the cancellation bound would be at most half of g.
This is a genuine contradiction whenever the residual is at most one quarter of g.
Each individual residual then bounds its own center by the stated R using the retained mass lower bound and the minor operator norm.
Consequently the reduction from all complex centers to a fixed disk has no assumption about how those centers depend on the matrices or vectors.

### Lawful second-input conditioning and subspace nets

After the first matrix and a test vector are fixed, the deterministic bipartition can be oriented so that the input half of the vector has norm at least one divided by the square root of two.
Conditioning on second-matrix coordinates within the output half fixes the diagonal block contribution and the scalar shift, and leaves precisely the cross-half unordered pairs fresh.

No mirrored entry is counted as a second independent variable.

There are at least N/5 output rows for sufficiently large N under the stated deletion and erasure bounds.
The rectangular threshold can therefore be divided by the square root of two uniformly in the vector, affine center and first-matrix realization.
A measurable orthonormal-basis rule makes a fixed Euclidean sphere net into a lawful first-input-measurable net.
Its cardinality has exponent twice the complex dimension, and the operator norm of the shifted second minor is at most K plus R plus one on the guard.
The stated net radius leaves strict slack above beta for all unit vectors in the subspace.

### Spectral windows and the lifted subspace

The fixed finite-second-moment diagonal assumption is present exactly where C186's original-law expected semicircle limit is invoked.
It is not inferred from the auxiliary lemma's broader diagonal scope.
A sufficiently short real interval has uniformly small semicircle mass by bounded density; intersections of complex disks with the real line are intervals, singletons or empty sets, all with zero-mass boundary.
Weak convergence of the deterministic expected measures therefore controls each fixed window count.
The center nets are finite and fixed before the large-size threshold is selected, so neither a convergence rate nor uniform weak convergence over moving centers is used.

Changing an upper-row block changes a Hermitian matrix by rank at most two.

The slack between half of kappa times N and kappa times N absorbs this error simultaneously for all deletions without renormalizing the minor.

The sparse lemma fixes g and the center bound first; the second-input threshold and sphere-net radius can then be fixed before kappa and the window radius.
