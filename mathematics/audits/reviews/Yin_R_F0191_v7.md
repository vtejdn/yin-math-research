### The mixed coefficient bound has no dimension loss

Removing a diagonal from either quadratic factor leaves its fourth sign component unchanged, since that diagonal form is constant on the sign support.
The symmetric rank-two U has nuclear norm at most ||a|| ||b||, and its diagonal has nuclear norm at most the same quantity by Cauchy--Schwarz.
Thus the off-diagonal U has nuclear norm at most two even though diagonal removal need not preserve its rank.
The other off-diagonal matrices have operator norm at most 2m and Frobenius norm at most R.
The nuclear/operator trace inequality used here follows directly from the displayed singular-value decomposition argument and does not require positivity.

Each off-diagonal quadratic coefficient is 2A_ij/N, so the fourth product coefficient has exactly the stated 4/N^2 factor and the three unordered pairings with both assignments.
When two pairings agree, the unrestricted sums pair U with B or C and the other two residual matrices with each other.
The nuclear/operator bound controls the first factor by Cm, and Frobenius Cauchy--Schwarz controls the second by R^2.
The excluded shared-vertex terms have absolute value at most a fixed multiple of the stated row-product sum.
The residual row product is at most 4m^2, while the remaining absolute U/B entry sum is at most 2R.
This gives Cm^2 R, including the equal-edge case, and each other assignment is just a permutation of the residual matrices.

Different pairings form a four-cycle.
Cyclically placing the single U factor first in the unrestricted trace gives the bound Cm^3 using its nuclear norm and three residual operator norms.
Adjacent vertex coincidences vanish because the diagonals are zero.
For either opposite-vertex coincidence, symmetry lets the remaining sum be written as one U/residual row product times a residual/residual row product, bounded by Cm^2 R as above.
Their intersection is bounded by the same nonnegative sum, so deleting repeated vertices causes no omitted collision term.
Since m is at most R, both error scales are bounded by CmR^2.
There are only finitely many coefficient assignments, and multiplying the two coefficient factors contributes 16/N^4.
