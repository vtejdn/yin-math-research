### Conditional shifts and normalization

Word reversal without conjugating coefficients gives transposition on every real symmetric input tuple.
Thus each permitted shift is complex symmetric, and the separately required identity for the whole \(H\) gives symmetry of \(H\) and, wherever it exists, of its inverse.
Individual factor symmetry alone would not establish that last condition.

At each fixed size, conditioning on all inputs except the distinguished matrix preserves that matrix's entire Wigner law and makes the relevant polynomial shift deterministic.
All norm and invertibility events used to truncate a shift belong to that same conditioning sigma-field.
The norm-event deduction gives polynomial entry bounds with any prescribed polynomial failure probability using only the expected singular tail and second moments.
Consequently the uniform normalized small-value theorem applies first to each \(T_\nu\), and then to the globally defined shift \(Q-zH^{-1}\) of \(W_1\).
Defining that shift to be zero on the bad event preserves independence and symmetry.
The fixed scalar \(c\) is absorbed by fixed exponents or constants, and the identity \(R-z=(W_1+Q-zH^{-1})H\) is exact on the good event.
Repeated distinguished indices and dependence among the factors affect neither their separate conditional estimates nor the finite union bound.
Only fixed-size conditioning is used; no independence of complete matrix sequences is assumed.

### Singular products and logarithmic tails

The two-factor singular product inequality follows from intersecting a lower-bound subspace for the right factor with the inverse image of one for the left factor.
The inverse image has codimension no larger than the original subspace, including for a singular linear map.
Iteration therefore gives the displayed increasing-index inequality with the subtraction \(L-1\).
For \(k\ge2L(q_N+1)\), distributing \(k+L-1\) into nearly equal positive integers gives every required factor index and gives \(r_i-1\ge k/(2L)\).
The resulting lower bound is a fixed multiple of \((k/N)^L\), with \(L=K+1\) independent of \(N\).
The fixed scalar factor changes that multiple only.
For fixed \(L\) the threshold is below \(N\) eventually, and the excluded bottom range is \(O(N^{3/4})\).

The least-singular floor bounds the normalized bottom contribution by \(O(N^{-1/4}\log N)\), which tends to zero.
The remaining small-index logarithms are bounded by an integrable multiple of \(\log(N/k)\); their normalized limsup is at most \(C\varepsilon+L\varepsilon(1-\log\varepsilon)\).
Indices above \(\varepsilon N\) are eventually separated from zero by a fixed multiple of \(\varepsilon^L\).
Sending the singular-value cutoff to zero and then \(\varepsilon\) to zero proves the claimed negative logarithmic uniform integrability.
All probability failures are summable and require only first Borel--Cantelli, so the stated arbitrary cross-size coupling is preserved.
The case \(K=0\) has a deterministic invertible scalar carrier and uses the single-factor intermediate estimate directly.
