## Obligations and decisive checks

### Fourth coefficients and surviving matching

The first off-diagonal row is independent of the conditioning that fixes the full second input, first principal minor and exposed first diagonal.
For a degree-at-most-four polynomial, only a raw monomial with four distinct coordinates can contribute to a four-sign Walsh coefficient.
Its coefficient after the affine two-copy substitution is exactly \(c_I\prod_{j\in I}b_j\); the factor \(N^{-2}\) is already included in \(c_I\).
Translations, repeated-coordinate monomials and lower-degree terms cannot create another fourth contribution.
Arbitrarily large pair values can enlarge fourth coefficients and produce arbitrary lower coefficients; neither creates an upper-coefficient restriction in the MNV input.

With \(S:=\sum_I c_I^2\), deleting coefficients smaller than \(\sqrt S/(\sqrt2 n^2)\) removes at most \(S/2\), since there are at most \(n^4\) fourth monomials.
Each greedy selected edge removes at most \(4\tau S\) remaining energy, so a maximal selection has at least \((8\tau)^{-1}\) edges.
The prescribed \(r_N:=\lfloor(16t_N)^{-1}\rfloor\) is therefore available on every good conditioning and can be chosen measurably before the pairs.
The retained coefficient threshold is at least \(\kappa a_i/(\sqrt2 N^4)\).

The two-copy selection has exactly the original row law, including for nonsymmetric or atomic laws.
Moreover \(\mathbb E b_j^2=1/2\), so a fixed positive threshold \(b_0\) has positive exceedance probability \(p_0\).
Disjoint four-sets use disjoint pairs, hence their survival indicators are independent with probability \(q:=p_0^4\).
For their sum \(V\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{V\le qr_N/2\}
 \le e^{qr_N/2}(1-q+qe^{-1})^{r_N}
 \le e^{-q(1-e^{-1}-1/2)r_N}.
\]
Thus the stated fourth-coefficient threshold survives on a matching of order \(r_N\), without any upper amplitude cutoff or fourth moment.

### Support guard, graph distance and finite interval

The scalar identity \(|a_j|+|b_j|=\max(|\xi_j'|,|\xi_j''|)\) gives the uniform bound over every remaining sign row.
The paired square sum divided by \(N\) converges in probability to two, because its summands are integrable with mean two and \((N-1)/N\to1\).
Consequently the radius-two support guard fails with exactly the stated conditional probability \(e_N\to0\).
It is measurable before the signs, and no independence from matching survival is needed.

In the graph input, \(U:=\binom{I_2}{-ZB}\Gamma^{-1/2}\) has orthonormal columns, and its transpose annihilates the other columns.
Invertibility of the retained minor makes this their entire orthogonal complement.
Hence the projected exposed block has the two singular values of \(D:=\Gamma^{-1/2}S\), with \(\|D\|\le\|L_N\|\) and \(\det D=p_i/\sqrt{\det\Gamma}\).
The resulting inclusion \(|p_i|\le b_Nr\sqrt{\det\Gamma}\) is therefore valid on the norm-guarded distance event.

At \(r:=c_1b_N^{-1}N^{-4}\), the graph guard makes the interval radius at most \(c_1a_iN^{-4}\).
Choosing \(2c_1<b_0^4\kappa/\sqrt2\) makes its length strictly less than the surviving coefficient threshold.
After scaling, the MNV polynomial is real and multilinear in independent fair signs, with degree four and matching rank at least \(\lfloor p_0^4r_N/2\rfloor\).
The first MNV exponent is \(1/(4\cdot4+1)=1/17\).
The rank is eventually at least two, and its fixed-factor comparison with \(r_N\), together with eventual decrease of \(\sqrt{\log r}\,r^{-1/17}\), gives the displayed error uniformly in all conditioned coefficients.
The norm event stays inside the estimated event and is never added to the conditioning.
Separate enlarged spaces for different physical indices suffice because each has the original parent marginal.

### Fixed rectangular input and the full-matrix average

The fixed-cutoff distance argument uses only the finite second moment: a fixed truncation retains conditional variance at least \(1/2\), while excessive discarded coordinates have exponentially small probability.
Projection of the comparison space to retained coordinates and enlargement by the deterministic shift plus conditional mean leaves codimension of order the dimension.
For the complementary projection \(P\), centered independent real coordinates give \(\mathbb E\|Py\|^2=v_R\operatorname{Tr}P\), also when \(P\) is complex.
The accepted convex Lipschitz concentration bound makes the median of \(\|Py\|\) of order the square root of that codimension and gives exponentially small lower-tail failure.
This rechecks the uniformity in deterministic shifts needed here.

For each crossing rectangle, no mirrored pair occurs twice, so its columns and their coordinates are independent.
The inverse-Gram diagonal formula gives the sum of reciprocal squared column distances; it yields the stated \(a/\sqrt N\) normalized least-value bound.
A sufficiently small fixed support proportion makes the support-count entropy smaller than its exponential failure exponent.
Conditioning on the entire independent \(B_N\) therefore permits both shifts \(B_N\) and \(\alpha B_N\) with the same uniform constants.
Across disjoint physical supports the identity coupling is zero, so the two-channel rectangle is a direct sum of those two rectangles.

If a unit vector with residual at most \(a/(4\sqrt N)\) had at most \(\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\) physical blocks larger than \(a/(8b_NN)\), its complementary norm would be at most \(a/(8b_N\sqrt N)\).
The rectangle lower bound would then force a residual at least \(3a/(8\sqrt N)\), a contradiction.
For each of the resulting many large blocks, orthogonal projection away from all other columns gives \(d_i\le8b_NNu/a\).
This argument does not require invertibility of the full matrix or its minor, and applies to a real least singular vector even at zero singular value.
The indicator count yields the factor \(2/(\delta N)\), so both the pair-guard and MNV failures enter through their average, without an additional factor of \(N\).
Substituting \(u:=c b_N^{-2}N^{-5}\), with \(c\le ac_1/8\), gives the exact asserted full-matrix scale for every deterministic \(b_N\ge1\).

### Separate selected-sign energy and load

Revealing the signs at \(|b_j|>T\) leaves independent fair signs at the complementary, pair-measurable index set.
A four-distinct monomial touching a revealed index has remaining degree at most three.
Thus the claimed unrevealed fourth coefficients are exact, with no contribution hidden by the reveal operation.

For \(\beta_j:=b_j^2\mathbf1_{\{|b_j|\le T\}}\), monotone convergence gives a fixed \(T\) with \(\mu:=\mathbb E\beta_j\ge1/4\).
The variables are independent and bounded by \(T^2\), so \(\mathbb EH=\mu^4S\), and disjoint products have zero covariance.
Intersecting products have covariance of absolute value at most \(T^{16}\), while
\[
 \sum_{I\cap J\ne\varnothing}c_I^2c_J^2
 \le\sum_j\ell_j^2
 \le (\max_j\ell_j)\sum_j\ell_j
 =4\tau S^2.
\]
Chebyshev at a deficit \(\mu^4S/2\) therefore gives exactly \(16T^{16}\mu^{-8}\tau\).
Every unrevealed coordinate load is at most \(T^8\ell_j\); division by \(H\ge\mu^4S/2\) gives \(\widehat\tau_4\le2T^8\mu^{-4}\tau\), and taking square roots gives the stated energy bound.
No moment of order four is used, and this separate statement is not needed for the shorter matrix proof.

### Normalization finding and rechecked corollaries

Without an explicit \(N^{-1/2}\) scaling of the second off-diagonal entries, the original corollary's claimed boundary weak law does not follow: unscaled fair-sign entries give \(\|y_i\|^2=N-1\).
The corrected frozen hypothesis \((B_N)_{jk}=\eta_{jk}/\sqrt N\) removes that issue.
Its row squared norms have the same law for every physical index and converge in probability to one, so their averaged failure tends to zero as asserted.
The two boundary columns have orthogonal auxiliary supports; their operator norm is at most \(2(1+\max\{1,|\alpha|\})\) on the two radius-two row guards.
Intersecting the good event with the measurable second-row guard therefore permits the stronger uniform minor condition and adds only its averaged failure.

Under the corrected optional diagonal hypothesis, each input has expected squared Frobenius norm \((N-1)+\mathbb E\zeta_j^2\).
The squared norm inequality for the two diagonal auxiliary blocks and the identity couplings gives \(\mathbb E\|L_N\|_{\rm F}^2\le CN\).
Since \(\|L_N\|\le\|L_N\|_{\rm F}\), Markov gives \(CN^{1-2\beta}\); inserting \(b_N=N^\beta\) gives \(cN^{-5-2\beta}\).
These computations verify the corrected hypotheses and affected conclusions without claiming the original unnormalized wording was sufficient.
