## Whole-proof finding

No substantive gap was found in the complete fixed conditional statement and argument under their accepted inputs.
The independent obligations were to transfer an actual-minor identity to a potentially noncommuting measurable approximant, control the signed original-row error and graph area at the claimed scales, preserve all conditioning and marginal quantifiers, and verify precisely what the additional mean approximation hypothesis implies.
The full proof discharges these obligations.
It does not establish that extra actual-model probability hypothesis.

The model section correctly separates the free gap from finite-dimensional inverse tails.
For each fixed \(0<g<\operatorname{dist}(0,\sigma(L))\), the identity \(X_N=Y_N(g)-D_N(g)\) holds with the stated extended-value convention at zero.
The function \(x\mapsto\tfrac12\log\max(g^2,x)\) is continuous, nondecreasing and of logarithmic growth on \([0,\infty)\).
Applying C186v4 to \(Q=L^2\) gives \(Y_N(g)\to v\) almost surely under each coupling, since clipping changes no free spectral value.
Hence the upper limsup bound is unconditional.
The added sentence about \(D_N(g)\to0\) in probability on a deterministic subsequence is also valid: it gives \(X_N\to v\) in probability there, and C190v1 then supplies the lower limsup under every coupling.
This is a sufficient total-loss direction, not an established loss estimate for the model.

The exact resolvent identity keeps the correct factor order:
\[
 f_\eta(Z)-f_\eta(E)
 =(I-i\eta Z)^{-1}(Z-E)(I-i\eta E)^{-1}.
\]
It follows from the inverse-difference identity without assuming that \(Z\) and \(E\) commute.
Their real symmetry makes each outside factor a contraction.
The nuclear-norm ideal property gives the first comparison, while spectral calculus on \(E\) gives
\(\|f_\eta(E)-E\|_1\le\eta\|E\|_F^2\).
The normalized partial trace is bounded by nuclear norm, and
\(\|g_E\|\le\sqrt{3n}\|E\|_F/N\le h_K\).
These yield exactly \(u_N(\eta)=\eta K+\delta_N\), with no rank or physical-spread premise.

The C194v18 fixed-eta input is the unconditional expectation relation for the actual pencil and its actual internal deleted resolvents.
Its assumptions hold for the same original laws, common shift, fixed deletion count and parent-N normalization.
No equation is assigned to a matrix whose inverse is \(E\).
The displayed difference of \(B_Eg_E\) and \(B_\eta g_\eta\) has the correct bound
\([a+\Lambda(2h_K+u)]u+\eta(h_K+u)\).
At fixed \(K\), one first chooses \(\eta\) small, then takes \(N\) large so that \(\delta_N\) and the deletion normalization error are small.
Markov's inequality for the unconditional input then gives the relative marginal defect estimate uniformly over all allowed measurable approximants.
The original diagonal error has vanishing marginal probability by its finite second moment.
No division by the probability of the approximation branch or union over all deletion pairs occurs.

The approximant is measurable under the original minor sigma-field, which is contained in \(\mathcal F_{T,i}\).
The latter adds only exposed diagonal variables and remains independent of all fresh border coordinates.
C194v18's quadratic estimate therefore applies conditionally to each \(U_aEU_b^T\), with fixed Frobenius scale and the exact mean \(\mathcal S(g_E)\).
The resulting \(q_N(K)\to0\) is uniform on the branch; neither symmetry of these coefficient matrices nor a fourth moment is required.

For \(H=Z-E\), selfadjoint order gives
\(-R^T|H|R\preceq R^THR\preceq R^T|H|R\).
Its conditional expected norm is bounded by the expected trace of the positive matrix on the right.
The original covariance computes that trace exactly as
\[
 N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}
       [(\textstyle\sum_j A_j^2\otimes I_n)|H|]
 \le\Lambda\delta_N.
\]
This argument retains all original input coordinates and requires no independence between the residual and remainder quadratic forms.
The two errors may be added in expectation.

On the good conditioning, \(B_Eg_E\) is invertible and the displayed formula for \(B_E^{-1}\) gives norm at most \(2h_K\).
The fixed diagonal bound and a fresh-row error at most \((8h_K)^{-1}\) therefore leave \(s_{\min}(S)\ge c_K\).
Markov's inequality gives precisely the stated \(\epsilon_N\), with its cap at one.
The determinant conclusion follows from the product of all three singular values.
This is an estimate for the complete Schur block and determinant, including lower degrees and repeated-coordinate contributions.

The orthonormal graph formula is valid because the actual retained block \(M\) is invertible.
It identifies the actual block distance, not a surrogate statistic.
The inequality
\[
 \|Z\|_F\le\|E\|_F+\|Z-E\|_1
\]
gives the claimed energy bound and hence the exact conditional graph-trace upper bound \(V_N\).
On the good numerator event,
\(s_{\min}(F)\ge c_K/\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma}\).
The determinant bound follows from
\(\det\Gamma\le(\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma/3)^3\).
Markov's inequality with non-strict trace thresholds justifies the non-strict endpoints in both graph-tail estimates.
In particular, the squared distance threshold \(N^{-1}\) and the \(2/3\) power of the determinant threshold \(N^{-3/2}\) both multiply \(V_N\) by \(N^{-1}\).
The displayed expansion of \(V_N/N\) tends to zero for fixed \(K\) and \(\delta_N\to0\).
No arbitrary growing energy cutoff is substituted for that fixed \(K\).

For the parent application, the complement of the approximation branch is paid through its explicitly assumed average \(\overline b_N\).
The additional good-conditioning error is paid through \(\rho_N\), and the conditional graph bound is integrated only on its valid good event.
This gives the stated mean distance bound without needing all minors to be good simultaneously.
The positive complex-direction frame is used here through C194v17; the earlier local calculation itself did not use it or the free gap.

The Frobenius guard from C186v22 is an elementary calculation independent of its surrounding spectral-point theorem.
The same finite-second-moment block weak law, negligible random diagonal and common vanishing translation give \(Q_A\) and a fixed \(B\sqrt N\) norm threshold here.
With \(u_N=a_{\rm R}N^{-2}/(8B)\), the exact rectangle distance threshold is
\[
 8(B\sqrt N)Nu_N/a_{\rm R}=N^{-1/2}.
\]
Its required range holds for sufficiently large \(N\).
Thus C194v17 gives the displayed \(N^{-2}\) parent floor, using only the integrated distance average and without a corank or distance-event independence assumption.

The common atom-avoiding shift can be chosen below \(N^{-3}\) for the finite family of actual selfadjoint minors.
That argument does not invoke a free spectral point and changes no original fresh-row law.
It supplies invertibility only: the approximation probability premise must separately hold for the same chosen shift.
The singular-value perturbation bound removes this shift after halving the floor constant, because \(N^{-3}=o(N^{-2})\).
The shift is removed before logarithms.
If the mean premise is asserted only on a deterministic infinite set of dimensions, every floor and logarithmic probability limit in this application retains that restriction.

The original unshifted middle theorem has the required complex-direction frame and original law at \(m=3\).
Its increasing profile beyond \(12\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil+2\) supplies the middle modulus \(Ct(1+|\log t|)\).
The new \(N^{-2}\) floor controls the remaining bottom contribution by \(O(N^{-1/4}\log N)\).
Zeros have infinite negative logarithm and are excluded only on the floor event.
Together with the original Frobenius positive-tail bound and C186v4 weak singular-law convergence, bounded continuous clipping proves \(X_N\to v\) in probability along the dimensions carrying the mean premise.
The free gap supplies the finite free target and vanishing free negative tail; it is not used as a finite-matrix floor.

The all-dimension upper bound from \(Y_N(g)\) and a deterministic favorable subsequence inside those dimensions give the every-coupling limsup.
The lower step is exactly the sufficient direction of C190v1, or first Borel--Cantelli for summable marginal lower-tail probabilities.
It preserves the full within-size input laws and needs no independence between dimensions.
The conclusion is a limsup over all dimensions, while a full-sequence probability limit is claimed only when the approximation premise holds eventually along all dimensions.
No full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic or raw-log expectation limit is inferred.

The spectral comparisons retain the residual energy condition of the conditional theorem.
For a spectral head, its nuclear norm is the sum of the absolute inverse eigenvalues, so small total head nuclear norm suffices without any physical delocalization.
The example pole \(N^{3/4}\) has normalized squared energy \(N^{1/2}\) and normalized nuclear size \(N^{-1/4}\), as claimed.
It is only a comparison of allowed scales, not a positive-probability model construction.

For spectral removal of \(k_N\) modes, the alternative bound
\(\eta K+k_N/(N\eta)\) is valid: each removed resolvent eigenvalue has modulus at most \(1/\eta\).
A rank count \(k_N=o(N)\) therefore suffices for the background equation at fixed \(\eta\), followed by \(\eta\downarrow0\).
It does not supply the signed head-row bound or the graph-trace bound used here.
The proof correctly leaves poles of order \(N\) outside its small-error argument and does not claim an actual-model counterexample in that larger regime.
Arbitrary growing pole counts are allowed only when the same total nuclear error and residual energy conditions hold.

The canonical gap-cutoff approximation is a bounded Borel function of the actual minor, so it is minor-measurable even on singular branches.
Its squared Frobenius norm divided by \(N\) is at most \(3/g^2\).
On invertible minors the nuclear remainder is exactly the stated inverse-first-moment tail.
Assigning infinite error at zero preserves the invertibility obligation.
Weak convergence and the free gap control the normalized number of gap modes, but not this inverse tail.

Finally, the parent-tail comparison
\(D_N(g)\le(g/3)\delta_N^{\rm par}\) has the correct normalization and extended-zero convention.
It is a sufficient route directly to the total-log-loss condition and bypasses the local Schur/floor deduction if separately established.
The mean minor approximation premise has a different role in the distance average.
Neither probability premise is proved in the candidate, and neither is asserted necessary for the determinant contract.
