### Original laws and constant-scale cuts

Only the off-diagonal laws enter the rectangle argument.
They form a fixed finite collection of centered variance-one real laws, so common positive \(a_0,p_0\) can be chosen for their symmetrized differences.
Conditional on their magnitudes, the independent differences admit independent fair signs, including at zero.
The second and fourth moments of the sign sum give
\[
 \mathbb E_\varepsilon\left|\sum_\ell\varepsilon_\ell b_\ell\right|
 \ge \frac{(\sum_\ell b_\ell^2)^{1/2}}{\sqrt3}.
\]
This fourth-moment calculation concerns signs with fixed coefficients and imposes no fourth moment on the original entries.
For \(\sum t_\ell^2=1\), the variable \(U\) in the proof satisfies \(\mathbb E\sqrt U\ge\mathbb EU\ge p_0\).
The triangle inequality \(\mathbb E|X-X'|\le2\mathbb E|X|\) therefore yields the displayed uniform L1 lower bound with the correct direction and constant.

For a fixed real unit auxiliary vector \(a\), output projection is a contraction and the raw projected row covariance is exactly \(I_k\otimes G_a\).
The complex positive-frame hypothesis implies \(G_a\succeq\chi I_m\) when tested on real vectors, and the operator-norm estimate gives \(G_a\preceq\Lambda I_m\).
After whitening, isotropy means that the squared coefficients of each unit linear form in the independent original coordinates sum to one.
The same L1 constant is consequently valid for all column dimensions \(mk\).
The rows are iid: a cut uses disjoint physical row and column sets, hence no mirrored random variable occurs twice, and the real symmetry introduces no orientation-dependent law.
All diagonal randomness and the deterministic physical diagonal disappear from this cut.

KM15 applies with dimension \(p=mk\) and row count \(l\).
Undoing whitening and changing its \(l^{-1/2}\) normalization to the original \(N^{-1/2}\) gives \(c_3\sqrt{\chi l/N}\), as claimed.
The factor from projecting output can only decrease the norm of the full cut, so the lower bound transfers in the required direction.
Real and complex least singular values of this real matrix agree.
Choosing sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\) satisfies both the aspect-ratio condition and the entropy inequality.
For every fixed deletion bound, \(N-|D|=N-O(1)\), and the polynomial number of deletions is absorbed in the exponential margin after the support union.
The argument is asymptotic in \(N\); finitely many smaller dimensions do not affect the asserted constants or subsequent limits.
The same event works for every scalar diagonal shift because the cuts contain none of its entries.
It does not cover an arbitrary off-cut deterministic shift.

### Diffuse covariance, Gaussian approximation and quadratic windows

Conditioning on \(\mathcal F\) fixes \(e,T_N,B,E,\lambda\) while leaving the border coordinates independent with their stated laws.
The splitting into \(z_T\) and \(h\) uses disjoint sets of these coordinates and therefore preserves conditional independence.
For each real test vector, the frame bound gives
\[
 \chi\sum_{k\in T_N}\|e_k\|^2 I_3
 \preceq\Sigma_T\preceq\Lambda I_3.
\]
Thus the lower covariance bound is fixed and strictly positive, and the full second moment of \(z\), as well as each split part, is at most \(\Lambda\).
Writing each summand as \(v_{j,k}\xi_{j,k}\), with \(v_{j,k}:=A_je_k\), gives \(\max\|v_{j,k}\|\le C_A\nu_N\) and \(\sum\|v_{j,k}\|^2\le\Lambda\).
These two bounds imply the stated uniform Lindeberg estimate directly from the fixed finite-second-moment tails.

For a smooth compactly supported test function, the Taylor remainder is controlled by its Hessian modulus for small increments and by a fixed Hessian bound times the squared increment for large ones.
The first two terms cancel in each replacement because the other summands are independent and the replacement Gaussian has the same vector mean and covariance.
The Gaussian remainder is controlled by
\[
 \sum_{j,k}\mathbb E\|v_{j,k}g_{j,k}\|^4
 =3\sum_{j,k}\|v_{j,k}\|^4
 \le3\Lambda C_A^2\nu_N^2\longrightarrow0,
\]
where the \(g_{j,k}\) are independent standard real Gaussians.
This verifies the finite-dimensional adaptation without a quantitative central-limit rate.
Second-moment tightness completes the passage from smooth tests to weak convergence along every covariance-convergent subsequence.

The admissible \(B\)'s lie in a compact set with \(s_{\min}(B)\ge b\), and \(r_N=N/\lambda\) lies in a fixed compact interval containing zero.
After discarding \(\|h\|>R\) with probability at most \(\Lambda/R^2\), conditioning on \(h\) does not change \(z_T\)'s law.
A contrary sequence of conditional small-ball probabilities can therefore be reduced to convergent deterministic parameters \(B,r,h,\Sigma_T\).
The limiting covariance is nonsingular, and so is \(\operatorname{adj}(B)\).
After Gaussian whitening and orthogonal diagonalization, the quadratic part has a nonzero diagonal coefficient.
Conditioning on all other Gaussian coordinates leaves a nonzero quadratic polynomial of one continuous Gaussian coordinate, which has at most two roots.
The limiting polynomial has no atom at zero, including for indefinite \(B\), either pole sign and the limit \(r=0\).
Portmanteau for fixed closed windows, followed by shrinking the window and then letting \(R\) grow, proves the claimed uniform qualitative bound for every deterministic shrinking window.

### Same-row residual error and complete graph normalization

The rank-one determinant formula is polynomial and remains valid when \(S_E\) is singular.
On \(\|z\|\le R\) and \(\|S_E-B\|\le1\), determinant and adjugate are uniformly Lipschitz on the resulting bounded set of three-by-three matrices.
Because \(|r_N|\le c_0^{-1}\), the claimed error bound \(C_R\|S_E-B\|\) follows.
Conditional Markov and \(\mathbb E\|z\|^2\le\Lambda\), with \(N\) tending to infinity before \(R\), make this error uniformly small in probability.
No factorization of the joint law of \(S_E\) and \(z\) is used.
Uniform convergence of this error in probability permits a deterministic \(\eta_N\downarrow0\) such that its probability of exceeding \(\eta_N\) tends to zero uniformly.
The quadratic-window bound at \(C/N+\eta_N\) then gives \(\mathbb P\{|H_N|\le C/N\mid\mathcal F\}=o(1)\) uniformly.
Consequently no rate for \(q_N\to0\) is hidden in this step.

The conditional covariance of the border gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{Tr}(R^TE^2R)\mid\mathcal F]
 \le\frac{\Lambda}{N}\|E\|_F^2\le\Lambda K.
\]
Thus the two guards \(\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_E\le N\) and \(\|z\|^2\le N\) have total conditional failure \(O(N^{-1})\).
Since \(\Gamma_E\) is positive definite, these guards give \(\det\Gamma_E\le N^3\) and \(\|\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma_E)\|\le N^2\).
Using \(Ee=0\), the squared inverse has exactly the two stated terms, and the second rank-one determinant identity yields
\[
 (N/|\lambda|)^2\det\Gamma
 =r_N^2\det\Gamma_E+N z^T\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma_E)z
 \le C N^4.
\]
Hence \(|\det F|\le N^{-3}\) implies \(|H_N|\le C/N\) on these guards.
This estimate is uniform even for arbitrarily large \(|\lambda|\).

For the actual parent in block order \(\begin{pmatrix}D&R^T\\R&M\end{pmatrix}\), the columns other than the exposed physical block have full column rank because \(M\) is invertible.
Their orthogonal complement has basis
\[
 V:=\begin{pmatrix}I_3\\-M^{-1}R\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad V^TV=\Gamma.
\]
The matrix \(V\Gamma^{-1/2}\) is an orthonormal basis, and its transpose applied to the exposed column block is exactly \(F=\Gamma^{-1/2}S\).
This verifies the singular-value identification with \(Q_iC_i\), without assuming that the parent is invertible.
Moreover \(\|F\|\le\|C_i\|\le\|L_N+\theta_NI\|\).
On the displayed parent norm event, \(d_i\le B_0^{-2}N^{-4}\) therefore implies \(|\det F|\le d_i B_0^2N\le N^{-3}\).
The parent norm is intersected only after this deterministic implication, so it never alters the conditional border law.
Uniformity permits one deterministic \(\beta_N\to0\) and subsequent integration over the admitted conditionings.

### The fixed-norm minor branch

The decomposition implies \(Me=\lambda^{-1}e\), so its residual is eventually at most \(a/4\).
For any cut support \(J\), restriction of the eigenvector equation gives
\[
 a\|e_J\|\le a/4+B_*\|e_{J^c}\|.
\]
If \(\|e_{J^c}\|<\rho:=\min\{1/2,a/(4B_*)\}\), the left side is at least \(\sqrt3a/2\) and the right side at most \(a/2\), a contradiction.
There are at most \(\sqrt N\) blocks larger than \(N^{-1/4}\), and for large \(N\) they fit in a support of size \(\lfloor\delta(N-|D|)\rfloor\).
The set of all remaining blocks then has squared mass at least \(\rho^2\) and maximum block size at most \(N^{-1/4}\).
This set is measurable from the chosen minor eigenvector.

For conditional use, the cut conditions needed here involve this minor alone and are \(\mathcal F\)-measurable.
The simultaneous original constant-cut event bounds the probability that any of these minor conditions fails.
Charging that exponential exception requires no conditioning on the stronger full-parent event.
A fixed \(B_*\) is essential to the positive constant \(\rho\); a growing norm bound does not give this conclusion.
Finite second moments do not generally give such a fixed norm bound: an off-diagonal law with tail exponent strictly between two and four can have its largest normalized entry diverge in probability, and matrix norm dominates each entry.
The positive-frame setting does not remove this obstruction, since a heavy-tailed identity channel can coexist with bounded independent channels furnishing a positive frame.

### Parent averaging, the logarithm and the coupling quantifiers

The adopted C194v17 inequality applies to the original shifted parent, with \(b_N=B_0\sqrt N\) and \(u_N=c_*N^{-11/2}\).
Its small-residual hypothesis is satisfied for all large \(N\), and its distance threshold is
\[
 \frac{8b_NNu_N}{a_{\rm old}}
 =\frac{8B_0c_*}{a_{\rm old}}N^{-4}.
\]
Taking \(c_*\le a_{\rm old}/(8B_0^3)\) permits the proved distance estimate at every physical index.
Splitting each event according to \(\mathcal B_{i,N}\) leaves a fixed multiple of the mean branch failure plus \(\beta_N\), in addition to the parent norm and exponential rectangle exceptions.
There is no index-union loss and no requirement that the different distance events be independent.
The hypothesized mean coverage therefore gives the stated floor along \(\mathcal N\).

Weyl's inequality transfers this floor to the unshifted parent with constant \(c_*/2\) when \(\theta_N=o(N^{-11/2})\).
The weaker condition \(\theta_N\to0\) alone would not justify this transfer or the subsequent original-matrix logarithmic conclusion.
With the stronger shift condition retained, C187v3 applies directly to the unshifted original laws and coefficients.
There are only \(O(N^{3/4})\) bottom singular values outside its bound; on the transferred probability floor their normalized negative-log contribution is \(O(N^{-1/4}\log N)\).
The remaining negative tail is bounded by \(Ct(1+|\log t|)\) outside a summably small exception.
C186v4 supplies weak convergence of the singular laws and the positive-tail control for this fixed matrix pencil.
The normalization is \(1/(3N)\) throughout.
Applying these facts to a doubly clipped logarithm and then removing its two cutoffs proves convergence in probability along the deterministic infinite set.

When zero is outside the free spectrum, the limiting logarithm is finite.
The accepted regularized upper bound, or equivalently weak convergence with the positive-tail control and a fixed lower clipping, gives the all-dimension almost-sure upper limsup under every allowed coupling.
Probability convergence along \(\mathcal N\) allows a deterministic increasing \(N_k\in\mathcal N\) with lower-deviation probabilities at tolerance \(1/k\) bounded by \(2^{-k}\).
First Borel--Cantelli uses only these marginal probabilities and yields the reverse limsup for every cross-size coupling, exactly at the used scope of C190v1.
This argument gives neither summability on the full sequence nor its almost-sure logarithmic limit.
