### Exact source model and comparison with the original diagonal laws

JSS20 Theorem 1.3 requires a real symmetric matrix with independent identically distributed centered variance-one entries on and above the diagonal.
It does not require a symmetric scalar law, a fourth moment, a density or an algorithmic selection.
For each fixed input direction, replacing only the diagonal by independent copies of its own off-diagonal law therefore gives exactly that model.
The source bound \(C\sqrt{N/\varepsilon}\) becomes \(C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\) under the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
The union over the fixed number \(d\) of directions gives the stated \(2d e^{-c\varepsilon N}\) exception without requiring the separate modification events to be independent.

The zeroed source rectangle need not be principal or preserve symmetry.
Removing both its row and column index sets leaves a principal block on which the modified and original matrices coincide.
Compression of an arbitrary matrix is contractive for its Euclidean operator norm, so symmetry of the modified matrix is unnecessary.
Taking the union of these removed sets over the \(d\) inputs gives one common physical core and loses at most \(2d\lceil\varepsilon N\rceil\) coordinates.
With \(\varepsilon:=\eta/(4d)\), this is at most \(\eta N\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).

For every fixed finite-second-moment law, the displayed estimate
\[
 N\mathbb P\{|X|>\sqrt N/2\}
 \le4\mathbb E[X^2\mathbf1_{\{|X|>\sqrt N/2\}}]\longrightarrow0
\]
follows from tail integrability of \(X^2\).
The finite union over directions and coordinates consequently makes every normalized diagonal difference at most one, with exception at most the stated \(d_N\to0\).
No variance-one assumption on the original diagonal law is used in this comparison.
The existence of a common core satisfying the original-input norm tests is a Borel event of the original matrices alone.
A successful coupled construction implies this event, so integration over the auxiliary diagonals gives the same lower probability bound for that original event.
This step does not assert that a core selected using the auxiliary variables is itself measurable in the original matrices.
The finite enumeration of all qualifying original index sets supplies the claimed original-measurable selection separately.
