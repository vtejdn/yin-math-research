## Finding

All three bounds hold at their stated scopes.

For the spectral event, [C186v4](../../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#finite-variance-weak) gives convergence of expected masses on each of the finitely many fixed mesh intervals because the semicircle law has no atoms at their endpoints.
In the [independent row partition](../../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration), each change costs at most two in either cumulative count and hence at most four in an interval count.
Bounded differences gives an exponential error for a fixed positive mass margin, common to the finite mesh.
Principal-minor interlacing at both endpoints then works on this single event for every adaptive deletion of size \(d\), with a loss bounded by \(2d\) per interval.

After removing the coordinates with \(|h_i|+|(Bh)_i|>L/\sqrt N\), the norm guard limits \(d\) to \(2(1+K^2)N/L^2\).
The retained real channel in the one-channel case has squared norm at least \(v/2\), and the two-channel hypothesis lower bounds the full real Gram matrix.
For each pair of channels, the complete symmetric-direction covariance identity has \((i,j)\) entry \(\delta_{ij}\langle u,t\rangle+t_i u_j\).
The four products in the block expansion therefore give the displayed polynomial block and four rank-one errors.
The stacked omitted diagonal matrix has squared norm \(O_K(\ell L^2/N)\) by the coordinate cutoff, before the final \(N^{-1}\) covariance normalization.
For each eigenvalue of the minor, the determinant of its Gram block has degree \(2\ell\) and leading coefficient \(4^\ell\det\operatorname{Gram}(x_a)\).
A small least eigenvalue makes the determinant small since the other eigenvalues are bounded on the guard.
Factoring over \(\mathbb C\) places the eigenvalue of the minor within one of \(2\ell\) real windows of length \(2w\).
Removing those blocks and the range of the rank perturbation leaves the claimed count above \(\kappa/2\) for sufficiently large \(N\).

Coordinate restriction can only decrease singular values, and intersecting the good left singular space with the range of the real exterior projection loses at most \(2\ell|I|\) dimensions.
For a pair in \(I\times I\), the \(Ag\) output is supported on \(I\) and the \(J\)-output of \(BAh\) lies in \(\operatorname{ran} B_{JI}\).
Thus the exterior event is measurable in the entries outside \(I\times I\), and the interior conditional multiplication in the argument is valid at the [stated identification scope](../../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#one-color-root-reconstruction).
Each retained normalized column is \(O_K(L/N)\) since both endpoints pass the coordinate cutoff.
In \(m=\lfloor(\ell-\tau)N\rfloor\) directions the multiplier \(\sqrt N/a\) raises the covariance floor to one, the column bound to \(O(N^{-1/2})\), and the event radius to \(b/a\).
The independent surviving strict upper multipliers have a common finite truncated second moment of at least one half for a sufficiently large cutoff.
The discarded entries and all diagonal entries may be fixed independently, resulting in a translation.
At \(t=C'/\sqrt N\) both the short-column inequality and \(t\sqrt m\ge b/a\) hold for large fixed \(C'\).
Proposition 2.4 gives \((CC'/\sqrt N)^m\), with the fixed exponential factor absorbed by the slack from \((\ell-\tau)/2\) to \(\ell/2-\tau\).
The accepted input is the proposition itself, inspected on printed and PDF page 14 of [Koehler and Sohn, arXiv:2608.27372v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27372v1#page=14).
The source proof was outside this review.

For the phase input, successive conditional squarings give its sixteen-corner probability lower bound.
Degree four and the disjoint supports force one directed path using the four rectangles in the order \(B,A,B,A\), with coefficient one from \(ABAB\).
Conditioning on successive difference rectangles permits four applications of the [rectangular fourth-moment estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_F0196_v1.md#affine-small-ball), since the normalized raw differences have variance one and bounded fourth moment and each output has at least \(N/5\) independent rows.
The resulting exponential error survives the sixteenth root and the sparse net when the support fraction is sufficiently small.
On the guard the real polynomial \(H=(M-\bar z I)(M-zI)\) has bounded norm.
For each rotated near-mode the nonzero imaginary part of \(z\) gives a fixed positive lower bound for the norm of the real part, while \(H\) maps this real part to a vector of norm at most \((3K^2+|z|)\|(M-zI)h\|\).
The simultaneous sparse exclusion and normalization of a truncation yield exactly the deleted-phase lower bound used in v33.

At fixed nonreal \(z\) the \(B\)-measurable centers can be filtered using the deterministic Gram condition because every center within \(a_0/\sqrt N\) of a good guarded near-mode inherits a deleted real-phase norm of at least \(d-a_0/\sqrt N\).
The guard bounds the residual increase by \((3K^2+|z|)a_0/\sqrt N\).
The conditional two-channel exponent leaves \((\chi-\tau)N\log N\) of decay after summing the dictionary, with each exceptional event charged once.
For the partial alphabet, a mesh of spacing \(N^{-1}\) on a bounded square contributes \(O(\sqrt{s}/N)\) error over \(s=\lceil\gamma N\rceil\) sites.
Its \(k=\lfloor N^\alpha\rfloor\) center list costs \(o(N\log N)\) in log cardinality, while site labels and the complex tail contribute at most \((\alpha s+N-s)\log N+O(N)\).
Normalization costs at most twice the approximation error and the entropy saving persists at \(\gamma=1\) and \(\alpha=0\).
The resulting guarded bounds are summable for every coupling across sizes.
The norm-guard complement and roots outside these classes remain beyond the finding, as does the planar logarithmic defect.
