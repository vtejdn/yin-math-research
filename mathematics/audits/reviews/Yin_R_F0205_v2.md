### Original complex covariance and the positive weight

Direct multiplication verifies both companion border matrices.
For a right graph, the fresh raw coefficient vectors are \(\alpha_1\overline{Bu}+\alpha_3\overline u\) and \(2\alpha_1\overline{Au}+\alpha_2\overline u\).
For a left graph, they are \(2\alpha_1\overline{Bt}+\alpha_2\overline t\) and \(\alpha_1\overline{At}+2\alpha_3\overline t\).
Every fresh coordinate may have its original law or its conjugate, but the coordinates are independent under local-minor conditioning.

The lower auxiliary equations of \(Qv\) and \(Q^*w\) give the stated graph errors and positive root masses.
When \(\alpha_1\) is not small, conjugation and division by its nonzero fixed coefficients reduce the two coefficient norms to the all-complex-center joint gap.
When it is small, the remaining two root coefficients dominate the bounded \(A,B\) terms.
The perturbation from an exact graph to its actual low singular vector has coefficient norm at most \(Ca_*\|\alpha\|\), uniformly in dimension.
A sufficiently small fixed \(a_*\) therefore gives the covariance lower bound for every vector in the stated singular range.
The calculation uses centered second absolute moments; it does not require vanishing pseudo-variance or independent real and imaginary parts.

The left and right components of the Hermitian border are functions of the same raw entries.
Their independence is neither available nor used.
The exact positive weight
\[
 W_h=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
 h^2(QQ^*+h^2I)^{-1},\,h^2(Q^*Q+h^2I)^{-1}\right)
\]
makes their squared weighted norms add without cross terms.
Expansion in the two singular bases, including zero singular spaces, gives the required full auxiliary-direction covariance.

C186v4 applies to the fixed Hermitian matrix pencil, with scalar normalization \(1/(6N)\).
Each singular value occurs twice in its even trace, so the sum of its \(3n\) singular weights divided by \(N\) converges to \(3q(h)\).
A bounded physical deletion gives a principal submatrix of this same dilation.
The test \(h^2/(t^2+h^2)\) has finite total variation, so interlacing gives uniformity over all the bounded deletions without a union over weak-convergence probabilities.
The high singular weights are at most \(3Nh^2/a_*^2\).
Since \(J(h)\) diverges, the chosen \(h_0\) makes this loss at most one quarter of the main trace scale.
Thus the lower covariance is \(cNq(h)\), with constants independent of fixed \(h\le h_0\).

### Fixed cutoff, concentration and lawful conditioning

### Root singular residuals and singular-head graphs

For a retained root singular residual \(E\), the identities \(ME=\sum v_jv_j^*\) and \(EM=\sum u_ju_j^*\) are orthogonal projections, including choices of singular bases inside ties.
The graph identities
\[
 Q_DW=\begin{bmatrix}M\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 VQ_D=\begin{bmatrix}M&0&0\end{bmatrix}
\]
therefore bound both \(Q_DWEV\) and \(WEVQ_D\) in operator norm by \(C_K\).

The two high-space identities in the proof have the correct singular orientations.
For \(Q_D=X\Sigma Y^*\), they are obtained respectively by left multiplication with \(Y_>\Sigma_>^{-1}X_>^*\) and right multiplication with the same inverse map.
They bound \((I-P_R)T\) and \(T(I-P_L)\) separately.
No commutation of \(T=WEV\) with either projector and no orthogonality of the two graph pieces is assumed.
The two-term square inequality gives the row-column bound and, separately, the physical-diagonal bound with high contribution \(O_K(Nh^{-2})\).

Removing at most \(k_N\) root singular terms makes \(W(G-E)V\) have rank at most \(k_N\).
The elementary rank-approximation inequality applies to the actual companion inverse and gives
\[
 \left(\sum_{j>k_N}\mathfrak q_{j,D}^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le C_K\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\sqrt{2n}.
\]
C197v1 makes its left side divided by \(\sqrt N\) diverge uniformly.
This proves the required own-scale divergence for all such root residuals and justifies the two limiting steps in their spread estimates.
Extracting \(E,BE,2EB,2BEB\) from \(WEV\) gives precisely the asserted blocks.

After removing the first \(j-1\) root inverse modes, the remaining energy is at most \(nq_j^2\).
Its divergence relative to \(N\) gives \(q_j\to\infty\), uniformly for each fixed head index.
The exact pairs satisfy \(Mu_j=q_j^{-1}v_j\) and \(M^*v_j=q_j^{-1}u_j\).
Their graph images have bounded norm, high singular part at most \(1/(hq_j)\), and low physical part at most \(C_K\sqrt{\rho(h)}\).
This proves all six graph-vector coordinate bounds, uniformly over any fixed number of heads and tied-space choices.
