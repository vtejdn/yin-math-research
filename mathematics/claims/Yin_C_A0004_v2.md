## Statement

Let \(G\) be either \(\mathbb Z^2=\langle u,v\rangle\) or \(F(u,v)\), with canonical group trace \(\tau_G\), and set
\[
b_1=1-u,\quad b_2=1-v,\quad a=v+v^{-1},\quad
D=\binom{b_1^*}{b_2^*},\quad L=D^*D=4-u-u^{-1}-v-v^{-1},
\qquad H=\begin{pmatrix}a&D^*\\D&0_2\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]
All matrix traces are unnormalized unless explicitly divided by three.
Let \(P_e\) denote the three-coordinate identity-cell projection, and, for \(z\in\mathbb C_+\), define
\[
G_e(z)=P_e(z-H)^{-1}P_e,\qquad
\mathcal K_e(z)=zI_3-G_e(z)^{-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
The [first-return convention](../claims/Yin_C_A0004_v1.md#complete-first-return) is \(s_P(t)^{-1}=I-tf_P(t)\), so \(\mathcal K_e(z)=f_{P_e}(1/z)\).

The following statements hold.

1. Both models have complete zero projection \(0\oplus P\), where
   \[
   P=I_2-\operatorname*{s-lim}_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}D(L+\varepsilon)^{-1}D^*,\qquad
   (\operatorname{Tr}_2\otimes\tau_G)(P)=1.
   \tag{3}
   \]
   Their normalized zero atom is \(1/3\).
   Their edge-cell residues are, respectively,
   \[
   C_Z=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-(1/2-1/\pi)\\-(1/2-1/\pi)&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
   C_F=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1/6\\-1/6&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
   \tag{4}
   \]
   Both have rank two and trace one; neither is a projection.
2. The complete Green and first-return matrices are (6)--(8) in the lattice case and (17)--(18) in the free case, with the specified physical branches.
   The lattice complement is absolutely continuous on \([-4,0]\cup[\sqrt5-1,\sqrt5+1]\), and its boundary is (10)--(15).
   The free zero atom is isolated by the positive bound (20), with analytic complementary Green matrix and expansions (21)--(22).
3. In the lattice case, the kernel is densely generated by translates of one finitely supported cycle, but its orthogonal projection has no continuous Fourier representative and does not belong to \(M_2(C_r^*(\mathbb Z^2))\).
   In the free case, there is no nonzero finitely supported kernel vector, while the projection has the exact exponentially decaying coefficients (19) and admits finite-propagation approximations in operator norm at a geometric rate.
4. The coefficient identity (23), the collective-holonomy condition below, the general gap criterion (24), and the factored edge-perturbation condition (25) hold under their separately stated hypotheses.
   They give quantitative projection constraints and additional sufficient conditions while retaining the obstruction to arbitrary group-ring integrality.

## Argument

<a id="common-block-and-projection"></a>
### Common block formula and the complete projection

The two-loop graph has one vertex, and its loop labels are \(u^{-1}\) and \(v^{-1}\).
The [accepted incidence certificate](../claims/Yin_C_A0004_v1.md#geometric-integer-certificate), using [C_A0003's actual transport theorem](../claims/Yin_C_A0003_v1.md#finite-graph-certificate), gives \(\ker D=0\) and \(\dim_G\ker D^*=2-1=1\).
For \(H(x,y)=0\), its lower equation forces \(x=0\); hence \(\ker H=0\oplus\ker D^*\), independently of \(a\).
Write the polar decomposition \(D=V L^{1/2}\).
Injectivity gives \(V^*V=I\), and \(VV^*\) projects onto \(\overline{\operatorname{ran}D}\).
Since \(L(L+\varepsilon)^{-1}\to I\) strongly, (3) equals \(I_2-VV^*\) and is the complete orthogonal projection.
In particular, \(L^{-1}\) in a later projection formula is not silently assumed bounded in the lattice case.

Define the bounded inverse
\[
Q(z)=(z^2-za-L)^{-1}.
\]
For nonreal \(z\), its existence follows directly from
\(\operatorname{Im}(z-a-L/z)=(\operatorname{Im}z)(I+L/|z|^2)\) in the upper half-plane, with the adjoint estimate giving surjectivity.
Eliminating the lower block gives, with the displayed order of noncommuting factors,
\[
(z-H)^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}
zQ&QD^*\\DQ&z^{-1}(I_2+DQD^*)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]
The finite matrix \(-\operatorname{Im}G_e(z)\) is positive definite, so the inverse in (2) exists.
The full leaf-layer first-return operator is also explicit:
\(f_{\rm leaves}(t)=tD(I-ta)^{-1}D^*\).
It is a different compression from (2), and its entrywise trace cannot replace the inverse of \(G_e\).

<a id="lattice-complete-matrices"></a>
### Lattice: complete matrix formulas

Under Fourier transformation put
\[
x=|1-e^{i\theta}|^2,\quad y=|1-e^{i\phi}|^2,\quad a=2-y,\quad L=x+y.
\]
The variables \(x,y\) are independent with probability density
\(d\nu(t)=\mathbf1_{(0,4)}(t)dt/(\pi\sqrt{t(4-t)})\).
Set
\[
Q_z(x,y)=\frac1{z^2-2z-x-(1-z)y},\qquad
q=\mathbb E Q_z,\quad X=\mathbb E(xQ_z),\quad
Y=\mathbb E(yQ_z),\quad Z=\mathbb E(xyQ_z).
\]
Odd sine terms integrate to zero separately in each variable.
Thus (5) becomes the explicit full-cell formula
\[
G_{e,Z}(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
zq&X/2&Y/2\\
X/2&(1+X)/z&Z/(4z)\\
Y/2&Z/(4z)&(1+Y)/z
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6}
\]
Write
\[
r=\tfrac12(X,Y)^{\mathsf T},\quad
M=\begin{pmatrix}1+X&Z/4\\Z/4&1+Y\end{pmatrix},\quad
h=q-r^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}r.
\tag{7}
\]
Here and below the transposes in analytic matrix formulas are bilinear transposes, not conjugate transposes.
The edge-cell Green compression \(M/z\) has strictly negative imaginary part, so \(M\) is invertible; the full Green inverse then implies \(h\ne0\).
The complete first-return matrix is
\[
\mathcal K_{e,Z}(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
z-(zh)^{-1}&r^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}/h\\
M^{-1}r/h&z(I_2-M^{-1})-(z/h)M^{-1}rr^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (6)--(8) use explicit integrals of the known Fourier symbol rather than an equation involving an unknown full resolvent.

The edge projection in (3) has almost-everywhere symbol
\[
P_Z(\theta,\phi)
=I_2-\frac1{x+y}
\begin{pmatrix}x&\bar b_1 b_2\\\bar b_2 b_1&y\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{9}
\]
Its diagonal averages are \(1/2\), and its off-diagonal average is
\(-\tfrac14\mathbb E[xy/(x+y)]\).
For fixed \(y>0\),
\(\mathbb E_x[x/(x+y)]=1-\sqrt{y/(y+4)}\).
Integration in \(y\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\frac{xy}{x+y}
=2-\frac1\pi\int_0^4\frac{y\,dy}{\sqrt{16-y^2}}
=2-\frac4\pi.
\]
This proves \(C_Z\) in (4), whose eigenvalues are \(1/\pi\) and \(1-1/\pi\).

<a id="lattice-boundary"></a>
### Lattice: the logarithmic boundary and one-sided accumulation

All asymptotics in this subsection are along \(z=i\eta\), \(\eta\downarrow0\), unless a real spectral variable is specified.
The full matrix expansion is determined by
\[
q(i\eta)=-\frac1{4\pi}\log\frac{16}{\eta}+\frac i8
+O\bigl(\eta\log(1/\eta)\bigr),
\tag{10}
\]
\[
\begin{split}
X(z)&=-\tfrac12-zq(z)-z/(2\pi)+o(z),\\
Y(z)&=-\tfrac12-zq(z)-z\bigl(\tfrac12-1/(2\pi)\bigr)+o(z),\\
Z(z)&=-4\kappa+z(3/\pi-1)+o(z),\qquad \kappa=\tfrac12-1/\pi.
\end{split}
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, the root-edge entries approach \(-1/4\), the root entry is \(zq\to0\), and
\[
G_{e,Z;EE}(z)=\frac{C_Z}{z}-q(z)I_2+
\begin{pmatrix}
-1/(2\pi)&(3/\pi-1)/4\\
(3/\pi-1)/4&-1/2+1/(2\pi)
\end{pmatrix}+o(1).
\tag{12}
\]

For a justified endpoint estimate in (10), compare \(Q_z\) with \(-(L+2z)^{-1}\).
At \(z=i\eta\), both denominator moduli are bounded below by a fixed positive constant times \(L+\eta\).
For the true denominator, use \(2-y\ge1\) when \(L\le1\), and its real part \(-L-\eta^2\) when \(L\ge1\).
Since \(y\le L\), the resolvent difference has expectation bounded by
\[
C\eta\,\mathbb E\frac{L}{(L+\eta)^2}
+C\eta^2\mathbb E\frac1{(L+\eta)^2}
=O\bigl(\eta\log(1/\eta)\bigr).
\]
The density of \(L=x+y\) is bounded near zero: the convolution of the two inverse-square-root endpoint densities has a finite limit there.
Away from zero the displayed denominators have a fixed lower bound, which suffices even at the middle spectral singularity.
The [exact square-lattice Green formula](../ledger/Yin_L_A0016_v1.md#exact-tree-and-lattice-transforms) and [DLMF 19.12.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/19.12.E1) give
\[
-\mathbb E(L+2z)^{-1}
=-\frac1{4\pi}\log(16/z)+O\bigl(z\log z\bigr).
\]
Here \(k'=\sqrt{1-[4/(4+2z)]^2}\), and the convergent DLMF series uses the principal logarithm and \(d(0)=2\log2\).
It is valid for small nonzero complex \(k'\), so it applies to this imaginary-axis approach and supplies the sign \(+i/8\) in (10).

For (11), direct algebra gives
\(X+(1-z)Y=(z^2-2z)q-1\).
Symmetrizing the difference under \(x\leftrightarrow y\) gives
\[
\frac{X-Y}{z}\longrightarrow
\frac12\mathbb E\frac{(x-y)^2}{(x+y)^2}
=\frac12-\frac1\pi=\kappa.
\]
The denominator lower bounds above justify dominated convergence, since \((x-y)^2\le L^2\).
The needed integral is
\[
\mathbb E\frac{xy}{(x+y)^2}
=\frac2\pi\int_0^4\frac{dy}{(y+4)^{3/2}\sqrt{4-y}}
=\frac1{2\pi}.
\]
The first equality follows by differentiating the preceding fixed-\(y\) formula for \(\mathbb E_x[x/(x+y)]\), and the second by \(y=4\sin^2t\).
Also,
\[
\frac{Z(z)-Z(0)}z\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\frac{xy(2-y)}{L^2}
=\frac1\pi-\tfrac12(2-4/\pi)=3/\pi-1.
\]
The bound \(xy\le L^2/4\) justifies this last limit.
These identities prove (11)--(12), not merely their traces.

Let \(P_+=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\) and \(P_-=I_2-P_+\).
Since \(M\to C_Z\), \(r\to-\tfrac14(1,1)^{\mathsf T}\), and \(h=q-\pi/8+o(1)\), (8) yields
\[
\begin{split}
(\mathcal K_{e,Z})_{00}(i\eta)&\sim-\frac{4\pi i}{\eta\log(16/\eta)},\\
(\mathcal K_{e,Z})_{01}(i\eta),\ (\mathcal K_{e,Z})_{02}(i\eta)
&\sim\frac{\pi^2}{\log(16/\eta)},\\
(\mathcal K_{e,Z})_{EE}(i\eta)
&=-i\eta\bigl((\pi-1)P_++(\pi-1)^{-1}P_-\bigr)
+O\bigl(\eta/\log(1/\eta)\bigr).
\end{split}
\tag{13}
\]
The exact trace identity is
\(\operatorname{Tr}_3G_{e,Z}(z)=z^{-1}+(2z-2)q+Y\).
Consequently the normalized scalar transform is
\[
g_{H,Z}(i\eta)
=\frac1{3i\eta}+\frac1{6\pi}\log\frac{16}{\eta}
-\frac16-\frac i{12}+o(1).
\tag{14}
\]

The nonzero fiber eigenvalues are
\[
\lambda_\pm(x,y)=\frac{2-y\pm\sqrt{(2-y)^2+4(x+y)}}2.
\]
The positive root is greater than one, since its characteristic polynomial at one is \(-1-x<0\).
Implicit differentiation gives
\(\partial_x\lambda=(2\lambda-a)^{-1}\) and
\(\partial_y\lambda=(1-\lambda)/(2\lambda-a)\).
Thus the negative branch decreases in both variables, while the positive branch increases in \(x\) and decreases in \(y\).
The corner values give the exact bands \([-4,0]\) and \([\sqrt5-1,\sqrt5+1]\).
For fixed \(y\), each branch has nonzero derivative in \(x\), proving absolute continuity of its pushforward law.
The only zero contact is \((\theta,\phi)=(0,0)\), where
\(\lambda_-=-(\theta^2+\phi^2)/2+O((\theta^2+\phi^2)^2)\).
In polar coordinates, solving \(-\lambda_-(r,\omega)=\varepsilon\) gives \(r^2=2\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)\), with the same differentiated error uniformly in angle.
The Haar area factor \((2\pi)^{-2}\) therefore gives the unnormalized continuous density and normalized small-window mass
\[
\rho_Z(-\varepsilon)=\frac1{2\pi}+O(\varepsilon),\quad
\rho_Z(+\varepsilon)=0\quad(0<\varepsilon<\sqrt5-1),\qquad
\mu_{H,Z}((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
=\frac13+\frac\varepsilon{6\pi}+O(\varepsilon^2).
\tag{15}
\]
The full operator residue converges strongly but not in norm: its restriction to the injective active complement has norm one for every \(\eta>0\), because zero belongs to its spectrum.
The single-cell residue does converge in finite-dimensional matrix norm to \(0\oplus C_Z\).

<a id="free-complete-matrices"></a>
### Free group: complete tree-cavity matrices

Put \(S=u+u^{-1}+v+v^{-1}\), \(w=4-z^2\), and \(t=1-z\).
Then \(Q=-R\), where
\(R=[w-(u+u^{-1})-t(v+v^{-1})]^{-1}\).
The physical cavity functions satisfy
\[
h_u=(w-h_u-2t^2h_v)^{-1},\qquad
h_v=(w-2h_u-t^2h_v)^{-1},\qquad
g=(w-2h_u-2t^2h_v)^{-1}.
\tag{16}
\]
They are actual diagonal inverses on the two deleted-edge subtrees, with
\(h_u(0)=h_v(0)=1/3\), \(g(0)=3/8\).
Equivalently, their large imaginary-parameter branch has \(h_u,h_v\sim-1/z^2\).
Every such subtree has \(L_{\rm sub}=4I-S_{\rm sub}\ge(4-2\sqrt3)I\), by the [accepted four-regular-tree spectral bound](../ledger/Yin_L_A0016_v1.md#exact-tree-and-lattice-transforms) and compression.
The imaginary-part estimate used for (5) also applies to \(a_{\rm sub}\) and \(L_{\rm sub}\), so all these cavity inverses exist analytically throughout \(\mathbb C_+\).
The factors in (16) are \(t^2\), not \(|t|^2\): both oriented coefficients in the analytic pencil are \(t\).
Thus (16) has an operator-defined physical branch, not an arbitrary root selection of algebraic equations.

Set \(x_F=h_u\), \(y_F=t h_v\), \(c=(1-x_F,1-y_F)^{\mathsf T}\).
Tree separation along the unique reduced-word path gives the coefficient of \(R\) at a word with \(n_u,n_v\) letters of the two types as \(g x_F^{n_u}y_F^{n_v}\).
This follows by successive off-diagonal Schur elimination, each crossing contributing its edge weight times its forward cavity diagonal.
Taking the coefficients of words of length zero, one and two in (5) gives
\[
G_{e,F}(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-zg&-gc_1&-gc_2\\
-gc_1&(1-2gc_1)/z&-gc_1c_2/z\\
-gc_2&-gc_1c_2/z&(1-2gc_2)/z
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{17}
\]
Let
\(M_F=\begin{pmatrix}1-2gc_1&-gc_1c_2\\-gc_1c_2&1-2gc_2\end{pmatrix}\)
and \(W=g+g^2c^{\mathsf T}M_F^{-1}c\).
The same strict-imaginary-part argument as before gives \(M_F^{-1}\) and \(W\ne0\) in \(\mathbb C_+\).
The complete first-return matrix is
\[
\begin{split}
(\mathcal K_{e,F})_{00}&=z+(zW)^{-1},\\
(\mathcal K_{e,F})_{0E}&=(g/W)c^{\mathsf T}M_F^{-1},\\
(\mathcal K_{e,F})_{E0}&=(g/W)M_F^{-1}c,\\
(\mathcal K_{e,F})_{EE}
&=z\bigl[I_2-M_F^{-1}+(g^2/W)M_F^{-1}cc^{\mathsf T}M_F^{-1}\bigr].
\end{split}
\tag{18}
\]

<a id="free-projection-and-gap"></a>
### Free group: the nonlocal projection and the isolated atom

The bound \(L\ge\gamma I\), \(\gamma=4-2\sqrt3>0\), makes (3) the bounded formula \(P_F=I_2-DL^{-1}D^*\).
The regular-tree Green formula at spectral parameter four gives
\(\kappa_F(h)=\tfrac38\,3^{-|h|}\) for the coefficient of \(L^{-1}\).
For \(g_1=u\), \(g_2=v\), all edge-projection coefficients are therefore
\[
(P_F)_{ij}(h)=\delta_{ij}\delta_{h,e}-\kappa_F(h)
+\kappa_F(g_i h)+\kappa_F(hg_j^{-1})
-\kappa_F(g_i h g_j^{-1}).
\tag{19}
\]
At \(h=e\), this is \(C_F\) in (4), with eigenvalues \(1/3,2/3\).
For example, \((P_F)_{11}(v^n)=-3^{-n}/6\ne0\) for every \(n\ge1\), so the projection has infinite support.
It has pointwise exponential coefficient decay, but no absolute summability is inferred from that bound on a group with exponential volume growth.

The polar isometry identifies the active complement with
\(B_{\rm act}=\begin{pmatrix}a&L^{1/2}\\L^{1/2}&0\end{pmatrix}\).
Since \(\|a\|=2\), for real \(\lambda\) with \(|\lambda|<\delta_0\),
\(L+\lambda a-\lambda^2\ge(\gamma-2|\lambda|-\lambda^2)I>0\), where
\[
\delta_0=\sqrt{5-2\sqrt3}-1>0,\qquad
\sigma(H)\cap(-\delta_0,\delta_0)=\{0\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Hence the complementary resolvent is analytic across zero and
\(\|i\eta(i\eta-H)^{-1}-(0\oplus P_F)\|=O(\eta)\).
No assertion about the entire free-model spectral type away from this gap is needed.

Differentiating (16) at zero gives
\(h_u'=-1/5\), \(h_v'=-2/15\), \(x_F'=-1/5\), \(y_F'=-7/15\), and \(g'=-9/32\).
Substitution into (17) gives the complete local expansion
\[
\begin{split}
(G_{e,F})_{00}&=-3z/8+O(z^2),\\
(G_{e,F})_{01}&=-1/4+9z/80+O(z^2),\\
(G_{e,F})_{02}&=-1/4+z/80+O(z^2),\\
(G_{e,F})_{EE}&=\frac{C_F}{z}
+\begin{pmatrix}9/40&-1/24\\-1/24&1/40\end{pmatrix}+O(z),\\
g_{H,F}(z)&=\frac1{3z}+\frac1{12}+O(z).
\end{split}
\tag{21}
\]
Here \(g_{H,F}=\operatorname{Tr}_3G_{e,F}/3\).
In (18), \(W(0)=3/4\) and \(W'(0)=-9/16\), so
\[
\begin{split}
(\mathcal K_{e,F})_{00}&=4/(3z)+1+O(z),\\
(\mathcal K_{e,F})_{01}&=1+O(z^2),\\
(\mathcal K_{e,F})_{02}&=1+z/2+O(z^2),\\
(\mathcal K_{e,F})_{EE}&=-zI_2/2+O(z^2).
\end{split}
\tag{22}
\]
In particular, a first-return pole in the root coordinate does not imply an atom in that coordinate: its root Green function vanishes at zero.

<a id="projection-locality-and-index"></a>
### What the index controls about a nonlocal projection

For a projection \(P\in M_E(\mathcal N(G))\), let \(P(g)\) be its matrix of group Fourier coefficients and \(C=P(e)\).
Equivalently, \(P_{ij}(g)=\langle P_{ij}\delta_e,\delta_g\rangle\).
Parseval's identity applied to each entry and \(PP^*=P\) give the matrix identity
\[
C-C^2=\sum_{g\ne e}P(g)P(g)^*,\qquad
\sum_{g\ne e}\|P(g)\|_{\rm HS}^2
=\operatorname{Tr}C-\operatorname{Tr}C^2.
\tag{23}
\]
The positive matrix series converges entrywise, with absolutely convergent scalar entries by Cauchy--Schwarz.
This is a necessary exact relation between the local compression and all off-cell coefficients, not a way to assign arbitrary projection data.
For an incidence certificate with \(\operatorname{Tr}C=r=E-N\), its right-hand trace is at most \(r-r^2/E\), by \(\operatorname{Tr}C^2\ge r^2/E\).
It vanishes exactly when every off-cell coefficient vanishes.
For the present two laws the off-cell squared masses are
\[
\frac2\pi\left(1-\frac1\pi\right)\quad\hbox{for }\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad \frac49\quad\hbox{for }F_2.
\]
Thus the same integer trace coexists with different nonlocal mass, full-rank local residues and noninteger local eigenvalues.

The lattice cycle vector \((-b_2,b_1)^{\mathsf T}\) is finitely supported and lies in \(\ker D^*\).
Almost every Fourier fiber of the kernel is its one-dimensional span, so the closure of its polynomial translates is the whole kernel.
More explicitly, the closure of the range of its bounded column multiplication is the orthogonal complement of its adjoint kernel, which is exactly this measurable fiber line; trigonometric polynomials are dense in the input \(L^2\) space.
Nevertheless (9) has different directional limits at \((0,0)\), already in its diagonal entries \(y/(x+y)\) and \(x/(x+y)\).
It cannot have a continuous representative, since any such representative would agree with the continuous symbol off that point and inherit the incompatible limits.
The identification \(C_r^*(\mathbb Z^2)=C(\mathbb T^2)\) follows here directly from Fourier transformation and uniform closure of trigonometric polynomials.
Hence \(P_Z\notin M_2(C_r^*(\mathbb Z^2))\); in particular its coefficients are not absolutely summable and it is not finite propagation.

For the free group, \(L^{-1}=\tfrac14\sum_{n\ge0}(S/4)^n\) converges in norm because \(\|S/4\|=\sqrt3/2<1\).
Truncating produces finite-propagation approximations to \(P_F\) at a geometric norm rate, proving \(P_F\in M_2(C_r^*(F_2))\).
The approximating operators need not be projections.
In contrast, \(\ker D^*\) contains no nonzero finitely supported vector, by the [finite-forest leaf argument](../claims/Yin_C_A0004_v1.md#noncompact-edge-modes).
The two examples therefore distinguish finite-support generation of a kernel from regularity of its orthogonal projection in both directions.

There is a finite probabilistic interpretation of (23).
For the two cell edges and the positive contraction \(C\), define the determinantal subset law by inclusion probabilities \(\mathbb P(F\subset\Xi)=\det C_F\).
Explicitly its two-edge and empty probabilities are \(\det C\) and \(\det(I-C)\), with singleton probabilities \(C_{ii}-\det C\).
These are nonnegative and sum to one for the two matrices (4).
Then \(\mathbb E|\Xi|=\operatorname{Tr}C=1\), while
\(\operatorname{Var}|\Xi|=\operatorname{Tr}(C-C^2)\) is exactly the off-cell squared mass in (23).
Thus the integer mean does not force an almost surely fixed number of occupied cell edges.
This uses the standard determinantal convention, with no inference that an arbitrary expected integer proves the group-ring conjecture.

<a id="collective-holonomy-condition"></a>
### Infinite collective holonomy as a broader geometric certificate

Let \(G\) now be any discrete group, with no torsion-free assumption, and let \(D_\Gamma\) be a finite regular-transport incidence matrix with the positive weights and unit phases of C_A0003.
In each connected component choose a spanning tree and root.
The tree equations express every vertex coordinate as \(x_i=W_i\xi\), where \(W_i\) is a phase times a left regular group unitary.
For each remaining edge the equation is \(\eta_e\lambda_G(k_e)\xi=\xi\).
Let \(K\le G\) be the subgroup generated by these actual loop group labels \(k_e\).
If \(K\) is infinite in every connected component, then \(D_\Gamma\) is injective.
Indeed, each loop equation forces the absolute value of \(\xi\) to be invariant under the corresponding group translation.
It is then constant on every \(K\)-orbit in \(G\), all of which are infinite, so square summability forces \(\xi=0\).
No consistency assumption on the phases is needed for this implication; inconsistent phases can only impose further zero conditions.
This proves the same atom formula \(E-N\) for \(\begin{pmatrix}A&D_\Gamma^*\\D_\Gamma&0\end{pmatrix}\), for every bounded selfadjoint \(A\).
The criterion is independent of the choice of tree, since changing root and tree conjugates the holonomy subgroup.
As before, actual group relations must be used to certify infinitude; formal nonempty words alone do not suffice.

For example, if an infinite group is generated by \(g_1,\ldots,g_r\), then
\(D=(1-g_1^{-1},\ldots,1-g_r^{-1})^{\mathsf T}\)
is injective and the associated block has zero atom dimension \(r-1\).
This remains true when individual generators have finite order.
It broadens the geometric certificate while also showing that this structured integer atom alone cannot characterize torsion-freeness or prove the general Atiyah contract.
For finite holonomy subgroups, the orbit argument supplies no such injectivity conclusion.

<a id="gap-and-deformation-conditions"></a>
### Further sufficient conditions: gap, regularity and edge perturbations

Let \(D\in M_{E,N}(\mathcal N(G))\) be bounded and injective, let \(A=A^*\in M_N(\mathcal N(G))\), and put \(L=D^*D\).
For \(H_A=\begin{pmatrix}A&D^*\\D&0\end{pmatrix}\), the polar isometry identifies its active complement with \(\begin{pmatrix}A&L^{1/2}\\L^{1/2}&0\end{pmatrix}\).
One has the exact equivalence
\[
0\notin\sigma(H_A|_{(\ker H_A)^\perp})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L\ge\gamma I_N\ \hbox{for some }\gamma>0.
\tag{24}
\]
If the lower bound holds, the active operator has bounded inverse
\(\begin{pmatrix}0&L^{-1/2}\\L^{-1/2}&-L^{-1/2}AL^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}\).
If also \(\|A\|\le a_0\), the gap contains the interval with radius
\((\sqrt{a_0^2+4\gamma}-a_0)/2\), by the same Schur inequality used in (20).
Conversely, if there is no lower bound, choose unit vectors \(f_n\) with \(\|L^{1/2}f_n\|\to0\).
The vectors \((0,Vf_n)\) lie in the active complement and satisfy \(\|H_A(0,Vf_n)\|\to0\), ruling out a gap.
This condition controls boundary regularity independently of the already fixed integer index.

If \(D\) is finite propagation and \(\gamma I\le L\le MI\), then
\[
L^{-1}=M^{-1}\sum_{n\ge0}(I-L/M)^n
\]
gives finite-propagation approximations to \(P=I-DL^{-1}D^*\).
The error after degree \(n\) is bounded by
\(\|D\|^2\gamma^{-1}(1-\gamma/M)^{n+1}\).
When \(L=MI\), the constant inverse is exact and the series terminates.
Thus a verified incidence energy gap gives operator-norm locality in addition to the integer trace, without an assumption about individual projection coefficients.

The integer atom survives a class of genuine edge-edge interactions.
Let \(C_0=C_0^*\in M_N(\mathcal N(G))\), assume \(I_N-AC_0\) is injective, and define
\[
\widetilde H=
\begin{pmatrix}A&D^*\\D&DC_0D^*\end{pmatrix}.
\quad\text{Then}\quad
\ker\widetilde H=0\oplus\ker D^*.
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed, its lower equation is \(D(x+C_0D^*y)=0\), hence \(x=-C_0D^*y\).
Its upper equation becomes \((I-AC_0)D^*y=0\), so \(D^*y=0\) and \(x=0\).
An independently checkable sufficient condition is \(\|A\|\|C_0\|<1\), which makes \(I-AC_0\) boundedly invertible by its Neumann series.
For finite group-ring coefficients this norm condition can be certified by the finite coefficient-sum bounds on operator norms.
Applied to a nonflat finite transport incidence matrix, (25) preserves both the actual projection and the atom dimension \(E-N\), even without a gap.
For the present models, \(C_0=\epsilon I\) with real \(|\epsilon|<1/2\) supplies explicit additional group-ring matrices with the same nonlocal projection.
This is a sufficient structured perturbation condition, not invariance under arbitrary edge potentials.

<a id="determinant-calibration-and-limits"></a>
### A determinant calibration and the remaining limits

The exact finite-cell determinant is \(\det G_{e,Z}=\det(M)h/z\) on the lattice and \(\det G_{e,F}=-\det(M_F)W/z\) on the free group.
Thus
\[
\det G_{e,Z}(i\eta)\sim
-\frac{\det C_Z}{4\pi i\eta}\log(16/\eta),\qquad
\det G_{e,F}(z)\sim-\frac1{6z}.
\]
Both have boundary growth exponent one, although their local residue matrices have rank two: a vanishing root direction cancels one of the two edge pole directions, with a logarithmic factor remaining on the lattice.
This is a calculation for these models, not a general identification of \(\det G_e\) with the group-operator determinant or its logarithmic derivative with the full spectral trace.

The index provides the trace and the projection relations, not the rationality of individual local eigenvalues, finite support, a spectral gap or a universal boundary exponent.
The distinct-coupling comparison establishes each of these separations explicitly.
The extension in (25) handles an additional structured edge block, but no conversion of every group-ring matrix into these forms has been established.
Flat transport directions and unrestricted couplings retain the [previous arbitrary-kernel obstruction](../claims/Yin_C_A0004_v1.md#scope-and-obstruction).
