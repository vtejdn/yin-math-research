# Infinite commuting projection orbits and exact path atoms

Date: 2026-11-11 (Asia/Hong_Kong).
This continuation of the [rank-one height model](../claims/Yin_C_A0005_v1.md#statement) serves [S_A0006](../strategies/Yin_S_A0006.md) under the unchanged [A0001 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract).
It resolves the zero-potential projection-middle specialization with arbitrary commuting translates, including infinitely many distinct translates.
The preceding version retains its general singular-middle, finite-section and noncommuting-transport results.
Provenance: project deduction by the main agent with two development helpers, with the lamplighter comparison and its arbitrary-parameter construction checked against LNW08 at the exact scope recorded below.
No historical priority, new Atiyah group class or Task completion is asserted.

## Statement

**Definition (commuting transport orbit and finite-path starts).**
Let \(G=N\rtimes_\sigma\langle t\rangle\), where \(t\) has infinite order and \(\sigma=\operatorname{Ad}(t)\) on \(M_0=\mathcal N(N)\).
Use the canonical trace \(\tau=\tau_G\), its restriction \(\tau_N\), and unnormalized matrix traces.
Let \(p\in M_0\) be a projection whose translates commute pairwise, and define
\[
p_j=\sigma^{-j}(p),\qquad
e_\ell=(1-p_{-1})\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}p_j\right)(1-p_\ell)
\quad(\ell\ge1),\qquad
p_\infty=\bigwedge_{j\in\mathbb Z}p_j.
\tag{1}
\]
The products and countable meets are in the abelian von Neumann algebra generated by the \(p_j\).
The projection \(e_\ell\) specifies a retained path of exactly \(\ell\) vertices starting at zero, with deleted neighbors at \(-1\) and \(\ell\).
Set
\[
T=t+t^{-1},\qquad
H_p=\begin{pmatrix}T&1-p\\1-p&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad R=pTp\ \text{on }p\ell^2(G).
\tag{2}
\]
The trace of a kernel inside the \(p\)-corner is not renormalized.
Write \(\nu_G(H_p)=(\operatorname{Tr}_2\otimes\tau)(1_{\{0\}}(H_p))\) and \(\nu_p(R)=\tau(1_{\{0\}}(R|_p))\).

1. There is an orthogonal strong decomposition
   \[
   p=p_\infty+\sum_{\ell\ge1}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\sigma^j(e_\ell),
   \qquad
   \tau(p)=\tau(p_\infty)+\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell\,\tau(e_\ell).
   \tag{3}
   \]
   Half-infinite retained components have zero projection.
   The residual on \(p_\infty\) has no kernel.
2. The exact nullity and the normalized two-coordinate zero atom are
   \[
   \boxed{\nu_G(H_p)=\tau(p)+
      \sum_{\ell\ {\rm odd}}\tau(e_\ell),\qquad
      \mu_{H_p}(\{0\})=\frac12\left[\tau(p)+
      \sum_{\ell\ {\rm odd}}\tau(e_\ell)\right].}
   \tag{4}
   \]
   The first term is precisely the common kernel of the middle coefficients \(b=1-p,c=0\) in v1.
   For every integer \(L\ge0\),
   \[
   0\le\nu_p(R)-\sum_{\substack{\ell\le L\\\ell\ {\rm odd}}}\tau(e_\ell)
      \le\frac{\tau(p)-\tau(p_\infty)}{L+1}.
   \tag{5}
   \]
3. Formulas (9)--(11) give the actual residual and full kernel projections, the complete residual spectral measure and its Cauchy transform.
   In particular the all-open component and accumulation of nonzero path eigenvalues produce no additional zero residue.
4. The torsion-free group \(\mathbb Z\wr\mathbb Z\) admits such an infinite orbit in its von Neumann algebra with arbitrary parameter \(0<\theta<1\), for which
   \[
   \tau(e_\ell)=(1-\theta)^2\theta^\ell,\qquad
   \nu_G(H_p)=\frac{2\theta}{1+\theta},\qquad
   \mu_{H_p}(\{0\})=\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}.
   \tag{6}
   \]
   These matrices are not group-ring matrices.
   For a torsion-free abelian coefficient group \(N\), scalar group-ring projections are only \(0,1\), so this scalar projection-middle family has no nontrivial group-ring infinite orbit.
   More generally, a commuting family of actual projections in \(M_d(\mathbb CN)\) has at most \(2^d\) distinct members; in particular its transport orbit cannot be infinite.
   For the entrywise group-automorphism transport in this model, commutation of \(P\in M_d(\mathbb CN)\) with \(\sigma(P)\) already forces \(P=\sigma(P)\); the corresponding \(2d\)-coordinate projection-middle model has only its common-kernel contribution, as in (18).
   For general torsion-free coefficient groups, the conditional construction below identifies the remaining scalar group-ring projection premise rather than assuming it away.
   Matrix projections and spectral projections require their separately stated qualifications.

## Argument

### Finite starts and the missing infinite components

All finite products in (1) are projections because the translated projections commute.
With this orientation, \(\sigma(p_j)=p_{j-1}\).
The projection \(\sigma^j(e_\ell)\), \(0\le j<\ell\), describes the retained interval containing zero with endpoints \(-j,\ell-1-j\) and deleted neighbors \(-j-1,\ell-j\).
Distinct such intervals give orthogonal projections: if their endpoints differ, one interval requires a site to be retained where the other requires it to be deleted.
They all lie under \(p=p_0\), and their traces equal \(\tau(e_\ell)\).
Also
\[
\sigma^{-1}(e_\ell)\le1-p,\qquad
\sigma^\ell(e_\ell)\le1-p.
\tag{7}
\]
These are the two exterior boundary conditions, not additional kernel equations to be discarded.

For completeness, the possible one-sided infinite starts are
\[
a_+=(1-p_{-1})\bigwedge_{j\ge0}p_j,\qquad
a_-=(1-p_1)\bigwedge_{j\le0}p_j.
\]
For \(k>0\), \(\sigma^k(a_+)\) requires a retained site at \(-1\), whereas \(a_+\) requires that site to be deleted.
Thus all integer translates of \(a_+\) are pairwise orthogonal and have the same trace.
Finiteness of \(\tau(1)=1\) forces \(\tau(a_+)=0\), and faithfulness gives \(a_+=0\).
The reversed argument proves \(a_-=0\).
Every site of a half-infinite retained interval is in a translate of one of these two start projections, so those components vanish as projections.

In the joint spectral representation of the countably generated abelian algebra, a retained site belongs to a finite interval, a half-infinite interval, or the full integer line.
The last event is exactly \(p_\infty\).
Equivalently, this partition follows by expanding the countable commuting products according to the nearest deleted site in each direction.
The preceding orthogonality and vanishing of the two one-sided cases prove the strong decomposition (3).
Normality of the trace gives its mass identity.
The shift fixes \(p_\infty\), so it commutes with \(t\).
No independence, ergodicity, finite orbit or amenability assumption was used.

### Path blocks and the exact kernel dimension

Put \(E_\ell=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\sigma^j(e_\ell)\).
The map
\[
U_\ell:(e_\ell\ell^2(G))^\ell\longrightarrow E_\ell\ell^2(G),
\qquad U_\ell(x_0,\ldots,x_{\ell-1})=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}t^jx_j
\]
is a right-\(G\)-equivariant unitary, since its summand ranges are exactly those orthogonal translated projections.
Compression by \(p\) kills the two exterior terms by (7).
Interior terms move between consecutive summands with coefficient one.
Consequently \(E_\ell\) reduces \(R\), and
\[
U_\ell^*RU_\ell=J_\ell\otimes I,
\qquad
(J_\ell)_{j,j+1}=(J_\ell)_{j+1,j}=1,\quad
(J_\ell)_{jj}=0.
\tag{8}
\]
The sine vectors with coordinates \(\sin(\pi kj/(\ell+1))\), \(1\le j\le\ell\), give the \(\ell\) distinct eigenvalues \(2\cos(\pi k/(\ell+1))\), \(1\le k\le\ell\).
Thus the path nullity is one for odd \(\ell\) and zero for even \(\ell\).
Each scalar eigenvector gives one copy of the \(e_\ell\)-module and hence trace \(\tau(e_\ell)\).

On the remaining corner, \(R=p_\infty T\).
The left regular action of the infinite cyclic subgroup \(\langle t\rangle\) decomposes \(\ell^2(G)\) into copies of its bilateral shift.
The multiplier \(2\cos x\) is nonzero almost everywhere, so \(T\) is injective.
Its reducing restriction to \(p_\infty\) is injective as well.
Combining this fact with (3) and (8) proves \(\nu_p(R)=\sum_{\ell\ {\rm odd}}\tau(e_\ell)\).

The kernel equations of \(H_p\) first require \(x=px\).
The remaining equation requires \(Rx=0\) and determines the \((1-p)\)-part of the lower coordinate as \(-(1-p)Tx\).
Its \(p\)-part is free.
Thus the bounded equivariant map
\[
(x,z)\longmapsto (x,z-(1-p)Tx),
\qquad x\in\ker(R|_p),\quad z\in p\ell^2(G),
\]
is an isomorphism onto the complete kernel.
This rechecks the bounded projection-middle interface from [v1](../claims/Yin_C_A0005_v1.md#projection-middle-and-finite-orbits) without an affiliated-domain substitution.
It proves (4).

For omitted lengths \(\ell>L\), \(1\le\ell/(L+1)\).
Summing this inequality against \(\tau(e_\ell)\) and using (3) proves (5).
In addition, \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}e_\ell=p(1-p_{-1})\), since a start that never ends has zero projection.
It follows that
\(\sum_{\ell\ {\rm odd}}\tau(e_\ell)\le1-\tau(p)\), and hence \(0\le\nu_G(H_p)\le1\).
This agrees with the rank-one finite-section bound and includes the degenerate cases \(p=0\) and \(p=1\).

### Actual projections and boundary residues

For \(\ell=2m+1\), define
\[
V_\ell=(m+1)^{-1/2}\sum_{r=0}^m(-1)^rt^{2r}e_\ell,\qquad
k_R=\sum_{\ell\ {\rm odd}}V_\ell V_\ell^*.
\tag{9}
\]
The orthogonality of the translated site projections gives \(V_\ell^*V_\ell=e_\ell\).
The alternating even-site vector is the normalized zero eigenvector of \(J_\ell\), so \(V_\ell V_\ell^*\) is its residual zero projection.
Their strong sum is orthogonal and equals \(1_{\{0\}}(R|_p)\).

Define the bounded column operator \(W=(I,-(1-p)T)^{\mathsf T}\).
The free lower \(p\)-module is orthogonal to the range of \(Wk_R\).
Moreover \(k_RW^*Wk_R\ge k_R\), so it is boundedly invertible on its support.
The actual full projection is therefore
\[
P_{\ker H_p}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&p\end{pmatrix}+
Wk_R[k_RW^*Wk_R]^{-1}_{k_R}k_RW^*.
\tag{10}
\]
The second term is zero when \(k_R=0\).
Its trace is \(\tau(k_R)\), as follows from the partial isometry \(Wk_R[k_RW^*Wk_R]^{-1/2}_{k_R}\).
One must use this global Gram inverse.
Different retained paths may share a deleted boundary site, so their lower graph coordinates need not be orthogonal.
The orthogonality of the residual projections in (9) does not justify summing separately normalized full-\(H_p\) graph projections.

Let \(\mu_R\) be the spectral measure of the residual inside \(p\), of total mass \(\tau(p)\), and let \(\mu_{\rm arc}\) be the probability law of \(2\cos x\) for uniform \(x\in[0,2\pi]\).
For \(n\ne0\), \(\tau(p_\infty t^n)=0\), since \(p_\infty\in\mathcal N(N)\) and \(t^n\) has nonzero height.
The spectral measure of \(t\) weighted by \(p_\infty\) is therefore \(\tau(p_\infty)\) times Haar measure on the circle.
Together with the finite path blocks, this gives
\[
\begin{split}
\mu_R&=\tau(p_\infty)\mu_{\rm arc}
 +\sum_{\ell\ge1}\tau(e_\ell)
    \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\delta_{2\cos(\pi j/(\ell+1))},\\
g_R(z)&=\frac{\tau(p_\infty)}{\sqrt{z^2-4}}
 +\sum_{\ell\ge1}\tau(e_\ell)
    \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac1{z-2\cos(\pi j/(\ell+1))}.
\end{split}
\tag{11}
\]
Here \(g_R(z)=\tau(p(zp-R)^{-1}_p)\) for \(z\notin\mathbb R\), and the square-root branch is characterized by \(\sqrt{z^2-4}\sim z\) at infinity.
Absolute convergence follows from (3) and the bound \(1/|\operatorname{Im}z|\) for each resolvent term.
The zero residue is
\(\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}i\eta g_R(i\eta)=\sum_{\ell\ {\rm odd}}\tau(e_\ell)\).
Indeed \(i\eta/(i\eta-x)\) has modulus at most one and converges pointwise to \(1_{\{0\}}(x)\), so dominated convergence applies to the finite measure \(\mu_R\).
The arcsine component has no atom, even though its support contains zero.
Accumulation of nonzero eigenvalues of long paths likewise adds no zero residue.
Formula (11) is a law for \(R|_p\); the bounded kernel graph is not a unitary equivalence of the full operators and does not transfer this complete law to \(H_p\).
For \(g_{H_p}(z)=\tfrac12(\operatorname{Tr}_2\otimes\tau)((z-H_p)^{-1})\), only the established kernel identity is used to obtain \(\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}i\eta g_{H_p}(i\eta)=\mu_{H_p}(\{0\})\) from (4).

### Torsion-free realization in the von Neumann algebra

Take \(N=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z\), let \(t\) shift its coordinates, and set \(G=N\rtimes\mathbb Z=\mathbb Z\wr\mathbb Z\).
A finite-order element of \(G\) has zero height and hence lies in the torsion-free group \(N\), so \(G\) is torsion free.
Under Fourier transformation, \(M_0=L^\infty(\mathbb T^{\mathbb Z})\) with product Haar measure.
For an arc \(A\subset\mathbb T\) of measure \(0<\theta<1\), take \(p=\mathbf1_A(z_0)\).
Its translated projections commute, are independent under \(\tau\), and are pairwise distinct because \(\tau(p_j(1-p_k))=\theta(1-\theta)>0\) for \(j\ne k\).
The two deleted endpoints and \(\ell\) retained vertices in (1) yield
\[
\tau(e_\ell)=(1-\theta)^2\theta^\ell,\qquad
\tau(p_\infty)=0,\qquad
\sum_{\ell\ {\rm odd}}\tau(e_\ell)
 =\frac{(1-\theta)^2\theta}{1-\theta^2}
 =\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}{1+\theta}.
\tag{12}
\]
This proves (6).
For example \(\theta=1/2\) gives nullity \(2/3\) and normalized atom \(1/3\); \(\theta=\sqrt2-1\) gives \(2-\sqrt2\) and \(1-1/\sqrt2\), respectively.
The commuting geometry and an ambient torsion-free group alone therefore impose no integer arithmetic on these von Neumann coefficients.

### Exact group-ring admissibility and its limitations

If \(N\) is torsion-free abelian, every scalar element of \(\mathbb CN\) has support in a finitely generated subgroup \(\mathbb Z^d\).
A scalar selfadjoint idempotent then becomes a continuous \(\{0,1\}\)-valued Laurent polynomial on the connected torus \(\mathbb T^d\), hence is \(0\) or \(1\).
The arc projection in (12) is neither, so it is not in \(\mathbb CN\).
Also
\[
\mathbb CG\cap\mathcal N(N)=\mathbb CN,
\tag{13}
\]
since membership in \(\mathcal N(N)\) makes every Fourier coefficient outside \(N\) vanish.
Thus embedding it into the larger group does not restore finite support.
The coupling \(1-p\), and hence \(H_p\), is not a group-ring input.
For this abelian coefficient class, requiring \(H_p\in M_2(\mathbb CG)\) forces \(p=0\) or \(p=1\), giving \(\nu_G(H_p)=0\) or \(1\), respectively.

For comparison, let \(F\) be a nontrivial finite group and take \(G=F\wr\mathbb Z\).
The average of the zeroth lamp group is a projection \(p\) in the actual group ring, with \(\theta=1/|F|\).
Different lamp copies commute, and the canonical trace factors across them.
Consequently the projections have infinitely many distinct commuting translates and the same calculation gives
\[
\nu_G(H_p)=\frac{2}{|F|+1},\qquad
\mu_{H_p}(\{0\})=\frac1{|F|+1}.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(F=C_2\), \(p=(1+a_0)/2\) and \(\tau(e_\ell)=2^{-(\ell+2)}\).
These are genuine group-ring matrices over groups with torsion.
They do not refute the torsion-free A0001 contract.

There is a precise conditional limitation for a general torsion-free coefficient group.
Suppose a torsion-free group \(K\) admitted a nontrivial scalar projection \(e\in\mathbb CK\).
Faithfulness gives \(0<\theta=\tau_K(e)<1\).
The restricted wreath product \(K\wr\mathbb Z\) is torsion free by the same height argument, since the restricted direct sum of copies of \(K\) is torsion free.
Place \(e\) in the zeroth lamp copy.
Its translates in different copies commute and have product traces, so they yield the actual group-ring version of (12) and (6).
Conversely, a nontrivial infinite orbit in the specified scalar projection-middle group-ring model already requires a nontrivial scalar projection \(p\in\mathbb CN\), by (13).
Thus the infinite commuting-orbit requirement by itself does not eliminate this scalar group-ring realization premise.
No assertion about the currently settled general status of that premise is needed for this implication or for the abelian exclusion proved above.

These statements concern scalar coefficient projections.
They do not exclude matrix projections, such as the constant rank-one projection defining the two outer coefficients.
For torsion-free abelian \(N\), an actual \(Q\in M_d(\mathbb CN)\) with \(Q=Q^*=Q^2\) becomes a continuous projection matrix on a finite connected torus.
Its rank is a continuous integer-valued function and therefore constant, so its unnormalized canonical matrix trace is an integer.
However a kernel or spectral projection of a group-ring matrix belongs a priori only to \(M_d(\mathcal N(N))\).
Continuity or finite support of the original matrix does not establish those properties for its kernel projection.
The preceding constant-rank argument consequently does not supply the missing trace arithmetic for arbitrary spectral projections.

### A trace lower bound forces a finite Boolean orbit

There is a stronger consequence when the trace constraint holds for every finite intersection, not just for the individual transported projections.
Let \((\mathcal M,\operatorname{tr})\) be a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful trace \(\operatorname{tr}(1)=D<\infty\), and let \(\mathcal P\) be any pairwise commuting family of projections.
Suppose that every nonzero projection of the form
\[
\prod_{q\in F}q^{\epsilon_q}(1-q)^{1-\epsilon_q},
\qquad F\subset\mathcal P\text{ finite},\quad \epsilon_q\in\{0,1\},
\tag{15}
\]
has trace at least a fixed \(\delta>0\).
Each finite subfamily then partitions \(1\) into at most \(\lfloor D/\delta\rfloor\) nonzero Boolean atoms.
Choose a finite subfamily attaining the maximal possible number \(m\) of such atoms, which exists because these numbers are bounded integers.
For any further \(q\in\mathcal P\), adjoining \(q\) cannot split one of these atoms \(a\) into two nonzero pieces \(aq,a(1-q)\), because that would increase the number of atoms.
Consequently \(q\) is a sum of some of the same \(m\) atoms.
Thus
\[
|\mathcal P|\le2^m\le2^{\lfloor D/\delta\rfloor}.
\tag{16}
\]
For a transport orbit the automorphism permutes its generated finite Boolean algebra and therefore its minimal atoms.
The orbit is periodic and the finite-orbit path method applies.
This is an independently useful discreteness criterion, but its premise must be established for the actual intersections.

Apply it to \(\mathcal P\subset M_d(\mathbb CN)\) when \(N\) is torsion-free abelian.
Every finite Boolean product remains a projection matrix with finite group-ring support.
The connected-torus rank argument just proved gives a positive integer trace for every nonzero such product.
Hence \(D=d,\delta=1\), proving the bound \(2^d\) and ruling out an infinite commuting orbit even at finite matrix size.
This argument is specific to actual group-ring projections with that independently established trace property; it does not invoke the general Atiyah assertion.

Integer trace of each member alone is insufficient.
In the Bernoulli construction with \(\theta=1/2\), the commuting matrix projections
\[
Q_j=\operatorname{diag}(p_j,1-p_j)\in M_2(M_0)
\]
are infinitely many distinct members, each with unnormalized trace one.
For \(r\) distinct indices, however,
\[
(\operatorname{Tr}_2\otimes\tau)(Q_{j_1}\cdots Q_{j_r})=2^{1-r}.
\tag{17}
\]
Their finite intersections violate every uniform positive trace lower bound.
This identifies why control of individual ranks does not suffice to control the path weights.

### Augmentation rigidity for abelian group-ring matrix projections

For torsion-free abelian \(N\), the actual group-automorphism transport gives a stronger conclusion than finiteness of the orbit.
Let \(P=P^*=P^2\in M_d(\mathbb CN)\), and assume only that \(P\) commutes with \(\sigma(P)\), where \(\sigma\) is induced entrywise by an automorphism of \(N\).
The augmentation \(\varepsilon:\mathbb CN\to\mathbb C\), extended entrywise, satisfies \(\varepsilon\circ\sigma=\varepsilon\).
Thus \(E=P(I_d-\sigma(P))\) is a group-ring matrix projection with
\(\varepsilon(E)=\varepsilon(P)(I_d-\varepsilon(P))=0\).
Place the finite support of \(E\) in a free abelian subgroup of finite rank.
On its connected dual torus the continuous projection \(E\) has constant rank, and its value at the trivial character is the zero matrix.
Hence \(E=0\).
The same argument gives \((I_d-P)\sigma(P)=0\), so \(P=\sigma(P)\).
In particular no nonconstant commuting transport orbit occurs for these actual group-ring matrix projections.

For the explicitly enlarged zero-potential model
\[
H_P=
\begin{pmatrix}
(t+t^{-1})I_d&I_d-P\\
I_d-P&0
\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2d}(\mathbb CG),
\qquad G=N\rtimes_\sigma\mathbb Z,
\]
the equality \(P=\sigma(P)\) makes \(P\) commute with \(t\).
Its residual \(P(t+t^{-1})I_dP\) is injective on the \(P\)-corner.
The same bounded kernel equations as before consequently give
\[
P_{\ker H_P}=\operatorname{diag}(0,P),\qquad
\nu_G(H_P)=(\operatorname{Tr}_d\otimes\tau_N)(P)
 =\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}\varepsilon(P)\in\{0,\ldots,d\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The last equality follows from the same constant-rank argument, now for \(P\).
The probability zero atom uses normalization by \(2d\).
This certificate needs only adjacent commutation and is proved without a general Atiyah theorem.
Its augmentation argument does not cover a transport that also conjugates the matrix coordinates by an unrelated unitary, because that transport need not fix \(\varepsilon(P)\).

### Source comparison and continuation boundary

LNW08, [arXiv v2, Section 2B](../literature/pdf/LNW08/arxiv-v2.html#S2.SS2), constructs orthogonal finite-cluster projections; its Theorem 1.5 relates the finite-lamp walk law to percolation.
Its [Section 4A](../literature/pdf/LNW08/arxiv-v2.html#S4.SS1) already realizes arbitrary parameters with a convolution projection on \(\mathbb Z\) whose Fourier transform is an interval indicator, and explains the loss of finite-support eigenfunctions.
The inspected scope is those statement/construction interfaces, with no complete audit of the paper's proofs.
The present path proof handles arbitrary commuting translated projections without independence and identifies the entire remaining infinite component on the height line.
The bounded graph formula supplies the additional two-coordinate kernel; the arbitrary-parameter realization itself is prior literature.
The source's torsion-free base-group subsection must not be read as making a finite-lamp wreath product torsion free.

The path weights are now explicit traces of actual start projections, with the complete infinite remainder and a certified truncation error.
This supplies a geometric atom formula but does not by itself make those weights integral.
A useful next extension is to start with genuine finite group-ring middle coefficients, derive their spectral/support projections, and determine which transported intersections and weights their algebraic origin permits.
Beyond the abelian group-ring rigidity just proved, matrix coefficient projections over nonabelian coefficient groups and support projections outside the group ring remain separate potential extensions, retaining their unnormalized trace and actual commutation hypotheses.
Replacing these questions by freely chosen measurable projections, or by integer weights as an assumption, would discard the missing A0001 input.
