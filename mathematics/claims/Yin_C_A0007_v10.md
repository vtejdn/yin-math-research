# The constant field of a recovered cyclic boundary

## Statement

Let \(G=N\rtimes\langle z\rangle\) with \(z\) of infinite order, let \(H=\langle z\rangle\), and write \(U(N),U(G),U(H)\) for the actual group affiliated rings.
Let \(D\subset U(N)\) be a unital division subring invariant under conjugation by \(z\), and put \(k_0=D\cap\mathbb C1\), identified with a subfield of \(\mathbb C\).
The skew Laurent ring with coefficients in \(D\) has a fraction division ring \(F\) inside \(U(G)\), and
\[
                          F\cap U(H)=k_0(z).
\]
For any subfield \(k\subset\mathbb C\) and sofic \(N\), the division closure of \(k[N]\) in \(U(N)\) intersects the scalar complex numbers exactly in \(k\).
This assertion about scalars does not require the division closure itself to be a division ring.

In the conjugation-orbit setting of [v8](Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#statement), suppose \(D\) is closed under adjoints and both selfadjoint summands of the bounded pencil have entries in \(F\).
Then the expected kernel support \(q=s(E_H(p))\) and every determined bounded edge action \(b_jq\), where \((Y_j-b_j)p=0\), have entries in \(k_0(z)\).
With full support this includes all entries of \(b_j\).
In particular for the free cyclic amalgam \(F(z,a)*_{\langle z\rangle}F(z,b)\) and vertex pencils over a conjugation-stable \(k\), the determined entries belong to \(k(z)\).
For full support and bounded invertibility of \(E_H(p)\), this controls the actual norm subordination boundary under the freeness hypothesis of v8.

## Argument

### Extracting the coefficients at infinity

The [slice proof in v8](Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#the-fraction-ring-in-the-regular-representation) uses only that the nonzero end coefficients are affiliated units, so gives the stated embedding without requiring \(D\) to contain \(\mathbb C\).
For \(b\in F\cap U(H)\) choose nonzero \(Q\) and a Laurent polynomial \(P\) over \(D\) with \(Qb=P\).
The [common left clearing and scalar functional in v8](Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#its-intersection-with-the-edge-algebra) give \(b=s(z)/q(z)\in\mathbb C(z)\) from this identity.
The zero case is immediate.
Clear the scalar Laurent denominator on the right to obtain \(Q s=P q\).
This identity holds coefficient by coefficient over the subring of \(U(N)\) generated by \(D\) and \(\mathbb C\), since a finite sum \(\sum C_i z^i=0\) has all \(C_i=0\) by common bounded left clearing and orthogonality of group slices.
In this multiplication the right-hand scalar coefficients are fixed by conjugation.

Write the Laurent series of \(s/q\) at infinity as \(\sum_{j\le m}\gamma_j z^j\).
In the formal coefficient identity \(Q(s/q)=P\), let \(u\in D^\times\) be the highest coefficient of \(Q\).
At the highest degree, \(u\gamma_m\) equals a coefficient of \(P\).
At each successive degree \(u\gamma_j\) is a coefficient of \(P\) minus a sum of coefficients of \(Q\) multiplied by already determined \(\gamma_{j'}\) with \(j'>j\).
Induction gives \(\gamma_j\in D\cap\mathbb C=k_0\) for every \(j\).
These formal comparisons use a scalar series on the right, so no coefficient of \(D\) is moved past a power of \(z\).

For clarity, a complex rational function in \(t=z^{-1}\) with Laurent coefficients in \(k_0\) is rational over \(k_0\).
After multiplying by a power of \(t\), let its Taylor series be \(f\).
Take a complex polynomial denominator of degree \(e\) with constant coefficient one and a numerator of degree at most \(h\).
Vanishing of the coefficients of \(f(1+d_1t+\cdots+d_et^e)\) above degree \(h\) is a consistent affine linear system over \(k_0\).
Its augmented rows span a finite-dimensional space and a finite row basis retains the entire system.
Gaussian elimination gives a solution in \(k_0^e\), and the resulting numerator has coefficients in \(k_0\).
This proves \(b\in k_0(z)\), and the reverse inclusion follows from the inclusion of \(k_0,z,z^{-1}\) in \(F\).

### Scalars in the division closure on a sofic group

For every finite group-ring matrix \(M\) on a sofic group and every field automorphism \(\alpha\) of \(\mathbb C\), one has
\[
                         \operatorname{rk}_N(\alpha M)=\operatorname{rk}_N(M).
\]
Indeed both ranks are limits along the same sofic approximation by [Jaikin-Zapirain, Theorem 1.3](../audits/literature/JZ19/Yin_U001.md#rank-and-approximation-definitions).
The approximating finite matrices have equal ordinary ranks by the minor test.
For arbitrary \(N\) the finitely many group labels lie in a finitely generated sofic subgroup and the two regular ranks are unchanged by subgroup induction.
This comparison acts on coefficients in finite matrices and requires no extension of \(\alpha\) to the affiliated algebra.
[Corollaries 1.6 and 1.7 of the same article, p. 471](../literature/pdf/JZ19.pdf#page=8), respectively establish scalar intersection for the rank-metric closure of the regular ring for countable sofic groups and conjugation-stable fields, and state the rank equality for two arbitrary coefficient embeddings on a sofic group.

Every element in the division closure of \(k[N]\) is represented by a finite expression over \(k[N]\) using addition, negation, multiplication and inverses of actual affiliated units.
Such an expression admits a representation \(uA^{-1}v\), where \(A\) is a square matrix over \(k[N]\) invertible over \(U(N)\), and \(u,v\) are a row and a column over \(k[N]\).
For an input \(x\) take \(A=(1), u=(x), v=(1)\).
Sums use a block diagonal matrix and negation changes the row sign.
For a product of two representations use the diagonal blocks \(A_1,A_2\) and upper right block \(-v_1u_2\), extracting the inverse with outer row \((u_1,0)\) and column \((0,v_2)^{\mathsf T}\) in block notation.
For the inverse of a unit represented by \(r=uA^{-1}v\), take the bordered matrix with rows of blocks \((A,v)\) and \((-u,0)\).
Its scalar Schur complement is \(r\), so the matrix is invertible and its bottom-right inverse entry is \(r^{-1}\).

If the expression equals \(\lambda1\), the matrix with rows of blocks \((A,v)\) and \((u,\lambda)\) has rank exactly the size of \(A\), by its zero Schur complement.
For any \(\alpha\) fixing \(k\) the same holds with \(\alpha(\lambda)\) in the lower corner.
Subtracting the first block by Schur elimination now gives rank zero for \(\alpha(\lambda)-uA^{-1}v\), hence \(\alpha(\lambda)=\lambda\).
The fixed field of all automorphisms of \(\mathbb C\) over \(k\) is \(k\).
Indeed an algebraic element outside \(k\) has a different conjugate in characteristic zero and the corresponding embedding extends to \(\mathbb C\) by a transcendence basis and algebraic closure.
For a transcendental element, include it in a transcendence basis, send it to itself plus one with the other basis elements fixed, and extend to the algebraic closure.
This proves the scalar assertion.

### Applying the finite recovery formula

Under the orbit and adjoint hypotheses the [projection and translate-join proof](Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#kernel-projections-and-the-translate-sum) forms the analytic kernel projection over \(F\) by Gaussian elimination and its Gram inverse.
The integer stopping argument there uses the division-ring rank and the orbit hypothesis and does not use any inclusion \(\mathbb C\subset D\).
It gives both the edge support \(q\in M_n(F)\) and the [corner formula](Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#deficient-expected-support) \(b_jq=L_j(S+I-q)^{-1}\in M_n(F)\).
Both matrices have entries in \(U(H)\), proving the claim by the intersection just computed.
In the free example take \(D\) to be the division closure of \(k[N]\) for the free kernel \(N\) of the retraction to \(H\).
It lies in the known complex free-group division ring in \(U(N)\), so every one of its nonzero elements is an affiliated unit and its own inverse lies in \(D\).
Conjugation stability of \(k\) gives adjoint stability of \(D\), and the group automorphism induced by \(z\) preserves \(D\).
The group \(N\) is free and sofic, hence \(k_0=k\).
The group, orbits, conditional freeness and source hypotheses for the norm-boundary assertion are checked in the [free cyclic application](Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#the-free-cyclic-amalgam).
For an arbitrary group the argument leaves the existence of this coefficient division ring and the residual rank correction in the [free-spanning theorem](Yin_C_A0007_v9.md#statement) open.
