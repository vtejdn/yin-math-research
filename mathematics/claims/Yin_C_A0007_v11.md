# Prescribed rational subordination boundaries and independent compression defects

## Statement

Let \(k\subseteq\mathbb C\) be a subfield closed under complex conjugation, and give \(k(z)\) the involution determined by conjugation on \(k\) and \(z^*=z^{-1}\).
Fix \(r\ge1\) and \(F=F^*\in M_r(k(z))\) with no poles on the unit circle.
Write \(H=\langle z\rangle\) and \(\iota(F)=\operatorname{diag}(0_r,F,0_r)\).
For an operator \(V\) write \(\pi_V\) for the projection onto its kernel and \(\nu(V)\) for its kernel dimension with the unnormalized matrix trace and canonical group trace.
All edge expectations \(E_H\) act entrywise.

There are finite selfadjoint \(3r\)-square vertex matrices \(X_a,X_b\) over \(k\) in the free cyclic amalgam
\[
 G_0=\mathbb F(z,a)*_H\mathbb F(z,b)=\mathbb F(z,a,b)
\]
such that \(K=X_a+X_b\) satisfies \(E_H(\pi_K)\ge\gamma I_{3r}\) for some \(\gamma>0\), and the two norm subordination limits at zero are \(-\iota(F)\) and \(+\iota(F)\), respectively.
The total nullity is \(r\), and the two shifted vertex nullities are \(2r\) each.
Thus every bounded selfadjoint rational matrix over the field in the [cyclic boundary theorem](Yin_C_A0007_v10.md#statement) occurs on a coordinate block of an actual boundary.
For a rational function, a pole on the unit circle after cancellation precludes essential boundedness there.

Moreover, for every torsion-free group \(\Gamma\) and \(P\in M_{m,r}(k\Gamma)\), \(m\ge1\), we give a finite selfadjoint additive matrix \(Y=Y_a+Y_b\) over a larger noncentral cyclic amalgam.
Its expected-kernel support is the constant projection \(q=\operatorname{diag}(I_{4r},0_m)\), and the compression \(qYq\) to its range has actual boundaries \(-\iota(F)\oplus0_r\) and \(+\iota(F)\oplus0_r\).
Their extensions by zero give the unique actions on \(q\) determined by \((Y_j-b_j)\pi_Y=0\).
The row from \(q\) into \(1-q\) uses the same middle \(r\) coordinates on which \(F\) acts, and
\[
   \nu_q(qYq)-\nu(Y)=\operatorname{rk}_{\Gamma}(P).
   \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\nu_q\) counts the kernel inside the range of \(q\).
In particular, fixing the entire compressed pair, its two boundary functions and the expected-kernel support leaves the rank of an arbitrary original finite matrix in the correction.

## Argument

### Clearing the boundary denominator inside each vertex

Choose a common Laurent denominator \(d\in k[z,z^{-1}]\) for \(F\) with no zero on the circle.
Such a denominator is obtained by multiplying the reduced denominators of the entries, since each reduced denominator is coprime to its numerator and therefore has no circle zero.
Put \(Q=d^*d\), so \(Q\) is strictly positive on the circle and \(QF\) is a Laurent polynomial matrix over \(k\).
Choose an integer \(c>\|F\|\) and put \(M=F+cI_r\), which is positive with a bounded inverse.
Write \(A=(a-1)I_r\), \(B=(b-1)I_r\), and suppress the identity in the notation for the scalar matrix \(QI_r\).
Use the rows
\[
   R_a=(QA,I_r,0),\qquad R_b=(0,I_r,QB),
   \qquad R=\begin{pmatrix}R_a\\R_b\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Define \(X_a=R_a^*MR_a-\iota(F)\) and \(X_b=-R_b^*MR_b+\iota(F)\).
In their respective vertex rings the matrices are
\[
 X_a=\begin{pmatrix}
 A^*QMQA & A^*QM & 0\\
 MQA & cI_r & 0\\
 0 & 0 & 0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 X_b=-\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&0\\
 0&cI_r&MQB\\
 0&B^*QM&B^*QMQB
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The entries of \(QM=MQ\) and \(QMQ\) belong to \(k[z,z^{-1}]\), since \(Q\) is a scalar matrix within the edge algebra.
Formula (2) keeps \(A,B\) in their displayed positions.
Indeed \(z\) fails to commute with \(a\) in the first vertex and with \(b\) in the second, by free reduced words.

Let \(R_1\) denote \(R\) with \(Q=1\), and let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(I_r,Q,I_r)\).
The identity \(R=\operatorname{diag}(Q,Q)R_1D^{-1}\) gives \(\ker R=D\ker R_1\).
The adjoint \(R_1^*\) sends \((v,w)\) to \((A^*v,v+w,B^*w)\), so it is injective since \(a,b\) each have infinite order and left translation by either has no nonzero invariant \(\ell^2\)-vector.
It follows that \(R^*\) is injective and \(R\) has rank \(2r\).
As \(K=R^*\operatorname{diag}(M,-M)R\), the invertibility of \(M\) gives \(\ker K=\ker R\) and \(\nu(K)=r\).
Each row \(R_j\) is onto, because its middle entry is \(I_r\), so each signed form \(\pm R_j^*MR_j\) has nullity \(2r\).

The [tree computation for \(Q=1\)](Yin_C_A0007_v6.md#exact-expected-projection-from-the-degree-three-tree), amplified by \(I_r\), gives \(E_H(\pi_{R_1})\ge\gamma_0 I_{3r}\) for \(\gamma_0=(9-\sqrt{33})/24\).
The range of \(D\pi_{R_1}\) is \(\ker K\), giving \(D\pi_{R_1}D^*\le\|D\|^2\pi_K\).
Taking \(E_H\) and using its edge bimodularity yields
\[
     E_H(\pi_K)\ge \gamma I_{3r},\qquad
     \gamma=\frac{\gamma_0}{\|D\|^2\|D^{-1}\|^2}>0.
     \tag{3}
\]
The two vertex von Neumann algebras are free over \(\mathcal N(H)\) by the amalgam normal form, and their matrix amplifications are free over the amplified edge algebra by multiplying entries of centered alternating products.
Consequently (3) allows the application of [BBL21, arXiv v2, Theorems 3.2 and 3.5(vii)](../audits/literature/BBL21/Yin_U001.md#general-base-algebra-and-the-a0001-strategy-interface).
The norm limits \(d_j=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\omega_j(it I_{3r})\) exist and satisfy \((X_j-d_j)\pi_K=0\).
The respective candidates \(-\iota(F)\) and \(+\iota(F)\) satisfy these equations because \(R_a\pi_K=R_b\pi_K=0\).
For each \(j\) the difference between the candidate and \(d_j\) therefore annihilates \(E_H(\pi_K)\), proving equality by (3).
This realizes the full rational matrix class on the middle coordinates by extending the [positive inverse Laurent family](Yin_C_A0007_v7.md#statement).

### An arbitrary finite-matrix rank on the same coordinates

Given \(\Gamma,P\), introduce another free generator \(u\) and set
\[
   G_1=\Gamma*\mathbb F(z,a,u),\qquad G_2=\mathbb F(z,b),
   \qquad G=G_1*_H G_2=\Gamma*\mathbb F(z,a,b,u).
\]
The free product normal form embeds \(G_0,\Gamma,G_1,G_2\) in \(G\) and keeps \(H\) noncentral in both vertices.
The free products of the displayed torsion-free groups are torsion-free.
All operators below act on amplifications of \(\mathcal H=\ell^2(G)\).
Subgroup induction over right cosets preserves kernel projections in the embedded group von Neumann algebras, their group traces, and the edge expectation of entries from \(\mathcal N(G_0)\).
In particular (3) still holds.

Let \(S=(0,I_r,0)\), let \(T=P(S,-uI_r)\), and put \(D_b=(3+b+b^{-1})I_m\ge I_m\).
With the first block of size \(4r\), set
\[
 Y_a=\begin{pmatrix}X_a\oplus0_r&T^*\\T&0_m\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Y_b=\begin{pmatrix}X_b\oplus0_r&0\\0&D_b\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Y=Y_a+Y_b .
 \tag{4}
\]
Every entry of \(T\) lies in \(kG_1\).
For a kernel vector \((v,w,y)\) in block sizes \(3r,r,m\), the second row equation \(-u^*P^*y=0\) implies \(P^*y=0\).
The first equation then gives \(Kv=0\), and pairing the last equation \(P(Sv-uw)+D_by=0\) with \(y\) gives \(y=0\).
Conversely the remaining equations suffice, proving
\[
    \ker Y=\{(v,w,0):v\in\ker K,\ P(Sv-uw)=0\}.
    \tag{5}
\]
The kernel of the compression on the \(q\)-space is \(\ker K\oplus\mathcal H^r\), of dimension \(2r\).
On this space the image of \(T\) is exactly \(P\mathcal H^r\), since \(w\) is unrestricted and \(u\) is unitary.
By (5) its kernel there identifies with \(\ker Y\).
The polar decomposition of this restricted operator and subgroup induction for \(P\) give (1), hence \(\nu(Y)=2r-\operatorname{rk}_{\Gamma}(P)\).
This computes the [off-corner rank in the support-compression identity](Yin_C_A0007_v5.md#the-exact-compression-formula-and-its-domains).

It remains to identify the actual expected support of the uncompressed kernel.
Define \(Jv=(v,u^{-1}Sv,0)\).
Equation (5) gives \(\operatorname{ran}(J\pi_K)\subseteq\ker Y\), and \(\|J\|^2\le2\), so \(J\pi_KJ^*\le 2\pi_Y\).
For \(Z\in\mathcal N(G_0)\), free reduced words give \(E_H(Zu)=0\) and \(E_H(u^{-1}Zu)=\tau_{G_0}(Z)1\), first for finite sums and then by \(L^2\)-continuity.
Applying these facts entrywise to the blocks of \(J\pi_KJ^*\) yields
\[
 E_H(J\pi_KJ^*)=\operatorname{diag}
   \big(E_H(\pi_K),(\operatorname{id}_{M_r}\otimes\tau_{G_0})(S\pi_KS^*)\otimes1,0_m\big).
\]
Applying the group trace entrywise to \(S E_H(\pi_K)S^*\ge\gamma I_r\) shows that the second block is also at least \(\gamma I_r\).
As \(\pi_Y\le q\) by (5), we obtain
\[
     (\gamma/2)q\le E_H(\pi_Y)\le q,\qquad s(E_H(\pi_Y))=q.
     \tag{6}
\]

The compressed sum on \(q\mathcal H^{4r+m}\) is \(K\oplus0_r\), and its expected kernel projection is \(E_H(\pi_K)\oplus I_r\).
Each compressed vertex matrix acts on \(\ker K\oplus\mathcal H^r\) by its respective candidate \(-\iota(F)\oplus0_r\) or \(+\iota(F)\oplus0_r\).
The same BBL21 existence and uniqueness argument gives these candidates as the norm limits.
Let \(b_a=(-\iota(F))\oplus0_r\oplus0_m\) and \(b_b=\iota(F)\oplus0_r\oplus0_m\).
Equations (4) and (5) give \((Y_j-b_j)\pi_Y=0\) for each vertex, using \(T(v,w)=0\) on this kernel.
Any other bounded edge solution \(d_j\) has \((d_j-b_j)E_H(\pi_Y)=0\), hence \(d_jq=b_jq\) by (6).
The norm-limit assertion concerns the compressed problem, where the expected kernel has a bounded inverse.
For the full matrix the argument determines only the actions on \(q\).

The group \(\Gamma\) embeds in the first vertex, so a separate integrality theorem for that entire vertex would already control \(\operatorname{rk}_\Gamma(P)\).
The construction leaves open a transfer theorem using independently controlled vertices and information beyond these compressed boundaries.
