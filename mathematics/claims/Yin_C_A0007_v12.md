# Degree bounds for cyclic edge supports and boundary actions

## Statement

Let \(G=N\rtimes\langle z\rangle\), where \(z\) has infinite order and every element outside \(H=\langle z\rangle\) has infinite orbit under conjugation by \(z\).
Write \(B=\mathcal N(H)\), let \(D\) be a unital division subring of the affiliated algebra \(U(N)\) closed under adjoints and invariant under \(\sigma=\operatorname{Ad}(z)\), and put \(k=D\cap\mathbb C1\), viewed as a conjugation-stable subfield of \(\mathbb C\).
Use the fraction division ring \(F\subset U(G)\) of \(D[z,z^{-1};\sigma]\) from the [cyclic constant-field theorem](Yin_C_A0007_v10.md#statement).
Let \(Y=Y_1+Y_2\) be an \(n\)-square matrix, \(n\ge1\), with bounded selfadjoint summands over this skew Laurent ring.
Choose integers \(\ell\le 0\le u\) so that all exponents of the entries of \(Y,Y_1,Y_2\) lie in \([\ell,u]\), and put \(w=u-\ell\).
Let \(p=P_{\ker Y}\), \(q=s(E_H(p))\), where the subgroup expectation acts entrywise, and consider any \(b_j\in M_n(B)\) satisfying \((Y_j-b_j)p=0\).
The [finite recovery and scalar descent](Yin_C_A0007_v10.md#applying-the-finite-recovery-formula) give \(q,b_jq\in M_n(k(z))\).

Set \(a=\operatorname{rank}_F Y\), \(s=\operatorname{rank}_{k(z)}q\), and \(L=aw\).
There is one nonzero polynomial \(\Delta\in k[z]\), with no zero on the unit circle, such that every entry of \(q\) and of every determined action \(b_jq\) has the form \(P/\Delta\) with \(P\in k[z]\).
The same \(\Delta\) works for all such summands with \(Y\) and \([\ell,u]\) fixed.
For \(s>0\) the degrees satisfy
\[
       \deg\Delta\le 2sL-\ell,\qquad \deg P\le 2sL+w .
       \tag{1}
\]
For \(q=I_n\) the bounds improve to
\[
       \deg\Delta\le nL-\ell,\qquad \deg P\le nL+w .
       \tag{2}
\]
When \(s=0\), all the determined entries vanish and one can use \(\Delta=1\).
Thus the finite poles of the entire collection, counted with their maximum multiplicities across entries, have total multiplicity at most the stated denominator bound.
For summands free over \(M_n(B)\) with \(E_H(p)\) boundedly invertible, (2) applies to the actual norm subordination limits at zero by the [kernel relation for those limits](../audits/literature/BBL21/Yin_U001.md#general-base-algebra-and-the-a0001-strategy-interface).

The linear dependence on \(w\) is necessary even at size three with full expected support.
For every \(m\ge1\) there are finite pencils of width \(2m\) whose actual boundary has \(2m\) distinct finite poles, and others with a pole of order \(m\).
At width two the poles can approach the unit circle arbitrarily closely.

## Argument

### Polynomial columns for the kernel

Multiplying \(Y\) on the left by \(z^{-\ell}I_n\) gives a skew polynomial matrix \(A\) of degree at most \(w\) with the same right kernel over \(F\).
Choose \(a\) rows and \(a\) columns of \(A\) with invertible intersection over \(F\), using Gaussian elimination over the division ring.
The chosen rows span the left row space.
For each column index outside the chosen \(a\), seek a right null vector supported on that column and the \(a\) chosen columns, with skew polynomial coordinates of degree at most \(L\).
On the chosen rows multiplication by this submatrix is right \(D\)-linear, and the output coordinates have degree at most \(L+w\).
Writing polynomials with their coefficients on the right gives dimension \(h+1\) over \(D\) in degrees \(0,\ldots,h\).
The domain dimension exceeds the target dimension by
\[
             (a+1)(L+1)-a(L+w+1)=1 .
\]
A nonzero polynomial solution therefore exists.
Its coordinate at the additional column is nonzero by invertibility of the chosen square submatrix, and the spanning property of the chosen rows makes it a solution on every row of \(A\).
The \(n-a\) solutions obtained this way are right independent over \(F\), by their distinct additional coordinates, and hence form a right kernel basis.
When \(a=0\) one can take the coordinate columns, and when \(a=n\) the kernel is zero.
Write \(V\) for the matrix of basis columns.
The [affiliated kernel comparison](Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#kernel-projections-and-the-translate-sum) identifies \(VF^{n-a}=pF^n\) inside \(F^n\) and identifies \(p\) with the analytic kernel projection.
If \(p=0\) the claimed zero case follows, so assume \(p\ne0\).

### Scalar polynomial columns spanning the expected support

For any \(k\)-linear functional \(\phi:D\to k\), define
\[
          \Phi_\phi\!\left(\sum_h z^h d_h\right)
                   =\sum_h z^h\phi(d_h)
\]
on skew Laurent polynomials, applying it entrywise to columns.
Because the coefficients \(d_h\) are on the right and \(k\) is central and fixed by \(\sigma\), this map commutes with left multiplication by \(k[z,z^{-1}]\).
In particular, if \(C\) has entries in \(k(z)\) and \(v,v'\) are skew Laurent polynomial columns satisfying \(Cv=v'\), then \(C\Phi_\phi(v)=\Phi_\phi(v')\), by first clearing a common scalar denominator on the left.

The columns \(\Phi_\phi(v)\), as \(v\) ranges over the columns of \(V\) and \(\phi\) over these functionals, span precisely \(qk(z)^n\) over \(k(z)\).
Indeed \(qv=v\) since \(p\le q\), so \(q\Phi_\phi(v)=\Phi_\phi(v)\).
For the opposite inclusion take any row over \(k(z)\) annihilating all the scalar columns.
After clearing its scalar denominator it is a polynomial row \(\lambda\), and \(\Phi_\phi(\lambda v)=0\) for every \(v,\phi\).
The \(k\)-linear functionals separate points of \(D\), so coefficient comparison gives \(\lambda V=0\).
As \(VF^{n-a}=pF^n\), this implies \(\lambda p=0\).
The polynomial row is bounded and belongs to the edge algebra, hence \(\lambda E_H(p)=E_H(\lambda p)=0\) and \(\lambda q=0\) by the definition of support.
Restoring the cleared denominator shows that both column spaces have the same annihilator over \(k(z)\), proving equality.

Choose \(s\) independent scalar columns from this family to form a matrix \(U\in M_{n,s}(k[z])\), of degree at most \(L\).
For its \(\alpha\)-th column \(\Phi_{\phi_\alpha}(v_\alpha)\), form the corresponding column \(\Phi_{\phi_\alpha}(Y_jv_\alpha)\) of a matrix \(T_j\).
Each entry of \(T_j\) has exponents in \([\ell,u+L]\).
Since \(b_jqv_\alpha=Y_jv_\alpha\) and \(b_jq\) is scalar rational entrywise, clearing its scalar denominator as above gives
\[
                   (b_jq)U=T_j .
                   \tag{3}
\]
In particular the choice of \(U\) uses \(Y\) alone and may be kept for every right side in (3).

### A common denominator

Use the involution \(z^*=z^{-1}\) and conjugation on \(k\), and put \(U^\#=z^L U^*\) and \(H_U=U^\#U\).
These are ordinary polynomial matrices of degrees at most \(L\) and \(2L\), respectively.
An \(s\)-row minor of \(U\) is a nonzero polynomial, so \(U\) has rank \(s\) at all but finitely many points of the unit circle.
At each such point \(U^*U\) is positive definite, hence \(\det H_U\ne0\) as a polynomial.
The orthogonal projection there onto the range of \(U\) is \(U H_U^{-1}U^\#\).
It equals \(q\) almost everywhere, since \(qU=U\) and the rational projection \(q\) has rank \(s\) away from finitely many points by its minors.
Thus the rational matrix identities are
\[
            q=U H_U^{-1}U^\#,\qquad b_jq=T_j H_U^{-1}U^\# ,
            \tag{4}
\]
where the second equality uses \(b_jq=(b_jq)q\) and (3).

Take \(\Delta_0=z^{-\ell}\det H_U\), of degree at most \(2sL-\ell\).
The adjugate of \(H_U\) has degree at most \(2(s-1)L\), including the size-one case.
In (4) the numerator for \(b_jq\) is \(z^{-\ell} T_j\operatorname{adj}(H_U)U^\#\), of degree at most \((w+L)+2(s-1)L+L=2sL+w\).
For \(q\) the same computation with \(U\) in place of \(T_j\) bounds its numerator degree by \(2sL-\ell\).
With full support \(U\) is square and (3) gives \(b_j=T_jU^{-1}\).
Now \(\Delta_0=z^{-\ell}\det U\) yields the sharper degrees in (2) by the adjugate formula, and the identity matrix uses the same denominator.

Every entry considered is essentially bounded on the unit circle.
A reduced rational function with a pole there is essentially unbounded on every circle arc containing that pole.
For each irreducible factor of \(\Delta_0\) over \(k\) with a root on the circle, boundedness therefore forces its full power in \(\Delta_0\) to divide every numerator, by coprimality in \(k[z]\).
Removing all such powers gives the claimed common \(\Delta\) and preserves the degree bounds.

### Boundary poles attained by finite vertex matrices

Use the [size-three finite vertex pencils](Yin_C_A0007_v7.md#statement) on the free cyclic amalgam with positive Laurent polynomial \(h\) and actual boundaries \(\mp h^{-1}e_{22}\).
In the cyclic splitting that kills \(a,b\) under the retraction to \(\langle z\rangle\), the factors \(a-1,b-1\) and their adjoints have exponent zero.
Thus the displayed entries of those pencils have exponents in \([-m,m]\) whenever \(h\) does.
At either endpoint \(i=\pm m\), a nonzero scalar coefficient \(h_i\) of \(h\) gives the nonzero coefficient \(h_i(a-1)^*\sigma^i(a-1)\) in the upper left entry, since both factors are nonzero in \(D\).
For \(h=3+z^m+z^{-m}\) the nonzero boundary entry is, up to sign,
\[
                \frac{z^m}{z^{2m}+3z^m+1}.
\]
The two roots of \(x^2+3x+1\) are distinct and nonzero, so the denominator has \(2m\) distinct nonzero roots and shares no factor with the numerator.
For \(h=(3+z+z^{-1})^m\) the denominator is instead \((z^2+3z+1)^m\), giving order \(m\) at each of its two roots.
Both choices are strictly positive on the circle.
Finally \(h=2+\varepsilon+z+z^{-1}\) with rational \(\varepsilon>0\) is strictly positive and has width two.
The two poles of its inverse converge to \(-1\) as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

These bounds measure the determined edge action from finite skew Laurent coefficients.
For a deficient support the nullity correction still involves the rank of the off-corner map on the compressed kernel, as in the [compression identity](Yin_C_A0007_v5.md#the-exact-compression-formula-and-its-domains).
The [prescribed-boundary construction](Yin_C_A0007_v11.md#statement) holds the compressed pair and its boundaries fixed while that correction carries the rank of an arbitrary original finite matrix over a torsion-free group.
