# Kernel dimensions from two vertex ranks on the expected support of a graph

## Statement

Let \(G=G_1*_H G_2\), put \(M_j=\mathcal N(G_j)\), \(M=\mathcal N(G)\) and \(B=\mathcal N(H)\), and write \(E\) for the entrywise trace-preserving expectation onto the matrix algebra over \(B\).
All dimensions and ranks use the unnormalized matrix trace with the canonical group trace.
For a bounded rectangular operator \(V\), \(\operatorname{rk}(V)\) is the trace of its closed range projection and \(\nu(V)\) is the trace of its kernel projection.
Fix \(m,n\ge1\), \(Y_j\in M_{m,n}(M_j)\), and \(Y=Y_1+Y_2\).
In the direct sum of the input and output spaces, form the two vertex rows
\[
       R_1=(Y_1,-I_m),\qquad R_2=(Y_2,I_m).
\]
Let \(P\) project onto their common kernel, the graph of \(Y_1\) on \(\ker Y\), and let \(Q=s(E(P))\).
Then
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \nu(Y)=\operatorname{tr}(Q)
       -\operatorname{rk}_{G_1}(R_1Q)-\operatorname{rk}_{G_2}(R_2Q).}
 \tag{1}
\]
In fact the right hand side, as a function of the edge projection in place of \(Q\), attains its maximum at \(Q\).
The identity allows nonselfadjoint rectangular inputs and arbitrary expected support.

There is a smaller formula when the individual vertex actions on the kernel are determined by edge columns.
Put \(p=\pi_{\ker Y}\), \(q=s(E(p))\), and suppose bounded \(d_j\in M_{m,n}(B)\) satisfy \(d_jq=d_j\) and \(Y_jp=d_jp\).
It suffices to supply \(d_1\), since one may then take \(d_2=-d_1\).
The two vertex matrices \(V_j=Y_jq-d_j\), with all their output rows retained, satisfy
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \nu(Y)=\operatorname{tr}(q)
       -\operatorname{rk}_{G_1}(V_1)-\operatorname{rk}_{G_2}(V_2).}
 \tag{2}
\]
Neither formula requires a positive lower bound on the nonzero spectrum of the expected kernel.

For \(H=\langle z\rangle\cong\mathbb Z\), let \(k\subseteq\mathbb C\) be a conjugation-stable field and \(Y_j\in M_{m,n}(kG_j)\).
If \(Q\) has entries in \(k(z)\), (1) expresses \(\nu(Y)\) as an integer minus the ranks of two finite vertex group-ring matrices over \(k\).
The same conclusion follows from (2) when \(q,d_1\) have entries in \(k(z)\).
In either case independent integrality of the two indicated vertex ranks suffices.

## Argument

### Free vertex projections with a common kernel of full expected support

Let two unital von Neumann subalgebras \(A_1,A_2\) of a finite tracial von Neumann algebra \(A\) be free over their common unital subalgebra \(B_0\), with its trace-preserving expectation \(E_0\).
Take projections \(e_j\in A_j\), set \(e=e_1\wedge e_2\), and assume \(s(E_0(e))=1\).
We prove that \(e_1\vee e_2=1\) and hence
\[
        \operatorname{tr}(e)=\operatorname{tr}(e_1)+\operatorname{tr}(e_2)
                             -\operatorname{tr}(1).
        \tag{3}
\]
For projections \(a,b\), the polar decomposition of \((1-a)b\) has initial projection \(b-(a\wedge b)\) and range projection \((a\vee b)-a\), giving the projection dimension formula used here.

First suppose \(E_0(e)\) has a bounded inverse.
The positive operators \(X_j=1-e_j\) are free over \(B_0\), and the kernel of their sum is \(e\), by the sum of their quadratic forms.
Normalize the finite faithful normal trace to a state and apply [Belinschi, Bercovici and Liu, arXiv v2, Theorems 3.2 and 3.5(vii), pp. 6–8](../audits/literature/BBL21/Yin_U001.md#general-base-algebra-and-the-a0001-strategy-interface) at the selfadjoint edge point zero.
Their norm boundary values \(b_j\in B_0\) satisfy \((X_j-b_j)e=0\) because \(e\) is the meet of the two shifted kernel projections.
Since \(X_je=0\), bimodularity gives \(b_jE_0(e)=0\), and invertibility gives \(b_j=0\).
Their trace identity now gives (3) after restoring the original trace scale, so \(e_1\vee e_2=1\) by the dimension formula and faithfulness.

For general full support, let \(0<t<1\) and \(h_t=1_{[0,t)}(E_0(e))\in B_0\).
The two enlarged projections \(e_{j,t}=e_j\vee h_t\) still belong to the respective free algebras.
Their meet \(e_t\) dominates \(e\) and \(h_t\), hence
\[
 E_0(e_t)\ge \tfrac12(E_0(e)+h_t)\ge \tfrac t2\,1.
\]
The bounded-inverse case yields \((e_1\vee e_2)\vee h_t=e_{1,t}\vee e_{2,t}=1\).
By the dimension formula \(\operatorname{tr}(1-(e_1\vee e_2))\le\operatorname{tr}(h_t)\).
Full support gives \(h_t\downarrow0\) as \(t\downarrow0\), so normality and faithfulness give \(e_1\vee e_2=1\), as required.
This limit proves the trace assertion without taking a limit of the subordination functions.

### Two rows on the support of their common kernel

Consider any two bounded rectangular vertex matrices \(L_j\), possibly with different output sizes, with a common input space of size \(N\).
Let \(P_0\) project onto their common kernel and \(Q_0=s(E(P_0))\).
Then \(P_0\le Q_0\) because \(E((1-Q_0)P_0(1-Q_0))=0\) and the expectation is faithful.
The common kernel projection of \(L_1Q_0,L_2Q_0\) on the entire input space is exactly \(P_0+1-Q_0\).
Indeed their restrictions to the range of \(Q_0\) have common kernel \(P_0\), and both vanish on the range of \(1-Q_0\).
The expectation of this full-space projection is \(E(P_0)+1-Q_0\), with support the identity.
The vertex matrix algebras are free over \(M_N(B)\): the group normal form gives freeness for the subgroup expectation, and expansion of entries in centered alternating matrix products gives its matrix amplification.
The individual full-space kernel projections of \(L_jQ_0\) lie in those two algebras by spectral calculus of \((L_jQ_0)^*(L_jQ_0)\).
Applying (3) to them and subtracting \(\operatorname{tr}(1-Q_0)\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(P_0)=\operatorname{tr}(Q_0)
                    -\operatorname{rk}_{G_1}(L_1Q_0)-\operatorname{rk}_{G_2}(L_2Q_0).
 \tag{4}
\]
The ranks of the vertex matrices agree with their ranks in \(M\) because the inclusions of the vertex von Neumann algebras are normal and trace preserving.
For any edge projection \(U\), the common kernel of \(L_1,L_2\) inside \(U\) is \(P_0\wedge U\).
The stacked operator on \(U\) therefore has rank \(\operatorname{tr}(U)-\operatorname{tr}(P_0\wedge U)\), bounded above by \(\sum_j\operatorname{rk}(L_j U)\) because its range lies in the direct sum of the individual closed ranges.
Hence \(\operatorname{tr}(U)-\sum_j\operatorname{rk}(L_j U)\le\operatorname{tr}(P_0)\), proving maximality at \(Q_0\).

For the rows \(R_j\) of (1), the two kernel equations at \((x,y)\) say \(y=Y_1x=-Y_2x\).
The map \(x\mapsto(x,Y_1x)\) is bounded below, maps \(\ker Y\) onto this closed graph, and is equivariant for the right group action.
Its polar decomposition on \(\ker Y\) therefore gives \(\operatorname{tr}(P)=\nu(Y)\).
Formula (4) and its maximization statement now give (1).

### Determined edge columns

For (2), the same support argument gives \(p\le q\).
As \((d_1+d_2)p=Yp=0\), taking the expectation gives \((d_1+d_2)E(p)=0\).
The range of \(E(p)\) is dense in the range of \(q\), so boundedness and \(d_jq=d_j\) give \(d_1+d_2=0\).
Each \(V_j\) annihilates \(p\) and \(1-q\).
Conversely, if \(V_1x=V_2x=0\), then \(Yqx=(V_1+V_2)x=0\), so \(qx\in p\mathcal H^n\), where \(\mathcal H=\ell^2(G)\).
The full common kernel is thus \(p+1-q\), whose expectation has full support.
Applying (3) to the two vertex kernel projections and subtracting \(\operatorname{tr}(1-q)\) yields (2).
The proof assumes the indicated kernel relation for the edge columns and requires no assertion about norm boundary limits for \(Y\).

### Clearing cyclic denominators in the vertex matrices

For a projection over \(k(z)\) its rank over the field is the rank of almost every unit-circle fiber, by its minors.
Its group trace with the unnormalized matrix trace is that integer under Fourier transformation.
Choose a nonzero common polynomial denominator \(\Delta\in k[z]\) for all entries of \(Q\) in (1), or of \(q,d_1\) in (2).
Each entry is bounded on the circle in the essential sense, so every reduced denominator has no circle zero: a rational pole makes the entry unbounded on arcs of positive measure.
The product of the reduced denominators can thus be used for \(\Delta\).
Right multiplication of a vertex matrix by the diagonal edge matrix \(\Delta I\) on its input preserves its range, since this matrix is boundedly invertible.
For (1) the cleared matrices are \(R_j(Q\Delta)\).
For (2) they are \(Y_j(q\Delta)-d_j\Delta\).
The factors in parentheses have polynomial entries over \(k\), so these products are finite matrices in their respective vertex group rings.
The right-sided order allows \(H\) to be noncentral in either vertex.
The rationality assertion here is a sufficient condition on the edge data.
For general finite group-ring inputs over a cyclic edge, rationality of the expected graph support in (1) remains open here.

### The correction for prescribed boundaries

In [the prescribed-boundary family](Yin_C_A0007_v11.md#an-arbitrary-finite-matrix-rank-on-the-same-coordinates), keep its notation \(R_a,R_b,M,S,u\), and write \(P_\Gamma\in M_{m,r}(k\Gamma)\) for its arbitrary finite input.
There \(q\) is the constant projection onto the first \(4r\) coordinates, and the determined columns are the stated opposite edge matrices supported on the middle \(r\) coordinates.
The remaining \(m\) input columns of each \(V_j\) vanish.
On the first \(4r\) input coordinates, written \((v,w)\) with sizes \(3r,r\), the two nonzero output expressions are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_a(v,w)&=(R_a^*MR_av,\ 0,\ P_\Gamma(Sv-uw)),\\
 V_b(v,w)&=(-R_b^*MR_bv,\ 0,\ 0).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Each \(R_j\) maps onto \(r\) coordinates through an identity block, and \(M\) is positive and boundedly invertible, so \(R_j^*MR_j\) has rank \(r\).
In the first formula one can prescribe \(Sv-uw\) arbitrarily for each fixed \(v\), by varying \(w\) since \(u\) is unitary.
Its closed range is therefore the direct sum of the closed range of \(R_a^*MR_a\) and that of \(P_\Gamma\) in their separate output blocks.
The two vertex ranks in (2) are \(r+\operatorname{rk}_\Gamma(P_\Gamma)\) and \(r\), respectively, by subgroup induction.
Consequently (2) gives \(\nu(Y)=2r-\operatorname{rk}_\Gamma(P_\Gamma)\).
The compression has nullity \(2r\) in the cited calculation, so its full defect is the contribution of the bottom rows of the first vertex column.
