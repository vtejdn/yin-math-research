# Normal compressed transport and a finite incidence boundary for constant potentials

Date: 2026-11-12 (Asia/Hong_Kong).
This development for [A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract) changes the coefficient interface of the [rank-two abelian classification](../claims/Yin_C_A0005_v4.md#statement).
Its kernel projection comes from a specified finite regular-transport tree over a general torsion-free coefficient group, with an isometric group-ring frame and explicitly invertible Gram matrices.
No division property for that coefficient group's actual rational closure is assumed.
Normal compressed hopping gives an exact residual spectral constraint: its selfadjoint part has no nonzero atoms, and its zero atom is precisely the hopping kernel.
The constructed family has noncommuting transport with an infinite orbit, yet every nonzero real scalar potential has an independently computed integer kernel dimension.
At zero potential the same family retains precisely the unresolved scalar kernel of \(1+u+v\).
This establishes a sufficient structured-matrix certificate and a precise limitation, without a new Atiyah group class or a historical-novelty claim.

## Statement

In an actual group extension by an infinite cyclic group, normal compressed hopping has the exact kernel formula (3), for every real scalar potential.
The finite torsion-free construction (6)--(18) has an explicit trace-one group-ring kernel frame, independently invertible finite Gram matrices, and the complete kernel projections (19) and (22).
For nonidentity coefficient input its projection orbit is infinite and noncommuting.
Every nonzero scalar potential gives total kernel dimension one; zero potential retains exactly one plus the coefficient nullity of the specified trinomial.
No coefficient division premise, zero-potential injectivity, whole-group Atiyah property or new group-class coverage is asserted.

## Argument

### Normal homogeneous hopping and scalar potentials

Let \(N\) be any discrete group, let \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(N)\), and let \(G=N\rtimes_\sigma\langle t\rangle\), where the quotient generated by \(t\) is infinite cyclic.
Use canonical regular traces and unnormalized matrix traces.
Let \(p\in M_d(\mathcal N(N))\) be a projection, and work on the corner \(p\ell^2(G)^d\).
Write
\[
K=ptp|_p,\qquad R_\varepsilon=K+K^*+\varepsilon p,
\qquad \varepsilon\in\mathbb R.
\tag{1}
\]
Suppose that \(K\) is normal.
Equivalently, the following actual projection-compression identity holds:
\[
p\sigma(p)p=p\sigma^{-1}(p)p.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed the two sides are \(KK^*\) and \(K^*K\), respectively.
This condition does not assert that \(p\) commutes with \(\sigma(p)\).

Let \(\delta=P_{\ker(K|_p)}\).
The exact conclusions are
\[
\boxed{
P_{\ker(R_\varepsilon|_p)}=
\begin{cases}
\delta,&\varepsilon=0,\\
0,&\varepsilon\ne0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\delta=p\wedge\sigma^{-1}(I_d-p)=p\wedge\sigma(I_d-p).
}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(K+K^*\) has no spectral atoms away from zero.
If \(\varepsilon\ne0\), it is \(R_\varepsilon\), rather than \(K\), whose actual affiliated inverse is independently established.
No bound for that inverse and no integer trace for \(\delta\) are asserted.
The conclusion needs the height action and normality, but not torsion-freeness or a division coefficient algebra.

### Normal spectral proof

The height character defines trace-preserving automorphisms \(\alpha_\theta\) of \(M_d(\mathcal N(G))\), fixing every coefficient from \(\mathcal N(N)\) and multiplying \(t\) by \(e^{i\theta}\).
It fixes \(p\) and satisfies \(\alpha_\theta(K)=e^{i\theta}K\).
The spectral theorem for the bounded normal operator \(K\) gives a projection-valued measure \(E_K\) on \(\mathbb C\).
Its finite scalar measure
\[
\mu(\Omega)=(\operatorname{Tr}_d\otimes\tau_G)(E_K(\Omega))
\tag{4}
\]
has total mass \((\operatorname{Tr}_d\otimes\tau_G)(p)\).
Functoriality of normal spectral calculus under \(\alpha_\theta\) and trace preservation make \(\mu\) invariant under every complex rotation.

Let \(L_\varepsilon=\{z\in\mathbb C:2\operatorname{Re}z+\varepsilon=0\}\).
The normal spectral theorem identifies the kernel projection of \(R_\varepsilon\) with \(E_K(L_\varepsilon)\).
Rotation invariance and Tonelli's theorem for the finite positive measure give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(L_\varepsilon)
&=\int_{\mathbb C}\frac1{2\pi}
  \int_0^{2\pi}
   \mathbf1_{\{2\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\theta}z)+\varepsilon=0\}}
   \,d\theta\,d\mu(z)\\
&=\begin{cases}\mu(\{0\}),&\varepsilon=0,\\0,&\varepsilon\ne0.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
For \(z\ne0\), the equation in the indicator has at most two solutions for \(\theta\) modulo \(2\pi\), so its angular measure is zero.
For \(z=0\), it holds for every \(\theta\) exactly when \(\varepsilon=0\).
At zero potential, \(E_K(\{0\})\le E_K(L_0)\), and equality of their traces gives equality of projections by faithfulness.
At nonzero potential, faithfulness gives the zero kernel projection.
Since \(K\) is normal, its kernel equals its adjoint kernel.
The elementary compressed-transport kernel equations identify those two kernels with the two intersections in (3).
This proves all of (3).

Finite-trace polar decomposition now supplies the affiliated corner inverse of \(R_\varepsilon\) when \(\varepsilon\ne0\).
Every operator used before that conclusion was bounded; the proof does not substitute vectors into an unbounded inverse.
The argument refines the retained [homogeneous character-orbit mechanism](../ledger/Yin_L_A0007_v1.md#the-character-orbit-certificate) by using normal spectral sets rather than point eigenprojections.
It does not remove the zero spectral projection of the original homogeneous operator.

## Specified group and finite regular-transport input

Let \(H\) be a torsion-free group, and choose \(u,v\in H\), allowing equality and identity values.
Put
\[
c=1+u+v\in\mathbb CH,
\qquad
(w_1,\ldots,w_6)=(u,u^{-1},v,v^{-1},u^{-1}v,v^{-1}u).
\tag{6}
\]
Take
\[
N=H*F(z_1,\ldots,z_6),\qquad
\sigma|_H=\mathrm{id},\qquad\sigma(z_j)=z_jw_j,
\qquad G=N\rtimes_\sigma\langle t\rangle.
\tag{7}
\]
These prescriptions define an automorphism: the inverse fixes \(H\) and sends \(z_j\) to \(z_jw_j^{-1}\).
The free product \(N\) is torsion-free.
Indeed a cyclically reduced free-product word of length at least two has powers of strictly growing reduced length, while an element conjugate into a factor has finite order only if that factor does.
Both factors are torsion-free.
A finite-order element of \(G\) maps trivially to its infinite cyclic quotient and then belongs to torsion-free \(N\), so \(G\) is also torsion-free.

For a nine-vertex star with root vertex zero, specify transport from the root to its eight leaves by
\[
d=(1,1,z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4,z_5,z_6)^{\mathsf T}\in M_{8,1}(\mathbb CN).
\]
The incidence matrix, its isometric kernel frame and its kernel projection are
\[
A=(-d\ I_8)\in M_{8,9}(\mathbb CN),\qquad
V=\frac13\binom{1}{d}\in M_{9,1}(\mathbb CN),\qquad
p=VV^*\in M_9(\mathbb CN).
\tag{8}
\]
Each row of \(A\) is the finite transport equation \(\xi_j-d_j\xi_0\), exactly the [regular-transport incidence convention](../claims/Yin_C_A0003_v1.md#finite-graph-certificate) with the edge directed from leaf \(j\) to the root and label \(d_j\).
There are no loops or nontrivial cycle equations in this tree.
Since \(d^*d=8\), one has \(V^*V=1\), \(AV=0\), and every vector in \(\ker A\) has the form \((\xi_0,d\xi_0)\).
Thus (8) is the complete actual orthogonal kernel projection, with unnormalized trace one.
The frame is a bounded equivariant isometry with bounded inverse \(V^*\) on its range.
No measurable projection is inserted as input, and no coefficient rational closure is assumed to be a division ring.

### Gram and normalization units before using the residual

The row Gram operator and its explicit inverse are
\[
AA^*=I_8+dd^*,\qquad
J=(AA^*)^{-1}=I_8-\frac19dd^*.
\tag{9}
\]
Multiplication verifies (9), using \((dd^*)^2=8dd^*\).
In particular the inverse itself still has finite group-ring coefficients.
Let
\[
B=A^*A,\qquad q=I_9-p=A^*JA,\qquad
X=p+A^*J^2A.
\tag{10}
\]
These are bounded finite group-ring matrices.
The identities \(Bp=0\), \(AA^*J=JAA^*=I_8\), and \(A^*JA=q\) give
\[
(B+p)X=X(B+p)=I_9,\qquad BX=XB=q,\qquad Xp=pX=p.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the Gram and normalization inverses are completely certified before any target scalar or residual is declared injective.
The same facts follow from \(AA^*\ge I_8\), but the polynomial formulas retain the exact finite coefficients.

## Normal hopping, noncommuting transport and the exact scalar interface

Let \(T=(t+t^{-1})I_9\), and set \(K=ptp|_p\).
The isometric compression of its forward coefficient is
\[
C=V^*\sigma(V)
=\frac19\left(3+\sum_{j=1}^6w_j\right)
=\frac19c^*c.
\tag{12}
\]
This identity retains the order \(u^{-1}v\) and \(v^{-1}u\), with no commutation assumption on \(u,v\).
Since \(\sigma\) fixes \(H\), it fixes \(C\), so \(tC=Ct\).
Consequently
\[
V^*KV=Ct,\qquad
V^*(K+K^*)V=C(t+t^{-1}),\qquad
(Ct)^*(Ct)=(Ct)(Ct)^*=C^2.
\tag{13}
\]
This proves the normality premise (2) directly.
It uses only the positive scalar \(c^*c\), not its inverse.

Let \(e=P_{\ker_H c}\in\mathcal N(H)\).
It is the actual spectral kernel projection, with no assertion that \(e\) belongs to the rational closure.
Because \(C=c^*c/9\), \(\ker C=\ker c\).
Also \(t\) commutes with \(\mathcal N(H)\), including \(e\).
Thus the two transported defects coincide and are
\[
\delta=p\wedge\sigma^{-1}(I_9-p)
=p\wedge\sigma(I_9-p)=VeV^*.
\tag{14}
\]
Their common trace is \(\dim_H\ker c\), which has not been independently shown to vanish in general.

### Noncommutation and infinite transport orbit

Suppose that at least one of \(u,v\) is nonidentity.
Then
\[
[p,\sigma(p)]\ne0.
\tag{15}
\]
To prove this independently of the unknown kernel of \(c\), observe that
\[
V^*\sigma(p)V=CC^*=C^2.
\]
If \(p\) and \(\sigma(p)\) commuted, \(p\sigma(p)p\) would be a projection, so \(C^2\) would be a projection.
The operator \(C=c^*c/9\) is a positive contraction because \(\|c\|\le3\).
Its functional calculus would then make \(C\) itself a projection.

But \(C\) is convolution by a finitely supported probability measure with nonnegative real coefficients, symmetric support and a positive identity coefficient.
After coincident labels are combined, its support \(S\) still contains \(u,v\) and their inverses.
The identity \(C^2=C\) would imply \(S^2=S\): every product of two positive coefficients is positive, so there is no support cancellation.
A finite subset of a group that contains the identity and is closed under multiplication is a finite subgroup, since positive powers of each element eventually repeat and yield its inverse.
Torsion-freeness of \(H\) would force \(S=\{1\}\), contrary to the choice of \(u,v\).
This contradiction proves (15), including all coincidences among the six labels in (6).
No estimate of \(\dim_H\ker c\) enters this proof.

The orbit of \(p\) is infinite under the same hypothesis.
Some \(w_j\ne1\), and the root-to-corresponding-leaf entry of \(\sigma^n(p)\) is \(z_jw_j^n/9\).
Equality \(\sigma^n(p)=p\) for \(n\ne0\) would imply \(w_j^n=1\), contradicting torsion-freeness.
Thus normal compressed hopping is compatible with an infinite, genuinely noncommuting orbit of an actual finite group-ring projection.
When \(u=v=1\), all \(w_j=1\), \(\sigma\) is the identity, and the projection is stationary instead.

### Canonical trace product and complete residual law

The subgroup generated by \(H\) and \(t\) is the actual direct product \(H\times\mathbb Z\).
Indeed \(t\) commutes with \(H\), and the unique semidirect-product normal form makes \(ht^n=1\) imply \(h=1,n=0\).
Its regular von Neumann algebra embeds in \(\mathcal N(G)\) with its canonical trace: the restriction of left convolution decomposes over right cosets into copies of the subgroup regular representation.
For \(a\in\mathcal N(H)\), this trace is \(\tau_G(at^n)=0\) when \(n\ne0\) and \(\tau_H(a)\) when \(n=0\).
Equivalently the commuting coefficient and cyclic algebras have the actual tensor-product trace.
This is a direct-product regular-trace fact, not freeness or an imposed law on abstract unitaries.

Let \(\mu_C\) be the spectral probability measure of \(C\) under \(\tau_H\).
In the trace-one \(p\)-corner, the complete spectral law of \(R_\varepsilon\) is
\[
\mu_{R_\varepsilon}(E)
=\int_{[0,1]}\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\mathbf1_E\bigl(\varepsilon+2r\cos\theta\bigr)
\,d\theta\,d\mu_C(r).
\tag{16}
\]
This follows from the bounded commuting spectral calculus for \(C\) and \(t\), after the isometric compression (13).
For each \(r>0\), the inner measure has no atoms, including at its spectral endpoints.
For \(r=0\), it is a point mass at \(\varepsilon\).
Hence the only possible atom of this residual law is at \(\varepsilon\), with mass
\[
\mu_C(\{0\})=\tau_H(e)=\dim_H\ker(1+u+v).
\tag{17}
\]
At zero energy, (16) proves exactly the two cases in (3) and (14).
The scalar \(t+t^{-1}\) is independently an affiliated unit by its bilateral-shift Fourier multiplier, so at \(\varepsilon=0\) the same kernel equality also follows from (13) and the commutation with \(C\).
No use of \(C^{-1}\) is legitimate or needed.

## Complete finite matrix kernels with and without the potential

The finite input family is the selfadjoint eighteen-square matrix
\[
\mathcal H_\varepsilon=
\begin{pmatrix}
(t+t^{-1}+\varepsilon)I_9&B\\ B&0
\end{pmatrix}\in M_{18}(\mathbb CG),
\qquad \varepsilon\in\mathbb R,
\tag{18}
\]
where \(B=A^*A\) is the explicitly given finite matrix in (8)--(10).
For example \(\varepsilon=1\) is a single concrete nonzero-potential instance; no smallness or dominance condition is imposed.
The complete result is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon\ne0:\quad &P_{\ker\mathcal H_\varepsilon}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&p\end{pmatrix},
&\dim_G\ker\mathcal H_\varepsilon&=1,\\
\varepsilon=0:\quad &&\dim_G\ker\mathcal H_0&=1+\dim_H\ker c.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{19}
\]
The first formula gives unnormalized rank seventeen and probability-normalized zero atom \(1/18\).
At zero potential the probability-normalized zero atom is \((1+\dim_H\ker c)/18\).

For the first case, the lower bounded equation gives \(x=px\).
Multiplying the upper equation by \(p\) gives \(R_\varepsilon x=0\), and (3) forces \(x=0\).
The remaining equation gives \(y=py\).
This proves the actual projection identity, not just a rank equality.

For the second case, let \(\delta=VeV^*\) as in (14).
The complete bounded kernel parametrization is
\[
\ker\mathcal H_0
=\left\{\binom{x}{-XTx+z}:
x\in\delta\ell^2(G)^9,\quad z\in p\ell^2(G)^9\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed \(pTx=0\) on \(\delta\), so \(Tx\in q\ell^2(G)^9\), and (11) gives \(B(-XTx)=-Tx\).
Conversely the two bounded equations force \(x\in\delta\), and then determine the \(q\)-part of \(y\) as in (20).
The parametrization and its inverse are bounded; the inverse reads the upper coordinate and the \(p\)-part of the lower coordinate.
It proves the second dimension formula in (19).

There is also a complete orthogonal projection formula at zero potential.
Define
\[
F_\delta=\binom{\delta}{-XT\delta},\qquad
D_\delta=F_\delta^*F_\delta
=\delta+\delta T X^2T\delta\ge\delta.
\tag{21}
\]
The inverse \((D_\delta)^{-1}_\delta\) is a bounded corner inverse, independently certified by this lower bound.
For the zero corner the ensuing term is defined to be zero.
Then
\[
\boxed{
P_{\ker\mathcal H_0}
=F_\delta(D_\delta)^{-1}_\delta F_\delta^*
 +\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&p\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{22}
\]
The first summand is the orthogonal projection onto the closed graph in (20) with \(z=0\).
It is orthogonal to the lower \(p\)-coordinate because \(pXT\delta=pT\delta=0\).
Together the two summands exhaust (20).
The only unresolved spectral object in (22) is \(e=P_{\ker c}\); the Gram inverse has no such unresolved admissibility premise.

As a separate normalization check, the already known unit \(\operatorname{diag}(I_9,X)\) sends (18) by congruence to the projection-middle matrix with off-diagonal block \(q\).
This preserves closed-kernel dimension and uses only the explicit finite inverses in (11).
The bounded proofs of (19)--(22) do not depend on treating that congruence as unitary or applying an unbounded inverse to a Hilbert vector.

## Degeneracies, previous mechanisms and the missing scalar unit

The formulas hold for all \(u,v\in H\) and all real \(\varepsilon\).
The element \(c\) is never zero in \(\mathbb CH\), since its augmentation is three.
Faithfulness of the regular representation therefore gives \(0\le\dim_H\ker c<1\), without proving that this dimension is zero.
If \(u=v=1\), then \(c=3\), \(C=1\), \(e=0\), and all potentials, including zero, give kernel dimension one.
If exactly one of \(u,v\) is identity, \(c=2+w\) is independently invertible by strict coefficient dominance.
If \(u=v\ne1\), \(c=1+2u\) has the same dominance certificate.
If \(v=u^{-1}\ne1\), it is a nonzero cyclic Laurent polynomial and is an affiliated unit by the cyclic Fourier test.
More generally, cyclic or independently established abelian coefficient cases retain their usual cyclic or Laurent-field certificates.
These cases are already covered and give \(e=0\).

For the free calibration \(H=F(u,v)\), the height on \(H\) assigning both generators degree one gives the independently invertible constant coefficient of \(1+u+v\), so the existing graded-companion argument proves \(e=0\) in \(\mathcal U(H)\).
The subgroup regular-representation comparison transports that unit certificate to \(\mathcal U(G)\); the character of \(H\) need not extend to \(G\).
Then \(N\cong F_8\), and \(G\) is free-by-cyclic, within the known [Lin93 coverage](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#group-class-and-exact-theorems).
No claim is made that arbitrary torsion-free \(H\), \(N\), or \(G\) in (7) lies outside or inside a further known class.
The nonzero-potential calculation requires none of their whole-group Atiyah assertions.

The finite input is controlled by the existing regular-transport theorem, but that theorem only supplies the flat-tree kernel frame and its integer trace.
It does not assert integer trace for the new intersection \(\delta\).
The commuting-orbit formula does not apply when some \(u\) or \(v\) is nonidentity, by (15), and the transport orbit is then infinite.
The rank-two abelian-field classification does not apply: this is a rank-one frame over an arbitrary coefficient group, with no division coefficient algebra.
The zero-defect criterion cannot be used before proving \(e=0\), which is exactly the missing scalar input.
The zero-residual case does not apply because \(C\ne0\), and (13), with the independent cyclic unit \(t+t^{-1}\), makes \(K+K^*\ne0\).

For \(\varepsilon\ne0\), the compressed scalar
\[
\varepsilon+\frac19c^*c(t+t^{-1})
\tag{23}
\]
has independently proved invertibility despite the possibly unknown extreme coefficient \(c^*c\).
This proof uses actual normality and the canonical product trace, not a formal extra variable, an assumed coefficient inverse, or a zero-defect saddle.
It is an application of elementary normal spectral averaging, rather than a claimed new general rank principle.
It supplies an actual rational inverse in the ambient rational closure because (23) is an actual invertible group-ring element.

At \(\varepsilon=0\), however, both the residual and the exact correction in (19) retain \(\ker c\) unchanged.
The family therefore neither proves nor disproves injectivity of the positive-coefficient trinomial \(1+u+v\) over an arbitrary torsion-free group.
This is the same missing kind of scalar-unit certificate exposed by [route four](../strategies/Yin_S_A0004.md#mechanism-and-obstruction), but no equivalence with its differently signed \(1+u-v\) subfamily or with the all-matrix Task is asserted.
The finite star and all its Gram inverses remain certified even if that last scalar input is unresolved.

Passing through nonzero potentials does not supply the missing zero-potential limit.
On the coefficient subspace \(e\), the compressed scalar (23) is precisely \(\varepsilon e\); its inverse restricts to \(\varepsilon^{-1}e\) when \(\varepsilon\ne0\).
Thus a hypothetical nonzero \(e\) is fully compatible with every nonzero-potential certificate and creates an inverse singularity as \(\varepsilon\to0\).
Formula (16) describes this exactly as an atom of mass \(\tau_H(e)\) moving from \(\varepsilon\) to zero.
The new normality constraint controls where that possible atom can occur; it does not determine its arithmetic or remove it at zero potential.
