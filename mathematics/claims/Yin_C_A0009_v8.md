## Exact conclusions and the remaining finite configurations

For every actual torsion-free group, write \(T=1+a-b\) and \(\delta=\dim_G\ker T\), using the unnormalized canonical trace.
The negative ten-cycle classes have eighteen orbits under the two kernel-dimension-preserving substitutions below.
Ten of these orbits are excluded if \(\delta\ge2/9\), leaving exactly the twenty-eight free cyclic classes in the eight explicitly unresolved rows of the table below.
This is a necessary condition, not a classification of actual possible groups or kernels.
In addition, \(\delta\ge4/19\) implies \(C_{10}\in10\mathbb Z\) and \(C_{10}\le10\).

The new analytic ingredients are the following actual central-power estimates.
If \(r^2=s^3=z\ne e\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dim_G\ker\bigl(r-z(s-1)\bigr)&\le1/6,\\
\dim_G\ker\bigl(r-(1-s^{-1})\bigr)&\le1/6,\\
\dim_G\ker\bigl(r\pm(s-z)\bigr)&\le1/10.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
All the arguments take place in the subgroup generated by the actual labels, including any additional relations it has.

## Cyclic and cyclic-normalizer rows inside the actual group

For row 1, \(AAAAB=e\) gives \(b=a^{-4}\).
For row 3, put \(c=a^{-1}b^{-1}\); its representative \(ABABB=e\) gives \(c^2b^{-1}=e\), hence \(b=c^2\) and \(a=c^{-3}\).
For row 4, \(b^5=e\) gives \(b=e\), so \(T=a\).
For row 16, \(Abbbbb=e\) gives \(a=b^5\).
For row 18, put \(c=a^{-1}b^2\); then \(AbbAbbb=e\) is \(c^2b=e\), giving \(b=c^{-2}\) and \(a=c^{-5}\).

For row 13 use \(ABABab=e\), put \(x=ba\), \(t=a\), and substitute \(b=xt^{-1}\).
The relator becomes \(x^{-2}txt^{-1}=e\), so \(txt^{-1}=x^2\).

If x is the identity it is cyclic; otherwise the actual cyclic-normalizer argument of [C_A0009 v4](../claims/Yin_C_A0009_v4.md#a-cyclic-normalizer-exclusion-valid-in-the-actual-quotient) supplies a nontrivial homomorphism onto \(\mathbb Z\), and its checked Lin92 interface makes the original trinomial injective.

## Central compression for the four quantitative rows

Work in \(H=\langle r,s\rangle\) with \(r^2=s^3=z\ne e\).
The element z is central in H and has infinite order, so its canonical spectral law is Haar and every singleton spectral projection is zero.
All kernels in (1) commute with the central bounded functions of z.
Write \(p\) for the kernel projection of the displayed operator, \(A=psp\), and \(R=prp\).
The relations \(r^*=z^{-1}r\) and \(s^2=zs^*\) hold as bounded operators.

For \(r-z(s-1)\), its kernel equation gives \(R=z(A-p)\), and its norm square gives \(A+A^*=p\).
Comparing \(R^*=z^{-1}R\) gives \(A^*-zA=(1-z)p\), hence
\[
(1+z)A=zp.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(r-(1-s^{-1})\), one instead has \(R=p-A^*\); its norm square and the same adjoint identity give exactly (3).
For either sign in \(r\pm(s-z)\), write \(R=\eta(A-zp)\), where \(\eta\in\{1,-1\}\).
The norm square and adjoint identity respectively give
\[
z^{-1}A+zA^*=p,\qquad
A^*-z^{-1}A=(z^{-1}-1)p.
\]
Eliminating \(A^*\) yields
\[
(1+z)A=z^2p.
\tag{4}
\]
These are bounded identities; division by \(1+z\) is initially used only on the central cuts \(f_N=1_{\{|1+z|\ge1/N\}}\), which increase to one.
On each cut, equations (3)--(4) give \(Af_N=c_j(z)pf_N\), where
\[
c_j(z)=\frac{z^j}{1+z},\qquad j=1\text{ or }2.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(\theta=\operatorname{Arg}z\in(-\pi,\pi]\), \(\lambda_k=\exp(i(\theta+2\pi k)/3)\), and
\[
E_k=\frac13(1+\lambda_k^{-1}s+\lambda_k^{-2}s^2),
\qquad 0\le k\le2.
\]
The E_k are the three orthogonal root projections of s, by finite Fourier multiplication and \(s^3=z\).
Using \(s^2=zs^*\), their compressions on the cuts are
\[
pE_kpf_N=t_k^{(j)}(\theta)pf_N,\qquad
t_k^{(j)}(\theta)=\frac13\left(1+
\frac{\cos((j-5/6)\theta-2\pi k/3)}{\cos(\theta/2)}\right).
\tag{6}
\]
For \(-\pi<\theta<\pi\), the denominator is positive.
Positivity of \(pE_kp\) forces p to vanish wherever any weight is strictly negative, using central cuts with \(t_k^{(j)}\le-\varepsilon\) and then normality of the trace exactly as in [C_A0009 v5](../claims/Yin_C_A0009_v5.md#three-root-projections-and-the-exact-allowed-half-circle).

For completeness the allowed sets can be obtained directly from (6).
For an angle \(\phi\), the minimum of the three cosines \(\cos(\phi-2\pi k/3)\) is at most \(-1/2\).
Thus nonnegative weights require \(|\theta|\le2\pi/3\).
For \(0\le\theta\le2\pi/3\) and j = 1, the minimum is \(\cos(\theta/6+2\pi/3)\); comparison with \(-\cos(\theta/2)\) gives \(\theta\le\pi/2\).
For j = 2 put \(\phi=7\theta/6\).
On \(0\le\phi\le2\pi/3\), the minimum is \(\cos(\phi+2\pi/3)\).
In its first half \(0\le\phi\le\pi/3\), the comparison gives \(\theta\le\pi/5\); in its second half it gives \(\theta\ge\pi/2\).
On \(2\pi/3\le\phi\le7\pi/9\), the minimum is \(\cos\phi\), and the comparison gives \(\theta\le3\pi/5\).
Negative angles permute the same three weights.
Except for the finite endpoint sets, all weights are strictly positive exactly on
\[
I_1=(-\pi/2,\pi/2),\qquad
I_2=(-\pi/5,\pi/5)\cup(\pi/2,3\pi/5)
\cup(-3\pi/5,-\pi/2).
\tag{7}
\]
Endpoint sets have zero central spectral projection, so \(p=1_{I_j}(\theta)p\).
Their Haar measures are \(1/2\) and \(3/10\), respectively.

The synthesis row \((p\ sp\ s^2p)\) has Gram \((ps^{l-k}p)_{0\le k,l\le2}\).
The central three-point Fourier matrix diagonalizes that Gram as three times the diagonal matrix with entries \(t_k^{(j)}p\), as in the [exact bounded-cut argument of v5](../claims/Yin_C_A0009_v5.md#three-columns-give-the-conditional-dimension-bound).
On the increasing central cuts inside I_j where all weights are at least \(1/N\), the synthesis is injective on three copies of the cut kernel and its range lies in the cut ambient module.
Polar decomposition and the unnormalized trace therefore give \(3\tau(p)\le\mu(I_j)\) after taking the limit.
This proves all bounds in (1), with complete domain control and no assumed direct-integral representation.

## Exact changes of generators for rows 2, 11, 12 and 17

The following substitutions are invertible group-basis changes.
In each row the original relator is equivalent to \(r^2=s^3=z\); this equivalence follows by direct substitution and cyclic conjugation, without commuting r and s.

The remaining row 15 also admits a common-two-three description, but the present estimate does not settle it.

\]
