## Statement

Let U, V and T be algebraically independent over the complex numbers, and define
\[
 k=\mathbb C(U,V),\qquad L=k(T),\qquad
 W=\frac{T^2}{UV},\qquad
 d=\frac{4(V-W)}{(1-V)(1-W)},\qquad
 e=\frac{4U}{1-U}.
\]
Write
\[
 \Delta=(1-V)(1-W),\qquad
 h=(1-U)(1-V),\qquad p=T^2-UV.
\]
Every displayed coefficient used as a quaternion parameter is a nonzero element of its specified rational field.
For a field F of characteristic different from two, the notation \((a,b)_F\) denotes the quaternion algebra generated by i and j with \(i^2=a\), \(j^2=b\) and \(ij=-ji\).
The fixed input identifies
\[
 Q=d(H_1^2-UH_2^2)-U(R_1^2-VR_2^2)+e(S_1^2-WS_2^2)
 \tag{1}
\]
with an Albert form of
\[
 \mathcal B=(U,d)_L\otimes_L(UV,e)_L.
 \tag{2}
\]
At the accepted scope of Lew06, Theorem 6(1), already checked in L_A0028_v3, division of (2) is equivalent to anisotropy of (1).
No isomorphism between (2) and the original group crossed product is needed or asserted here.
Then \(\mathcal B\) is a division algebra of degree four over L, and Q is anisotropic over L.
Through the fixed actual-model equivalence, all rational solutions of (15) are excluded and every finite complex group-ring matrix over the Promislow group has integer von Neumann kernel dimension.
This is a certificate for that known group class, with no assertion for arbitrary torsion-free groups.

The external search then located two directly relevant primary sources.

[Bec17, *Biquaternion division algebras over rational function fields*, preprint dated 10 October 2017, Section 2, pp. 2--3](../literature/pdf/Bec17--preprint.pdf#page=2), gives the characteristic restriction, the quaternion-to-Brauer-class map and the modulo-two symbol relations used below.
In additive Brauer notation \([a,b]\), these are bilinearity in both arguments, invariance under multiplying an argument by a square, and the Steinberg relation \([a,1-a]=0\).
We accept these foundational identities at that inspected scope.
Symmetry follows by exchanging the quaternion generators, and \([a,a]=0\) over our fields because minus one is a square and \([a,-a]=0\).
The source also states that a quaternion algebra is either split or division.
Its page 3 says that a biquaternion has zero divisors exactly when its class is representable by a single quaternion symbol; we also accept that class-invariant criterion.
The paper's later Bezoutian obstruction is not used.

The decisive input is [Siv10, *Applications of conics to certain quadratic forms over the rational function fields*, Theorem 1.10, pp. 6--7](../literature/pdf/Siv10--preprint.pdf#page=6).
For characteristic different from two, a central finite-dimensional division algebra D/k, and \(a,b\in k^\times\) with a and ab nonsquare, it states
\[
 D_{k(t)}\otimes_{k(t)}(a,t^2-b)_{k(t)}\text{ is division}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D_{k(\sqrt a)}\text{ and }D_{k(\sqrt{ab})}\text{ are division}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The [university submission page](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/lag/man/415.html) identifies this eleven-page preprint as submitted on 26 December 2010.
The characteristic convention, complete theorem statement and its short proof were read.
The proof invokes a conic index-reduction result and a valuation index formula; their original proofs were not re-audited.
The text identifies (3) as another proof of Rowen--Sivatski--Tignol, Theorem 4.1.
We accept (3) as an external theorem after the exact hypothesis checks below, without claiming an independent source-proof audit.
Both publicly available university-hosted originals were acquired on 2026-11-12 (Asia/Hong_Kong) at the linked local paths, with matching title pages and eleven and eight pages respectively.
The canonical bibliography records these inspected preprint versions; no publisher edition or general redistribution licence is inferred from public access.

## Brauer simplification with all square classes retained

All equations in this section are equalities of Brauer classes, with addition corresponding to tensor product.
Because four is a square, bilinearity and Steinberg give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [\mathcal B]
 &=[U,d]+[UV,U/(1-U)]\\
 &=[U,d]+[U,U]+[V,U]+[U,1-U]+[V,1-U]\\
 &=[U,dV]+[V,1-U].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
The inverses in a quaternion slot have the same square class as the original elements.

Set \(N=T^2-UV^2\).
Then
\[
 dV=-\frac{4N}{U\Delta},\qquad
 N=T^2\left(1-U(V/T)^2\right).
 \tag{5}
\]
The Steinberg relation applied to \(U(V/T)^2\), together with square invariance, gives \([U,N]=0\).
Also \([U,-4/U]=0\), because the second slot has the square class of \(-U\).
Consequently (5) implies \([U,dV]=[U,\Delta]\), and hence
\[
 [\mathcal B]=[U,\Delta]+[V,1-U].
 \tag{6}
\]
This cancellation is a rational identity, not the adjoining of a square root of \(\Delta\).

Now
\[
 \Delta=-\frac{(1-V)p}{UV}.
\]
Using that minus one is a square gives
\[
 [\mathcal B]=[U,p]+[U,V]+[U,1-V]+[V,1-U].
 \tag{7}
\]
Define the constant quaternion algebra
\[
 D_0=(Uh,Vh)_k.
 \tag{8}
\]
Its class is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [D_0]
 &=[U,V]+[U,h]+[h,V]+[h,h]\\
 &=[U,V]+[U,1-V]+[V,1-U].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]
Here \([h,h]=0\), while \([U,1-U]=[V,1-V]=0\).
Combining (7)--(9) yields the required constant/nonconstant separation:
\[
 \boxed{
 [\mathcal B]=[D_0\otimes_k L]+[U,T^2-UV].
 }
 \tag{10}
\]
In particular the relevant constant algebra is D_0, rather than the different quaternion obtained by specializing T to zero.

## Division of the two required constant extensions

The elements U and V are nonsquare in k, by their odd valuations at U = 0 and V = 0.
Write
\[
 k_U=k(\sqrt U)=\mathbb C(u,V),\quad U=u^2,
 \qquad
 k_V=k(\sqrt V)=\mathbb C(U,v),\quad V=v^2.
\]
These are fields of characteristic zero with the indicated pairs of algebraically independent variables.
Formula (9) gives
\[
 [D_0\otimes_k k_U]=[V,1-u^2],\qquad
 [D_0\otimes_k k_V]=[U,1-v^2].
 \tag{11}
\]
We prove that both displayed quaternion classes are nonsplit by an elementary rational valuation argument.

First let \(F_0=\mathbb C(u)\) and \(c=1-u^2\in F_0\).
The valuation of c at u = 1 is one, so c is nonsquare in F_0.
For \(r,s\in F_0(V)\), not both zero, the binary expression
\[
 n_c(r,s)=r^2-cs^2
\]
is nonzero and has even V-adic valuation.
Indeed, put \(m=\min\{v_V(r),v_V(s)\}\), assigning infinity to a zero entry, and divide the pair by \(V^m\).
At least one resulting residue in F_0 is nonzero.
Cancellation in the residue of \(r^2-cs^2\) would make c a square in F_0, so no cancellation occurs and the valuation is exactly 2m.
This argument uses the discrete valuation at V = 0 on the rational field, whose residue field is F_0 and whose residue characteristic is zero.
It requires no completion or henselian extension.

The conjugation norm in \((V,c)_{F_0(V)}\), after an ordering of its four coordinates, is
\[
 n_c(r,s)-V n_c(t,w).
 \tag{12}
\]
When both coordinate pairs are nonzero, the two summands have opposite valuation parity and cannot cancel.
If exactly one pair is nonzero, its summand is nonzero by the preceding binary argument.
Thus the norm is nonzero on every nonzero quaternion, and conjugation divided by the norm supplies its inverse.
This proves that \((V,1-u^2)_{k_U}\) is a division algebra.
Interchanging U with V and u with v gives the complete proof that \((U,1-v^2)_{k_V}\) is division, now using the U-adic valuation with residue field \(\mathbb C(v)\).

Consequently both algebras on the left side of (11) are division: they have quaternion degree two, and their Brauer classes are nonzero.
It follows that D_0 itself is division, since a split quaternion algebra over k remains split after every field extension.
The quaternion dichotomy is the only classification fact needed at this step.

## Applying the global criterion

Apply (3) with
\[
 D=D_0,\qquad a=U,\qquad b=UV,\qquad t=T.
\]
The field k has characteristic zero, D_0 is central of degree two and has just been proved division, and T is transcendental over k.
The parameter a is nonsquare, and \(ab=U^2V\) is nonsquare because its square class is V.
Moreover
\[
 k(\sqrt a)=k_U,\qquad k(\sqrt{ab})=k_V,
\]
since U is a nonzero element of k.
The two division hypotheses in (3) are exactly those proved in (11)--(12).
The criterion therefore proves that
\[
 D_0\otimes_k L\otimes_L(U,T^2-UV)_L
 \tag{13}
\]
is a division algebra of degree four over L.

Equation (10) says that (13) and \(\mathcal B\) have the same Brauer class.
By the class-invariant biquaternion criterion checked in Bec17, the division algebra (13) has a class which is not a single quaternion symbol.
The same is true of \(\mathcal B\), so it too is division.
In index language, their common class has index four.
The accepted Albert theorem now gives
\[
 \boxed{Q\text{ is anisotropic over }\mathbb C(U,V,T).}
 \tag{14}
\]
The external input here is a global rational-function index criterion, not an inference from isotropy or splitting at individual completions.

## Consequences for the retained mixed norm equation

By the fixed equivalence (18) of L_A0028_v2, (14) excludes every rational mixed solution in the actual Promislow extension.
In the notation of that material, with \(F=\mathbb C(x,y,z)\), \(\beta(x,y,z)=(x^{-1},y,z^{-1})\) and \(\sigma_a(x,y,z)=(x,y^{-1},z^{-1})\), the simultaneous equations
\[
 \sigma_a(f)f+xz\,\sigma_a(g)\beta(g)=x,
 \qquad
 \sigma_a(f)g+\frac{xz}{y}\sigma_a(g)\beta(f)=0
 \tag{15}
\]
have no solution \((f,g)\in F^2\).
The conclusion includes the mixed case with both entries nonzero, which the earlier separate valuation exclusions did not reach.
It therefore proves division of the actual algebra \(\mathscr A\) in L_A0028_v2 and fourfold field-rank divisibility for every rational equivariant idempotent in every matrix size.
Together with the retained rational Gram construction and exact normalized trace, it yields integer von Neumann kernel dimensions for all finite complex-coefficient matrices over P.
The group is already covered by known results, and no new group class is claimed.

The explicit isotropic vectors in L_A0028_v3/v4 remain valid over their stated larger fields.
They do not contradict (14), because an anisotropic form can become isotropic after extension and their required square roots are absent from L.
Those fixed failures still explain why the local certificate attempts did not prove this global result.
