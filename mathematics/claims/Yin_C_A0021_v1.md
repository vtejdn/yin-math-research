# Laurent kernels on four cyclic cosets and expansion of support under a zero product

## Statement

Let \(G\) be a torsion-free discrete group, \(H=\langle t\rangle\le G\) infinite cyclic, \(R=\mathbb C[H]=\mathbb C[t,t^{-1}]\) and \(F=\mathbb C(t)\).
Write \(r(T)\) and \(\nu(T)\) for the regular range and kernel dimensions of a scalar \(T\in\mathbb C[G]\), using left convolution and the canonical trace.
For a finite nonempty \(I\subset G/H\), let \(V_I\) be the space of square-summable functions on the union of those left cosets, embedded by zero extension into \(\ell^2(G)\).
Choosing representatives identifies the finitely supported vectors on these cosets with \(R^{|I|}\) by \( (f_i)\mapsto\sum_i g_i f_i\).
Let \(T_I\) be the Laurent matrix of left multiplication by \(T\) on these vectors, using the occupied output cosets \((\operatorname{supp}T)I\).
Since \(T\) commutes with right multiplication by \(H\), these are matrices of \(R\)-module maps.
Changes of representatives multiply the corresponding coordinates by Laurent monomials.

1. Whenever \(0<\nu(T)<1\),
   \[
              \dim_F\ker(T_I:F^{|I|}\longrightarrow F^{|(\operatorname{supp}T)I|})
                  < |I|\,\nu(T).
   \tag{1}
   \]
2. Suppose \(a,b\in\mathbb C[G]\) satisfy \(ab=0\) and \(r(a)=r(b)=1/2\).
   If \(\operatorname{supp}b\) meets exactly four left \(H\)-cosets \(I\), then \(a_I\) has rank three over \(F\), and every three of its columns are independent.
   Its Laurent kernel is free of rank one, generated by the four entries of \(b\) divided by their Laurent greatest common divisor.
   The same assertions apply to \(b^*\) and the four occupied cosets of \(a^*\) if such a cover is given, even for another infinite cyclic subgroup.
3. Under the same zero-product and half-rank hypotheses, for every nonempty finite \(X\subset G/H\),
   \[
      |(\operatorname{supp} b)X|\ \ge\ 2\lfloor |X|/2\rfloor+3,\qquad
      |(\operatorname{supp} a^*)X|\ \ge\ 2\lfloor |X|/2\rfloor+3 .
   \tag{2}
   \]
   Thus two distinct input cosets require at least five output cosets, and four require at least seven.
   The sets in (2) count the union of all possible output cosets before coefficient cancellation.

## Strict dimension bound on a finite coset window

Put \(m=|I|\), let \(p\in\mathcal N(G)\) be the regular kernel projection of \(T\), and denote the coordinate projection onto \(V_I\) by \(e_I\).
Under \(V_I\simeq\ell^2(H)^m\), the compression \(C=e_Ipe_I|_{V_I}\) is a positive contraction in \(M_m(\mathcal N(H))\).
Its unnormalized \(H\)-trace is \(m\nu(T)\), since at each \(\delta_{g_i}\) the diagonal coefficient is the canonical trace of \(p\).
The full Hilbert kernel on this window is \(V_I\cap p\ell^2(G)\).
Its projection \(P\) in \(V_I\) is dominated by \(C\), since \(C\) acts as the identity on that intersection.
Fourier transformation on \(H\) realizes the restricted convolution as the same finite Laurent matrix \(T_I\) evaluated on the circle.
A nonzero maximal minor over \(F\) vanishes at only finitely many circle points, while all larger minors vanish identically.
The zero matrix has rank zero on every fiber.
Hence \(\operatorname{Tr}_{H,m}P=\dim_F\ker T_I\), giving the weak inequality in (1).

If equality holds, faithfulness gives \(C=P\).
From \(C-C^2=e_Ip(1-e_I)pe_I|_{V_I}=0\) it follows that \((1-e_I)pe_I=0\), so \(e_I\) commutes with \(p\).
Let \(Y\subset G\) be the union of the cosets in \(I\), and let \(L=\{s\in G:sY=Y\}\).
For a nonzero Fourier coefficient \(\widehat p(s)\), the matrix entry of left convolution by \(p\) between \(\delta_g\) and \(\delta_{sg}\) is nonzero for every \(g\).
Commutation with \(e_I\) forces \(g\in Y\) exactly when \(sg\in Y\), hence \(s\in L\).
The group conditional expectation therefore gives \(p=E_Lp\in\mathcal N(L)\).
The [cyclic stabilizer argument](Yin_C_A0019_v1.md#the-stabilizer) applies to any nonempty finite union of left \(H\)-cosets, so \(L\) is cyclic or trivial.

Now \(Tp=0\), hence \(p T^*=0\).
In the decomposition of the nonzero vector \(T^*\delta_e\) along right cosets \(Lg\), choose a nonzero component \(f g\), where \(f\in\mathbb C[L]\).
The action of \(p\in\mathcal N(L)\) preserves each such coset, so \(pf=0\) on the chosen copy.
Over an infinite cyclic \(L\), the nonzero Laurent polynomial \(f\) vanishes on a null set of the circle, forcing the Fourier multiplier \(p\) to vanish almost everywhere.
For trivial \(L\) this follows from \(f\) being a nonzero scalar.
This contradicts \(\nu(T)>0\), proving (1).

## The four-column relation

In the four occupied cosets write \(b=\sum_{i=1}^4g_i f_i\), \(0\ne f_i\in R\).
The equation \(ab=0\) places \(f=(f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)^{\mathsf T}\) in \(\ker a_I\), and (1) gives dimension strictly below \(4(1-r(a))=2\).
Thus \(\ker_F a_I=Ff\).
A relation among any three columns would have a zero coordinate, which is impossible for a nonzero element of this line.
For any three independent rows of \(a_I\), the four signed maximal minors give a nonzero vector on this same line by cofactor expansion.
In particular all four of these minors are nonzero.

Let \(h\) be a greatest common divisor of the \(f_i\) over the Laurent principal ideal domain \(R\) and put \(u_i=f_i/h\).
If \(w\in R^4\) is in the kernel, then \(w=\xi u\) with \(\xi\in F\).
Bézout's identity gives \(c_i\in R\) with \(\sum_i c_i u_i=1\), hence \(\xi=\sum_i c_i w_i\in R\).
Conversely \(Ru\) belongs to the kernel because \(a_Iu=0\) in \(F\) and all entries are Laurent polynomials.
This proves the Laurent assertion.
It also forces the graph on the four columns that joins two columns when some output row is nonzero in both to be connected.
Otherwise the restriction of \(f\) to any one component would give a nonzero relation on a proper subset, because different components use disjoint sets of rows.

## Combining the two factors on a coset space

Put \(m=|X|\), \(Y=(\operatorname{supp}b)X\subset G/H\), and \(k=|Y|\).
The two finite Laurent matrices \(b_X:R^m\to R^k\) and \(a_Y:R^k\to R^{|(\operatorname{supp}a)Y|}\) have zero composition, by associativity in the group ring.
This identity persists over \(F\).
By (1), \(\dim_F\ker b_X<m/2\), hence \(\operatorname{rank}_F b_X\ge\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\).
Its image lies in the Laurent kernel of \(a_Y\), of dimension strictly below \(k/2\) by (1).
The resulting strict inequality \(k>2(\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1)\) proves the first bound in (2).
Apply the same argument to \(b^*a^*=0\), using preservation of regular rank under adjoints, to obtain the second.

For the [complex Atiyah question](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract), these conditions give tests on product cosets and exact Laurent relations for the complementary half-ranks left open by the [joint Fourier estimates](../ledger/Yin_L_A0036_v1.md#nontrivial-intersection-and-the-remaining-gap).
They exclude any candidate failing (2), even if its two individual supports satisfy the four-coset bounds.
A four-column relation with all its proper subsets independent can still satisfy the local rank test, and the estimates here do not exclude all such zero products.
