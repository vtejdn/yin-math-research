# Rank bounds on the exposed coefficient sums of a zero product

## Statement

Let \(a,b\ne0\) in \(\mathbb C[G]\) satisfy \(ab=0\) and \(r(a)+r(b)=1\), where \(r\) is regular rank for the canonical group trace.
Write \(A,B\) for the supports. Assign real weights \(u_x,v_y\) such that \(u_x+v_y\) is constant on each fiber of the actual product table \((x,y)\mapsto xy\).
Write \(a_+,b_+\) for the respective subfactors retaining only the terms of maximal weight, and \(a_-,b_-\) for their minimal counterparts.
Then
\[
 a_+b_+=a_-b_-=0,\qquad r(a_\pm)\le r(a),\quad r(b_\pm)\le r(b).
\]
The weights are tested on the finite table itself and need not be characters of \(G\).
When \(G\) is torsion-free and both ranks are \(1/2\), every one of the four exposed supports contains at least four points and meets at least three cosets in its own cyclic cover, for either fixed coset orientation.
The two maximal supports together contain at least sixteen points counted with their separate cardinalities, and the same is true at the minima.

If every actual product has exactly two representations and both ranks are \(1/2\), there is another zero product on the same two supports, of the same two ranks, in which all coefficients have modulus one.
There is also an integer-lattice criterion for the binomial coefficient equations. If the only real table weights are constant on each side, every solution of those equations can be normalized by two scalars to have roots of unity as coefficients, preserving its original ranks.

## Argument

### Simultaneous generic rank and degeneration

For completeness the polynomial calculation underlying the [central-variable rank bound](Yin_C_A0012_v2.md#statement) has the following form on the complex parameter line.
Let \(P(t)\) be a matrix polynomial of degree at most \(d\) with bounded coefficients in a finite von Neumann algebra.
Fix \(t_0\) and a finite set of \(M>d\) distinct complex numbers.
Use input vector polynomials of degree \(<M-d\) in \(t-t_0\) with coefficients in the initial projection space of \(P(t_0)\).
Multiplying by \(P\) is injective there, since the first nonzero input coefficient gives a nonzero coefficient of the same degree in the output.
Evaluation on the \(M\) points is injective on the resulting polynomials of degree \(<M\) by the scalar Vandermonde identity.
The resulting module map has target in the sum of the closed ranges of the \(P(t_j)\).
Dimension comparison gives \((M-d)r(P(t_0))\le\sum_j r(P(t_j))\).
Putting \(R=\sup_{t\in\mathbb C}r(P(t))\) yields \(\sum_j(R-r(P(t_j)))\le dR\) for each finite set, with the bound automatic when \(M\le d\).
Thus only countably many points of the complex line have rank below \(R\).

For fixed exposed supports, the equalities of weights to be retained, together with the strict inequalities to the other weights, are a rational system of linear conditions.
Rational points are dense in its real solution subspace, so weights giving those same faces can be chosen integral by clearing denominators.
Subtracting the minimum on each side separately gives nonnegative exponents.
Set \(P(t)=\sum a_x t^{u_x}x,\ Q(t)=\sum b_y t^{v_y}y\).
Every coefficient equation of \(ab=0\) has a common power of \(t\), hence \(P(t)Q(t)=0\).
The two generic ranks are attained simultaneously away from a countable set.
Their sum is at most one, since the range of \(Q(t)\) is contained in the kernel of \(P(t)\).
Each supremum is at least its value at \(1\), where the two ranks already sum to one.
Thus both individual ranks are bounded everywhere by their respective ranks at \(1\).
At \(t=0\) this proves the assertion for the minima; negating both weight families gives the maxima.
The same proof works for Laurent polynomials at nonzero \(t\) after a separate scalar monomial shift on each side.

Apply the [two-coset rank bound](Yin_C_A0019_v1.md#consequences-for-group-ring-products) to a nonzero extreme part in a torsion-free group, using adjoints for right-coset covers.
Any set of at most three points fits in at most two cosets of some infinite cyclic subgroup by placing a pair in the same coset (a monomial already has rank one).
This proves the individual restrictions at rank one half.
The sixteen-point restriction for each pair follows from the unique-product theorem as in [the finite product-table test](Yin_C_A0020_v1.md#a-finite-test-on-the-product-table).

### Equal moduli for the paired coefficient equations

For a two-element fiber \(\{(x,y),(x',y')\}\) the two nonzero terms cancel, so \(|a_x|\,|b_y|=|a_{x'}|\,|b_{y'}|\).
The finitely generated multiplicative subgroup of \(\mathbb R_{>0}\) generated by the original moduli is free abelian.
Choose a basis \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_s\); the integral coordinates of the moduli in each basis direction give table weights \(u_{xi},v_{yi}\).
Deform the coefficients by the monomials \(\prod_i t_i^{u_{xi}},\prod_i t_i^{v_{yi}}\).
Starting with all parameters \(1\), choose them one at a time on the circles \(|t_i|=\rho_i^{-1}\).
For fixed other parameters, the preceding polynomial argument preserves both half ranks except at countably many values as long as the starting pair has both half ranks.
Each circle therefore supplies a choice preserving both ranks.
After the last choice every coefficient has modulus one, and all fiber equations and all support points are preserved.

More directly, orient each paired fiber arbitrarily and put \(d_e=e_x+e_y-e_{x'}-e_{y'}\) in the free lattice on the disjoint union of row and column labels.
Nonzero complex coefficients satisfying all binomials exist exactly when every integral dependency \(\sum n_e d_e=0\) has even \(\sum n_e\).
Necessity follows by multiplying the required character values \((-1)^{n_e}\).
For sufficiency this parity condition defines a character on the sublattice spanned by the \(d_e\), sending each generator to \(-1\).
It extends to the free ambient lattice: in Smith normal form take the required roots of its values on the diagonal generators, and assign arbitrary nonzero values on complementary basis vectors.
This test concerns coefficient cancellation alone.
If the table has only the two constant real weight directions, its relation lattice has finite index in the lattice of vectors of row sum and column sum zero.
For each ratio of two row coefficients, raising it to that finite index yields a value in \(\{1,-1\}\); the same holds for the columns.
Dividing by a reference coefficient on each side proves the roots-of-unity assertion for the given solution.

The finite linear equalities and face inequalities give exclusions even for a group with finite abelianization.
They can leave only the constant gradings, and a fiber with more than two terms need not impose additive equalities on the logarithms of coefficient moduli.
Neither step supplies a general exclusion for the four-coset problem.
