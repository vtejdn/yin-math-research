# The smallest subgroup carrying a complementary pair of group-ring kernels

## Statement

Let \(G\) be a discrete group and \(a,b\in\mathbb C[G]\) nonzero, with finite supports \(A,B\).
Represent the algebra by left convolution on \(\ell^2(G)\) and use the canonical trace of \(\mathcal N(G)\).
Write \(i(c)\) and \(f(c)\) for the initial and final projections of \(c\), and \(r(c)\) for their common trace.
Suppose
\[
                          ab=0,\qquad r(a)+r(b)=1 .
\]
Then \(p:=i(a)=1-f(b)\) is a nonzero proper projection.
Set
\[
 D:=\langle\operatorname{supp}(p\delta_e)\rangle,\qquad
 J:=\langle A^{-1}A\cap BB^{-1}\rangle.
\]
For any subgroup \(L\le G\), split \(a\) and \(b\) along the occupied cosets \(x_iL\) and \(Ly_j\), respectively, as
\[
               a=\sum_i x_i a_i,\qquad b=\sum_j b_j y_j,\qquad
               a_i,b_j\in\mathbb C[L].
\]
Then \(a_i b_j=0\) for every \(i,j\) if and only if \(D\le L\).
In particular \(D\le J\).
For the splits over \(D\), letting \(A_i:=\operatorname{supp}(a_i)\) and \(B_j:=\operatorname{supp}(b_j)\) in \(D\), one has
\[
             D=\left\langle\bigcup_i A_i^{-1}A_i\right\rangle
              =\left\langle\bigcup_j B_jB_j^{-1}\right\rangle.            \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(D\) is finitely generated and nontrivial.
It can also be recovered from a finite family of partitions of the actual coefficient sums of \(ab\).
Among the groups generated by row differences within zero-sum blocks, \(D\) is the attained least subgroup.
If no coefficient fiber has a nonempty proper vanishing subsum, then \(D=J\).

Suppose in addition that \(G\) is torsion-free, \(r(a)=r(b)=1/2\), and each of \(A,B\) is covered by at most five cosets of an infinite cyclic subgroup.
For each factor these may be left cosets or right cosets, with independent choices of subgroup and orientation for the two factors.
Then \(D/[D,D]\) is finite.
For the primitive four-right-coset and four-left-coset pair of the [coefficient collision theorem](Yin_C_A0020_v1.md#primitivity-and-coefficient-collisions), one has \(D=J\).

## Localization of the support projections

The product hypothesis gives \(f(b)\le1-i(a)\).
Equality follows from the rank sum and faithfulness of the trace.
Both \(p\) and \(1-p\) are nonzero since \(a,b\ne0\).
For any subgroup \(L\), the trace-preserving conditional expectation \(E_L\) onto \(\mathcal N(L)\) restricts Fourier coefficients to \(L\).
Thus \(p\in\mathcal N(L)\) exactly when \(D\le L\).
The projection \(1-p\) gives the same subgroup because its Fourier coefficients away from the identity are the negatives of those of \(p\).
If \(D\) were trivial, \(p\) would be a scalar projection, contradicting \(0<\tau(p)<1\).

First assume \(D\le L\).
In the equation \(a(1-p)=0\), the vectors \(x_i a_i(1-p)\delta_e\) lie in the mutually disjoint coordinate spaces \(\ell^2(x_iL)\).
Hence \(a_i(1-p)=0\) for every \(i\), using \(\|c\delta_e\|_2^2=\tau(c^*c)\).
Likewise \(pb=0\) gives \(p b_j=0\) by the disjoint right cosets \(Ly_j\).
Consequently \(a_i b_j=a_i p b_j=0\).

Conversely assume that all \(a_i b_j\) vanish, and form
\[
                     P:=\bigvee_i i(a_i),\qquad Q:=\bigvee_j f(b_j)
\]
in \(\mathcal N(L)\).
Each \(f(b_j)\) is orthogonal to every \(i(a_i)\), hence \(P\perp Q\).
The identities \(aP=a\) and \(Qb=b\) give \(p\le P\) and \(1-p\le Q\).
Since \(Q\le1-P\), these inequalities imply \(P=p\) and \(Q=1-p\).
Therefore \(D\le L\).
The inclusion and the product condition are independent of the representatives chosen for the occupied cosets.

For \(L=J\), any equality \(xy=x'y'\) with \(x,x'\in A\), \(y,y'\in B\), gives \(x^{-1}x'=yy'^{-1}\in J\).
Thus all terms contributing to one coefficient of \(ab\) come from a single \(x_iJ\) and a single \(Jy_j\).
Their sum is zero by assumption, so each \(x_i a_i b_j y_j\) vanishes separately.
This proves \(D\le J\).

Now take \(L=D\).
The join of the \(i(a_i)\) is the support projection of the bounded positive element \(\sum_i a_i^*a_i\), since its kernel is the intersection of the individual kernels.
This element belongs to the group algebra of \(\langle\bigcup_i A_i^{-1}A_i\rangle\), hence its support projection \(p\) belongs to the von Neumann algebra of that subgroup by spectral calculus.
The subgroup is contained in \(D\), and the Fourier-support characterization forces the reverse inclusion.
The same argument for \(\sum_j b_j b_j^*\) and \(1-p\) gives the second equality in (1).
Both generating sets in (1) are finite.
In particular \(i(a_i)\le p\), \(f(b_j)\le1-p\), and the ranks of all slices are bounded by \(r(a)\) and \(r(b)\), respectively.
Ranks of elements in \(\mathbb C[D]\) have the same values in the two regular representations, by the trace-preserving subgroup inclusion.

## Recovery from vanishing coefficient subsums

For \(w\in AB\), put \(F_w:=\{(x,y)\in A\times B:xy=w\}\) and give the entry \((x,y)\) weight \(a_x b_y\).
Choose, for every \(w\), a partition of \(F_w\) into nonempty blocks, each of total weight zero.
The full fibers form one such system \(\mathcal P\), and there are only finitely many choices.
For each system let \(L_{\mathcal P}\) be generated by all \(x^{-1}x'\) with \((x,y),(x',y')\) in a common block.
Within a block \(x^{-1}x'=yy'^{-1}\in L_{\mathcal P}\), so its entries have their rows in one left \(L_{\mathcal P}\)-coset and their columns in one right \(L_{\mathcal P}\)-coset.
At each \(w\) the sum restricted to any fixed pair of these cosets is a union of zero-sum blocks.
Thus every pair of corresponding slices has zero product, giving \(D\le L_{\mathcal P}\) by localization.

Conversely, for each \(w\) partition the fiber by its pairs \((xD,Dy)\).
All these blocks have zero sum since all the \(D\)-slice products vanish, and their generating differences lie in \(D\).
This partition therefore attains \(D=L_{\mathcal P}\).
The full-fiber partition gives \(J\), since every common difference \(x^{-1}x'=yy'^{-1}\) comes from a collision \(xy=x'y'\).
If no \(F_w\) has a nonempty proper vanishing subsum, this is the only possible partition, and \(D=J\) for the original factors at any complementary ranks.
In particular this holds when every fiber has size two or three, because all entries are nonzero and their total is zero.

## Characters and the two orientations of cyclic cosets

Assume the additional cyclic-cover and half-rank hypotheses.
If \(D\) has a nonzero homomorphism to \(\mathbb Z\), dividing by the positive generator of the image gives an epimorphism \(\chi:D\to\mathbb Z\).
By (1) there is an index \(i\) on which \(\chi\) takes at least two distinct values on \(A_i\), and there is an index \(j\) on which it takes at least two distinct values on \(B_j\).
Fix these indices.
The corresponding nonzero slices \(a_i,b_j\) have product zero and each has rank at most \(1/2\) in \(D\).

Each of \(A_i\) and \(B_j\) is covered inside \(D\) by at most five cosets of a cyclic subgroup, possibly trivial.
Indeed \(A_i=x_i^{-1}(A\cap x_iD)\) and \(B_j=(B\cap Dy_j)y_j^{-1}\).
Left or right translation of a family of cosets preserves its orientation and translates the representatives, conjugating the common subgroup when necessary.
Each translated coset meeting \(D\) intersects it in a coset of the intersection of its subgroup with \(D\), in the same orientation.
This works separately for each selected slice, with one common subgroup for that slice.

Let \(c\) be either selected slice, and let \(c_+\) and \(c_-\) be its respective highest and lowest \(\chi\)-degree parts.
The [regular rank bound for extreme degrees](Yin_C_A0020_v1.md#ranks-of-the-extreme-degree-terms) yields
\[
                          r_D(c_+),\,r_D(c_-)\le r_D(c)\le 1/2 .          \tag{2}
\]
Write \(H_c\) for the cyclic subgroup of the cover just obtained in \(D\).
If \(\chi(H_c)=0\), each occupied coset of \(H_c\) has constant degree.
Since the support of \(c\) has varying degrees, the highest and lowest parts occupy disjoint nonempty collections of these cosets.
With at most five cosets in all, one of the two parts occupies at most two.
For infinite \(H_c\), the [two-coset rank bound](Yin_C_A0019_v1.md#consequences-for-group-ring-products) gives rank greater than \(1/2\), applying it to the adjoint for a right-coset cover.
For trivial \(H_c\) that part has at most two terms.
A monomial is invertible, and a binomial in distinct group elements has rank one since a unit translate makes it a nonzero Laurent polynomial in an infinite-order element.
Both cases contradict (2).

It follows separately for \(a_i\) and \(b_j\) that \(\chi\) is nonzero on the corresponding cyclic subgroup, and hence injective there.
Every occupied coset then contributes at most one point of any fixed degree.
In particular the supports of \((a_i)_+\) and \((b_j)_+\) each have at most five points.
Their product is the homogeneous part of \(a_i b_j\) in the sum of the two highest degrees, so it vanishes.
But the two finite nonempty supports have cardinality sum at most ten and therefore have a uniquely represented product by [Nielsen–Soelberg, Theorem 1.4](../audits/literature/NS24/readme.md).
The coefficient there is a product of two nonzero complex numbers, a contradiction.
Hence \(\operatorname{Hom}(D,\mathbb Z)=0\), and finite generation from (1) now gives finiteness of the abelianization.

Under the same half-rank and cyclic-cover hypotheses, take any homomorphism \(\varphi:J\to\mathbb Z\).
It vanishes on \(D\).
In each fiber \(F_w\), all rows belong to the same \(x_0J\).
With \(x_0\) fixed for that coset, write each row as \(x=x_0 u\), \(u\in J\).
If two entries lie in one \((xD,Dy)\) block, then their \(u\)'s differ by right multiplication by an element of \(D\), so they have the same \(\varphi(u)\).
For every \(w\) and \(n\in\mathbb Z\) this gives the necessary coefficient test
\[
             \sum_{\substack{(x,y)\in F_w\\ \varphi(x_0^{-1}x)=n}}
                     a_x b_y=0 .                                        \tag{3}
\]
Each displayed sum is a union of the zero-sum blocks used above.
Changing \(x_0\) within its coset adds a fixed integer to all row degrees in that fiber and leaves the test equivalent.
For a character of \(G\), one may use its values on \(x\) directly.
Given the coefficient table and character values, a single nonzero subsum in (3) rules out simultaneous half ranks.

## Primitive pairs and scope for A0001

Under the primitive hypotheses of the four-coset coefficient collision theorem, every left annihilator of \(b\) supported in \(A\) is a scalar multiple of \(a\), and every right annihilator of \(a\) supported in \(B\) is a scalar multiple of \(b\).
For the \(D\)-slices, \(x_i a_i b=0\) by \(a_i(1-p)=0\) and \(b=(1-p)b\).
Similarly \(a b_j y_j=0\).
The supports on each side are disjoint, so the respective uniqueness statements force just one occupied \(x_iD\) and just one occupied \(Dy_j\).
Thus \(A^{-1}A\) and \(BB^{-1}\) lie in \(D\), giving \(J\le D\) and equality.

For [A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract), the character obstruction applies to the intrinsic support subgroup of a hypothetical complementary pair with the specified covers.
In groups with nontrivial finitely generated subgroups of finite abelianization, this conclusion leaves open the existence of such a pair.
