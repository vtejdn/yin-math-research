# Algebraic units and norm obstructions in unsaturated product fibers

## Coefficient table and statement

Let \(A,B\) be finite nonempty subsets of a group \(G\), and suppose each coefficient of \(a\) on \(A\) and \(b\) on \(B\) is nonzero and complex.
Set \(M=\mathbb Z^{A\sqcup B}\) with separate row and column basis vectors, and attach \(v_{xy}=e_x+e_y\) to the representation \(xy\) for \(x\in A,y\in B\).
The coefficients define a multiplicative character \(z:M\to\mathbb C^\times\) by \(z(v_{xy})=a_x b_y\) and the prescribed values on the basis.
The equation \(ab=0\) means that \(\sum_{xy=g} z(v_{xy})=0\) for each \(g\in AB\).
Throughout, a fiber consists of the actual representations of one group element.

Choose an orientation in each two-term fiber, and let \(L\le M\) be generated by all the resulting differences.
Write \(S=\operatorname{sat}(L):=(L\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q)\cap M\).
Use the [two-term character condition](Yin_C_A0025_v1.md#argument) to enumerate all characters \(\rho:S\to\mathbb C^\times\) taking value \(-1\) on each chosen difference.
Call each such \(\rho\) a branch.
An inconsistent assignment on \(L\) gives no branches.
Otherwise there are finitely many branches, with values roots of unity, since \(S/L\) is finite and the values on \(L\) lie in \(\{1,-1\}\).
They can be obtained from Smith normal form by taking all the specified roots.

The following tests apply to products with fibers of any size and to each potential branch separately.

1. Starting with \(U=S\), project all the \(v_{xy}\) of a fiber to \(M/U\).
   If they span an affine line over \(\mathbb Q\) with a unique term at each of its two ends, adjoin the direction of the line to \(U\) and saturate.
   Repeat with the enlarged lattice until no direction can be added.
   Every \(z\) solving the coefficient equations has \(z(u)\) an algebraic unit for every \(u\) in the resulting lattice.
   In particular a triple with three distinct collinear images can force its direction to consist of algebraic-unit ratios even if none of its differences lay in \(S\).
2. In any triple write its term vectors as \(v_0,v_1,v_2\), choosing a pair with \(v_1-v_0\in S\), and put \(\zeta=\rho(v_1-v_0)\), \(d=v_2-v_0\).
   A branch with \(\zeta=-1\) is impossible.
   Otherwise \(z(d)=-(1+\zeta)\) is forced, including when \(d\notin S\).
   For any list of these triples indexed by \(j\), let \(d_j,\zeta_j\) denote their data.
   For \(\zeta_j\ne-1\), set \(m_j=\operatorname{ord}(-\zeta_j)>1\), and for each rational prime \(p\) put
   \[
    c_{pj}=
      \begin{cases}
        1/\varphi(m_j),&m_j\text{ is a positive power of }p,\\
        0,&\text{otherwise},
      \end{cases}                                                      \tag{1}
   \]
   where \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient function.
   For the terminal lattice \(U\) of item 1, every feasible branch satisfies
   \[
         \sum_j n_jd_j\in U,\quad n_j\in\mathbb Z
            \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
          \sum_j n_jc_{pj}=0\quad\text{for each }p.                    \tag{2}
   \]
   Thus a nonnegative relation involving a triple for which \(m_j\) is a prime power excludes the branch if that triple's multiplier in the relation is positive.
3. If all within-fiber differences together span at most one rational direction modulo \(S\), the existence of coefficients making the product zero is decided by a finite branch calculation using ordinary one-variable polynomial gcds.
   This test allows differences outside \(S\), arbitrary complex coefficient phases, and any number of terms with the same image modulo \(S\).

## Propagation along a projected line

An algebraic unit is a complex algebraic number whose value and inverse are both integral over \(\mathbb Z\).
Algebraic units form a group closed under taking arbitrary complex roots of positive integer order.
Indeed a root of an algebraic integer is integral by transitivity of integrality, and the same argument applies to its inverse when the original number is a unit.
This proves the assertion on \(U=S\), since the possible values there are roots of unity.

Assume the assertion holds on \(U\).
The quotient \(M/U\) is free abelian.
Choose a lift \(w\in M\) of a generator of the saturation of the one-dimensional group of projected differences.
Choose a term \(v_0\) of smallest projected exponent, reversing orientation if desired.
Write
\[
                   v_i-v_0=u_i+k_i w,\qquad u_i\in U,\quad
                   \min_i k_i=0,\quad K=\max_i k_i>0 .
\]
After division by \(z(v_0)\), the fiber equation says
\[
                    P(X):=\sum_i z(u_i) X^{k_i}=0,\qquad X=z(w).
\]
The unique terms at degrees \(0,K\) have algebraic-unit coefficients.
All remaining coefficients, including sums at repeated exponents, are algebraic integers.
Dividing by the leading coefficient makes \(P\) monic with algebraic-integer coefficients, so \(z(w)\) is integral over \(\mathbb Z\).
Applying the same argument to the reciprocal polynomial \(X^K P(X^{-1})\) shows that \(z(w)^{-1}\) is integral.
Thus \(z\) has unit values on \(U+\mathbb Zw\) and on its saturation.
All eligible directions at one step can also be adjoined together before saturation, since the argument applies to each in the original quotient.
Every successful step increases rank, so this construction terminates in at most \(\operatorname{rank}M-\operatorname{rank}S\) steps.

## The arithmetic contribution of a pinned pair

Dividing the triple equation by \(z(v_0)\) gives \(1+\zeta_j+z(d_j)=0\).
All prescribed values must be nonzero, which explains the exclusion at \(\zeta_j=-1\).
For each integer relation in (2),
\[
            \prod_j\big(-(1+\zeta_j)\big)^{n_j}
                = z\!\left(\sum_j n_jd_j\right)
\]
would be an algebraic unit by item 1.
The product on the left belongs to the cyclotomic field \(E\) generated by the finitely many branch values.
Its absolute field norm to \(\mathbb Q\) must therefore equal one, since its norm and reciprocal norm are both rational integers.

For a root of unity \(\omega\ne1\) of order \(m\), the norm of \(1-\omega\) in \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\) equals \(\Phi_m(1)\), by multiplying over the primitive \(m\)-th roots.
For \(m>1\) this value equals \(p\) when \(m\) is a power of a prime \(p\), and equals one otherwise.
Indeed evaluating \((T^m-1)/(T-1)\) at 1 gives \(m=\prod_{e\mid m,\ e>1}\Phi_e(1)\).
Induction gives the stated positive values, since the contributions of the prime-power divisors of \(m\) multiply to \(m\).
With \(\omega=-\zeta_j\), the normalized exponent of \(p\) in the absolute norm from \(E\) is
\[
      \frac{\operatorname{ord}_p |N_{E/\mathbb Q}(-(1+\zeta_j))|}
           {[E:\mathbb Q]}
      =\frac{\operatorname{ord}_p\Phi_{m_j}(1)}{\varphi(m_j)}=c_{pj}.
\]
This uses the field norm in the tower \(E/\mathbb Q(\omega)/\mathbb Q\).
Additivity of these exponents proves (2).
Vanishing of the finitely many relevant prime coordinates need only be checked on an integer basis of the relations among the images of \(d_j\) in \(M/U\).

In particular, if \(S/L\) is a \(2\)-group, every \(\zeta_j\) has order a power of two since \(z(L)\subseteq\{1,-1\}\).
On a branch surviving the test \(\zeta_j\ne-1\), each \(m_j\) is a positive power of two, including \(m_j=2\) when \(\zeta_j=1\).
Thus a nonzero nonnegative relation among the \(d_j\) modulo \(U\) excludes every branch in this case.
For instance, suppose one triple has a pinned pair and a third term with \(d\notin S\), while a second triple has three distinct images on a line parallel to \(d\) in \(M/S\).
The second triple makes \(z(d)\) an algebraic unit, and the first requires it to have norm divisible by \(2\) in its cyclotomic field, unless that branch was already excluded at \(-1\).
This pair of conditions is impossible even though neither triple has all its differences in \(S\).
The argument concerns their coefficient constraints whenever the stated projected configurations occur in an actual table.

## Exact test with one remaining direction

Fix a branch \(\rho\) on \(S\).
When every fiber difference lies in \(S\), compute each normalized fiber sum from \(\rho\) and retain the branch exactly if all the sums vanish.
Otherwise suppose the differences together span a rational line modulo \(S\).
Choose \(w\) lifting a primitive generator of its saturated direction in \(M/S\), and in each fiber choose one term \(v_0\).
The decompositions \(v_i-v_0=u_i+k_iw\) with \(u_i\in S\) give Laurent polynomials
\[
                      P_g(T)=\sum_{xy=g}\rho(u_{xy})T^{k_{xy}} .
\]
Combine like exponents, including their phases.
If all these polynomials vanish identically, every nonzero choice of \(T\) solves the branch equations.
Otherwise discard identically zero polynomials and divide each remaining one by its lowest monomial power so the resulting ordinary polynomials have nonzero constant term.
They have a common root in \(\mathbb C^\times\) if and only if their gcd over the cyclotomic coefficient field has positive degree.
Necessity follows by the polynomial Bézout identity when the gcd is constant, and any root of a nonconstant gcd suffices.
A passing value defines a character of \(S+\mathbb Z w\) sending \(w\) to \(T\) and extending \(\rho\).
Since \(w\) is primitive modulo the saturated lattice \(S\), this subgroup is a direct summand in \(M\).
Assigning any nonzero values to a complementary basis extends the character and yields nonzero coefficients at all support points.
This proves both directions of item 3.

These tests apply to actual group-product fibers within overlapping [cyclic coset covers](../ledger/Yin_L_A0037_v1.md#the-full-coefficient-equations).
Multiple remaining directions and vanishing subsums can survive the unit and norm tests.
Even when the coefficient equations have a nonzero solution, its regular ranks and the half-rank hypotheses of [the localization theorem](Yin_C_A0024_v1.md#statement) require a separate argument.
