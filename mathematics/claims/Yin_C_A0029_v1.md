# Complex zero products and finite-characteristic ranks on fixed amenable supports

## Statement

Let \(G\) be a discrete amenable group and \(A,B\subset G\) finite nonempty sets.
For a field \(k\) and nonzero \(c\in k[G]\) with support \(S\), let \(R_F(c)\) be the rank of left convolution from \(k[F]\) to \(k[SF]\) for a finite nonempty \(F\subset G\).
Write \(r_k(c)\) for the limit of \(R_F(c)/|F|\) along a left Følner net in \(G\).
The limit below is independent of the net, and \(r_{\mathbb C}(c)\) equals the canonical von Neumann range dimension of left convolution on \(\ell^2(G)\).

Fix \(\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]\).
The following are equivalent, using the same group and exactly the supports \(A,B\) in all three statements.

1. There are \(a,b\in\mathbb C[G]\) with \(ab=0\), \(r_{\mathbb C}(a)=\alpha\) and \(r_{\mathbb C}(b)=\beta\).
2. For every \(\epsilon>0\) and every sufficiently large rational prime \(p\), there are a finite extension \(k_p/\mathbb F_p\) and \(a_p,b_p\in k_p[G]\) such that \(a_p b_p=0\) and
   \[
          \alpha-\epsilon\le r_{k_p}(a_p)\le\alpha,\qquad
          \beta-\epsilon\le r_{k_p}(b_p)\le\beta .
   \]
3. There is a sequence of distinct rational primes \(p_j\to\infty\) and pairs over the prime fields \(\mathbb F_{p_j}\) with zero product and respective ranks converging to \(\alpha,\beta\).

In the converse, convergence along a sequence of finite extensions of those prime fields also suffices.
In particular a complex complementary half-rank pair on specified torsion-free amenable supports exists exactly when the simultaneous finite-characteristic rank limits can be \((1/2,1/2)\).
The finite-characteristic quantities are dimensions of finite linear images divided by the input window size.

## A finite-window bracket valid over every field

Put \(T_F:=\bigcap_{s\in S}sF\) and let \(I_F(c)\) be the rank of the same matrix as \(R_F(c)\) restricted to its output rows in \(T_F\).
The matrix entries are coefficients of \(c\) or zero.
For any fixed \(F\) we shall prove
\[
  \frac{I_F(c)}{|F|}
    \ \le\ \liminf_{\Omega}\frac{R_\Omega(c)}{|\Omega|}
    \ \le\ \limsup_{\Omega}\frac{R_\Omega(c)}{|\Omega|}
    \ \le\ \frac{R_F(c)}{|F|},\qquad
  0\le R_F(c)-I_F(c)\le |SF\setminus T_F| .                         \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\Omega\) runs along any left Følner net, and the last inequality counts the deleted rows.

For a finite nonempty \(\Omega\), choose \(R_\Omega(c)\) independent input columns of its convolution matrix, indexed by \(P\subset\Omega\).
For every \(g\in G\), the columns indexed by \(P\cap Fg\) remain independent as vectors on \(G\).
After right translation back to \(F\) there are at most \(R_F(c)\) of them.
Each point of \(P\) belongs to exactly \(|F|\) of the translates \(Fg\), all indexed by \(g\in F^{-1}\Omega\).
Thus
\[
                     |F|R_\Omega(c)\le |F^{-1}\Omega|R_F(c).
\]

For the other inequality, let \(V\subset k[\Omega]\) be the exact kernel of convolution on that window, extending its vectors by zero on \(G\setminus\Omega\).
Choose \(\dim V\) coordinates \(Q\subset\Omega\) on which restriction identifies \(V\) with \(k^Q\), by row reduction on any basis of \(V\).
The restrictions of \(V\) to \(Fg\) span a space of dimension at least \(|Q\cap Fg|\), since projection onto these latter coordinates is surjective.
They satisfy every convolution equation on \(T_Fg\), because each such row depends only on inputs from \(Fg\).
Their dimension is therefore at most \(|F|-I_F(c)\).
Counting the translates through \(Q\) gives
\[
                  |F|\big(|\Omega|-R_\Omega(c)\big)
                      \le |F^{-1}\Omega|\big(|F|-I_F(c)\big).
\]
The Følner property gives \(|F^{-1}\Omega|/|\Omega|\to1\) for every fixed nonempty \(F\), proving the two outer bounds of (1).
Choosing \(F\) itself increasingly left invariant gives \(|SF\setminus T_F|/|F|\to0\), by comparison of the finitely many sets \(sF\) with \(F\).
For any two choices of the Følner net \(\Omega\), their lower and upper limits all lie in the same interval for each fixed \(F\), so the arbitrarily small interval lengths force equality.
Equivalently, \(r_k(c)\) belongs to every interval \([I_F(c)/|F|,R_F(c)/|F|]\), and these intervals have arbitrarily small lengths.
Their lengths are bounded uniformly in the field and coefficients for fixed \(F,S\).
The bounds may be tested using finite windows in the subgroup generated by \(A\cup B\) for both factors, since that subgroup is amenable and the corresponding convolution matrices agree with the ambient ones.

Over \(\mathbb C\), let \(P_c\) be the range projection and let \(\rho:=\tau_G(P_c)\).
For any finite coordinate set \(E\), the trace of the compression of \(P_c\) to \(\ell^2(E)\) is \(|E|\rho\) by right translation invariance.
Its compression to \(SF\) dominates the orthogonal projection onto \(c\mathbb C[F]\), of ordinary dimension \(R_F(c)\).
The kernel projection of \(c\) has trace \(1-\rho\) by polar decomposition, and its compression to \(F\) dominates the projection onto \(\ker(c)\cap\mathbb C[F]\).
Consequently \(|F|\rho\le R_F(c)\le |SF|\rho\).
Taking a Følner limit gives \(r_{\mathbb C}(c)=\rho\).
These estimates for scalar group-ring operators also agree with the [amenable finite-support localization bound](Yin_C_A0001_v2.md#rational-kernel-localization).

## Keeping two prescribed ranks in reductions

Assume item 1 and fix \(\epsilon>0\).
Choose a single \(F\) for which \(|SF\setminus T_F|/|F|<\epsilon\) for both \(S=A\) and \(S=B\).
For each factor choose a nonzero minor of its interior matrix on \(F\) of the maximal possible rank there, using the empty minor \(1\) at rank zero.
Take the \(\mathbb Q\)-subalgebra of \(\mathbb C\) generated by the finitely many support coefficients, their inverses and the inverses of the two selected minors.
It is nonzero and finitely generated, so its quotient by a maximal ideal is a number field by Zariski's lemma.
This specialization preserves the equations \(ab=0\), the support points and the two selected nonzero minors.
It preserves every vanishing minor on every other finite window too, since each minor is an integer polynomial in the original coefficients.
This use of a finitely generated coefficient algebra extends the [finite-equation specialization for a zero product](../ledger/Yin_L_A0030_v1.md#the-remaining-coefficient-field-is-not-the-main-analytic-difficulty) by prescribing the two needed minors.

Choose an integral primitive element \(\theta\) for the number field, scaling an ordinary primitive element by a nonzero integer if necessary.
All the finitely many specialized coefficients, their inverses and the inverses of the selected minors belong to \(\mathbb Z[\theta,1/d]\) for some positive integer \(d\).
Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[T]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\theta\).
For every rational prime \(p\nmid d\), any root of its reduction in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\) defines a homomorphism from \(\mathbb Z[\theta,1/d]\) to a finite extension \(k_p\) of \(\mathbb F_p\).
Indeed \(\mathbb Z[\theta]\simeq\mathbb Z[T]/(P)\) by monic division.
The resulting group-ring coefficients still have the same supports, have zero product and preserve the selected minors.
The ranks on the chosen interior windows equal the original ones, giving the two lower bounds in item 2 by (1).
For every finite window \(E\), vanishing minors give \(R_E(a_p)\le R_E(a)\) and \(R_E(b_p)\le R_E(b)\).
Taking limits yields the two upper bounds simultaneously.
For a fixed pair originally defined over a number field, the same proof using that field gives convergence of its reduction ranks uniformly over the residue maps as \(p\to\infty\) outside the finitely many excluded primes for each tolerance.

For each fixed \(\epsilon\), there are arbitrarily large primes in this construction for which one can take \(k_p=\mathbb F_p\).
Indeed a nonconstant integer polynomial has values divisible by infinitely many rational primes.
To see this, choose an integer \(x\) with \(P(x)\ne0\) and let \(m\) be a multiple of the primes to exclude, including the prime divisors of \(d\).
The integer \(P(x+mP(x)t)/P(x)\) is \(1\bmod m\) and has absolute value greater than one for some integer \(t\).
Any of its prime divisors is outside the excluded set and divides a value of \(P\), giving a root in \(\mathbb F_p\).
Choosing successively increasing primes as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves item 3 from item 1.

## Constructing a complex pair from a sequence of reductions

Suppose we have pairs with ranks tending to \((\alpha,\beta)\) over fields of distinct positive characteristics tending to infinity, with the same fixed supports and with product zero.
Let \(X\) be variables for the finitely many coefficients on the disjoint row and column labels.
Enumerate \(\mathbb Z[X]\), which is countable.
By passing successively to infinite subsequences and taking a diagonal subsequence, arrange that each polynomial in this ring is either eventually zero or eventually nonzero when evaluated at the coefficient tuples.
The polynomials eventually zero form an ideal \(\mathfrak p\).
It is prime because evaluation takes values in fields and the vanishing status of each factor eventually stabilizes.
It meets \(\mathbb Z\) only in zero since the residue characteristics tend to infinity.
No coordinate variable belongs to \(\mathfrak p\), and every integer polynomial expressing a coefficient of \(ab=0\) belongs to it.

The fraction field of \(\mathbb Z[X]/\mathfrak p\) is a finitely generated field of characteristic zero and embeds in \(\mathbb C\).
One may send a finite transcendence basis to algebraically independent complex numbers and extend to the finite algebraic extension by algebraic closedness.
The images of the coordinates give a complex zero product with exactly the required supports.
For any fixed finite \(F\), the rank of every interior and full matrix for either new factor agrees with the corresponding ranks on the chosen subsequence eventually.
This follows from the stabilized zero patterns of its finitely many minors.
Thus the limiting rank \(\alpha\) and the complex Følner rank of the first factor both lie in the same interval in (1) for each \(F\).
Their difference is zero by taking windows with arbitrarily small relative boundary.
The same argument gives rank \(\beta\) for the second factor.
The complex identification above proves item 1.
This argument gives both converse implications, choosing a sequence from item 2 with \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).

## Scope for A0001

For the amenable [A0001 problem](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract), these conditions apply independently of the dimension and saturation of the Laurent coefficient equations on the supports.
To exclude a complex half-rank pair on a fixed table in a fixed amenable group, it suffices to find a positive tolerance and arbitrarily large primes for which no pair over any finite extension has both ranks in \([1/2-\epsilon,1/2]\).
Finite-group quotients would introduce a separate approximation problem.
The two-sided passage uses Følner windows in the original group and yields limits of ranks in varying characteristics.
Exact half rank at any one prime and rank approximation in a general nonamenable group remain separate questions.
