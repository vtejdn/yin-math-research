# Finite exclusion of complementary half ranks on a component of the coefficient equations

## Statement

Fix finite nonempty supports \(A,B\) in a discrete group \(G\).
Impose the actual coefficient equations \(\sum_{xy=w}a_x b_y=0\) for all \(w\in AB\), with every coefficient nonzero and one coordinate of each factor normalized to one.
Let \(Z\) be this complex algebraic set, and let \(V\) be a nonempty irreducible component, or the whole nonempty set.
Write \(r(c)\) for the trace of the range projection of regular convolution by \(c\).
Define
\[
          t(V)=\sup_{(a,b)\in V}\min\{r(a),r(b)\}.
\]
The [component sampling theorem](Yin_C_A0030_v1.md#statement) shows that this supremum is attained, is at most \(1/2\), and equals \(1/2\) precisely when \(V\) contains a double half-rank pair.

For finite nonempty \(F\subset G\), let \(R_F(c)\) be the ordinary rank of the complex matrix of \(c:\mathbb C[F]\to\mathbb C[SF]\), where \(S\) is its support.
Put
\[
  U_F(V)=\max_{(a,b)\in V}\frac{\min\{R_F(a),R_F(b)\}}{|F|},
  \qquad \Lambda_F=\frac{\max\{|AF|,|BF|\}}{|F|}.
\]
Then
\[
            U_F(V)/\Lambda_F\ \le\ t(V)\ \le\ U_F(V).                    \tag{1}
\]
For any fixed \(F\), \(U_F(V)\) is determined by finitely many minors and the original coefficient equations, with the additional equations for \(V\) when restricting to a component.
On an irreducible component, almost every sample from a law with a density on its smooth locus attains both maximal window ranks simultaneously for every window in a prescribed countable collection.
This assertion also holds for the point mass on a point component.

In every group \(U_F(V)<1/2\) excludes a double half-rank pair.
For torsion-free \(G\), \(U_F(V)\le 1/2\) already excludes one.
If the subgroup generated by \(A\cup B\) is amenable, the following equivalence holds:
\[
 V\text{ has no double half-rank pair}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{some finite }F\text{ has }U_F(V)<1/2.                           \tag{2}
\]
Thus the negative answer has a finite certificate even when the generic ranks of both factors are below \(1/2\).
Along any left Følner sequence in this finitely generated subgroup the gap in (1) tends to zero uniformly over the coefficients.
The statement assumes access to the required finite multiplication data and gives no a priori bound on the size of a distinguishing window.

On an irreducible component of an arbitrary group, the following separate certificate is complete for the case in which at least one generic rank is strictly larger than \(1/2\).
At an exact sample \(c=a\) or \(b\) on that component test the finite moments
\[
 h_k(c)=1-\tau\!\left[\left(1-\frac{c^*c}{|S|\|c\|_2^2}\right)^k\right].
\]
Some point and finite \(k\) satisfy \(h_k(c)>1/2\) if and only if that factor's generic rank exceeds \(1/2\).
In that case one sample from a law with a density on the smooth locus succeeds almost surely as \(k\) increases.
A verified strict moment bound at any one point excludes double half rank throughout that component.

## Argument

Let \(P,Q\) be the range and kernel projections of \(c\).
The compression of \(P\) to \(SF\) dominates the projection onto \(c\mathbb C[F]\).
The compression of \(Q\) to \(F\) dominates the projection onto the exact finite kernel in \(\mathbb C[F]\).
Translation invariance gives the traces \(|SF|r(c)\) and \(|F|(1-r(c))\) of these compressions.
Consequently
\[
          |F|r(c)\ \le\ R_F(c)\ \le\ |SF|r(c).
\]
This is the finite compression argument in the [window comparison](Yin_C_A0029_v1.md#a-finite-window-bracket-valid-over-every-field), and these particular inequalities require no amenability.
Taking the smaller of the two ranks at each pair and then the supremum proves (1).
The window ranks are bounded integers, so their stated maximum exists.

If \(R_F(c)=|F|r(c)\), faithfulness of the ordinary trace makes the kernel compression equal to the projection onto the exact finite kernel.
Writing \(e\) for coordinate projection onto \(F\), the identity \(eQe-(eQe)^2=eQ(1-e)Qe\) implies \(Qe=eQ\).
Every nonzero Fourier coefficient \(\widehat Q(g)\) then has \(gF=F\), since the matrix entries between \(\delta_y\) and \(\delta_{gy}\) are constant in \(y\).
The left stabilizer of \(F\) is finite, as it acts faithfully by permutations on the nonempty finite set.
In a torsion-free group this stabilizer is trivial, so \(Q\) is a scalar projection.
It follows that \(0<r(c)<1\) forces the strict inequality \(|F|r(c)<R_F(c)\).
At a double half-rank pair both finite ranks are therefore strictly greater than \(|F|/2\).

To test whether the window maximum is at least \(j/|F|\), impose the two rank conditions \(R_F(a),R_F(b)\ge j\) together with the defining equations and nonvanishing constraints.
Each rank condition is a finite disjunction of nonvanishing minors.
These are polynomial feasibility tests over the algebraic numbers for the full variety and for a specified geometric component defined by algebraic coefficients.
Geometric components of this variety defined over \(\mathbb Q\) admit such equations over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
On an irreducible component each maximal rank is witnessed by a minor that is nonzero somewhere, so both maxima occur together on a dense Zariski-open subset.
The omitted proper closed sets have measure zero on the smooth locus by the analytic zero-set argument in the [sampling theorem](Yin_C_A0030_v1.md#argument).
Countable unions preserve nullity.
An upper bound on the maximum can be certified by infeasibility of the next rank threshold.
A numerically low matrix rank at an arbitrary sample alone supplies no such certificate.

In the amenable case choose increasing left invariance for the finite sets \(A,B\) in their generated subgroup.
Along a left Følner sequence, both unions \(AF,BF\) have size \((1+o(1))|F|\).
Equation (1) now gives \(U_F(V)\to t(V)\).
If no half pair exists then \(t(V)<1/2\) by attainment, proving the forward implication of (2).
The reverse implication follows directly from (1).
For a group with verified multiplication on finite windows and an effective enumeration of such windows, it suffices to search for a certificate without knowing the size of the rank gap.

Finally \(\|c\|\le\sqrt{|S|}\|c\|_2\), and the spectral theorem gives \(h_k(c)\uparrow r(c)\).
The simultaneous almost-sure generic ranks on a component and the bound on their sum follow from the [fixed-support theorem](Yin_C_A0030_v1.md#statement).
It follows that if either generic rank exceeds one half, almost every sample eventually yields the strict inequality.
Conversely a single such moment at any point gives a strict lower bound on that factor's generic rank, which excludes a double half-rank point on the same component.
Tests at exact points can use any proved lower bound for the projection trace, including the [two-polynomial Gram bound](Yin_C_A0027_v1.md#two-moment-tests).

## A four-coset table with both kinds of component

This comparison identifies which conclusions about the coefficient equations need torsion-freeness.
Take \(G=C_4\times\langle t\rangle\), with \(s\) generating \(C_4\) and \(t\) of infinite order.
Use \(A=B=\{s^i t^j:0\le i<4,\ 0\le j\le1\}\).
Each side occupies exactly four cosets of \(\langle t\rangle\), and the actual fibers at heights \(0,1,2\) have sizes \(4,8,4\), respectively, for each \(s\)-coordinate.
There are no binary equations.
Under Fourier transformation in \(C_4\), the four coupled equations at each height become the coefficients of \(P_d(t)Q_d(t)=0\) for \(0\le d<4\), where \(P_d,Q_d\) have degree at most one.
Each frequency must vanish identically on at least one side because \(\mathbb C[t]\) is a domain.
For every nonempty proper set \(I\) of frequencies, the locus \(P_d=0\) for \(d\notin I\), \(Q_d=0\) for \(d\in I\) meets the exact coefficient torus.
Indeed generic independent entries on the allowed frequencies have every inverse Fourier coefficient nonzero in both layers.
These assignments give all irreducible components: they are restrictions of linear spaces before separate scalar normalization, and each admits points with exactly its prescribed frequency sets, so none is contained in another.
The regular ranks on their dense open sets are \(|I|/4\) and \((4-|I|)/4\), by the direct sum of four circle multipliers.

For \(|I|=1\) and \(I=\{0\}\), write \(p=(1+s+s^2+s^3)/4\).
The exact point \(a=(1+t)p,\ b=(1+t)(1-p)\) can be rescaled for the chosen coordinate chart.
For \(X=b^*b/\tau(b^*b)\), the moments \(\mu_j=\tau(X^j)\) at \(j=1,2,3,4\) are \(1,2,40/9,280/27\), because the constant coefficient of \(|1+t|^{2j}\) is \(\binom{2j}{j}\).
The first lower bound \(1/\mu_2=1/2\) leaves equality possible.
Projecting the range projection of \(X\) onto the span of \(X,X^2\) in trace \(L^2\) gives the sharper lower bound
\[
 r(b)\ge
 \frac{\mu_4-2\mu_2\mu_3+\mu_2^3}{\mu_2\mu_4-\mu_3^2}
 =\frac35>\frac12.
\]
Hence this one point rules out double half rank throughout its component.
Taking \(F=C_4\) also gives \(U_F=1/4\) on that component, since in each active frequency the input is one-dimensional.
For \(|I|=2\) both generic ranks really equal \(1/2\) and every actual coefficient equation still vanishes.
The group in this comparison has torsion.
The existence of a component with a complex zero product in a torsion-free group remains an additional requirement in the [four-coset investigation](Yin_C_A0020_v1.md#statement).
