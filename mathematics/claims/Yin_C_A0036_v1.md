# Prime-index descent and homogeneous units of prime order

## Statement

Say that a torsion-free discrete group satisfies complex Atiyah if every finite matrix over its complex group ring has integer regular rank, with the unnormalized matrix trace.
Write \(U_K\) for the regular affiliated algebra and \(D_K\) for the division closure of \(\mathbb C[K]\) in \(U_K\).
For a torsion-free group, complex Atiyah is equivalent to \(D_K\) being a division ring. [Lück and Linnell, Theorem 2.3(3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.06906v3#page=10)

Let \(K\triangleleft L\) be torsion-free groups with \(L/K=C_p\), for a prime \(p\), and assume complex Atiyah for \(K\).
Choose \(t\in L\) lifting a generator, put \(D=D_K\), \(h=t^p\in K\), and write \(\alpha(d)=tdt^{-1}\).
The following conditions are equivalent.

1. Some finite matrix over \(\mathbb C[L]\) has nonintegral regular rank.
2. The coset \(D^\times t\) inside \(U_L^\times\) contains an element of order \(p\).
3. There is a \(b\in D^\times\) with
   \[
             \alpha^{p-1}(b)\cdots\alpha(b)b=h.                        \tag{1}
   \]
4. Some square matrix over \(\mathbb C[L]\) has regular nullity exactly \(1/p\).

All finite matrix ranks and nullities over \(\mathbb C[L]\) belong to \((1/p)\mathbb Z\), even if these equivalent conditions hold.
For odd \(p\) this rules out a matrix of nullity \(1/2\) in \(L\).

More generally, let \(H\triangleleft G\) be torsion-free groups with \(G/H\) finite and \(H\) satisfying complex Atiyah.
For every prime \(p\) in the reduced denominator of a nonintegral matrix rank over \(\mathbb C[G]\), there is an intermediate pair \(H\le K\triangleleft L\le G\) as above satisfying conditions 1–4 for this same \(p\).
The result concerns finite extensions of a group already satisfying complex Atiyah.

## Restriction to a subgroup and the actual crossed product

For a matrix \(A\) over the group ring of a group \(G\) and a finite-index subgroup \(J\), express its left action in a basis of left coset representatives of \(G/J\) over the right module \(\mathbb C[J]\).
This gives a finite matrix \(\rho_J(A)\) with entries in \(\mathbb C[J]\).
On \(\ell^2(G)=\bigoplus_{xJ}x\ell^2(J)\) this is the restriction representation, extended normally to the group von Neumann algebra and then to the affiliated algebras.
For any bounded kernel or range projection, the diagonal traces at the representatives \(x\) agree with the diagonal trace at the identity because the group von Neumann algebra commutes with right translations.
Summing these traces over the representatives and the original matrix coordinates gives
\[
             r_J(\rho_J(A))=[G:J]r_G(A),                                \tag{2}
\]
and the same equality for nullity.
The argument applies to affiliated matrices in the restriction representation as well.
In particular, applying (2) with \(J=H\) proves the rational denominator bound dividing \([G:H]\).

In the prime-index setting the subalgebra of \(U_L\) generated by \(D\) and \(t\) is
\[
                   B=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-1}Dt^i.
\]
Conjugation preserves \(D\), and \(\alpha^p=\operatorname{Ad}_h\).
For directness, clear the finitely many affiliated coefficients by a common bounded left denominator in \(\mathcal N(K)\) that is a unit of \(U_K\).
The resulting bounded summands applied to \(\delta_e\) have mutually orthogonal supports in the cosets \(Kt^i\).
Each coefficient must vanish since the identity vector separates the group von Neumann algebra and right translation gives the same separating vector in its coset copy.
The subgroup inclusion preserves affiliated invertibility by the right coset decomposition into copies of \(\ell^2(K)\).
Thus \(D\) and \(t\) are in \(D_L\).
Conversely, multiplication on the left by an ambient-invertible \(X\in M_n(B)\) is injective on the finite-dimensional right \(D\)-space \(M_n(B)\).
It is therefore onto and its solution \(XY=1\) is the ambient inverse.
Consequently \(B=D_L\) and it is also the actual rational closure.
The bounded denominator and affiliated rank inputs are those in the [index-two calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0003_v1.md#actual-affiliated-embedding) and the [Lin93 audit](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#affiliated-closure-and-rank-conventions).

## A one-dimensional module at a prime-index failure

The left regular \(B\)-module is semisimple.
Indeed, given a left ideal \(I\subset B\), choose a left \(D\)-linear projection \(P:B\to I\).
If \(T\) is left multiplication by \(t\), average the maps \(T^iPT^{-i}\) over \(0\le i<p\).
Each map is left \(D\)-linear, maps into \(I\), and is the identity on \(I\).
Since \(T^p\) is left multiplication by \(h\in D^\times\), conjugation by \(T\) permutes these maps cyclically.
The average commutes with \(T\) and is \(B\)-linear.
Thus each left ideal is a direct summand, and finite dimension over \(D\) makes \(B\) semisimple Artinian.

Fix a primitive complex \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\).
The automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(B\) fixing \(D\) and sending \(t\) to \(\zeta t\) has order \(p\) and fixed ring \(D\), by the direct sum above.
It permutes the central primitive idempotents transitively, since the sum over any orbit is a nonzero idempotent in the division ring \(D\).
There are either \(p\) simple blocks or one.
In the first case all \(p\) blocks have equal left \(D\)-dimension, hence dimension one.
In the second case write \(B=M_s(\Delta)\) for a division ring \(\Delta\).
Its left regular module is the sum of \(s\) isomorphic simple left ideals, whose dimensions over \(D\) agree by left \(B\)-linearity of the isomorphisms.
Thus \(s\dim_D I=p\) for any such ideal \(I\).
If \(B\) fails to be a division ring then \(s=p\) in this second case.
In both cases of failure there is a left ideal \(I=Dv\) of dimension one.
This argument allows arbitrary dimension of \(D\) over its center.

Since \(t\) is invertible and \(tv\in I\), write \(tv=bv\) for a \(b\in D^\times\).
Iterating \(p\) times gives \(hv=\alpha^{p-1}(b)\cdots\alpha(b)bv\).
The map \(d\mapsto dv\) identifies \(D\) with \(I\) as a left \(D\)-space, proving (1).
Conversely, \(u=b^{-1}t\) satisfies
\[
           u^p=\bigl(\alpha^{p-1}(b)\cdots\alpha(b)b\bigr)^{-1}h .
\]
Its grade in \(L/K\) has order \(p\), so (1) is equivalent to \(u\) having order \(p\).
Every element of \(D^\times t\) has this form.

## The rank and a finite matrix over the group ring

Given such a \(u\), form \(e_j=p^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\zeta^{-jk}u^k\) for \(j=0,\ldots,p-1\).
These are pairwise orthogonal algebraic idempotents with sum one by \(u^p=1\).
Each is nonzero by independence of the grades \(0,\ldots,p-1\).
Their right ideals \(e_jB\) have equal right \(D\)-dimensions because \(\sigma\) fixes \(D\) and permutes the idempotents cyclically.
Since \(\dim B_D=p\), each has dimension one.
As \(ue_j=\zeta^j e_j\), the kernel of left multiplication by \(u-1\) on \(B_D\) is exactly \(e_0 B\).
The right \(D\)-matrix of this map in the basis \(1,t,\ldots,t^{p-1}\) is its restriction matrix in (2).
Gaussian elimination over \(D\) gives the same rank after passing to \(U_K\).
Thus \(\nu_L(u-1)=1/p\), and \(t-b=b(u-1)\) has that nullity as well.
This proof uses algebraic idempotents without assuming they are selfadjoint affiliated projections.

Since \(b\in D\) is in the rational closure of \(\mathbb C[K]\), it has a realization \(b=e_i^{\mathsf T}Q^{-1}e_j\) for a square \(Q\) over \(\mathbb C[K]\) invertible in \(M_n(U_K)\). [Matrix-inverse characterization of rational closure](../audits/literature/Lin06/Yin_U001.md#matrix-localization-and-fixed-ambient-closures)
The matrix
\[
                     \begin{pmatrix}t&e_i^{\mathsf T}\\ e_j&Q\end{pmatrix}
                        \in M_{n+1}(\mathbb C[L])
\]
reduces by invertible block operations over \(U_L\) to the diagonal blocks \(t-b\) and \(Q\).
It therefore has nullity \(1/p\), proving condition 4, which implies condition 1 by rank-nullity.
Conversely condition 1 makes \(B=D_L\) a nondivision ring by the torsion-free rank criterion, completing the equivalences.

## Finding the prime in a finite extension

Given the exceptional matrix over \(\mathbb C[G]\), choose \(p\) dividing the reduced denominator of its rank.
Let \(G_P\) be the inverse image of a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup \(P\) of \(G/H\).
The index \([G:G_P]\) is prime to \(p\), so its restriction matrix still has \(p\) in the reduced denominator by (2).
This is the torsion-free restriction argument of [Linnell and Schick, Lemma 2.4](https://www.uni-math.gwdg.de/schick/publ/extAtiyah.pdf#page=6).
A composition series for the finite \(p\)-group \(P\) lifts to a subnormal chain from \(H\) to \(G_P\) with all successive quotients \(C_p\).
The first member \(L\) failing complex Atiyah and its predecessor \(K\) have the required properties.
Only the witness at \(G_P\) is obtained by restriction of the original matrix.
The prime-index witness in \(L\) comes from the one-dimensional module argument.

## An abelianized norm obstruction and the limit of integer valuations

Let \(M=D^\times/[D^\times,D^\times]\) with additive notation and \(N=1+\alpha+\cdots+\alpha^{p-1}\) on \(M\).
Since \(\alpha^p\) is inner and \(\alpha(h)=h\), the class of \(h\) defines an element
\[
                            [h]\bmod NM\in M^{C_p}/NM .
\]
Replacing \(t\) by \(dt\) with \(d\in D^\times\) leaves the induced action on \(M\) unchanged and changes the class of \(t^p\) by \(N[d]\).
If the displayed class is nonzero, (1) is impossible and \(L\) satisfies complex Atiyah.
The obstruction also works after passing to any equivariant abelian quotient in which this class remains nonzero.
A vanishing class gives only the abelianized norm identity.

In particular an \(\alpha\)-invariant integer-valued valuation \(\nu\) on \(D^\times\) with \(p\nmid\nu(h)\) excludes (1).
Such a valuation exists exactly when the quotient homomorphism \(L\to C_p\) lifts to \(\mathbb Z\), allowing a choice of generator of \(C_p\).
Indeed, given the valuation, put \(\chi(k)=p\nu(k)\) on \(K\) and \(\chi(t)=\nu(h)\).
Invariance supplies the conjugation relation and \(p\chi(t)=\chi(h)\) supplies the power relation, so the normal form \(kt^i\) gives a well-defined homomorphism \(L\to\mathbb Z\).
Its reduction modulo \(p\) has kernel \(K\).

Conversely, given such a \(\chi\), put \(\lambda(k)=\chi(k)/p\) and \(K_0=\ker\lambda\).
Then \(\lambda(h)=\chi(t)\) is prime to \(p\).
Write \(\lambda(K)=m\mathbb Z\) with \(m>0\) and choose \(s\in K\) with \(\lambda(s)=m\).
The group \(K_0\) inherits complex Atiyah from \(K\) because a matrix supported on a subgroup acts on right coset copies of its regular representation with the same trace.
The ring \(D\) is the classical skew fraction field of \(D_{K_0}[s^{\pm1};\operatorname{Ad}_s]\).
Indeed, the Laurent grading is faithful by clearing coefficients on distinct cosets, every nonzero Laurent polynomial is invertible in the division ring \(D\), and its skew fraction field is an actual division subring containing \(\mathbb C[K]\).
Minimality of the division closure gives equality. [Skew Laurent realization](../ledger/Yin_L_A0003_v1.md#a-leading-coefficient-constraint-on-every-rational-solution)
The least Laurent degree times \(m\) extends by differences on fractions to a valuation with restriction \(\lambda\) on \(K\).
Conjugation by \(t\) preserves \(K_0\) and sends \(s\) to \(k_0s\) with \(k_0\in K_0\).
It preserves the least degree of each nonzero polynomial and hence the valuation on fractions.
The resulting valuation has the required value \(\chi(t)\) on \(h\).

For a finitely generated \(L\), the integer-valuation test is thus decidable from a presentation of its abelianization, the images of generators, and the given quotient character by integer linear algebra.
In particular it cannot detect a prime-index extension with \(L_{\rm ab}\) finite.
Testing the class in the abelianization of the actual division-ring units, or the ordered norm (1), can require information beyond these integer characters.
Torsion-freeness excludes \(b\in K\) solving (1) because \(b^{-1}t\) would be a torsion element of \(L\).
The prime-index result leaves open whether the larger rational coset contains such an element for any torsion-free \(L\) satisfying the subgroup hypothesis.
