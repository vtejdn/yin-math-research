# Modular kernel counts and coset compression of finite annihilators

## Statement

Let \(G\) be a group, \(0\ne A\in\mathbb Z[G]\), \(p\) a prime and \(L=(\sum_g|A_g|^2)^{1/2}\ge1\).
For nonempty finite \(T\subseteq G\), restrict left multiplication by \(A\) to input support \(T\) and output support \((\operatorname{supp} A)T\).
Write \(d_T\) for the ordinary dimension of its complex kernel, and \(K_{T,e}\) for its finite kernel over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\).
The quantities
\[
 \rho=\sup_T\frac{d_T}{|T|},\qquad
 \rho_e=\sup_T\frac{\log_p|K_{T,e}|}{e|T|}
\]
satisfy \(\rho_e\downarrow\rho\), with \(0\le\rho_e-\rho\le(\log_p L)/e\).
There is a nonzero finite complex \(v\) with \(Av=0\) if and only if \(\rho>0\).

Suppose \(V_e\) is a finite annihilator of \(A\) modulo \(p^e\) reducing to a fixed nonzero \(c\in\mathbb F_p[G]\).
Write \(D_e=\operatorname{supp} V_e\) over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\), \(C=\operatorname{supp}c\), and \(s=|C|\).
If no nonzero finite complex vector is killed by \(A\), then for every finite nonempty \(T\)
\[
           e\, |CT|\log p\ \le\ s\,|D_eT|\log L.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(D_e\) lies in a union of \(k_e\) left cosets of a single amenable subgroup \(H_e\), then \(e\log p\le s k_e\log L\).
The subgroup and coset representatives may change with \(e\).
Consequently an \(o(e)\) coset cover along an unbounded sequence of precisions produces a nonzero complex annihilator for the same integral \(A\), regardless of the number of points in each coset.

Suppose in addition that \(a=A\bmod p\) has \(ab=1,\ ba\ne1\) in \(\mathbb F_p[G]\).
Then \(A\) has no finite complex right inverse and no nonzero finite complex left annihilator.
Every right inverse \(B_e\) modulo \(p^e\), for any choice of its reduction, has
\[
           e\log p\le |\operatorname{supp}B_e|\log L.          \tag{2}
\]
This last bound holds even if a finite complex right annihilator of \(A\) exists.
There is also a spectral restriction on the residual element \(D=(1-AB)/p\) of any integral lift \(B\) of \(b\).
If \(A\) has positive analytic nullity, then \(1/p\) is an \(\ell^2\)-eigenvalue of \(D\), already in the subgroup generated by its support.
That subgroup must be nonsofic.
The integer operator \(1-pD\) would itself violate scalar analytic kernel integrality, while having no nonzero finite complex annihilator on either side.
For a sufficient condition, take \(0\ne c\) with \(ac=0\) and an integral lift \(C_0\) of \(c\), and write \(F=AC_0/p\).
A finite-dimensional rational span of the iterates \(\operatorname{ad}_D^j(F)\) yields an exact integral annihilator reducing to \(c\), up to sign.
The [Mghirbi–Douglas source witnesses](../audits/literature/MD26/Yin_U001.md#md26-u001) have \(ab=1,\ ac=0,\ c\ne0\).
They admit right inverses and annihilators with this fixed reduction \(c\) at every precision.
For these torsion-free groups any violation of (1) resolves [A0002](../tasks/Yin_T_A0002_v1.md#contract) negatively.

## Argument

Let \(M_T\) be the integer matrix of the finite support restriction, let \(r_T=|T|-d_T\) and let \(d_1,\ldots,d_{r_T}\) be its nonzero positive Smith invariants.
Smith normal form gives exactly
\[
 \log_p |K_{T,e}|
     =e d_T+\sum_{i=1}^{r_T}\min(e,v_p(d_i)).                  \tag{3}
\]
This counts all modular vectors, including those divisible by \(p\).
The product of the positive invariants is the gcd of the maximal-rank minors, using one as the empty product.
Some nonzero minor has absolute value at most \(L^{r_T}\), because each restricted column has Euclidean norm at most \(L\).
Thus the difference between \(\log_p|K_{T,e}|/e\) and \(d_T\) lies in \([0,r_T\log_p L/e]\).
After division by \(|T|\) the bound is uniform in \(T\), and each quotient decreases weakly with \(e\) by (3).
Taking suprema proves the assertions about \(\rho_e\).
Any one finite complex kernel vector gives a positive contribution for its support, and without such a vector all \(d_T\) vanish.
Compression of the analytic kernel projection to input coordinates \(T\) has ordinary trace \(|T|\tau_G(p_A)\) and acts as the identity on the finite kernel, so \(\rho\le\tau_G(p_A)\).
Only the finite algebraic kernel is characterized by positivity of \(\rho\).

### Independent translates and amenable cosets

Put \(h=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\operatorname{span}\{ct:t\in T\}\).
Choose a basis from these translates.
The union of the supports of the basis vectors covers \(CT\), since every translate is their linear combination.
Each basis vector has support of size \(s\), hence \(h\ge |CT|/s\).
The corresponding \(h\) vectors \(V_et\) supported on \(D_eT\) are in the modular kernel.
Their reductions have an invertible \(h\)-row minor, which stays invertible modulo \(p^e\).
Consequently these vectors span a free submodule of cardinality \(p^{eh}\).
In the absence of a finite complex annihilator, (3) on \(D_eT\) bounds the cardinality of the entire modular kernel by \(L^{|D_eT|}\).
This proves (1); the exact translate rank \(h\) can also be kept.

Suppose \(D_e\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^k g_iH_e\).
Write the elements of \(D_e\) in those cosets as \(g_i h\) and collect the finitely many \(h\)'s into a set \(P\subset H_e\).
For any \(\varepsilon>0\), a nonempty finite left Følner test set \(T\subset H_e\) can be chosen with \(|PT|\le(1+\varepsilon)|T|\), including when \(H_e\) is finite.
Thus \(|D_eT|\le k(1+\varepsilon)|T|\), while \(|CT|\ge |T|\).
Apply (1) at the given precision and let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
This is a bound on the number of occupied cosets, independent of the spread of the support inside \(H_e\).
For \(L=1\) the hypotheses of a nonzero modular annihilator are impossible.

### Inhomogeneous right inverses

If a finite complex left annihilator existed, its finite homogeneous system of integer equations would have a nonzero rational solution.
Choose a primitive integral such vector \(w\).
Then \(\bar w a=0\), so \(\bar w=\bar w ab=0\), contradicting primitivity.

If \(A X=1\) had a finite complex solution, the finite inhomogeneous system on its support would also have a rational solution.
Write it \(X=Y/d\) with \(Y\) integral and \(d>0\) minimal.
Direct finiteness of the complex group algebra gives \(YA=d\) as well as \(AY=d\), by the faithful finite tracial regular representation.
If \(p\mid d\), reduction gives \(\bar Y a=0\), whence \(\bar Y=0\) by multiplication by \(b\).
This contradicts denominator minimality.
If \(p\nmid d\), reduction gives a two-sided inverse of \(a\), contradicting \(ab=1\ne ba\).

Now take \(T=\operatorname{supp} B_e\) and use the finite matrix \(M=M_T\), adjoining a row at the identity with zeros if necessary.
The identity column \(u=\delta_1\) has no rational preimage, so \([M\mid u]\) has rank \(r+1\), where \(r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M\).
Choose \(r\) original columns and \(r+1\) rows giving a nonzero \((r+1)\)-minor with last column \(u\).
Modulo \(p^e\), \(u\) is a linear combination of all original columns since \(AB_e=1\).
Multilinearity makes the minor zero modulo \(p^e\), as every \(r+1\) original columns are dependent over the rationals and all their corresponding minors are zero as integers.
The unit length of \(u\) bounds the absolute value of the selected nonzero determinant by \(L^r\).
This gives \(p^e\le L^r\le L^{|T|}\).

For a source right inverse \(b\), take an integral lift \(B\), put \(E=1-AB\in p\mathbb Z[G]\) and use \(B\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}E^j\bmod p^e\).
For a lift of \(c\), the [geometric correction in v2](Yin_C_A0039_v2.md#all-precisions-from-one-modular-right-inverse) supplies \(V_e\) reducing to that same \(c\).
Taking \(T=\{1\}\) in (1) recovers the previous fixed-vector support bound.
The existence of many precisions and the size bound for right inverses do not give an upper bound on the coset count for annihilators.

### A rational eigenvalue in the subgroup of the residual element

Write \(f=AB=1-pD\) and \(H=\langle\operatorname{supp}D\rangle\).
For any integral \(D\), neither \(fv=0\) nor \(vf=0\) has a nonzero finitely supported complex solution.
Such a solution on a given support would give a primitive integral solution by rational row reduction, contradicting \(f\equiv1\bmod p\).
In particular \(f\ne0\).
In the regular representation, the closed range of \(AB\) is contained in the closed range of \(A\).
Polar decomposition and the finite trace equate the dimensions of initial and final supports, hence \(\nu_G(f)\ge\nu_G(A)\).
One also has \(\nu_G(f)\ge\nu_G(B)\) from inclusion of kernels.
If both \(A,B\) are injective as bounded regular operators then their product is injective.
Thus \(\nu_G(f)>0\) holds exactly when at least one factor has positive analytic nullity.
As a nonzero scalar group-ring element, \(f\) has \(\nu_G(f)<1\).

Using right cosets \(Ht\), the regular action of \(D\) on \(G\) splits into copies of its action on \(H\).
Thus \(\nu_G(f)=\nu_H(1-pD)\), and nonzero kernel here is precisely the eigenvalue \(1/p\) for the integer operator \(D\) on \(\ell^2(H)\).
For a sofic group every eigenvalue of a regular integer group-ring matrix is an algebraic integer, including for a nonselfadjoint matrix.
This is [Thom, Theorems 1.1(i) and 4.2(i), arXiv v3, pp.1 and 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701294v3#page=6).
The rational number \(1/p\) is not an algebraic integer.
This proves the subgroup restriction; the geometric source's nonsoficity of the whole ambient group supplies no converse.
In particular, one representative \(D-AW\) supported in a sofic subgroup, for \(W\in\mathbb Z[G]\), suffices to exclude a complex annihilator for the fixed \(A\): replace \(B\) by \(B+pW\).
This restricts the entire choice of residual representatives when seeking a counterexample.
It also locates a conditional analytic counterexample inside \(H\), with no finite annihilator for this particular \(1-pD\).
The distinction between the two algebraic annihilator directions is real for group rings in general; see [Handelman and Lawrence, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972700022887).

### A finite adjoint-orbit test for an exact annihilator

Set \(\partial X=DX-XD\), \(Z_0=C_0\) and \(Z_{j+1}=BA Z_j-Z_j AB\).
Multiplying on the left by \(A\) and inducting gives
\[
                 AZ_j=p(-p)^j\partial^jF,\qquad
                 Z_j\bmod p=(-1)^j c .
\]
Indeed \(AZ_{j+1}=[AB,AZ_j]\), \(AB=1-pD\), \(ba c=0\) and \(ab=1\).
If the iterates of \(F\) span a finite-dimensional space \(W\), a basis has finite total support \(S\).
The lattice \(W\cap\mathbb Z^S\) spans \(W\) because its spanning iterates are integral.
It is preserved by \(\partial\), which preserves both \(W\) and integrality.
Its characteristic polynomial gives a monic integer polynomial \(h(X)=\sum_{j=0}^k h_j X^j\) with \(h(\partial)F=0\).
When \(F=0\) take \(h=1\).
Now
\[
       V=\sum_{j=0}^k h_j(-p)^{k-j} Z_j,\qquad
       AV=p(-p)^k h(\partial)F=0,\qquad V\bmod p=(-1)^k c.
\]
A rational dependence among the initial iterates proves finite dimensionality by propagation, so the sufficient condition can be verified with finite group-ring calculations.
For the source group it would prove both a finite complex annihilator and a nonintegral analytic trace at the residual eigenvalue.
The proof in the preceding section excludes such a finite orbit whenever \(D\) is supported in a sofic subgroup.
