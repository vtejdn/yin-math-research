# Torsion-free matching presentations with a prescribed finite quotient

## Statement

Let \(F\) be the free group of the rose in Ope26f, with signed alphabet \(T\) consisting of \(8258\) inverse pairs.
Fix a finite group \(P\), a surjection \(\eta:F\to P\), and a finite list \(\mathcal Q\) of finite groups.
Thus \(P\) can be any finite group generated by at most \(8258\) elements, with its chosen generating tuple.
Write \(m=|P|\), \(v=16513\), and take \(n\) through multiples of \(2v^2m\).
There exists a sequence of legitimate choices of the original prescribed types and matchings, with \(n\) vertices on each side, for which the following conclusions hold.

1. Coning the graphs to the same rose gives a finitely presented torsion-free group \(G_n\) with a finite two-dimensional classifying space.
   Both graphs are connected, the two root factors are nonzero, and their product is zero in \(\mathbb F_2[G_n]\).
2. The map \(\eta\) induces a surjection \(\pi_n:G_n\to P\).
   For every \(Q\in\mathcal Q\), every homomorphism \(G_n\to Q\) factors uniquely through \(\pi_n\).
3. For each root factor, the number of vertex occurrences over each element of \(P\) is exactly \(n/m\), and the number of actual surviving support labels over each element is even and lies between \(n/m-o(n)\) and \(n/m\).
   Each quotient product has exactly \(n^2/m\) occurrence pairs, and its true support rectangle has \(n^2/m-o(n^2)\) cells, a multiple of four.
4. On the true supports, the equations obtained by projection to \(P\), or by any homomorphism to a listed target, admit a simultaneous exact lift with individual coefficients in \(\{1,-1\}\) at the characteristic-two all-ones point.

One may take as the list all finite groups up to any prescribed order, represented up to isomorphism.
All targets are fixed before taking \(n\to\infty\).
The theorem concerns projected coefficients, leaving the original characteristic-zero coefficient equations open.
The [seven-color case](../ledger/Yin_L_A0047_v1.md) includes detailed estimates for its specific law.

## Matching within the prescribed color classes

On each side prescribe a balanced partition of each source vertex type into \(m\) equal parts indexed by \(P\).
This is possible at the specified multiples because all the source counts are integral at \(2v^2\).
Keep the two source root vertices fixed.
For one \(t\) in each inverse pair, each side and each \(c\in P\), match the color-\(c\) vertices carrying \(t\) by a uniform bijection to the color-\(c\eta(t)\) vertices carrying \(\bar t\).
All these bijections are independent, and the reverse reading uses the inverse bijection.
The common block size is \(N=n\rho/m\), where \(\rho=129/v\) is the source marginal of every signed label.
Taking the union over \(c\) gives a bijection of the required full signed-letter classes since \(c\mapsto c\eta(t)\) permutes \(P\). [Ope26f, prescribed proportions and matchings, §2.1](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=4)
Transport along a word \(W\) multiplies its starting color on the right by \(\eta(W)\).
The right regular action is free, so every graph loop has label in \(\ker\eta\).
The cone group has the normal closure of all such loops as its relation subgroup in \(F\), proving the asserted factorization of \(\eta\) for every tuple.

## Girth conditioning and bounded patterns

Choose a sufficiently small fixed \(c_0>0\) with \(c_0\log(2m|T|/\rho)<1\) and \(2c_0\log(132)<1\).
Set \(L=\lfloor c_0\log n\rfloor\).
The count of oriented cycle descriptions of length \(k<L\) and the bound \((N-k)^{-k}\) on their distinct matching prescriptions give an expected number of edges on short cycles at most \(2\sum_{k<L}k(2m|T|/\rho)^k\).
This is at most \(n^{\gamma+o(1)}\) for \(\gamma=c_0\log(2m|T|/\rho)<1\).
For \(\gamma<\gamma'<1\) at least half of the tuples have at most \(n^{\gamma'}\) bad edges for large \(n\).
Switch a bad edge with a distant nonbad edge in the same color block, inverse pair and side.
There are \(N\) candidate edges, of which at most \(n^{\gamma'}+O(132^{2L+2})=o(n)\) are excluded.
The source's distance argument creates no new short cycle, so at most \(n^{\gamma'}\) switches reach the girth event.
Every tuple has at most \(O(n^2)\) possible transpositions, hence the number of starting tuples per possible endpoint is at most \(\exp(o(n))\).
Uniformity of the block law therefore gives probability at least \(\exp(-o(n))\) to the girth event. [Ope26f, Lemma 2.2 and its switch](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=5)

In the girth-conditioned law, for \(s\) previous prescriptions, the same switch with a distant unprescribed edge in the block bounds the conditional probability of one additional pair by \(1/(N-s-O(132^{2L+2}))\) when the denominator is positive.
Indeed the output and prescribed pair uniquely determine the inverse transposition.
This gives \((m/(n\rho))^E\exp(o(L))\) for \(E=O(L)\) distinct feasible prescriptions.
For \(k\le\varepsilon n\) with sufficiently small fixed \(\varepsilon\), a \(k\)-set failing to double on adding its neighbors gives a \(2k\)-set with \(r=\lceil129k/2\rceil\le N/3\) edges.
There are at most \((C n/k)^{2k}\) choices for the set and \((Ck)^r\) ways to choose the \(r\) prescriptions inside it, and the conditional probability of the latter is at most \((2/N)^r\).
The resulting \([C'(k/n)^{62.5}]^k\) is summable to \(o(1)\) over this range by splitting at \(\sqrt n\) and decreasing \(\varepsilon\).
Thus all small sets expand with probability \(1-o(1)\), and balls on a geodesic give diameter \(O(L)\) for every component as in the [source's Lemma 2.3](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=6).

In the bounded-pattern proof, fix a permitted signed labeling of a multiplicity stage \(\Delta_j\) with \(V_j\) vertices, \(E_j\) edges and \(k_j\in\{0,1\}\) prescribed images.
It is a union of boundedly many whole marked chains at the source's fixed length, path and comparison bounds.
Consequently its component count \(b_j\) and \(|E_j-V_j|\) are bounded independently of \(n\).
On choosing one initial \(P\)-color per component, every other color is determined by the ordered products along paths, and inconsistent assignments are discarded.
Every unfixed vertex-image count in (3.10), including the bound for two specified letters at an internal turn, is divided by \(m\) since every type is balanced.
The edge bound above costs \(m^{E_j}\) relative to the original count.
Together with the at most \(m^{b_j}\) initial choices, the only additional factor is at most \(m^{b_j+E_j-V_j+k_j}=\exp(O(1))\).
Thus the \(\exp(o(L))\) allowance in (3.10) absorbs it.
The word counts in (3.11) can ignore colors, and the deterministic chain, bin and multiplicity estimates, including the squared-turn-weight bound, have their original inputs.
Multiplying the stage bounds and selecting one small stage as in Proposition 3.1 gives the same probability \(o(1)\) for each fixed forbidden-pattern test.
This estimate is uniform in the prescribed root names, since a prescribed image contributes at most one choice. [Ope26f, Proposition 3.1 and estimates (3.10)–(3.11)](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=12)

The source's deterministic planar reduction, on the diameter event, uses one choice of the pattern bounds fixed before \(n\).
Thus reduced spherical arrangements are absent with probability \(1-o(1)\) under girth conditioning.
Proposition 5.1, Corollary 5.3 and §6 then give the finite classifying space, torsion-freeness, root separation and characteristic-two zero product for these graphs. [Ope26f, Proposition 4.5 and §§5–6](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=18)

## Connectedness and factorization through the finite quotient

On a chosen side consider an additional marking by elements of a fixed finite set.
For each signed label prescribe a permutation of this set, taking inverse permutations on inverse labels, and require the two ends of every edge to have the corresponding additional markings.
Write \(v_{\tau,c}\) for the distribution of the additional marking in original type \(\tau\) at prescribed \(P\)-color \(c\), and \(v_{t,c}\) for its distribution among vertices of color \(c\) carrying \(t\).
Write \(\lambda_\tau\) for the original positive type fraction.
Feasibility requires \(v_{t,c}\) to map under its prescribed permutation to \(v_{\bar t,c\eta(t)}\).
For each feasible set of histograms, multinomial counting in the independent blocks bounds the log expected number of compatible markings by
\[
 n(A-B)+O(\log n),\quad
 A=\sum_{\tau,c}\frac{\lambda_\tau}{m}H(v_{\tau,c}),\qquad
 B=\frac12\sum_{t,c}\frac{\rho}{m}H(v_{t,c}).
\]
Indeed the number of markings with the specified type histograms is a product of multinomial coefficients, and for one oriented matching block the probability is \(\prod_q(Nv_{t,c}(q))!/N!\).
The paired endpoint distributions have the same entropy \(H\), and the number of histograms is polynomial in \(n\).
Concavity gives \(B\ge (129/2)A\) because every type carries at least 129 labels. [Finite-target entropy calculation](Yin_C_A0041_v1.md#strict-entropy-loss)

Zero exponent forces every type and every signed label at a fixed \(c\) to be monochromatic for the additional marking.
Any pair of types on a side shares an ordinary letter, so the marking would be a single function \(f(c)\) of its \(P\)-color.
For a cut indicator transported by identity permutations, \(f(c\eta(t))=f(c)\) and surjectivity makes \(f\) constant.
On the compact feasible histogram set with cut density in \([\varepsilon,1/2]\), the maximum exponent is therefore strictly negative when that set is nonempty.
The exponential bound survives division by the girth probability \(\exp(-o(n))\).
Together with small-set expansion this excludes components of size at most \(n/2\) on either side with probability \(1-o(1)\), hence gives connectedness.

For a homomorphism \(\theta:F\to Q\), take its right multiplications as the prescribed permutations.
For \(\theta\) to descend to the cone group a compatible additional marking must exist, even on the first side alone.
Indeed graph loops have trivial image under any descended map and word transport then defines this marking on each component.
Zero exponent would give \(f(c\eta(W))=f(c)\theta(W)\) for all words \(W\).
For \(W\in\ker\eta\), canceling \(f(c)\) on the left gives \(\theta(W)=1\), exactly the kernel inclusion needed for \(\theta\) to factor through \(\eta\).
For nonfactoring \(\theta\) compactness therefore bounds the exponent strictly below zero.
Finitely many \(\theta\) and \(Q\) are involved, so with exponentially high conditional probability no such \(\theta\) descends.
Factorization of each descended map through \(\pi_n\) is unique by surjectivity.

## The histogram after collecting actual group labels

On connected sides, write \(c_A,c_B\) for the prescribed root colors.
The two quotient labels of vertices of color \(c\) are \(c_A^{-1}c\) on the first side and \(c^{-1}c_B\) on the inverse-label second side.
Both formulas are permutations of \(P\); they require no commutativity.
Each class therefore has exactly \(n/m\) occurrences on each side, and a product \(g\in P\) has \(m(n/m)^2=n^2/m\) pairs, by choosing its first class \(i\) and then \(i^{-1}g\).

A path of trivial group label to a different vertex witnesses equality of the two group labels computed from any common root.
Uniform rooted separation above bounds the probability of this event by \(o(1)\) for each vertex, because changing the two chosen roots leaves the group determined by the graph unchanged.
The expected number of vertices having such a collision is \(o(n)\), under girth conditioning.
Markov's inequality gives a deterministic error bound \(o(n)\) below which the collision count falls with probability \(1-o(1)\).
On the simultaneous connectedness and separation events, all vertices outside this set survive individually on their true supports.
Within each quotient class the count after collecting equal labels is consequently in \([n/m-o(n),n/m]\), with unchanged parity since each removal count is even.
Here \(n/m\) is even.
Summing the products of the two corresponding class cardinalities for \(i,i^{-1}g\) proves the true pair-count assertion, including divisibility by four.

For sufficiently large admissible \(n\) all the asserted events hold simultaneously with positive conditional probability, so one can select a sequence as stated.
Choose opposite integer signs on pairs of surviving labels within every \(P\)-class on each side.
Every coefficient still reduces to one, and each factor has zero pushforward to \(\mathbb Z[P]\).
The same signs solve the projected equations after every further homomorphism from \(P\), including all listed targets for the selected group.
This conclusion uses the actual collected supports, while a solution over \(\mathbb Z[G_n]\) would also have to satisfy the finer fibers before projection.
