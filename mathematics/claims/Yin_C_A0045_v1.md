# Testing erasure of quotient classes by equality of vertex labels

## Statement

Let two finite connected rooted graphs immerse in a rose with free group \(F\).
Cone both graphs to the rose, obtaining \(G=F/N\), where \(N\) is normally generated by the labels of the closed walks.
For each vertex \(x\) choose a word \(w_x\) read from the root on its side and write \(g_x=w_xN\).
Replacing the words by other path choices preserves these group labels.
For the second factor one may use \(g_x^{-1}\), which gives the same equality classes on that side.
Fix a known quotient \(\eta:G\to C_7\), and on each side prescribe quotient colors that must vanish from the actual characteristic-two support.
A color is erased exactly when every occurring group label above it has even multiplicity.
One may also prescribe a finite set of pairs of vertices on the same side whose labels must remain distinct, for example a marked vertex against every other vertex on its side.

1. On the original graphs, erasure has an exact finite list of alternatives, each requiring finitely many specified words to belong to \(N\).
   Synchronization of graph edges and the prescribed separations give finite tests rejecting candidate alternatives before word equality is decided.
   If reduced arrangements with only ordinary boundaries are forbidden in the sense of Ope26f, every off-diagonal synchronous component on one side is a tree.
2. There is a separate exact criterion for the existence of a further torsion-free quotient of \(G\) in which the prescribed erasures and separations hold and \(\eta\) still descends.
   Nonexistence of any such quotient has a certificate obtainable by finite enumeration whenever it occurs.
3. For any lower girth bound, there are two finite connected immersed rooted graphs of equal even order satisfying the source's prohibition of reduced arrangements for the two prescribed roots, with a free cone group and an actual quotient onto \(C_7\), whose true supports occupy precisely \(D=\{0,1,2,4\}\) and \(-D\), with odd multiplicity at each of those colors.
   On each side all three missing colors occur before collection and are erased in pairs.
   This example uses different vertex types from Ope26f; its factors have nonzero product in the original group.

The last assertion distinguishes the root-separated cone topology from the fixed types and the original group-ring cancellation of the [source construction](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=24).

## Synchronous components and protected vertices

On one side \(Y\), form the graph of ordered pairs \((x,y)\) of distinct vertices with an edge of signed label \(t\) when both coordinates can read \(t\), moving to \((x',y')\).
An immersion gives at most one outgoing edge per signed label at each vertex.
The partial maps for each \(t\) are injective by the inverse reading, so distinct coordinates remain distinct.
Along such an edge, \(g_{x'}g_{y'}^{-1}=(g_x t)(g_y t)^{-1}=g_xg_y^{-1}\).
Thus each component consists entirely of pairs with equal labels or entirely of pairs with unequal labels.
If a component contains \((x,y)\) and \((x,z)\), this same identity forces \(g_y=g_z\); the conclusion also holds with the coordinates interchanged.
In particular, any component containing a prescribed unequal pair is unavailable for pairing vertices with equal labels.

Under the prohibition of ordinary reduced spherical arrangements, a component cannot contain a cyclically reduced nonempty closed path.
Indeed either coordinate projection preserves immersion, as consecutive inverse signed readings in the pair graph would uniquely reverse the same edge.
Read the first projected cyclic path on one disk and the inverse of the second on another.
Connect corresponding occurrences by disjoint arcs in the complementary annulus on the sphere, using opposite cyclic orders.
Paired signed letters are inverse.
Their underlying unoriented edges in \(Y\) are different: the original synchronized steps have the same signed reading, so using the same edge would give the same start in both coordinates.
This is an arrangement with two ordinary boundaries under [Definition 4.1, pp. 14–15](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=14), a contradiction.
Finiteness therefore gives trees, including isolated vertices.
The tree property concerns common paths and does not by itself determine which components have equal group labels.

For every class to be erased, choose a perfect matching of its vertices into unordered pairs.
An odd-size class gives no alternative and an empty class requires the empty matching.
An alternative consists of these choices on all specified classes on both sides.
It works in \(G\) exactly when \(w_x^{-1}w_y\in N\) for every chosen pair, with the required separations also holding in \(G\).
Indeed vertices with each shared group label can be paired if and only if their number is even.
When the four desired colors are already odd, their continued occupancy is automatic in every quotient retaining \(\eta\), as their parity cannot change.
A spanning tree on each side provides finitely many fundamental closed walks normally generating \(N\).
Thus for each alternative one can enumerate proofs for its necessary word equalities in the original finite presentation.
This semidecides erasure when the separations are already known.

For a finite rejection test, discard potential pairings in components meeting a known unequal pair in either order.
If the remaining unordered graph on any class has no perfect matching, that class cannot be erased.
For each surviving alternative one may further close its equalities on each side under symmetry, transitivity, and every signed letter readable at both endpoints.
One may initialize the closure with any already proved equalities, including the same-coordinate consequence above.
This process terminates on the finite vertex pairs.
If it forces a prescribed unequal pair, the alternative is impossible.
Passing these graph tests alone need not certify the equality of two words in \(G\).

In particular a singleton group label in a color prevents erasure of that color.
Certifying one such vertex for each occupied class protects the whole occurrence set.
In the exceptional two-quadruple case, one protected vertex in an occupied class outside the four odd colors on either side suffices to exclude the [obstruction at precision eight](Yin_C_A0042_v1.md#completing-the-lift-and-distinguishing-the-two-precisions).
For the actual Ope26f type data the [neighbor argument](Yin_C_A0043_v1.md#neighbor-constraints) guarantees an outside occurrence on each side in that case.
For a fixed graph the source guarantees separation from its two prescribed roots, and the outer endpoint in its minimal disk picture stays fixed during surgery. [Ope26f, Proposition 5.1 and §§5.2–5.3](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=20)
To certify additional vertices geometrically one may exclude the exceptional-interval arrangements with each of those vertices as starting root.
In a random law with a uniform \(o(1)\) failure estimate for each prescribed root, a fixed finite list chosen before the matchings has simultaneous separation with probability \(1-o(1)\) by a union bound.
This statement does not supply such an estimate under an arbitrary conditioning on quotient colors, or for roots chosen after observing their collisions.

## The universal torsion-free quotient for one pairing alternative

Write \(\widetilde\eta:F\to C_7\) for the lifted quotient character.
For one alternative put
\[
 N_0=\langle\!\langle N,\,w_x^{-1}w_y\text{ for all chosen pairs}\rangle\!\rangle_F,\qquad
 N_{j+1}=\langle\!\langle z\in F:\ z^k\in N_j\text{ for some }k\ge1\rangle\!\rangle_F,\qquad
 N_\infty=\bigcup_{j\ge0}N_j .
\]
This is an increasing sequence of normal subgroups.
The group \(F/N_\infty\) is torsion-free, since \(z^k\in N_\infty\) places \(z^k\) in one finite stage and hence \(z\) in the next.
Every homomorphism from \(F/N_0\) to a torsion-free group kills \(N_\infty/N_0\) by the same implication at every stage.
Consequently the alternative is realized in some torsion-free quotient with the designated character and separations if and only if
\[
 N_\infty\subseteq\ker\widetilde\eta,\qquad
                  w_x^{-1}w_y\notin N_\infty
                     \quad\text{for every required unequal pair }(x,y).
\]
Necessity follows by factoring the character through that quotient.
For sufficiency the universal quotient \(F/N_\infty\) itself works.
The color condition cannot be dropped merely because all initial pairings have equal color: a later step can kill an element whose nonzero color has order seven.

All stages are effectively enumerable from the finite graphs and alternatives.
At stage zero enumerate products of conjugates of the finitely many defining words and their inverses.
For the next stage enumerate words and positive exponents, watch for their freely reduced powers in the enumeration of the preceding stage, and enumerate the normal closure of every root so found.
Dovetail the stages to enumerate \(N_\infty\).
A failure of either displayed condition thus has a finite witnessed derivation, either a word of nonzero color or a required difference appearing in this enumeration.
There are only finitely many pairing alternatives.
If none works in any admissible torsion-free quotient, parallel enumeration eventually witnesses failure for each one.
This does not give a decision procedure for arbitrary instances.
Existence in a further quotient tests consistency of the added equalities, whereas occurrence in \(G\) requires membership of the differences in the original \(N\).

## A connected aspherical example with paired erasures on both sides

Use forward coordinates in \(C_7\) on both graphs before inverting the second factor.
Set \(D=\{0,1,2,4\}\), \(E=\{3,5,6\}\).
Fix \(\ell\ge3\) at least the desired girth and choose \(d_3=1,d_5=2,d_6=1\), so both \(d_k\) and \(k+d_k\) are in \(D\).
At the first graph's root wedge three otherwise disjoint simple cycles reading
\[
                 W_k=a_k c_k^{\ell-2} b_k,\qquad k\in E,
\]
with all nine letters distinct and of respective colors \(d_k,0,-d_k\).
Each cycle has length \(\ell\); its internal vertices have color \(d_k\).
The second graph starts as a single root.
At each of the two roots and for each \(k\), add a pendant edge of its own fresh letter \(z_{Y,k}\) of color \(k\), followed by a path on new vertices reading \(W_k\).
Each of these attached arms has just two vertices of color \(k\), its initial vertex after the bridge and its final vertex; every intervening vertex has color \(k+d_k\).
No arm for another missing color has an internal vertex of color \(k\).
The maps to the common rose are immersions, including at the wedge and along the chains of \(c_k\).
All cyclically immersed closed paths lie in the first graph's core, and the two graphs together have girth \(\ell\).

The cone group has precisely the normal closure of \(W_3,W_5,W_6\) as its relation subgroup.
Eliminating the three \(b_k\) gives a free group.
The cone complex is aspherical: the fundamental loops forming the chosen cycle basis give a homotopy-equivalent rose-with-relator-disks model relative to the rose, as in [Ope26f, Lemma 5.2](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=20).
Each \(b_k\) occurs exactly once in one relator and nowhere in the other relators, giving an elementary collapse of its disk and rose edge.
The letter colors therefore give a surjective character to \(C_7\).
In each attached arm the two labels of color \(k\) coincide, from the relation \(W_k=1\).
Both erased occurrences have group label \(z_{Y,k}\), so the whole class vanishes exactly in that factor over \(\mathbb F_2\).

For every color of \(D\) with currently even occurrence count on a side, attach one additional leaf there by an edge at its root with a fresh letter of that color.
All four counts in \(D\) now have odd parity and each of the three missing colors still has two occurrences.
Each side has even total size.
Equalize their sizes if needed by adding two fresh pendant edges at a time of color zero to the smaller side.
Each fresh letter is a free generator in the resulting cone group.
Odd total multiplicity in each color of \(D\) forces a nonempty odd true support count there after collection.
The forward true colors are now exactly \(D\) on both sides, and the inverse convention gives \(D,-D\) for the actual two factors.

To check the full prescribed-root arrangement prohibition, every ordinary boundary runs in the wedge of cycles and traverses some \(b_k\)-edge, since deleting those three core edges leaves a tree there.
Among all core paths that letter can only be read on a single underlying edge.
If all boundary paths stay in the core, an occurrence of such a letter on an ordinary disk cannot be paired with a different underlying edge.
If an exceptional interval based at a prescribed root enters a pendant arm or leaf, it must cross its fresh bridge exactly once: a reduced path in the dangling tree cannot return through that bridge.
This fresh letter has no other occurrence available for pairing, whether on that interval or any ordinary disk.
These arguments also cover an interval based on the second side, which is a tree.
Thus there is no reduced arrangement of Definition 4.1 for the prescribed roots, although all six specified pairs of non-root vertices collide.

The two quotient masks have zero product over \(\mathbb F_2[C_7]\) and the [seven-fiber computation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0046_v1.md#why-four-appears-in-the-identity-coefficient) gives their precision-eight lifting obstruction.
The corresponding product in the original group ring has identity coefficient one.
Every non-root forward label on the second side contains one fresh second-side bridge or leaf letter, which survives free elimination of the \(b_k\) and is absent from all first-side labels.
On the first side a non-root core label is \(a_k c_k^j\) with \(0\le j\le\ell-2\), and every non-root label in a pendant tree contains its fresh letter.
Hence only the two roots have coincident forward labels across the two sides.
Thus the example lacks the simultaneous odd-degree cancellation of the source's two factors and does not exhibit an Ope26f matching with these erasures.
Whether a matching with all the source's type and cancellation conditions realizes them remains unresolved by these tests.
