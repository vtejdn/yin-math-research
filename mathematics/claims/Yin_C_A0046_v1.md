# Two-vertex simultaneous components force incompatible letter ratios

## Statement

Take the full vertex sets of the two graphs in the matching model of [Ope26f, §§1–2.1](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=2).
For an inverse pair whose two letters are ordinary, choose one orientation \(t\), and write \(\bar t\) for the opposite letter.
There are \(r=8255\) such pairs.
Keep just their edges in each of the two graphs, giving subgraphs on the original vertex sets.
Let \(\Lambda_A,\Lambda_B\le\mathbb Z^r\) be generated by signed letter-count vectors of all closed walks on the respective subgraphs, with traversal of \(\bar t\) counted negatively.
Put \(\ell(v)=\sum_{t=1}^r v_t\bmod 2\).

Give every vertex on side \(A\) a nonzero complex coefficient \(U_x\), and every vertex on side \(B\) a nonzero coefficient \(V_y\), used on the inverse group labels of the second factor.
For each two-vertex connected component \(C\) of the simultaneous-step graph on \(A\times B\), impose
\[
                        \sum_{(x,y)\in C} U_x V_y=0.                    \tag{1}
\]
Use a matching tuple of girth at least three, as in the source.

1. Equations (1) admit nonzero complex vertex coefficients if and only if \(\ell\) vanishes on \(\Lambda_A\cap\Lambda_B\).
2. In the source's girth-conditioned ensemble, with probability \(1-\exp(-\Omega(n))\), both lattices have finite odd index.
   On that event (1) has no solution in units of any commutative ring with \(1\ne-1\).
   In particular even these component equations cannot lift through the all-ones point modulo four in the integers of any finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\), with the modulus measured in powers of two.
3. The first implication from a solution to letter ratios also holds for actual group-product equations on occurrence vertices if the complete two-cell product fibers of the displayed kind retain a connected spanning bipartite graph at every ordinary inverse pair, as made precise below.
   The source proves constancy of the group product on each simultaneous component and permits products from different components to coincide.
   The high-probability conclusion in item 2 concerns (1).

The two full sides are connected with probability tending to one by [the connectivity calculation](Yin_C_A0040_v1.md#the-probability-of-a-cut).
On that event the source's two selected root components give precisely the input vertex lists used here.
The deterministic lattice tests allow the retained ordinary-edge subgraphs themselves to be disconnected.

## Isolated edges and their bipartite graphs

Write \(q=128,\ k=q+1,\ v=q^2+q+1,\ p=k/v,\ m=np\).
For a signed letter \(t\) denote its two sets of incident vertices by \(A_t,B_t\), of size \(m\) each, and write \(x^t,y^t\) for the matched endpoints.
A pair \((x,y)\in A_t\times B_t\) determines the edge to \((x^t,y^t)\).
For \(t,\bar t\) both ordinary, this edge is an entire component if the ordinary lines of \(x,y\) differ and at least one of \(x,y\) has no extras, and the corresponding two conditions hold at \(x^t,y^t\).
Indeed each endpoint then has just one common outgoing label.
Conversely a two-vertex component consists of a single edge, since the large-girth side graphs have no loops or parallel edges and the labelled maps are immersions.
Its label pair cannot include an extra, since at an endpoint sharing an extra letter the two sides share both extras of the \(A\)-vertex and at least one ordinary point.
It therefore arises from an ordinary–ordinary inverse pair.

Form a bipartite graph between \(A_t\) and \(B_t\) by joining \(x\) to \(y\) whenever their edge is an entire two-vertex component.
For a fixed \(x\), among \(B_t\) the fraction on its line is \(1/k\), because the \(k\) lines through an ordinary point are equally represented.
The fraction with extras is \(p\).
At the opposite endpoint the same bounds apply, since \(y\mapsto y^t\) bijects \(B_t\) with \(B_{\bar t}\).
For a fixed \(y\) the bounds on the \(A\)-side are \(1/k\) and \(3p/2\) at either endpoint.
The union bound gives minimum degree at least
\[
                          (1-2/k-3p)m>m/2.                              \tag{2}
\]
Two vertices of the same part have a common neighbor, and every vertex in the other part has at least one neighbor.
Thus the graph is connected and spans both parts, for every choice of the bijections.

On an edge of this bipartite graph, (1) for its component gives
\[
             \frac{U_{x^t}}{U_x}\,\frac{V_{y^t}}{V_y}=-1.               \tag{3}
\]
The \(B\)-weights belong to inverse group labels, but the simultaneous step still takes the vertex coefficient from \(V_y\) to \(V_{y^t}\).
Equation (3) therefore uses the forward vertex ratios on both sides.
Connectedness forces the first ratio to be one fixed \(a_t\) over all \(A_t\), and the second to be \(b_t=-a_t^{-1}\) over all \(B_t\).
The reverse orientation uses the inverse ratios.
Conversely such ratios on the two ordinary-edge subgraphs ensure (1) on all two-vertex components.

## The two cycle lattices

Products of ratios telescope on closed walks, so \(a^v=1\) for \(v\in\Lambda_A\), and \(a^v=(-1)^{\ell(v)}\) for \(v\in\Lambda_B\).
In a ring with \(1\ne-1\) this requires \(\ell=0\) on their intersection.
Over \(\mathbb C\) the requirement is also sufficient.
Indeed it gives a single well-defined character on \(\Lambda_A+\Lambda_B\) by multiplying the trivial character on \(\Lambda_A\) and the sign character on \(\Lambda_B\).
This extends to \(\mathbb Z^r\) since the roots required in Smith coordinates exist in \(\mathbb C^\times\).
Set \(a_t\) to its coordinate values and \(b_t=-a_t^{-1}\).
Choose one initial unit in every connected component of each retained side subgraph and transport along paths with its ratios.
All closed-walk products are one, so this defines vertex weights on both sides and proves the converse.
The lattices can be computed from fundamental closed walks of a spanning forest on each retained side.

If both indices are finite and odd, their intersection contains \(d\mathbb Z^r\) for the odd integer \(d=[\mathbb Z^r:\Lambda_A][\mathbb Z^r:\Lambda_B]\).
The vector \(d e_t\) has \(\ell(d e_t)=1\), giving the ring-independent obstruction.
For any finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\), its valuation ring modulo four has \(1\ne-1\) even if the extension is ramified, and a lift of a nonzero residue coefficient is a unit.

## Odd indices in the original matching ensemble

The lattice on a side has finite odd index precisely when its image modulo two spans \(\mathbb F_2^r\), by integer diagonal form.
Failure means that some nonzero binary letter assignment \(c\in\mathbb F_2^r\) vanishes on all retained closed walks.
Equivalently there are binary vertex colors \(f\) with \(f(x^t)=f(x)+c_t\) for every retained matching, by path transport in each component.

Use the [matching and entropy count](Yin_C_A0041_v1.md#strict-entropy-loss) with two colors on one entire side, imposing compatibility only for the retained matchings.
The original unconditioned law makes these bijections independently uniform.
Let \(\lambda_\tau>0\) be the fraction of vertices of full prescribed type \(\tau\), \(P_\tau\) its binary color distribution, and \(P_t\) the resulting color distribution on the vertices carrying \(t\).
For each fixed set of type histograms, the expected number of compatible colorings is at most \(\exp(n(F_0-F_1)+O(\log(n+1)))\) with
\[
      F_0=\sum_\tau\lambda_\tau H(P_\tau),\qquad
      F_1=\frac12\sum_{t\ \mathrm{retained\ signed}}p\,H(P_t).
\]
Incompatible label histograms yield zero, and the number of possible histograms is polynomial in \(n\) since all types are fixed.
Each ordinary line has \(129\) points and at most three are paired to extras, hence concavity gives \(F_1\ge(126/2)F_0\).
Equality \(F_0-F_1=0\) requires every type and every retained letter class to be monochromatic.
The incidence graph of full types and retained ordinary points is connected on either side.
Types on one line share retained points.
For any two distinct lines choose a retained point on each, different from their common intersection; the line through these two points supplies a type connecting their colors.
Thus all vertices must have the same color.
Each retained letter occurs, so matching compatibility forces every \(c_t=0\).

For fixed \(c\ne0\), the compatible type distributions are a compact set, being given by the closed equations identifying the opposite-label color distributions up to the shifts \(c_t\).
The continuous exponent \(F_0-F_1\) is strictly negative throughout this set when it is nonempty.
The histogram bound and a finite union over assignments show that failure has probability \(\exp(-\Omega(n))\) before conditioning.
The [subexponential cost of logarithmic girth](Yin_C_A0041_v1.md#girth-conditioning-and-the-torsion-free-realizations) preserves an exponential bound on either side.
The union bound proves the two-sided assertion.
The estimate remains exponential under further conditioning on the source's geometric safeguards of probability \(1-o(1)\) in the girth-conditioned law.

## What survives collection of the actual products

On connected full sides, attach \(g_x,h_y^{-1}\) to the vertices as in [Ope26f, §6](../literature/pdf/Ope26f.pdf#page=24).
Suppose that for every retained inverse pair one keeps only those bipartite edges from (2) for which the two occurrences form the entire fiber of \(g_x h_y^{-1}\) in the occurrence rectangle.
If each remaining graph is still connected and spanning, any zero product on this rectangle with unit occurrence weights must satisfy (3) on it.
Hence the same necessary intersection condition follows for those actual coefficient equations.
In particular it suffices that at each vertex fewer than \((1/2-2/k-3p)m\) incident good partners are lost in passing to complete group-product fibers, since each remaining graph then has minimum degree greater than \(m/2\).
If the group labels on each individual side are distinct, this applies directly to the fixed supports.
For a lift at the original characteristic-two point one can also distribute a coefficient on an odd-multiplicity label among its occurrences as one lifted coefficient and cancelling pairs \(1,-1\), and use only cancelling pairs at an even-multiplicity label.
This gives unit occurrence weights for any putative lift through the prescribed residue point.

No such connectivity or loss bound for the complete group-product fibers is established here.
Distinct simultaneous components could merge at one group product even if the two individual vertex lists were injective.
Consequently failure of (1) obstructs the [component-equation lifting test](Yin_C_A0038_v1.md#the-simultaneous-step-system-in-ope26f), while the full complex group-product equations of the source construction remain open.
