# Stability of ordinary-letter cycle lattices and coefficient equations under product-fiber mergers

## Statement

Use either the original matching model of Ope26f or the [balanced planted \(C_7\) model](../ledger/Yin_L_A0047_v1.md#a-different-choice-of-matchings), with its stated logarithmic girth conditioning.
In each of the two side graphs retain just the \(r=8255\) inverse pairs with both letters ordinary.
Orient each pair once.
For a retained subgraph \(H\), write \(\Lambda(H)\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\) for the lattice generated by signed letter counts of all closed walks of \(H\), including all its components.

1. There are constants \(\varepsilon,c>0\) such that, with probability at least \(1-e^{-cn}\) for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\), the following holds on both sides simultaneously.
   After **any** deletion of at most \(\varepsilon n\) retained edges on each side, both remaining lattices have finite odd index in \(\mathbb Z^r\).
2. Consider any tuple with both full side graphs connected, girth at least three, the source type counts, and the deletion property in item 1.
   Write \(g_x,h_y^{-1}\) for the factor labels on the two occurrence lists.
   Call \((x,y)\) impure if the occurrence fiber of \(g_xh_y^{-1}\) contains more than one connected component of the simultaneous-step graph.
   There is a constant \(\delta>0\), depending only on \(\varepsilon\) and the type frequencies, with the following property.
   If \(J\) is the number of impure occurrences, then for every commutative ring with \(1\ne-1\) and every choice of unit coefficients on the two actual binary-collected supports, the resulting elements \(a,b\) satisfy
   \[
                       J+|\operatorname{supp}(ab)|>\delta n^2 .
   \]

Combining the two assertions with the [two-root product-fiber estimate](Yin_C_A0049_v1.md#statement) gives a common constant \(c_*>0\) for the following conclusion in the two specified laws.
With probability tending to one, \(|\operatorname{supp}(ab)|\ge c_*n^2\) simultaneously for all these rings and coefficients on the actual supports.
In particular this holds for arbitrary nonzero complex coefficients.
It also holds for all lifts of the marked coefficients in the valuation ring modulo four of any finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\), including ramified extensions.
The probability event is a property of the matching tuple alone.

## Approximate binary color equations

Treat the original model as having a single planted color and the other model as having color group \(P=C_7\).
Put \(d=|P|\), \(p=129/16513\), and write \(\eta(t)\in P\) for the prescribed shift along a letter \(t\), zero in the original model.
Each full vertex type \(\tau\) has fixed frequency \(\lambda_\tau>0\) and is split equally among the \(d\) planted colors.
On one side each oriented ordinary inverse pair and planted color \(z\) use an independent uniform bijection between the \(np/d\) vertices of color \(z\) carrying \(t\) and the \(np/d\) vertices of color \(z+\eta(t)\) carrying \(\bar t\).
We use the unconditioned matching law first.

A sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^r\) has finite odd index precisely when its reduction spans \(\mathbb F_2^r\), by Smith normal form.
Suppose that after at most \(\varepsilon n\) edge deletions a side fails this test.
There is then a nonzero assignment \(s\in\mathbb F_2^r\) annihilating all remaining closed-walk vectors.
Transport a binary value along paths in each component of the remaining subgraph, using \(s_t\) in both directions on the inverse pair.
The annihilation property gives well-defined bits \(f(x)\) on all vertices.
All but at most \(\varepsilon n\) of the original retained edges satisfy
\[
                         f(x^t)=f(x)+s_t.                               \tag{1}
\]
Conversely deleting the violating edges of any such coloring gives a lattice annihilated by \(s\).
This equivalence allows the deleted edges to depend on the realized matching.

For a proposed binary coloring let \(P_{\tau,z}\) be its two frequencies on full type \(\tau\) and color \(z\).
Write \(u_{t,z}=p^{-1}\sum_{\tau:t\in S_\tau}\lambda_\tau P_{\tau,z}\) for the endpoint distribution on one block.
For each chosen orientation \(t\) and color \(z\), let \(J_{t,z}\) be the \(2\times2\) table of joint frequencies of the two endpoint bits under the matching.
Its marginals must be \(u=u_{t,z}\) and \(v=u_{\bar t,z+\eta(t)}\).
Writing \(N=np/d\), the probability of one specified integral table for a fixed vertex coloring is
\[
            \frac{\prod_i(Nu_i)!\,\prod_j(Nv_j)!}
                 {N!\,\prod_{i,j}(NJ_{t,z}(i,j))!}.
\]
Indeed the source vertices of each bit are first allocated by destination bit, and then the allocated vertices are bijected with the individually named destinations.
The number of vertex colorings with the type histograms is the product of their type-and-color multinomial coefficients.
Writing \(H\) for natural-log entropy and \(I(J)=H(u)+H(v)-H(J)\), the expected number with specified histograms and joint tables is at most
\[
        \exp\{n(A-B)+O(\log(n+1))\},\qquad
        A=\sum_{\tau,z}\frac{\lambda_\tau}{d}H(P_{\tau,z}),\qquad
        B=\sum_{t\ {\rm oriented},z}\frac{p}{d} I(J_{t,z}).            \tag{2}
\]
The factorial estimates hold uniformly at zero counts, and the number of possible histograms and tables is polynomial in \(n\) because the index sets are fixed.
The number of violations divided by \(n\) is the continuous nonnegative function
\[
       D_s=\sum_{t\ {\rm oriented},z}\frac{p}{d}
                              \sum_{j\ne i+s_t}J_{t,z}(i,j) ,
\]
where the inner sum ranges over the two bits \(i,j\).

## A uniform gap under deletions

Impose only the probability-simplex and marginal constraints in (2), giving a compact space independent of \(n\).
When \(D_s=0\) each joint distribution is supported on the bijection \(i\mapsto i+s_t\).
Then \(I(J)=H(u)=H(v)\), and the sum for \(B\) becomes a half sum of entropy over both signed labels and all colors.
Concavity of entropy gives
\[
        B\ \ge\ \tfrac12\sum_{\tau,z}
               \frac{\lambda_\tau}{d}\,|S_\tau\cap T_{\rm ret}|\,H(P_{\tau,z})
             \ \ge\ 63 A .
\]
Here \(T_{\rm ret}\) denotes the ordinary letters paired with other ordinary letters, and every line has \(129\) points of which at most three have been removed.
If \(A-B=0\) then \(A=B=0\).
At each planted color all types and all retained letters are monochromatic.
The incidence graph of full types and retained points is connected, as in the [cycle-lattice calculation](Yin_C_A0046_v1.md#odd-indices-in-the-original-matching-ensemble).
Indeed two types on the same line share retained points, and two distinct lines can be joined through a third line using a retained point on each away from their common intersection.
Thus the binary bit is a single value \(f(z)\) for each planted color.
Every retained label occurs at every planted color, giving
\[
                             f(z+\eta(t))=f(z)+s_t .
\]
Each \(\eta(t)\) has odd order, including order one.
Iteration through that order forces \(s_t=0\).
This holds even if the retained letters do not generate all of \(P\).

Consequently for every \(s\ne0\), \(A-B\) is strictly negative on the compact zero set \(D_s=0\), whenever that set is nonempty.
Since there are finitely many \(s\), there exist \(\varepsilon,\gamma>0\) such that \(A-B\le-\gamma\) whenever \(s\ne0\) and \(D_s\le\varepsilon\).
Otherwise a sequence with \(D_s\) tending to zero and exponent approaching or exceeding zero has a convergent subsequence with fixed nonzero \(s\), contradicting the zero-defect calculation.
Equation (2), with the polynomial number of tables and the fixed number of assignments, now gives the exponential bound for (1) with at most \(\varepsilon n\) violations.
The two sides can use a common \(\varepsilon\) by taking the smaller of their bounds.
The source-compatible girth conditioning has probability at least \(\exp(-o(n))\) in the [original model](Yin_C_A0041_v1.md#girth-conditioning-and-the-torsion-free-realizations) and the [planted model](../ledger/Yin_L_A0047_v1.md#girth-local-expansion-and-the-cost-of-a-matching-prescription).
Dividing by that probability and taking a union bound over both sides proves item 1.

## Actual product fibers after edge removal

Let \(M=np\), \(k=129\), and \(\kappa=2/k+3p<1/4\).
For each chosen orientation \(t\), use the bipartite graph on the \(M\) vertices of \(A_t\) and the \(M\) vertices of \(B_t\) whose edges are two-vertex simultaneous components.
Its minimum degree is at least \((1-\kappa)M\), by the [fixed type count](Yin_C_A0046_v1.md#isolated-edges-and-their-bipartite-graphs).
Fix unit coefficients on the occurrence vertices and denote the resulting group-ring elements by \(a,b\).
Remove a bipartite edge whenever that component is not the whole actual group-product fiber on the occurrence rectangle.
These removals number at most the total number \(J\) of impure occurrences, since each such component belongs to just one oriented inverse pair and uses separate occurrences.
Also remove an edge whenever its component is the whole fiber and the coefficient of this group product in \(ab\) is nonzero.
Distinct edges removed for this second reason have distinct group products, because a whole fiber here consists of a single edge with its two endpoints.
If \(h_t\) is the number removed at inverse pair \(t\), it follows that \(\sum_t h_t\le J+|\operatorname{supp}(ab)|\).

For each \(t\) discard from its bipartite graph every vertex incident to at least \(M/8\) removed edges.
The total number discarded over both parts of that graph is at most \(16h_t/M\).
Choose \(\delta>0\) with \(16\delta/p\le\varepsilon\) and \(16\delta/p^2\le 1/8\).
Suppose \(J+|\operatorname{supp}(ab)|\le\delta n^2\).
Then at most \(M/8\) vertices were discarded at any one letter pair, and at most \(\varepsilon n\) were discarded over all letter pairs.
In the surviving bipartite graph every vertex still has more than \((3/4-\kappa)M>M/2\) neighbors.
Both parts are nonempty. Any two vertices in the same part have a common neighbor, proving connectedness.

Along every surviving bipartite edge the full product coefficient is zero and has exactly the two summands \(U_xV_y+U_{x^t}V_{y^t}\).
It follows from connectedness that \(U_{x^t}/U_x=a_t\) on the surviving \(A_t\) and \(V_{y^t}/V_y=b'_t=-a_t^{-1}\) on the surviving \(B_t\).
Discard on each original side the undirected matching edge with forward endpoint \(x\in A_t\) or \(y\in B_t\) whenever that endpoint was discarded at \(t\).
Each remaining oriented matching edge, and hence its reverse by inversion, has the stated constant ratio.
At most \(\varepsilon n\) edges were discarded on either side.
By hypothesis the two resulting cycle lattices have finite odd indices.
If \(d_0\) is the product of those indices, each lattice contains \(d_0 e_t\) for every retained coordinate.
Telescoping around closed walks therefore gives \(a_t^{d_0}=(b'_t)^{d_0}=1\).
This contradicts \(a_t b'_t=-1\) since \(d_0\) is odd and \(1\ne-1\).

Finally, any proposed unit coefficients on the binary-collected supports can be distributed back to unit coefficients on the occurrence lists.
For a group label of odd multiplicity use its assigned coefficient once and cancel the remaining occurrences in pairs \(1,-1\).
For a label of even multiplicity use only such cancelling pairs.
This preserves both group-ring elements and their product exactly, proving item 2.

## Positive density for the actual collected supports

Under each of the two laws the [purity and connectedness conclusion](Yin_C_A0049_v1.md#statement) gives connected full sides and \(J/n^2\to0\) in probability.
This input concerns equalities between pairs of products and goes beyond the [singleton estimates for individual lists](Yin_C_A0040_v1.md#distinct-labels-away-from-the-roots).
Intersect the event \(J\le(\delta/2)n^2\) and full-side connectedness with the deletion event in item 1.
The joint probability tends to one and item 2 gives the product bound with \(c_*=\delta/2\), using the smaller constants for the two laws.
Further conditioning on their torsion-free geometric safeguards of probability \(1-o(1)\) preserves this conclusion.
No random choice of coefficients or count over coefficient rings enters the proof.
In a characteristic-zero field every nonzero coefficient is a unit.
For the ring \(\mathcal O_L/4\mathcal O_L\) of any finite extension \(L/\mathbb Q_2\), every lift of one in its residue field is a unit, and \(2\ne0\) in this quotient even when the ramification index exceeds one.
Thus the stated lower bound applies to these products, ruling out exact lifting at the marked point on the indicated supports.
The result is for these specific pairs of supports in the two matching laws and does not determine complex group-ring products on other supports or regular matrix ranks in general.
