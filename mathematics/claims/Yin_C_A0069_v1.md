# A finite product-fiber test for nonclosed matrices on an individual four-line group

## Statement

Fix a finitely generated torsion-free group \(G\), including any single safeguarded four-line realization.
Let \(A\) be a finite complex group-ring matrix with \(m\) columns, \(M>\|A\|^2\), \(Q=I-A^*A/M\), and \(p\) the orthogonal kernel projection.
For an integer \(L\ge1\) choose a constant unitary \(W\) of order \(m\), which may be the identity, and compute the finite matrix
\[
 T=W^*Q^L W,\qquad S_{ij}=\sum_{g\in G}|\widehat T_{ij}(g)|.
\]
Amplitudes at the same endpoint and at the same pair of matrix coordinates are added before taking the absolute value.
If every connected diagonal block of the symmetric nonnegative matrix \(S\) has spectral radius at most one, then \(p\) has **finite group support**.
It is determined by finite matrix kernels and by which of finitely many closed words in \(G\) are the identity.
In particular \(I-p\) itself is a group-ring matrix of closed range with the same kernel as \(A\).
For some constant \(C\) depending on the fixed input, for every \(l\ge k\ge1,\ R\ge1\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|Q^k-p\|_2^2,\ \|Q^l-Q^k\|_2^2 &\le C(1+k)^{-1/2},\\
 \sum_g(1-e^{-|g|/R})^4\|\widehat{Q^k}(g)\|_{\rm HS}^2
    &\le C\{R^{-4}+(1+k)^{-1/2}\}.                                  \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
For \(3<\beta<4\), all schedules \(k_j=\lceil2^{\kappa j}\rceil\) with \(\kappa>2\beta\) pass the [corrected moment test](Yin_C_A0062_v1.md#statement) after discarding any initial repeated times.
The construction below on the original four-line group requires the whole group for both the input support and the kernel-projection support under independent translations of the coordinates.
It passes the finite test at \(L=2\), fails at \(L=1\) in the same coordinates of the test, and gives the sharp threshold \(\kappa>2\beta\).
This sufficient condition does not assume a second matrix with the same kernel.

## Finite and infinite components of the lifted graph

Work in the constant coordinates of \(T\).
Let \(P\) have group coefficients \(P_{ij}(g)=|\widehat T_{ij}(g)|\).
It is selfadjoint, and each of its powers dominates the corresponding power of \(T\) entry by entry in coefficient absolute value, by expanding the true finite product fibers.
On an irreducible block of \(S\), its positive Perron vector \(\phi\) gives \(\|P\|\le\rho(S)\) by the weighted row-sum bound, and the same bound holds for \(T\).
Indeed summing the absolute quadratic form uses \(2|ab|\le(\phi_j/\phi_i)|a|^2+(\phi_i/\phi_j)|b|^2\) on an edge between states \(i,j\).
Different blocks have no entries between them.
If the block radius is less than one, \(T^n\) decays there in operator norm and the block contributes zero to the projection.

Suppose a block has radius one.
Use a vertex \((j,x)\) for each state \(j\) and \(x\in G\), and draw an edge from \((j,x)\) to \((i,gx)\) for \(P_{ij}(g)>0\).
Reverse edges have the same weight.
On the finite state graph choose a base state and a spanning tree.
Let \(w_i\) be the displacement, acting by left multiplication, along the chosen tree path to state \(i\).
The closed walks at the base state generate the subgroup \(K\le G\) of displacements generated by the finitely many \(w_i^{-1}g w_j\).
Tree edges and identity loops may be included in this list.
If all these words are trivial, every lifted component has exactly one vertex of each state, at coordinates \((i,w_i x)\) for a common \(x\).
On these components \(T\) acts by identical copies of the finite Hermitian coefficient matrix, with an entry at only \(w_iw_j^{-1}\) for each state pair \(i,j\).
Its eigenspace at one, computed on that single finite matrix and copied back with these displacements, is the required part of \(W^*pW\).
On the complement its powers converge exponentially to zero since \(0<T\le I\).

If one displacement is nonidentity, it has infinite order by torsion-freeness.
Fix such a loop and base it also at every state using tree paths.
Write \(s_i\) for its infinite-order conjugate there.
These finitely many paths and their right translates imply the quadratic form comparison
\[
 I-P\ \ge\ c\,\operatorname{diag}_i\big(2I-s_i-s_i^{-1}\big)             \tag{2}
\]
for some \(c>0\).
The corresponding finite-state path comparison is classical. [Diaconis and Saloff-Coste, Theorem 2.1, p. 698](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~lsc/papers/comp-rev.pdf#page=4)
In detail, the form of \(I-P\) is the sum, over directed lifted edges, of half the weight \(P_{ij}(g)\phi_i\phi_j\) times
\(\big|f(i,gx)/\phi_i-f(j,x)/\phi_j\big|^2\), by \(S\phi=\phi\).
The squared difference from \((i,x)\) to \((i,s_i x)\) divided by \(\phi_i^2\) is bounded by the path length times the sum of these edgewise squares on its path.
Each edge has uniformly bounded multiplicity after summing over \(x\) and the finitely many states, since any one position in a fixed path specifies the starting \(x\) uniquely.
There are finitely many positive edge weights and state weights.
This proves (2) on finitely supported vectors and hence throughout the Hilbert space.
The right side of (2) has no atom at zero and trace mass \(O(\sqrt t)\) on \([0,t]\) for \(t\downarrow0\), by Fourier transform on the right cosets of each \(\langle s_i\rangle\).
The subspace inequality for spectral sublevels transfers the upper bound to \(I-P\), including the absence of the zero atom. [Trace comparison](Yin_C_A0062_v1.md#a-rational-example-using-the-whole-group)

There is also a uniform holding probability on the block.
Indeed \(T\ge(1-\|A\|^2/M)^L I>0\), hence \(P_{ii}(e)\ge(1-\|A\|^2/M)^L\).
Choose \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) less than this bound.
Subtracting \(\epsilon I\) leaves nonnegative coefficients with Perron row sums \((1-\epsilon)\phi_i\).
The preceding norm bound gives \(\sigma(P)\subseteq[2\epsilon-1,1]\).
On the positive half of this interval, integration against the edge bound at one gives \(\tau(P^{2n}1_{[0,1]}(P))=O((1+n)^{-1/2})\), by \((1-t)^{2n}\le e^{-2nt}\) and integration by parts.
The negative half contributes at most the block size times \((1-2\epsilon)^{2n}\).
Now \(\|T^n\|_2^2=\tau(T^{2n})\le\tau(P^{2n})\) on this block by coefficient domination at the identity.
Thus no kernel projection survives on infinite components.

Adding the blocks, undoing the constant unitary and using spectral monotonicity between consecutive multiples of \(L\) gives the first error bound in (1).
The increment bound follows pointwise on the positive spectrum from \(0\le Q^k-Q^l\le Q^k-p\).
Multiplication of Fourier coefficients by \((1-e^{-|g|/R})^2\) contracts the trace \(L^2\) norm.
For the finite support of \(p\) its squared norm after that multiplication is \(O(R^{-4})\); the triangle inequality gives (1).
Consequently the exterior weighted square tail of \(p\) vanishes beyond the largest of the finite component displacements.
The argument is a sufficient test for a fixed \(A,L,W\); failure of the Perron bound gives no conclusion on its own.

## Cancellation after two steps on the original group

On a fixed four-line group choose a nonidentity element \(z\) of infinite order.
In its cyclic group ring fix \(a=1/64\) and take \(0<\varepsilon<a/4\) sufficiently small.
Put \(q_0=1-6a+a\sum_{j\in\{1,3,4\}}(z^j+z^{-j})\) and set
\[
 q=q_0+\varepsilon(2-z^2-z^{-2}).
\]
The coefficients sum to one, and their absolute values sum to \(1+4\varepsilon\).
The coefficient of \(q_0^2\) is strictly positive at every exponent from \(-8\) through \(8\), since every such exponent is a sum of two elements of \(\{0,\pm1,\pm3,\pm4\}\).
Thus \(q^2\) has positive coefficients on that interval for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), with total one.
One may take \(\varepsilon=2^{-20}\), since each coefficient there starts at least \(a^2\) and the change at any exponent is at most \(8\varepsilon+16\varepsilon^2<a^2\) by the convolution \(\ell^1\) bound.
Let \(A_c\) be the column with entries
\[
 \sqrt{a-4\varepsilon}(1-z),\quad
 \sqrt\varepsilon(1-z)^2,\quad
 \sqrt a(1-z^3),\quad \sqrt a(1-z^4).
\]
Then \(I-A_c^*A_c=q\), using \(|1+z|^2+|1-z|^2=4I\).
Here \(q>0\) as \(q_0\ge1-12a>0\).
Its distance from one on the circle, \(1-q(e^{i\xi})\), is comparable to \(\xi^2\) near zero and vanishes nowhere else, by the displayed sum of squares and its first term.
The column has no kernel and has nonclosed range.
The true finite return integrals on the circle give \(\|q^k-q^l\|_2^2\asymp k^{-1/2}\) when \(l/k\) stays in a compact subinterval of \((1,\infty)\).
The upper bound follows by integrating \(e^{-c k\xi^2}\) near the origin and an exponentially small remainder elsewhere.
For the lower bound integrate over \(k^{-1/2}<|\xi|<2k^{-1/2}\).

To require full group support, choose a power of two \(r\) large enough for a list of \(r\) elements \(h_i\) containing the identity and generators of the group, with repetitions allowed.
The column \(v=(h_i/\sqrt r)_i\) is an isometry, so \(P_f=vv^*\) is a projection with entries \(h_i h_j^{-1}/r\).
Let \(S_0\) be a constant real orthogonal Walsh matrix of size \(r\) with first row constant, and set \(P_f'=S_0P_f S_0^*\).
Use the block diagonal matrix
\[
          A=A_c\oplus (I_r-P_f')/2,\qquad M=1 .
\]
Its true kernel projection is \(0\oplus P_f'\).
The \(11\) entry of \(P_f'\) is \(r^{-2}\sum_{i,j} h_i h_j^{-1}\) with positive coefficients at the identity and every generator.
Its identity coefficient is strictly smaller than one as the list contains different elements.
Thus the corresponding entry of the input and that of the projection each force the whole group under independent row and column translations by taking differences within the translated support.
The test in the constant coordinates \(W=1\oplus S_0\) sees \(q\oplus(3I_r/4+P_f/4)\).
The finite-state component has nonnegative group coefficients with row sums one at every power and trivial closed-word displacements.
On the cyclic component the signed one-step obstruction disappears after squaring.
The geometric schedules are sharp as claimed since the cyclic time increment has the matching lower bound.
Here the disjoint components and the constant change of basis are part of the construction.
For a general fixed matrix on the same four-line group, finding a passing time and basis, or obtaining the endpoint bounds despite every such failure, remains open.
