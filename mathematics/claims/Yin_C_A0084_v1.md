# Certifying finite signed shells with kernel and cokernel witnesses

## Statement

Let \(A\in M_{r,m}(\mathbb CG)\) on a single finitely generated group with word length, \(Q=I-A^*A/M\), \(M>\|A\|^2\), and \(p=1_{\{1\}}(Q)\).
One can certify a finite temporal truncation for each spatial shell using only finite product tables, finite-dimensional null vectors from balls and known norm majorants.
The test uses neither a prior value for \(\tau_m(p)\) nor a previously proved return exponent or closed-range representative.
For an arbitrarily prescribed error tolerance, success is guaranteed when **either** Hilbert kernel, of \(A\) or of \(A^*\), is generated as a closed right group module by its finitely supported vectors.
In particular injectivity of either operator suffices.
The finite error certificate remains valid without any such density assumption.
Determining the finite data as an algorithm presupposes exact group-word comparisons and coefficient linear algebra, or adequate certified versions for the chosen input.

The certificate bounds the norm error of the finite first-return array on the entire group, so it has no shell-volume factor.
For \(\beta\ge0,\ \eta>0\) and a dyadic shell \(S_j=\{2^j\le|g|<2^{j+1}\}\), denote by \(H_j\) the norm on that shell of the finite signed array computed below, with coordinates at all matrix entries and both phases.
If each temporal truncation is certified to error \(2^{-(\beta/2+\eta)j}\), then for every \(J\ge0\),
\[
 \|\widehat p\|_{\beta,\ |g|\ge 2^J}
    \le C_\beta\left(\sum_{j\ge J}2^{\beta j}H_j^2\right)^{1/2}
          +C_{\beta,\eta}2^{-\eta J}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
Conversely the displayed norm of the finite arrays is at most a constant times the true exterior norm plus \(C_{\beta,\eta}2^{-\eta J}\).
Here \(\|\,\cdot\,\|_\beta^2\) uses weight \((1+|g|)^\beta\).
There is no asserted uniform time horizon across inputs or automatic summability of the measured \(H_j\)'s.
This separates verifying that a finite time is adequate from bounding spatial contributions over infinitely many shells.

## Finite lower bounds on the unknown trace

For \(B=A,A^*\) solve \(Bx=0\) on column vectors of group polynomials supported in the radius-\(s\) ball.
If \(w\) bounds the propagation of \(B\), one only needs its equations at endpoints in the radius-\(s+w\) ball.
Treat a complex vector-space basis of this finite nullspace as columns of a polynomial matrix \(W_s(B)\).
With \(b_s>\|W_s W_s^*\|\), obtainable from finite coefficient absolute sums, set
\[
       L_{s,k}(B)=I-(I-W_sW_s^*/b_s)^k,\qquad k\ge1 .
\]
This includes \(L_{s,k}=0\) for an empty basis.
The column convolutions take their values in \(\ker B\) by the exact product identities.
Consequently \(0\le L_{s,k}(B)\le p_B\), where \(p_B\) is the kernel projection of \(B\), and increasing \(k\) gives the projection onto the closed module generated by those columns as strong limit.
Indeed the vectors in the range of \(W_s\) on finitely supported inputs are precisely finite sums of their right translates with coefficients.
As \(s\) increases, the closed spans so obtained increase to the closed module generated by all finite null vectors.
Write \(d_B=\tau(p_B)\) and \(d_B^{\rm fin}\) for the dimension of that last module, both with the unnormalized trace of the appropriate matrix size.
Then \(\sup_{s,k}\tau(L_{s,k}(B))=d_B^{\rm fin}\) by normality.

Rectangular polar decomposition gives \(d_A-d_{A^*}=m-r\).
Choose independently two pairs of finite indices and set
\[
 \ell=\max\left(\tau_m L_{s,k}(A),\ m-r+\tau_r L_{s',k'}(A^*)\right)\le d_A .
\]
Every trace here is a finite identity-endpoint sum.
Thus
\[
 d_A-\sup\ell=\min(d_A-d_A^{\rm fin},\,d_{A^*}-d_{A^*}^{\rm fin}).      \tag{2}
\]
In particular the second term allows an input to be certified even if its positive-dimensional kernel contains no nonzero finitely supported vector.

## A fully finite error bound at all endpoints

For real \(0<z<1\), let \(R_z=(1-z)(I-zQ)^{-1}\).
For \(K\ge0\) compute the upper polynomial
\[
 U_{z,K}=(1-z)\sum_{l=0}^{K}z^l Q^l+z^{K+1}I .
\]
Since \(0\le Q\le I\), \(U_{z,K}\ge R_z\ge p\) and these three operators commute.
It follows that the nonnegative number
\[
              v=\tau_m(U_{z,K}^2)-\ell
\]
is an explicit upper bound for \(\|R_z-p\|_2^2=\tau_m(R_z^2)-d_A\).
The infimum of \(v\) over the finite choices and rational \(z\in(0,1)\) equals the right side of (2).
Indeed, for fixed \(z\), \(U_{z,K}\to R_z\) in norm, and \(R_z\to p\) in trace \(L^2\) as \(z\uparrow1\).
The lower trace estimates can be chosen independently.

For each endpoint \(g\ne e\), each input coordinate pair and either phase, form the [original padded first-return recursion](Yin_C_A0067_v1.md#statement) through time \(N\).
Write \(d=\sum_t t(f_t^--f_t^+)\) for the exact signed mean, \(b_n=\sum_{t=1}^n(f_t^+-f_t^-)\), and \(T_{z,N}=\sum_{n=0}^N z^n b_n\) for the finite array.
Over all these coordinates in unweighted \(\ell^2\), one has
\[
       \|T_{z,N}-d\|_2
       \ \le\ 32(m+1)\sqrt v+\sqrt{128m}\,\frac{z^{N+1}}{\sqrt{1-z^2}} . \tag{3}
\]
For the second term apply the [joint time and endpoint square bound](Yin_C_A0076_v1.md#the-joint-square-bound-across-time-and-endpoints) to the power-series tail.
For the first term compare \(\mathcal P(R_z)/(a_+(z)a_-(z))\) with \(\mathcal P(p)/(a_+(1)a_-(1))\) in that claim's notation.
Polarization has norm at most \(\sqrt2\) from trace \(L^2\), each padded denominator lies in \([1/2,1]\), and its change is at most \(\tau_m(R_z-p)\le\sqrt m\,\|R_z-p\|_2\).
The two partial derivatives of \(1/(ab)\) on this interval have absolute value at most eight, and \(\|p\|_2\le\sqrt m\).
These facts give (3) with the stated larger constant and require no rate of convergence of ordinary returns.

When (2) is zero, enumerate choices of the finite indices and rational \(z\), increasing \(N\) as necessary, to obtain a strict certificate (3) below any positive tolerance.
Conversely, if the right side of (2) is positive, this specific upper-bound procedure cannot reach arbitrarily small tolerances.
Once a choice has passed for a shell, only that shell's endpoint entries of \(T_{z,N}\) are needed for \(H_j\).
Different shells may use different certified times and parameters.
Multiplying their errors by the largest square-root weight on each shell and summing squares gives \(C_{\beta,\eta}2^{-\eta J}\) in norm for the exterior error.
The [pointwise comparison of signed means and Fourier coefficients](Yin_C_A0067_v1.md#statement) proves both assertions surrounding (1).

## Scope on matrices with nonclosed range

The procedure terminates at every precision on each dense-range matrix, including the [original-group cyclic-minor inputs](Yin_C_A0081_v1.md#statement) before doing any Laurent factorization.
For matrices with the [free-subgroup row-product property](Yin_C_A0082_v1.md#statement) the original shell estimates there already give an exponential bound for the exact signed array.
Whenever (3) passes at all the specified tolerances for such an input, its measured shell norms therefore obey \((\sum_{j\ge J}2^{\beta j}H_j^2)^{1/2}\le C(e^{-c2^J}+2^{-\eta J})\).
The [dimension-four lattice comparison](Yin_C_A0068_v1.md#statement) also has dense range and hence admits this finite precision certification even though no closed-range matrix over its reduced group algebra has the same kernel.
Its independently obtained exterior squared tail is \(O(R^{\beta-4})\) for \(\beta<4\); in that comparison group (1) then gives the corresponding decay of the measured array up to the selected error.
The extra commuting factors in that example are not asserted to occur inside a four-line group.

The condition in (2) can fail on the actual group despite good spatial behavior.
Take the [two-row matrix \(A_0\) with no finite null vectors](Yin_C_A0064_v1.md#the-row-map-and-its-kernel) on its fixed four-line free-pair event, and set \(A=A_0\oplus A_0^*\).
\(A_0\) has dense nonclosed range, nullity one and exponentially localized kernel projection, whereas \(A_0^*\) is injective.
For \(A\) and \(A^*\) both Hilbert nullities are one and both finite-vector dimensions vanish.
The infimum of \(v\) for this displayed full matrix is exactly one even though its projection has an exponential tail.
Recognizing its blocks separately supplies additional information. This example is a limit of the stated certificate, not an impossibility for other estimates based on the operator.
