# A normality criterion equivalent to the complex zero-divisor conjecture

## Statement

For every discrete group \(G\), every matrix size \(s\), and \(D,X\in M_s(\mathbb C[G])\), write \(\partial_D X=DX-XD\).
If \(\partial_D^2 X=0\), then \(\partial_D X\) is nilpotent in that same matrix algebra.
More generally, if the complex span of \(\partial_D^j X\), \(j\ge0\), is finite dimensional and \(\partial_D X\ne0\), the matrix algebra contains a nonzero nilpotent.

For each fixed torsion-free group \(G\), the following assertions are equivalent for the scalar group algebra:

1. \(\mathbb C[G]\) is a domain.
2. \(\mathbb C[G]\) has no nonzero nilpotent elements.
3. For every \(D,X\in\mathbb C[G]\), finite dimensionality of the span of \(\{\partial_D^j X:j\ge0\}\) implies \(DX=XD\).
4. For every \(D\in\mathbb C[G]\), \(\partial_D^3(D^*)=0\) implies \(DD^*=D^*D\).

Thus the exact [A0002 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_A0002_v1.md#contract) can be tested by a single finite-support element and its usual group-algebra adjoint.
The equivalent condition still quantifies over all such elements.
The nilpotence assertion for matrices applies also to groups with torsion; the domain equivalence concerns scalars and uses primeness.
There is a support test for condition 4.
For a real group character on the subgroup generated by the support of \(D\), a singleton highest layer forces its label to commute in the group algebra with the entire lowest layer whenever \(\partial_D^3(D^*)=0\).
If there are just two distinct layers and the highest is a singleton, any nonnormal solution then gives a nonnormal solution supported on the lowest layer alone.

## Argument

### A factorial bound over integral group rings

First work over \(M_s(\mathbb Z[G])\) with the operator norm of its left regular representation.
Put \(Y=\partial_D X\) and suppose \(\partial_D Y=0\).
The iterated Leibniz rule yields
\[
               m!\,Y^m=\partial_D^m(X^m).
\]
Indeed any surviving term differentiates each of the \(m\) factors exactly once, and all \(m!\) orders give the same ordered product.
Since \(\|\partial_D W\|\le2\|D\|\|W\|\), one has
\[
                    \|Y^m\|\le (2\|D\|\|X\|)^m/m! .
\]
Every nonzero integral group-ring matrix has norm at least one, since applying it to a suitable identity-coordinate basis vector detects a nonzero integer coefficient.
The upper bound falls below one for large \(m\), forcing \(Y^m=0\).
The norm inequality and factorial identity are the classical bounded-operator argument of [Kleinecke, formulas (1)–(2), pp.535–536](../literature/pdf/Kle57.pdf); see also [Shirokov, pp.167–168](../literature/pdf/Shi56.pdf).
The discreteness of the integral coefficients gives exact nilpotence here.

### Arbitrary complex coefficients

Let \(K\subset\mathbb C\) be the field generated over \(\mathbb Q\) by the finitely many coefficients of \(D,X\).
It is finite of degree \(d\) over \(k=\mathbb Q(t_1,\dots,t_l)\) for a finite transcendence basis, allowing \(l=0\).
Left multiplication of \(K\) on the \(k\)-vector space \(K\) defines an injective homomorphism \(K\longrightarrow M_d(k)\).
Extension coefficientwise, and then by matrix blocks, embeds the given matrices in \(M_{sd}(k[G])\).
No adjoint preservation is required in this step, since the identity \(\partial_D^2 X=0\) uses only multiplication and subtraction.

Choose a common nonzero central \(q\in\mathbb Z[t_1,\dots,t_l]\) clearing all denominators of the matrix entries of both images.
The scaled images \(\widehat D=q\iota(D)\), \(\widehat X=q\iota(X)\) belong to \(M_{sd}(\mathbb Z[t_1,\dots,t_l][G])\).
View the independent variables as the coordinate generators of \(\mathbb Z^l\), embedding this ring into \(M_{sd}(\mathbb Z[G\times\mathbb Z^l])\).
The double commutator of the scaled matrices is zero and \([\widehat D,\widehat X]=q^2\iota(Y)\).
Apply the integral proof in this larger regular representation.
The resulting vanishing of a power is an exact ring identity.
Returning to \(k[G]\) and cancelling the central nonzero \(q^{2m}\) proves \(Y^m=0\) for the original complex coefficients.

### A finite-dimensional commutator orbit

Let \(W\) be the finite-dimensional complex orbit span.
If \(\partial_D|_W\) has a nonzero eigenvalue \(\lambda\), choose \(0\ne V\in W\) with \(\partial_D V=\lambda V\).
Then \(\partial_D(V^m)=m\lambda V^m\).
For \(m|\lambda|>2\|D\|\), the commutator norm bound forces \(V^m=0\).
If every eigenvalue is zero, the endomorphism of \(W\) is nilpotent.
When \(\partial_D X\ne0\), take the last nonzero iterate \(Y=\partial_D^jX\), \(j\ge1\).
It is the commutator of \(D\) with \(\partial_D^{j-1}X\) and commutes with \(D\).
The preceding theorem makes \(Y\) a nonzero nilpotent.
This proves \(2\Rightarrow3\).

### From a single element back to a domain

If \(\partial_D^3(D^*)=0\), the orbit of \(D^*\) spans at most three dimensions.
Thus 3 gives normality, proving 4.
Conversely, if a ring contains a nonzero nilpotent, a suitable nonzero power \(V\) of it has \(V^2=0\).
Every term of \(\partial_V^3(V^*)\) contains \(V^2\) on one side of \(V^*\), so this triple commutator is zero.
A nonzero square-zero bounded operator is not normal, by the spectral theorem or the \(C^*\)-norm identity for normal elements.
Thus 4 implies 2.

Finally 1 implies 2 in any domain.
For the reverse implication, [Connell, Theorem 8, p.675](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1963-067-0) gives primeness of \(\mathbb C[G]\) since \(\mathbb C\) is prime and \(G\) has no nontrivial finite normal subgroup.
If nonzero \(\alpha,\beta\) satisfy \(\alpha\beta=0\), primeness implies that \(\beta\mathbb C[G]\alpha\ne0\).
Some group element \(g\) therefore gives \(0\ne V=\beta g\alpha\), and \(V^2=0\).
This proves 2 implies 1 in the same group.
For the universal assertion alone, there is also a direct construction: in \(G*\langle z\rangle\), free normal forms give \(0\ne\beta z\alpha\), and its square again vanishes.
This last construction preserves torsion-freeness without selecting \(g\) in the original group.

In particular, any noncommuting finite adjoint orbit of an actual integer residual \(D\) and correction \(F\) in the [fixed-lift test](Yin_C_A0039_v3.md#a-finite-adjoint-orbit-test-for-an-exact-annihilator) already yields a nonzero nilpotent by the argument above.
The modular test additionally identifies a right annihilator of its prescribed \(A\) with a prescribed nonzero reduction, and also covers the case in which \(D,F\) commute.

### Testing the extreme layers of one element

Write \(\chi:\langle\operatorname{supp}D\rangle\to\mathbb R\) for the character, and \(P,Q\) for the highest and lowest homogeneous parts of \(D\), with degrees \(h,l\).
In the triple commutator the component of degree \(3h-l\) is precisely \(\partial_P^3(Q^*)\), since the three unstarred factors can attain degree \(h\) only on the top layer and the starred one attains degree \(-l\) only on the bottom layer.
Suppose \(P=c u\) with \(u\) a single group label and \(c\ne0\).
Then \(\partial_P^3(Q^*)=c^3(\operatorname{Ad}u-I)^3(Q^*)u^3\).
On \(\ell^2(G)\), conjugation by \(u\) permutes the group basis unitarily.
By the spectral theorem its difference from the identity has the same kernel as its third power.
Consequently \(uQ^*u^{-1}=Q^*\) and taking adjoints also gives \(uQu^{-1}=Q\).
In particular conjugation must permute the bottom labels with their coefficients, and two singleton extrema must commute in the group.
If \(D=c u+Q\) has only these two layers, \(u,u^*\) commute with \(Q,Q^*\).
It follows that \([D,D^*]=[Q,Q^*]\) and \(\partial_D^3(D^*)=\partial_Q^3(Q^*)\).
Thus a counterexample of minimal support to condition 4 in a fixed group cannot have this two-layer form.
