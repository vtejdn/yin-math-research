# Integral elimination for three left labels in every characteristic

## Statement

Let \(G\) be torsion-free, let \(g_1,g_2,g_3\) be distinct, and fix a nonempty set \(B\subset G\) of size \(b<\infty\).
For a field \(k\), call a projective row \([a_1:a_2:a_3]\) admissible if left multiplication by \(\sum_i a_i g_i\) kills a nonzero vector in \(k[B]\).
The right vector may use a subset of \(B\).
All three coordinates of an admissible row are nonzero.

For each ordered ratio \(a_j/a_i\) there is an explicit monic integer polynomial of degree \(b^2\) with constant coefficient \(1\) that it satisfies, in every characteristic.
For any algebraically closed field there are at most \(b^2\) admissible projective rows on this table.
In characteristic zero all ratios of every such row are algebraic units and the joint field generated by its normalized coordinates has degree at most \(b^2\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
If the original right vector has support exactly \(B\), another vector with that exact support can be chosen over this same field without changing the row.
At every finite place one obtains a nonzero zero product after reduction, retaining all three left labels and a nonempty subset of \(B\) on the right.
Over an algebraic closure of a prime field of positive characteristic the normalized row field has degree at most \(b^2\) over the prime field as well.

For a complex admissible row, and \(b\ge2\), each embedding of its row field satisfies
\[
                    \max_{i,j}|\sigma(a_i/a_j)|
                          \le \csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{b+1}\right).
\]
Thus even with three-cell fibers the possible normalized left coefficients for a prescribed \(b\) lie in an effectively bounded finite set independent of the group.
For purely paired fibers the [phase theorem](Yin_C_A0099_v1.md#statement) gives the stronger root-of-unity conclusion.

## Argument

### Three square submatrices

Put \(A=\{g_1,g_2,g_3\}\).
In the \(|AB|\)-by-\(b\) matrix \(M(X)\), the entry at \((w,h)\) is \(X_i\) if \(w=g_i h\), and zero if there is no such index.
For each \(i\) take the \(b\) rows \(g_i h\) in the same order used for the columns \(h\in B\), and write \(D_i(X)\) for their determinant.
These homogeneous integral polynomials of degree \(b\) vanish on every admissible row by the rank condition.
For \(i\ne j\), setting the third coordinate to zero gives
\[
                D_i(X_i,X_j)=X_i^b .
\]
Indeed the submatrix is \(X_i I+X_j N\), where \(N\) is the partial translation by \(g_i^{-1}g_j\) restricted to \(B\).
Its finite graph consists of paths because the translating element has infinite order.
Ordering those paths makes \(N\) strictly triangular.
This also proves the asserted absence of a zero coordinate in an admissible row over every field.
At the point with just \(X_j\) nonzero, \(D_i=0\) for \(i\ne j\): a nonzero determinant of the restricted permutation would require \(g_iB=g_jB\), impossible for a finite nonempty set under a nontrivial torsion-free translation.

### Computing a monic eliminant and counting the rows

Keep \(X_1=u,X_2=v\) and treat \(X_3=T\) as an indeterminate.
The polynomial \(D_3(u,v,T)\) is monic of degree \(b\) in \(T\), so the quotient by it is a free \(\mathbb Z[u,v]\)-module with basis \(1,T,\ldots,T^{b-1}\).
Define \(R(u,v)\) as the determinant of multiplication by \(D_1+D_2\) on this module, using the [finite free norm](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BD2).
Division by the monic polynomial computes all the entries by integer polynomial arithmetic.
The result is homogeneous of degree \(b^2\): the entry in row \(i\), column \(j\), in the basis of powers numbered from zero, has degree \(b+j-i\) in \(u,v\), because the reduction preserves total homogeneous degree.
Every nonzero product in the determinant has total degree \(b^2\).

At \(v=0\) the defining relation is \(T^b=0\).
On this truncated polynomial module multiplication is triangular with constant diagonal \((D_1+D_2)(u,0,0)=u^b\).
At \(u=0\) the diagonal is \(v^b\) for the same reason.
Hence
\[
             R(u,0)=u^{b^2},\qquad R(0,v)=v^{b^2}.
\]
If \([1:x:y]\) is admissible, evaluation at \(T=y\) on the specialized quotient is a nonzero linear functional that kills every multiple of \(D_1+D_2\).
Thus \(R(1,x)=0\), and \(R(1,X)\) is the claimed monic polynomial with constant term one.
Permuting the three labels gives each asserted ratio.
This construction uses the actual rows of the multiplication table and imposes no restriction on how often double or triple outputs occur.

Over an algebraically closed field, fix \(x_0\) and let \(q\) be the number of distinct \(y\) giving admissible \([1:x_0:y]\).
These \(y\)'s are among the roots of the monic degree-\(b\) polynomial \(D_3(1,x_0,T)\).
Their evaluation functionals on its quotient are linearly independent by polynomial interpolation.
They kill the image of the norm matrix before taking its determinant, so its rank at \(x_0\) is at most \(b-q\).
After a constant invertible row change at least \(q\) rows of the polynomial matrix vanish at \(x_0\).
Its determinant \(R(1,x)\) is therefore divisible by \((x-x_0)^q\).
Summing over \(x_0\) gives the joint bound \(b^2\), including when a fiber has multiple distinct right kernels or the eliminant has repeated roots.
The three determinant conditions used here are necessary.
For deciding admissibility of a candidate one still tests the rank of the full matrix \(M(1,x,y)\).
For instance in the infinite cyclic group take the row labels \(z^{-1},z,1\) and \(B=(1,z)\).
The three determinants are \(u^2,v^2,T^2-uv\), so \(R(u,v)=(u^2+v^2)^2\).
The nonzero choices \(x=i,\ y^2=i\) satisfy \(R(1,x)=D_3(1,x,y)=0\); the first minor is one and excludes them.

### Arithmetic and archimedean consequences

In characteristic zero a root of a monic integral polynomial with constant coefficient one is an algebraic integer with integral inverse, by reversing the polynomial.
Write \(K=\mathbb Q(a_2/a_1,a_3/a_1)\).
The rank condition on the full matrix is expressed by integral minors and is preserved by every embedding of \(K\).
The distinct embeddings give distinct normalized rows, so \([K:\mathbb Q]\le b^2\).
The same proof uses separability of finite fields in positive characteristic.
Over \(K\) a kernel basis is obtained by ordinary row reduction and spans the kernel after any field extension.
If there was a witness with every coordinate nonzero, none of the coordinate hyperplanes contains this \(K\)-kernel.
The infinite field \(K\) admits a vector outside their finite union.
For reduction at any chosen finite place, first normalize the left row by its first coefficient.
All its coordinates are local units, and division of the right vector by a coordinate of least valuation makes all its entries integral at that place with at least one unit.
Reduction therefore gives the stated zero product on the same group.

For the archimedean bound use any one embedding and a nonzero right kernel vector, deleting zero coordinates if needed.
Write \(M_0\ge r\ge m>0\) for the three moduli of the normalized row.
The two largest labels and the remaining label obey the [finite-chain estimate](Yin_C_A0099_v1.md#the-finite-chain-estimate), with longest chain at most \(b\).
Putting \(c=\cos(\pi/(b+1))\) gives
\[
 m^2\ \ge\ M_0^2+r^2-2cM_0 r
             =(r-cM_0)^2+(1-c^2)M_0^2 .
\]
This proves the claimed ratio bound at every embedding.
For \(b=1\) no nonzero right annihilator can exist.
For fixed \(b\ge2\), a minimal polynomial for a ratio has degree \(d\le b^2\), and its coefficient of degree \(d-j\) has absolute value at most \(\binom dj\csc(\pi/(b+1))^j\) by the elementary symmetric formulas.
Finite enumeration of these integer polynomials and their roots gives the stated group-independent set.
Rational left coefficients in particular normalize to signs, consistent with [Abdollahi and Taheri, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.09494v1#page=2).
Neither the finite candidate set nor the degree bound decides whether any of its rows occurs in a zero product on some torsion-free group with arbitrary \(b\).
