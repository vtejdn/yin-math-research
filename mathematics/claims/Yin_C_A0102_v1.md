# Noncommuting tracial models with an exact first failing rational power

This construction tests the role of the canonical group trace in the coefficient-transfer statements [C96](Yin_C_A0096_v2.md#statement), [C97](Yin_C_A0097_v2.md#statement) and [C100](Yin_C_A0100_v1.md#statement).
It uses noncommuting quaternion coefficients and a single number-field generator.
All ranks below are ranks in the explicitly specified tracial representations, not regular ranks for the canonical trace of the free group.
No counterexample to either Kaplansky or Atiyah is asserted.

## Statement

For every integer \(j\ge1\), there are a unitary representation \(\pi\) of the free group \(F_2=\langle x,y\rangle\) in a finite von Neumann algebra \((\mathcal M,\tau)\), with faithful normalized trace, and a number field \(K\subset\mathbb C\) of degree \(2^j\), with the following properties.

1. For every \(1\le n\le j\), every finite rectangular matrix over \(\mathbb Q[F_2^n]\), evaluated through \(\pi^{\otimes n}\), has integral tracial rank for the unnormalized matrix amplification of \(\tau^{\otimes n}\).
2. A nonzero scalar element \(P\in K[F_2]\) has tracial rank \(1/2\) under every complex embedding of its coefficient field.
   The field is generated by one explicit primitive element \(\alpha\), so \(P\) is the evaluation at \(\alpha\) of a polynomial in one central scalar parameter with coefficients in \(\mathbb Q[F_2]\).
3. There is a \(2^j\)-square rational group-ring matrix on \(F_2^{j+1}\) whose tracial rank is \(1/2\).
   Thus \(j+1\) is the first failing rational power for this model.
4. Let \(D\) be the actual division closure of \(\pi(\mathbb Q[F_2])\) in \(U(\mathcal M)\).
   There is
   \[
   C\in M_{2^{j-1}}(D)
   \]
   for which each of the \(2^j\) conjugates of \(\alpha\) has kernel dimension \(1/2\) for \(C-\sigma(\alpha)I\).
   In particular \(j=2\) gives a two-square rational affiliated matrix and one quartic generator, despite division-ring control in each of the first two independent copies.
5. For actual nonidentity elements \(g,h\) of a torsion-free group, the affiliated Cayley transforms
   \[
   (g-1)(g+1)^{-1},\qquad (h-1)(h+1)^{-1}
   \]
   in the regular representation cannot anticommute.
   This last assertion uses the actual group labels and concerns individual group elements, not arbitrary pairs of rational affiliated elements.

The pulled-back trace on the group algebra is not canonical: \(\tau(\pi(x))<0\).
The representation also annihilates an explicit nonzero rational group-ring element exhibited below.
Faithfulness of the trace on \(\mathcal M\) consequently does not give a faithful group-algebra or full group \(C^*\)-algebra representation.
The representation is not claimed to factor through the reduced group \(C^*\)-algebra.
The model therefore isolates a limitation of tracial and tensor-rank arguments without deciding the corresponding assertion for the regular representation.

## Argument

### Quaternion algebras with independent variable slots

Choose distinct positive primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_j\), with \(p_1=5\); when \(j\ge2\), take \(p_2=2\).
Put
\[
 c_1=-p_1=-5,\qquad c_i=p_i\ (i>1),\qquad
 g(t)=\prod_{i=1}^j(c_i-t).
 \tag{1}
\]
These rational square classes are independent.
Indeed a product over a nonempty subset has an odd valuation at each prime appearing in that subset and therefore cannot be a rational square.
Let
\[
 K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{c_1},\ldots,\sqrt{c_j}),\qquad [K:\mathbb Q]=2^j.
 \tag{2}
\]
We use the usual multiquadratic basis consisting of the products of distinct square roots.
Its automorphisms are the independent sign changes.
For \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j\}\), write \(k_I=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{c_i}:i\in I)\).

For a central variable \(t\), let \(Q(t)\) be the four-dimensional quaternion algebra over \(\mathbb Q(t)\), with basis \(1,u,v,uv\) and relations
\[
 u^2=t,\qquad v^2=g(t),\qquad uv=-vu.
 \tag{3}
\]
For algebraically independent \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\), form the tensor product
\[
 \mathcal A_{n,I}
 =\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^n(t_\ell,g(t_\ell))_{\,k_I(t_1,\ldots,t_n)}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The factors in this formula have their coefficients extended to the common central field indicated.
We claim that \(\mathcal A_{n,I}\) is a division algebra whenever
\[
 n+|I|\le j.
 \tag{5}
\]

The proof is by induction on \(n\), simultaneously for all such \(I\).
For \(n=0\), it is the field \(k_I\).
For the next step choose \(h\notin I\), and set
\[
 F_0=k_I(t_1,\ldots,t_{n-1}),\qquad
 B=\mathcal A_{n-1,I},\qquad
 A=\mathcal A_{n-1,I\cup\{h\}}.
\]
Both prior tensor products are division algebras by induction.
The algebra \(A\) is the constant quadratic extension of \(B\), and as a \(B\)-vector space
\[
 A=B\oplus B\sqrt{c_h}.
 \tag{6}
\]
There is an order-two automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(A\) fixing \(B\) and sending \(\sqrt{c_h}\) to \(-\sqrt{c_h}\).
Independence of the square classes ensures that the constant extension has degree two.

Consider the skew Laurent-series division ring \(A((W;\sigma))\), with
\[
 Wa=\sigma(a)W\quad(a\in A).
 \tag{7}
\]
Every nonzero series is invertible: factor its first nonzero monomial and invert the remaining \(1+\) positive-order series by the formal geometric series.
The variable \(W^2\) is central.
Expand at \(t_n=c_h\), writing \(z=t_n-c_h\), and put
\[
 c=-\prod_{i\ne h}(c_i-c_h)\ne0.
 \]
There are scalar formal power series \(d(z),e(z)\in\mathbb Q[[z]]\), each with constant term one, for which
\[
 c_h+z=c_h d(z)^2,\qquad
 g(c_h+z)=c z e(z)^2.
 \tag{8}
\]
They are the binomial square roots of \(1+z/c_h\) and
\(\prod_{i\ne h}(1-z/(c_i-c_h))\), respectively.
Define the image of the last factor by
\[
 z=\frac{W^2}{c},\qquad
 u_n=d(z)\sqrt{c_h},\qquad v_n=e(z)W.
 \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) give \(u_n^2=t_n\), \(v_n^2=g(t_n)\), and \(u_nv_n=-v_nu_n\).
All earlier quaternion generators commute with these two images.
This defines a homomorphism from the tensor algebra (4) into \(A((W;\sigma))\).
The central rational function field embeds by the Laurent expansion at \(t_n=c_h\).

Here injectivity can be checked directly.
Write an element using the basis of the last quaternion factor as
\[
 x_0+x_1u_n+x_2v_n+x_3u_nv_n,
 \tag{10}
\]
where the \(x_i\) have coefficients in \(B\) and central rational dependence on \(t_n\).
Their expansions belong to \(B((z))\).
Its image has even and odd \(W\)-parts
\[
 \bigl(x_0+x_1d(z)\sqrt{c_h}\bigr)
 \;+\;
 \bigl(x_2e(z)+x_3d(z)e(z)\sqrt{c_h}\bigr)W.
 \tag{11}
\]
If it vanishes, the even and odd parts vanish separately.
Equation (6), applied coefficientwise to the Laurent series, then makes all four \(x_i\) zero, since \(d,e\) are units.
Thus (4) embeds in a division ring.
It is finite-dimensional over its central field, so absence of zero divisors makes it a division algebra: multiplication by a nonzero element is an injective endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space and is consequently surjective.
This completes the induction and proves (5).

### A positive tracial realization

Let \(J=[-3,-2]\), equipped with normalized Lebesgue measure.
For \(t\in J\), both \(t\) and \(g(t)\) are strictly negative.
Define
\[
 u(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}i\sqrt{-t}&0\\0&-i\sqrt{-t}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 v(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{-g(t)}\\-\sqrt{-g(t)}&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{12}
\]
These are continuous skew-adjoint matrix functions satisfying (3), and \(uv=-vu\ne0\).
Take
\[
 \mathcal M=M_2(L^\infty(J)),\qquad
 \tau(X)=\int_J\frac12\operatorname{Tr}_2(X(t))\,dt.
 \tag{13}
\]
The trace is faithful, positive and normalized.
Rational functions in \(t\) are defined almost everywhere: a nonzero rational denominator has only finitely many zeros on \(J\).
The realization (12) consequently embeds \(Q(t)\) into \(U(\mathcal M)\).
Indeed \(Q(t)\) is a division algebra by (5), and the rational coefficients of the algebraic inverse of a nonzero element evaluate almost everywhere and give its actual affiliated inverse.

For \(n\) independent samples, use the product measure on \(J^n\) and
\[
 \mathcal M^{\overline\otimes n}
 =M_{2^n}(L^\infty(J^n)),\qquad
 \tau^{\otimes n}=\int_{J^n}2^{-n}\operatorname{Tr}_{2^n}.
 \tag{14}
\]
When \(n\le j\), (5) embeds \(\mathcal A_{n,\varnothing}\) as a division algebra of actual affiliated operators.
The same inverse argument applies because a nonzero rational function of the independent real variables has a Lebesgue-null zero set.
Gaussian operations over that division algebra show that every finite matrix with entries in it has integer tracial rank.
Equivalently, after evaluation its ordinary fiber rank is \(2^n\) times its division-algebra rank almost everywhere, and the normalization in (14) cancels that factor.

Now put
\[
 U=(1+u)(1-u)^{-1},\qquad V=(1+v)(1-v)^{-1}.
 \tag{15}
\]
These bounded continuous functions are unitaries.
Define the representation of \(F_2\) by \(\pi(x)=U,\ \pi(y)=V\).
They are noncommuting: commutation of \(U,V\) would, by the inverse Cayley formulas, imply commutation of \(u,v\).
Their rational group algebra lies in \(Q(t)\).
Conversely
\[
 u=(U-1)(U+1)^{-1},\qquad
 v=(V-1)(V+1)^{-1},\qquad t=u^2
 \tag{16}
\]
puts \(Q(t)\) in its actual division closure.
The matrices \(1+U,1+V\) have bounded inverses, by (15) and compactness of \(J\).
Thus the division closure denoted by \(D\) in the statement is exactly this \(Q(t)\).

Every rational group-ring matrix on \(F_2^n\), represented through \(\pi^{\otimes n}\), has entries in the division algebra from (14) when \(n\le j\).
This proves assertion 1, for all finite rectangular matrix sizes.
Faithful group-algebra realization is not a premise of this argument.

For clarity, the generated image \(C^*\)-algebra is \(M_2(C(J))\).
The inverse Cayley formulas supply \(u,v\), and \(u^2=tI\) supplies all scalar continuous functions of \(t\).
Dividing \(u,v\) by their nonvanishing continuous scalar factors in (12) supplies two constant noncommuting Pauli matrices, which generate \(M_2(\mathbb C)\).
The trace is faithful on this image algebra.
On the other hand,
\[
 \tau(U)=\int_J\frac{1+t}{1-t}\,dt<0.
 \tag{17}
\]
The canonical group trace of the generator \(x\) is zero, so the pulled-back trace is explicitly noncanonical.
There is also an explicit algebraic kernel.
Since \(u^2=tI\), the inverse Cayley identity gives
\[
 U+U^{-1}=2\frac{1+t}{1-t}I,
\]
which commutes with \(V\).
Thus \(\pi\) annihilates
\[
 yxy^{-1}+yx^{-1}y^{-1}-x-x^{-1}\in\mathbb Q[F_2].
\]
Its four free-group words are distinct, so this is a nonzero element of the abstract group algebra.

### A finite scalar group-ring element of rank one half

In the scalar extension by \(K\), expand
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^j(\sqrt{c_i}+u)=a(t)+b(t)u,\qquad a,b\in K[t].
 \tag{18}
\]
Since \(u^2=t\) and all the square roots are central,
\[
 a(t)^2-tb(t)^2
 =\prod_{i=1}^j(\sqrt{c_i}+u)(\sqrt{c_i}-u)
 =g(t).
 \tag{19}
\]
This is an algebraic identity; it does not identify \(a-bu\) with the Hilbert-space adjoint of \(a+bu\).
Let
\[
 Z=v-a(t)-b(t)u.
 \tag{20}
\]
In the two-dimensional complex realization its determinant is
\[
 \det Z=a(t)^2-tb(t)^2-g(t)=0.
 \tag{21}
\]
The element \(Z(t)\) is nonzero for every \(t\in J\).
Indeed the four matrices \(1,u(t),v(t),u(t)v(t)\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb C\), and the coefficient of \(v(t)\) in (20) is one.
Thus \(Z(t)\) has ordinary rank exactly one.
Every complex embedding of \(K\) merely changes signs of the square roots in (18); (19)--(21) and nonvanishing hold unchanged.
All those evaluated elements have normalized tracial rank \(1/2\).

To obtain a finite group-ring element, clear the Cayley denominators in their actual order:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P={}&(1+x)^j(y-1)\\
 &-\left(\prod_{i=1}^j
     \bigl((\sqrt{c_i}+1)x+(\sqrt{c_i}-1)\bigr)\right)(1+y)
 \quad\in K[F_2].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22}
\]
The displayed product is a polynomial in the single group generator \(x\), so its factors commute with one another.
The rightmost factor \(1+y\) retains the displayed position.
Direct substitution gives
\[
 \pi(P)=(1+U)^j\,Z\,(1+V).
 \tag{23}
\]
Both outer factors are boundedly invertible.
Hence \(\pi(\sigma P)\) has rank \(1/2\) for every embedding \(\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\).
In particular \(P\) is a nonzero element of the abstract group ring.

One primitive element suffices.
Take
\[
 \alpha=\sum_{i=1}^j\sqrt{c_i}.
 \tag{24}
\]
The \(2^j\) independent sign choices give distinct conjugates, by linear independence of the corresponding multiquadratic basis vectors.
Thus \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Expressing every coefficient of \(P\) in the basis
\(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{2^j-1}\) gives a polynomial
\(\mathcal P(T)\in\mathbb Q[F_2][T]\), with central \(T\) and degree less than \(2^j\), such that \(\mathcal P(\alpha)=P\).
This proves assertion 2 using one algebraic scalar parameter, rather than many independent transcendental parameters.

### The first failing rational power

Let \(d=2^j\), and use the regular representation of \(K\) on a rational basis to restrict scalars.
For any \(n\ge1\), take the exterior tensor
\[
 P^{\otimes n}\in K[F_2^n]
\]
and apply restriction of scalars to obtain a \(d\)-square matrix
\[
 R_n\in M_d(\mathbb Q[F_2^n]).
 \tag{25}
\]
Over \(\mathbb C\), a constant invertible change of basis decomposes it into the \(d\) blocks \((\sigma P)^{\otimes n}\).
The trace convention in (14), with unnormalized amplification by \(M_d\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_{\tau^{\otimes n}}(\pi^{\otimes n}(R_n))
 =\sum_{\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
       \operatorname{rk}_{\tau}(\pi(\sigma P))^n
 =2^j\,2^{-n}.
 \tag{26}
\]
One can see the product formula directly in the finite matrix fibers: each conjugate of the entire product (23) has ordinary fiber rank one in dimension two.
A tensor of \(n\) such fibers has rank one in dimension \(2^n\).
Constant invertible changes of basis preserve the tracial ranks.

At \(n=j+1\), (26) is \(1/2\).
Together with assertion 1, this proves the exact first-failure assertion.
In particular the strict threshold at coefficient degree \(2^j\) cannot be replaced by a non-strict one in a coefficient-transfer statement based only on these tracial-model hypotheses.
This is a limitation within the model class, not an optimality assertion for the canonical group trace.

### One number-field generator inside a matrix affiliated algebra

The same construction realizes the fractional eigenvalue masses inside a single matrix over \(D\).
Let \(\widehat P=R_1\in M_d(D)\), and let \(T_\alpha\in M_d(\mathbb Q)\) be multiplication by \(\alpha\) in the chosen rational basis of \(K\).
Each coefficient matrix in the restriction of scalars commutes with \(T_\alpha\), so
\[
 T_\alpha\widehat P=\widehat P T_\alpha.
 \tag{27}
\]
Equation (26) at \(n=1\) gives rank \(d/2\) and nullity \(d/2\).
The actual rank over the division algebra \(D\) has the same value.
Choose a right \(D\)-basis of this kernel, arranged as a matrix
\[
 W\in M_{d,d/2}(D),\qquad LW=I_{d/2}
 \tag{28}
\]
for some left inverse \(L\) over \(D\).
Invariance under \(T_\alpha\) gives a matrix
\[
 C=L T_\alpha W\in M_{d/2}(D),\qquad
 T_\alpha W=WC.
 \tag{29}
\]
The minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(\mathbb Q\) annihilates \(C\).

At almost every \(t\), the constant decomposition by embeddings of \(K\) identifies
\(\ker\widehat P(t)\) with the direct sum of the ordinary one-dimensional kernels of \(\pi(\sigma P)(t)\).
On its \(\sigma\)-summand, \(T_\alpha\) acts by \(\sigma(\alpha)\).
The matrix \(W(t)\) identifies this space with the full fiber space of \(C(t)\).
Every conjugate \(\sigma(\alpha)\) therefore has ordinary eigenspace dimension one in a fiber of dimension \(d\).
The \(M_{d/2}(\mathcal M)\) trace is \(1/2\) times that ordinary fiber trace, so
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{d/2}\otimes\tau
 \bigl(P_{\ker(C-\sigma(\alpha)I)}\bigr)=\frac12
 \quad\text{for every }\sigma.
 \tag{30}
\]
The rational functions arising in (28)--(29) define affiliated operators; boundedness of these basis changes is unnecessary for the fiberwise kernel calculation.

For \(j=2\), one can take \(\alpha=i\sqrt5+\sqrt2\), with minimal polynomial
\[
 T^4+6T^2+49.
 \tag{31}
\]
Then \(C\in M_2(D)\) has the four conjugate eigenvalues
\(\pm i\sqrt5\pm\sqrt2\), each with unnormalized kernel dimension \(1/2\).
The pencil \(C-TI_2\) is affine in the single number-field parameter.
The coefficient division algebra and its independent tensor square are both division algebras.
This gives an actual noncommuting tracial realization of the fractional-mass pattern that a companion or Sylvester argument must distinguish, while retaining the noncanonical-trace qualification.

### Exclusion for Cayley transforms of actual group elements

Let \(g,h\ne1\) belong to a torsion-free group, represented regularly, and set
\[
 u=(g-1)(g+1)^{-1},\qquad
 v=(h-1)(h+1)^{-1}.
 \tag{32}
\]
The [cyclic affiliated calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#cyclic-rational-functions) makes \(g\pm1\), \(h\pm1\), and hence \(u,v\), affiliated units.
It uses the Haar spectral law of an infinite-order group element.
Suppose \(uv=-vu\).
Then \(v\) commutes with \(u^2\), so \(h=(1+v)(1-v)^{-1}\) also commutes with \(u^2\).
Writing \(S=g+g^{-1}\), the cyclic identities are
\[
 u^2=(S-2)(S+2)^{-1},\qquad
 S=2(1+u^2)(1-u^2)^{-1}.
 \tag{33}
\]
All displayed inverses exist; in particular \(1-u^2=4(S+2)^{-1}\).
It follows that \(hSh^{-1}=S\).
Linear independence of the actual group basis, and \(g\ne g^{-1}\), imply
\[
 hgh^{-1}=g\quad\hbox{or}\quad hgh^{-1}=g^{-1}.
\]
In the first case the Cayley transforms commute, contradicting anticommutation because \(u,v\) are units over characteristic zero.

In the second case \(huh^{-1}=-u\), and therefore \(uhu^{-1}=-h\).
Conjugating the rational expression for \(v\) gives
\[
 uvu^{-1}=(-h-1)(-h+1)^{-1}=(h+1)(h-1)^{-1}=v^{-1}.
\]
Anticommutation instead gives \(uvu^{-1}=-v\).
Hence \(v^2=-1\).
Multiplying this last identity by \((h+1)^2\) gives \(2(h^2+1)=0\), impossible by the group-basis independence.
This proves assertion 5.
The argument explains a specific failure of the quaternion model to preserve actual group labeling.
It does not exclude anticommuting rational affiliated pairs that are not Cayley transforms of individual group elements.

### Scope relative to the existing coefficient-transfer arguments

The coefficients \(u,v\) in (18)--(20) do not commute.
They do not lie in a common abelian affiliated algebra, so the joint scalar-sample hypothesis of [C100](Yin_C_A0100_v1.md#common-roots-for-two-independent-samples) is absent.
In a matrix fiber, deterministic root multiplicities can produce normalized masses \(1/2\).
The one-dimensional scalar-sample argument does not turn those multiplicities into integer unnormalized module dimensions.

Likewise the [JZ19 source interface](../audits/literature/JZ19/Yin_U001.md#hilbert-schmidt-mechanism) does not supply a degree-independent conclusion from rational integrality on two coordinates alone.
Its transcendental exclusion combines the centralizer lower bound with an arithmetic strict-eigenvalue theorem and convergence from the stated sofic approximations.
Its [coefficient induction](../audits/literature/JZ19/Yin_U001.md#base-change-proof) retains those approximation and coefficient-field hypotheses.
The representation here has the noncanonical moment (17) and is not such a sofic approximation to the regular trace.
The construction therefore neither contradicts JZ19 nor settles whether additional information available for a canonical group representation forces the desired matrix coefficient transfer.
