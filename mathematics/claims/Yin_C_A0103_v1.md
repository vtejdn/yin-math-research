# Degree, grading and arithmetic bounds for minimal zero-product supports

## Statement

Let \(G\) be a group, let \(k_0=\mathbb Q\) or \(\mathbb F_p\), and let \(\bar k_0\) be an algebraic closure.
Let \(A=\{g_1,\ldots,g_a\}\) and \(B=\{h_1,\ldots,h_b\}\) be finite sets of distinct group elements.
Assume **geometric inclusion-minimality**: a nonzero zero product exists on subsets of \(A,B\) over \(\bar k_0\), but none exists on \(A',B'\) if \(A'\subseteq A,\ B'\subseteq B\) are nonempty and at least one inclusion is strict.
Thus every nonzero zero product on these allowed supports uses every label.
This assumption concerns all coefficient choices, not only shrinking one factor while fixing the coefficients of the other.
For complex coefficients, the existence and boundary-emptiness conditions are equivalent to those over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), since their equations are defined over \(\mathbb Q\).
Minimality only among rational points over a finite field is not sufficient for the positive-characteristic statement.
One has \(a,b\ge2\).
Put
\[
                   d=a+b-2,\qquad
                   N=\binom{a+b-2}{a-1}.
\]

For each actual product \(w\in AB\), set
\[
                         F_w(X,Y)=\sum_{g_i h_j=w}X_iY_j.
\]
Let \(Z\) be their scheme-theoretic zero locus in
\(\mathbb P^{a-1}_{k_0}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}_{k_0}\).
The following bounds retain its scheme structure.

1. The scheme \(Z\) is nonempty, finite, and contained in the coefficient torus.
   Its length \(\ell\) satisfies
   \[
                             1\le\ell\le N.                  \tag{1}
   \]
   At every geometric point, each factor is the unique projective annihilator of the other on its allowed support.
   In particular each of the two projective projections is injective on geometric points.

2. Normalize \(X_1=Y_1=1\).
   In the free abelian group \(M=\mathbb Z^{a+b-2}\), write \(\epsilon_1=\eta_1=0\), with \(\epsilon_i\), \(i>1\), and \(\eta_j\), \(j>1\), its respective row and column bases.
   Define the normalized table grading
   \[
    \Gamma=M/
       \left\langle
       \epsilon_i+\eta_j-\epsilon_{i'}-\eta_{j'}:
                        g_i h_j=g_{i'}h_{j'}\right\rangle.
                                                                    \tag{2}
   \]
   This finite abelian group is effectively computed by integer Smith normal form.
   If \(q=|\Gamma|\) and \(R\) is the finite normalized coefficient algebra of \(Z\), then
   \[
                      \ell=\dim_{k_0}R
                            =q\,\dim_{k_0}R_0,\qquad q\le N.
                                                                    \tag{3}
   \]
   Here \(R_0\) is its degree-zero subalgebra.
   Both the row degrees alone and the column degrees alone generate \(\Gamma\).
   Thus \(\Gamma\) has a generating set of size at most \(\min(a-1,b-1)\).
   The full order \(q\), including its \(p\)-part in characteristic \(p\), occurs in (3).

3. Group translation gives supports
   \[
        A_0=g_1^{-1}A,\qquad B_0=Bh_1^{-1},
             \qquad 1\in A_0\cap B_0.
   \]
   They lie in the common subgroup
   \[
                  H=\langle A_0\rangle=\langle B_0\rangle.
                                                                    \tag{4}
   \]
   The abelianization \(H_{\mathrm{ab}}\) is a quotient of \(\Gamma\).
   Consequently
   \[
               |H_{\mathrm{ab}}|\mid q,\qquad
               |H_{\mathrm{ab}}|\le N.
                                                                    \tag{5}
   \]
   These conclusions hold in each indicated characteristic under its geometric minimality assumption.
   For torsion-free \(G\), the subgroup \(H\) is torsion-free as well.

4. For a normalized geometric coefficient point let \(K\) be the field over \(k_0\) generated by all its coordinates.
   It is finite, and is already generated by either factor's normalized coordinates.
   Evaluate \(R_0\) at this point and denote its image field by \(F\subseteq K\).
   Then
   \[
                       q[F:k_0]\le\ell\le N.                 \tag{6}
   \]
   In characteristic zero,
   \[
                           [K:F]\le q,\qquad [K:\mathbb Q]\le N.
                                                                    \tag{7}
   \]
   In characteristic \(p\), write \(q=q_p q'\), where \(q_p\) is a power of \(p\) and \(p\nmid q'\).
   Then
   \[
                  [K:F]\le q',\qquad
                  [K:\mathbb F_p]\le N/q_p.                  \tag{8}
   \]
   If \(e\) is the exponent of \(\Gamma\), put \(e'=e\) in characteristic zero and remove its \(p\)-part in characteristic \(p\).
   After adjoining \(\mu_{e'}\), the extension
   \[
                         K F(\mu_{e'})/F(\mu_{e'})
   \]
   is finite Galois and abelian, with Galois group injecting into the character group of \(\Gamma\) in that characteristic.
   Its degree therefore divides \(q\) in characteristic zero and \(q'\) in characteristic \(p\).
   The unextended degree \([K:F]\) is only bounded above here, not asserted to divide those integers.

5. In characteristic zero, the character group of \(\Gamma\) acts freely on the finite geometric coefficient set.
   Its number of points is a multiple of \(q\) and at most \(N\).
   In characteristic \(p\), the number of geometric points is a multiple of \(q'\) and at most \(N/q_p\).
   Scheme length and number of geometric points need not coincide.

The field \(F\) has an explicit description: it is generated by the values of all degree-zero Laurent monomials in the normalized coefficient coordinates.
A finite integer basis of the kernel \(M\to\Gamma\) supplies a finite set of monomial generators.
The analogous construction for the quotient grading by \(H_{\mathrm{ab}}\) gives the same bounds with \(|H_{\mathrm{ab}}|\) in place of \(q\), and describes the quotient by actual group-character twists.

The assumptions are exact coefficient minimality and an actual multiplication table.
The finite grading and coefficient tests do not certify that an abstract partition is realizable in a group, much less a torsion-free group.
No conclusion that the original zero-divisor problem is solved is asserted.

## The finite Segre section and its length

A nonzero zero product gives a projective point, so \(Z\) is nonempty.
Geometric minimality says that every geometric point has all row and column coordinates nonzero.
If a positive-dimensional projective component existed, a hyperplane in the Segre embedding defined by \(X_iY_j=0\) would meet it.
It cannot contain that component because \(X_iY_j\) is nonzero at every point of \(Z\).
The intersection would be a zero product with a missing row or column, a contradiction.
Thus \(Z\) is zero dimensional.
It is projective and hence finite over \(k_0\).
Since its entire geometric support lies in the coefficient torus, it is contained in that open subscheme, including its possible nilpotent structure.

The [Segre embedding](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01WD) is given by all the products \(X_iY_j\).
Every \(F_w\) is a linear form in these coordinates.
The Hilbert polynomial of the Segre variety \(X=\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}\), with respect to this embedding, is
\[
             P_X(n)=\binom{n+a-1}{a-1}\binom{n+b-1}{b-1}
             =\frac{n^d}{(a-1)!(b-1)!}+O(n^{d-1}).
                                                                    \tag{9}
\]
The section count is the dimension of the bihomogeneous polynomials of degree \((n,n)\); equivalently it follows from [projective-space cohomology](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01XS) and the product formula.
Thus the Segre degree is \(d!/((a-1)!(b-1)!)=N\).

To control length rather than just geometric points, work over \(\bar k_0\).
Choose \(d\) linear combinations of the \(F_w\) successively so that each is a non-zero-divisor on the current positive-dimensional intersection.
This is possible: their common base scheme is the finite \(Z\), so no positive-dimensional component is contained in every member of the linear system.
The initial \(X\) is smooth and Cohen–Macaulay.
Successive non-zero-divisor hyperplane sections remain Cohen–Macaulay and pure dimensional, so their associated points are the generic points of those components.
Avoiding finitely many proper linear subspaces in the space of sections provides each choice over the infinite algebraically closed field.

The resulting intersection \(Y\) has dimension zero and contains \(Z\) as a closed subscheme.
At each cut the exact hyperplane sequence replaces the Hilbert polynomial by \(P(n)-P(n-1)\).
After \(d\) cuts, (9) becomes the constant \(N\).
Hence \(Y\) has length \(N\), and the closed immersion \(Z\subseteq Y\) gives \(\ell\le N\).
No transversality or reducedness was assumed, and length is unchanged by the field extension.

Fix now one geometric left factor.
If its kernel on the \(b\) allowed column labels had dimension at least two, imposing that one column coordinate be zero would leave a nonzero kernel vector.
This would give a forbidden smaller support.
The kernel is therefore one dimensional.
Applying the same argument with the two sides exchanged proves the two uniqueness assertions.

This recovers the finiteness consequence implicit in [C33's projective boundary argument](Yin_C_A0033_v1.md#cutting-a-component-by-product-coordinates), now with a symmetric explicit length bound.
For \(a=3\), the bound becomes \(b(b+1)/2\).
It applies under the stronger joint minimality assumption and does not replace the \(b^2\) bound of [C101](Yin_C_A0101_v1.md#statement) for arbitrary allowed right supports.

## The normalized coefficient algebra and a finite basis

In the chart \(X_1=Y_1=1\), let
\[
 R=k_0[X_2^{\pm1},\ldots,X_a^{\pm1},
             Y_2^{\pm1},\ldots,Y_b^{\pm1}]/(F_w:w\in AB).
                                                                    \tag{10}
\]
This is the coordinate algebra of the entire \(Z\), by the preceding torus containment.
The degree of \(X_i\) is the image of \(\epsilon_i\) in \(\Gamma\), and that of \(Y_j\) is the image of \(\eta_j\).
Every \(F_w\) is homogeneous under (2), so
\[
                            R=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}R_\gamma.
\]
Every degree \(\gamma\) has a Laurent monomial representative, whose image is a unit in the nonzero algebra \(R\).
In particular every \(R_\gamma\) is nonzero.
Finite dimension of \(R\) forces \(\Gamma\) to be finite.
Choosing one monomial unit \(u_\gamma\) of each degree gives
\[
                    R_\gamma=u_\gamma R_0,\qquad
                    R=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}u_\gamma R_0.
                                                                    \tag{11}
\]
Multiplication by \(u_\gamma\) is an isomorphism from \(R_0\) to \(R_\gamma\).
Thus (11) is a free \(R_0\)-basis of size \(q\), and proves (3).
It does not depend on the existence of \(q\) distinct roots of unity in the coefficient field.

More explicitly, write
\[
                  \Gamma\cong\bigoplus_{\nu=1}^r
                                    \mathbb Z/n_\nu\mathbb Z
\]
and choose monomial units \(U_\nu\) with the corresponding basis degrees.
Then \(c_\nu=U_\nu^{n_\nu}\in R_0^\times\), and
\[
 R\cong
 R_0[T_1^{\pm1},\ldots,T_r^{\pm1}]
       /(T_\nu^{n_\nu}-c_\nu:1\le\nu\le r).
                                                                    \tag{12}
\]
The map sends \(T_\nu\) to \(U_\nu\).
The monomials with \(0\le j_\nu<n_\nu\) span because every degree has such a representative.
They are independent over \(R_0\) because they have distinct \(\Gamma\)-degrees and are units.
This proves the isomorphism and supplies the stated exact finite basis.

Both sets of coordinate degrees generate \(\Gamma\) separately.
To see this in every characteristic, quotient \(\Gamma\) by the subgroup generated by the row degrees and call the quotient \(Q\).
Take a normalized geometric solution \((u_i,v_j)\), and work over the group algebra \(\bar k_0[Q]\).
Keep all \(u_i\) fixed and replace \(v_j\) by \(v_j t^{\bar\eta_j}\), where \(t^q\) denotes the group basis element.
Homogeneity makes this another normalized right kernel vector for the same constant left factor.
Ordinary row reduction over \(\bar k_0\) shows that its one-dimensional kernel remains that same line after extension to any \(\bar k_0\)-algebra.
The normalization \(v_1=1\) therefore forces \(t^{\bar\eta_j}=1\) for every \(j\).
Distinct elements of \(Q\) remain distinct basis vectors of its group algebra, even when \(Q\) has \(p\)-torsion.
Thus \(Q=0\).
Exchanging the sides proves the column assertion.

Integer Smith normal form computes the rank and invariant factors in (2).
An infinite quotient, an order greater than \(N\), or failure of either separate generation test excludes the assumed geometric minimality and existence.
These are finite tests on the supplied table.

## The symmetric supporting subgroup

Normalize group labels by replacing a zero product with
\[
                (g_1^{-1}\alpha)(\beta h_1^{-1})=0.
\]
This does not change the product partition or its minimality.
Let \(H=\langle A_0\rangle\).
Left multiplication by a factor supported in \(H\) preserves each right coset \(Ht\).
Decompose the right factor into these cosets.
Every nonzero component is separately annihilated; minimality leaves only one component.
Since \(1\in B_0\), it is the coset \(H\), so \(B_0\subseteq H\).

Now put \(K=\langle B_0\rangle\).
Right multiplication by the right factor preserves the left cosets \(tK\).
The corresponding decomposition of the left factor, and \(1\in A_0\), give \(A_0\subseteq K\).
Thus \(H=K\), proving (4).
This uses the correct coset side on each factor.

Sending row degrees to \([g_1^{-1}g_i]\) and column degrees to \([h_jh_1^{-1}]\) in \(H_{\mathrm{ab}}\) respects (2), since equal products have equal abelianized sums.
The row labels generate \(H\), so the resulting map \(\Gamma\to H_{\mathrm{ab}}\) is surjective.
This proves (5).
If desired, connectedness of the row and column collision graphs, forced by the same minimality argument, also gives
\[
       H=\left\langle
                    (A_0^{-1}A_0)\cap(B_0B_0^{-1})
           \right\rangle.
\]
This is the collision localization from [C20](Yin_C_A0020_v1.md#primitivity-and-coefficient-collisions), with the needed uniqueness now supplied by geometric minimality rather than the half-rank and coset hypotheses used there.

## Joint coefficient fields and character quotients

Evaluation of (10) at a geometric point has image a finite-dimensional domain over \(k_0\), hence a field \(K\).
Thus every normalized coefficient is algebraic over the prime field.
Let \(K_L\) be generated by the left coefficients.
The right-kernel matrix has entries in \(K_L\), and its kernel is one dimensional after extending to \(\bar k_0\).
Row reduction over \(K_L\) produces the unique normalized right vector there.
Consequently \(K=K_L\).
The symmetric argument gives equality with the field \(K_R\) generated by the right coefficients.

The image of \(R_0\) is a finite-dimensional domain, hence a field \(F\).
Every degree-zero element is represented by a linear combination of Laurent monomials of degree zero.
This proves the monomial description of \(F\).
An integer basis of the kernel \(M\to\Gamma\) generates those monomials multiplicatively, so finitely many values suffice.
The quotient map \(R_0\to F\) and (3) give (6).

Evaluate (12) at this invariant point.
Writing \(\theta_\nu=U_\nu(\alpha,\beta)\), one obtains
\[
                  K=F(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r),\qquad
                  \theta_\nu^{n_\nu}\in F^\times.
                                                                    \tag{13}
\]
In characteristic zero successive adjoining gives \([K:F]\le\prod n_\nu=q\).
Together with (6), this proves (7).
In characteristic \(p\), \(F\) is a finite field and therefore perfect.
If \(n_\nu=p^{s_\nu}m_\nu\) with \(p\nmid m_\nu\), the unique \(p^{s_\nu}\)-th root in \(F\) of \(\theta_\nu^{n_\nu}\) equals \(\theta_\nu^{m_\nu}\).
Thus the same argument gives \([K:F]\le\prod m_\nu=q'\), proving (8).
For an arbitrary imperfect coefficient base, this removal of the \(p\)-part would require an additional hypothesis; the statement uses the perfect prime fields.

After adjoining the indicated roots of unity, all roots of the separable binomials for the \(\theta_\nu\) lie in the resulting compositum.
It is therefore a Galois extension.
An automorphism is determined by the ratios \(\sigma(\theta_\nu)/\theta_\nu\), which lie in the respective root groups.
Since these roots are fixed in the base, the ratio assignment is an injective group homomorphism into the character group of \(\Gamma\), with its \(p\)-part removed in characteristic \(p\).
This proves the abelian and divisibility conclusions.

Two geometric normalized points have the same degree-zero monomial values exactly when their coordinate ratios give a character of \(\Gamma\).
Indeed those ratios define a character of \(M\); equality of all invariant monomials says it is trivial on the kernel \(M\to\Gamma\).
Conversely a character twist fixes all degree-zero monomials and preserves every homogeneous equation.
The action is free because the coordinate degrees generate \(\Gamma\) and every coordinate is nonzero.
In characteristic zero each such orbit has \(q\) points.
In characteristic \(p\) it has \(q'\) points.
For the following common formula use \(q'=q\) and \(q_p=1\) in characteristic zero.
For each geometric point of \(R_0\), the binomial fiber (12) has exactly those \(q'\) points and length \(q\), because each \(p\)-power factor appears with its full multiplicity.
Hence the total point count is a multiple of \(q'\) and is at most \(\ell/q_p\), proving assertion 5.

Finally coarsen the grading through \(\Gamma\to H_{\mathrm{ab}}\).
Its degree-zero algebra is the direct sum of the \(R_\gamma\) with trivial image in \(H_{\mathrm{ab}}\).
The same unit-basis proof makes \(R\) free over that algebra of rank \(|H_{\mathrm{ab}}|\).
Repeating (12)–(13) gives the stated invariant-field and splitting conclusions for actual group-character twists.
The finer table grading can give a larger divisor and a smaller invariant-field bound than the abelianization alone.

## Reduction primes and the boundary condition

Complex geometric minimality must not be assumed to hold automatically in every characteristic.
There is a finite effective way to identify the boundary exceptions for a fixed complex-minimal table.
For each deletion of one row or one column, use the integral multiplication matrix of [C93](Yin_C_A0093_v1.md#statement).
Its rational row rank is full, since that deleted-support rectangle has no complex zero product.
Let \(d_i>0\) be the gcd of its full-size minors.
A rectangle with a one-element side has no zero product in any characteristic and can be assigned \(d_i=1\).
Put \(\Delta=\prod_i d_i\).

By the same all-characteristic rank criterion, the prime divisors of \(\Delta\) are exactly those characteristics in which some row- or column-deleted rectangle has a nonzero zero product.
Every proper support pair lies in at least one such rectangle.
Thus these are exactly the characteristics with a boundary solution on the original allowed supports.
The original full rectangle has a nonzero projective solution in every characteristic, since its rational rank deficiency in C93 means that all full minors are identically zero as integers.
For \(p\nmid\Delta\), it is therefore geometrically minimal and all the characteristic-\(p\) conclusions above apply.
For \(p\mid\Delta\), a boundary solution certifies that the original pair fails geometric minimality in that characteristic.
The boundary criterion does not determine whether a full-support solution exists there.

At a finite place of a complex coefficient field above a prime \(p\nmid\Delta\), scale each factor by a coordinate of least valuation.
Reduction gives two nonzero factors.
No coordinate can disappear, since that would be a boundary solution.
Consequently every ratio of coefficients on each side is a local unit there.
This supplies an effective finite exceptional-prime set for the unit assertion.
It does not claim that the normalized coefficients are algebraic units at every prime, as in the more specialized three-label conclusion of C101.

## A characteristic-two multiplicity check and finite enumeration

Let \(G=C_2=\langle t\rangle\), with \(A=B=\{1,t\}\).
This support pair is geometrically minimal in every characteristic, because a monomial factor is a unit.
After normalizing the identity coefficients, the equations are
\[
                          1+xy=0,\qquad x+y=0.
\]
Thus
\[
             R=k_0[x^{\pm1}]/(x^2-1),\qquad
             \Gamma\cong C_2,\qquad N=\ell=q=2.
\]
In characteristic zero there are two geometric points.
In characteristic two there is one point, with length two.
Counting only ordinary characters in characteristic two would miss the order of \(\Gamma\); the unit grading retains it.
This example has torsion and calibrates the scheme and characteristic distinction only.

For fixed \(a,b\) there are finitely many partitions of the \(ab\) cells.
The actual group tables are among the partitions whose fibers are partial matchings.
For any supplied partition, C93 tests geometric existence and boundary emptiness by rational rank computations, while (2) is an integer Smith-form calculation.
For a characteristic-zero minimal partition, the finite algebra (10) then determines an effectively enumerable finite list of normalized algebraic coefficient points, of total length at most \(N\).
Thus there is a finite coefficient list for fixed minimal support sizes, before imposing group realizability.
The enumeration can be very large and supplies no efficient complexity bound or group-realization algorithm.
The new quantitative bounds supplement C33 and C93; neither minimality nor a passing coefficient table furnishes a torsion-free group counterexample.
