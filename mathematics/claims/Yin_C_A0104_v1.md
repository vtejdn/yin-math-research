# Divisibility of rank denominators for Galois coefficient fields

## Statement

Let \(j\ge2\) and assume rational matrix integrality on the \(j\)-fold direct power of a torsion-free group \(G\).
Write \(D_t\) for the actual division closure of \(\mathbb Q[G^t]\) in the affiliated algebra and \(D=D_1\).
For any number field \(K\subset\mathbb C\), the subring \(E=DK\subset U(G)\) generated by \(D\) and the central scalars \(K\) is a simple Artinian ring.
Write \(E\simeq M_s(V)\) with \(V\) a division ring.
All finite matrices over \(K[G]\) have unnormalized regular rank in \(\frac1s\mathbb Z\).
Always \(s^j\le [K:\mathbb Q]\).
If \(K/\mathbb Q\) is Galois, the stronger assertion is
\[
                     s^j\ \mid\ [K:K\cap D].
\]
Here the intersection means actual scalars in \(U(G)\).
Thus two coordinates suffice for full integrality at every matrix size for every Galois number field of squarefree degree, with no soficity assumption.
More generally the same conclusion holds if the indicated degree is free of nontrivial \(j\)-th power divisors.
These hypotheses concern rational ranks on the stated group power and do not establish them for arbitrary torsion-free groups.

## Argument

### Scalar extension at the first coordinate

The [division-ring criterion and subgroup comparison](Yin_C_A0096_v1.md#alternation-in-actual-affiliated-rings) make every \(D_t\), \(1\le t\le j\), a division ring.
Coordinate inclusions preserve their actual rational expressions.
Let \(Z\) be the center of \(D\) and \(k=D\cap\overline{\mathbb Q}\), using the actual complex algebraic scalars.
Every element of \(D\) algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\) belongs to \(k\).
Indeed the [square test](Yin_C_A0096_v2.md#the-square-test-for-a-scalar-affiliated-element) makes \(x-\lambda\) an affiliated unit at every \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\) if \(x\) is nonscalar.
An annihilating scalar polynomial would then factor into units in \(U(G)\).
In particular \(k\) is relatively algebraically closed in \(Z\), since algebraicity over the algebraic field extension \(k/\mathbb Q\) implies algebraicity over \(\mathbb Q\).

Put \(L=kK\) inside \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
A minimal polynomial over \(k\) of a primitive generator of the finite separable extension \(L/k\) stays irreducible over \(Z\).
To see this, place an algebraic closure of \(k\) in an algebraic closure of \(Z\).
The coefficients of any monic factor over \(Z\) are algebraic over \(k\), as elementary symmetric functions in roots of this polynomial.
They would belong to \(k\), proving irreducibility.
Consequently \(Z\otimes_k L\) is a field \(Z'\).

For any finite field extension \(W/Z\), the ring \(D\otimes_Z W\) is simple.
In a nonzero two-sided ideal choose a nonzero sum \(\sum_{i=1}^m d_i\otimes w_i\) of minimal length, with the \(w_i\) independent over \(Z\).
Multiply by \(d_1^{-1}\) on the left to make \(d_1=1\).
Its commutator with any \(d\otimes1\) is in the ideal and has shorter length, so it vanishes.
Independence of the \(w_i\) forces every remaining \(d_i\) to be central.
The chosen element is now a nonzero \(1\otimes w\), which is a unit.
The ring has finite right dimension over \(D\) and hence is right Artinian.
This proof needs no finite-dimensionality of \(D\) over its center.

In particular \(D\otimes_k L=D\otimes_Z Z'\) maps injectively into \(U(G)\): its evaluation map is unital and its source is simple.
Its image is \(E\), since \(kK\) is the \(k\)-linear span of \(K\) and the scalars commute with \(D\).
The Artin–Wedderburn theorem for simple Artinian rings gives the asserted matrix form, at the same ring-theoretic scope as the [semisimple decomposition used in JZ19](../audits/literature/JZ19/Yin_U001.md#jz19-u001).
The diagonal matrix units of \(M_s(V)\) decompose the identity into \(s\) equivalent idempotents in \(U(G)\).
Their principal right modules are isomorphic by the off-diagonal matrix units, so their regular dimensions are \(1/s\) by additivity and \(\tau_G(1)=1\).
Rectangular elimination over \(V\) therefore shows that all the indicated ranks are integer multiples of \(1/s\).
This argument uses module dimensions for idempotents and does not require the chosen algebraic matrix units to be selfadjoint.

### Independent systems of matrix units

Put \(E_j=D_jK\) inside \(U(G^j)\).
Each of the \(j\) independent coordinates contains a copy of the preceding matrix units for \(E\), because its copy of \(D\) lies in \(D_j\) and it sends the scalars \(K\) to the same scalars.
Products of their matrix units, indexed by tuples of \(j\) indices, give one unital system of \(s^j\)-by-\(s^j\) matrix units in \(E_j\).
The matrix-unit identities follow by commutation across coordinates, and the diagonal sum is one.
For any unital algebra of finite right \(D_j\)-dimension containing such a system, this dimension is divisible by \(s^j\).
In fact the diagonal units split the right module into \(s^j\) summands of equal dimension, since left multiplication by the off-diagonal units implements right-linear isomorphisms.
In particular \(E_j\) has right dimension at most \([K:\mathbb Q]\), by expanding its central coefficients in a rational basis.
This proves \(s^j\le[K:\mathbb Q]\) for any number field.

### The divisor in the Galois case

Take \(k'=K\cap D\), so \(k'\subset D_j\) consists of scalars and \(K/k'\) is Galois when \(K/\mathbb Q\) is Galois.
The abstract algebra \(B=D_j\otimes_{k'} K\) has right \(D_j\)-dimension \([K:k']\).
It is a finite product of simple rings.
Indeed, over the center of \(D_j\) the separable minimal polynomial of a primitive generator of \(K/k'\) factors as distinct irreducibles.
Its factors give a direct product of fields by the Chinese remainder theorem, and tensoring \(D_j\) with each field gives a simple ring by the argument above.

For each \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/k')\), the map
\[
              \pi_\sigma:B\longrightarrow U(G^j),\qquad
              d\otimes a\longmapsto d\,\sigma(a)
\]
has image exactly \(E_j\).
Together these maps are injective: using a primitive generator, the scalar Vandermonde matrix of all its conjugates is invertible over \(\mathbb C\), so vanishing of all images forces each \(D_j\)-coefficient of the power-basis expansion to be zero.
Every simple factor of \(B\) therefore survives in at least one image.
Its surviving copy is a direct factor of \(E_j\), since ideals in the finite product are sums of whole simple factors.
Project the system of \(s^j\)-by-\(s^j\) matrix units in \(E_j\) onto that nonzero direct factor.
It remains a unital system there and its equivalent summands split the right \(D_j\)-dimension of this factor into a multiple of \(s^j\).
The identifications preserve the right \(D_j\)-actions, using projection onto the factor for these actions.
Summing over all factors of \(B\) proves \(s^j\mid[K:k']\).
This use of Galois stability compares the image rings as sets and imposes no equality of ranks of the conjugates of a specified matrix.
