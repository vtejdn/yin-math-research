# Two scalar tests for singular elements at a fixed inverse layer

## Statement

For a discrete group \(K\), let \(U_K\) be the algebra of operators affiliated with its group von Neumann algebra, with canonical trace \(\tau_K\).
Set \(D_0(K)=\mathbb C K\) and let \(D_{j+1}(K)\) be the unital subring of \(U_K\) generated by \(D_j(K)\) and the inverses of those elements of \(D_j(K)\) that are units of \(U_K\).
Write \(\nu(x)\) for the trace of the kernel projection of a scalar affiliated operator and \(P_j(K)\) for the assertion that every nonzero \(x\in D_j(K)\) is a unit of \(U_K\).
In this finite tracial setting, a scalar is a unit precisely when its nullity is zero.
Each \(D_j\) is closed under adjoints since taking adjoints preserves actual inverses and reverses products.
Inclusion of a subgroup preserves kernels, product, adjoint and actual inverse by the right-coset decomposition of the regular representation, and therefore preserves each inverse layer.

Let \(W=K\wr\mathbb Z\), let \(t\) be the shift, and embed \(K\) at the origin.
Set \(T=t+t^{-1}\).
For each \(j\ge0\), \(P_j(K)\) is equivalent to the following scalar identity over \(W\) for every \(x\in D_j(K)\):
\[
                \nu(x^*T x)=\nu\bigl(x^*(T-2)x\bigr).
                \tag{1}
\]
Both test elements belong to \(D_j(W)\).
The right side always equals \(\nu(x)\).
If \(0<\alpha=\nu(x)<1\), the left side exceeds the right side by exactly
\[
                    \frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2-\alpha}.
                    \tag{2}
\]
The affine scalar pencil \(x^*(T-\lambda)x\) then has infinitely many distinct nullities, already in the complex linear span of \(x^*Tx\) and \(x^*x\).

There is also a test using the rate at which positive nullities can decrease with polynomial degree.
Let \(H\) be the group of permutations of \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\) generated by \(s(q)=2q\) and \(u(q)=2q+1\), and set
\[
       V_K=\left(\bigoplus_{q\in\mathbb Z[1/2]}K_q\right)\rtimes H
\]
using the permutation action on sites.
If \(x\in D_j(K_0)\) has nullity \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), there are positive \(h_m\in D_j(V_K)\) with
\[
                 \nu(h_m)=\alpha^{\,2^m}\qquad(m\ge0).
                 \tag{3}
\]
In the letters \(x,x^*,s^{\pm1},u^{\pm1}\), \(h_m\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(2m+2\).
After constructing \(x\) and \(x^*\), the recurrence uses no more inversions and requires a bounded number of arithmetic gates per step when the previous output is reused.
If \(x\in\mathbb C K\) has word radius \(R\) in a finite symmetric generating set of \(K\), the group-ring scalar \(h_m\) has word radius at most \(2R+2m\) in the resulting generating set of \(V_K\) enlarged by the affine generators and their inverses.
Both ambient groups are torsion-free if \(K\) is torsion-free, finitely generated if \(K\) is finitely generated, and amenable if \(K\) is amenable.

## The scalar pencil and its exact nullities

Work on right modules over \(U=U_W\), with dimension given on a projection module by its trace.
Let \(p\) be the left support of \(x\), the projection onto the closure of its range, and let \(e\) be its right support.
The polar partial isometry identifies \(e\) with \(p\), so \(\tau(p)=\tau(e)=1-\alpha=:\beta\).
The affiliated inverse on the support gives an isomorphism \(x:eU\to pU\), while \(\ker x^*=(1-p)U\).
For \(H_\lambda=T-\lambda\) and a vector \(z\in eU\), put \(w=xz\).
Then \(x^*H_\lambda xz=0\) holds exactly when \(pH_\lambda p w=0\), since \(w\in pU\).
Splitting the full domain as \((1-e)U\oplus eU\) proves
\[
      \nu(x^*H_\lambda x)
        =\alpha+\tau\!\left(1_{\{\lambda\}}\bigl(pTp|_p\bigr)\right)
        \qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb R).
        \tag{4}
\]
The corner trace here is unnormalized.
This argument works for unbounded \(x\).
The support projections and partial inverse serve only to compute the trace in \(U\).

Suppose \(0<\beta<1\).
The integer translates of \(p\) belong to distinct copies of the coefficient von Neumann algebra.
They commute and have product traces, by the [lamp-coordinate trace calculation](Yin_C_A0017_v2.md#copies-in-the-regular-representation).
Consequently the [Dirichlet path decomposition of this compression](Yin_C_A0005_v2.md#actual-projections-and-boundary-residues) applies.
The all-occupied event has trace zero since the first \(n\) specified sites are all occupied with probability \(\beta^n\).
A path of exactly \(\ell\ge1\) occupied sites starting at the origin with its two neighbors vacant has trace \(\alpha^2\beta^\ell\).
Each of its eigenvalues \(2\cos(\pi k/(\ell+1))\), \(1\le k\le\ell\), contributes that start trace once to the corner law.
For coprime \(0<a<b\) the eigenvalue \(2\cos(\pi a/b)\) therefore occurs exactly at lengths \(\ell=nb-1\), \(n\ge1\).
Summing these contributions in (4) yields
\[
 \nu(x^*(T-\lambda)x)-\alpha=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\alpha^2\beta^{b-1}}{1-\beta^b},
       &\lambda=2\cos(\pi a/b),\quad 0<a<b,\quad\gcd(a,b)=1,\\[4pt]
 0,     &\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{5}
\]
Taking \(\lambda=0\) gives \(b=2\) and excess \(\alpha\beta/(1+\beta)\).
Taking \(\lambda=2\) gives zero excess.
The excesses in (5) decrease strictly as \(b\) ranges over the integers \(b\ge2\).
If \(x=0\), the pencil has constant nullity one.
If \(x\) is a unit, both outer factors are invertible and \(T-\lambda\) has nullity zero for every real \(\lambda\), since it is a nonzero Laurent polynomial in the bilateral shift.
These cases establish (1) and (2), including the equivalence for the original fixed group \(K\).
The [earlier matrix pencil](../ledger/Yin_L_A0033_v1.md#a-fixed-hermitian-pencil) uses the same path law in a matrix realization.
Here the congruence by \(x\) realizes the law on its range support with scalar coefficients at the original inverse layer.

## Degree and nullity on the dyadic sites

Put \(h_0=x^*x\) and define
\[
                 h_{m+1}=s h_m s^{-1}+u h_m u^{-1}.
                 \tag{6}
\]
The level-\(m\) sites are the integers from \(0\) to \(2^m-1\).
Indeed \(s\) takes the preceding sites to their doubles and \(u\) takes them to their doubles plus one, which partitions the new interval into its even and odd sites.
It follows inductively that \(h_m\) is the sum of \(2^m\) copies of \(x^*x\), one at each of these distinct sites.
In the joint spectral calculus these are nonnegative possibly unbounded random variables, affiliated with the abelian von Neumann algebra generated by their spectral projections.
Their sum vanishes exactly where they all vanish.
Its kernel projection is therefore the product of their kernel projections, whose trace is \(\alpha^{2^m}\) by independence of the sites.
Each \(h_m\) is positive and nonzero since \(0<\alpha^{2^m}<1\).
Conjugating by \(s\) or \(u\) increases the word degree by at most two, and addition takes the larger of the two bounds.
This proves (3) and the degree and radius estimates.

The group \(H\) consists of increasing affine maps \(q\mapsto 2^nq+c\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), \(c\in\mathbb Z[1/2]\).
A finite-order map of this form has \(n=0\) and then \(c=0\).
The multiplier homomorphism exhibits \(H\) as an extension of an abelian translation group by an infinite cyclic group, hence \(H\) is amenable.
As \(us^{-1}\) translates by one and its conjugates by \(s^{-d}\) translate by \(2^{-d}\), the action is transitive.
Thus the generators of \(K_0\) together with \(s,u\) generate \(V_K\).
Finite-order elements project to the identity in \(H\) and have finite-order coordinates in the direct sum, proving torsion-freeness when \(K\) is torsion-free.
For amenable \(K\), the direct sum is a directed union of finite products of amenable groups, so the extension \(V_K\) is amenable.
The same properties of \(K\wr\mathbb Z\) follow by the [ordinary shift argument](Yin_C_A0017_v2.md#copies-in-the-regular-representation).

## A sufficient estimate for one induction step

Fix \(k\ge0\) and a group \(K\) satisfying \(P_k(K)\).
Take any finite adjoint-closed alphabet \(F\) in \(D_k(K_0)\) enlarged by inverses of finitely many nonzero elements there and their adjoints.
Adjoin \(s^{\pm1},u^{\pm1}\) and let \(B_L(F)\) be the complex span in \(U_{V_K}\) of words of length at most \(L\) in this combined alphabet, including the empty word.
Suppose for every such \(F\) that there are constants \(c,d>0\), depending on \(F\) and \(K\), such that every positive \(y\in B_L(F)\) with \(\nu(y)>0\) satisfies
\[
                   \nu(y)\ge \exp\bigl(-c(1+L)^d\bigr)
                   \qquad(L\ge0).
                   \tag{7}
\]
Then \(P_{k+1}(K)\) follows.
Indeed any \(x\in D_{k+1}(K)\) is a polynomial of some finite degree \(D\) in such an alphabet before the affine generators are adjoined.
If \(x\) were nonzero and singular, the recurrence (6) would give \(h_m\in B_{2D+2m}(F)\) with \(-\log\nu(h_m)=2^m(-\log\alpha)\).
For large \(m\) this exceeds \(c(1+2D+2m)^d\), contradicting (7).
Conversely, universal \(P_{k+1}\) for torsion-free groups would imply (7) on all the torsion-free test groups, since positive nullities in the indicated layer would then equal one.
Neither identity (1) for arbitrary \(x\) nor estimate (7) follows here from \(P_k(K)\) alone.
