# Central specialization preserves each scalar inverse layer

## Statement

For a discrete group \(H\), construct \(D_0(H)=\mathbb CH\) and \(D_{j+1}(H)\) by adjoining to \(D_j(H)\) the scalar inverses that exist in \(L^0(\mathcal N(H))\) and taking the generated algebra.
Let \(P_j(H)\) say that every nonzero element of \(D_j(H)\) is invertible in this affiliated algebra.
The finite layers are the [construction of Yin, Remark IV.5.6(iv)](../audits/literature/Yin20/Yin_U002.md#61-definition-iv55-and-all-parts-of-remark-iv56).
For every torsion-free abelian group \(Z\) and every fixed \(j\ge0\),
\[
                         P_j(H\times Z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_j(H).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
In particular this equality of the two tests holds at a specified depth, without assuming an integer matrix rank theorem for \(H\).

For the first Schur complement there is a test requiring just one specialization.
Suppose \(G=H\times\langle z\rangle\), with \(z\) infinite cyclic, and \(P_0(H)\) holds.
Let \(a,b,c,d\) be Laurent polynomials in \(z\) over \(\mathbb CH\), with \(a\ne0\), and write \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
If at some complex \(\lambda\ne0\) the specialized matrix \(M(\lambda)\) has regular rank two over \(H\), then \(d-ca^{-1}b\) is invertible over the affiliated algebra of \(G\).
A polynomial or Laurent family can therefore have a certified nonzero Schur complement even if its entries each contain several distinct powers of the central generator and involve noncommuting coefficients from \(H\).

In particular let \(H_1*H_2\) embed in \(H\) and suppose \(P_0(H_1),P_0(H_2)\) hold.
Take \(a,b\in\mathbb C[H_1\times\langle z\rangle]\) and \(c,d\in\mathbb C[H_2\times\langle z\rangle]\), with \(a\ne0\).
Then the complement is a unit unless the second row is zero or there is \(f\in\mathbb C(z)\) with \(b=fa,\ d=fc\), in either of which cases it is zero.
This conclusion permits the two row groups to overlap along the entire central cyclic group.

## A bound on the total rank lost by specialization

For an \(m\)-by-\(n\) polynomial \(T(s)\) of degree at most \(D\) with bounded coefficients over a finite tracial von Neumann algebra, normalize the scalar trace by \(\tau(1)=1\) and use the unnormalized matrix trace.
Let \(r(s)\) be its regular rank, and \(r_*=\sup_{s\in\mathbb C}r(s)\).
For every finite set \(E\subset\mathbb C\),
\[
                     \sum_{s\in E}(r_*-r(s))\ \le\ D\min(m,n).         \tag{2}
\]
The existing [Vandermonde argument](Yin_C_A0022_v1.md#simultaneous-generic-rank-and-degeneration) gives the sharper right side \(D r_*\), which already suffices here.
In particular all but countably many complex values have the same maximal rank.
Multiplying a Laurent polynomial by a power of \(s\) gives the corresponding assertion at nonzero values, with the range of exponents as a degree bound.

The [central-variable specialization theorem](Yin_C_A0012_v2.md#statement) also treats rational expressions with a declared evaluation domain.
We apply these results while keeping track of the depth of each inverse.

## The rank at a generic phase for a finite inverse expression

First take \(Z=\mathbb Z\).
Fourier transformation in the central coordinate realizes the regular algebra as \(L^\infty(\mathbb T)\,\overline\otimes\,\mathcal N(H)\) with Haar measure and the product trace.
Bounded group-ring Laurent polynomials act fiberwise by evaluation.
Affiliated sums, products, kernels, and existing inverses respect this decomposition, as follows from their measurable graphs and the kernel projections or equivalently finite affiliated matrix operations.
In particular a square bounded denominator has affiliated inverse exactly when its fibers have full rank almost everywhere.
It suffices throughout to restrict \(H\) to the countable subgroup generated by the finitely many group elements in the expression, using the copies of its regular action on cosets.

Fix one scalar expression \(x\in D_j(H\times\mathbb Z)\) of depth at most \(j\).
Here all inverses appearing in the expression exist at the stage at which they are taken.
Off a Haar-null set the same finite expression is defined in the fiber and has depth at most \(j\) over \(H\), by induction on its gates.
Its scalar rank is constant almost everywhere, even without \(P_j(H)\).
Indeed use the [finite polynomial realization of a defined expression](Yin_C_A0012_v1.md#rational-expressions-and-explicit-polynomial-schur-realizations) to write \(x=d-cL^{-1}b\), with a square group-ring denominator \(L\) of size \(h\) and a group-ring border \(B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}L&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
The constant-polynomial case may be used directly.
Almost every \(L(\lambda)\) has rank \(h\), hence (2) gives the same full rank off a countable subset of the circle.
Outside a countable set \(B(\lambda)\) also has constant rank.
At almost every \(\lambda\), elimination therefore gives the same scalar rank \(\operatorname{rk}_H B(\lambda)-h\), equal to \(\operatorname{rk}_{H\times\mathbb Z}x\) by integration.
If \(P_j(H)\) holds, this constant is zero or one.
Faithfulness and affiliated invertibility at scalar rank one give \(P_j(H\times\mathbb Z)\).
The converse follows from the trace-preserving subgroup inclusion, which preserves all the allowed inverses and their depths.
For arbitrary torsion-free abelian \(Z\), each expression uses a finitely generated subgroup of \(Z\) isomorphic to some \(\mathbb Z^d\).
Iteration and the subgroup argument give (1).

## A second-order certificate on the actual regular group

The nonzero coefficient polynomial \(a\) has full scalar rank over \(G\) by (1) at \(j=0\).
The specified rank-two evaluation of \(M\) forces its maximal rank to be two, so (2) and the Fourier decomposition give \(\operatorname{rk}_G M=2\).
The [Schur rank identity](../ledger/Yin_L_A0009_v1.md#the-exact-size-reducing-step) gives \(\operatorname{rk}_G(d-ca^{-1}b)=1\).
For example, take arbitrary \(a_0,d_0\ne0\) in \(\mathbb CH\) and any four \(A,B,C,D\) in the same group ring, and set
\[
 M(z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a_0+(z-1)A &(z-1)B\\
 C &d_0+(z-1)D
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
At \(z=1\) the triangular matrix has two affiliated units on the diagonal by \(P_0(H)\).
This proves invertibility of the actual group-ring matrix over the affiliated algebra of \(G\), and hence of its Schur complement, for all four choices of coefficient corrections.
The same evaluation argument works at any size with a full-rank specialization.
The structure being used is the Haar decomposition in an independent central direction.
A constant family over \(H\) keeps exactly its original rank at every phase, so (2) supplies no new exclusion for an arbitrary unresolved matrix over \(H\) itself.

## Two rows sharing the central cyclic subgroup

For a nonzero group-ring Laurent polynomial \(a(z)\), some coefficient at a group element of \(H_1\) is a nonzero scalar Laurent polynomial \(a_g(z)\).
It has only finitely many zeros on the circle.
Thus \(a(\lambda)\ne0\) almost everywhere and is a unit in the affiliated algebra of the fiber by \(P_0(H_1)\).
The same Fourier argument gives the required global inverse.
A nonzero second row also specializes to a nonzero row outside a finite set.
Apply the [two-row free product criterion](Yin_C_A0111_v1.md#statement) on these fibers.
By (2) their common generic rank is one or two.
If it is one, then at almost every phase both rows have their second entry equal to a scalar times their first, with the same scalar for both rows.
The coefficient at \(g\) of the first row forces this scalar to equal \(f(\lambda)=b_g(\lambda)/a_g(\lambda)\).
Clearing \(a_g\) in both rows now gives scalar Laurent coefficient identities almost everywhere, hence identically.
This proves \(b=fa,\ d=fc\) in the central rational extension.
Conversely these identities give zero Schur complement since \(f\) is central.
In the other case generic rank two gives invertibility, and a zero second row gives zero directly.
