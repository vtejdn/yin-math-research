# A field from two coefficients at one additional inverse layer

## Statement

Use the [inverse layers](../audits/literature/Yin20/Yin_U002.md#61-definition-iv55-and-all-parts-of-remark-iv56) starting at \(D_0(G)=\mathbb CG\) in \(U=L^0(\mathcal N(G))\).
Suppose \(P_k(G)\) holds, meaning that every nonzero element of \(D_k(G)\) is a unit of \(U\).
Let \(a,b\in D_k(G)\) be complex linearly independent and put \(x=a^{-1}b\).
Evaluation \(t\mapsto x\) embeds the commutative rational function field \(\mathbb C(t)\) in \(D_{k+1}(G)\).
In particular, arbitrarily many rational operations in this normalized coefficient require at most one layer beyond the original pair.
The elements \(a,b\) may fail to commute.

Write \(F=\mathbb C(x)\) for the image field.
The space \(aF\) is closed under scalar Schur updates \(v-wp^{-1}u\) when the four entries belong to \(aF\) and \(p\ne0\).
Every resulting entry and the inverse of every nonzero pivot belong to \(D_{k+1}(G)\).
Every rectangular matrix with entries in this space has integer regular rank, equal to the ordinary rank of the matrix of rational functions after the common left factor \(a\) is removed.
This includes every group-ring matrix of any size whose entries span a complex space of dimension at most two under \(P_0(G)\).
In particular, if \(a\ne0\) is the leading entry in a two-by-two matrix and the entry space has dimension at most two, \(d-ca^{-1}b\) has full rank exactly when the associated two-by-two commutative determinant is nonzero.

If the four entries of \(M\in M_2(D_k(G))\) span a complex space of dimension exactly three, their unique constant linear relation, up to scaling, gives a further reduction.
Represent it by a two-by-two matrix of coefficients.
When this matrix has rank one, \(P_k(G)\) suffices for integer rank of the original two-by-two matrix.
When it has rank two, constant invertible row and column operations bring the original matrix to the form \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&-a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(a,b,c\) independent.
Set \(x=a^{-1}b,\ y=a^{-1}c\).
The rank is then \(1+\operatorname{rk}(1+yx)\).
Although each separate rational function field is contained in \(D_{k+1}(G)\), a proof that \(1+yx\) is zero or a unit for arbitrary such triples remains open here.
Conversely this three-coefficient problem suffices for every two-by-two input over \(D_k(G)\) under \(P_k(G)\).
Thus the first Schur problem is equivalent to excluding a proper nonzero kernel for
\[
                 1+(p^{-1}v)(p^{-1}u)
\]
for every complex linearly independent triple \(p,u,v\in D_k(G)\).
This equivalence concerns the two-by-two step and does not assume that the subring generated by the two ratios is a division ring.
The three-coefficient case already has fractional rank for a polynomial algebra in a general finite tracial representation satisfying the scalar hypothesis, as exhibited below.

## Rational functions and Schur complements

In a finite tracial algebra the canonical dimension of the closed range is invariant under multiplication by an affiliated unit, using affiliated module rank.
For each \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\), \(b-\lambda a\ne0\), hence it is an affiliated unit by \(P_k(G)\).
The identity and inverse in their required order are
\[
               x-\lambda=a^{-1}(b-\lambda a),\qquad
               (x-\lambda)^{-1}=(b-\lambda a)^{-1}a .
\]
Both expressions belong to \(D_{k+1}(G)\).
Factoring every nonzero scalar polynomial into linear factors over \(\mathbb C\) shows that its value at \(x\) is an affiliated unit whose inverse already belongs to this layer.
The polynomial evaluations commute and their inverses commute with them.
The ordinary universal property of the fraction field gives the asserted embedding, including injectivity since every nonzero polynomial evaluates to a unit.
Only the full rank of each nonzero member of the original two-dimensional space is needed in this proof.

For a matrix with entries \(a f_{ij}(x)\), remove \(a\) from every row by a diagonal affiliated unit.
Ordinary elimination over \(F\) gives its rank because the resulting invertible row and column operations are also affiliated units.
Alternatively, the ordered scalar update in the original coordinates is
\[
       a f-a g(a h)^{-1}a l=a(f-gh^{-1}l),\qquad (a h)^{-1}=h^{-1}a^{-1}.
\]
The right sides stay in \(aF\) and \(Fa^{-1}\), respectively, and both spaces are contained in \(D_{k+1}(G)\).
If the original entry space has dimension one, a single nonzero common factor reduces the question to a constant matrix; dimension zero is immediate.
For dimension two, each normalized original entry is a scalar affine polynomial in \(x\), giving the determinant test at size two.

## The single relation at coefficient dimension three

Write the coefficient relation on \(M\) as \(\operatorname{tr}_{2}(K^tM)=0\), where the notation means the sum of the four entrywise products with constant coefficients and uses no tracial permutation of operator entries.
Under \(M\mapsto PMQ\) the relation matrix becomes \(P^{-t}K Q^{-t}\), for \(P,Q\) invertible over \(\mathbb C\).
This yields the usual two complex rank classes.
For rank one we can put a zero in a corner.
After permutations the result is triangular, and independence of the other three entries makes the two diagonal entries nonzero.
They are affiliated units by \(P_k(G)\).
For rank two we may take the relation matrix to be the identity, giving the claimed opposite diagonal entries.
Removing \(a\) on the left of each row then gives \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\y&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\); its Schur complement is \(-1-yx\).
The original nonscalar choices can overlap completely in \(G\).
This reduction singles out a multiplicative expression in two distinct normalized ratios beyond the single-field argument.

## Reducing every two-by-two input to three coefficients

If a matrix \(M\) has a zero entry, permutations place it in triangular form.
An upper triangular two-by-two matrix with nonzero diagonal entries is an affiliated unit by \(P_k(G)\).
If a diagonal entry is zero, its rank is zero or one by a nonzero scalar pivot or by vanishing of the entire matrix.
For an input \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with all entries nonzero, use the identity
\[
       \begin{pmatrix}d&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
       M
       \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&a\end{pmatrix}
       =
       \begin{pmatrix}da&dba\\-c&-da\end{pmatrix}.                    \tag{1}
\]
The multipliers are affiliated units under \(P_k(G)\), and all the new entries remain in \(D_k(G)\) using multiplication alone.
Put \(p=da,\ u=dba,\ v=-c\).
If the three coefficients are dependent, the two-coefficient argument already computes the rank.
If they are independent, the right side has rank \(1+\operatorname{rk}(1+(p^{-1}v)(p^{-1}u))\) by the previous section.
Conversely every independent triple occurs as the entries of its own trace-zero input \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p&u\\v&-p\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
In particular at depth zero every normalization in (1) was performed inside the original group ring.
Whenever \(a,d\ne0\), the scalar \(1+(p^{-1}v)(p^{-1}u)\) in (1) equals \(a^{-1}d^{-1}(d-ca^{-1}b)a\) and has the same regular rank as the original Schur complement.

For the finite tracial [direct-sum construction](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#why-infinite-orbits-and-scalar-injectivity-do-not-finish-the-argument), use its three operators \(X_1,X_2,X_3\).
Their nonzero scalar polynomials all have rank one, and the symmetric matrix with rows \((X_1,X_2),(X_2,X_3)\) has rank \(3/2\).
Multiplying it on the right by the constant invertible matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) gives our normal form with \(a=X_2,\ b=-X_1,\ c=X_3\).
These coefficients are independent and \(a\) is a unit.
Consequently \(1+(a^{-1}c)(a^{-1}b)\) has rank \(1/2\) even though every nonzero polynomial in the triple has rank one.
This comparison is in the indicated finite tracial representation; the remaining group question uses regular convolution and its canonical trace.
