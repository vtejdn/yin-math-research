# One denominator and triangular matrix tests for each inverse layer

## Statement

Let \(B=D_k(G)\) in the affiliated algebra \(U=L^0(\mathcal N(G))\), with the scalar inverse layers of [Yin, Remark IV.5.6(iv)](../audits/literature/Yin20/Yin_U002.md#61-definition-iv55-and-all-parts-of-remark-iv56).
For \(p\in B\cap U^\times\), let \(B[p^{-1}]\) mean the subalgebra of \(U\) generated by \(B\) and the one indicated inverse.
Then \(D_{k+1}(G)\) is the directed union of these subalgebras.
Every element may be written as a noncommutative polynomial in finitely many elements \(p^{-1}a_i\) for a single such \(p\) and \(a_i\in B\).
The same \(p^{-1}\) may occur more than once in a word.

Equivalently, each element of \(D_{k+1}(G)\) can be written
\[
        d-cL^{-1}b,\qquad L=pI_q-N,\qquad N\in M_q(B)
             \text{ strictly upper triangular},                       \tag{1}
\]
where \(d,c,b\) have entries in \(B\), respectively a scalar, a row and a column.
One can choose \(q\) equal to the total number of occurrences of \(p^{-1}\) in an expanded sum of alternating words with coefficients in \(B\).
The case \(q=0\) denotes an element of \(B\).
For \(q>0\), \(N\) can be restricted to disjoint chains of consecutive superdiagonal entries; the converse holds even for unrestricted strictly upper triangular \(N\).
Thus, assuming \(P_k(G)\), the full next-layer statement \(P_{k+1}(G)\) is equivalent to requiring
\[
      \operatorname{rk}_G
          \begin{pmatrix}pI_q-N&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
                     \ \in\{q,q+1\}                                   \tag{2}
\]
for every \(q\ge1\), nonzero \(p\in B\), and the stated border and chain coefficients in \(B\).
There is no extra invertibility premise for a matrix denominator.
The \(q=1\) case is precisely the one Schur step and admits the [three-coefficient normal form](Yin_C_A0116_v1.md#reducing-every-two-by-two-input-to-three-coefficients).
At \(k=0\) all the matrices tested in (2) are over the original complex group ring.

## Rewriting the actual inverses

List the finitely many elements \(u_1,\ldots,u_l\in B\cap U^\times\) whose inverses occur as generators in a chosen expression in the next layer.
Take \(p=u_1\cdots u_l\).
Associativity and the specified order give
\[
          u_i^{-1}=(u_{i+1}\cdots u_l)\,p^{-1}\,(u_1\cdots u_{i-1}).
\]
For the empty list use \(p=1\).
In particular \(B[u^{-1}],B[v^{-1}]\subseteq B[(uv)^{-1}]\), proving directedness and the union assertion.
Rewriting each ordinary coefficient \(a\) as \(p^{-1}(pa)\) and \(p^{-1}\) as \(p^{-1}1\) gives the description using normalized ratios.
No commutation of \(p\) with the numerator coefficients has been used.
Under \(P_k(G)\), every complex linear combination of the normalized ratios and \(1=p^{-1}p\) is zero or a unit, since its numerator is again in \(B\).
Their nonlinear combinations are the additional issue.

## Chain realization and rank

Distribute products in the finite expression and collect the terms without an inverse into \(d\).
Each remaining word is
\[
                     v_0p^{-1}v_1p^{-1}\cdots p^{-1}v_h,\quad h\ge1 .
\]
For this word take a chain of \(h\) states and put \(v_i\) in position \((i,i+1)\) of \(N\) for \(1\le i<h\).
Write \(P=pI_h\).
Since \(P^{-1}N\) is strictly upper triangular, one has the ordered finite identity
\[
 (P-N)^{-1}
       =\left(\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}(P^{-1}N)^j\right)P^{-1}.
\]
The \((1,h)\) entry equals \(p^{-1}v_1p^{-1}\cdots v_{h-1}p^{-1}\), including the single-state case.
Place \(-v_0\) at the first position of the border row, and \(v_h\) at the last position of the column.
The signed Schur term of this chain contributes the prescribed word.
Taking the direct sum of the chains gives (1) with precisely the indicated number of states.
The same finite inverse formula for any strictly upper triangular \(N\) proves the converse inclusion.
Multiplying the bordered matrix by invertible triangular affiliated matrices isolates \(L\) and \(d-cL^{-1}b\), as in [the affiliated elimination identity](../ledger/Yin_L_A0009_v1.md#the-exact-size-reducing-step).
The first term has rank \(q\); the second has scalar rank in \([0,1]\).
Such a scalar is zero or a unit exactly at the two endpoints, proving (2) in both directions.
This is a restricted realization at a fixed layer of the [general polynomial realization](Yin_C_A0012_v1.md#rational-expressions-and-explicit-polynomial-schur-realizations).
The expression can require many occurrences in this construction even at depth one.
Its repeated denominator has not supplied a proof of (2) for general chain lengths.
