# Full regular rank from a two-dimensional unitary deformation

## Statement

Let \(G=G_L*_H G_R\) be a discrete amalgamated product.
Suppose there are unitary representations \(\pi_j:G_j\to U(2)\) whose restrictions to \(H\) agree and are diagonal in the same orthonormal basis.
For \(c=\sum_g c_g g\in\mathbb C G_j\) put
\[
 T_j(c)_{kl}=\sum_g c_g\,\pi_j(g)_{kl}\lambda_G(g)\in\mathcal N(G).
\]
Here \(\lambda_G\) denotes left regular convolution, and all ranks use the canonical group trace and the unnormalized matrix trace.
Choose \(p_L,u\in\mathbb C G_L\), \(p_R,v\in\mathbb C G_R\), and set
\[
 p=p_L+p_R,\qquad M=\begin{pmatrix}p&u\\v&-p\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad L=T_L(p_L),\quad R=T_R(p_R).
\]
If the scalar operators \(L_{12}\) and \(R_{21}\) both have regular rank one, then
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_G(p)=1,\qquad \operatorname{rk}_G(M)=2,\qquad
 \operatorname{rk}_G\bigl(1+(p^{-1}v)(p^{-1}u)\bigr)=1.
\]
The inverse and products in the last formula are taken in the algebra \(\mathcal U(G)\) of affiliated operators.
The hypotheses use only \(p_L,p_R\) and the two representations, so the conclusion holds simultaneously for all the indicated \(u,v\).
This gives the full-rank endpoint of the [three-coefficient Schur problem](Yin_C_A0116_v1.md#statement) on this class of inputs without a scalar integrality assumption on \(G\).

The same criterion holds when \(G\) is generated by the two indicated subgroups with common subgroup \(H\), provided the deformed vertex representations below extend to representations of \(G\) for infinitely many distinct unit phases.
An infinite cyclic edge already gives examples with three independent coefficients, finite abelianization and an infinite ordinary rank defect for the syllable projection.
A torsion-free quotient retains the two vertex groups while a mixed commutation relation reduces the admissible phases to exactly two.

## Regular-trace identity and the specialization

For \(|t|=1\), put \(D_t=\operatorname{diag}(t,1)\).
The vertex maps \(\pi_L\) and \(D_t\pi_RD_t^{-1}\) agree on \(H\), hence define a unitary representation \(\pi_t\) of the amalgamated product.
In the more general setting use only phases for which this extension exists.
The twisted left translations \(\lambda_G(g)\otimes\pi_t(g)\) are intertwined with \(\lambda_G(g)\otimes I_2\) by the unitary
\[
 W_t(\delta_h\otimes w)=\delta_h\otimes\pi_t(h)w,\qquad
 W_t(\lambda_G(g)\otimes I_2)W_t^*=\lambda_G(g)\otimes\pi_t(g).
\]
For a rectangular group-ring matrix \(B\), apply this intertwiner on each input and output copy.
The closed range projection of the twist \(B_t\) is the output conjugate of the amplified closed range projection of \(B\).
Since \(W_t\) fixes both auxiliary basis vectors over \(h=e\), summing the diagonal entries of this projection at the identity gives
\[
                 \operatorname{rk}_G B_t=2\operatorname{rk}_G B.
                 \tag{1}
\]

Write \(U=T_L(u)\), \(V=T_R(v)\) and \(A=L_{11}+R_{11}\).
In every right-vertex block the deformation multiplies its \(12\) entry by \(t\), its \(21\) entry by \(t^{-1}\), and preserves its diagonal.
Use input order \((x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2)\) for \(M_t\), with auxiliary coordinates \(1,2\) inside each ordinary coordinate.
Multiply the second and fourth output rows by \(t\), then order the rows as \(2,4,3,1\) and the columns as \(x_1,y_1,y_2,x_2\).
The result agrees on admissible phases with a matrix polynomial \(F(t)\) of degree at most one with coefficients in \(\mathcal N(G)\).
Indeed the terms with negative exponent occurred only in the second auxiliary rows, whose other entries have exponent zero.
At \(t=0\) these operations give
\[
 F(0)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 R_{21}&0&0&0\\
 V_{21}&-R_{21}&0&0\\
 V_{11}&-A&-L_{12}&0\\
 A&U_{11}&U_{12}&L_{12}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{2}
\]
For the scalar \(p_t\), multiply its second row by \(t\) and swap the rows to obtain a polynomial \(K(t)\) of degree at most one with
\[
                       K(0)=\begin{pmatrix}R_{21}&0\\ A&L_{12}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The full-rank scalar diagonal entries are injective by faithfulness of the trace and equality of initial and final support traces.
Solving the homogeneous equations successively gives \(\operatorname{rk}_G F(0)=4\) and \(\operatorname{rk}_G K(0)=2\).

For either polynomial \(Q\), apply the [Vandermonde rank bound](Yin_C_A0022_v1.md#simultaneous-generic-rank-and-degeneration) at \(0\) to any \(N>1\) distinct admissible unit phases \(t_j\).
It gives \((N-1)\operatorname{rk}_G Q(0)\le\sum_{j=1}^N\operatorname{rk}_G Q(t_j)\).
Row rescaling and permutations are invertible on these phases, so (1) gives \(2N\operatorname{rk}_G M\) and \(2N\operatorname{rk}_G p\) for the respective sums.
Sending \(N\) to infinity proves both full-rank assertions.
In the affiliated algebra a scalar of full regular rank is a unit.
Eliminating the upper left pivot \(p\) in \(M\) leaves \(-p-vp^{-1}u=-p[1+(p^{-1}v)(p^{-1}u)]\), which must have rank one.

## A double over an infinite cyclic subgroup

Take two copies \(P_j=\langle a_j,b\mid a_j^{-1}b^2a_j=b^{-2},\ b^{-1}a_j^2 b=a_j^{-2}\rangle\) of the Promislow group and identify the subgroups \(H=\langle b\rangle\).
Each vertex group is torsion-free with a normal \(\mathbb Z^3\) of index four generated by \(a_j^2,b^2,(a_jb)^2\), as in [Gardam, Section 3.1](../literature/pdf/Gar21--arxiv-v4.pdf#page=4).
Thus \(H\) is infinite cyclic and \(a_j\notin H\), since their cosets in the four-element quotient are distinct nontrivial elements.
In \(G=P_1*_H P_2\), the amalgam normal form embeds both vertices with intersection exactly \(H\).
Every finite-order element fixes a vertex in the Bass–Serre tree, since its finite orbit spans a finite tree and the action preserves the two vertex types.
Vertex stabilizers are conjugates of the torsion-free \(P_j\), so \(G\) is torsion-free.
Abelianizing the presentation gives \(G_{\rm ab}\cong(C_4)^3\), generated freely modulo fourth powers by \(a_1,a_2,b\).
In particular every real additive character vanishes, and neither vertex has a retraction onto \(H\), since its own abelianization is \((C_4)^2\).

On each vertex send \(a_j\) to \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(b\) to \(D=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)\).
Here \(D^2=D^{-2}=-I\) and \(J^2=J^{-2}=I\), so both defining relations hold and the edge images agree.
Take
\[
 p_L=1-b+a_1,\quad p_R=a_2,\qquad
 u=1+b+a_1,\quad v=1+b+a_2 .
\]
The coefficients at \(a_1,a_2,1\) in \(\alpha p+\beta u+\gamma v=0\) give respectively \(\alpha+\beta=0,\ \alpha+\gamma=0,\ \alpha+\beta+\gamma=0\), proving independence.
We have \(L_{12}=\lambda_G(a_1)\) and \(R_{21}=\lambda_G(a_2)\), both unitaries.
The specialization therefore computes the exact ranks of \(p,M\) and the Schur expression above.

To see the obstruction for an ordinary finite-rank cut, let \(E\) be the coordinate projection onto group elements whose reduced amalgam word ends with a nonedge syllable from \(P_1\), excluding elements of \(H\).
The last vertex type is well-defined by normal form.
For every \(n\in\mathbb Z\), \(E\delta_{b^n}=0\), while \(a_1b^n\in P_1\setminus H\) and \(a_2b^n\in P_2\setminus H\).
Thus \([E,\lambda_G(p)]\delta_{b^n}=\delta_{a_1b^n}\), so this commutator has infinite ordinary Hilbert-space rank.

This double is already covered by [Linnell's free-by-elementary-amenable theorem](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#group-class-and-exact-theorems).
Indeed folding the two copies onto \(P\) is injective on each vertex group, so its kernel has trivial vertex stabilizers and acts freely on the Bass–Serre tree.
The kernel is free, and the quotient \(P\) is virtually abelian.
That theorem yields integer ranks for the entire double, while the deformation determines the full-rank value for the displayed family.

## A mixed relation allowing exactly two phases

In the abstract quotient formed by adding \([a_1,a_2]=1\), the original family descends precisely when \(J\) commutes with \(J_t=D_tJD_t^{-1}\).
Their products are \(\operatorname{diag}(t^{-1},t)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})\), respectively, so this happens exactly at \(t=\pm1\).
The same restriction occurs in a concrete torsion-free quotient retaining both vertex groups and three independent coefficients.
In \(P\times P\), let \(\Gamma\) be generated by
\[
       a_1=(a,a),\qquad a_2=(a,a^{-1}),\qquad b=(b,b).
\]
The assignment \(a\mapsto a^{-1},b\mapsto b\) preserves both vertex relations and is an involution, so the two vertex maps are the diagonal embedding and the graph of this automorphism.
The two images agree on \(b\) and the first projection embeds each vertex back onto \(P\), giving a quotient map \(G\to\Gamma\).
The generators \(a_1,a_2\) commute and are distinct by the infinite order of \(a\), and their common first coordinate \(a\) differs from \(1,b\).
Thus the same coefficient comparison proves independence in \(\mathbb C\Gamma\).
At \(t=1\) the original representation factors through the first projection.
Let \(\eta:P\to\mathbb Z/4\) send \(a\) to \(1\) and \(b\) to \(0\).
Multiplying that representation by the character \((g,h)\mapsto i^{\eta(g)-\eta(h)}\) fixes the images of \(a_1,b\) and negates the image of \(a_2\), giving \(t=-1\).
The commutation relation rules out every other phase.
This tests the necessity of checking relations before using (1) on a quotient.
It leaves the ranks on a quotient with too few admissible phases to other arguments and leaves the general three-coefficient Schur problem open.
