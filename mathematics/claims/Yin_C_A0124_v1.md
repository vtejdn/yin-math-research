# Detecting Schur kernels by direct sums in a free cyclic extension

## Statement

Let \(D_0(G)=\mathbb CG\), and let \(D_{j+1}(G)\) be the ring generated by \(D_j(G)\) and the inverses of its elements that are units in the affiliated algebra \(\mathcal U(G)\).
Use the regular trace normalized on one group coordinate and the unnormalized trace on matrices.
For \(S\in D_j(G)\) with \(0<\alpha=\operatorname{rk}_G S<1\), write \(P\) for its orthogonal kernel projection and \(\delta=1-\alpha\).
In the free product \(H=G*\langle t\rangle\), the same operator has the same rank and kernel projection under the canonical embedding.
If \(G\) is torsion-free then \(H\) is torsion-free.

1. If \(\alpha=p/q\) in lowest terms, then \(P\in D_{j+p}(H)\).
   For every positive integer \(m\) with \(m\delta\ge1\), the matrix \(S I_m\) factors through \(m-1\) coordinates over \(D_{j+p+1}(H)\).
   In particular, for a two-by-two group-ring matrix \(A\) with affiliated-unit top-left entry \(a\) and \(S=d-ca^{-1}b\), take \(j=1\). The same ring contains a factorization of \(A^{\oplus m}\) through \(2m-1\) coordinates.
2. If \(\alpha\) is irrational, then for every \(\eta>0\) some positive contraction \(B\) in a finite inverse layer of \(H\) satisfies \(BP=PB=P\) and \(\operatorname{rk}_H(B-P)<\eta\).
   This assertion concerns approximation in the rank metric.

The coefficient group may be taken to be the subgroup containing the group elements used by a fixed expression for \(S\) and by \(A\).
Consequently these factorizations take place inside the original group whenever it already contains the indicated free cyclic extension of that coefficient subgroup.

## Free conjugates and sums of positive operators

For a subgroup inclusion, convolution acts on each right coset as a copy of the original representation.
The resulting normal trace-preserving inclusion of group von Neumann algebras extends by spectral calculus to affiliated operators, preserves ranks and respects all the indicated inverses.
Free product normal form gives torsion-freeness when both factors are torsion-free.
It also shows that the groups \(t^{-i}Gt^i\) at distinct integer positions generate their free product, so their von Neumann algebras are freely independent for the group trace.
Write \(x=S^*S\) at position zero.

Suppose \(u,v\) are positive affiliated operators belonging to free subalgebras, of ranks \(r,s\in(0,1)\).
The kernel projection of \(u+v\) is the meet of their kernel projections, by positivity in the affiliated algebra.
Thus the [free projection intersection formula](../audits/literature/CK14/Yin_U001.md#ck14-u001) gives \(\operatorname{rk}(u+v)=\min(1,r+s)\).
When \(r+s\ge1\), \(u+v\) is an affiliated unit.
In that case the matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}u+v&u\\u&u\end{pmatrix}
\]
is a constant invertible congruence of \(\operatorname{diag}(v,u)\).
Eliminating its first diagonal entry gives the positive Schur complement
\[
             u-u(u+v)^{-1}u=u(u+v)^{-1}v
\]
of rank \(r+s-1\).
Congruence in the affiliated algebra preserves positivity and rank, so these calculations do not require bounded inverses or a spectral gap.

Start with one conjugate of \(x\) at a positive position and successively adjoin conjugates at unused positive positions.
At each step use the sum when the two ranks add to less than one and the positive Schur complement when they add to more than one.
The output belongs to the algebra affiliated to the positions already used, so its support stays free from the next support.
After \(n\) positions the resulting positive \(y_n\) has rank \(\{n\alpha\}\) and belongs to \(D_{j+\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor}(H)\) as long as no \(k\alpha\) for \(1\le k\le n\) is integral.
Indeed each passage over one adds exactly one inverse to the depth bound.

## The kernel projection and its approximants

Take \(y=y_n\) of rank \(\delta+\epsilon\ge\delta\), with \(\epsilon<\alpha\), and set \(u=x^2+y\).
The support of \(x^2\) has trace \(\alpha\) and is free from the support of \(y\), so \(u\) is an affiliated unit, including at \(\epsilon=0\).
Define
\[
 B=1-xu^{-1}x,\qquad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}u&x\\ x&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Eliminating the bottom-right entry of \(K\) gives \(y\), so \(K\) is positive and has rank \(1+\operatorname{rk}y\).
Eliminating \(u\) gives the positive \(B\) and the same rank is \(1+\operatorname{rk}B\).
Since \(xu^{-1}x\ge0\), \(B\) is a bounded positive contraction.
The identity \(xP=0\) gives \(BP=PB=P\).
Thus \(B\) is the identity on the range of \(P\), and
\[
 \operatorname{rk}(B-P)=\operatorname{rk}B-\tau(P)=\epsilon .
\]
This construction uses one further inverse after \(y_n\).

For \(\alpha=p/q\), taking \(n=q-1\) gives \(\epsilon=0\) and \(B=P\in D_{j+p}(H)\).
For irrational \(\alpha\), density of the fractional parts of \(n\alpha\) gives \(0<\epsilon<\min(\eta,\alpha)\) for any prescribed \(\eta>0\).
The resulting \(B\) lies in \(D_{j+\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor+1}(H)\).

## Removing a coordinate from the direct sum

In the rational case put \(t_i=t^{i-1}\), and form the column \(v=(Pt_1,\ldots,Pt_m)^{\mathsf T}\).
It is killed by \(SI_m\).
The positive operator \(Q=\sum_i t_i^*Pt_i\) is a sum of freely independent projections, because \(P\) belongs to the original von Neumann algebra of \(G\).
Iteration of the free projection formula gives \(\operatorname{rk}Q=\min(1,m\delta)=1\).
An invertible row operation with first row \((1,t_2^*,\ldots,t_m^*)\) and the remaining standard rows makes the first entry of \(v\) equal to \(Q\).
Scale by \(Q^{-1}\) and clear the lower entries to obtain an invertible matrix \(W\) over \(D_{j+p+1}(H)\) such that \(Wv=e_1\).
The first column of \((S I_m)W^{-1}\) vanishes, giving the asserted factorization.
Elementary Schur elimination makes each copy of \(A\) equivalent to \(\operatorname{diag}(1,S)\) over the layer containing \(a^{-1}\), which lifts this compression to \(A^{\oplus m}\).

For [A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract), the exact rational-trace kernel projection constructed above lies in \(D_{j+p}(H)\cap\mathcal N(G)\).
Membership in \(D_\infty(G)\) and exclusion of such projections using torsion-freeness remain open here.
The group retraction sending \(t\) to \(1\) kills \(t-1\), which has an affiliated inverse upstairs, so it does not specialize all the inverses in the extension.
For irrational trace the rank-metric estimate leaves open whether any single finite layer attains zero error or detects the amplified rank drop.
