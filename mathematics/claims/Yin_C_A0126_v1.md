# A half-dimensional Schur kernel for a nonregular trace on a free group ring

## Statement

There is a faithful unitary representation of the torsion-free group \(F_3\) in a finite tracial von Neumann algebra with the following properties.
Every nonzero element of \(\mathbb C F_3\) evaluates to an injective operator.
A two-by-two matrix over this same group ring has an affiliated-unit first pivot and a nonzero Schur complement \(R\) whose orthogonal kernel projection \(e\) has trace \(1/2\).
Write \(D_0\) for the represented group ring and let \(D_{j+1}\) be generated by \(D_j\) and its elements' inverses whenever they are units of the ambient affiliated algebra.
The union \(D=\bigcup_jD_j\) embeds as a ring into a division ring and \(e\notin D\).
On adjoining a free Haar unitary, the same projection belongs to the second scalar inverse layer of the enlarged coefficient algebra.
The trace used for this representation differs from the regular trace of \(F_3\).

## Cayley generators and scalar injectivity

Use the [MSY23 semicircular example](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U005.md#example-524-integral-rank-need-not-preserve-free-field-rank) and its [two-summand comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#why-infinite-orbits-and-scalar-injectivity-do-not-finish-the-argument).
Let \(s,t\) be a standard free semicircular pair in its tracial von Neumann algebra \(M_Y\), and \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) a standard free semicircular triple in \(M_Z\).
Set
\[
 Y=(t^2,tst,ts^2t),\qquad Z=(z_1,z_2,z_3),\qquad X_i=Y_i\oplus Z_i .
\]
The trace on \(M_Y\oplus M_Z\) gives weight \(1/2\) to each summand.
Set \(\phi(v)=(1+iv)(1-iv)^{-1}\).
Sending the free generators \(u_i\) to the unitaries \(U_i=\phi(X_i)\) defines a group representation.
We use the free-field realization of each free semicircular family and its equality of polynomial matrix rank with free polynomial inner rank, as in the [semicircular comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#why-infinite-orbits-and-scalar-injectivity-do-not-finish-the-argument) and the [free-field rank criterion](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U004.md#theorem-56-exact-equivalences).

For any nonzero \(b\in\mathbb CF_3\), the formal power series \(b(\phi(v_1),\phi(v_2),\phi(v_3))\) in free noncommuting indeterminates is nonzero.
To see this, write every supported group element as a reduced product \(u_{i_1}^{a_1}\cdots u_{i_k}^{a_k}\) of nonzero powers, with adjacent indices distinct.
Choose a maximal syllable length and an index sequence attaining it with a nonzero coefficient.
A constant is immediate if this length is zero.
Extract the series terms with exactly this sequence of nonempty runs of variables.
Only the supported group words with that index sequence contribute.
For each such word the extracted coefficients form the tensor product of the one-variable coefficient lists of \(\phi(v)^{a_j}-1\).
These one-variable series are linearly independent as \(a_j\) varies over any finite set of distinct nonzero integers, because a nonconstant rational parameter admits no nontrivial complex Laurent-polynomial identity.
Finitely many coefficient evaluations distinguish any given finite-dimensional span, so the tensors of these independent coefficient lists remain independent.
The selected part of the series is therefore nonzero.

The automorphism of the free field that scales \(t\) by a nonzero real \(\lambda\) and fixes \(s\) scales all three \(Y_i\) by \(\lambda^2\).
If the rational evaluation of \(b\) at \(\phi(Y_i)\) vanished there, it would vanish for all these scalings.
For sufficiently small \(\lambda\) the defining expressions, including inverses of the Cayley generators, have norm-convergent power series.
Their first nonzero homogeneous formal coefficient remains nonzero on evaluation at \(Y\), because the monomials in \(Y\) are distinct concatenations of the uniquely decodable blocks \(t s^j t\), \(0\le j\le2\), and \(s,t\) are algebraically free.
The coefficient is the corresponding Taylor coefficient in \(\lambda\), a contradiction.
Scaling all \(z_i\) by \(\lambda\) gives the same nonvanishing for \(Z\).
In both semicircular free fields the resulting nonzero element is an affiliated unit.
Consequently the represented \(b\) is injective in the two-summand representation, and in particular the group and group-ring representations are faithful.

The original group generator \(u_1\) has regular trace zero.
Here \(\operatorname{Im}\phi(t^2)=2t^2(1+t^4)^{-1}\) has strictly positive trace, whereas \(\operatorname{Im}\phi(z_1)\) has zero trace by symmetry.
Hence the imaginary part of the representing trace of \(u_1\) is strictly positive.

## A matrix over the group ring and its half kernel

Let \(L_i=1+U_i\), which has bounded inverse \((1-iX_i)/2\).
Since \(iX_iL_i=U_i-1\), the matrix
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}L_1&0\\0&L_3\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}X_1&X_2\\X_2&X_3\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}L_2&0\\0&L_2\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac1i\begin{pmatrix}
 (U_1-1)L_2&L_1(U_2-1)\\ L_3(U_2-1)&(U_3-1)L_2
 \end{pmatrix}
\]
has entries in the represented group ring.
The first pivot is an affiliated unit since \(X_1\) is one in each summand.
With \(r=X_3-X_2X_1^{-1}X_2\), the Schur complement of \(A\) is \(R=L_3rL_2\).
Indeed these factors occur on the two outside edges of both terms in the complement, while the inverse pivot cancels the other diagonal multipliers.
In the \(Y\)-summand the identity \(Y_2Y_1^{-1}Y_2=Y_3\) holds in the affiliated algebra.
In the \(Z\)-summand the polynomial matrix with rows \((z_1,z_2),(z_2,z_3)\) has rank two, since the formal matrix evaluates to \(I_2\) at \((1,0,1)\) and is therefore full.
The free-field rank formula at \(Z\) now makes its Schur complement an affiliated unit.
Thus \(R\) is zero in the first summand and a unit in the second, and \(e=1\oplus0\) is its orthogonal kernel projection.

## Why the kernel projection fails to descend

The union \(D\) is also the actual scalar inverse closure of the polynomial algebra in the \(X_i\).
Indeed the Cayley transform and \(X_i=(U_i-1)(1+U_i)^{-1}/i\) express each generating family by legal rational operations on the other.
Every member of \(D\) has a single rational expression in the \(X_i\) whose inverses are legal in both summands.
For any such expression \(f\), take a common polynomial-matrix representation \(f=uL^{-1}v\) with \(L(Y),L(Z)\) both affiliated units.
One can construct it recursively with polynomial row and column vectors.
For polynomials take \(L=1\).
Additions use diagonal sums of the two pivots, and products use the upper triangular pivot with diagonal \(L_1,L_2\) and off-diagonal \(-v_1u_2\).
Inverting a legal \(f\) uses the bordered pivot \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}L&v\\u&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), whose inverse has bottom-right entry \(-f^{-1}\).
These operations retain both evaluation domains.

Let \(n\) be the size of \(L\) and \(Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}L&v\\u&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
Schur elimination gives
\[
 n+\operatorname{rk}f(Y)=\operatorname{rk}Q(Y)
 \le \rho_{\mathbb C\langle v_1,v_2,v_3\rangle}(Q)
 =\operatorname{rk}Q(Z)=n+\operatorname{rk}f(Z).
\]
Evaluating every formal polynomial factorization at \(Y\) gives the inequality, and the free-field realization of \(Z\) gives the equality there.
In particular, \(f(Z)=0\) forces \(f(Y)=0\).
Projection to the \(Z\)-summand thus embeds \(D\) into the realized free skew field on \(Z\).
Since \(e\) is nonzero and has zero \(Z\)-summand, it lies outside \(D\).

In a tracial free product adjoin a Haar unitary \(w\) free from the original coefficient algebra, and start the enlarged inverse layers at the algebra generated by the represented group ring and \(w,w^*\).
With \(x=R^*R\) and \(y=w^*xw\), the supports of \(x^2\) and \(y\) are free of trace \(1/2\).
The [two Schur eliminations](Yin_C_A0124_v1.md#the-kernel-projection-and-its-approximants) give \(e=1-x(x^2+y)^{-1}x\), with the inverse legitimate in the enlarged affiliated algebra.
Here \(R\) belongs to the first enlarged layer, so the displayed projection is in the second.
This example isolates the need for information specific to the regular trace in the [Atiyah question](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract).
