# Scalar right annihilators and regularity of the division closure

## Statement

Let \(U\) be the ring of operators affiliated to a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal finite trace.
Let \(D\subseteq U\) be a unital division-closed \(*\)-subring, so the inverse in \(U\) of any \(D\)-element that is an affiliated unit belongs to \(D\).
For \(x\in D\), write \(\operatorname{ann}_D(x)=\{d\in D:xd=0\}\), a right ideal, and let \(p_x\) be the orthogonal projection onto the Hilbert space kernel of \(x\).
Positivity refers to affiliated operators and includes unbounded operators.
The following conditions are equivalent.

1. \(D\) is von Neumann regular.
2. Every positive nonunit \(c\in D\) has \(\operatorname{ann}_D(c)\ne0\), and \(\operatorname{ann}_D(x)\) is finitely generated as a right ideal for every \(x\in D\).
3. Every positive nonunit \(c\in D\) has \(\operatorname{ann}_D(c)\ne0\), and \(D\) is right coherent.

The positive nonunit test in conditions 2 and 3 is equivalent to requiring a nonzero right annihilator for every nonunit in \(D\).
For a finitely generated amenable group \(G\), take \(D=D_G=\operatorname{Div}_U(\mathbb CG)\) in \(U=L^0(\mathcal N(G))\).
The positive nonunit test then holds automatically.
Consequently the unrestricted finite-rank equation in [A0003](../tasks/Yin_T_A0003_v3.md#contract) characterizes \(D_G\) exactly when every scalar right annihilator in \(D_G\) is finitely generated, or equivalently when \(D_G\) is right coherent.
This statement applies to the torsion-free amenable case without a division-ring hypothesis.

## Argument

### Detection of a nonunit

By division closure, a member of \(D\) is a unit of \(D\) exactly when it is a unit of \(U\).
Finiteness of the von Neumann algebra makes an affiliated operator a unit exactly when its kernel projection is zero.
The positive operator \(x^*x\) has the same kernel projection as \(x\).
The involution on \(U\) is proper, and more generally \(\sum_i v_i^*v_i=0\) implies \(v_i=0\) for each \(i\), by positivity and polar decomposition.
In particular \(x^*xz=0\) implies \(xz=0\), since \((xz)^*(xz)=0\).
Applying the positive nonunit test to \(x^*x\) therefore gives the test for every nonunit.

Suppose \(D\) is regular and \(x\in D\).
Choose \(b\in D\) with \(xbx=x\).
Then \(e=1-bx\) is idempotent and \(\operatorname{ann}_D(x)=eD\), because \(xe=0\) and \(ez=z\) whenever \(xz=0\).
If \(e=0\), then \(bx=1\) forces \(p_x=0\), and \(x\) is a unit.
Thus every nonunit has a nonzero right annihilator.
Regularity is preserved by matrix rings, so every finite rectangular matrix \(B\) over \(D\) has a rectangular inner inverse \(C\) with \(BCB=B\), by putting \(B\) in a square off-diagonal block and taking the corresponding block of an inner inverse.
The kernel of \(B:D^n\to D^m\) is \((1-CB)D^n\), generated by the columns of \(1-CB\).
This is the finite-free kernel characterization of right coherence and proves condition 3.
Taking scalar matrices shows that condition 3 implies condition 2.

### Recovering a kernel projection from generators

Assume condition 2 and choose \(y_1,\ldots,y_r\in D\) generating \(\operatorname{ann}_D(x)\) on the right.
Allow the family to be empty for a zero annihilator.
Set
\[
        Y=\sum_{i=1}^r y_i y_i^*,\qquad S=x^*x+Y\in D.
\]
If the positive element \(S\) were a nonunit, take \(0\ne z\in D\) with \(Sz=0\).
The equality
\[
       0=z^*Sz=(xz)^*(xz)+\sum_i(y_i^*z)^*(y_i^*z)
\]
gives \(xz=0\) and \(y_i^*z=0\) for every \(i\).
As \(xz=0\), write \(z=\sum_i y_i d_i\) with \(d_i\in D\).
Then \(z^*z=\sum_i d_i^*y_i^*z=0\), contradicting \(z\ne0\).
It follows that \(S^{-1}\in D\).

Since \(xy_i=0\), the left support of \(y_i\) is dominated by \(p_x\).
Thus \(Y\) is supported on \(p_x\) and \(x^*x\) is supported on \(1-p_x\).
In particular \(p_x S=Y\), which gives the bounded projection \(p_x=YS^{-1}\in D\) by multiplication in \(U\).
The element \(x^*x+p_x\) is an affiliated unit, acting as the identity on \(p_x\) and injectively on \(1-p_x\).
The scalar inverse operations of \(D\) therefore give \(b=(x^*x+p_x)^{-1}x^*\in D\).
The identity \(bx=1-p_x\) implies \(xbx=x\), proving regularity.
This argument applies even when the nonzero spectra of the positive operators accumulate at zero.

### The amenable case

Let \(x\in D_G\) be a nonunit.
Its Hilbert space kernel has positive von Neumann dimension.
The matrix rational closure used in [complete amenable kernel localization](Yin_C_A0001_v2.md#rational-kernel-localization) contains \(D_G\), since it is a subring containing the group algebra and closed under inverses of affiliated units.
The cited result applies to unbounded rational operators and implies that \(\ker x\cap\mathbb CG\), identifying \(a\) with \(a\delta_e\), is dense in \(\ker x\).
Choose a nonzero vector \(a\delta_e\) in that intersection.
Then \(p_xa\delta_e=a\delta_e\), hence \(p_x a=a\) because the trace vector separates bounded group von Neumann algebra elements.
It follows that \(xa=xp_xa=0\) in \(U\), so \(\operatorname{ann}_{D_G}(x)\) contains the nonzero group-ring element \(a\).

Regularity implies \(p_x\in D_G\) by the preceding equivalence and its projection formula.
Conversely membership of every such projection gives the inner inverse above.
The [unrestricted equation criterion](Yin_C_A0131_v1.md#which-rationality-is-detected) now gives the claimed equivalences for \(G\).
Right coherence of the original group algebra gives a separate [sufficient condition via finitely supported kernel generators](../ledger/Yin_L_A0076_v1.md#amenable-groups-with-a-right-coherent-complex-group-algebra).
