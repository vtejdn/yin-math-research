# Two-column kernel projections for traces agreeing with the regular trace on a finite ball

## Statement

Let \(\Gamma=F(a,b,x_1,x_2,x_3)\) have its standard symmetric word metric, and write \(\tau_{\mathrm{reg}}\) for its regular trace.
For every integer \(r\ge1\) there is a unitary representation \(\pi_r\) of \(\Gamma\) in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\) such that the following assertions hold.

1. The trace \(\tau\circ\pi_r\) agrees exactly with \(\tau_{\mathrm{reg}}\) on the ball of radius \(r\), on each subgroup generated by two of the five displayed generators, and on \(F(b,x_1,x_2,x_3)\).
2. Every nonzero member of \(\mathbb C\Gamma\) acts injectively, so every one-column matrix over the represented group algebra has kernel projection zero or one.
3. Write \(D_r\) for the scalar division closure of \(\pi_r(\mathbb C\Gamma)\) in the affiliated algebra.
   There is a matrix \(A_r\in M_2(\mathbb C\Gamma)\) whose kernel projection after evaluation has lower right entry outside \(D_r\).
   The evaluated matrix has nullity \(1/2\) for the unnormalized matrix trace.
   Its scalar Schur complement belongs to \(D_r\) and its kernel projection lies outside \(D_r\).

The group and marking are fixed, and the witnessing matrix is allowed to depend on \(r\).
The traces converge pointwise on the group algebra to \(\tau_{\mathrm{reg}}\).
Thus even with the marginal agreements in assertion 1, finitely many joint moments and scalar injectivity do not suffice to derive rationality of two-column kernel projections for general tracial representations.
The [deletion question for \(D_G\)](../tasks/Yin_T_A0003_v3.md#contract) concerns the regular trace itself.

## Argument

### A representation on two summands

Use the free semicircular pair \(s,t\) and the free semicircular triple \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) from the [two-summand construction](Yin_C_A0126_v1.md#cayley-generators-and-scalar-injectivity).
Set
\[
  Y=(t^2,tst,ts^2t),\qquad Z=(z_1,z_2,z_3),\qquad
  \phi(v)=(1+iv)(1-iv)^{-1}.
\]
For each of \(M_Y=W^*(s,t)\) and \(M_Z=W^*(Z)\), take its reduced tracial free product with \(\mathcal N(F(a,b))\).
Denote the two resulting algebras by \(N_Y,N_Z\), and equip \(N=N_Y\oplus N_Z\) with the average of their tracial states.
Define \(\pi\) on the \(x_i\) by \(\phi(Y_i)\oplus\phi(Z_i)\) and on \(a,b\) by the corresponding free Haar generators in both summands.
We use \(a,b\) also for the Haar unitaries in the individual summands.

The mixed tuples \((s,t,a,b)\) and \((z_1,z_2,z_3,a,b)\) each consist of freely independent atomless normal operators.
Every unstarred polynomial matrix evaluated on either tuple has the same affiliated rank as its formal free polynomial inner rank.
Indeed, Theorem 4.1 of Arizmendi, Cébron, Speicher and Yin, with the common atomic denominator \(n=1\), assigns the same matrix rank to any two free normal tuples of the same length with atomless marginals.
Taking a comparison tuple of free semicirculars gives the stated inner rank by the free-field realization theorem.
This equality for all square polynomial matrices also identifies the rational algebra of each mixed tuple in its affiliated ring with the free field, by Theorem 5.6 of Mai, Speicher and Yin. [Atomless case of the common matrix model](../audits/literature/ACSY24/Yin_U002.md#2-theorem-41-a-common-denominator-matrix-model) · [Free-field rank criterion](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U004.md#theorem-56-exact-equivalences)

In either summand the group ring on \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) embeds by the [Cayley specialization argument](Yin_C_A0126_v1.md#cayley-generators-and-scalar-injectivity), and the ring on \(a,b\) embeds by its regular representation.
Their algebraic free product embeds in the reduced tracial free product.
Indeed the centered alternating tensor spaces for these two subalgebras embed in the corresponding Hilbert tensor products of the trace-zero \(L^2\) spaces, because the factor traces are faithful.
Acting on the trace vector identifies different alternating patterns with orthogonal summands of the free product Hilbert space.
This gives injectivity on the direct sum of the reduced tensor spaces and the constants, which is the algebraic free product.
The group ring \(\mathbb C\Gamma\) is this algebraic free product.
Its images belong to the respective mixed-tuple free fields, since the Cayley generators and their inverses are scalar rational expressions and \(a^{-1},b^{-1}\) are legal there.
Every nonzero group-ring element therefore gives an affiliated unit in each summand.
A nonzero one-column matrix contains an injective entry, proving assertion 2 for \(\pi\).

### The generic summand detects vanishing in the division closure

Write \(D=\operatorname{Div}_{L^0(N)}\pi(\mathbb C\Gamma)\), \(W_Y=(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3,a,b)\), and \(W_Z=(z_1,z_2,z_3,a,b)\).
Every element of \(D\) is the value of one scalar rational expression \(f\) in five unstarred variables, admissible at both tuples.
This follows recursively from the definition of \(D\), writing the inverses of the unitary generators by their rational formulas.
For such an expression choose a polynomial matrix \(L\), a polynomial row \(u\), and a polynomial column \(v\) with \(f=uL^{-1}v\), where \(L(W_Y)\) and \(L(W_Z)\) are affiliated units.
The construction starts with a scalar unit pivot for a polynomial, uses block diagonal sums and block triangular products, and for an inverse borders the current pivot by \(v,u,0\).
The last pivot is invertible at both evaluations exactly when the scalar being inverted is a unit there. [Domain-preserving construction](Yin_C_A0126_v1.md#why-the-kernel-projection-fails-to-descend)

Put \(m=\operatorname{size}L\), \(Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix} L&v\\u&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), and let \(\rho(Q)\) be its inner rank over the free polynomial algebra on the five variables.
Schur elimination at the two legal pivots gives
\[
  m+\operatorname{rk}f(W_Y)=\operatorname{rk}Q(W_Y)
       \ \le\ \rho(Q)
       \ =\ \operatorname{rk}Q(W_Z)=m+\operatorname{rk}f(W_Z).
\]
For the inequality, a polynomial factorization through \(\rho(Q)\) columns evaluates at \(W_Y\) and bounds the affiliated rank there.
The equality uses the polynomial matrix rank formula for the generic mixed tuple \(W_Z\).
Since rank is faithful in each summand, \(f(W_Z)=0\) implies \(f(W_Y)=0\).
The coordinate map onto the second summand of \(L^0(N)\) therefore embeds \(D\) into the free field realized by \(W_Z\).

Use the group-ring matrix \(A\) on \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) of the [two-by-two Schur computation](Yin_C_A0126_v1.md#a-matrix-over-the-group-ring-and-its-half-kernel).
Its first pivot is an affiliated unit in both summands and its scalar Schur complement is zero at \(Y\) and a unit at \(Z\).
These relations persist in \(L^0(N_Y)\) and \(L^0(N_Z)\), since each normal trace-preserving factor inclusion carries affiliated inverses to affiliated inverses.
The scalar Schur complement belongs to \(D\) by the legal pivot inversion, while its kernel projection \(1\oplus0\) has zero second coordinate and lies outside \(D\).
Schur elimination gives respective nullities one and zero and therefore average nullity \(1/2\).
Let \(P=p_{\ker\pi(A)}\), where \(\pi\) acts entrywise on \(A\).
Its \(Z\)-summand vanishes.
If the lower right corner of its \(Y\)-summand also vanished, positivity of the projection would force its second row and column to vanish.
Its range would then be confined to the first input coordinate, where the injective first pivot makes the kernel zero.
This contradicts nullity one in \(N_Y\).
Thus \(P_{22}\) is nonzero with zero \(Z\)-summand and lies outside \(D\).

### Agreement with the regular trace

Let \(\psi:\Gamma\to F(a,b)\) fix \(a,b\) and kill the \(x_i\).
In both summands every group word \(g\) with \(\psi(g)\ne1\) has trace zero.
To see this, grade the algebraic free product of the two group algebras by \(F(a,b)\), assigning degree one to the entire first factor on the \(x_i\) and degree \(h\) to a group element \(h\) of the second factor.
The first factor splits into constants and centered elements of degree one, while the centered space of the second is spanned by the group elements \(h\ne1\).
Thus the centered alternating word decomposition respects this grading, with the empty word in degree one.
The free product trace vanishes on every nonempty centered alternating word, giving the assertion in every nonidentity degree.

Write \(b_p=a^pba^{-p}\) for all \(p\in\mathbb Z\), and put \(m_i=i(2r+1)\) for \(i=1,2,3\).
Define an automorphism \(\alpha_r\) of \(\Gamma\) by fixing \(a,b\) and sending \(x_i\) to \(x_i b_{m_i}\).
Sending \(x_i\) to \(x_i b_{m_i}^{-1}\) gives its inverse.
We check that \(\psi\alpha_r\) kills no nonidentity word of length at most \(r\).
The total \(a\)-exponent is preserved, so it suffices to take a word of exponent zero.
The kernel of the \(a\)-exponent in \(\Gamma\) is freely generated by \(b_p\) and \(x_{i,p}=a^p x_i a^{-p}\), for all \(p\in\mathbb Z\).
Indeed collecting powers of \(a\) expresses every element of exponent zero in these letters, and expanding any nonempty reduced product leaves a nonempty word after free reduction.
In the expansion, consecutive letters with different indices are separated by a nonzero power of \(a\), and each run at one index is a reduced word in \(b,x_1,x_2,x_3\).
Collecting a word of length at most \(r\) uses only indices \(|p|\le r\), since these indices are prefix exponents.
Under \(\psi\alpha_r\) the basis elements \(b_p,x_{i,p}\) map respectively to \(b_p,b_{p+m_i}\).
The four index intervals with centers \(0,m_1,m_2,m_3\) and radius \(r\) are disjoint.
The images are therefore distinct members of the free basis \((b_p)_{p\in\mathbb Z}\) of the exponent kernel in \(F(a,b)\).
The resulting reduced word is nonempty.

The four elements \(b,x_1,x_2,x_3\) also have images at four distinct indices, proving injectivity of \(\psi\alpha_r\) on their entire subgroup.
For a pair containing \(a\), its images are \(a,b_p\) for some \(p\), which form a free basis of \(F(a,b)\) by simultaneous conjugation by \(a^p\).
Taking \(\pi_r=\pi\circ\alpha_r\) now gives the claimed trace agreements.
The trace is nonregular since \(\alpha_r^{-1}(x_1)\) has the strictly positive imaginary trace computed in the [original Cayley model](Yin_C_A0126_v1.md#cayley-generators-and-scalar-injectivity).
The automorphism preserves the entire represented algebra, so \(D_r=D\) and scalar injectivity persists.
For \(A_r=\alpha_r^{-1}(A)\), applied entrywise, the evaluated matrix and its kernel projection equal the ones above.
