# Surjectivity of Laurent evaluation and the complex strong Atiyah conjecture

## Statement

For a finitely generated discrete group \(G\), put \(A_G=\mathbb CG\), \(U_G=L^0(\mathcal N(G))\), and \(D_G=\operatorname{Div}_{U_G}(A_G)\).
Let \(S_G=\operatorname{Div}_{U_G((z))}(A_G[z])\) for one central formal variable \(z\), and let \(Z\) denote the positive generator of the second factor of \(G\times\mathbb Z\).
The [injective evaluation homomorphism](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#statement) \(\Phi_G:S_G\longrightarrow D_{G\times\mathbb Z}\) sends \(z\) to \(Z\).

1. For fixed \(G\), the map \(\Phi_G\) is surjective if and only if, for every \(n\ge1\) and \(X\in M_n(D_G)\), there exists \(m\ge0\) such that
   \[
                   \ker_{U_G} X^m=\ker_{U_G}X^{m+1}.
   \]
   These are kernels on right \(U_G\)-module columns, with \(X^0=I_n\).
2. Surjectivity of \(\Phi_G\) for every finitely generated torsion-free \(G\) is equivalent to the complex strong Atiyah conjecture for every such group.
   In fact, if \(D_K\) fails to be a division ring for one finitely generated torsion-free group \(K\), then \(G=K\wr\mathbb Z\) has a bounded scalar \(v\in D_G\) with \(\operatorname{rk}_G(v^m)=r^m\) for every \(m\ge1\) and some \(0<r<1\).
   The scalar \((Z-v)^{-1}\) belongs to \(D_{G\times\mathbb Z}\) and lies outside the image of \(\Phi_G\).

The [finite-rank deletion question](../tasks/Yin_T_A0003_v3.md#contract) asks whether regular formal germs have their constant coefficients in \(D_G\).
The surjectivity assertion in part 2 asks whether every element of the division closure after adjoining \(Z\) has a formal Laurent representative.

## Argument

### Resolvents of matrices with affiliated entries

Fix \(n\) and \(X\in M_n(D_G)\).
The polynomial \(sI_n-X\) has full affiliated rank off a countable subset of \(\mathbb C\).
Indeed \(I_n-s^{-1}X\) tends to \(I_n\) in measure as \(|s|\to\infty\), so its ranks have supremum \(n\).
The [generic rank estimate for affiliated coefficients](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#full-rank-away-from-a-countable-set) gives the assertion.
Consequently \(ZI_n-X\) is an affiliated unit by Fourier transformation in the central factor, and its inverse has entries in \(D_{G\times\mathbb Z}\) by [scalar Gram elimination](Yin_C_A0132_v1.md#argument).
Here the constant copy of \(D_G\) lies in both \(S_G\) and \(D_{G\times\mathbb Z}\) since the two inclusions of \(U_G\) preserve all its units.

The formal matrix \(zI_n-X\) has a Laurent inverse exactly when the kernels of powers of \(X\) stabilize.
We recall the [kernel splitting argument](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#resolvent-poles-and-strict-inclusion) since \(X\) here can be unbounded.
If the kernels agree at \(m,m+1\), their preimages under successive powers of \(X\) show agreement at every larger exponent.
The modules \(\operatorname{im}X^m\) and \(\ker X^m\) then intersect trivially and their dimensions sum to \(n\).
Their sum is the image of a finite matrix over \(U_G\), hence is generated by a projection, so equality of dimensions gives the whole free module.
Likewise \(\operatorname{im}X^{m+1}=\operatorname{im}X^m\) by inclusion, equality of dimensions and projection generation.
On this direct sum \(X\) is invertible on the image summand and nilpotent on the kernel summand.
The two geometric series give an inverse of \(zI_n-X\) with a finite lower bound on its exponents.
Conversely, if an inverse has no coefficient below degree \(-m\), its action on \(v_0\in\ker X^{m+1}\) is \(\sum_{j=0}^m z^{-j-1}X^jv_0\), by multiplying this finite sum by \(zI_n-X\).
The coefficient of \(z^{-m-1}\) gives \(X^mv_0=0\).

If \(\Phi_G\) is onto, lift the entries of \((ZI_n-X)^{-1}\) to \(S_G\).
Injectivity of \(\Phi_G\) carries both inverse identities back to the formal matrix, proving necessity in part 1.

### From polynomial denominators to one matrix

Suppose the kernels of powers stabilize for every square matrix over \(D_G\).
Every scalar in \(D_{G\times\mathbb Z}\) is a constant matrix coefficient of \(L(Z)^{-1}\) for some square Laurent polynomial matrix \(L\) over \(A_G\) whose evaluated matrix is an affiliated unit.
Indeed the [block realization of scalar expressions](Yin_C_A0141_v1.md#recovering-a-laurent-coefficient-from-finitely-many-equations) uses only sums, products and legal inverses and therefore applies in this affiliated ring.
Multiplying \(L(z)\) by a central power of \(z\) clears negative exponents, and that power and its inverse belong to \(S_G\).
It suffices to prove formal invertibility for the resulting polynomial \(L\).

Let \(n\) be the size of \(L\).
Fourier transformation gives a nonzero \(c\) on the unit circle for which \(L(c)\) is an affiliated unit.
Its inverse has entries in \(D_G\) by scalar Gram elimination.
Write
\[
       L(c)^{-1}L(c+w)=I+\sum_{j=1}^d B_jw^j,\qquad B_j\in M_n(D_G).
\]
The constant case is immediate.
For \(d\ge1\) let \(T\) be the \(d\)-by-\(d\) block matrix with first row \((-B_1,\ldots,-B_d)\), identity blocks immediately below the diagonal and zero elsewhere.
In \(I-wT\) the trailing \(d-1\) block rows successively express the \(j\)-th unknown in terms of \(w\) times the preceding unknown and its prescribed right-hand side.
Thus their unit lower triangular block eliminates to the Schur complement \(I+\sum_{j=1}^d B_jw^j\) on the first coordinate, including at \(w=z-c\).
For \(X=I+cT\), the full companion pencil there equals
\[
       I-(z-c)T=(1-z/c)\left(X+\frac{z}{c-z}I\right).
\]
Finite stabilization for \(X\) gives an inverse of \(uI-X\) in \(U_G((u))\) at matrix size \(dn\).
Substituting \(u=-z/(c-z)\) in that inverse is legal because this central series has order one with invertible first coefficient.
After multiplying by the appropriate scalar unit this gives a Laurent inverse of the companion pencil, hence of its Schur complement and of \(L(z)\).
The [formal scalar-pivot theorem](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#formal-inversion-by-scalar-pivots) gives \(L(z)^{-1}\in M_n(S_G)\).
Its image is \(L(Z)^{-1}\) by the inverse identities, proving sufficiency in part 1.

### Independent lamp coordinates

For torsion-free \(K\), the complex strong Atiyah conjecture for \(K\) is equivalent to \(D_K\) being a division ring. [Lück and Linnell, Theorem 2.3(3)](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#starting-references)
Indeed, over a division ring, Gaussian elimination reduces every group-ring matrix to an identity block and a zero block, giving integer affiliated rank.
Conversely, for \(x=\alpha L^{-1}\beta\in D_K\) with an invertible group-ring pivot \(L\) of size \(n\), Schur elimination gives \(\operatorname{rk}_K x=\operatorname{rk}_K\left(\begin{smallmatrix}L&\beta\\\alpha&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)-n\).
Under integer group-ring ranks every nonzero \(x\) therefore has rank one, making it an affiliated unit whose inverse belongs to \(D_K\).
If \(D_G\) is a division ring, kernels of powers of every \(n\)-by-\(n\) matrix over \(D_G\) stabilize over that division ring by dimension at most \(n\).
Row and column reduction over \(D_G\) preserves these integer ranks after extension to \(U_G\), so equality of dimensions for the nested projection-generated kernels gives stabilization over \(U_G\).
Part 1 gives surjectivity.

Now take a nonzero nonunit \(x\in D_K\), and set \(a=x^*x(1+x^*x)^{-1}\).
Division closure and the involution give \(a\in D_K\), while spectral calculus makes it a bounded positive contraction with the same kernel as \(x\).
Its rank \(r\) lies strictly between zero and one.
Let \(G=(\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb Z}K_j)\rtimes\langle t\rangle\), with the lamp coordinates indexed by \(K_j=t^{-j}K_0t^j\), and view \(a\) at position zero.
Subgroup inclusions of group von Neumann algebras preserve the trace and affiliated inverses, so \(v=ta\) is a bounded member of \(D_G\).
The group \(G\) is finitely generated by generators of \(K_0\) and \(t\), and it is torsion-free since both its base and its quotient are torsion-free.
For \(a_j=t^{-j}at^j\) one has
\[
       v^m=t^m a_{m-1}\cdots a_0,\qquad
       \operatorname{rk}_G(v^m)=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\operatorname{rk}_{K_j}(a_j)=r^m .
\]
Indeed the \(m\) factors occupy distinct lamp coordinates.
In their product group the positive product is an elementary tensor and its support is the tensor product of the supports, whose trace is the displayed product.
Embedding into \(G\) and multiplying by \(t^m\) preserve rank.
Consequently no two consecutive kernels agree.
The resolvent argument above places \((Z-v)^{-1}\) in \(D_{G\times\mathbb Z}\), and any preimage in \(S_G\) would give a formal inverse to \(z-v\), contradicting strict kernel growth.
This proves part 2.

### Constant terms at finite order

Write \(O_G=S_G\cap U_G[[z]]\), and let \(R_G\) be the \(*\)-regular closure of \(A_G\) in \(U_G\).
For each \(N\ge1\) truncation gives a ring isomorphism
\[
              O_G/z^NO_G\ \simeq\ R_G[z]/(z^N).
\]
Indeed every coefficient lies in \(R_G\) and every element of \(R_G\) occurs as a constant coefficient. [Constant-term theorem](Yin_C_A0141_v1.md#statement)
Lift any prescribed truncated polynomial one coefficient at a time, adding \(z^k f_k\) with \(f_k\in O_G\) whose constant coefficient corrects the degree \(k\) discrepancy.
The kernel of truncation is \(z^NO_G\) since \(z^{-1}\in S_G\).
The inverse limit thus identifies the \(z\)-adic completion of \(O_G\) with \(R_G[[z]]\).

For example, for a rectangular \(X\) over \(A_G\) put \(B=X^*X\) and let \(P\) be its kernel projection.
The matrix \(F=z(B+zI)^{-1}\) has entries in \(O_G\), equals the identity on \(P\), and has order at least one on \(I-P\). [Formal positive resolvent](Yin_C_A0141_v1.md#realizing-every-element-of-the-regular-closure)
Hence \(F^N=P+O(z^N)\) entrywise for every \(N\).
For \(x\in D_G\), its affiliated relative inverse lies in \(R_G\), so truncation gives inner inverse equations \(xyx=x\) in \(O_G/z^NO_G\) at every \(N\).
An exact solution \(y\in O_G\) exists if and only if \(x\) has an inner inverse in \(D_G\).
Indeed the formal identity specializes to \(xy(s)x=x\) with \(y(s)\in D_G\) outside a countable set of scalars, by [preservation of formal identities](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#preservation-of-formal-identities).
Conversely an inner inverse in \(D_G\) gives a constant solution.
Thus finite truncations provide the approximations for every \(G\), while exact rational solvability for every \(x\) is the regularity of \(D_G\) asked for in the deletion problem.
