# Rational expected supports on cyclic subgroups of virtually locally indicable groups

## Statement

For a countable discrete group \(G\), let \(U_G=L^0(\mathcal N(G))\) and let \(D_G\) be the division closure of \(\mathbb CG\) in \(U_G\).
Suppose \(G\) has a subgroup \(K\) of finite index \(d\) that is locally indicable, meaning that each nontrivial finitely generated subgroup of \(K\) maps onto \(\mathbb Z\).
Let \(H=\langle h\rangle\leq G\) be infinite cyclic.
Then
\[
                         D_G\cap U_H=\mathbb C(h).
\]
For every orthogonal projection \(p\in M_n(D_G)\), the support of its entrywise trace-preserving conditional expectation satisfies
\[
                Q=s(E_H(p))\ \in M_n(\mathbb C(h)).
\]
In particular this holds for the kernel projection of every finite matrix over \(\mathbb CG\).
The trace of \(Q\), with unnormalized matrix trace and the canonical group trace, equals its rank over \(\mathbb C(h)\).

There is a finite construction of \(Q\).
Take copies \(G_j\) of \(G\) for \(j\in\mathbb Z\), identify the copies of \(H\) in their free product with amalgamation \(\Gamma=*_{H,j\in\mathbb Z}G_j\), and let \(p_j\) be the image of \(p\) in the \(j\)-th copy.
With \(r=(\operatorname{Tr}_n\otimes\tau_G)(p)\), the number \(m=d(n-r)\) is a nonnegative integer and
\[
                         Q=\bigvee_{j=0}^{m}p_j
                          \qquad\text{in }M_n(\mathcal N(\Gamma)).
\]
Thus for \(Y=Y_1+Y_2\) with group-ring vertex inputs in a splitting \(G=G_1*_H G_2\) satisfying the stated hypothesis on \(G\), one obtains a maximizing edge projection over \(\mathbb C(h)\) by applying the construction to the kernel of the stacked vertex rows. [Two vertex rank formula](Yin_C_A0007_v13.md#statement)

## Argument

### A cyclic intersection in a locally indicable group

First suppose that \(G\) itself is locally indicable.
We use the analytical Hughes-free division rings of Jaikin-Zapirain and López-Álvarez, in particular their Corollary 6.3 for \(\mathbb C\).
For every \(T\leq G\) the subgroup inclusion identifies \(D_T\) with the division subring generated by \(\mathbb CT\) in \(U_G\).
Indeed \(U_T\) embeds compatibly with adjoints and affiliated inverses, and scalar rational operations in the subgroup have the same values in either ring.
If a finitely generated \(T\) admits an epimorphism \(\phi:T\to\mathbb Z\) with kernel \(N\) and \(\phi(t)=1\), Hughes freeness identifies \(D_T\) with the Ore field of \(D_N[t^{\pm1};\sigma]\), where \(\sigma\) is conjugation by \(t\).
This field embeds over the skew Laurent polynomials in the formal Laurent series division ring \(D_N((t;\sigma))\). [Hughes freeness and the analytic division ring](../audits/literature/JZLA20/Yin_U001.md#hughes-free)

We also use a property of minimizing rational expressions proved in the argument for Corollary 6.10 of the same paper.
For \(0\ne x\in D_G\), choose a rational expression \(\alpha\) realizing its minimum tree complexity.
Write \(\alpha=\alpha' u\) with \(\alpha'\) primitive, \(u\in\mathbb C^\times G\), and let \(T\) be the image in \(G\) of the source subgroup of \(\alpha'\).
In the authors' terminology the source is the least subgroup of the homogeneous units over which the expression can be written up to a right homogeneous unit.
The group \(T\) is finitely generated, and \(\alpha'\) minimizes the \(T\)-complexity of its value \(b'\in D_T\).
If \(T\ne1\), then for each epimorphism \(T\to\mathbb Z\) as above the Laurent series of \(b'\) has at least two nonzero homogeneous terms.
Indeed a one-term series would have the same complexity as that term, and Proposition 5.1 would place every minimizing expression for \(b'\) in \(\operatorname{Rat}(\mathbb C^\times N)t^i\) for that degree \(i\).
The minimal-source property would then give \(T\leq N\).
Here the smaller-complexity invertibility premise holds because \(D_T\) is a division ring, and Proposition 5.1 may be applied in \(D_N((t;\sigma))\) with coefficient division ring \(D_N\).
Writing \(u=\lambda g\), we thus have \(x=bg\) with \(g\in G\) and \(b=\lambda b'\in D_T\), whose nonzero degrees are the same as those of \(b'\). [Proposition 5.1](../audits/literature/JZLA20/Yin_U001.md#key-induction) and [Corollary 6.10, arXiv v4, p. 34](../literature/pdf/JZLA20--arxiv-v4.pdf#page=34)

Now take \(0\ne x\in D_G\cap U_H\).
The bounded transform \(F(x)=x(1+x^*x)^{-1/2}\) belongs to \(\mathcal N(H)\).
For every group unitary \(v\) we have \(F(xv)=F(x)v\) by functional calculus.
Thus \(F(x)=F(b)g\), and the nonzero Fourier vector \(F(x)\delta_e\) is supported in \(H\cap Tg\).
Choose \(k\) in this intersection and put \(J=H\cap T\), \(s=kg^{-1}\in T\), and \(c=xk^{-1}=bs^{-1}\in D_T\).
Since \(H\cap Tg=Jk\), the Fourier vector of \(F(c)\) is supported in \(J\).
The canonical subgroup expectation therefore fixes \(F(c)\), and recovering \(c=F(c)(1-F(c)^*F(c))^{-1/2}\) in the affiliated algebra gives \(c\in U_J\).
For trivial \(J\) this gives \(x=ck\in\mathbb CH\).

For nontrivial \(J\), choose a generator \(w\) of \(J\), and choose an epimorphism \(\phi:T\to\mathbb Z\) with kernel \(N\) by local indicability.
If \(\phi(w)=0\), then \(c\in D_T\cap U_N=D_N\).
To verify this last intersection, write \(Bc=A\) in \(U_T\) with skew Laurent polynomials \(A=\sum A_it^i\), \(0\ne B=\sum B_it^i\) over \(D_N\), using the Ore description of \(D_T\).
As \(c\in U_N\), the coefficient of \(t^i\) on the left is \(B_i\sigma^i(c)\in U_N\).
Distinct powers of \(t\) are linearly independent over \(U_N\) in this identity.
Indeed for a finite row \(C\) of such coefficients the left multiplier \((1+CC^*)^{-1/2}\) belongs to \(\mathcal N(N)\), is a unit in \(U_N\), and makes every entry of \(C\) bounded.
The bounded summands have disjoint Fourier cosets \(Nt^i\).
Thus \(B_i\sigma^i(c)=A_i\), and one \(B_i\ne0\) gives \(c\in D_N\).
It follows that \(b=cs\) has one homogeneous term, since \(s\in T\) is a group monomial, contradicting the minimum-expression property.
This argument compares affiliated operators first and only then uses the Laurent embedding of \(D_T\).

It remains that \(\phi(J)=a\mathbb Z\) for an integer \(a>0\).
Choose the sign of \(w\) so that \(\phi(w)=a\).
Then \(T'=\phi^{-1}(a\mathbb Z)=N\rtimes\langle w\rangle\) has finite index in \(T\) and contains \(J\).
The [finite-index intersection formula](../ledger/Yin_L_A0073_v1.md#the-finite-index-coefficient-matrices) gives \(c\in D_T\cap U_{T'}=D_{T'}\).
Inside \(U_{T'}\), the division ring \(D_{T'}\) is the Ore field of \(D_N[w^{\pm1};\operatorname{Ad}(w)]\).
For this assertion \(T'\) is finitely generated as a finite-index subgroup of \(T\).
The [cyclic fraction intersection](Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#its-intersection-with-the-edge-algebra) gives \(D_{T'}\cap U_J=\mathbb C(w)\).
Its hypotheses hold since \(D_N\) is a complex division subring of \(U_N\) stable under adjoints and conjugation by \(w\).
That calculation clears the skew denominator on the left and takes a linear functional on coefficient vectors in \(L^2(\mathcal N(N))\) to obtain a nonzero scalar Laurent denominator.
Consequently \(x=ck\in\mathbb C(h)\).
The reverse inclusion follows from inversion of nonzero cyclic Laurent polynomials in \(U_H\).

### Passage to finite index

Let \(K\leq G\) be locally indicable of index \(d\), and let \(J=H\cap K=\langle h^a\rangle\) with \(a=[H:J]\).
The left cosets \(h^jK\), \(0\leq j<a\), are distinct.
Include these representatives in a left transversal \(S\) of \(G/K\).
The [finite-index coefficient decomposition](../ledger/Yin_L_A0073_v1.md#the-finite-index-coefficient-matrices) gives
\[
           U_G=\bigoplus_{s\in S}sU_K,\qquad
           D_G=\bigoplus_{s\in S}sD_K,\qquad
           U_H=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{a-1}h^jU_J .
\]
For \(x\in D_G\cap U_H\), uniqueness in the first decomposition puts each coefficient of its last decomposition in \(D_K\cap U_J=\mathbb C(h^a)\), by the locally indicable case.
This proves \(D_G\cap U_H=\mathbb C(h)\).

We also recall two consequences of the same coefficient representation.
The ring \(D_G\) is regular by [finite-index ascent of regularity](../ledger/Yin_L_A0073_v1.md#ascent-and-descent-of-kernel-projections), since \(D_K\) is a division ring.
In particular its matrix algebras contain the kernel and range projections of their matrices, and the join of finitely many rational projections is rational by taking the range projection of their concatenation.
Every projection \(e\in M_n(D_G)\) has trace in \(d^{-1}\mathbb Z\).
Indeed the coset realization embeds it into \(M_{dn}(D_K)\), where its unnormalized trace is its integer rank over the division ring by row reduction and polar decomposition.
The trace in this realization is \(d\) times the original trace, since the diagonal trace vector on each coset representative is a right translate of the identity trace vector.

### Finitely many copies recover the expected support

The fold homomorphism \(\pi:\Gamma\to G\) restricts to the canonical isomorphism on each \(G_j\).
View \(\Gamma\) as the fundamental group of the line of vertex groups \(G_j\) with each adjacent pair amalgamated over \(H\).
The fold kernel acts freely on its Bass–Serre tree, since the vertex stabilizers are conjugates of the \(G_j\), and consequently is free.
Indeed the quotient is a graph with that tree as its universal cover and fold kernel as its group of deck transformations.
Thus \(\pi^{-1}(K)\) is locally indicable.
A nontrivial finitely generated subgroup with nontrivial image in \(K\) maps onto \(\mathbb Z\) through its image, and one with trivial image is a nontrivial free group.
The subgroup \(\pi^{-1}(K)\) has index \(d\) in the countable group \(\Gamma\).
All the finite-index conclusions just established apply to \((\Gamma,H)\).

Identify \(G\) with \(G_0\), and write \(\theta\) for the automorphism of \(\Gamma\) shifting every copy by one, fixing \(H\) pointwise.
Every element of \(\Gamma\setminus H\) has infinite orbit under \(\theta\).
Indeed a reduced amalgam word outside \(H\) has a nonempty finite sequence of vertex indices, which is determined by its group element, and \(\theta\) translates every index by one.
The fixed algebra of \(\theta\) in \(\mathcal N(\Gamma)\) is exactly \(\mathcal N(H)\), since a fixed operator has square-summable Fourier coefficients constant on those orbits.

Put \(q_\ell=\bigvee_{j=0}^{\ell}\theta^j(p)\).
Each \(q_\ell\in M_n(D_\Gamma)\), and every strict increase raises the trace by at least \(1/d\).
At any plateau \(q_{\ell+1}=q_\ell\), we have \(\theta(q_\ell)\leq q_\ell\), and equality of their traces gives equality of the projections.
It follows entrywise that \(q_\ell\in M_n(\mathcal N(H))\).
For any projection \(p\), \(Q=s(E_H(p))\) is the least edge projection dominating it.
Indeed \(E_H((1-Q)p(1-Q))=0\) and faithfulness give \(p\leq Q\), and any dominating edge projection dominates the support by bimodularity.
The expectation from \(\Gamma\) agrees on \(G_0\) with the expectation used in the statement, by subgroup Fourier restriction.
Thus \(\theta\) fixes \(Q\), every \(q_\ell\leq Q\), and a plateau forces \(q_\ell=Q\).
Starting at trace \(r\), either a plateau occurs before \(m=d(n-r)\) strict increases, or \(q_m\) has trace \(n\) and is the identity.
In both cases \(Q=q_m\) belongs entrywise to \(D_\Gamma\cap U_H=\mathbb C(h)\).
Under Fourier transformation each minor of this rational projection is a rational function on the circle, so its pointwise rank equals its field rank almost everywhere.
Integration gives the asserted trace.
