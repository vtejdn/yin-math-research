# How many free copies of a projection span the support of its expectation?

## Statement

Let \(B\subset M\) be finite von Neumann algebras with a faithful normal finite trace \(\tau\) on \(M\) and trace-preserving conditional expectation \(E:M\to B\).
Fix a projection \(p\in M\), set \(Q=s(E(p))\), and write \(\operatorname{rk}(x)=\tau(s(x^*x))\).
For an integer \(k\ge1\), take the tracial free product of \(k\) copies of \(M\) with amalgamation over \(B\).
Write \(p_j\) for the copy of \(p\) in the \(j\)-th factor and \(q_k=\bigvee_{j=1}^k p_j\) for the projection onto their closed span.
Use the same notation for the extended trace and expectation.
Then \(q_k\leq Q\) and
\[
 \tau(Q-q_k)=
 \max_{\substack{e\in B\text{ a projection}\\ e\le Q}}
             \{\tau(e)-k\operatorname{rk}(pe)\}.
\]
The maximum is attained at \(e=s(E(Q-q_k))\).
Thus \(q_k=Q\) exactly when \(k\operatorname{rk}(pe)\geq\tau(e)\) for every edge projection \(e\leq Q\).
For \(Q\ne0\), some finite number of copies spans \(Q\) exactly when
\[
        \inf_{\substack{0\ne e\le Q\\ e\in B\text{ a projection}}}
              \frac{\operatorname{rk}(pe)}{\tau(e)}>0 .
\]
In particular, if \(E(p)\geq\varepsilon Q\) for a scalar \(\varepsilon>0\), then \(k\geq1/\varepsilon\) suffices.
The two-copy example below attains \(q_2=Q\) with zero in the spectrum of \(E(p)\) on \(Q\).

For countable discrete groups \(H\leq G\), the formula applies to \(M=M_n(\mathcal N(G))\), \(B=M_n(\mathcal N(H))\) with the unnormalized matrix trace, using the group \(\Gamma=*_{H,j=1}^kG_j\) of \(k\) copies of \(G\).
Let \(U_L=L^0(\mathcal N(L))\) and let \(D_L\) denote the division closure of \(\mathbb CL\) in \(U_L\).
Suppose \(p\in M_n(D_G)\) and the \(k\)-copy inequality gives \(q_k=Q\).
Then \(Q\in M_n(D_\Gamma)\) if \(D_\Gamma\) is von Neumann regular, or if \(k\tau(p)=\tau(Q)\).
Under either condition an additional intersection identity \(D_\Gamma\cap U_H=D_H\) gives \(Q\in M_n(D_H)\).

## Argument

For a projection \(v\), \(s(E(v))\) is the least edge projection dominating \(v\).
Indeed \(Q_v=s(E(v))\) satisfies \(E((1-Q_v)v(1-Q_v))=0\), so faithfulness gives \(v\leq Q_v\), and every dominating edge projection dominates \(Q_v\) by bimodularity.
Applying this to each copy shows \(p_j\le Q\).
Set \(r=Q-q_k\).
For any edge projection \(e\leq Q\), the common kernel inside \(e\) of the \(k\) rows \(p_j e\) has projection \(r\wedge e\).
The range of the stacked rows lies in the direct sum of their individual closed ranges, hence
\[
             \tau(e)-\tau(r\wedge e)
                    \ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^k\operatorname{rk}(p_je)
                    =k\operatorname{rk}(pe).
\]
This bounds the displayed maximum by \(\tau(r)\).

Now choose \(e=s(E(r))\), so \(r\leq e\leq Q\).
For \(1\leq j\leq k\), let \(v_j=1-s(e p_j e)\) be the kernel projection of \(p_j e\) on the entire space, and put \(w_j=\bigwedge_{i=1}^jv_i\).
The full meet is \(w_k=(1-e)+r\), and \(E(w_k)=(1-e)+E(r)\) has support \(1\).
Consequently \(E(w_j)\) has full support at every step.
For \(j\geq2\), the pair \(w_{j-1},v_j\) belongs to two algebras free over \(B\), the algebra generated by the preceding factors and the \(j\)-th factor.
Their meet is \(w_j\), so the [two-projection theorem at full expected support](Yin_C_A0007_v13.md#free-vertex-projections-with-a-common-kernel-of-full-expected-support) gives
\[
              \tau(w_j)=\tau(w_{j-1})+\tau(v_j)-\tau(1).
\]
Iterating from \(w_1=v_1\) gives \(\tau(1-e)+\tau(r)=\tau(1)-k\operatorname{rk}(pe)\).
This is equality at the claimed maximizing projection, including \(k=1\).
The criterion for finite capture follows by taking the infimum of the ratios for nonzero \(e\), since \(e=0\) always gives zero in the maximum.
If \(E(p)\geq\varepsilon Q\), the positive contraction \(epe\) satisfies
\[
               \operatorname{rk}(pe)=\tau(s(epe))
                       \geq\tau(epe)=\tau(eE(p)e)\geq\varepsilon\tau(e).
\]
For \(Q=0\) faithfulness gives \(p=q_k=0\) for all \(k\).

In the group case the amalgamated group normal form gives freeness over \(\mathcal N(H)\), and expanding entries of centered alternating products gives freeness of the matrix algebras over \(M_n(\mathcal N(H))\).
If \(D_\Gamma\) is regular, the projection onto the closed range of \(W=[p_1,\ldots,p_k]\) is rational by [matrix regularity and relative inverses](../audits/literature/JZ19/Yin_U001.md#regular-closure-definitions).
When \(q_k=Q\) and \(k\tau(p)=\tau(Q)\), let \(P=\operatorname{diag}(p_1,\ldots,p_k)\).
The initial support of \(W\) is at most \(P\) and has trace \(\tau(Q)=\tau(P)\) by polar decomposition, hence equals \(P\).
The Gram matrix \(W^*W+1-P\) is therefore an affiliated unit, and
\[
                Q=W(W^*W+1-P)^{-1}W^* .
\]
The expression follows by polar decomposition on the initial support, and the inverse has entries in \(D_\Gamma\) by [matrix Gram elimination](Yin_C_A0132_v1.md#argument).
In both cases \(Q\) already lies in \(M_n(\mathcal N(H))\), so the stated intersection identity suffices for descent.
For the [two-vertex atom formula](Yin_C_A0142_v1.md#recovering-the-atom-from-its-expected-support), the projection of interest is itself a group-ring kernel whose rationality can be unknown.
The rank criterion determines its analytic join from copies, while the conclusions concerning \(D_\Gamma\) assume rationality of the original projection.

### Two copies suffice without a gap in the expectation

Let \(G=\langle h\rangle\times\langle t\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\), \(H=\langle h\rangle\), \(f=h-1\), and let \(p\) be the range projection of the column \(V=(1,ft)^{\mathsf T}\).
The Gram scalar is \(V^*V=1+f^*f\), so \(p=V(1+f^*f)^{-1}V^*\in M_2(D_G)\) has unnormalized trace \(1\).
Fourier restriction to \(H\) kills the off-diagonal entries and gives
\[
                    E_H(p)=(1+|f|^2)^{-1}
                       \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&|f|^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Under Fourier transformation \(f(z)=z-1\) is nonzero almost everywhere and arbitrarily small on sets of positive measure, so \(Q=I_2\) and \(E_H(p)\) has no positive lower bound.
In the two-copy amalgam \(\langle h\rangle\times F(t_1,t_2)\), subtracting the first of the columns \((1,ft_j)^{\mathsf T}\) from the second gives \((0,f(t_2-t_1))^{\mathsf T}\).
Both \(f\) and \(t_2-t_1=t_1(t_1^{-1}t_2-1)\) are affiliated units, since an infinite-order group translation has no nonzero invariant vector in the regular representation and its difference from \(1\) has dense range.
The matrix of the two columns is therefore an affiliated unit.
Its range projection is \(q_2=I_2\), while \(q_1\) has trace \(1\).
