# Kernel projections from commuting spectral data

## Statement

Let \(B=A_1\ge0,A_2,\ldots,A_\ell\) be commuting bounded selfadjoint operators in a finite von Neumann algebra \(M\) with faithful normal tracial state.
Write \(\mu\) for their joint spectral law on \(\mathbb R^\ell\), \(U\) for the affiliated algebra and \(T\) for the division closure of the unital complex algebra generated by the \(A_i\) in \(U\).
Let \(V\subset\mathbb C^\ell\) be the Zariski closure of the support of \(\mu\), with irreducible components \(V_1,\ldots,V_s\).
Join \(i\) to \(j\) by an edge exactly when \(\mu(V_i\cap V_j)>0\).
The projections in \(T\) are precisely the indicators, up to \(\mu\)-null sets, of unions of the \(V_i\) selected by taking entire connected components of this finite graph.

Let \(p=1_{\{0\}}(B)\), and let \(V_+\) be the Zariski closure of the support of the restriction of \(\mu\) to \(A_1>0\), taking the closure of the empty set to be empty.
Then
\[
                p\in T\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                \mu\bigl(\{A_1=0\}\cap V_+\bigr)=0.
\]
Here and below \(A_1\) denotes the first coordinate when applied to the joint law.
When this condition holds, one can choose a real nonnegative polynomial \(b\) on the real joint spectrum, vanishing on \(V_+\cap\mathbb R^\ell\), and recover \(p=b(A)(B+b(A))^{-1}\).

There is a sharp obstruction to approximating the kernel by rational expressions using these commuting data.
Let \(L(z)\in M_n(T[z])\) have degree at most \(d\) and be invertible in \(M_n(U((z)))\), for a central variable \(z\).
Let \(f=\alpha L^{-1}\beta\) with constant complex row and column, and let \(N\ge1\).
Either of the conditions
\[
 f-p\in z^NU[[z]],\qquad
 f\in U[[z]],\quad f(0)=p,\quad f^2-f\in z^NU[[z]]
\]
implies \(p\in T\) as soon as \(N>(n-1)d\).
The coefficients in \(T\) may change with \(N\) and have unrestricted algebraic complexity.
For each \(n\ge2\) and \(d\ge1\), the boundary order \(N=(n-1)d\) is achievable with \(p\notin T\), even for a positive two-by-two matrix over the group ring of the infinite cyclic group.
In that example the actual kernel projection has group-ring entries, and one additional commuting group-ring observable suffices to recover it by a scalar rational function.

## Argument

### Which parts of the joint law can be separated

The joint spectral representation identifies the algebra generated by the \(A_i\) with the polynomial functions modulo those vanishing \(\mu\)-almost everywhere.
An affiliated inverse of a polynomial is legal exactly when its zero set has measure zero.
The ring of quotients \(u(A)/v(A)\) with these denominators is already closed under every legal scalar inverse and hence is \(T\).
Polynomial identities holding \(\mu\)-almost everywhere hold throughout \(V\), by continuity on the support and Zariski closure.

If \(u/v\) is idempotent, then \(u(u-v)=0\) on \(V\).
On each irreducible \(V_i\) either \(u=0\) or \(u=v\) identically.
The part of \(V_i\) outside the remaining components has positive measure, since otherwise those remaining components would already contain the support.
Thus the denominator cannot vanish identically on \(V_i\).
At an intersection of components assigned different values the denominator vanishes, so that intersection has measure zero.
This proves that the component values are constant along each edge of the graph.

Conversely, let \(W_0,W_1\) be the respective unions of the unselected and selected components, with \(\mu(W_0\cap W_1)=0\).
For each \(j=0,1\), choose finitely many defining polynomials for \(W_j\) and let \(b_j\) be the sum of their absolute squares on real tuples, a polynomial in the real coordinates.
Use \(b_j=1\) for the empty set and \(b_j=0\) for all of affine space.
Then \(b_0/(b_0+b_1)\) is a legal quotient with the specified values almost everywhere.
This proves the graph description.

For the particular kernel indicator, a representation \(p=u/v\) forces \(u\) to vanish on \(V_+\) and \(u=v\ne0\) almost everywhere on \(A_1=0\).
Conversely let \(b\) be the sum of absolute squares of defining equations of \(V_+\) as above.
Under the stated zero-measure condition \(B>0\) on the positive part and \(b(A)>0\) almost everywhere on the zero part, so \(B+b(A)\) is positive and nonzero almost everywhere.
The quotient in the statement is therefore the required indicator.

### A sharp degree bound for commuting measurements

In the joint spectral representation the finitely many matrix coefficients of \(L\) have simultaneous measurable scalar entries.
Almost every matrix \(L(\lambda,z)\) has nonzero determinant as a polynomial in \(z\).
If the determinant were identically zero on a set of positive measure, its indicator in the abelian algebra would support a nonzero Laurent-polynomial kernel column.
Such a column is obtained by choosing a maximal nonzero minor on each of the finitely many possible pivot and rank strata, using its cofactors to clear denominators, and choosing a coordinate vector on rank zero.
It would give a nonzero formal kernel column for \(L\) in the ambient algebra.
Thus \(\det L\) has a scalar formal inverse fiberwise almost everywhere.
Its first nonzero degree lies between zero and \(nd\), so the fiber inverses have a common finite lower bound on their orders.
Their measurable coefficients assemble into the affiliated Laurent inverse, by the adjugate formula and uniqueness.

Write \(a(z)=\alpha\,\operatorname{adj}(L(z))\,\beta\) and \(c(z)=\det L(z)\), so \(f=a/c\) and \(\deg_z a\le(n-1)d\).
If the second jet condition holds, then on \(B>0\) the factor \(f-1\) is a formal unit, and on \(B=0\) the factor \(f\) is a formal unit.
Consequently it implies the first jet condition fiberwise, hence also in \(U[[z]]\).
On \(B>0\) this gives \(a=cf\in z^NU[[z]]\).
If \(N>(n-1)d\), all coefficients \(a_j\in T\) vanish there.
On \(B=0\) the constant coefficient of \(f\) is one, so at almost every such fiber at least one \(a_j\) is nonzero, since \(c\) is a nonzero polynomial there.
For \(S=\sum_j a_j^*a_j\in T\), the positive affiliated element \(B+S\) is therefore a unit and \(p=S(B+S)^{-1}\in T\).
This argument needs no uniform bound on denominators appearing in the coefficients of \(L\).

### A cyclic-group example attaining the bound

Let \(h\) be the generator of the infinite cyclic group, \(l=2-h-h^{-1}\), \(C=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&h\\h^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}\), and \(B=2lC\).
Then \(C\) is an orthogonal projection, \(l\) has zero kernel, and \(p=I_2-C\) has group-ring entries.
Use the normalized matrix trace and first take \(B\) as the only spectral observable.
On each Fourier fiber the eigenvalues of \(B\) are \(0\) and \(2l\).
Thus the scalar law has an atom of mass \(1/2\) at zero, while its positive support is Zariski dense in the affine line.
The spectral criterion gives \(p\notin T\).
Adding the single observable \(a=2lI_2\) gives the rational identity \(p=(a-B)a^{-1}\).
In this pair of coordinates the two spectral branches intersect only at \((0,0)\), a joint null set.

For \(k=n-1\ge1\) and \(w=z^d\), take the transfer function
\[
                  f=\frac{w^k}{B+w^k}.
\]
For an \(n\)-state realization of degree \(d\), solve the equations
\[
             By_0+y_k=1,\qquad
             y_j-wy_{j-1}=0\quad(1\le j\le k)
\]
with constant right side and select output \(y_k\).
The determinant is \(B+w^k\), up to sign, with a legal Laurent inverse equal to \(z^{-kd}\) on the kernel and a regular power series on its complement.
Thus \(f=p+O(z^{kd})\), with the coefficient at degree \(kd\) equal to \(B^{-1}\) on its positive support and zero on its kernel.
The same order holds for the idempotent defect, since its leading coefficient on the positive support is the negative of that nonzero coefficient.
This gives equality at the boundary for both tests.
