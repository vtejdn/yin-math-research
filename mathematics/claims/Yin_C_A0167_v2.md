# Rank-one commutator sums with a prescribed list of group elements

## Statement

Let \(G\) be discrete with its canonical regular trace, let \(H=\ell^2(G)\), and fix a finite list \(s_1,\ldots,s_k\) of elements of \(G\), allowing repetitions.
Write \(R_s\) for right multiplication by \(s\) and use the unnormalized trace for affiliated matrix ranks.
The following are equivalent.

1. Some bounded operators \(T_i\) of ordinary complex rank at most one give a sum \(\sum_i[R_{s_i},T_i]\) of ordinary complex rank one.
2. For some \(l\), some constant columns \(c_i,d_i,\alpha\in\mathbb C^l\), and the group-ring matrix \(K\) with columns \(c_i s_i-d_i\), one has
   \[
          0<\operatorname{rk}_G[K,\alpha]-\operatorname{rk}_G K<1.
   \]
3. Some matrix \(K\) of this same column form on the prescribed list has noninteger regular rank.

In items 2 and 3 one can restrict the number of rows to at most \(2k\).
Thus prescribing the number of occurrences of a label in the list prescribes a bound on the rank of its finite-rank input in the [commutator criterion](Yin_C_A0157_v1.md#separating-the-ranges-and-excluding-rank-one).
No torsion or scalar injectivity hypothesis is needed for the equivalence.

Suppose a label \(s\) occurs just once and the group generated by the list admits an integer character zero on every other entry and nonzero on \(s\).
The three equivalent existence statements are unchanged by deleting this entry.
In particular, labels with independent images in the rational abelianization, each used once, cannot give a rank-one sum.
More generally one can iteratively delete such entries until the remaining list belongs to a subgroup satisfying complex strong Atiyah and having no torsion, in which case the original list gives no rank-one sum.
The character is required on the subgroup at that step.

For an individual boundary represented by a nonzero tensor \(\xi\otimes\eta\), the construction in the forward direction gives an increment between \(\operatorname{rk}_G\xi\) and \(1-\operatorname{rk}_G\eta\), inclusive, regarding the two vectors as affiliated scalars.
In particular a two-by-two group-ring matrix \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with affiliated-unit \(a\) and Schur scalar \(S=d-ca^{-1}b\) of rank \(\delta\in(0,1)\) gives increment exactly \(\delta\).
Writing \(M=\sum_g M_g g\) with complex two-by-two coefficient matrices, the list can use each \(g\ne1\) at most \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}M_g\) times.

There is also a quantitative deletion test when only a finite character is available.
If an isolated label occurs once and has image of order \(n\ge2\) under a circle character trivial on all other entries of the list, assume all pencils on the shortened list have integer ranks.
The distance of every constant-column rank increment on the longer list from the nearest integer is at most \(1/n\).
In particular for \(n\ge3\) this excludes a half-rank Schur scalar whenever its specified list above meets those hypotheses.

## Passing from a commutator sum to a fractional column increment

Identify \(H=L^2(\mathcal N(G),\tau)\) with its vectors in the affiliated algebra \(U\).
For \(u,v\in H\) the tensor \(u\otimes v\) represents the operator \(f\mapsto u\tau(vf)\).
The commutator with \(R_s\) corresponds to \(us\otimes v-u\otimes sv\) by the [finite-rank tensor identity](Yin_C_A0131_v1.md#balancing-and-ordinary-finite-rank).
Write the \(i\)-th input as \(u_i\otimes v_i\), allowing zeros.
Choose a basis row \(w\) of the span of \(u_i,u_is_i\).
Write \(u_i=wc_i\) and \(u_is_i=wd_i\) with constant columns.
Then \(wK=0\), and \(w\) has at most \(2k\) entries.

For a nonzero rank-one output there are nonzero \(\eta\in H\) and \(\xi=w\alpha\in H\) such that the output tensor is \(\xi\otimes\eta\).
Equating the second factors in the basis \(w\) gives \(K v=-\alpha\eta\), where \(v\) stacks the \(v_i\).
Let \(E\) be the orthogonal projection onto the complement of the closed image of \(K\).
Affiliated matrix operations give \(w=wE\) and \(E\alpha\eta=0\).
Since \(w\alpha\ne0\), the column \(E\alpha\) has positive rank.
Its right kernel contains the nonzero right ideal \(\eta U\), so its rank is strictly less than one.
More precisely \(\xi=wE\alpha\) has rank at most that of \(E\alpha\), and the kernel dimension of the column is at least the rank of \(\eta\).
Its rank is the claimed increment, by projecting the newly adjoined column onto the old image complement.

## Constructing the rank-one sum from the pencil

Suppose the increment is \(\delta\in(0,1)\).
Let \(p\) be the right kernel projection of \(E\alpha\) and put \(w=\alpha^*E\).
Then \(p\ne0\), \(wK=0\), and \(w\alpha=(E\alpha)^*E\alpha\ne0\).
Since \(E\alpha p=0\), there is a column \(z\) over \(U\) satisfying \(Kz=\alpha p\), for example the relative inverse of \(K\) applied to \(\alpha p\).
Choose a bounded affiliated unit \(t\) making all \(z_i t\) bounded, such as \(t=(1+\sum_i z_i^*z_i)^{-1/2}\).
For the \(i\)-th term take the tensor \(wc_i\otimes z_i t\).
The sum of their commutators has tensor
\[
  \sum_i (wd_i\otimes z_i t-wc_i\otimes s_i z_i t)
              =-\sum_j w_j\otimes(Kz)_j t
              =-(w\alpha)\otimes pt .
\]
Both factors are nonzero since \(t\) is an affiliated unit.
This is a nonzero rank-one sum with at most one input tensor in each prescribed position.

If a fractional increment occurs, some rank among \(K\) and the projection of \(K\) to the constant quotient by \(\mathbb C\alpha\) is noninteger.
Indeed adjoining \(\alpha\) gives one plus the rank in that constant quotient.
Both matrices have the required column form.
Conversely, take any noninteger-rank \(K\).
Successive addition of rows has increments in \([0,1]\), and one of these increments belongs to \((0,1)\).
For the corresponding row prefix \(K'\), adjoining the constant last coordinate column increases rank by one minus that fractional row increment.
This proves the equivalence with item 3.
A constant change of output coordinates confines every column of \(K\) to the span of its \(c_i,d_i\), of dimension at most \(2k\).
In the fractional-increment case \(\alpha\) lies in that span as well, since an independent constant component would give increment one.
Finally, a sum of \(m\) rank-one inputs at one label is an input of rank at most \(m\), and every finite-rank input admits such a decomposition.

## A pencil for the particular Schur scalar

Let \(p\) be the kernel projection of \(S\), \(y=ca^{-1}\), \(z=a^{-1}b\).
For \(f\in\mathbb CG\) put \(\Psi_f(l,r)=lf\otimes r-l\otimes fr\) over \(\mathbb C\).
The [four-term balance](../ledger/Yin_L_A0081_v1.md#the-four-terms-before-word-expansion) is
\[
 S\otimes p=\Psi_d(1,p)-\Psi_b(y,p)-\Psi_c(1,zp)+\Psi_a(y,zp).
\]
This follows from \(ya=c\), \(az=b\), and \(Sp=0\).
Expand directly in the group basis, allowing each element of the finite coefficient support as a translation label.
The coefficient tensor for the input at \(g\) has first factors \((1,y)\), second factors \((p,zp)\), and coefficient matrix
\[
          \begin{pmatrix}d_g&-c_g\\-b_g&a_g\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its ordinary tensor rank is at most the rank of this constant matrix, which equals the rank of \(M_g\) by the two-by-two minor test.
The label \(1\) contributes zero to the boundary.
Multiplication by common bounded affiliated units on the outer left and right clears the finite lists of tensor factors and commutes with these balancing operations.
It sends the output factors to \(\xi,\eta\) of ranks exactly \(\delta,1-\delta\), respectively.
Decompose the bounded input tensors at each label within the allowed rank.
The two bounds just proved now force the fractional increment to be exactly \(\delta\).
The labels are the actual group elements in the input matrix, and need not belong to an earlier marking of \(G\).

## Deleting a label separated by a character

Ranks of matrices on the list can be computed in the subgroup it generates by right coset decomposition of the left regular representation.
Suppose all pencils on the shortened list have integer rank.
Adding any collection of constant columns to one of them preserves integrality, by taking the quotient by the complex span of those columns and adding back its dimension.
Let \(B\) consist of the other columns and \(E\) project onto the complement of its closed image.
For the new column \(cs-d\), the ranks of \(Ec\) and \(Ed\) are therefore each zero or one.

For a unit phase the character twists fix \(B,E,c,d\) and multiply \(s\) by a phase which ranges over the full circle.
They preserve regular rank.
Thus \(zEcs-Ed\) has constant rank on the unit circle.
By the [polynomial rank inequality](Yin_C_A0022_v1.md#simultaneous-generic-rank-and-degeneration) this rank is at least \(\operatorname{rk}Ed\), its rank at zero, and at least \(\operatorname{rk}Ec\) by reversing the polynomial.
If either is one, the column adds exactly one rank.
If both are zero, it adds none.
It follows that all pencils on the longer list have integer rank.
Failure for the shorter list persists by setting the extra column to zero, which proves the deletion statement.
A remaining torsion-free subgroup satisfying strong Atiyah has integer rank for every remaining pencil.
For the empty list the initial matrix has rank zero.

## An isolated label of finite character order

Use the same shortened pencil \(B\) and projection \(E\) and set \(r=\operatorname{rk}B\).
The ranks of \(Ec,Ed\) are again each zero or one.
If both vanish the rank after appending \(cs-d\) is \(r\).
Otherwise consider the pencil \(zEcs-Ed\) at the \(n\) distinct character phases, where all ranks equal its rank at one.
If \(Ed\) is nonzero the value at zero has rank one.
If \(Ec\) is nonzero apply the same argument to the reverse pencil \(Ecs-zEd\), whose ranks on these phases are the same after replacing each phase by its inverse.
The [finite Vandermonde bound](Yin_C_A0022_v1.md#simultaneous-generic-rank-and-degeneration) gives rank at least \(1-1/n\) at one.
Thus the rank of the full pencil equals an integer minus a number in \([0,1/n]\), including the case when both endpoints vanish.
The same applies after quotienting by a nonzero constant column \(\alpha\).
The increment is one plus the quotient rank minus the original rank, hence differs from an integer by at most \(1/n\).
At \(n=2\) the estimate permits an increment of one half.

The [first version](Yin_C_A0167_v1.md#statement) gave existence of a fractional increment and deletion with an infinite character.
The two bounds on the tensor factors identify the actual increment for each Schur input in this version and permit the finite-order test at a specified kernel dimension.

In [A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract), this provides a finite-size test for the new commutator approach which takes account of the actual list of translations in the witness.
Repeated active labels and groups with no separating character remain available to a putative obstruction.
