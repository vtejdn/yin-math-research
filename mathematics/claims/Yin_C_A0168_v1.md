# An order-two character at the half-rank endpoint of a column pencil

## Statement

Let \(K\) be the subgroup generated by a finite list of group elements, and append a single label \(s\) in a discrete group \(G=\langle K,s\rangle\).
Assume that a circle character of \(G\) equals one on \(K\) and minus one on \(s\).
Suppose every pencil on the shortened list, with constant complex columns of coefficients as in the [prescribed-list criterion](Yin_C_A0167_v2.md#statement), has integer regular rank.
Use left convolution, right modules over the affiliated algebra \(U(G)\), and the unnormalized matrix trace.

Let \(L\) be a pencil on the longer list and \(\alpha\) a constant column in its output space.
If
\[
             \operatorname{rk}[L,\alpha]-\operatorname{rk}L=\tfrac12 ,
\]
then some \(g\in Ks\) satisfies \(g^2\in K\).
More precisely, for such a \(g\) the subgroup \(J=K\cap g^{-1}Kg\) is normalized by \(g\), and there is a unit \(b\) of the actual scalar division closure \(D(K)\) with
\[
              b\in U(J),\qquad (g b g^{-1})\,b=g^2.
\]
The proof gives \(b\) and its inverse in \(D(K)\cap U(J)\).

In particular a coset \(Ks\) containing no square root of an element of \(K\) excludes this increment.
This applies when \(K\) is closed under taking square roots in \(G\), since the character puts \(Ks\) outside \(K\).
It applies in particular to a malnormal \(K\) in a torsion-free \(G\).
These conclusions exclude a half-rank two-by-two Schur scalar whenever its list from the prescribed-list criterion satisfies these hypotheses.
In the general character-order-two case the norm equation supplies a necessary condition.

## Where a rank loss of one half occurs

Write \(L=[C,cs-d]\) and let \(\pi\) be the constant quotient of its output by the line spanned by \(\alpha\), choosing coordinates on that quotient.
A half increment forces \(\alpha\ne0\), and the increment equals \(1+\operatorname{rk}(\pi L)-\operatorname{rk}L\).
The [finite-character estimate](Yin_C_A0167_v2.md#an-isolated-label-of-finite-character-order) writes each of the ranks of \(L,\pi L\) as an integer minus a number in \([0,1/2]\).
If the appended column adds nothing or adds one, this loss is zero.
Otherwise the loss is one minus the rank of the appended column after projecting off the old image.
The two losses must differ by \(1/2\) up to sign because the increment is \(1/2\).
Thus one is \(1/2\) and the other is zero.
Work with the pencil realizing loss \(1/2\), retaining the notation \(C,c,d\) for its shortened matrix and its constant columns.

Let \(E\) project off the closed image of \(C\), and put \(e=Ec,\ f=Ed\).
The new column has \(\operatorname{rk}(es-f)=1/2\).
Both \(e\) and \(f\) have rank zero or one by integrality for the short pencil after adjoining a constant column, as one sees by quotienting by that column and adding its rank back.
Neither can be zero, so they have full initial support.
The character twist fixes \(C,E,e,f\) and preserves regular trace while it changes \(s\) into \(-s\).
The two operators \(es-f\) and \(es+f\) therefore have right kernel projections \(p_+,p_-\) of trace \(1/2\) each.

The entries of \(E\) belong to \(D(K)\).
Indeed every sublist of columns of \(C\) has integral rank by setting the omitted coefficient columns to zero.
Select a column just when the rank increases in the successive prefixes of \(C\).
Each positive increase is exactly one, and an increase of zero leaves the closed image unchanged.
The selected columns give a full-column-rank matrix \(C_0\) whose closed image equals that of \(C\).
For a nonempty selection,
\[
                     E=1-C_0(C_0^*C_0)^{-1}C_0^* .
\]
The positive full Gram matrix has an inverse with entries in \(D(K)\) by [affiliated Gram elimination](Yin_C_A0132_v1.md#argument). For the empty selection, \(E=1\).
Thus \(e,f\) are columns over \(D(K)\), and they are also bounded.

## Complementary eigenmodules and a relative norm

The right ideals \(p_+U(G)\) and \(p_-U(G)\) have zero intersection since \(es\) has zero kernel.
Their projection join has trace one by the finite projection dimension formula, hence equals one.
Their algebraic sum is all of \(U(G)\), because the image of the affiliated row \([p_+,p_-]\) is its full final support module, by the relative inverse of the row.
This last step permits complementary eigenmodules even if the two Hilbert-space ranges do not have closed algebraic sum.

On the two ideals the columns \(es\) and \(f\) agree and differ by a sign respectively, so their images over all of \(U(G)\) coincide.
As \(s\) is a unit, \(eU(G)=fU(G)\).
The full-column left inverse \((e^*e)^{-1}e^*\) gives the unique \(B\) with \(f=eB\), and the same argument in the reverse direction gives its inverse.
Both formulas have entries in \(D(K)\).
The unit \(B^{-1}s\) acts as \(+1,-1\) on the two complementary right ideals.
Consequently
\[
               (B^{-1}s)^2=1,\qquad B=sB^{-1}s .
\]

Write \(v\in\mathcal N(K)\) for the unitary polar factor of \(B\).
The polar factor of \(B^{-1}\) is \(v^*\), and multiplication on either side by a unitary carries the polar factor to the corresponding product on that side.
Uniqueness of polar decomposition now gives \(v=s v^*s\).
The Fourier support of \(v\) lies in \(K\), and that of \(s v^* s\) lies in \(sKs\).
The vector \(v\delta_1\) is nonzero, so some \(k\in K\) has \(sks\in K\).
With \(g=ks\) this gives \(g^2=k(sks)\in K\).
If \(K\) is malnormal and \(g\notin K\), then \(g^2\in K\cap gKg^{-1}\) forces \(g^2=1\).
Torsion-freeness excludes this choice, proving the stated consequence.

Put \(b=kB\).
Since \(b^{-1}g=B^{-1}s\), one has \(b=g b^{-1}g=(g b^{-1}g^{-1})g^2\).
Now \(g^2\in K\cap gKg^{-1}\), so \(b\) belongs to both \(U(K)\) and \(U(gKg^{-1})\).
The intersection of their group von Neumann algebras is \(\mathcal N(K\cap gKg^{-1})\), by the Fourier-support characterization of the trace-preserving subgroup expectations.
To obtain the same equality for affiliated algebras, take the bounded transform \(z(1+z^*z)^{-1/2}\) and recover \(z\) by spectral calculus within the intersection.
Because \(g^2\in K\), \(gKg^{-1}=g^{-1}Kg\). Conjugation by \(g\) interchanges \(K\) and \(gKg^{-1}\), preserving their intersection \(J\).
This proves \(b\in D(K)^\times\cap U(J)^\times\) and the displayed norm equation.
The same norm appears in the [index-two crossed-product calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0003_v1.md#complete-norm-criterion-and-the-half-rank-witness), obtained there under an index-two normal-subgroup and division-ring hypothesis.
Here the order-two character and the two extremal kernel dimensions produce it from the actual shortened list.
