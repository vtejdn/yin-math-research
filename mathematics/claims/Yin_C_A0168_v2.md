# Half-rank column increments outside a subgroup and a three-label Schur test

## Statement

Let \(K\) be generated by a finite list of labels, and let \(s\notin K\) be one more label in \(G=\langle K,s\rangle\).
Suppose every pencil on the shortened list in the sense of the [prescribed-list criterion](Yin_C_A0167_v2.md#statement) has integer regular rank.
Use left convolution, affiliated right modules, and the unnormalized matrix trace.
For any pencil \(L=[C,cs-d]\) on the longer list with constant columns \(c,d\), its regular rank is an integer minus a loss in \([0,1/2]\).

If the loss equals \(1/2\), or if for some constant column \(\alpha\) the increment \(\operatorname{rk}[L,\alpha]-\operatorname{rk}L\) equals \(1/2\), then some \(g\in Ks\) satisfies \(g^2\in K\).
For such a \(g\) the subgroup \(J=K\cap g^{-1}Kg\) is normalized by \(g\), and a unit \(b\in D(K)^\times\cap U(J)^\times\) satisfies
\[
                              (g b g^{-1})b=g^2.
\]
Here \(D(K)\) is the actual scalar division closure of \(\mathbb CK\) in the affiliated algebra \(U(K)\).
Thus a coset containing no square root of an element of \(K\) excludes the half endpoint.
This applies to a square-root-closed \(K\), and in particular to a malnormal \(K\) in a torsion-free group.
In a torsion-free group a free \(K\) also excludes this endpoint.

Suppose now that \(G\) is torsion-free and that every nonzero element of \(\mathbb CG\) acts injectively in its regular representation.
For \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b_0\\c_0&d_0\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\sum_h M_hh\) over \(\mathbb CG\), with \(a\ne0\), set \(S=d_0-c_0a^{-1}b_0\).
Then
\[
               \sum_{h\ne1}\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}M_h\le3
                    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
               \operatorname{rk}_G S\ne\tfrac12 .
\]
This excludes the half-dimensional kernel at the stated support cost.
Other nonintegral Schur ranks are outside this implication.

## Projection comparison for the extra label

Let \(E\) be the orthogonal projection off the closed image of \(C\), and set \(e=Ec,\ f=Ed\).
As \(C\) has coefficients in \(\mathbb CK\), these columns have entries in \(\mathcal N(K)\) under the trace-preserving subgroup embedding.
Quotienting by a nonzero constant column and adding back one computes the augmented short pencil's rank, so each column has rank zero or one after applying \(E\).
Appending \(cs-d\) adds the rank of \(es-f\).
If both \(e,f\) vanish the full rank is \(\operatorname{rk} C\). If just one vanishes it is \(\operatorname{rk} C+1\).
In these cases use loss zero.

When both have full initial support, work on \(U(G)^2\).
Let \(Q\in M_2(\mathcal N(K))\) be the kernel projection of \([-f,e]\), and let
\[
           V=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&s^{-1}\\s&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
be the graph projection of multiplication by \(s\).
The first coordinate identifies the intersection \(Q U(G)^2\cap V U(G)^2\) with \(\ker(es-f)\) by \(h\mapsto(h,sh)\).
Consequently the nullity \(\nu\) of the new column equals \(\operatorname{Tr}(Q\wedge V)\).
The image dimension of \([-f,e]\) is at least one, so \(\operatorname{Tr}Q\le1\).
For every \(u\in\mathcal N(K)\), \(\tau(us)=\tau(us^{-1})=0\), by Fourier support and \(s\notin K\).
Hence
\[
          \nu\le\operatorname{Tr}(QVQ)
                =\operatorname{Tr}(QV)
                =\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}Q\le\tfrac12 .
\]
The first inequality uses \(QVQ\ge Q\wedge V\), since the meet reduces both projections.
The rank is therefore \(\operatorname{rk}C+1-\nu\).

If the increment of a nonzero constant column \(\alpha\) is one half, let \(\pi\) be the constant output quotient by its span.
Then the increment is \(1+\operatorname{rk}(\pi L)-\operatorname{rk}L\).
The same bounds apply to \(\pi L\).
The two losses lie in \([0,1/2]\) and their difference is a half-integer, so one of them is \(1/2\).
Work with the pencil giving this endpoint, using \(C,E,e,f,Q,V\) for its own data.

## Equality and the relative norm

At equality \(\operatorname{Tr}Q=1\) and \(QVQ=Q\wedge V\) by faithfulness.
Writing \(W=Q\wedge V\), one has \(V(Q-W)=0\) since \((Q-W)V(Q-W)=0\).
Thus \(VQ=QV=W\), so \(2V-I\) preserves the affiliated kernel module of \([-f,e]\).
The image of \([-f,e]\) has dimension one, and both \(e\) and \(f\) have rank one.
Their final supports therefore equal the final support of the row block, by faithfulness and inclusion.
The affiliated images \(eU(G)\) and \(fU(G)\) coincide because each equals its final support module.

The entries of \(E\) belong to \(D(K)\).
Indeed, deleting columns by setting their coefficients to zero preserves the shortened-list hypothesis.
Retain a column of \(C\) just when the rank increases in successive prefixes. Each such increase is one, and a zero increase leaves the closed image unchanged.
The selected matrix \(C_0\) has full column rank and the same final support as \(C\).
With a nonempty selection \(E=1-C_0(C_0^*C_0)^{-1}C_0^*\), where the inverse is over \(D(K)\) by [affiliated Gram elimination](Yin_C_A0132_v1.md#argument).
For an empty selection \(E=1\).
It follows that \(f=eB\) with \(B=(e^*e)^{-1}e^*f\in D(K)\), and reversing \(e,f\) gives its inverse.
The kernel module of \([-f,e]\) now consists of \((h,Bh)\) for \(h\in U(G)\).
Its invariance under \(2V-I\) puts \((s^{-1}Bh,sh)\) in this graph for every \(h\).
Hence \(Bs^{-1}B=s\), or equivalently \(B=sB^{-1}s\).

Write \(v\in\mathcal N(K)\) for the unitary polar factor of \(B\).
The polar factor of \(B^{-1}\) is \(v^*\), and multiplication on either side by a unitary carries the polar factor to the corresponding product on that side.
Uniqueness of polar decomposition now gives \(v=s v^*s\).
The Fourier support of \(v\) lies in \(K\), and that of \(s v^* s\) lies in \(sKs\).
The vector \(v\delta_1\) is nonzero, so some \(k\in K\) has \(sks\in K\).
With \(g=ks\) this gives \(g^2=k(sks)\in K\).
If \(K\) is malnormal and \(g\notin K\), then \(g^2\in K\cap gKg^{-1}\) forces \(g^2=1\).
Torsion-freeness excludes this choice, proving the stated consequence.

Put \(b=kB\).
Since \(b^{-1}g=B^{-1}s\), one has \(b=g b^{-1}g=(g b^{-1}g^{-1})g^2\).
Now \(g^2\in K\cap gKg^{-1}\), so \(b\) belongs to both \(U(K)\) and \(U(gKg^{-1})\).
The intersection of their group von Neumann algebras is \(\mathcal N(K\cap gKg^{-1})\), by the Fourier-support characterization of the trace-preserving subgroup expectations.
To obtain the same equality for affiliated algebras, take the bounded transform \(z(1+z^*z)^{-1/2}\) and recover \(z\) by spectral calculus within the intersection.
Because \(g^2\in K\), \(gKg^{-1}=g^{-1}Kg\). Conjugation by \(g\) interchanges \(K\) and \(gKg^{-1}\), preserving their intersection \(J\).
This proves \(b\in D(K)^\times\cap U(J)^\times\) and the displayed norm equation.

## Free shortened subgroup and three labels

Suppose \(G\) is torsion-free and \(K\) is free.
The [subgroup intersection argument for free groups](Yin_C_A0169_v1.md#bounded-fourier-coefficients-and-a-finite-subgraph) gives \(D(K)\cap U(J)=D(J)\).
Here \(J\) is free, \(g\notin J\), and \(\langle J,g\rangle\) is torsion-free with normal subgroup \(J\) and quotient of order two.
It therefore has a skew-field division closure by the [free-by-elementary-amenable theorem](../audits/literature/Lin00/Yin_U004.md#the-division-ring-and-free-field-assertions-are-separate).
That skew field contains \(b\in D(J)\) and \(g\).
The norm equation gives \((b^{-1}g)^2=1\), forcing \(b^{-1}g=1\) or \(-1\) in the skew field.
This would give \(b=\pm g\in U(J)\), which is impossible by Fourier support.
Only the subgroup intersection theorem from the cited Claim enters this deduction.

For the two-by-two application suppose \(\operatorname{rk}_G S=1/2\).
The [Schur tensor construction](Yin_C_A0167_v2.md#a-pencil-for-the-particular-schur-scalar) gives a rank-one boundary whose two tensor factors both have affiliated rank \(1/2\), with total rank of inputs at most \(\sum_{h\ne1}\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}M_h\).
Combine opposite labels in one orientation and drop vanishing inputs without changing the boundary.
The [two- and three-input obstruction](Yin_C_A0166_v2.md#statement) leaves precisely three distinct active labels with input rank one each, and each pair freely generates a rank-two free group.
For this very boundary the [increment bounds for its two factors](Yin_C_A0167_v2.md#passing-from-a-commutator-sum-to-a-fractional-column-increment) force an increment of \(1/2\) for a pencil on those three labels.
Let \(K\) be the free subgroup on the first two labels.
If the third belongs to \(K\), all the pencil ranks, including the constant augmentation, are integers by the free-group Atiyah theorem and the subgroup embedding.
If the third lies outside \(K\), the shortened pencil has integer rank in that same free subgroup, and the free-subgroup exclusion just proved applies.
This contradiction proves the support-cost bound.

The [first version](Yin_C_A0168_v1.md#statement) used a character exchanging two exceptional phases.
The present bound and its equality case use only that the extra label lies outside the shortened subgroup.
