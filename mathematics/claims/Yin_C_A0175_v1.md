# Matrix dimensions needed to preserve denominators in a torsion-free wreath product

## Statement

Let \(a,t\) generate \(W=\mathbb Z\wr\mathbb Z\), where \(a\) is the lamp at height zero and \(t\) shifts the heights.
Write \(a_i=t^iat^{-i}\) and \(d_j=1-a_ja_0^{-1}\) for \(j\ge1\).
These \(d_j\) are units of the affiliated division closure \(D_W\) of \(\mathbb CW\).
For a finite set \(S\) of positive integers the smallest positive dimension of a complex representation of \(W\) in which all \(d_j,\ j\in S\), act invertibly is exactly
\[
                 \min\{q\ge1:\ q\nmid j\text{ for all }j\in S\}.
\]
The same lower bound holds for positive-dimensional representations over any commutative field.
In particular, for \(S=\{1,\ldots,r\}\) the minimum is \(r+1\).
If any discrete group \(H\) contains \(W\), there is no unital ring map \(D_H\to M_n(F)\) for a finite \(n\ge1\) and a commutative field \(F\).
This applies in particular to \(K\wr\mathbb Z\) whenever \(K\) contains an element of infinite order.
The extension is torsion-free whenever \(K\) is torsion-free.
Thus periodic matrix evaluations of the kind that detect a nonrational [binary lamplighter projection](Yin_C_A0173_v1.md#periodic-specialization-of-a-proposed-rational-projection) face an obstruction in the full division closure of such a group.

For \(W\) itself, allowing the period to increase does give exact tests of every fixed finite family of rational matrix expressions.
At period \(q\) take independent commuting variables \(x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1},z\) over \(\mathbb C\).
Represent the lamps as the cyclic translates of the diagonal \(\operatorname{diag}(x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1})\) and the height shift by a cyclic shift matrix whose \(q\)-th power is \(zI\).
For every fixed matrix of scalar rational expressions defined over \(D_W\), all its gates are defined in these matrix models at every sufficiently large \(q\).
Its ordinary rank over \(\mathbb C(x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1},z)\) then equals \(q\) times its unnormalized von Neumann rank.
The period may depend on the expressions.

## Argument

### Characters of the lamp subgroup

For \(j\ge1\) the group element \(a_ja_0^{-1}\) has infinite order, so \(1-a_ja_0^{-1}\) is injective in the left regular representation on every coset of its cyclic subgroup.
It is therefore an affiliated unit, and its inverse belongs to \(D_H\) for every overgroup \(H\) of \(W\).
For a group representation of finite dimension \(n\), extend scalars to an algebraic closure of its coefficient field.
The matrix algebra generated by the commuting lamps and their inverses is commutative and finite dimensional.
The represented space decomposes into its nonzero simultaneous generalized weight spaces for the characters of this algebra.
Indeed, a finite set of generating matrices suffices in a finite-dimensional algebra, and repeated decomposition into the generalized eigenspaces of these commuting matrices gives this decomposition.
Each lamp is an invertible matrix with a single eigenvalue on any one weight space.
Conjugation by the represented \(t\) permutes the weight spaces.
If a weight has orbit length \(q\), its eigenvalues at \(a_j\) and \(a_0\) agree whenever \(q\mid j\).
Their ratio then acts unipotently on that nonzero space, by simultaneous triangularization.
Hence \(d_j\) is singular.
Invertibility of all the indicated denominators requires each orbit length to satisfy the divisibility condition in the minimum, and every orbit has at most \(n\) members.

For the converse take \(q\) equal to that minimum, choose \(q\) distinct nonzero complex numbers and put them on the diagonal of \(a_0\).
Let \(t\) act by a \(q\)-cycle and obtain the other lamps by conjugation.
The resulting diagonal lamps commute, so this is a representation of \(W\).
For \(q\nmid j\) all diagonal entries of \(a_ja_0^{-1}\) differ from \(1\).
This proves the minimum formula, including for \(S\) empty.
For a proposed unital ring map \(D_H\to M_n(F)\), restriction to \(W\) gives a group representation preserving the inverses of \(d_1,\ldots,d_n\).
Their lower bound exceeds \(n\).
An infinite-order lamp in \(K\) embeds \(W\) in \(K\wr\mathbb Z\) by using its cyclic subgroup at each height.

### Exact normalized ranks for each fixed expression

The group \(W\) is amenable and locally indicable.
For local indicability, a nontrivial finitely generated subgroup either maps onto a nonzero subgroup of the heights or lies in the free abelian base.
Thus nonzero elements of \(\mathbb CW\) are affiliated units by [strong Atiyah integrality for locally indicable groups](../audits/literature/JZLA20/Yin_U001.md#jzla20-u001).
The group ring is an Ore domain by [Linnell's amenable localization theorem](../audits/literature/Lin06/Yin_U001.md#amenable-domain-localization).
Its classical quotient division ring embeds in the affiliated ring by the universal property and equals \(D_W\).

Write \(F_q=\mathbb C(x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1},z)\).
Every nonzero group-ring polynomial \(P=\sum_{j=l}^k c_jt^j\), with \(c_k\ne0\), acts by an invertible matrix in \(M_q(F_q)\) for all sufficiently large \(q\).
Indeed, the lamp indices in \(c_k\) are contained in one finite interval.
At periods greater than its diameter they reduce to distinct residues, also after every cyclic translation.
The diagonal matrix representing \(c_k\) then has nonzero Laurent polynomial entries on every diagonal site.
Adjoin \(y\) with \(y^q=z\) and change the basis diagonally so that the height shift matrix is \(y\) times the ordinary cyclic permutation matrix \(T\).
Such a basis is obtained successively around the cycle, with consistency given by the product \(z\) of its edge factors.
Lamp diagonals are unchanged by this basis change.
In the new basis the determinant of \(P\) as a Laurent polynomial in \(y\) has highest term of degree \(qk\) with coefficient \(\det(c_kT^k)\ne0\), where \(c_k\) denotes its evaluated diagonal.
It follows that \(P\) was already invertible over \(F_q\).

The coordinate evaluations thus give a map from \(\mathbb CW\) to \(\prod_{q\ge1} M_q(F_q)/\bigoplus_{q\ge1}M_q(F_q)\) which sends every nonzero element to a unit.
Ore localization extends it to a unital map on \(D_W\).
In this quotient, equality is eventual equality and a square matrix is invertible exactly when its coordinates are eventually invertible.
Induction on finitely many rational gates therefore computes their images by ordinary matrix operations at every sufficiently large period.
Given a rectangular matrix \(B\) over \(D_W\), elementary invertible row and column operations over the division ring reduce \(B\) to a rectangular diagonal with \(r\) ones.
These operations are invertible over the affiliated ring, so \(r\) is its unnormalized von Neumann rank.
The same finitely many identities, including inverses of the two change-of-basis matrices, hold in every sufficiently large model and yield ordinary rank \(qr\).
In particular, these exact ranks for individual expressions coexist with the obstruction to any one finite-dimensional evaluation of the entire division closure.
