# A rational lamplighter contraction with a nonrational projection at every interior rational

## Statement

Let \(\Gamma=C_2\wr\mathbb Z\) and let \(D_\Gamma\) be the division closure of \(\mathbb C\Gamma\) in its affiliated ring.
In the [Fourier representation of the lamps](Yin_C_A0138_v1.md#finite-height-relations-at-a-periodic-configuration), \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\) records the bit of the edge from height \(i\) to \(i+1\).
Use \(b\) for the multiplication operator on edges, \(e\) for the indicator of the vertices with \(b_{i-1}=0,\ b_i=1\), and \(q=1-e\).
Let \((\nabla v)_i=v_{i+1}-v_i\), \(g=\nabla q\), and put
\[
             P=g(g^*g+e)^{-1}g^*, \qquad H=P b P .
\]
Then \(P\) is an orthogonal projection and \(H\in D_\Gamma\) is a positive contraction with a purely atomic spectral measure supported on \([0,1]\).
Its point spectrum consists of all rational numbers in \([0,1]\).
The spectral projections at \(0\) and \(1\) belong to \(D_\Gamma\).
Every interior rational has a nonzero spectral projection satisfying the unrestricted finite-rank equation and lying outside \(D_\Gamma\).

More generally use the Bernoulli law with \(\Pr(b_i=1)=\beta\in(0,1)\) in the regular crossed product, and its intrinsic division closure generated by the cylinder functions and the shift.
The same assertions about the spectrum and rationality of its projections hold, with the unrestricted group equation above asserted for the group trace \(\beta=1/2\).
Write \(\mu_\beta\) for the trace spectral law of \(H\).
Then \(\mu_\beta(\{0\})=1-\beta\), \(\mu_\beta(\{1\})=\beta^2\), and for coprime positive \(a,c\),
\[
 \mu_\beta\!\left(\left\{\frac{c}{a+c}\right\}\right)
   =\beta(1-\beta)\frac{\beta^a(1-\beta)^c}{1-\beta^a(1-\beta)^c}.
\]
Its scalar Cauchy transform has no meromorphic continuation through any point of \([0,1]\) from the upper half-plane.

## Argument

### The edge projection on one cell

Almost surely the marked vertices are unbounded in each direction, by independence of disjoint adjacent pairs of bits.
Two successive marks bound a word \(1^n0^m\) with \(n,m\ge1\).
On the \(n+m\) edges of this cell, the gradients of vertex functions vanishing at the marks are exactly the vectors whose coordinates sum to zero, by taking successive partial sums.
The kernel of \(g\) consists precisely of vertex functions supported on the marks.
In particular \(g^*g+e\) is injective and has an affiliated inverse.
On each finite cell the formula for \(P\) projects onto the mean-zero edge vectors.
These finite cells together exhaust the regular representation almost surely, so the affiliated expression is the bounded orthogonal range projection there.
It belongs to the division closure, as does \(H\).

In a cell let \(l=n+m\) and write \({\bf 1}_n,{\bf 1}_m\) for the constant vectors on its two runs.
The mean-zero spaces on the two individual runs are eigenspaces of \(H\) for \(1\) and \(0\), of dimensions \(n-1,m-1\).
The constant vector on the whole cell contributes one further zero eigenvalue.
The remaining line in the mean-zero space is spanned by \((m{\bf 1}_n,-n{\bf 1}_m)\).
Multiplication by \(b\) followed by subtraction of the mean sends this vector to \(m/l\) times itself.
This diagonalizes the cell matrix, including the case of runs of length one.

### The atomic law

For a fixed cell type \((n,m)\), the probability that such a cell starts at height zero is
\[
                  w_{nm}=\beta(1-\beta)\,\beta^n(1-\beta)^m.
\]
Indeed the edge just before the cell is zero and the first edge after it is one.
The contribution of this type to the trace of any of its spectral projections is \(w_{nm}\) times its rank on one cell.
To see this, partition the diagonal trace at the origin by the distance of the origin from its preceding mark.
By shift invariance, summing these expectations is the same as summing the diagonal entries on the cell conditional on its starting at zero, multiplied by \(w_{nm}\).
The three spectral multiplicities on a cell are \(m,\ n-1,\ 1\) at \(0,\ 1,\ m/(n+m)\).
Summing the geometric series gives \(\sum_{n,m}m w_{nm}=1-\beta\) and \(\sum_{n,m}(n-1)w_{nm}=\beta^2\).
For the specified interior slope the sum is over \((n,m)=(ka,kc)\), \(k\ge1\), yielding the displayed mass.
Since every such mass is positive, these atoms are dense in the interval and exhaust the spectral law.

For \(z\) in the upper half-plane put \(F(z)=\int(z-t)^{-1}\,d\mu_\beta(t)\).
For each real \(r\), dominated convergence applied to \(iy/(r+iy-t)\), whose absolute value is at most one, gives
\[
                    \lim_{y\downarrow0}iyF(r+iy)=\mu_\beta(\{r\}).
\]
Each rational atom would therefore have to be a pole of any meromorphic continuation on its neighborhood.
Every neighborhood of a point in the interval contains infinitely many of these atoms accumulating inside it, contradicting discreteness of poles.
Thus this scalar resolvent already has the asserted boundary for the original rational operator \(H\).

### Rational and nonrational atoms

Let \(Q_+\) indicate vertices with both adjacent edges of bit one and let \(Q_-\) indicate those with both adjacent edges of bit zero.
For either \(Q\), the operator \(v=\nabla Q\) has kernel \(1-Q\) in the sense of its orthogonal projection.
Indeed \(\nabla\) is injective on square-summable sequences on the integer heights.
The range projection \(p_Q=v(v^*v+1-Q)^{-1}v^*\) therefore belongs to the division closure.
The finite-cell description shows that its range consists of the mean-zero spaces on the corresponding single runs.
The endpoint projections of \(H\) are consequently \(p_{Q_+}\) at one and \(I-P+p_{Q_-}\) at zero.

Fix \(r=c/(a+c)\) with coprime positive \(a,c\) and suppose \(p=1_{\{r\}}(H)\) belongs to the division closure.
Then periodic evaluation of its rational expression gives the kernel projection of \(H'-rI\), since \((H-rI)^2+p\) is an affiliated unit and the inverse survives evaluation. [Periodic specialization](Yin_C_A0173_v1.md#periodic-specialization-of-a-proposed-rational-projection)
At a periodic word \(1^n0^m\), the Bloch factor at the closing edge multiplies a zero marked endpoint when taking the gradient on \(q\).
Thus \(P'\) is again the mean-zero projection on these \(n+m\) edges.
The height-zero diagonal entry of \(p'\) is nonzero exactly when \(n/m=a/c\), since the eigenvector on the remaining line has first coordinate \(m\).
A rational realization of \(p\) would give a numerator for that entry with bounded array rank across all sufficiently large \(n,m\), with an everywhere nonzero denominator. [Two-run determinant expansion](Yin_C_A0173_v1.md#a-finite-sum-of-products-for-every-rational-numerator)
This expansion applies with the border at height zero, which is one of the fixed boundary coordinates.
Its restriction to \(n=ak,\ m=cs\) would be nonzero exactly when \(k=s\), giving unbounded rank over the Bloch field.
This proves nonmembership in the intrinsic division closure at all the stated weights.
At the group trace these kernel projections belong to the regular closure and hence satisfy the [unrestricted equation](Yin_C_A0131_v1.md#statement).
The group \(\Gamma\) has torsion, so the example supplies sharp instances of the distinction used in the [angular test](Yin_C_A0174_v1.md#statement).
