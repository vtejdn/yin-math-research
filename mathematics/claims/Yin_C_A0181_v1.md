# A finite window of conjugate subgroups for a translated column

## Statement

Let \(G=\langle K,s\rangle\) be a discrete group, \(K\) a subgroup, and \(e,f\) bounded columns of equal length over \(\mathcal N(K)\).
Assume that \(e\) or \(f\) has trivial right kernel in the regular representation.
For an integer \(m\ge2\), put
\[
 K_m=\langle s^i K s^{-i}:0\le i\le m-2\rangle,\qquad
 r_m=\big|\{K_ms^i:0\le i\le m-1\}\big|.
\]
With canonical scalar trace on one coordinate and unnormalized matrix trace, the right nullity satisfies
\[
                    \nu_G(es-f)\le\frac1{r_m}.
\]
In particular there is a bound \(1/m\) whenever none of \(s,s^2,\ldots,s^{m-1}\) belongs to \(K_m\).
Only the number of distinct cosets is needed when some collide.

Suppose \(K\) is generated by a finite list for which every [prescribed-list pencil](Yin_C_A0167_v2.md#statement) has integer rank.
After appending a single label \(s\), each pencil has rank an integer minus a number in \([0,1/r_m]\), and every increment from adjoining one constant column has distance at most \(1/r_m\) from the integers.
The estimate applies to the [particular increment of a Schur scalar](Yin_C_A0167_v2.md#a-pencil-for-the-particular-schur-scalar) when its prescribed list has that form.
Unbounded \(r_m\) is equivalent to the existence of an integer character zero on \(K\) and taking \(s\) to one.
Individual finite windows can give arbitrarily strong bounds across examples with no nontrivial character of any order separating \(s\) from \(K\).

## The equations on the window

On coordinates \(0,\ldots,m-1\) put \(e_i=s^i e s^{-i}\) and \(f_i=s^i f s^{-i}\), with entrywise conjugation.
Let \(Q\) be the orthogonal kernel projection of the block matrix for the equations
\[
                     e_i x_{i+1}=f_i x_i,\qquad 0\le i\le m-2 .
\]
Its entries lie in \(\mathcal N(K_m)\) by spectral calculus.
If \(e\) is injective, a solution with \(x_0=0\) vanishes successively at every coordinate.
Hence the first-coordinate map is injective on the solution module and \(\operatorname{Tr}Q\le1\).
If \(f\) is injective, use the last coordinate and the equations in reverse.
The same reasoning holds in affiliated modules, where each zero-kernel column has a left inverse between its supports.

Choose one index from each distinct coset and call the resulting set \(I\), of size \(r=r_m\).
Let \(C_I\) be restriction to those coordinates, and let \(Q_I\in M_r(\mathcal N(K_m))\) be the final support of \(C_I Q\).
Its unnormalized trace is at most \(\operatorname{Tr}Q\) by polar decomposition.
For \(h\in\ker(es-f)\) the vector with entries \(s^i h\) solves every window equation, since
\(e_i s^{i+1}h-f_i s^i h=s^i(es-f)h\).
Consequently the normalized column isometry \(W_I=r^{-1/2}(s^i)_{i\in I}\) takes this kernel into the range of \(Q_I\).
With \(V_I=W_IW_I^*\), dimension preservation and comparison of projections give
\[
 \nu_G(es-f)\le\operatorname{Tr}(Q_I\wedge V_I)
           \le\operatorname{Tr}(Q_I V_I).
\]
Indeed \(Q_I V_I Q_I\) dominates the meet, since the meet reduces both projections.
The entries of \(V_I\) are \(s^{i-j}/r\).
For \(i\ne j\) in \(I\), \(s^{i-j}\notin K_m\), so its product with any entry of \(Q_I\) has zero trace by Fourier support.
All diagonal entries of \(V_I\) equal \(1/r\).
This proves the sharper estimate \(\nu_G(es-f)\le\operatorname{Tr}(Q_I)/r\le1/r\).
Discarding the repeated cosets before forming the graph projection permits the bound even for unequal coset multiplicities.

For a short pencil \(B\), let \(E\) project off its closed image.
For the appended column \(cs-d\), set \(e=Ec,\ f=Ed\).
Each of these has rank zero or one by [integrality on the shortened list and constant quotient](Yin_C_A0168_v2.md#projection-comparison-for-the-extra-label).
If both vanish use loss zero; otherwise the rank of the full pencil is \(\operatorname{rk}B+1-\nu_G(es-f)\).
For a nonzero constant column \(\alpha\), the increment of adjoining it equals one plus the pencil rank in the quotient by its constant span minus the original pencil rank.
Both pencil ranks have the stated loss bound, so the increment differs from an integer by at most \(1/r_m\).
The zero constant column has increment zero.

## Finite windows and characters

If an integer character vanishes on \(K\) and sends \(s\) to one, each \(K_m\) is in its kernel and \(r_m=m\).
Conversely the quotient by the normal closure of \(K\) is cyclic, generated by the image of \(s\).
If it is finite of order \(d\) (including \(d=1\)), \(s^d\) is a word in finitely many conjugates \(s^i K s^{-i}\) with \(i\in\mathbb Z\).
Indeed the subgroup generated by all these conjugates contains \(K\), is normalized by \(s\), and is exactly the normal closure.
Conjugating this word by a sufficiently large positive power of \(s\) leaves \(s^d\) fixed and makes all its indices nonnegative.
Thus \(s^d\in K_m\) for all sufficiently large \(m\), giving \(r_m\le d\) there.
The finitely many earlier \(r_m\) are bounded as well.
This proves the converse and explains why a finite window can improve the global character bound but unbounded growth for a single pair gives the integer-character case.

For each \(N\ge2\), take
\[
    G_N=\langle s,t\mid t s^N t^{-1}=s^{N+1}\rangle,\qquad K=\langle t\rangle .
\]
It is an HNN extension of the infinite cyclic group on \(s\), identifying the subgroups generated by \(s^N\) and \(s^{N+1}\).
It is torsion-free: a non-base element has a cyclically reduced conjugate with stable letters or a conjugate in the base, and positive powers of a cyclically reduced word containing stable letters stay reduced with positive stable-letter length.
The exponent of \(t\) gives \(K\cong\mathbb Z\).
Pencils on this subgroup have integer regular rank by ordinary Laurent polynomial rank almost everywhere on the circle.
Killing \(t\) in the defining relation forces \(s=1\), so the quotient by the normal closure of \(K\) is trivial.

For \(m=N+1\), \(K_m\) is generated by \(t_i=s^i t s^{-i}\) with \(0\le i<N\).
In a nonempty freely reduced word in \(t_i^{\pm1}\), consecutive stable letters of opposite signs are separated in the expanded word by \(s^{j-i}\), with \(0<|j-i|<N\).
Divisibility by \(N\) or \(N+1\) would be necessary for the corresponding HNN reduction, so there is no pinch.
The [HNN group normal form](Yin_C_A0145_v1.md#argument) places this word outside the base group; multiplication by a base power at either end cannot remove any of its stable letters.
It follows that \(K_m\cap\langle s\rangle=\{1\}\), giving \(r_{N+1}=N+1\).
At the next step \(t_0t_N^{-1}=t s^N t^{-1}s^{-N}=s\) and \(t_0=t\), so \(K_{N+2}=G_N\) and \(r_{N+2}=1\).
Thus retaining the window of length \(N+1\) gives a loss bound \(1/(N+1)\) even though the global quotient detects no phase.
The calculation establishes the coset bound and does not assert the existence of a pencil attaining that loss.

More generally take the free product of \(G_N\) with any finitely generated torsion-free group \(\Gamma\), and enlarge the shortened subgroup to \(\langle\Gamma,t\rangle\).
Retraction onto \(G_N\) fixes \(s\) and sends every enlarged window subgroup into its counterpart above, so the same distinct powers survive at \(m=N+1\).
Killing the enlarged shortened subgroup still kills the whole group.
The loss bound then applies whenever the specified shortened list has the assumed integer ranks, independently of integrality for all matrices on the enlarged ambient group.
