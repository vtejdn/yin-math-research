# Ordered subgroup products forced by equality in a transfer window

## Statement

In the [finite-window transfer bound](Yin_C_A0183_v1.md#statement) let \(K\le G\), \(s\in G\), \(G_0=\langle K,s\rangle\), \(S=sI_h\), and \(M\in M_h(U(K))\).
For some \(m\ge2\) put \(K_m=\langle s^iKs^{-i}:0\le i\le m-2\rangle\) and \(r=|\{K_m s^i:0\le i<m\}|\ge2\).
Suppose equality \(\dim_G\ker(S-M)=h/r\) holds.
Define \(L_i=s^iKs^{-i}\) and \(F=\langle L_0,\ldots,L_{r-1}\rangle\).
Then \(F\) is the normal closure of \(K\) in \(G_0\), and \(G_0/F\) is cyclic of order dividing \(r\).
For every \(1\le j<r\) put
\[
 A_j=\langle L_0,\ldots,L_{j-1}\rangle,\qquad
 B_j=\langle L_j,\ldots,L_{r-1}\rangle .
\]
The stronger ordered membership \(s^r\in B_j A_j\) holds for every cut.
If \(M\) has entries in the actual division closure \(D(K)\), \(G_0\) is torsion-free and \(F\) is free, this equality requires \(h/r\) to be integral.

For the [four-site rectangle](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#the-four-group-elements-and-the-trace-bound), suppose \(G\) is torsion-free with scalar group-ring injectivity, \(h=2\), and \(K=\langle t\rangle\) is infinite cyclic.
Suppose \(M(z)=(Az+B)(Cz+D)^{-1}\) for constant complex two-by-two matrices with both linear factors invertible over \(\mathbb C(z)\).
If equality holds with \(2/r\notin\mathbb Z\), then
\[
 t\in \langle sts^{-1},\ldots,s^{r-1}ts^{1-r}\rangle,
 \qquad K_m=F,\qquad |G_0/F|=r .
\]
In particular a four-coset window can attain half nullity for a rectangle only when the global quotient by the normal closure of \(t\) has order exactly four.

## Cutting the ordered product

The [equality identity](Yin_C_A0183_v1.md#transfer-matrices-and-equality) gives \((S^{-1}M)^r=I_h\); in particular \(M\) is an affiliated unit matrix, also over \(U(K)\) by subgroup trace preservation.
Writing \(M_i=S^i M S^{-i}\) gives the ordered equation
\[
        M_{r-1}\cdots M_0=S^r .
\]
The left side is over \(U(F)\), so the bounded group element \(s^r\) belongs to \(\mathcal N(F)\), and its Fourier support gives \(s^r\in F\).
Conjugation by \(s\) sends the last generating subgroup to \(s^r K s^{-r}\subset F\).
Conjugation by \(s^{-1}\) sends the first to \(s^{-r}L_{r-1}s^r\subset F\).
Thus \(F\) is normal in \(G_0\), is generated by conjugates of \(K\), and the quotient assertion follows.

At cut \(j\) set \(V=M_{r-1}\cdots M_j\) and \(W=M_{j-1}\cdots M_0\).
These are unit matrices in \(M_h(U(B_j))\) and \(M_h(U(A_j))\), respectively, and \(V=S^r W^{-1}\).
Take a nonzero entry of \(V\).
Its nonzero bounded polar partial isometry lies in \(\mathcal N(B_j)\) and equals \(s^r\) times the polar partial isometry of the corresponding entry of \(W^{-1}\), since left multiplication by a unitary preserves the absolute value.
Some Fourier coefficient of this partial isometry is nonzero, so \(B_j\cap s^rA_j\ne\varnothing\), proving the ordered membership.
For any \(b\) in that intersection, the entries of \(b^{-1}V=(b^{-1}s^r)W^{-1}\) belong to \(U(A_j\cap B_j)\) by the [subgroup intersection formula](Yin_C_A0168_v2.md#equality-and-the-relative-norm).

In the stated free-kernel case [Linnell's Theorem 1.3](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#lin93-u001) places the actual division closure of \(\mathbb CG_0\) in a division ring inside \(U(G_0)\).
Hence the actual division closure is itself a division ring containing \(s\) and \(D(K)\).
Gaussian elimination over it computes integer regular ranks for \(S-M\).
Right-coset replication preserves these dimensions in \(G\), which proves the integrality restriction under the specified rational-entry condition.

## A constant point correspondence in a two-dimensional transfer

Suppose \(s\notin\langle t\rangle\).
If \(M(z)\in {\rm GL}_2(\mathbb C(z))\) sends a fixed nonzero constant vector \(c\) to a multiple of a fixed nonzero constant vector \(d\), then \(sI_2-M(t)\) has integer regular rank under scalar group-ring injectivity.
When \(c,d\) span the same line this is the [constant invariant-line test](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#constant-lines-and-a-character-test).
Otherwise conjugate by the constant basis \((c,d)\).
The resulting rational matrix has the form \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&g(t)\\f(t)&h(t)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
The first pivot \(s\) leaves the Schur complement \(s-h(t)-f(t)s^{-1}g(t)\).
A common Laurent denominator of \(h,f\) on the left and a Laurent denominator of \(g\) on the right turn this expression into a group-ring scalar.
Its rank is therefore zero or one, giving the assertion.
This argument allows an arbitrary second column.

Now apply the first cut with \(B_1=\langle L_1,\ldots,L_{r-1}\rangle\).
Choose \(b\in B_1\cap s^rK\) and let \(b^{-1}s^r=t^k\).
Then \(t^k M(t)^{-1}\) has entries in \(U(K\cap B_1)\).
If \(K\cap B_1\) is trivial, this makes \(M(t)\) a scalar monomial times a constant matrix, giving a constant point correspondence.
If the intersection is a proper nontrivial subgroup \(\langle t^e\rangle\), \(e\ge2\), its affiliated entries are measurable functions of \(z^e\) on the Fourier circle of \(t\).
Thus \(M(\zeta z)=\zeta^k M(z)\) for a primitive \(e\)-th root \(\zeta\), first almost everywhere and then as a rational identity.

Write \(N(z)=(Az+B)\operatorname{adj}(Cz+D)\), and divide all entries by their common polynomial gcd to obtain a primitive polynomial matrix \(N_0\).
Its entries have degree at most two and it is a nonzero rational scalar multiple of \(M\).
The matrices \(N_0(\zeta z)\) and \(N_0(z)\) are proportional over \(\mathbb C(z)\).
As both lists of entries are primitive, clearing a reduced scalar fraction shows its numerator and denominator are constants.
All nonzero coefficient matrices of \(N_0\) must therefore have degrees in one residue class modulo \(e\).
At most two degrees among \(0,1,2\) survive, so their complex linear span has dimension at most two.
Choose an invertible value of \(N_0(z)\) as one basis element \(A_0\) for this span, which exists by generic invertibility.
If the span has a second basis element \(A_1\), take an eigenvector \(c\) of \(A_0^{-1}A_1\); otherwise take any nonzero \(c\).
All values of \(N_0(z)c\) then belong to the constant line through \(A_0c\), giving the point correspondence for \(M\).

In a window with \(r\ge2\) one has \(s\notin K\), so both proper-intersection cases prove integer rank and contradict the assumed nonintegral endpoint.
It follows that \(K\subseteq B_1\), so \(F=B_1\).
Conjugate this equality by \(s^{-1}\) and use normality of \(F\) to obtain \(F=\langle L_0,\ldots,L_{r-2}\rangle\).
Since \(r\le m\), this group lies in \(K_m\), which in turn lies in \(F\).
The quotient is generated by the image of \(s\), has at most \(r\) elements, and contains the \(r\) distinct cosets detected by the window, proving its exact order.

## A cut which excludes the endpoint after the power enters the group

In the torsion-free group \(\langle s,t\mid t s^3 t^{-1}=s^4\rangle\), let \(t_i=s^its^{-i}\), \(K=\langle t\rangle\) and \(m=4\).
The [HNN window calculation](Yin_C_A0181_v1.md#finite-windows-and-characters) gives \(r=4\), while \(t_0t_3^{-1}=s\) puts \(s^4\) in \(F=\langle t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3\rangle=G_0\).
Nevertheless \(s^4\notin\langle t_2,t_3\rangle\langle t_0,t_1\rangle\).

To prove this, take a freely reduced word in \(t_2,t_3\) followed by one in \(t_0,t_1\), allowing inverses and empty words.
Inside either part, consecutive opposite stable letters at distinct sites have an intervening nonzero power of \(s\) of absolute exponent at most one.
Opposite stable letters at equal adjacent sites are excluded by free reduction.
Thus HNN normal form admits no reduction inside either part.
If the whole word belongs to \(\langle s\rangle\), all stable letters must be removed successively at the seam between the two parts; a remaining nonempty reduced part alone cannot represent a base element.
Starting with the seam \(s^k=1\), each removal replaces \(t_i^{\pm1}s^kt_j^{\mp1}\) by \(s^{k'}\) for \(i\in\{2,3\}\), \(j\in\{0,1\}\).
Writing \(a=i-j\in\{1,2,3\}\), the relation gives \(k'=(4k-a)/3\) for the positive left sign and \(k'=(3k+a)/4\) for the negative left sign, whenever the respective reduction is defined.
Both are integers then.
For \(k\le0\) the first is at most zero and the second is at most \(3/4\), hence also at most zero.
Induction shows that every base exponent obtained from this ordered product is nonpositive.
This excludes \(s^4\) and hence excludes the four-coset endpoint for every affiliated transfer over \(U(\langle t\rangle)\), independently of scalar group-ring injectivity.
