# The middle subgroup cut in a four-step matrix transfer

## Statement

Let \(G=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free.
Write \(t_i=s^its^{-i}\), \(N=\langle t_0,t_1,t_2\rangle\), \(q=s^2\) and \(g=s^4\).
Assume \(N\) is normal, \(G/N\) is cyclic of order four generated by \(sN\), and \(g\in N\).
Set
\[
             A=\langle t_0,t_1\rangle,\quad B=\langle t_2,t_3\rangle,
             \quad J=A\cap B,\quad\phi=\operatorname{Ad}(q).
\]
Take any two-by-two invertible rational matrix \(M(Z)\in{\rm GL}_2(\mathbb C(Z))\) with \(t\) of infinite order.
In the regular affiliated algebra \(U(G)\) put \(M_i=M(t_i)\) and \(W=M_1M_0\).
The [four-phase equality](Yin_C_A0183_v1.md#transfer-matrices-and-equality) gives the norm identity \(M_3 M_2 M_1 M_0=g I_2\) when its window has four distinct cosets and the kernel of \(s I_2-M_0\) has dimension \(1/2\).
The following assertions concern that norm identity.

* It forces \(g\in BA\) and \(J\ne\{1\}\).
  For every factorization \(g=b a\) with \(b\in B,\ a\in A\), set \(z=a q^{-1}\).
  Then \(z\) exchanges \(A\) and \(B\) by conjugation, normalizes \(J\), and \(1\ne z^2\in J\).
  The matrix \(L=W a^{-1}\) lies in \({\rm GL}_2(U(J))\) and satisfies \(L\operatorname{Ad}(z)(L)=z^{-2}I_2\).
* Suppose in addition that nonzero group-ring scalars are regularly injective, that \(\langle t_0\rangle\cap\langle t_1\rangle=\{1\}\), and that \(J\) is abelian with \(z\) centralizing \(J\) for some such cut.
  Then the norm identity excludes a half-dimensional kernel of \(sI_2-M(t)\).
  In particular an infinite cyclic \(J\) requires no separate centralization assumption.
  No rationality of the entries of \(L\) over \(J\) is needed.

## Regular Fourier support of the cut

Identify a group element multiplying a matrix with its scalar diagonal.
Since \(B=\phi(A)\), the norm identity is \(\phi(W)W=g\).
The matrix \(W\) is affiliated over \(A\), while \(W=V g\) with \(V=\phi(W)^{-1}\) affiliated over \(B\).
The bounded transform \(W(1+W^*W)^{-1/2}\) is affiliated and bounded over \(A\).
It also equals \(V(1+V^*V)^{-1/2}g\), by unitary conjugation in the functional calculus.
Each entry has Fourier support inside \(A\cap Bg\), and at least one entry is nonzero because \(W\) is invertible.
Thus \(g\in BA\).
For every chosen \(g=b a\), the matrix \(W a^{-1}=V b\) is affiliated over both \(A\) and \(B\), as is its inverse.
The intersection of their von Neumann algebras is the von Neumann algebra of \(J\): the subgroup conditional expectation retains exactly the Fourier coefficients on that subgroup.
The affiliated intersection is \(U(J)\), by the bounded transform and its recovery through spectral calculus, entrywise.
This proves the assertion about \(L\).

Put \(c=\phi^{-1}(b)\in A\).
Since \(\phi^2=\operatorname{Ad}(g)\) and \(\phi(g)=g\), applying \(\phi\) to \(g=\phi(c)a\) also gives \(g=\phi(a)c\).
Consequently
\[
                    z^2=a g^{-1}\phi(a)=b^{-1}\phi(a)=a c^{-1}\in J.
\]
Here \(a\in N\) while \(qN\) is the nonidentity element of order two in \(G/N\).
Thus \(z\ne1\), and torsion-freeness gives \(z^2\ne1\).
In addition \(zBz^{-1}=A\) and \(zAz^{-1}=z^2 B z^{-2}=B\); hence \(z\) normalizes \(J\).
The norm identity gives \((q^{-1}W)^2=1\), so also \((Wq^{-1})^2=1\) by conjugation by \(q\).
Substituting \(Wq^{-1}=L z\) yields the norm over \(J\).
For infinite cyclic \(J\), an automorphism fixing \(z^2\ne1\) fixes its generator, so \(z\) centralizes \(J\).

## A constant projective direction gives integer rank

Under the scalar injectivity hypothesis, suppose \(M(Z)c=d f(Z)\) for nonzero constant columns \(c,d\) and a scalar rational function \(f\).
Write \(U=U(G)\).
If \(c,d\) are dependent, a simultaneous constant change of basis makes \(M\) triangular with diagonal rational functions of \(t\).
Each diagonal scalar \(s-r(t)\) of the transfer is a unit: right multiplication by a polynomial denominator gives a nonzero group-ring scalar since \(s\notin\langle t\rangle\).
Thus the transfer has rank two.

For independent \(c,d\), use them as the first and second columns of a constant basis.
Then \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&k\\ f&h\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(k,f\ne0\).
Solving the first equation of the transfer for its second unknown leaves the scalar \((s-h)k^{-1}s-f\).
Multiply it on the right by \(s^{-1}\) to obtain \(s k^{-1}-h k^{-1}-f s^{-1}\).
A scalar polynomial in \(t\) on the left clears the denominators of \(h k^{-1}\) and \(f\), and another on the right clears that of \(k^{-1}\) in the first term.
The result is a group-ring scalar, with only affiliated units used for these multiplications.
It is either zero or an affiliated unit by hypothesis.
The original two-dimensional transfer therefore has integer rank, including when the polynomial vanishes identically.

## Tracing the middle two factors

Suppose a half-dimensional kernel occurs under the second set of assumptions in the statement.
Write \(M(Z)=H(Z)/D(Z)\) with \(H(Z)=\sum_i H_i Z^i\) a polynomial matrix and \(D\) a nonzero scalar polynomial.
The matrices \(H_i\) must span a complex space of dimension at least three.
Indeed choose a scalar \(z_0\) with \(H(z_0)\) invertible.
If the span had dimension at most two, normalization by \(H(z_0)^{-1}\) would give a space spanned by the identity and at most one additional matrix.
A common complex eigenvector \(c\) would give \(M(Z)c\) in the constant direction of \(H(z_0)c\), which the preceding argument excludes.

Use the ordinary two-by-two matrix trace, denoted \(\operatorname{tr}_2\), defined by summing the diagonal entries without interchanging affiliated factors.
Multiplying \(\operatorname{tr}_2 W\) on the left by \(D(t_1)\) and on the right by \(D(t_0)\) gives the group-ring scalar
\[
                      \sum_{i,j}\operatorname{tr}_2(H_iH_j)\,t_1^i t_0^j .
\]
All displayed group elements for distinct pairs of indices are distinct, since the two cyclic subgroups have trivial intersection.
The coefficient pairing \((C,E)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}_2(CE)\) on \(M_2(\mathbb C)\) is nondegenerate.
A subspace of dimension at least three cannot be contained in its orthogonal complement, so some coefficient is nonzero.
Scalar injectivity makes \(\operatorname{tr}_2 W\) an affiliated unit, hence so is \(\operatorname{tr}_2 L=(\operatorname{tr}_2 W)a^{-1}\).

The entries of \(L\) commute since \(J\) is abelian.
As \(z\) centralizes \(J\), the middle norm becomes \(L^2=z^{-2}I_2\).
Cayley-Hamilton over the commutative ring \(U(J)\) now gives
\[
                (\operatorname{tr}_2 L)L=(z^{-2}+\det L)I_2.
\]
The trace unit belongs to \(U(J)^\times\) by affiliated functional calculus, so \(L\) is a scalar matrix and \(W=L a\) commutes with every constant matrix \(X\).
It follows that
\[
 M_0 X M_0^{-1}=M_1^{-1} X M_1
   \ \in\ M_2\bigl(U(\langle t_0\rangle)\cap U(\langle t_1\rangle)\bigr)
   =M_2(\mathbb C).
\]
Faithful evaluation in the cyclic fraction field gives the same constant identity for \(M(Z)XM(Z)^{-1}\).
Comparison with its value at any regular invertible point shows \(M(z_0)^{-1}M(Z)\) commutes with every constant matrix, so it is scalar.
This supplies a constant projective direction and contradicts the half-dimensional kernel.

## A nonfree middle subgroup

Take the torsion-free Klein bottle group \(G=\langle r,v\mid r v r^{-1}=v^{-1}\rangle\), the semidirect product of the infinite cyclic groups generated by \(v\) and \(r\).
Put \(s=r,\ t=r^2 v\); these generate \(G\).
Then \(t_0=t_2=r^2v,\ t_1=t_3=r^2v^{-1}\), and \(g=r^4=t_0t_1\).
Inside the free abelian group \(\langle r^2,v\rangle\) the exponent vectors \((1,1),(1,-1)\) are independent.
Thus \(N=A=B=J\) is free abelian of rank two, and \(\langle t_0\rangle\cap\langle t_1\rangle=\{1\}\).
The subgroup \(N\) is normal, and setting \(t=1\) leaves the cyclic group of order four generated by \(r\).
Indeed \(v=r^{-2}\) then forces \(r^4=1\), and mapping \(r\) to \(1\) and \(v\) to \(2\) modulo four realizes the quotient.
The four window cosets of \(N\) are distinct.
Taking \(a=1,b=g\) gives \(z=q^{-1}=r^{-2}\), which centralizes \(J\).
The group satisfies scalar injectivity by the [torsion-free elementary amenable case](../audits/literature/Lin00/Yin_U004.md#the-division-ring-and-free-field-assertions-are-separate).
The middle norm calculation consequently excludes half-dimensional kernels for every such \(M\) in this example.
The example tests the actual four-coset endpoint with a nonfree kernel subgroup; it does not classify nonabelian intersections or cuts on which the relative conjugation acts nontrivially.
