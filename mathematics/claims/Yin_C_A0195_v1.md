# Relations detected by the two-dimensional phase test

## Statement

For a marked group \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\), there exist two fixed simple unitary spectra, one for each generator, with infinitely many distinct product traces in the [regular rank test](Yin_C_A0194_v1.md#statement) if and only if \(H\) has a quotient marked by the two standard generators of a free product of two nontrivial cyclic groups, allowing infinite cyclic factors.
More precisely, for a specified pair of spectra the orders of the two factors in the necessary quotient are the orders of the ratios of their respective eigenvalues in the circle.
Thus a perfect group can only give the finite alternative for a fixed pair of simple spectra.

For a finite presentation on the two labels the allowed phases for given spectra are exactly the unit-circle zeros of a single Laurent polynomial computed as a gcd of relator entries, with the zero ideal allowing the entire circle.
For the explicit torsion-free perfect group \(H_r\) and spectra used in the [perfect marking](Yin_C_A0194_v1.md#an-explicit-perfect-marking), the number of allowed phases is exactly \(8(n+1)\), \(r=12n+11\).

## Projective words in the phase parameter

Write \(S=aI+bJ_0,\ T_z=cI+dJ_z\) as in the [unitary normal form](Yin_C_A0194_v1.md#normal-form-for-a-pair-of-unitary-matrices).
Regard \(z\) as a formal central indeterminate.
These matrices and their inverses have entries in \(\mathbb C[z,z^{-1}]\), since both determinants are nonzero constants.
If infinitely many phases give actual representations of \(H\), each entry of each relator minus the identity vanishes identically as a Laurent polynomial.
Thus \(H\) maps onto the subgroup generated by the two projective matrices in \({\rm PGL}_2(\mathbb C(z))\).

In this group each generator has exactly the projective order of its eigenvalue ratio, by the distinct constant eigenvalues and diagonalizability.
The resulting map from the free product of the two cyclic groups is injective.
For the verification, a nontrivial reduced word is conjugate to a nonidentity element of one factor or to an alternating product
\[
                S^{i_1}T_z^{j_1}\cdots S^{i_\ell}T_z^{j_\ell},
                \qquad \ell\ge1 ,
\]
up to cyclic permutation.
Here no displayed power is scalar projectively.
Write \(S^{i_\alpha}=a_\alpha I+b_\alpha J_0\) and \(T_z^{j_\alpha}=c_\alpha I+d_\alpha J_z\), so \(b_\alpha d_\alpha\ne0\) for each block.
The coefficient of \(z^\ell\) in the ordinary matrix trace of the lifted alternating product is \(\prod_\alpha b_\alpha d_\alpha\).
Indeed the degree-one part of \(J_z\) is the matrix unit \(E_{21}\), and multiplying the resulting rank-one factors cyclically contracts each with the upper-right entry \(b_\alpha\).
Its trace is therefore nonconstant.
Its determinant is a nonzero constant, whereas any scalar matrix over \(\mathbb C(z)\) having constant determinant must itself be constant.
This proves projective nontriviality and the required injectivity.

Conversely a marked quotient onto the free product supplies representations for every phase by choosing two distinct admissible eigenvalues on each cyclic generator and using the free product presentation.
Every nontrivial free product of the indicated cyclic groups has nonzero abelianization, which proves the assertion for perfect groups.

## Finite relators and the exact count

For \(H=\langle s,t\mid w_1,\ldots,w_e\rangle\), evaluate the finitely many words at \(S,T_z\).
For a unit-circle \(z\), the assignment is a unitary representation if and only if every entry of every \(w_j(S,T_z)-I_2\) vanishes.
The Laurent polynomial ring over \(\mathbb C\) is a principal ideal domain, and a generator of the ideal of the entries has exactly their common zeros on \(\mathbb C^\times\).
This gives the claimed finite calculation once the spectra and presentation are specified.
It uses the full relator matrices, including their scalar signs.

In the concrete group \(H_r\), \(s^2=u\), \(w=u(st)^{-1}\) and \(w^r=u^{(5r-1)/6}\) hold inside the subgroup.
In any representation of the prescribed type, \(S^2=-I\) and \((5r-1)/6=10n+9\) is odd.
Thus \((-I)^r(ST_z)^{-r}=(-I)^{10n+9}\) forces \((ST_z)^r=I\).
The possible product traces are exactly the numbers \(2\cos(2\pi j/r)\) in the interval \([-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]\), using that both matrices have determinant one.
Neither endpoint occurs for these \(r\).
Taking one representative \(0\le j\le(r-1)/2\), the admissible values are precisely \(n+1,\ldots,5n+4\).
The [explicit representations](Yin_C_A0194_v1.md#counting-unitary-representations) realize all these values with two phases each.
The exact count quantifies the limit of this fixed-spectra test on each individual group, while the resulting upper bound decreases along the family.
