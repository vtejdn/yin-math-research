# Modulo thirteen homology in the index-twenty-six four-site rank test

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free, let every nonzero element of \(\mathbb CH\) be injective in its regular representation, and suppose \(H_{\rm ab}=C_{26}\).
Write \(N=[H,H]\).
For any constant complex two-by-two matrices \(A,B,C,D\), set \(K=B+At-s(D+Ct)\), with unnormalized matrix trace.
If \(K\) has regular rank \(3/2\), then
\[
                            H_1(N;\mathbb F_{13})=0 .
\]
In particular the abelianization of this finitely generated kernel is finite of order prime to thirteen.
The [four-column equivalence](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement) gives the same obstruction to a half-dimensional constant-column increment on the associated list.

The quantitative step also applies to an individual normalized rectangle without a scalar injectivity assumption.
Suppose there is a character with additive weights \((1,10)\) modulo 26 on the marked pair, and a homomorphism onto the dihedral group of order 26 sending \(s\) to a reflection and \(t\) to a generator of its rotations.
If \(A\) is invertible and \(U(y)=Ay+B,\ V(y)=Cy+D\) are both invertible over \(\mathbb C(y)\), with \(t\) of infinite order, then
\[
                            \dim_H\ker K\ \le\ 4/13 .
\]
The finite-dimensional representations below twist the regular representation itself, so all affiliated denominators are evaluated on their actual domain.

## The two pairs of extreme coefficients

Suppose \(\operatorname{rk}K=3/2\).
By the [order-26 companion construction](Yin_C_A0199_v1.md#the-two-extreme-coefficients-at-order-26), a shortest marked lift has nonzero weights of total absolute value seven and is primitive modulo 26.
For each such lift the polynomial of degree seven can be oriented with its leading corner invertible over the group ring.
In fact its other extreme corner is also invertible.
Indeed the companion acts by an operator of order dividing 26 on the kernel of its remainder and by the unit \(c\) on the complementary range of the remainder, by the [rank-one decomposition](Yin_C_A0199_v1.md#syzygies-and-the-single-denominator).
It is therefore invertible as an affiliated matrix.
Its kernel at zero is isomorphic by the shift equations to the kernel of its constant coefficient.
That coefficient is consequently of full rank and is a single group element times an invertible constant matrix.
Reversing the polynomial exchanges the two endpoints.

For the arithmetic of the two endpoint pairs, first allow exchange of coordinates and separate sign changes to arrange a positive shortest vector \((p,q)\) with \(p<q\).
The lattice modulo 26 is generated by this vector, since it has order 26.
For \((1,6)\), multiplication by four would give the lift \((4,-2)\) of width six.
Thus the remaining possibilities are \((2,5)\) and \((3,4)\).
They give second lifts \((-4,3)\) and \((-5,2)\), respectively: the determinant with the first is 26, so the second belongs to its cyclic line modulo 26 by primitivity.
Both have width seven.
Undoing the coordinate operations, there is a shortest lift in each of the two sign patterns (same or opposite).
Their two pairs of extreme sites between them contain all four corners, proving \(A,B,C,D\in{\rm GL}_2(\mathbb C)\).

The odd coordinate of a positive shortest pair is a unit modulo 26, and the even coordinate divided by the odd one modulo 26 is \(16=-10\) for \((2,5)\) and \(10\) for \((3,4)\).
Changing the generator of the cyclic character, orienting the labels and exchanging them can therefore give weights \((1,10)\).
These operations preserve a four-site input of the same rank with invertible corners.
Replacing \(s\) by \(s^{-1}\) uses \(s^{-1}K\), and replacing \(t\) by \(t^{-1}\) uses \(Kt^{-1}\).
Taking the adjoint replaces the ordered pair by \((t^{-1},s^{-1})\) and exchanges the coefficient sites.
The changes preserve the subgroup, its commutator subgroup and the scalar injectivity premise.

## From homology to an actual rotation and reflection

Use such a normalized pair, and put \(B_N=N/[N,N]\) with additive notation.
Conjugation by \(s\) gives an automorphism \(S\) of \(B_N\) with \(S^{26}=1\).
Since \(H\) is generated by \(s\) and \(N\), the coinvariants \(B_N/(S-1)B_N\) vanish: quotient by the inverse image of \((S-1)B_N\) makes \(N\) central and the resulting group abelian, whereas \(N=[H,H]\).
The subgroup \(N\) is finitely generated by its finite index, so \(S-1\) is an automorphism of the finitely generated abelian group \(B_N\).
In particular \(s^{26}\), fixed by \(s\), maps to zero in \(B_N\).

Let \(W=B_N/13B_N\).
If \(W\ne0\), then \(S-1\) is invertible on \(W\) and
\[
            0=S^{26}-1=(S-1)^{13}(S+1)^{13}
\]
in characteristic thirteen.
Consequently \(W/(S+1)W\ne0\).
Choosing any nonzero linear functional on this quotient gives a surjection \(\phi:N\to\mathbb F_{13}\) with \(\phi(sns^{-1})=-\phi(n)\).
The quotient \(H/[N,N]\) is the semidirect product of \(B_N\) with the cyclic group generated by the image of \(s\), since \(s^{26}\) maps to zero in \(B_N\) and that image has exact order 26 modulo \(B_N\).
Thus \(\phi\) extends to a surjection onto \(\mathbb F_{13}\rtimes\{1,-1\}\) sending \(s\) to a reflection.
The even weight of \(t\) puts its image among the rotations, and the two marked images generate the target, so \(t\) generates that rotation subgroup.
Conversely any such dihedral quotient sends \(N\) onto the rotation subgroup, its commutator group, and so gives \(W\ne0\).

For a presentation on the normalized pair this quotient has a finite-word test.
Assign \(s\) the permutation \(x\mapsto-x\) of \(\mathbb F_{13}\), and \(t\) the translation \(x\mapsto x+1\).
The sign on each relator is already positive by the original parity character.
Its translation is the sum of the \(t\)-letter exponents, each weighted by \((-1)\) to the number of preceding \(s^{\pm1}\) letters.
All these sums must vanish modulo 13, and that condition suffices since the assigned permutations generate the dihedral group.
This criterion tests group identities before any operator inversions.

## A phase-dependent dihedral representation

For every \(z^{26}=1\), choose a two-dimensional unitary representation of the dihedral quotient in which the image of \(s\) exchanges the two coordinates and the image of \(t\) acts diagonally with eigenvalues \(z^{10}\) and \(z^{-10}\).
These choices respect the relations since \((z^{10})^{13}=1\).
Tensor with the cyclic character having values \(z,z^{10}\) on \(s,t\).
The resulting twist of regular left convolution has the same regular trace and ranks as the two-fold amplification, by the [fiber unitary](Yin_C_A0118_v1.md#regular-trace-identity-and-the-specialization).
In the resulting two auxiliary coordinates \(s\) acts as the swap times \(zs\); \(t\) acts by \(z^{20}t=z^{-6}t\) and \(t\).

Both \(V\) blocks and both \(U\) blocks are affiliated units under the cyclic substitution, by Fourier transformation along the infinite cyclic group of \(t\).
Multiplying by the inverses of the diagonal \(V\) blocks gives diagonal blocks \(M(z^{-6}t),M(t)\), with \(M=UV^{-1}\), and off-diagonal blocks \(-zsI_2\).
Eliminate the second diagonal block and multiply the remaining block on the right by \(V(z^{-6}t)\).
Up to its overall sign this leaves
\[
             P(z)=z^2(sI_2)M(t)^{-1}(sI_2)V(z^{-6}t)-U(z^{-6}t).
\]
At every one of the 26 phases its nullity is \(2\dim_H\ker K\).

The matrix \(z^6P(z)\) is a polynomial with affiliated coefficients of degree at most eight and constant term \(-At\).
Take one bounded affiliated unit on the left clearing its finitely many coefficient entries into bounded operators; this is possible by the common left denominator property of the finite affiliated algebra.
This operation preserves all the ranks, and its constant coefficient has full rank since \(A\) is invertible.
The [finite-evaluation inequality](Yin_C_A0022_v1.md#simultaneous-generic-rank-and-degeneration) bounds the sum of its deficits from rank two at the 26 roots by \(8\cdot2\).
The resulting inequality \(26\cdot2\dim_H\ker K\le16\) proves the quantitative assertion.
For the normalized putative half-rank input all four corners were already shown invertible, so both cyclic pencils are generically invertible and this contradiction applies.
It follows that \(W=0\), as required.
