# Scalar inverse layers under an infinite cyclic extension

## Statement

Let \(\chi:G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z\) be an epimorphism of discrete groups with kernel \(K\).
Choose \(t\in G\) of height one and write \(\sigma=\operatorname{Ad}_t\) on \(K\) and its regular affiliated algebra \(U(K)\).
Use \(D_0(H)=\mathbb CH\), let \(D_{j+1}(H)\) be the subring of \(U(H)\) generated by \(D_j(H)\) and the inverses of its affiliated units, and write \(P_j(H)\) when every nonzero scalar of \(D_j(H)\) is an affiliated unit.
These are the [actual scalar inverse layers](Yin_C_A0129_v1.md#statement), with no matrix inverse in the definition.

For each \(j\ge0\) one has
\[
                           P_j(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_j(K).
\]
More precisely, for \(j=0\) without hypotheses, or for \(j\ge1\) assuming only \(P_{j-1}(K)\), there is a faithful ring map
\[
             D_j(G)\longrightarrow D_j(K)((T;\sigma)),
           \qquad \sum_i a_i t^i\longmapsto\sum_i a_i T^i
                   \quad(a_i\in\mathbb CK).
\]
The codomain consists of formal series bounded below in exponent, with \(T a=\sigma(a)T\).
If the first nonzero coefficient of the image of \(x\ne0\) is \(a\), then
\[
                         0<\operatorname{rk}_K a\le\operatorname{rk}_G x .
\]
In particular a fractional-rank scalar at the next layer yields a nonzero, noninjective coefficient at that same layer inside the kernel, under the preceding-layer hypothesis.
This rank inequality does not require preservation of the particular fractional value.
The equivalence extends to extensions by a torsion-free abelian quotient.
It concerns transfer between groups at fixed depth; an implication \(P_j(H)\Rightarrow P_{j+1}(H)\) for a fixed arbitrary group remains open.

## Realizing the height expansion faithfully

First take \(G\) finitely generated.
Let \(z\) be a central formal indeterminate and \(Z\) the generator of the new central factor in \(G\times\mathbb Z\).
There is an injective map from \(U(K)((T;\sigma))\) to \(U(G)((z))\) defined by \(a_iT^i\mapsto (a_it^i)z^i\) term by term.
Multiplication respects the displayed skew rule and equality is coefficientwise.
On the group ring of \(G\) the resulting map sends \(g\) to \(g z^{\chi(g)}\).

The [central Laurent evaluation theorem](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#preservation-of-formal-identities) identifies the scalar division closure of \(\mathbb CG[z]\) in \(U(G)((z))\) with a subring of \(U(G\times\mathbb Z)\), sending \(z\) to \(Z\).
On our Laurent polynomials its value is the graph embedding \(g\mapsto(g,Z^{\chi(g)})\).
That group embedding extends faithfully and trace-preservingly to affiliated operators by right-coset replication and the bounded transform.
In particular evaluation of the formal expression and graph inclusion of its actual value agree on the group ring.

Argue by induction on the gates of an expression of depth at most \(j\).
Finite sums and products preserve the agreement.
For an inverse at depth \(i+1\le j\), the previous expression has depth at most \(i\) and represents an actual unit.
Its formal value is nonzero by the agreement already established.
Its lowest homogeneous coefficient is \(b t^q\) with \(0\ne b\in D_i(K)\), which is a unit by \(P_i(K)\).
Consequently the formal inverse exists by factoring out the lowest monomial and inverting a series with constant coefficient one.
Every resulting coefficient after stripping its power of \(t\) belongs to \(D_{i+1}(K)\), using only finitely many products, conjugations and \(b^{-1}\) at any prescribed exponent.
Conjugation by \(t\) preserves all kernel layers.
This is a legitimate scalar inverse in the central Laurent division closure.
Its evaluation is the actual inverse of the previous graph value.
Thus every expression under consideration has a formal value evaluating to the graph image of its actual value.
Injectivity of central evaluation identifies formal values of expressions with equal actual value, and faithfulness of the graph map gives the claimed injection.
This argument makes no surjectivity assertion onto the entire skew series ring.

## Rank of the first coefficient

Take an expression representing \(x\) whose series starts with \(a t^q z^q\).
The [Laurent asymptotic in measure](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#statement) for this scalar expression gives \(s^{-q}x(s)\to at^q\) in measure as \(s\to0\) outside a countable set, where \(x(s)\) denotes evaluation of the height parameter.
For clarity its evaluations have the same rank as \(x\) away from a countable set.
Indeed on the unit circle evaluation on the input group is the regular character twist \(g\mapsto s^{\chi(g)}g\).
All original inverse gates remain defined there, preserving ranks.
A finite scalar expression has a polynomial realization as a corner of an inverse on its common domain, by the [block construction](Yin_C_A0141_v1.md#recovering-a-laurent-coefficient-from-finitely-many-equations).
Monomial clearing permits Laurent inputs on the punctured line.
At points where the realization is invertible, the corner rank is the rank of its polynomial border minus the pivot size.
The pivot and border have their generic maximal ranks off a countable set by the [finite-evaluation inequality](Yin_C_A0022_v1.md#simultaneous-generic-rank-and-degeneration).
Evaluating at a common generic point on the unit circle proves the rank assertion.

Rank is lower semicontinuous for convergence in measure in a finite tracial algebra.
For example, restrict the limiting operator to a spectral subspace of its absolute value on which it is bounded below by \(\epsilon>0\) and bounded above by a finite constant.
Intersect that subspace with a projection of complement trace tending to zero on which the difference has norm below \(\epsilon/2\).
The approximating operator is injective on this intersection, and the spectral subspaces exhaust the initial support on removing the two cutoffs.
Applying this to a sequence of generic parameters proves the inequality, since \(t\) is unitary and subgroup inclusion preserves rank.
If \(P_j(K)\) holds, every nonzero leading coefficient has rank one, hence so does \(x\).
Conversely subgroup inclusion preserves each finite inverse expression on \(K\), giving \(P_j(K)\) from \(P_j(G)\).

For a group \(G\) of arbitrary cardinality, each finite expression uses finitely many group elements.
Adjoin \(t\) to these elements and apply the preceding proof to their finitely generated subgroup \(G_0\); its kernel \(K\cap G_0\) inherits \(P_{j-1}\) if needed.
Regular subgroup inclusions and the same coefficientwise recursion give identical series upon enlarging \(G_0\), proving the result for \(G\).
If \(G/K\) is torsion-free abelian, the images of any finite support generate a finitely generated free abelian subgroup of the quotient.
Its full inverse image has a finite normal series of infinite cyclic extensions of \(K\).
Apply the equivalence along that series and use finite expressions again.

## A first Schur complement with a denominator supported on one exposed coset

Suppose \(P_0(K)\), let \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb CG\) with \(a\ne0\), and set \(S=d-ca^{-1}b\).
The result at depth zero gives \(P_0(G)\), so the pivot is an affiliated unit.
The depth-one expansion is uniquely determined by the four group-ring entries.
Suppose there is a torsion-free abelian subgroup \(A\le K\) normal in \(G\), such that the lowest height coefficient of \(a\), after stripping its \(t\)-power, has support in one coset of \(A\) in \(K\).
Then \(S\) is zero or an affiliated unit, and the original two-by-two matrix has integer nullity.
There is no support restriction on \(b,c,d\) or on the higher height coefficients of \(a\).

Indeed nonzero elements of \(\mathbb CA\) are affiliated units under \(P_0(K)\).
Let \(B\) be the subring of \(U(K)\) generated by \(\mathbb CK\) and their inverses.
Every element of \(B\) can be written \(p q^{-1}\) with \(p\in\mathbb CK\), \(0\ne q\in\mathbb CA\).
To see this, write products as finite sums of group translations with coefficients in the commutative fraction field of \(\mathbb CA\).
Normality moves each fraction to the right, and their finitely many denominators have a common multiple in \(\mathbb CA\).
It follows that each nonzero element of \(B\) has full rank under \(P_0(K)\), and conjugation by \(t\) preserves \(B\).
The inverse of the lowest coefficient of \(a\) lies in \(B\), by the single-coset hypothesis.
The recursion for all coefficients of \(S\) now uses only this subring.
Faithfulness distinguishes the identically zero series, and the rank of any first nonzero coefficient gives full rank for \(S\).
Taking \(A\) trivial gives the single-group-element extremum as a special case.
No spectral gap is needed for an inverse in this argument.

For example take \(K=\mathbb Z^2\times F(x,y)\), with free abelian generators \(u,v\), and let \(t\) fix \(x,y,v\) and send \(u\) to \(uv\).
This kernel has scalar injectivity by the [torsion-free free-by-abelian theorem](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#group-class-and-exact-theorems).
The pivot \(a=(1+u+v)x+yt\) meets the coset condition with a three-term extremum and its cyclic conjugates need not have the same denominator.
For \(b=x+ty,\ c=y+xt,\ d=1+t^2\), the constant coefficient of \(S\) is \(1-y(1+u+v)^{-1}\), which has full rank after clearing the denominator.
Both off-diagonal entries have more than one height.
The theorem gives the same zero-or-unit alternative for arbitrary \(b,c,d\) with this pivot, including cancellations in many initial degrees.
