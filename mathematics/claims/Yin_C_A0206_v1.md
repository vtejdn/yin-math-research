# Binary homology on the five-sheeted cover of the critical order-25 marking

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) have abelianization \(C_{25}\), with normalized character \(\chi(s)=1,\ \chi(t)=7\).
Put \(N=[H,H]\), \(J=\ker(\chi\bmod5)\), and let \(S\) act on \(W=H_1(N;\mathbb F_2)\) by conjugation by \(s\).
The following are equivalent:
* \(H_1(J;\mathbb F_2)\ne0\);
* the \(\Phi_5\) constituent occurs in \(W\), where \(\Phi_5\) is the fifth cyclotomic polynomial reduced modulo two;
* there is an actual signed permutation representation \(s\mapsto P,\ t\mapsto P^2E\) on five coordinates, where \(Pe_j=e_{j+1}\) cyclically and \(E=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1,-1)\).

Consequently under torsion-freeness and regular scalar group-ring injectivity, a four-site rectangle of rank \(3/2\) forces \(H_1(J;\mathbb F_2)=0\).
If the four corners and their assembled coefficient block are invertible and the displayed homology of \(J\) is nonzero, the regular nullity is at most \(108/250\) without assuming scalar injectivity, under the torsion-free hypothesis.
These rank statements use the [five-coordinate estimate](Yin_C_A0204_v1.md#five-signed-permutation-coordinates).
In the remaining half-rank case \(W\) is either zero or one irreducible module of dimension twenty over \(\mathbb F_2\).
In the latter case \(H\) has an explicit marked quotient \((C_2)^{20}\rtimes C_{25}\).

## The two cyclic covers

The quotient \(H/N\) is cyclic, so \(N=[H,H]\) is generated modulo \([N,N]\) by the commutators with \(s\).
Indeed after killing \([N,N]\) and \([s,N]\) the whole group is abelian.
Thus \(S-I\) is surjective on \(W\), and is bijective there since \(N\) is finitely generated by its finite index.
Conjugation by \(s^{25}\) is inner on \(N\), so \(S^{25}=I\).
The image of \(s^{25}\) in \(W\) is fixed by \(S\), hence zero.
The quotient of \(H\) by \([N,N]N^2\), where \(N^2\) denotes the subgroup generated by squares in \(N\), is therefore \(W\rtimes C_{25}\) with the marked lift \(s\) of order 25.

The element \(u=t s^{-7}\) belongs to \(N\).
In the split quotient, \(s\) and the image of \(u\) generate, so \(W\) is generated as an \(\mathbb F_2[S]\)-module by the single class \([u]\).
The polynomial \(X^{25}-1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) is square-free.
Its two nontrivial cyclotomic factors \(\Phi_5\) and \(\Phi_{25}\) are irreducible of degrees four and twenty: their root orbits under squaring have lengths \(\operatorname{ord}_5(2)=4\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_{25}(2)=20\).
For the latter, \(2^{10}\equiv-1\bmod25\) and \(2^4\not\equiv1\bmod25\).
The module \(W\) is a quotient of the regular cyclic module, so each can occur at most once.
The factor \(X-1\) is absent by invertibility of \(S-I\).

Every map from \(J\) to an elementary abelian 2-group kills \([N,N]N^2\).
In the preceding split quotient the image of \(J\) is \(W\rtimes\langle s^5\rangle\), with the second factor of order five.
Its maximal elementary abelian 2-quotient is \(W/(S^5-I)W\), precisely the \(\Phi_5\) constituent.
This computes \(H_1(J;\mathbb F_2)\) including its dimension, either zero or four.

## The marked representation and the residual quotient

Let \(A_5\) here denote the additive even-parity subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^5\), with cyclic permutation of coordinates.
It is the four-dimensional simple module for \(\Phi_5\), since it is the complement to constants in the regular permutation representation over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Identify its vectors with diagonal sign matrices.
If the \(\Phi_5\) summand occurs, the projection of \([u]\) to it is nonzero by cyclic generation.
Identifying that summand with \(A_5\), an invertible scalar from \(\mathbb F_{16}\) commuting with the coordinate shift can send this nonzero vector to the sign vector of \(P^2 E P^{-2}\).
The resulting equivariant quotient of \(W\rtimes C_{25}\) maps \(s\) to \(P\) and \(t=u s^7\) to \(P^2E\).
Conversely this marked representation has the full signed group \(A_5\rtimes C_5\) as image because the conjugates of \(P^2 E P^{-2}\) span the irreducible module.
The image of \(N\) contains that module by conjugating \(u\), and is contained in it by cyclic projection.
Thus \(W\) has the asserted \(\Phi_5\) quotient.
Combining the equivalences with the stated rank estimate and the [forced invertible corners at half rank](Yin_C_A0201_v1.md#statement) proves the five-sheeted obstruction.

When only the \(\Phi_{25}\) constituent occurs, identify \(W\) with the space of binary functions on \(\mathbb Z/25\) whose sum on each residue class modulo five is zero.
This is the kernel of the map summing coordinates over each such class and is exactly the twenty-dimensional simple summand of the regular representation of \(C_{25}\).
For any nonconstant sign vector \(e\) in that space, an equivariant isomorphism sends \([u]\) to the shift by seven of \(e\), again using multiplication by a nonzero scalar in the corresponding finite field.
The actual quotient then has \(s\mapsto P_{25},\ t\mapsto P_{25}^7\operatorname{diag}((-1)^{e_j})\), where \(P_{25}\) is the cyclic shift.
For example one may choose four negative signs in each residue class modulo five.
The five-dimensional estimate uses the \(\Phi_5\) quotient; it supplies no rank bound by itself for this different twenty-dimensional constituent.
