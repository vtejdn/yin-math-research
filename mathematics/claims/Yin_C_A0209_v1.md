# Ternary homology at the critical order-25 marking

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) have abelianization \(C_{25}\), with quotient character \(\chi(s)=1,\ \chi(t)=7\) in \(\mathbb Z/25\), and put \(N=[H,H]\).
Suppose \(H_1(N;\mathbb F_3)\ne0\).
For any complex two-by-two matrices \(A,B,C,D\) with \(A\) invertible, the regular kernel dimension of
\[
                        K=B+At-s(D+Ct)
\]
is at most \(8/25\), using the unnormalized matrix trace.
This estimate does not require torsion-freeness or scalar group-ring injectivity.

Consequently, if \(H\) is torsion-free, every nonzero element of \(\mathbb CH\) is regularly injective and \(K\) has rank \(3/2\), then \(H_1(N;\mathbb F_3)=0\).
Indeed \(A\) is then invertible by the [four-corner necessity](Yin_C_A0201_v1.md#statement).
Together with the [binary obstruction](Yin_C_A0208_v1.md#statement), this requires \(N_{\rm ab}\) to be finite of order coprime to six: \(N\) is finitely generated by its finite index, and both reductions of its abelianization vanish.

## The two possible simple components

Write \(W=N/[N,N]N^3\) additively, where \(N^3\) is the subgroup generated by cubes.
As in the [cyclic-cover construction](Yin_C_A0206_v1.md#the-two-cyclic-covers), conjugation by \(s\) acts by an operator \(S\) with \(S^{25}=1\).
The coinvariant space \(W/(S-I)W\) is zero: modulo this additional subgroup, \(W\) is central and the whole group is generated by \(W\) and the single image of \(s\), hence abelian.
The image of the commutator subgroup \(N\) must therefore vanish.
Since \(W\) is finite-dimensional, \(S-I\) is injective as well.
The image of \(s^{25}\) in \(W\) is fixed and hence zero.
It follows that \(H/[N,N]N^3=W\rtimes C_{25}\), with the marked lift \(s\) of order 25.
The class \(u\) of \(ts^{-7}\) generates \(W\) as an \(\mathbb F_3[S]\)-module since \(H\) is generated by \(s,t\).

Over \(\mathbb F_3\) the factors of \(X^{25}-1\) are \(X-1,\Phi_5,\Phi_{25}\), all simple and pairwise coprime.
Indeed \(3\) has order four modulo five, \(3^{10}\equiv-1\pmod{25}\) and \(3^4\not\equiv1\pmod{25}\), giving order twenty modulo 25.
Thus the respective primitive roots form single Frobenius orbits at both levels.
The cyclic module \(W\) has at most one copy of each factor and has no \(X-1\) component.
If \(W\ne0\), some quotient is therefore the simple module for \(\Phi_5\) or for \(\Phi_{25}\).

## Five entries with total weight minus six

For a \(\Phi_5\) quotient use \(l=5\) coordinates indexed modulo five; for a \(\Phi_{25}\) quotient use \(l=25\) coordinates indexed modulo 25.
Let \(P e_j=e_{j+1}\) be the cyclic permutation on these coordinates.
In the first case the sum-zero subspace in \(\mathbb F_3^5\) is the simple module for \(\Phi_5\).
In the second case the kernel of the map summing coordinates in each residue class modulo five is the simple module for \(\Phi_{25}\).
Indeed this equivariant surjection from the regular cyclic module on 25 points onto the permutation module on five leaves exactly the dimension-twenty simple summand as its kernel.

Partition the coordinates into one set of five for \(l=5\), or into the five residue classes of size five for \(l=25\).
In each set put exponent \(-2\) at its smallest index and \(-1\) at the other four.
The exponent vector \(e\) is nonzero over \(\mathbb F_3\) and sums to \(-6=0\bmod3\) in each set, so it belongs to the specified simple module.
For every cube root of unity \(w\) there is an actual unitary representation of \(H\) with
\[
                s\longmapsto P,\qquad
                t\longmapsto P^7\operatorname{diag}(w^{e_i}).
\]
To construct it, send \(W\) to the selected quotient, identify this quotient with the indicated permutation subspace and take diagonal powers of \(w\) entrywise.
Choose the identification so that the nonzero projection of the cyclic generator \(u\) corresponds to the shift by seven of \(e\).
Such an identification exists because the simple cyclic module is a one-dimensional vector space over its finite coefficient field, whose nonzero scalars act transitively on its nonzero vectors.
Then \(t=u s^7\) has precisely the stated image, with the translation order respected.

For every \(z^{75}=1\), take \(w=z^{25}\) and tensor the resulting representation by the cyclic character taking the marked values \(z^3,z^{21}\).
The new matrices are \(z^3P\) and \(P^7\operatorname{diag}(z^{b_i})\), where \(b_i=21+25e_i\).
There are four exponents \(-4\) and one exponent \(-29\) in each set of five.
Twist \(K\) by these matrices, retaining the regular translations in all coefficients.
Multiply the two input columns at position \(i\) by \(z^{-b_i}\).
The four terms \(B,At,-sD,-sCt\) then have exponents \(-b_i,0,3-b_i,3\), respectively.
At zero only \(At\) remains in every channel, permuted by \(P^7\); this coefficient matrix is invertible.
The two columns at that position have degrees at most \(3-b_i\).
Thus each set of five contributes at most \(2(4\cdot7+32)=120\) to the total column degree.

The [individual column-width estimate](Yin_C_A0204_v1.md#the-bound-using-individual-column-widths) applies since all 75 phases are actual unitary twists and the polynomial is full at zero.
Writing \(\delta=\dim_H\ker K\), it gives \(75l\delta\le120(l/5)=24l\), as asserted.
The vanishing conclusion concerns the stated critical marking; it does not dispose of its remaining homology at other primes.
