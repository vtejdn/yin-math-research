# Clearing the leading denominators of a Schur complement along a cyclic quotient

## Statement

Let \(\chi:G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z\) have kernel \(K\), choose \(t\) of height one, and put \(\sigma=\operatorname{Ad}_t\).
Assume \(P_0(K)\): every nonzero element of \(\mathbb CK\) is a unit of the regular affiliated algebra \(U(K)\).
For \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb CG\), \(a\ne0\), consider \(S=d-ca^{-1}b\).
The [fixed-depth height theorem](Yin_C_A0205_v1.md#statement) supplies \(P_0(G)\).
If \(k\) is the least height of \(a\), replace \(a,c\) by \(a t^{-k},c t^{-k}\), preserving \(S\).
Write the normalized pivot as \(a=p+\sum_{i>0}a_it^i\), where \(0\ne p\in\mathbb CK\).

Each of the following hypotheses implies that \(S\) is zero or an affiliated unit, so the original two-by-two matrix has integer regular nullity.

1. There is an amenable subgroup \(L\le K\) commensurated by \(G\), and the support of \(p\) is in a single coset of an amenable subgroup \(L'\le K\) commensurable with \(L\).
   Here commensurability means the intersection has finite index in both groups; commensuration means that every conjugate is commensurable with \(L\).
   There is no additional support condition on any other coefficient.
2. Let \(\Gamma\) be generated by \(t\), the supports of \(b,c,d\) after normalization, and the group elements in the positive-height terms of \(a\).
   Form the algebra \(R\subset\mathbb CK\) generated by \(1\) and \(\{\gamma p\gamma^{-1}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}\).
   It suffices that \(R\) is a right Ore domain.
   This holds in particular if these conjugates commute pairwise.
   The individual group elements in the support of \(p\) are not needed among the generators of \(\Gamma\).

The first condition extends the [single coset of a normal abelian subgroup](Yin_C_A0205_v1.md#a-first-schur-complement-with-a-denominator-supported-on-one-exposed-coset).
The second can apply when the differences in the leading support freely generate a nonabelian free group.
A finite expression with several inverse gates admits the same conclusion under the leading-coefficient unit condition below; the selected coefficients may come from different commensurable subgroups at different gates.

## Right fractions for commensurable amenable subgroups

For an amenable subgroup \(M\le K\), its group algebra is a domain by \(P_0(K)\) and is a right Ore domain by the [amenability and Ore theorem](../audits/literature/Bar19/Yin_U001.md#applicability-to-actual-affiliated-closures).
Write \(Q(M)\) for its classical right fraction division ring.
It embeds faithfully into \(U(K)\): extend the inclusion of \(\mathbb CM\) using actual affiliated inverses \(u^{-1}\) for \(0\ne u\in\mathbb CM\), which exist by \(P_0(K)\).
The extension is unital from a division ring and hence is injective.
It is the actual scalar division closure of \(\mathbb CM\) in \(U(K)\), since it is a division ring and each classical fraction uses a single allowed inverse.

If \(M_0\le M\) has finite index, every element of \(Q(M)\) can be written as \(xq^{-1}\) with \(x\in\mathbb CM\) and \(0\ne q\in\mathbb CM_0\).
To prove this, take representatives \(h\) for the finitely many left cosets \(hM_0\).
The right \(Q(M_0)\)-space \(V=\sum_h h Q(M_0)\) lies in \(U(K)\).
The sum is direct: clear one common right denominator in \(\mathbb CM_0\) by Ore, then use disjoint cosets in the group basis.
Left multiplication by any element of \(\mathbb CM\) preserves \(V\), and multiplication by a nonzero such element is injective since it is an affiliated unit.
It is consequently surjective on the finite-dimensional \(V\) and its inverse also preserves \(V\).
As \(1\in V\), acting by each fraction gives \(Q(M)\subset V\).
A further common right denominator in \(Q(M_0)\) gives the proposed form.
This argument does not require \(M_0\) normal in \(M\).

Put \(E=\sum_{h\in K}hQ(L)\), with finite sums in \(U(K)\).
For each \(h\in K\), moving \(q\in Q(L)\) past \(h\) on the right gives \(qh=h(h^{-1}qh)\).
The new fraction belongs to \(Q(h^{-1}Lh)\).
Applying the finite-index result with \(M_0=h^{-1}Lh\cap L\) expresses it with numerator in \(\mathbb CK\) and denominator in \(\mathbb CL\).
It follows that \(E\) is a subring, since one can move the first fraction past the next group element and multiply the rightmost fractions in \(Q(L)\).
Every element of \(E\) can be written \(xq^{-1}\), with \(x\in\mathbb CK\) and \(0\ne q\in\mathbb CL\), by a common right denominator for the finite sum.
It follows from \(P_0(K)\) that every nonzero member of \(E\) has full regular rank.
Commensuration by \(t^{\pm1}\) and the same finite-index argument give \(\sigma(E)=E\).

For every \(L'\le K\) commensurable with \(L\), the same argument gives \(Q(L')\subset E\), by passing through \(L'\cap L\).
In particular every nonzero member of \(Q(L')\) and its inverse belong to \(E\).
When \(\operatorname{supp}(p)\subset hL'\) or \(L'h\), factoring the group translation gives \(p\in E^\times\).
This also explains the stronger coefficient criterion \(p=h_1 q h_2\) with \(h_1,h_2\in K\) and \(0\ne q\in Q(L')\) whenever a rational coefficient is already present.

## Fractions of the actual conjugates

In the second case set \(H=\Gamma\cap K\) and let \(Q_R\) be the right fraction division ring of \(R\).
It embeds in \(U(K)\) by the same unit argument, because \(R\subset\mathbb CK\).
Conjugation by every \(\gamma\in\Gamma\) preserves \(R\), and preserves \(Q_R\) and its embedded inverses.
Thus \(E=\sum_{h\in H}hQ_R\) is a subring of \(U(K)\), invariant under \(\sigma^{\pm1}\).
For multiplication use \((h q)(h' q')=hh'(h'^{-1}q h')q'\).
Clearing a common right denominator in \(R\) puts each finite sum in the form \(xq^{-1}\) with \(x\in\mathbb CK,\ 0\ne q\in R\).
Every nonzero member therefore has full rank, \(p\in Q_R^\times\), and the stripped coefficients of all the remaining input terms are in \(\mathbb CH\subset E\).

A further sufficient Ore test is subexponential growth for every two-generated subalgebra of \(R\), measured by the dimensions of the powers of the span of \(1\) and the two generators.
Indeed if nonzero \(u,v\in R\) have \(uR\cap vR=0\), and \(W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}(1,u,v)\), then \(uW^n+vW^n\) is a direct sum of dimension \(2\dim W^n\) contained in \(W^{n+1}\).
This gives exponential growth.
Since \(R\) is a domain, existence of a nonzero common right multiple for every nonzero pair suffices for the classical right fractions.

## Which inverse gates are certified

In either construction \(E\) lies in the actual scalar division closure of \(\mathbb CK\), is invariant under \(\sigma^{\pm1}\), and all its nonzero elements are units in \(U(K)\).
An inverse of a nonzero member need not belong to \(E\).
For a formal series to keep its coefficients in \(E\) after inversion, require its leading coefficient to be a unit **of \(E\)**.
Indeed factor its lowest monomial from the series and use the geometric power series for the tail of strictly positive order.
At each given exponent only finitely many products of coefficients, their shifts and the inverse of the leading coefficient occur.
The normalized Schur pivot has height-zero coefficient \(p\in E^\times\) and all its other coefficients are in \(E\).
The formal series for \(S\) consequently has all coefficients in \(E\).
The [faithful height expansion and leading-rank inequality](Yin_C_A0205_v1.md#rank-of-the-first-coefficient) at depth one now give the asserted zero-or-unit alternative.

More generally, take a finite expression starting with group-ring Laurent inputs whose stripped coefficients lie in \(E\), and build it by sums, products and inverse gates.
At each inverse gate assume the formal value of its input is nonzero with leading coefficient in \(E^\times\).
Its entire formal skew series then lies in \(E((T;\sigma))\) by the same recursion, and its inverse at that gate also has coefficients there.
For the commensurated-subgroup construction a leading coefficient \(h_1 q h_2\) as above certifies such a gate; the subgroup \(L'\) can vary with the gate.
A final nonzero coefficient need only lie in \(E\).
This verifies admissibility of the specified expression, with no scalar induction hypothesis on a positive layer of \(K\), as follows.

Use the embedding \(a_iT^i\mapsto(a_it^i)z^i\) into \(U(G)((z))\) with a central formal variable.
The formal gates above, beginning with group-ring terms, are legal scalar inverses in the formal scalar division closure of \(\mathbb CG[z]\); negative powers use \(z^{-1}\).
By [faithful central evaluation](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#preservation-of-formal-identities), their images under \(z\mapsto Z\) are actual affiliated inverses in \(U(G\times\mathbb Z)\).
The initial evaluation is the subgroup embedding \(g\mapsto(g,Z^{\chi(g)})\).
An element affiliated to this subgroup which is an affiliated unit in the overgroup already has its inverse in the subgroup, by its kernel projection and polar decomposition.
Induction on the gates now identifies evaluation with the graph of the original expression and proves the gates admissible in \(U(G)\).
This use of formal evaluation needs only a finitely generated group for the chosen expression: adjoin \(t\) to its finitely many group-ring support elements.
Any intermediate stripped coefficients already defined over this smaller group have the same kernel projection in the larger group.
Whenever the leading coefficient is a unit upstairs it is a unit downstairs, and its inverse agrees there by subgroup inclusion.
This gives the identical formal series and permits the finitely generated form of central evaluation.

For a nonzero final series, the argument of the [leading-rank comparison](Yin_C_A0205_v1.md#rank-of-the-first-coefficient) applies to this particular formally admissible expression.
Its finite polynomial realization and border have their generic ranks outside a countable set, and comparison at generic unit phases gives the actual group rank.
The scaled values converge in measure to the leading coefficient times its height translation by the [central Laurent asymptotic](Yin_C_A0143_v1.md#statement).
Lower semicontinuity then bounds the original rank below by that of the leading coefficient, namely one.
The zero series evaluates to zero by the same faithful evaluation.
For example, starting at \(a^{(0)}=p\), use \(a^{(j+1)}=p+tF_j(a^{(j)})^{-1}B_j\) with nonnegative-height polynomial factors having coefficients in \(E\) and arising from group-ring inputs.
All these pivots keep leading coefficient \(p\), at arbitrarily large finite inverse depth.

## Two support tests

For a commensurated nonnormal example set
\[
 K=\langle u,l\mid l u^2 l^{-1}=u^3\rangle\times {\rm Heis}(\mathbb Z),\qquad
 L=\langle u\rangle\times{\rm Heis}(\mathbb Z),\qquad G=K\times\langle t\rangle.
\]
Here \({\rm Heis}(\mathbb Z)\) is the integral upper unitriangular three-by-three group, with generators \(x=I+E_{12}\) and \(y=I+E_{23}\).
Its elements are torsion-free and it is nilpotent, hence amenable.
The Baumslag-Solitar factor is the HNN extension of the infinite cyclic vertex group identifying \(2\mathbb Z\) with \(3\mathbb Z\), so it too is torsion-free by reduced normal form.
The subgroup \(\langle u\rangle\) is commensurated by \(l\), since the edge relation identifies finite-index subgroups, and by its own generator.
It is not normalized by \(l\), since \(l u l^{-1}\) is a reduced word with stable letters.
Thus \(L\) is amenable, commensurated and nonnormal in \(G\).
The affine action \(u:z\mapsto z+1,\ l:z\mapsto(3/2)z\) is injective on the vertex groups.
Its kernel acts freely on the Bass-Serre tree and is free, and its image is solvable.
Taking the product with the integral Heisenberg group gives \(K\) a normal free subgroup with elementary amenable quotient, so \(P_0(K)\) follows from [Linnell's theorem](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#group-class-and-exact-theorems).

The pivot \(a=1+u+x+y+lt\) has a lowest coefficient with noncommuting \(x,y\).
In fact its leading support is outside every coset of a normal amenable subgroup.
Such a coset would contain \(1\), hence be the subgroup itself and contain \(u\) and \(lul^{-1}\).
These two infinite cyclic generators generate their amalgam over \(\langle u^3\rangle=\langle (lul^{-1})^2\rangle\).
Indeed an alternating word reduced modulo that common subgroup expands into an HNN word where an inner segment \(l u^j l^{-1}\) has \(2\nmid j\), and a segment \(l^{-1}u^i l\) has \(3\nmid i\); any stable letters left by this expansion cannot cancel.
The common subgroup is central in this two-generator amalgam, and its quotient is \(C_3*C_2\), which is nonamenable.
This precludes the proposed normal amenable subgroup.
The first criterion nevertheless permits arbitrary \(b,c,d\in\mathbb CG\).

For the second test take \(F_i=F(x_i,y_i)\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z\), \(F'=F(v,w)\), and
\[
             K=\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z}F_i\Big)\times F',
       \qquad G=K\rtimes\langle t\rangle ,
\]
where \(t\) shifts the lamps and fixes \(F'\).
Any nontrivial finitely generated subgroup of \(K\) has nontrivial projection onto some free factor coordinate.
That image is a nontrivial finitely generated free group, so it maps onto \(\mathbb Z\).
Hence \(K\) is locally indicable and satisfies \(P_0(K)\) by the [locally indicable theorem](../audits/literature/JZLA20/Yin_U001.md#inspected-object-and-conclusion).
The height map also makes \(G\) torsion-free.

Use \(p=1+x_0+y_0\).
Allow arbitrary positive-height pivot terms with coefficients in \(\mathbb CF'\), and arbitrary \(b,c,d\in\mathbb C[F'\times\langle t\rangle]\).
The actual conjugates of \(p\) under \(\Gamma\) lie among \(1+x_i+y_i\).
The factor \(F'\) fixes them and different lamp groups commute, so \(R\) is commutative and meets the second criterion.
The support of \(p\) consists of three group elements and cannot belong to any coset of an amenable group, since the coset would contain \(1,x_0,y_0\) and hence its subgroup would contain \(F_0\).
Thus this localized calculation uses only the conjugation present outside the leading coefficient.
Both examples lie in known group classes; the two sufficient coefficient tests apply more generally under \(P_0(K)\).
