# The icosahedral quotient and the nullity of a four-site matrix

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) map onto the marked triangle group \(Q_r=\langle x,y\mid x^2=y^3=(xy)^r=1\rangle\), taking \(s,t\) to \(x,y\).
Here \(r\ge3\).
Let \(A,B,C,D\) be complex \(h\)-by-\(h\) matrices with \({\cal P}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B&A\\D&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) invertible.
Write \(\delta\) for the regular nullity of \(K=B+At-s(D+Ct)\) and \(\delta_-\) for that of \(K_-=B-At+s(D-Ct)\), with unnormalized matrix trace.
Let
\[
 k_r=2\,\#\{j\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}: |2\cos(j\pi/r)|<\sqrt3\}.
\]
For \(k_r>0\) the two nullities satisfy
\[
          3\delta\le 4h/k_r+\delta_-,\qquad
          3\delta_-\le 4h/k_r+\delta .
\]
In particular each is at most \(2h/k_r\).

At \(r=5\) the quotient is \(A_5\) in its order-two, order-three marking with product of order five, and \(k_5=8\).
The presentation follows also by setting \(x=ab,\ y=b^{-1}\) in [Kenne, p. 131, (ii)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972700026186).
Two stronger conclusions hold at this marking.

* If \(H\) also maps to \(C_{25}\) with marks \(1,7\), and \(B\) is invertible, then \(\delta\le41h/200\), in particular \(\delta\le41/100\) at size two.
  If \(H\) maps to \(C_{26}\) with marks \(1,10\) and \(A\) is invertible, then \(\delta\le21h/104\), in particular \(\delta\le21/52\) at size two.
  These estimates use no scalar injectivity.
* Under injectivity of every nonzero complex group-ring scalar, \(0<\delta<1\) implies \(\delta\le(2h-1)/8\) even without either cyclic quotient.
  At \(h=2\) the preceding unconditional estimate \(\delta\le1/2\) then gives \(\delta\le3/8\) in all cases.

The tests use only the marked quotients of the subgroup generated by the labels.
Thus under scalar injectivity the [assembled-block reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#the-four-group-elements-and-the-trace-bound) excludes a half increment in the four-column problem for the icosahedral marking.
A group with perfect commutator subgroup can have this quotient.
An ordinary two-dimensional unitary representation of \(A_5\) has determinant one by perfectness, so it sends the order-two generator to a scalar sign.
Its image is consequently abelian and trivial.

## Eight phases from a symmetric square

For a unit phase \(z\), set
\[
 U=i\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 V_z=\begin{pmatrix}c&d z^{-1}\\d z&c\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad c=1/2,\quad d=i\sqrt3/2 .
\]
Both matrices are special unitary, \(U^2=V_z^3=-I\), and
\(\operatorname{Tr}(UV_z)=-\sqrt3(z+z^{-1})/2\).
Each \(j\) counted above gives two distinct phases with trace \(2\cos(j\pi/r)\).
At those phases \((UV_z)^r=(-1)^j I\).
Different \(j\)'s give disjoint pairs, and both phases in each pair are reciprocal.
Their symmetric squares therefore satisfy all three relators exactly and give genuine three-dimensional unitary representations of \(Q_r\).

In the orthonormal symmetric tensor basis indexed by \(0,1,2\), the symmetric-square functor sends \(U\) to minus the reversing permutation.
It sends \(V_z\) to \(D_zT D_z^{-1}\) for \(D_z=\operatorname{diag}(1,z,z^2)\) and
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}
 c^2&\sqrt2 cd& d^2\\
 \sqrt2 cd& c^2+d^2&\sqrt2 cd\\
 d^2&\sqrt2 cd&c^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Write \(X=(X_0,X_1,X_2)\) for an input and \(Y=TX\).
After multiplication on the right by \(D_z\), the output in block row \(i\) is
\[
 z^i(BX_i+AtY_i)+z^{2-i}s(DX_{2-i}+CtY_{2-i}).
\]
Strip \(z\) from the middle row, obtaining a polynomial \(F(z)\) with respective row degrees at most \(2,0,2\).
At every selected unit phase its nullity is \(3\delta\) by [regular absorption](Yin_C_A0204_v1.md#the-bound-using-individual-column-widths).

At zero the first and third rows jointly require \(X_0=Y_0=0\), since \(s,t\) are units and \({\cal P}\) is invertible.
As \(T_{02}=d^2\ne0\), these conditions determine \(X_2\) from \(X_1\) and give \(Y_1=(d^2-c^2)X_1=-X_1\).
The remaining equation is precisely \(K_-X_1=0\).
This is a bounded graph parametrization of the kernel, hence \(\dim_H\ker F(0)=\delta_-\).

## Interpolation from the singular endpoint

Let \(k=k_r\) and take polynomial inputs of degrees less than \(k\) with every coefficient in \((\ker F(0))^\perp\).
Multiplication by \(F\) is injective there by examining the first nonzero coefficient of the input.
This domain has dimension \(k(3h-\delta_-)\).
The output coefficient space with row degrees less than \(k+2,k,k+2\) has dimension \(3hk+4h\).
Its evaluation at the \(k\) distinct phases is onto by scalar interpolation and has a bounded right inverse.
Outputs of multiplication evaluate into the direct sum of the closed ranges of \(F\) at those phases, whose codimension is \(3k\delta\).
Subtracting dimensions, as in the [different-output-degree proof](Yin_C_A0219_v1.md#interpolation-with-different-output-degrees), gives \(3k\delta\le4h+k\delta_-\).
Replacing \(A,D\) by their negatives preserves the invertibility of the assembled block by row and column signs and exchanges \(\delta,\delta_-\).
This gives the second inequality and the upper bound on their maximum.

For the additional cyclic estimate take odd \(m\) and odd integers \(a,b\) giving a cyclic marking modulo \(m\).
The Laurent pencil \(B+z^bAt-z^a s(D+z^bCt)\) at all \(m\)-th roots has nullity \(\delta\), and at their negatives it has nullity \(\delta_-\).
These \(2m\) parameters are distinct.
If its maximum rank is \(h\), the [degree bound](Yin_C_A0219_v1.md#statement) after a scalar monomial gauge gives
\[
                         \delta+\delta_-\le h(|a|+|b|)/m .
\]
At \((m,a,b)=(25,1,7)\) the constant coefficient \(B\) is a unit.
At order 26 reduce the marking modulo thirteen and use \((13,1,-3)\).
After multiplication by \(z^3\) the constant coefficient there is \(At\), a unit when \(A\) is invertible.
In both cases this certifies full rank at a nonzero parameter near zero.
When \(r=5\), combining the degree bound with \(3\delta\le h/2+\delta_-\) gives respectively \(41h/200\) and \(21h/104\).

## Four compensated two-dimensional phases

For the estimate with scalar injectivity retain just the four phases at \(r=5\) corresponding to \(j=1,3\) and fix one of them, \(z_0\).
The two matrices
\[
       U\otimes\overline U,\qquad V_z\otimes\overline{V_{z_0}}
\]
satisfy the relators of \(Q_5\): the scalar signs in the squares, cubes and fifth powers all cancel.
On the fixed second factor absorb its constant matrices into \(s_0=s\otimes\overline U,\ t_0=t\otimes\overline{V_{z_0}}\).
These are invertible matrices over the group ring and commute with the complex coefficient matrices on the separate \(h\)-coordinate factor.
Applying the [two-coordinate phase calculation](Yin_C_A0194_v1.md#normal-form-for-a-pair-of-unitary-matrices) on the first factor gives a linear pencil \(E_0+zE_1\) of size \(4h\), with both coefficients invertible over \(\mathbb CH\).
Indeed the elementary constant row and column operations there give the assembled block between diagonal factors with entries scalar multiples of \(s_0,t_0\).
Its first-coordinate swap \(J\) satisfies \(JE_0J=E_1\); thus \(L=-E_1^{-1}E_0\) satisfies \(JLJ=L^{-1}\).
Each of the four eigenspaces of \(L\) has dimension \(4\delta\) by genuine unitary absorption.

When \(0<\delta<1\), the original kernel has no nonzero group-ring column by scalar injectivity.
Neither does an allowed twisted kernel, since the untwisting unitary acts on each group fiber and preserves finite support.
The scalar monomial change at a phase preserves finite support as well.
Thus \(L\) has no nonzero scalar polynomial annihilator: for an allowed eigenvalue, a polynomial primary projection followed by the highest surviving power of \(L\) minus that eigenvalue would otherwise give a nonzero group-ring matrix with image in its eigenspace.

For the monic polynomial \(p\) of the four phases, the affiliated image of \(p(L)\) has dimension \(4h-16\delta\) by interpolation.
It is invariant under \(L,L^{-1}\) and \(J\), using reciprocity of the roots.
Its two swap eigenspaces are images of the group-ring matrices \((I\pm J)p(L)/2\).
Each nonzero one has dimension at least one because there is a nonzero scalar entry.
If their total dimension were less than two, \(J\) would act by a scalar sign on that image and \(JLJ=L^{-1}\) would give \((L^2-I)p(L)=0\), a forbidden polynomial identity.
Hence \(4h-16\delta\ge2\), proving the claimed bound.
