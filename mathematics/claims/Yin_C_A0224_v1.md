# A torsion-free family with perfect commutator subgroup and an icosahedral marking

## Statement

For each \(r=25+210k\), \(k\ge0\), there is a two-generator torsion-free group \(H_r=\langle s,t\rangle\) with abelianization \(C_{25}\) and marked images \(1,7\).
Its commutator subgroup is nontrivial and perfect, so \(H_r'/H_r''=0\).
Every solvable quotient of \(H_r\) factors through this cyclic abelianization, since the image of the perfect commutator subgroup is both solvable and perfect.
There is a simultaneous quotient \(H_r\twoheadrightarrow C_{25}\times A_5\) sending \(s,t\) to
\[
      (1,(1\,2)(3\,4)),\qquad (7,(1\,3\,5)).
\]
The two alternating-group permutations have orders two and three and their product has order five.
The groups for different \(r\) are nonisomorphic.

Let \(A,B,C,D\) be complex \(h\)-by-\(h\) matrices with \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B&A\\D&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) invertible.
For each of \(K=B+At-s(D+Ct)\) and \(K_-=B-At+s(D-Ct)\) the regular nullity on \(H_r\), with unnormalized matrix trace, satisfies
\[
                \dim_{H_r}\ker K,\ \dim_{H_r}\ker K_-
                         \ \le\ \frac{3h}{2(r-1)}.
\]
In particular at size two both nullities are at most \(3/(r-1)\), at most \(1/8\) for every group in the family and tending to zero as \(r\to\infty\).
The estimate requires no scalar injectivity assumption.

## Perfect subgroups of the triangle lifts

For \(n=7\) or \(n=r\), let \(\Delta_n\) be the orientation-preserving hyperbolic triangle group of orders \(2,3,n\), and let \(\Gamma_n\) be its full lift in the universal cover of \({\rm PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\).
The [lifted triangle presentation](../audits/literature/Mil75/Yin_U001.md#lifted-triangle-presentation) gives generators \(X,Y,Z\) with
\[
                        X^2=Y^3=Z^n=XYZ=c .
\]
Here \(c\) generates the infinite cyclic central kernel over \(\Delta_n\); since \(c=X^2\), the elements \(X,Y\) generate \(\Gamma_n\).
These groups are torsion-free: the universal cover acts faithfully by increasing lifts of the boundary-circle transformations to the real line, with the central full turn acting by unit translation.
An increasing homeomorphism of a line that moves a point has infinite order, as seen by iterating either the map or its inverse at that point. [Faithful lifted action](Yin_C_A0194_v1.md#an-explicit-perfect-marking)

Write \(d=n-6\), so \(d\equiv1\bmod6\).
In the abelianization the equations force \([X]=3w,[Y]=2w,[c]=6w,[Z]=w\), with \(w=[X]-[Y]\) and \(dw=0\).
Conversely these assignments in \(C_d\) respect the presentation and generate \(C_d\).
Put
\[
             x_n=Xc^{(d-1)/2},\qquad y_n=Yc^{(d-1)/3},
       \qquad N_n=\langle x_n,y_n\rangle,\qquad u=c^d .
\]
Both generators have zero class in \(C_d\).
They project onto the two vertex generators of \(\Delta_n\), and \(x_n^2=y_n^3=u\).
Since \(XY=cZ^{-1}\), one also has
\[
               (x_ny_n)^n=c^{n-1+5n(d-1)/6}=u^{(5n+1)/6}.
\]
In the abelianization of \(N_n\), write \([x_n]=3w_0,[y_n]=2w_0\), which is possible with \(w_0=[x_n]-[y_n]\) by the first relation.
The last relation reads \(5nw_0=(5n+1)w_0\), giving \(w_0=0\).
Thus \(N_n\) is perfect.
It surjects onto \(\Delta_n\) and contains \(c^d\), so \([\Gamma_n:N_n]\le d\).
Its inclusion in \(\Gamma_n'\), already of index \(d\), now gives \(N_n=\Gamma_n'\).
In particular \(\Gamma_7=N_7\) is perfect.

## Adjoining a central root

Use \(c\) for the central generator of \(\Gamma_7\).
Let \(L=(\Gamma_7\times\mathbb Z)/\langle(c,-25)\rangle\), and write \(v\) for the image of \((1,1)\).
The subgroup \(\Gamma_7\) embeds because no nonzero multiple of \((c,-25)\) has last coordinate zero.
The central \(v\) has infinite order, \(v^{25}=c\), and the quotient \(L/\Gamma_7\) is \(C_{25}\) generated by \(v\).
The homomorphism \(L\to\Delta_7\) has kernel \(\langle v\rangle\).

A nonidentity finite-order element of \(\Delta_7\) is conjugate into a vertex rotation subgroup: it fixes a point in the hyperbolic plane, and the triangle tiling has trivial orientation-preserving stabilizers away from the vertices.
The preimage of each vertex group is consequently conjugate to \(\langle W,v\rangle\), where \(W=X,Y,Z\) and \(W^m=v^{25}\) for \(m=2,3,7\), respectively. [Vertex stabilizer argument](Yin_C_A0204_v1.md#markings-with-the-stated-abelianizations)
If \(W^a v^b\) has finite order, its \(m\)-th power \(v^{25a+mb}\) is trivial.
Coprimality implies \(m\mid a\), and substituting \(W^m=v^{25}\) gives \(W^a v^b=1\).
The kernel itself is torsion-free, proving the assertion for \(L\).

Put \(s_0=vx_7,\ t_0=v^7y_7\).
Replacing \(x_7,y_7\) by \(s_0,t_0\) in any word changes it only by a central power of \(v\).
Commutators of any two substituted words therefore agree exactly with the original commutators.
As \(\Gamma_7\) is perfect and generated by \(x_7,y_7\), it is contained in \(\langle s_0,t_0\rangle\).
This subgroup also contains \(v=s_0x_7^{-1}\) and hence equals \(L\).
It follows that \(L'=\Gamma_7\) and \(L_{\rm ab}=C_{25}\) with marks \(1,7\).

## Generation and the simultaneous quotient

Define
\[
 H_r=N_r\times L,\qquad s=(x_r,s_0),\qquad t=(y_r,t_0).
\]
The subgroup generated by \(s,t\) projects onto both factors.
For any subgroup \(P\le A\times B\) projecting onto both factors, the sets \(K_A=\{a:(a,1)\in P\}\), \(K_B=\{b:(1,b)\in P\}\) are normal in the respective factors.
The rule \(aK_A\mapsto bK_B\) for \((a,b)\in P\) is a well-defined isomorphism, and \(P=A\times B\) whenever this common quotient is trivial.

In the common quotient of the two marked factors write \(\bar s,\bar t\) for their matched generators.
The power \((\bar s\bar t)^r\) is central by the relation in \(N_r\); the power \((\bar s\bar t)^7\) is also central since \((s_0t_0)^7=v^{56}(x_7y_7)^7\) is central in \(L\).
As \(\gcd(r,7)=1\), their product word \(\bar s\bar t\) itself is central.
The generators commute, so the quotient is abelian and, as a quotient of perfect \(N_r\), is trivial.
Thus \(s,t\) generate \(H_r\).
This direct product is torsion-free and \(H_r'=N_r\times\Gamma_7\) is infinite and perfect.

Map \(\Delta_r\) to \(A_5\) by its two vertex generators \(X\mapsto(1\,2)(3\,4)\), \(Y\mapsto(1\,3\,5)\).
Their product is \((1\,4\,3\,5\,2)\) with the rightmost permutation applied first, and \(5\mid r\).
The assignments satisfy the triangle presentation.
The order of the generated subgroup is divisible by \(2,3,5\), hence by \(30\).
An index-two subgroup of \(A_5\) is impossible because \(A_5\) is generated by three-cycles, all killed by a map to \(C_2\).
This proves surjectivity.
Projecting \(N_r\) to \(\Delta_r\) and using \(L\to C_{25}\) on the other factor gives the asserted simultaneous quotient.

Finally \(\Delta_n\) has trivial center, since an isometry centralizing its two vertex rotations fixes both distinct vertices and is the identity.
The kernel of \(N_r\to\Delta_r\) is central and likewise for \(L\to\Delta_7\).
Consequently \(H_r/Z(H_r)\simeq\Delta_r\times\Delta_7\).
Finite-order elements in each factor have orders dividing one of its three vertex orders, by the same tiling argument.
The largest finite order in the product is \(7r\), attained by the pair of rotations of orders \(r\) and 7, which are coprime.
This invariant distinguishes the groups for every \(r\) in the family.

## The numerical bounds

The projection \(H_r\to N_r\to\Delta_r\) sends \(s,t\) to the two vertex generators, so the [marked triangle quotient bound](Yin_C_A0223_v1.md#statement) gives an upper bound \(2h/k_r\) for each of the two nullities.
In the notation of that result the indices counted satisfy \(r/6<j<5r/6\).
Writing \(r=6m+1\) gives \(j=m+1,\ldots,5m\), whence \(k_r=8m=4(r-1)/3\).
Substitution proves the claimed bounds at every \(h\).
The simultaneous finite quotient also meets the order-25 icosahedral hypotheses of the same result, yielding the separate bound \(41h/200\) on \(\dim_{H_r}\ker K\) whenever \(B\) is invertible.
The stronger family estimate uses the full triangle quotient at the given value of \(r\).
