# Excluding the thirteenth cyclotomic quotient at the order-26 marking

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) have abelianization \(C_{26}\) with marked degrees \(1,10\), respectively, and assume that both \(s,t\) have infinite order.
Write \(N=[H,H]\), \(M=N_{\rm ab}\), \(S=\operatorname{Ad}_s\) on \(M\), and \(M_{13}=M/\Phi_{13}(S)M\).
If \(M_{13}\ne0\) and the complex two-by-two matrices \(A,B,C,D\) and \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B&A\\D&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) are invertible, the unnormalized regular nullity satisfies
\[
                     \dim_H\ker\bigl(B+At-s(D+Ct)\bigr)
                         \ \le\ 217/442 <1/2 .
\]
This estimate uses the infinite orders of the two labels and imposes no injectivity condition on general group-ring scalars.

In particular, at this normalized marking a torsion-free scalar-injective group supporting a rank-\(3/2\) rectangle has \(M_{13}=0\).
The [corner test](Yin_C_A0201_v1.md#statement) and [four-column reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#the-four-group-elements-and-the-trace-bound) give the coefficient hypotheses in this application.
By the [metabelian calculation](Yin_C_A0210_v1.md#the-cyclic-coefficient-module), \(M\) is then finite and \(\Phi_{13}(S)\Phi_{26}(S)M=0\).
Surjectivity of \(\Phi_{13}(S)\) on \(M\) now yields \(\Phi_{26}(S)M=0\).

## Simple quotients and their marked characters

Since \(N\) has finite index it is finitely generated.
As in the [split metabelian calculation](Yin_C_A0203_v1.md#from-homology-to-an-actual-rotation-and-reflection), \(S-I\) is onto \(M\): after killing \([N,N]\) and \([s,N]\), the group is generated by a central subgroup and the single image of \(s\), hence abelian.
Thus \(S-I\) is an automorphism on the finitely generated abelian group \(M\).
The class of \(s^{26}\) in \(M\) is fixed by \(S\) and hence zero.
The quotient by \([N,N]\) is \(M\rtimes C_{26}\) with marked lift \(s\), and \(u=ts^{-10}\) generates \(M\) as a \(\mathbb Z[S]\)-module.

On \(M_{13}\) the endomorphism \(S-I\) remains surjective, hence invertible.
Its determinant on the free abelian quotient therefore has absolute value one, whereas on each rational irreducible summand annihilated by \(\Phi_{13}\) the absolute determinant is \(\Phi_{13}(1)=13\).
Consequently \(M_{13}\) is finite.
In its reduction modulo 13 the polynomial \(\Phi_{13}\) is \((X-1)^{12}\), so invertibility of \(S-I\) also excludes a characteristic-13 simple quotient.
If \(M_{13}\ne0\), quotienting by a maximal proper invariant subgroup gives a simple module \(E=\mathbb F_p(\alpha)\) of characteristic \(p\ne13\), where the inverse multiplier \(\alpha\) of \(S\) is a primitive thirteenth root and the image of \(u\) is nonzero.
Its degree is the order of \(p\) modulo 13.

For any \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear functional \(\lambda:E\to\mathbb F_p\) put \(e_i=\lambda(\alpha^i)\) at the thirteen indices modulo 13.
Let \(P\) be the forward shift on these indices.
As in the [orbit formula](Yin_C_A0210_v1.md#a-representation-test-for-a-single-finite-character), at every \(z^p=1\) the assignments \(s\mapsto P,\ t\mapsto P^{10}\operatorname{diag}(z^{e_i})\) are actual unitary representations.
Indeed the diagonal character on an element \(v\in E\) at index \(i\) is evaluated on \(S^{-i}v\); the subsequent shift by ten in \(u s^{10}\) gives a fixed nonzero multiplier in the functional that may be absorbed into \(\lambda\).
On tensoring these matrices with the regular translations, absorption gives nullity \(13\delta\) at each phase, with \(\delta\) the nullity of the original rectangle.

## Cycle norms and an injective specialization at large primes

Use the graph on \(\mathbb Z/13\) with edges \(i\to i+1\) of cost zero and \(i\to i+10\) of cost \(w_i\), an integral lift of \(e_i\).
A block of the rectangle uses offsets \(0,1,10,11\) with costs \(0,0,w_i,w_i\).
Matchings correspond in cost to integral unit-capacity circulations: if \(n_i\) records the ten-step choice and \(x_j\) the one-step flow, then \(n_j+x_j=n_{j-10}+x_{j-1}\).
Conversely exactly \(1-n_j+n_{j-10}\) inputs occur at the intermediate index \(j\), of which \(x_j\) can continue while \(1-x_{j-1}\) end there.
This includes the two-step arrow and both assignments when two inputs meet, as in the [circulation proof](Yin_C_A0225_v1.md#projecting-a-matching-onto-two-directed-edge-sets).

For an oriented simple cycle let \(F_C(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{12}d_iX^i\) where \(d_i\) is the signed use of the ten-step edge at \(i\).
At a primitive complex thirteenth root \(\zeta\), this polynomial vanishes precisely when all its coefficients agree, since \(\Phi_{13}=1+X+\cdots+X^{12}\).
The only such undirected simple cycles are the pure unit-step and pure ten-step cycles: zero ten-step projection uses only unit steps, and a constant nonzero projection already uses thirteen distinct edges.
For all other cycles the [thirteen-point norm verifier](../computations/programs/Yin_J_A0009/verify_phi13.py) obtains a positive integer norm, at most 7111, whose prime factors are at most 3433.
It enumerates 2940 undirected simple cycles and 226 projection classes up to rotation and reversal, including the two pure classes.
For each class it multiplies the values at all twelve primitive roots in the prime field of order \(10000121\).
The integer norm is nonnegative by conjugate pairing and is at most \((169/12)^6<10000121\), since the squared Fourier coefficients sum to \(13\sum d_i^2\le169\).
Reduction modulo this single prime hence recovers the exact norm.
If \(F_C(\alpha)=0\), then \(p\) divides the integer resultant of \(\Phi_{13}\) and \(F_C\).
In particular each of the nonpure cycle polynomials has nonzero field value for every marking at \(p>3433\).

A field value of this kind excludes at most one hyperplane in the dual space of \(E\) when requiring \(\lambda(F_C(\alpha))\ne0\).
Rotation gives at most thirteen choices per class, and negation leaves each hyperplane unchanged.
The \(224\cdot13=2912\) possible hyperplanes together have fewer than \(|E|\) points when \(p>3433\), including when \(E=\mathbb F_p\).
Choose a functional outside them all.
Its nonzero multiples avoid the same cycles.
Use the [finite Fourier reweighting](Yin_C_A0226_v1.md#choosing-a-multiple-on-a-fixed-line-of-functionals) on its thirteen coordinates.
For centered representatives of the values after multiplication by \(c\), let \(D(c)\) be the sum of their absolute values.
Weight \(c\) by \(1+\cos(2\pi c e_j/p)\) at an index with \(e_j\ne0\).
The mean of \(D(c)\) with respect to this normalized weight is strictly less than \(13p/4-p/16\): each ordinary coordinate mean is at most \(p/4\), and the selected coordinate's negative first Fourier coefficient exceeds \(p/16\) in absolute value, while the other cosine contributions are nonpositive.
The zero multiplier has mass \(2/p\).
For \(p>3433\) one has \((1-2/p)(13/4-1/17)>13/4-1/16\), so there is a nonzero multiplier with \(D(c)<(13/4-1/17)p\).

For these exponents an optimum circulation can be reversed and its edges reweighted by shortest-path potentials, as in the [four-port specialization](Yin_C_A0226_v1.md#reversing-the-edges-of-a-minimum-circulation).
At each vertex the equations for its four port vectors are \(S_i=B X_i+A T_i\), \(Y_i=D X_i+C T_i\); the unit-step input is \(sY_{i-1}\) and the ten-step input is \(z^{w_{i-10}}tX_{i-10}\).
The coefficient assumptions make any two port vectors determine the remaining two, with invertible individual transfer blocks.
Reversing occupied edges inverts the relevant translations and gives two incoming and two outgoing ports.
Optimality excludes a negative directed cycle, so the potentials make all exponents nonnegative.
Any directed simple cycle remaining at zero must be pure: its integer cost is zero and reduction modulo \(p\) would contradict the chosen functional for any nonpure cycle.
A pure cycle occupies all thirteen vertices.
If its strongly connected component contained another edge, that edge together with the directed return path on the pure cycle would form a mixed simple directed cycle.
Thus each recurrent component consists of just one pure cycle.
The [single-cycle kernel argument](Yin_C_A0226_v1.md#why-each-recurrent-component-has-a-single-cycle) applies directly: a full circuit returns a vector by an invertible complex matrix times \(s^{\pm13}\) or \(t^{\pm13}\).
Both translations have infinite order, so the corresponding fixed-space equation has zero kernel by Fourier transformation on the cyclic subgroup.
Acyclic components are removed in topological order.
This gives an injective zero evaluation in the reweighted port system and hence an injective evaluation at a nonzero parameter by the finite polynomial rank estimate.
There the changes of port variables can be undone.
The [two-end interpolation inequality](Yin_C_A0219_v1.md#statement) now gives \(13p\delta\le2(U-L)\le2D(c)\), and \(2(13/4-1/17)/13=217/442\).

## All small primes and all factor degrees

For \(p\le3433,\ p\ne13\), the [finite-field verifier](../computations/programs/Yin_J_A0009/readme.md#results-and-limits) checks every monic irreducible marked factor of \(\Phi_{13}\), including the inverse choices.
It checks that the factors have total product \(\Phi_{13}\) and that every degree is precisely the multiplicative order of \(p\bmod13\); irreducibility follows since each primitive root has a Frobenius orbit of that degree.
If the marked factor is \(Q(X)=X^f+\sum_{j<f}q_jX^j\), choose \(f\) initial values for \(\lambda\) on \(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{f-1}\).
The recurrence \(e_{i+f}=-\sum_{j<f}q_j e_{i+j}\bmod p\) gives all thirteen exponents, and the program verifies periodicity.
When the positive representatives have sufficiently small sum, the polynomial is full at zero: the constant term \(B-sPD\) is invertible in the regular affiliated algebra by Fourier transformation in the infinite-order \(s\) and invertibility of \(B\).
The individual column degrees give the bound \(2\sum_i e_i/(13p)\).

For the other certificates, one can additionally tensor with the cyclic character with values \(z^{ap},z^{10ap}\) at the \(m=26p\) roots of unity and take \(z^{26}\) for the order-\(p\) character.
The resulting scalar exponents of \(s\) and the \(i\)-th source column of \(t\) are \(ap\) and an integer representative of \(10ap+26e_i\bmod m\).
With \(a=0\) the verifier instead uses the original \(m=p\) phases.
It verifies the two matching extrema by primal matchings and integer dual prices and checks on the tight graph that at least one endpoint is triangular with invertible one- or two-column diagonal blocks.
For a pair, the offsets are \(0,1\) from one source and \(10,11\) from the other to the same two adjacent rows.
After a row and column permutation, its block factors as \(\operatorname{diag}(I,-sI)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\operatorname{diag}(tI,I)\).
The [matched endpoint argument](Yin_C_A0217_v1.md#verifying-full-rank-away-from-zero) yields an injective nonzero specialization, and the bound is \(2(U-L)/(13m)\).
This works also if some of the \(m\) genuine parameter values induce the same representation.

At \(p=3\) the factors \(X^3+2X+2\) and \(X^3+X^2+2\) both use initial values \((0,0,1)\) and \(a=1\).
After centering all ten-step exponents modulo 78, lower the exponent at index 3 for the former factor or index 11 for the latter by 78, from 30 to \(-48\).
The unitary evaluations are unchanged, and in each case both endpoints pass with \(L=-130,\ U=91\).
The factor at \(p=2\) is tested with the same marked matching method.
In total the program verifies 1587 marked factors at 480 primes, of degrees \(1,2,3,4,6,12\).
The largest certified finite bound is \(11788/24011\) at \(p=1847\), smaller than \(217/442\).
