# A uniform order-26 half-nullity obstruction from every nonabelian solvable quotient

## Statement

Suppose \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) is torsion-free with abelianization \(C_{26}\) and marked images \(1,10\).
Let \(M=H'/H''\).
If \(M\ne0\), then for all complex two-by-two corners \(A,B,C,D\) with each corner and \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B&A\\D&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) invertible,
\[
         \dim_H\ker\bigl(B+At-s(D+Ct)\bigr)
                   \ \le\ \frac{217}{442}=\frac12-\frac{2}{221}.
\]
The coordinate trace is unnormalized.
This uniform gap for the normalized rectangle requires no scalar group-ring injectivity and includes infinite commutator abelianization.

If instead the two-generator torsion-free \(H\) satisfies regular scalar group-ring injectivity and merely admits a surjection onto \(C_{26}\), a rank-\(3/2\) rectangle with arbitrary complex two-by-two coefficient corners forces \(H_{\rm ab}=C_{26}\) and \(H'\) perfect.
All solvable quotients of \(H\) then factor through \(C_{26}\).
In particular the half constant-column increment is excluded in the four-column pencil on this pair if \(H\) has any nonabelian solvable quotient, or if \(H\) is residually solvable.
Together with the [order-25 result](Yin_C_A0229_v1.md#consequences-for-the-original-half-increment), any two-label group with a nonabelian solvable quotient still supporting a half increment under the scalar hypothesis has finite abelianization of order at most 24 and of order different from 23.

## From the commutator to one of three quotient tests

Put \(N=H'\) and \(S=\operatorname{Ad}_s\) on \(M\).
The subgroup \(N\) is finitely generated by finite index.
Since \(H\) is generated by \(s,N\), quotienting by the subgroup generated normally by \(H''\) and \([s,N]\) makes the quotient abelian.
Thus \(S-I\) is onto, and hence is an automorphism on the finitely generated abelian group \(M\).
The class of \(s^{26}\) is fixed and therefore zero, and
\[
                    H/H''=M\rtimes C_{26}
\]
with the marked lift \(s\).
The element \(u=ts^{-10}\) generates \(M\) as a module with the action of \(S\), as in the [actual metabelian quotient](Yin_C_A0210_v1.md#the-cyclic-coefficient-module).

First let \(L=M/(S+I)M\).
The automorphism \(S-I\) induces a surjection on \(L\), where it acts as multiplication by minus two.
Consequently \(L\) is a finite abelian group of odd order.
If \(L\ne0\), take a quotient of prime order \(p\).
The action is inversion and the marked images are a reflection for \(s\) and a rotation generating \(C_p\) for \(t\), using its even cyclic degree and the fact that \(s,t\) generate.
Thus the [odd dihedral bound](Yin_C_A0204_v1.md#the-reflection-and-rotation) gives nullity at most \(16/39\) when \(p=3\) and \(4/13\) when \(p\ge5\).

Suppose \(L=0\).
Now \(S+I\) is also an automorphism on \(M\), including when \(M\) has a free abelian part.
Because \(S^{26}=I\) and all its polynomial factors commute, one gets \(\Phi_{13}(S)\Phi_{26}(S)M=0\).
If \(M/\Phi_{13}(S)M\ne0\), the [thirteenth component bound](Yin_C_A0230_v1.md#statement) gives \(217/442\).
Otherwise \(\Phi_{13}(S)\) is surjective and \(\Phi_{26}(S)M=0\), so \(M=M/\Phi_{26}(S)M\).
For nonzero \(M\) choose a prime \(p\) with \(M/pM\ne0\) and then a simple quotient by a maximal proper invariant subspace.
This choice works for both finite and infinite finitely generated abelian groups.
The simple quotient has characteristic \(p\) and is annihilated by \(\Phi_{26}\).

## All characteristics of the twenty-sixth component

For \(p\le725547\) the [finite simple-factor theorem](Yin_C_A0235_v1.md#statement) gives \(217/442\) for every marking, including \(p=2,13\).
For larger \(p\) the simple factor is a field in which \(S^{-1}\) acts by a primitive twenty-sixth root.
In degree greater than one the [antiperiodic lift and single-cycle estimate](Yin_C_A0233_v1.md#statement) give nullity strictly below \(6/13\).
The same estimate holds in degree one outside the twenty split divisors in that theorem.
For all twenty exceptions the [marked endpoint certificates](Yin_C_A0234_v1.md#statement) give nullity smaller than \(9/20\).
All these estimates are at most \(217/442\).
The fixed hypotheses supply precisely the four invertible corners, the invertible assembly and the infinite orders of the marked generators needed by the various inputs.
This proves the uniform bound.

## Consequences for an arbitrary half-rank marking

Suppose \(H\) maps onto \(C_{26}\) and the rectangle has rank \(3/2\) under scalar injectivity.
The [cyclic normalization](Yin_C_A0201_v1.md#statement) gives weights \(1,10\) after changing labels in the indicated rank-preserving ways, and supplies all four invertible corners.
The [assembled coefficient reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement) gives the remaining invertibility.
The abelianization cannot be larger than the indicated quotient: a larger finite abelianization would have order at least 52, while an infinite finitely generated abelianization has arbitrarily large finite cyclic quotients.
Both alternatives violate the [abelian quotient order test](Yin_C_A0201_v1.md#consequence-for-finite-abelian-quotient-orders).
The first part therefore forces \(M=0\).
Equivalently \(N=H'\) is perfect, and its image in any solvable group is trivial.
Conversely \(M\ne0\) supplies a nonabelian metabelian quotient \(H/H''\).
Residual solvability and perfectness would give \(N=1\), which here would make the torsion-free group \(H\) equal to \(C_{26}\).
The equivalence with the half increment is the [four-column theorem](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement).

For the combined order conclusion, only abelianizations of orders 25 and 26 need to be considered beyond the cited order test.
An abelian group of order 26 is cyclic, and the cyclic case at 25 has already been excluded when a nonabelian solvable quotient exists.
In the noncyclic case of order 25 the two labels give a basis of \(C_5^2\).
The character lift \((1,0)\) in the [full character lattice](Yin_C_A0198_v1.md#the-lattice-of-all-finite-characters) has four times its width equal to four, strictly below the exponent five, excluding half rank.
At order 26 the perfect-subgroup case remains open.
A witness there still forces the [odd twisted norm alternative](Yin_C_A0213_v2.md#statement) on the actual subgroup \(N\).
