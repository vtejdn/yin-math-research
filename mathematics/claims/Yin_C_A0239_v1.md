# A rational matrix rank and divisibility of the unit class in a finite inverse layer

## Statement

Let \(X\in M_{n,m}(D_j(G))\) have nonintegral rational von Neumann rank \(p/q\) in lowest terms, where \(G\) is torsion-free.
Use the unnormalized matrix trace, let \(P\in M_n(\mathcal N(G))\) be its image projection, and write \(X_q=I_q\otimes X\), with \(q\) copies in direct sum.
Here \(D_0(G)=\mathbb CG\) and \(D_{i+1}(G)\) is the ring generated by \(D_i(G)\) and the inverses of its affiliated units.
Over \(H=G*\langle x\rangle\) there is a polynomial matrix \(V\) of size \(qm\) by \(p\) for which \(W=X_qV\) has a left inverse \(Y\) over \(D_{j+p}(H)\) satisfying
\[
         YW=I_p,\qquad WY=I_q\otimes P,\qquad X_q=W(YX_q).
\]
The image projection of the original matrix belongs to \(M_n(D_{j+p}(H))\), the inner rank of \(X_q\) in this layer is exactly \(p\), and in its algebraic \(K_0\) one has
\[
                    q[P]=p[1],\qquad [1]\in qK_0(D_{j+p}(H)).
\]
Applying the construction to \(X^*\) also recovers the kernel projection of \(X\).
For a scalar the depth for the projection alone already follows from the [free cyclic extension construction](Yin_C_A0124_v1.md#the-kernel-projection-and-its-approximants).

There are two tests in the original group.
Fix any group-ring presentation \(X=D-CA^{-1}B\) with \(A\) a square affiliated unit of size \(k\); a polynomial matrix itself permits \(k=0\).
For each polynomial \(V\in M_{qm,p}(\mathbb CG)\) let \(T(V)\) be the positive polynomial Gram matrix below, of size \(qk+p\).
Write \(d\) for the unnormalized matrix center-valued trace of the initial projection of \(X\) in the original group.
Then
\[
             \inf_V \dim_G\ker T(V)=\tau((p-qd)_+).
\]
In particular the infimum is zero whenever \(d=(p/q)1\), including when \(\mathcal N(G)\) is a factor.
Alternatively, let \(K\le G\) contain the supports of the fixed polynomial presentation and of an inverse expression for \(X\).
If every finite set of nonidentity words in \(K*\langle x\rangle\) survives some single substitution \(x\mapsto w\in G\) fixing \(K\), the infimum is already zero along a fixed polynomial family \(V(w)\) in one group element.
For \(m=1\) this family only needs the powers \(1,w,\ldots,w^{q-1}\).
In either zero-infimum case, a lower bound above zero on the strictly positive nullities of the indicated family forces all three displayed matrix identities and the divisibility in \(G\) itself.
In particular, if divisibility fails in this layer, the same fixed size and the same \(X\) yield strictly positive polynomial Gram nullities tending to zero.

## The finite frame in the free product

Take \(K\) as in the statement and form the free product \(K*\langle x\rangle\) inside \(H\).
Regular subgroup inclusion preserves the projections and scalar ranks by the decomposition into right cosets.
Put \(l=qm\) and let \(E\) be the initial projection of \(X_q\).
The matrix of scalar traces of its entries is Hermitian of ordinary trace \(p\).
Diagonalize this finite matrix and then conjugate by the normalized complex Fourier matrix to obtain a constant unitary \(U\) for which each diagonal entry of \(E'=U^*EU\) has trace \(\alpha=p/l\).
Let \(F\) consist of the first \(p\) columns of the normalized \(l\)-point Fourier matrix, so \(F^*F=I_p\) and every diagonal entry of \(FF^*\) equals \(\alpha\).
Since \(0<p<l\), these are proper projections where appropriate.
Choose a nonidentity \(h\in K\), which exists since constant complex matrices have integer rank.
It has infinite order and \(u_i=x^ihx^{-i}\), \(1\le i\le l\), freely generate cyclic groups jointly free from \(K\) by free product normal form.
Set \(L=\operatorname{diag}(u_i)\), \(V=ULF\), and \(Q=LFF^*L^*\).

For the normalized scalar matrix trace \(E'\) and \(Q\) are free projections.
As in the [single-column computation](Yin_C_A0232_v1.md#a-finite-column-attaining-the-rank), the diagonal of \(Q-\alpha I\) vanishes, and its off-diagonal entries are constants times \(u_i u_b^{-1}\) for \(i\ne b\).
Expand a cyclic alternating product of these entries with those of \(E'-\alpha I\), splitting each latter entry into its scalar trace and its centered part.
A nonzero scalar part lies off the diagonal, so the end inverse letter of one free block and the first letter of the next cannot cancel across it.
Any centered old entries are thus cyclically separated by nonempty reduced words in the independent free generators.
Their trace vanishes by free independence, and a term with no centered old entries is a nonidentity reduced word.
This proves vanishing of cyclic alternating centered moments.
Other alternating centered products reduce by cycling their end factors together and expanding in scalars and centered factors, since each projection generates a two-dimensional algebra.
When \(m=1\), take \(U=I\), \(u_i=x^{i-1}\), and \(E'=E\).
Now each old centered entry is diagonal and has trace zero, and every intervening nonzero new entry is a scalar multiple of a nonidentity power of \(x\).
Ordinary free independence again gives the required vanishing.

The [intersection formula for free projections](../audits/literature/CK14/Yin_U001.md#ck14-u001) gives \(Q\wedge(1-E')=0\) since both \(Q\) and \(E'\) have trace \(\alpha\).
Consequently \(EV\), and hence \(W=X_qV\), has full column rank \(p\).
Its affiliated image is contained in the affiliated image of \(X_q\); the two have dimension \(p\), so their support projections agree by faithfulness.
The same conclusions about rank and range hold whenever an original polynomial \(V\) gives full column rank, whether or not \(V\) is an isometry.

## Inner rank and the unit class

The positive \(p\)-by-\(p\) Gram matrix \(W^*W\) is an affiliated unit with entries in the star-closed ring \(D_j\).
Its inverse has entries in \(D_{j+p}\) by successive positive scalar pivots.
Indeed the first diagonal entry has zero kernel since any kernel vector in the first coordinate would be killed by the positive matrix.
The first Schur complement is a positive affiliated unit by triangular congruence, and the argument repeats in the next layer. [Positive pivot inversion](Yin_C_A0129_v1.md#finite-index-transfer-with-a-depth-bound)
Set \(Y=(W^*W)^{-1}W^*\).
Affiliated multiplication gives \(YW=I_p\) and \(WY\) is the selfadjoint idempotent onto the image of \(W\).
Thus \(WY=I_q\otimes P\) and \(X_q=W(YX_q)\) in the asserted finite layer.
Any factorization through \(r\) coordinates has von Neumann rank at most \(r\), so the inner rank is exactly \(p\).
The maps \(W,Y\) identify the right projective module of \(I_q\otimes P\) with the free rank-\(p\) module over that layer.
For integers \(u,v\) with \(up+vq=1\) this gives \([1]=q(u[P]+v[1])\) in \(K_0\).
No positivity of the resulting Grothendieck class is required.
Replacing \(X\) by \(X^*\) gives the kernel projection by taking the complement of its image projection.

## The exact original infimum

A fixed expression in a finite inverse layer has a polynomial presentation of the stated kind by the [block realization of sums, products and allowed inverses](Yin_C_A0129_v1.md#a-positive-matrix-over-the-original-group-ring), applied entrywise and assembled by direct sum.
For \(A_q=I_q\otimes A\), and likewise for \(B,C,D\), put
\[
       Z(V)=\begin{pmatrix}A_q&B_q V\\ C_q&D_q V\end{pmatrix},
                       \qquad T(V)=Z(V)^*Z(V).
\]
Eliminating the fixed affiliated unit \(A_q\) identifies the remaining block with \(X_qV\).
Thus \(\dim_G\ker T(V)=p-\operatorname{rk}_G(EV)\) for every \(V\).
In particular for the scalar first Schur complement one can take \(k=1\) and the test has size \(q+p\).

Let \(d_q=qd\), the center-valued dimension of \(E\).
The center-valued rank of \(EV\) is bounded by \(\min(d_q,p)\) because its domain has \(p\) coordinates and its image is contained in \(E\).
For the reverse bound, embed both domain and target as corners in one finite matrix von Neumann algebra.
On the central region \(d_q\le p\), comparison of projections gives a partial isometry from a subprojection of the domain identity onto \(E\).
On \(d_q>p\) it gives a partial isometry from the full domain identity into \(E\).
Their sum is a bounded rectangular partial isometry \(V_0=EV_0\) of scalar rank \(\tau(\min(d_q,p))\).
Approximate its finitely many entries in \(L^2\) by group-ring polynomials to obtain \(V_s\); multiplication by \(E\) preserves this convergence.
Put \(e_0=V_0^*V_0\) and let \(f_s\) be the kernel projection of \(EV_s\).
On \(b_s=e_0\wedge f_s\), \(V_0\) is isometric and \(EV_s\) is zero, hence
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(b_s)\le\|V_0-EV_s\|_2^2,\qquad
 \operatorname{rk}(EV_s)\ge\operatorname{Tr}(e_0)-\operatorname{Tr}(b_s).
\]
The second inequality follows by restricting \(EV_s\) to \(e_0\).
This proves the exact infimum on taking the trace and subtracting from \(p\).
Because \(\tau(d_q)=p\), this infimum vanishes precisely when \(d_q=p\) almost everywhere.

## A single substitution in the original group

The free frame above uses only fixed polynomial words and constant coefficients.
For \(w\in G\) substitute \(w\) for \(x\) in \(V\) and in the bounded group-ring matrix \(T(V)\).
A constant \(c\) larger than the sum of the norms of the coefficient matrices of the free polynomial \(T(V)\) bounds every substituted operator norm.
The positive contractions \((I-T(V)/c)^a\) dominate their respective kernel projections, and their free unnormalized traces tend to zero by injectivity at the free evaluation.
For each positive integer \(a\) the union of the nonidentity words in the diagonal supports of this polynomial is finite.
A single substitution preserving these words preserves its regular trace, which reads the identity coefficient of each diagonal entry. [Finite trace matching](Yin_C_A0236_v1.md#matching-a-finite-trace-moment-in-the-original-group)
The original nullities therefore have infimum zero along these substitutions.
If the positive nullities have a common strictly positive lower bound, some trial attains zero, including when there are no positive trials.
At that trial \(W\) has full column rank, so the same finite-layer proof applies in \(G\).
In either zero-infimum case, failure of the stated \(K_0\) divisibility forces every trial to have positive nullity, so distinct positive nullities tend to zero.
