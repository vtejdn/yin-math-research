# The middle subgroup intersection maps onto the six phases at cyclic order twenty-four

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity, and let \(\chi:H\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z/24\) satisfy \(\chi(s)=3,\ \chi(t)=4\).
Write \(F=\ker(\chi\bmod4)\) and \(\psi=\chi/4:F\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z/6\).
Put \(t_i=s^its^{-i}\) for all \(i\in\mathbb Z\) and
\[
 A_i=\langle t_i,t_{i+1}\rangle,\qquad J_i=A_i\cap A_{i+2}.
\]
If the two-by-two four-site group-ring matrix \(K=B_0+A_0' t-s(D+Ct)\), with complex constant coefficients, has regular rank \(3/2\), then
\[
                         \psi(J_i)=\mathbb Z/6\qquad(i\in\mathbb Z).
\]
In particular the absence of an element of \(\chi\)-degree \(4\bmod24\) in even one such intersection excludes this half-nullity input.
Under the same rank hypothesis, a nontrivial intersection \(\langle t_0\rangle\cap\langle t_1\rangle\) has the form \(\langle t^k\rangle=\langle t_1^k\rangle\) for \(k\ge1\).
This cyclic subgroup is normal in \(H\), is centralized by \(F\), and satisfies \(s t^k s^{-1}=t^{\pm k}\).
The surjectivity assertion applies whether or not the neighboring cyclic groups intersect nontrivially.

## The four-step product and its middle factors

The [four-site reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement) makes the assembled coefficient matrix invertible and gives an invertible rational transfer
\[
                  M(z)=(A_0'z+B_0)(Cz+D)^{-1}.
\]
Right multiplication by \(Ct+D\) identifies the rank of \(K\) with that of \(M(t)-sI\).
Here \(t\) has infinite order, so invertible rational matrices in \(t\) give affiliated units.
The element \(s\) is outside \(\langle t\rangle\) by reduction modulo four.
The four characters induced from this reduction fix \(t\) and give all fourth roots as multipliers of \(s\).
Hence \(s^{-1}M(t)\) has four distinct fourth-root eigenspaces of dimension \(1/2\); together they fill its two-coordinate affiliated module by interpolation and dimension.
Its fourth power is the identity, which gives the [ordered transfer equation](Yin_C_A0183_v1.md#transfer-matrices-and-equality)
\[
                  M(t_3)M(t_2)M(t_1)M(t_0)=gI,\qquad g=s^4 .
\]
In particular \(g\) belongs to \(\langle t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3\rangle\) by its Fourier support.
That subgroup is normal since conjugation by \(s^{\pm1}\) at either end can be rewritten using \(g^{\pm1}\) and the four generators.
Its cyclic quotient is generated by \(s\), has order dividing four, and still maps onto \(\mathbb Z/4\).
Thus it equals \(F\), with \(\psi(t_i)=1,\ \psi(g)=3\).

Write \(A=A_0,\ B=A_2,\ J=A\cap B,\ q=s^2,\ \phi=\operatorname{Ad}(q)\) and \(W=M(t_1)M(t_0)\).
Then \(B=\phi(A)\) and \(\phi(W)W=gI\).
The [middle-cut Fourier support calculation](Yin_C_A0188_v1.md#regular-fourier-support-of-the-cut) yields \(g=ba\) for some \(b\in B,a\in A\), and \(W a^{-1}\in{\rm GL}_2(U(J))\).
Indeed \(W=\phi(W)^{-1}g\) lies both over \(A\) and over \(B\) followed by right translation by \(g\).
Its nonzero bounded transform has an entry supported in \(A\cap Bg\), giving \(g=ba\); then \(W a^{-1}=\phi(W)^{-1}b\) is affiliated over both subgroups and hence over their intersection.

Let \(u=\phi^{-1}(b)\in A\).
Applying \(\phi\) to \(g=\phi(u)a\), using \(\phi(g)=g\) and \(\phi^2=\operatorname{Ad}(g)\), gives \(g=\phi(a)u\).
Therefore \(y=a q^{-1}\) satisfies
\[
             y^2=a g^{-1}\phi(a)=a u^{-1}=b^{-1}\phi(a)\in J .
\]
It has \(\psi(y^2)=2\psi(a)-3\bmod 6\), an odd residue.
Thus if \(\psi(J)\) is proper, it must equal the subgroup \(\{0,3\}\).

## The common rational matrix on neighboring cyclic groups

Suppose \(\psi(J)=\{0,3\}\), and let \(\zeta\) be a primitive cube root of unity.
The unitary character \(h\mapsto\zeta^{\psi(h)}\) of \(F\) fixes the affiliated matrices over \(J\).
Applied to \(W a^{-1}\), it gives
\[
        M(\zeta t_1)M(\zeta t_0)=\lambda M(t_1)M(t_0),
        \qquad \lambda=\zeta^{\psi(a)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
       M(\zeta t_1)^{-1}M(t_1)
             =\lambda^{-1}M(\zeta t_0)M(t_0)^{-1}
             \in M_2\big(U(\langle t_0\rangle\cap\langle t_1\rangle)\big).
\]
The affiliated subgroup intersection follows entrywise from the bounded transform and subgroup Fourier support as in the [middle cut](Yin_C_A0188_v1.md#regular-fourier-support-of-the-cut).

First consider the case where \(\langle t_0\rangle\cap\langle t_1\rangle\) is nontrivial, without imposing a condition on \(\psi(J)\).
Write the intersection as \(\langle t^u\rangle=\langle t_1^v\rangle\) with \(u,v>0\).
Then \(s\) commensurates \(\langle t\rangle\), as does \(t\), and hence so does \(H\).
For \(h\in H\) define \(m(h)=b/a\in\mathbb Q^\times\) by \(h t^a h^{-1}=t^b\) for any nonzero exponent pair.
This ratio is independent of the pair by taking common multiples and using the infinite order of \(t\).
Substitution after taking common multiples of exponents proves \(m(hk)=m(h)m(k)\).
Each \(t_i\) maps to one, so \(s^4\in F=\langle t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3\rangle\) gives \(m(s)^4=1\).
Since \(|m(s)|=u/v\), one has \(u=v=k\) and \(s t^k s^{-1}=t^{\pm k}\).
In particular \(\langle t^k\rangle\) is normal in \(H\), and every \(t_i\) commutes with \(t^k\) because it commutes with \(t_i^k=t^{\pm k}\).
Also \(t_2^k=t^k\), so \(t^k\in J\).
Under the proposed proper image its degree \(k\) belongs to \(\{0,3\}\) modulo six, forcing \(3\mid k\).

Put \(\mathbb K=\mathbb C\) in the trivial-intersection case and \(\mathbb K=\mathbb C(z^k)\) otherwise.
The displayed common affiliated matrix implies
\[
  L(z)=M(\zeta z)M(z)^{-1}\in M_2(\mathbb K),\qquad
  T(z)=M(z)^{-1}M(\zeta z)\in M_2(\mathbb K).
\]
For \(L\) use the right side of the displayed equation evaluated at \(t_0\); for \(T\) invert its left side evaluated at \(t_1\).
In the nontrivial case membership over \(\langle t_i^k\rangle\) says each entry is invariant under rotation by a \(k\)-th root of unity on the Fourier circle of \(t_i\).
Rationality makes this an identity of rational functions, whose rotation-invariant subfield is \(\mathbb C(z^k)\).
The trivial-intersection case gives constant entries by faithful cyclic evaluation.

## Three independent coefficients rule out a proper image

Write \(M(z)\) as a nonzero scalar rational function times a primitive polynomial matrix \(P(z)=H_0+zH_1+z^2H_2\), dividing out the gcd of the entries of \((A_0'z+B_0)\operatorname{adj}(Cz+D)\).
The three constant coefficient matrices must be complex-linearly independent.
Otherwise normalize the coefficient span by an invertible regular value \(P(z_*)^{-1}\) on the left.
The resulting space is spanned by the identity and at most one other matrix, hence has a common eigenvector.
This gives a constant point correspondence for \(M(z)\), which would give integer rank under the scalar injectivity hypothesis by the [two-dimensional transfer criterion](Yin_C_A0186_v1.md#a-constant-point-correspondence-in-a-two-dimensional-transfer).

By definition \(L(z)P(z)=P(z)T(z)\).
The elements \(1,z,z^2\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb K\): in the nontrivial case \(k\ge3\), so they are part of the standard basis over \(\mathbb C(z^k)\).
Hence \(LH_j=H_jT\) for each \(j\), in particular \(LP(z_*)=P(z_*)T\).
The three matrices \(P(z_*)^{-1}H_j\) are linearly independent even after scalar extension to \(\mathbb K\), and they all commute with \(T\).
A nonscalar two-by-two matrix over any field has a centralizer of dimension two: take a vector whose image is independent from it, and a commuting matrix is determined by the image of that vector.
Thus \(T\) is scalar, and \(P(\zeta z)\) and \(P(z)\) are proportional over \(\mathbb C(z)\).
Clearing the proportionality factor in lowest terms and using primitivity of both lists of entries shows this factor is constant.
Since \(1,\zeta,\zeta^2\) are distinct, the proportionality permits at most one nonzero coefficient \(H_j\), contradicting independence.
This proves \(\psi(J)=\mathbb Z/6\).
Conjugation by \(s^i\) carries \(J\) onto \(J_i\) and leaves \(\psi\) invariant, proving the assertion at every cut.
