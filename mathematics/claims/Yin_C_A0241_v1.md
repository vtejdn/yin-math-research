# The order-24 marking and four-term relations in its commutator quotients

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular group-ring scalar injectivity.
Suppose the four-site matrix \(B+At-s(D+Ct)\) with complex two-by-two coefficients has regular rank \(3/2\), using the unnormalized matrix trace, and \(H\) has an abelian quotient of order 24.
Then \(H_{\rm ab}=C_{24}\).
Up to exchanging or inverting the labels and choosing a generator of the quotient, their marked degrees are \(3,4\).
This is the only marking at order 24 not already excluded by the short-direction inequalities below.

Use these normalized labels and put \(g=ts^{-1}\), \(N=[H,H]\), \(M=N_{\rm ab}\), and let \(S\) be conjugation by \(g\) on \(M\).
Write \(M_r=M/\Phi_r(S)M\).
Then \(M_8=0\), \(|M_3|\) divides one of \(1,4,7,16\), and \(|M_{24}|\) divides one of
\[
                          1,\ 4,\ 16,\ 25,\ 64,\ 73,\ 97,\ 193,\ 196 .
\]
In particular there is no rational \(\Phi_{24}\) constituent, and the possible prime divisors of the twenty-fourth quotient lie in \(\{2,5,7,73,97,193\}\).
The conclusions concern the indicated quotients of \(M\).
A nonzero \(M\) and a perfect commutator subgroup both remain possible under these necessary conditions.

## All abelian quotients of order 24

If \(Q\) is an abelian quotient of order 24 of a two-generator group, its primary decomposition and the two-generator bound on its 2-primary part leave \(C_{24}\) and \(C_{12}\times C_2\).
With \(m=\exp Q\), take the inverse image \(\Lambda\le\mathbb Z^2\) of all characters on the marked pair, identifying their values with pairs of exponents modulo \(m\).
Its index is \(m^2/24\), and its least nonzero \(\ell^1\) length is at most \(\lfloor\sqrt{2[\mathbb Z^2:\Lambda]}\rfloor\) by the [character lattice bound](Yin_C_A0198_v1.md#the-lattice-of-all-finite-characters).
Every nonzero lift of length \(d\) satisfies \(m/2\le2d\) under the half-rank hypothesis.
For a lift with both coordinates nonzero the [single-site endpoint improvement](Yin_C_A0197_v1.md#a-common-deficit-at-the-roots-of-unity) requires \(m/2\le2d-1\).
It applies at the \(m\) distinct parameter values even when some of the character twists coincide.

At \(m=12\) the index is six and the shortest length is at most three.
Only an axial shortest vector of length three could escape these bounds.
On interchanging axes and signs let it be \((3,0)\).
The horizontal period is three and the vertical projection has period two by the index, so \(\Lambda\) has a basis \((3,0),(c,2)\) with \(|c|\le1\).
This gives a shorter lift or a nonaxial lift of length three.
Thus the noncyclic quotient is excluded.

At \(m=24\) the index is 24 and the shortest length is at most six.
The same reasoning leaves an axial shortest vector \((6,0)\) and a basis \((6,0),(c,4)\) with \(|c|\le3\).
For \(|c|\le2\) the second vector has length at most five or is nonaxial of length six.
Up to signs the remaining basis is \((6,0),(3,4)\).
Modulo 24 the vector \((3,4)\) has order 24, so it generates the entire marked character line.
This allows the stated quotient-generator normalization; inversions and interchange preserve the rectangle rank by the [label symmetries](Yin_C_A0196_v1.md#all-surjections-onto-the-quotient).
Conversely a vector in this lattice has the form \((6j+3k,4k)\); if \(k\) is odd its length is at least seven, while nonzero even \(k\) gives length at least eight.
Thus the axial six is the only boundary for the short-direction test.
Any larger finite abelianization surjecting onto \(C_{24}\) has order at least 48, and any infinite finitely generated abelianization admits cyclic quotients of arbitrarily large order.
Both contradict the [abelian quotient exclusion](Yin_C_A0201_v1.md#consequence-for-finite-abelian-quotient-orders).

## A four-step identity tested on the whole character group

By the [four-column reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement), both \(Ay+B\) and \(Cy+D\) are invertible over \(\mathbb C(y)\).
Set \(L(y)=(Ay+B)(Cy+D)^{-1}\).
The infinite order of \(t\) gives its regular affiliated evaluation \(L(t)\), and the nullity of \(sI-L(t)\) is \(1/2\).
There are four distinct twists changing the phase of \(s\) and fixing \(t\), since the marked character has values \(3,4\bmod24\).
Thus \(s^{-1}L(t)\) has nullity \(1/2\) at each fourth root as an eigenvalue.
Their affiliated eigenspaces are a direct sum of dimension two by interpolation and fill the two-dimensional module, yielding \((s^{-1}L(t))^4=I\) as in the [extremal transfer calculation](Yin_C_A0187_v1.md#when-the-finite-phase-comes-from-an-integer-character).
Only the four finite phases are used here.

The coinvariants \(M/(S-I)M\) vanish because quotienting \(H\) by \([N,N]\) and \([g,N]\) makes the image of \(N\) central, and this quotient is generated by that image and \(g\).
The finite index of \(N\) makes \(M\) a finitely generated abelian group, so \(S-I\) is an automorphism.
The fixed class of \(g^{24}\) vanishes in \(M\), giving \(H/[N,N]=M\rtimes\langle\bar g\rangle\) with cyclic factor of order 24.
In particular \(S^{24}=I\).

For \(r=3,8,24\) work in \(M_r\), an abelian group on which \(S^r=I\).
The map \(I-S^4\) is invertible in all three cases.
For \(r=3\), \(S^4=S\) and \(S-I\) remains surjective, hence invertible on the finitely generated quotient.
For \(r=8\), \(S^4=-I\) and reduction modulo two makes \((S-I)^4=0\), so the surjectivity of \(S-I\) gives \(M_8/2M_8=0\).
Consequently \(M_8\) is finite of odd order.
For \(r=24\), the relation \(S^8-S^4+I=0\) gives \(S^4(I-S^4)=I\).

In \(M_r\rtimes C_{24}\) conjugate the marked cyclic complement so that \(t=\gamma^4\) for its new generator \(\gamma\).
Indeed conjugating the old generator by \(b\in M_r\) adds \((I-S^4)b\) to its fourth power.
Then \(s=u\gamma^3\), and \(u\) generates \(M_r\) as a \(\mathbb Z[S]\)-module: after quotienting by its module span both marked images lie in the cyclic complement and still generate the image group.
Conjugation by the new generator acts as \(S\) on \(M_r\).

For every circle-valued character \(\psi\) of \(M_r\), use the unitary representation on \(r\) channels in which \(\gamma\) is the forward permutation \(P\) and \(b\in M_r\) acts diagonally at \(i\) by \(\psi(S^{-i}b)\).
This is well-defined since \(r\mid24\) and \(S^r=I\).
Twisting the regular representation preserves the four-step identity, by [regular absorption](Yin_C_A0194_v1.md#normal-form-for-a-pair-of-unitary-matrices).
In this identity \(L(P^4 t)\) is independent of \(\psi\) and only connects indices congruent modulo \(d=\gcd(4,r)\).
The denominator is a genuine affiliated unit since \(P^4t\) splits into finitely many scalar rotations of the infinite-order translation \(t\).
The other factor, the inverse of the twisted \(s\)-translation, shifts each channel by \(-3\), applies \(s^{-1}\), and at target \(i\) multiplies by \(\psi(-S^{-i}v)\) with \(v=S^{-3}u\).

Expand a diagonal block of the fourth power as a finite path sum.
At a term starting from channel \(i_0\), the four post-shift indices \(i_j\) satisfy \(i_j\equiv i_0-3j\bmod d\) for \(1\le j\le4\).
The character factor is \(\psi(-\sum_{j=1}^4 S^{-i_j}v)\), times an affiliated matrix independent of \(\psi\).
Since that diagonal block equals the identity for every \(\psi\), at least one path has \(\sum_{j=1}^4 S^{-i_j}v=0\) in \(M_r\).
Indeed the functions \(\psi\mapsto\psi(b)\) for distinct \(b\in M_r\) are linearly independent, by orthogonality on the compact dual; coefficient comparison takes place in the finite-dimensional complex span of the finitely many affiliated coefficients and the identity.
This does not require \(M_r\) to be finite or its affiliated coefficients to be bounded.
When \(d=4\), the four indices meet all residue classes modulo four.

## The norm of the resulting relation

Multiplying the four-term relation by a power of \(S\) normalizes one term to \(v\).
For \(r=3\), the other three exponents can be arbitrary modulo three.
For \(r=8,24\), they have residues \(1,2,3\bmod4\) after a permutation.
Thus \(F(S)v=0\) for one \(F(X)=1+X^a+X^b+X^c\) in the indicated lists.
Since \(v\) is a cyclic generator and \(\Phi_r(S)=0\), there is a surjection of abelian groups \(\mathbb Z[X]/(\Phi_r,F)\twoheadrightarrow M_r\).

The source is the cokernel of multiplication by \(F\) on the free integral power basis of \(\mathbb Z[X]/\Phi_r\); if its determinant is nonzero its order is the absolute determinant.
The [four-term computation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_A0010/readme.md) checks all \(27,8,216\) normalized exponent lists, respectively.
It forms columns \(F,XF,\ldots,X^{\varphi(r)-1}F\bmod\Phi_r\) in that basis and computes their integral determinants by fraction-free elimination.
For \(r=3\) the absolute values are \(1,4,7,16\); for \(r=8\) the sole absolute value is eight; for \(r=24\) they are the nine integers in the statement.
This gives the claimed order divisors, including \(M_8=0\) by oddness.
Since \(M_{24}\) is finite, the rational quotient by \(\Phi_{24}(S)\) vanishes; the finite-order action on \(M\otimes\mathbb Q\) is semisimple, so it has no rational \(\Phi_{24}\) constituent.
