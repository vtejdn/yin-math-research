# A fixed pencil with dense singular parameters and an injective generic value

## Statement

Suppose \(G\) is finitely generated and torsion-free and \(S\in D_j(G)\) is a nonzero singular scalar in the actual regular affiliated algebra.
Write \(\alpha=\operatorname{rk}_G S\), put \(m=\lceil1/(2\alpha)\rceil\) and \(\delta=1-m\alpha\), so \(0<\delta\le1/2\).
Use the canonical group trace, with unnormalized matrix extensions.
There is a positive contraction \(b\in D_{j+1}(K)\), where \(K=G*\langle x\rangle\), whose atoms at zero and at one both have mass \(\delta\).
In the torsion-free group \(W=K\wr\mathbb Z\) there are a fixed Hermitian matrix \(B\) over \(\mathbb CW\), a single constant coordinate projection \(E_{11}\), and a fixed finite group support for all \(B-\lambda E_{11}\), with the following properties.

* The pencil is injective outside a countable subset of \([-2,2]\).
* Every \(2\cos(\pi a/b_0)\) for \(0<a<b_0\), \(a,b_0\) integers, is a singular parameter with nullity at least \(\delta^{b_0}\).
* The sum of its positive nullities at all distinct real parameters is at most one.
  In particular on any sequence of distinct prescribed cosine parameters its positive nullities tend to zero.

The same nullities occur on the positive polynomial matrices \((B-\lambda E_{11})^2\) for real \(\lambda\), at fixed size and fixed group support.
Here a fixed support means a common finite set containing all supports.

Consequently the full complex strong Atiyah conjecture across torsion-free groups is equivalent to each of the following tests.

1. On each finitely generated torsion-free group, at each fixed size and fixed finite support, strictly positive nullities of positive group-ring matrices have some positive lower bound.
2. For every finitely generated torsion-free group, every Hermitian group-ring pencil \(B-\lambda E_{11}\) with affiliated-invertible complementary principal block has only finitely many real singular parameters or is singular for every real parameter.

Under strong Atiyah, a pencil in the second test has at most one singular parameter.
In the counterexample direction the construction permits a change of group and requires no rationality of \(\alpha\).
The [earlier fixed pencil on paths](../ledger/Yin_L_A0033_v1.md#a-fixed-hermitian-pencil) gives small positive excesses over a positive common nullity.
Here the nullities themselves approach zero and the generic kernel vanishes.

## Constructing two endpoint atoms in one inverse layer

The conjugate copies \(x^iGx^{-i}\) at distinct positions are freely independent for the scalar trace by free product normal form.
Take \(a\) to be the sum of the conjugates of \(S^*S\) at positions \(1,\ldots,m\), and take \(c\) to be the same sum at positions \(m+1,\ldots,2m\).
Both are positive affiliated elements of \(D_j(K)\).
The support of a positive sum is the join of its supports.
The [free intersection formula](../audits/literature/CK14/Yin_U001.md#ck14-u001), applied to complements, gives rank \(m\alpha\) for each sum and rank one for \(a^*a+c^*c\).
Indeed if \(\alpha\ge1/2\) then \(m=1\), and if \(\alpha<1/2\) then \(1/2\le m\alpha<1/2+\alpha<1\).
Thus the column \(J=(a,c)^{\mathsf t}\) is injective, and
\[
              R=J(J^*J)^{-1}J^*,\qquad b=R_{11}
\]
are respectively an orthogonal projection and a positive contraction, with entries in \(D_{j+1}(K)\).

In affiliated right modules, \(J\) identifies its domain with the image of \(R\).
The first-coordinate projection of this image has rank \(\operatorname{rk}a=m\alpha\), which is the rank of \(b\) by the Gram identity \(b=(e_1^*R)(e_1^*R)^*\).
Also \((1-b)z=0\) precisely when \((z,0)\) is in the image of \(R\), since \(1-b\) is the first compression of the projection \(I-R\).
The vectors of this form are exactly the images \(Jv\) with \(cv=0\).
Injectivity of \(J\) gives dimension \(\delta\), proving the two atom masses without taking a scalar support projection in the finite layer.
This construction includes the case \(m\alpha=1/2\).

## Isolated intervals in the regular wreath product

Let \(t\) be the shift of \(W\) and set \(T=b t+t^{-1}b\), a bounded selfadjoint scalar of norm at most two in \(D_{j+1}(W)\).
The copies of the bounded selfadjoint \(b\) in the distinct lamp groups commute and their joint trace is the product trace.
Together with the shift they have the tracial representation on the product of their scalar spectral laws and the bilateral shift, because every nonzero power of the shift with a lamp coefficient has trace zero. [Regular lamp coordinates](Yin_C_A0017_v2.md#copies-in-the-regular-representation)
Thus \(T\) is fiberwise a nearest-neighbor adjacency operator with independent edge weights of that law, and the regular trace of a spectral projection is its expected diagonal entry at zero.
This observation only involves the commutative algebra generated by those particular lamp variables.

For an interval of \(\ell\ge1\) consecutive vertices, require its two boundary edges to have weight zero and its \(\ell-1\) internal edges to have weight one.
The event has probability \(\delta^{\ell+1}\).
On it the interval reduces \(T\) and the standard path eigenvectors give eigenvalues \(2\cos(\pi a/(\ell+1))\) for \(1\le a\le\ell\).
For a fixed length and frequency the normalized eigenvectors of all such intervals in a fiber are orthogonal, since two distinct qualifying intervals cannot share a vertex.
Their rank-one projections sum to a subprojection of the corresponding spectral atom.
Taking the expected diagonal entry at zero and summing its \(\ell\) possible positions in the interval gives at least \(\delta^{\ell+1}\), since the squared coordinates of each normalized eigenvector sum to one.
This proves the lower bound, including when the law of \(b\) has additional atoms or a continuous part.
Distinct real spectral projections of the one-coordinate selfadjoint \(T\) are orthogonal and have total trace at most one, so its atomic set is countable and its atom masses vanish along any sequence of distinct atoms.

## Linearization with one varying diagonal entry

Use a fixed polynomial presentation \(T=D-CA^{-1}F\) with \(A\) a square affiliated unit, by the [block realization of finite inverse expressions](Yin_C_A0129_v1.md#a-positive-matrix-over-the-original-group-ring).
It is enough to write
\[
 B-\lambda E_{11}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  (D+D^*)/2-\lambda & F^*/\sqrt2 & C/\sqrt2\\
  F/\sqrt2 &0&A\\
  C^*/\sqrt2 &A^*&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The complementary block is an affiliated unit, and elimination gives scalar Schur complement \((T+T^*)/2-\lambda=T-\lambda\).
The nullity of the polynomial pencil is exactly the scalar atom mass.
Squaring preserves its nullity for every real \(\lambda\).
All the matrices in the square pencil have a common finite support, and their coefficients are bounded on \([-2,2]\).

Under strong Atiyah all positive polynomial nullities are positive integers.
The [actual division closure criterion](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#starting-references) also makes the scalar Schur complement at zero of any pencil in the second test an element of a division ring in the affiliated algebra.
In fact the inverse of its complementary polynomial block has entries in that closure by Gram elimination.
A scalar in this division ring minus \(\lambda\) has nontrivial kernel only when it equals zero, which holds for at most one parameter.
Conversely failure of the conjecture supplies a finitely generated torsion-free \(G\) with a nonzero singular scalar in its actual division closure, by restriction to the finite coefficient support and the same criterion.
That scalar belongs to some finite layer.
The resulting \(K\) and \(W\) are finitely generated and torsion-free by free product normal form and the wreath projection to \(\mathbb Z\).
The displayed pencil then violates both tests.
