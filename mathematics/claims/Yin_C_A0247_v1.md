# A first-layer drop in inner rank for the four-step closed chain

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity, and suppose \(\chi:H\twoheadrightarrow C_{24}\) has marks \(3,4\).
Put \(F=\ker(\chi\bmod4)\), \(t_i=s^i t s^{-i}\), \(g=s^4\), and \(\psi=\chi/4:F\to C_6\).
Suppose \(B+At-s(D+Ct)\) has regular rank \(3/2\), with complex two-by-two coefficients.
Write \(P_i=At_i+B,\ Q_i=Ct_i+D\), and form the square polynomial matrix of size eight over the original subgroup \(F\),
\[
       L=\begin{pmatrix}
         P_0&-Q_1&0&0\\
         0&P_1&-Q_2&0\\
         0&0&P_2&-Q_3\\
         -gQ_0&0&0&P_3
       \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its regular rank is six.
Its inner rank over \(\mathbb CF\) is at least seven, and its inner rank in the first scalar inverse layer \(D_1(F)\) is exactly six.
In fact six suffices already in the subring of \(U(F)\) generated by \(\mathbb CF\) and the four scalar inverses \(\det(Ct_i+D)^{-1}\), where determinants are taken in the corresponding cyclic group rings.
This holds even for transfers with poles on the unit circle.
It proves a necessary rank drop for this explicit larger matrix whenever a half-nullity rectangle exists.

## Four polynomial blocks and three unit pivots

The cyclic group-ring coefficients of each \(Q_i\) commute.
Its determinant is a nonzero polynomial in \(t_i\) by the [four-site reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement).
Since \(t_i\) has infinite order, this scalar is an affiliated unit by the bilateral shift representation.
The adjugate formula thus puts \(Q_i^{-1}\) in the stated subring.
Write \(M_i=P_i Q_i^{-1}\).
The [ordered equality over the actual subgroup](Yin_C_A0240_v1.md#the-four-step-product-and-its-middle-factors) is \(M_3M_2M_1M_0=gI_2\).

In the four block variables \(z_i=Q_i x_i\), the first three equations are \(M_i z_i-z_{i+1}=0\) for \(0\le i<3\).
Keep \(z_0\) and use \(y_{i+1}=z_{i+1}-M_i z_i\) as the other three variables.
This triangular change and its inverse have entries in the stated subring.
The last equation has the expression
\[
   (M_3M_2M_1M_0-gI_2)z_0+M_3M_2M_1y_1+M_3M_2y_2+M_3y_3 .
\]
Its first coefficient vanishes, and the terms in \(y_1,y_2,y_3\) are removed with multiples of the first three block rows.
Permutations and signs now give equivalence to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_6,0_2)\).
This proves the regular rank and an upper bound of six on inner rank over the specified subring.
A factorization through fewer than six coordinates over any intermediate ring in \(U(F)\) would give smaller regular rank, proving the exact first-layer result.

## A rank of at least seven in a scalar character

The character at \(\zeta\in\mu_6\) sends each \(t_i\) to \(\zeta\) and \(g\) to \(\zeta^3\).
Set \(P(\zeta)=A\zeta+B\), \(Q(\zeta)=C\zeta+D\).
The image of \(L\) is \(I_4\otimes P(\zeta)-S_\zeta\otimes Q(\zeta)\), where \(S_\zeta\) sends \((x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)\) to \((x_1,x_2,x_3,\zeta^3x_0)\).
The scalar matrix \(S_\zeta\) is diagonalizable with the four distinct eigenvalues \(\lambda^4=\zeta^3\).
Thus the complex nullity of this character evaluation is precisely
\[
                 \sum_{\lambda^4=\zeta^3}
                         \dim_{\mathbb C}\ker(P(\zeta)-\lambda Q(\zeta)).
\]

If this sum were at least two for every \(\zeta\in\mu_6\), choose two roots in each fiber, with the same root selected twice only when the corresponding block has nullity two.
Identify these roots with \(w\in\mu_{24}\) by \(w^4=\zeta,\ w^3=\lambda\).
For each selected \(w\), the polynomial \(D_*(w)=\det(B+w^4A-w^3D-w^7C)\) has a zero there.
A double selection means that the entire two-by-two numerical block is zero, so \(D_*\) vanishes there to at least order two.
This polynomial is nonzero: at generic fixed \(w^4=z\) the denominator \(Cz+D\) is invertible, so at most two of the four distinct \(w^3\) can be eigenvalues of \((Az+B)(Cz+D)^{-1}\).
Its support is contained in \(\{0,3,4,6,7,8,10,11,14\}\).
The [six-fiber divisibility obstruction](Yin_C_A0244_v1.md#an-obstruction-for-six-numerical-matrices) rules out exactly such a polynomial, with all choices of repeated roots included.
This argument evaluates only the polynomial blocks; it also applies at roots where \(Q(\zeta)\) is singular.

Therefore for some \(\zeta\) the polynomial matrix \(L\) has complex rank at least seven at that character.
Any factorization over \(\mathbb CF\) specializes to a complex factorization through the same number of coordinates, proving the base-ring lower bound.
In particular that character cannot extend as a ring homomorphism to \(\mathbb C\) on the actual subring used in the six-coordinate factorization.
The proof of the lower bound requires no specialization of affiliated inverses.
