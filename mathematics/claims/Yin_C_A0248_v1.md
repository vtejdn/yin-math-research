# Propagation of one rational rank to every rational nullity in a single group

## Statement

If any torsion-free group has a finite complex group-ring matrix of nonintegral rational regular rank, there is a single finitely generated torsion-free group \(J\) with the following properties.

* For each integer \(d>1\), a finite scalar inverse layer of \(J\) contains a unital copy of \(M_d(\mathbb C)\).
* Every rational in \([0,1]\) is the trace of an orthogonal projection in \(D_\infty(J)\) and the exact unnormalized nullity of a finite positive matrix over \(\mathbb CJ\).

The matrix size and inverse layer may depend on the rational number; the group \(J\) does not.
Consequently for any one prescribed \(d>1\), excluding the divisibility \([1]\in dK_0(D_\infty(H))\) for every finitely generated torsion-free \(H\) would exclude **all** nonintegral rational regular ranks across torsion-free groups.
In particular \(d=2\) suffices for their universal exclusion.
An irrational input is covered by the [fixed-pencil obstruction](Yin_C_A0246_v1.md#statement), which does not assert a rational nullity from that input.

## Exact atom masses and an independent lamp

Restrict to the subgroup generated by the coefficients of the original matrix.
Its regular rank is preserved by the right-coset decomposition.
If the reduced denominator of that rank is \(q>1\), the [free cyclic construction](Yin_C_A0242_v1.md#splitting-the-identity-into-equivalent-summands) gives a finitely generated torsion-free group \(K\) containing, in a finite inverse layer, an orthogonal projection \(P\) of trace \(1/q\).
Indeed its scalar Schur residual has rank \(1/q\) and its image projection is obtained by the one-column rectangular inverse.
Let \(W=K\wr\mathbb Z\), with shift \(t\), and put \(T=Pt+t^{-1}P\).
At the different lamp sites the copies of \(P\) are independent Bernoulli edge weights with probability \(\theta=1/q\) for weight one.

For an integer \(h\ge2\) take \(\lambda_h=2\cos(\pi/h)\).
Every connected component of this random line is almost surely a finite path, since a specified infinite ray has probability zero of having every edge open, and there are countably many rays.
The event that a path with \(\ell\) vertices starts at a specified site has probability \((1-\theta)^2\theta^{\ell-1}\).
The eigenvalue \(\lambda_h\) occurs once on that path exactly when \(h\mid\ell+1\), by the simple path spectrum.
For every eligible length, the sum of the diagonal entries of its normalized eigenline projection is one, so its contribution to the regular trace is exactly that start probability. [Path decomposition](Yin_C_A0005_v2.md#actual-projections-and-boundary-residues)
It follows that
\[
   \nu_W(T-\lambda_h)=\sum_{j\ge1}(1-\theta)^2\theta^{jh-2}
                   =\frac{(q-1)^2}{q^h-1}.
\]
The variable \(P\) here labels edges, yielding the exponent \(\ell-1\) in the path probability.

In a second copy of \(W\) put \(P_e\) equal to the product of \(e\) distinct lamp translates of \(P\), including \(P_0=1\) for the empty product.
These are commuting projections in a fixed finite inverse layer and \(\tau(P_e)=q^{-e}\).
In the direct product \(L=W\times W\) form the bounded scalar
\[
           X_{e,h}=(T-\lambda_h)\otimes P_e+1\otimes(1-P_e).
\]
It belongs to a finite inverse layer of \(L\) by subgroup inclusion.
On the second-coordinate projection \(P_e\) it acts by the first factor, and on its complement it is the identity.
The product trace gives the exact kernel dimension
\[
                \nu_L(X_{e,h})=\frac{(q-1)^2}{q^e(q^h-1)}.
\]

## Producing every denominator in one further extension

Factor a given \(d>1\) as \(d_0d_1\) so that all prime divisors of \(d_0\) divide \(q\) and \(\gcd(d_1,q)=1\).
Take \(e\ge0\) with \(d_0\mid q^e\), and then \(h\ge2\) with
\[
                         q^h\equiv1\bmod d_1(q-1)^2.
\]
Such \(h\) exists by invertibility of \(q\) modulo this modulus, including the vacuous modulus one.
Thus \((q^h-1)/(q-1)^2\) is integral and divisible by \(d_1\).
The actual nullity above is exactly \(1/D\), where
\[
                    D=q^e\,\frac{q^h-1}{(q-1)^2},\qquad d\mid D.
\]
In particular the scalar has rank \((D-1)/D\) in lowest terms.
Apply the [matrix-unit construction](Yin_C_A0242_v1.md#statement) in the same group \(J=L*\mathbb Z\) for all choices of \(e,h\).
It supplies a unital \(M_D(\mathbb C)\) at a finite depth and hence its unital subalgebra \(M_d(\mathbb C)\) via tensoring with \(I_{D/d}\).
This one group \(J\) is finitely generated and torsion-free.

Inside these units take the sum \(u\) of \(k\) diagonal idempotents, \(0\le k\le d\).
They give isomorphic right projectives and a direct decomposition of the scalar module into \(d\) summands, so \(u\) has regular rank \(k/d\).
For completeness the orthogonal image projection of the possibly nonselfadjoint \(u\) belongs to the next scalar inverse layer.
Put \(s=u+u^*-1\).
Then \(s^2=1+(u-u^*)^*(u-u^*)\) is an affiliated unit, so \(s\) is an affiliated unit.
The identities \(su=u^*s\) and \(us=uu^*\) show \(p=us^{-1}=s^{-1}u^*\) is selfadjoint with \(p^2=p\).
Its image agrees with that of \(u\) because \(s^{-1}\) is invertible.
Thus \(p\) is the required projection.
A [polynomial block presentation](Yin_C_A0129_v1.md#a-positive-matrix-over-the-original-group-ring) of \(1-p\) gives a square group-ring matrix with an affiliated-unit complementary block and nullity \(k/d\); its positive Gram matrix has the same kernel.
Finally, unital \(d\)-square matrix units imply \([1]\in dK_0\) by their \(d\) isomorphic summands.
Together with the [same-group converse from a divisible unit](Yin_C_A0242_v1.md#recovering-a-group-ring-rank-from-the-unit-class), this proves the universal consequence.
