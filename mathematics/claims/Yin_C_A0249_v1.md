# Cubic phase translation and a uniform metabelian order bound at order twenty-four

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular group-ring scalar injectivity.
Suppose the regular rank of a complex two-by-two four-site rectangle is \(3/2\) and \(H\) has an abelian quotient of order 24.
Use the [normalized degrees \(3,4\) in \(H_{\rm ab}=C_{24}\)](Yin_C_A0241_v1.md#statement).
Write \(N=[H,H]\), \(M=N_{\rm ab}\), \(S=\operatorname{Ad}(ts^{-1})\) on \(M\), and \(M_r=M/\Phi_r(S)M\).
Then
\[
               |M_2|\in\{1,3\},\qquad |M_4|\in\{1,5,9,13\}.
\]
In particular
\[
 |M|\le1956864,\qquad
 p\mid |M|\ \Longrightarrow\ p\in\{2,3,5,7,13,73,97,193\}
 \quad\text{for every prime }p .
\]
The full metabelian quotient has order \(24|M|\).
These are necessary conditions and leave open whether any of these orders can occur with half rank.

## The cubic translation of two or four character weights

Write the rectangle as \(K=B+At-s(D+Ct)\).
The [four-column reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement) applied to its adjoint with interchanged inverse labels gives \(\det(B-yD)\ne0\) over \(\mathbb C(y)\).
Indeed the two terms of that first cyclic pencil in the adjoint rectangle are \(B^*\) and \(-yD^*\).
The determinant condition is the only coefficient assumption needed for the positive-phase calculation below.

Fix \(r=2\) or \(4\).
The [cyclic module construction](Yin_C_A0241_v1.md#a-four-step-identity-tested-on-the-whole-character-group) gives \(M_r\rtimes C_{24}\) with \(S\) acting by the standard cyclic generator.
Both \(M_r\) have finite odd order by [the prime-power argument at these two factors](Yin_C_A0245_v1.md#the-integral-orders-and-the-finite-commutator-abelianization).
On \(M_2\), \(1-S^3=2\); on \(M_4\), the product \((1-S)(1-S^3)\) is \(2\) since \(S^2=-1\).
Thus in both cases \(1-S^3\) is invertible.
Conjugate the cyclic complement by a translation chosen using this inverse to obtain a generator \(\gamma\) with \(s=\gamma^3\), and write \(t=v\gamma^4\).
Conjugation by \(\gamma\) still acts by \(S\) on \(M_r\), and \(v\) generates \(M_r\) as a module: after killing the submodule it generates the two marked images lie in the cyclic complement.

For an additive character \(\eta:M_r\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) of order \(k\), write \(e_i/k=\eta(S^{-i}v)\) modulo one at \(i\bmod r\).
Let \(P\) be the forward shift on \(r\) coordinates.
For every \(w^k=1\) the assignment \(s\mapsto P^3,\ t\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(w^{e_i})\) is an actual representation.
Indeed use the corresponding power of the character on the abelian subgroup, put \(\gamma\mapsto P\), and use \(P^4=I\) in these two cases.
The frequencies are opposite in pairs since \(S^{r/2}=-1\).
Suppose all have representatives with \(k/3<e_i<2k/3\).
For each \(z^{3k}=1\) use \(w=z^3\) and tensor with the cyclic character fixing \(s\) and sending \(t\) to the cube root \(z^{2k}\).
The latter character comes from reduction of the marking modulo three.
Each resulting exponent of \(t\) can be taken as \(b_i=3e_i-k\), between zero and \(k\); opposite original weights have \(e_i+e_{i+r/2}=k\), giving \(b_i+b_{i+r/2}=k\).

In the regular twist the group translations \(s,t\) remain in the coefficients and each of the two columns in channel \(i\) has degree at most \(b_i\).
The polynomial at zero is \(B-sP^3D\), which has full rank by diagonalizing \(P^3\) and applying the infinite cyclic spectral model of \(s\) to \(B-yD\).
At each of the \(3k\) distinct phases its nullity equals \(r\,\dim_H\ker K\) by absorption.
The [individual-column interpolation bound](Yin_C_A0204_v1.md#the-bound-using-individual-column-widths) gives
\[
                  3kr\,\dim_H\ker K\le 2\sum_i b_i=rk.
\]
Thus a character with these representatives excludes half nullity.

## Two torsion coordinates over the Gaussian integers

The cyclic module \(M_2\), on which \(S=-1\), is cyclic as an abelian group.
If its odd order \(k\) is at least five, choose the character taking \(v\) to \((k-1)/(2k)\).
The numerator \((k-1)/2\) is relatively prime to \(k\), and both representatives are in the required open interval.
This excludes every such \(k\).

For \(M_4\), use the two coordinates \((\eta(v),\eta(Sv))\).
They distinguish the characters because \(v,Sv\) generate this module over \(\mathbb Z\).
The full inverse image \(\Lambda\) of these character points in \(\mathbb R^2\) is a lattice containing \(\mathbb Z^2\) with covolume \(1/|M_4|\).
Precomposing by \(S\) sends these coordinates to \((y,-x)\), so the lattice is invariant under rotations by a quarter turn.
It is a scaled and rotated square lattice.
Indeed multiply by \(|M_4|\) to obtain an ideal of \(\mathbb Z[i]\); this ring is Euclidean by nearest Gaussian integer division, so that ideal is generated by one element.
The side length of a square fundamental domain for \(\Lambda\) is therefore \(|M_4|^{-1/2}\).
Every point is within distance \(1/\sqrt{2|M_4|}\) of a lattice point.

If \(|M_4|>18\), apply the distance bound at \((1/2,1/2)\).
It gives two coordinates strictly between \(1/3\) and \(2/3\), and their negatives have representatives in the same interval.
The four character values required above are precisely these two coordinates and their negatives.
Consequently \(|M_4|\le18\).
Since \(M_4\) is a cyclic finite module over \(\mathbb Z[i]\), its order is the norm of a Gaussian integer, namely the generator of the annihilator ideal.
The odd sums of two squares at most 18 are \(1,5,9,13,17\).
At order 17 this quotient is \(\mathbb F_{17}\) with \(S\) acting by \(4\) or \(-4\), and \(v\) is nonzero.
Take a character of value \(6/17\) on \(v\); the value at \(Sv\) has representative \(7/17\) or \(10/17\).
All four opposite-pair values are in the same open interval, so order 17 is excluded as well.

Finally the [partial-product filtration](Yin_C_A0245_v1.md#the-integral-orders-and-the-finite-commutator-abelianization) gives \(|M|\mid\prod_{r\mid24,\ r>1}|M_r|\).
The previous [third, eighth and twenty-fourth quotient bounds](Yin_C_A0241_v1.md#statement) and [sixth and twelfth quotient bounds](Yin_C_A0245_v1.md#statement) give
\[
                    |M|\le 3\cdot16\cdot13\cdot4\cdot1\cdot4\cdot196=1956864 .
\]
The same divisor lists give the stated restriction on the primes of \(|M|\).
