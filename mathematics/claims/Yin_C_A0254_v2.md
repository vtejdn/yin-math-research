# A finite test by marked relators for the three traceless commutator equations

## Statement

Let \(G\) be a discrete group with a finite generating list, and use the [two affine matrices \(A,B\) obtained from finite local relations](Yin_C_A0254_v1.md#the-original-group-ring-matrices) of a traceless system of size two on this list.
Write \(F\) for the free group on the positions of the marking and \(\pi:F\to G\) for evaluation.
For \(K=A,B\), use \(K_F\) for the matrix at the free generators, \(n_K\) for its number of columns and \(r_K\) for its free polynomial inner rank.
Write \(K_F=\sum_w K_w w\), with constant complex coefficient matrices.
Choose a real \(C\ge2\) at least \((\sum_w\|K_w\|_{\mathrm{op}})^2\) for each \(K\).
For integers \(m\ge1\) define the finite group-ring coefficients \(c_{K,m}(w)\) by
\[
   \operatorname{tr}_{n_K}\!\big((I-K_F^*K_F/C)^m\big)
             =\sum_{w\in F} c_{K,m}(w)w ,
   \qquad
   \epsilon_{K,m}=c_{K,m}(1)-(n_K-r_K).
\]
Here the coordinate trace is unnormalized.

At least one of these same two matrices satisfies, for every \(m\ge1\),
\[
      \sum_{1\ne w\in\ker\pi}\operatorname{Re}c_{K,m}(w)
                          \ \ge\ \tfrac12-\epsilon_{K,m}.
\]
The nonnegative errors for both matrices tend to zero and each coefficient sum uses only freely reduced words of length at most \(2m\).
In particular, as soon as both errors are at most \(1/4\), the proposed witnesses require a nonidentity word whose coefficient has positive real part for one of the two matrices to become the identity in \(G\).
The test applies to the actual coefficients of the local relations.

If both matrices have integer coefficients, one may always use
\[
    n=\max(1,n_A,n_B),\qquad
    m=\left\lceil C^{8n+1}\log(8n)\right\rceil .
\]
The marking then has a nontrivial freely reduced relator of length at most \(2m\).
The same conclusion holds if the two matrices can separately be multiplied by nonzero scalars to make their coefficients integral, using the norm bound after rescaling.
The free-inner-rank loss, original group increments, numerical label tests and projective orbit constraints remain those of the [local-map theorem](Yin_C_A0254_v1.md#statement).

## Coefficient comparison with the free group

The regular rank of \(K_F\) equals \(r_K\) because the free Haar generators realize the free field and its matrix ranks, with zero padding for rectangular matrices. [Free Haar field realization](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U005.md#proposition-63-the-haar-example-and-its-sign) and [free-field rank characterization](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U004.md#theorem-56-exact-equivalences)
The [two-sided evaluation obstruction](Yin_C_A0254_v1.md#numerical-labels-and-the-free-inner-rank-loss) supplies one \(K\) with \(\operatorname{rk}_G K\le r_K-1/2\).
The norm bound ensures \(0\le K^*K\le C I\) in both regular representations, since every group element acts unitarily.
Thus the trace of the positive contraction \((I-K^*K/C)^m\) is at least the kernel dimension in either group and converges to that dimension by spectral calculus.
In \(F\) its trace is the identity coefficient, proving the assertion on \(\epsilon_{K,m}\).
In \(G\) its trace is the sum of the finite coefficients at all words mapping to the identity.
Its real value is at least \(n_K-r_K+1/2\) for the selected \(K\), giving the weighted inequality.
The word-length bound follows from the affine form, supported on the identity and individual free generators.

## An explicit radius for integer coefficients

For each integer \(R\ge1\) there is a finite quotient of \(F\) in which every nonidentity freely reduced word of length at most \(R\) survives.
For example on the ball of radius \(R\) extend each generator's partial left translation to a permutation of the ball, completing a bijection between the unused domain and range points.
Use the finite permutation group generated by these permutations as quotient.
Applying the permutations along a reduced word of length at most \(R\) to the identity follows the actual translated suffixes in the ball, so the resulting permutation is nonidentity.

For a matrix \(K\) as above in a finite quotient \(Q\), the ordinary Gram matrix in its regular permutation representation is an integer positive semidefinite matrix of size \(n_K|Q|\), with spectrum in \([0,C]\).
The product of its nonzero eigenvalues is at least one, by the first nonzero coefficient of its monic integer characteristic polynomial, also for the empty product.
For \(0<\eta<1\), if \(j\) eigenvalues with multiplicities are in \((0,\eta)\), this product is at most \(\eta^j C^{n_K|Q|}\).
Consequently the spectral measure normalized by \(|Q|\) assigns \((0,\eta)\) mass at most \(n\log C/\log(1/\eta)\).

With the above quotients as \(R\to\infty\), all fixed moments of the Gram spectral measures agree eventually with those in the free group, by expansion in finitely many words.
Their common compact support and fixed total mass give weak convergence by uniform polynomial approximation.
The same upper bound on the mass of the open interval \((0,\eta)\) holds in the free group by lower semicontinuity of the measure of an open set under weak convergence.
Now take \(\eta=C^{-8n}\).
In the integral for \(\epsilon_{K,m}\), the contribution of \((0,\eta)\) is at most \(1/8\).
On \([\eta,C]\) the integrand is at most \(\exp(-m\eta/C)\), giving a contribution at most \(n\exp(-m\eta/C)\le1/8\) at the stated \(m\).
This proves the simultaneous \(1/4\) error bound for the two matrices.

## A triangular test on the two local relation matrices

For a torsion-free \(G\) the [free inner rank loss](Yin_C_A0254_v1.md#numerical-labels-and-the-free-inner-rank-loss) also gives a finite test directly on the entries.
Suppose each of \(A,B\) has a square submatrix of size its free inner rank that becomes triangular after row and column permutations, with every diagonal entry evaluating to a nonzero group monomial or a nonzero binomial on two distinct group elements.
Each such diagonal is an affiliated unit: for a binomial, remove the first group translation and use the bilateral shift of the infinite-order quotient of its two group elements.
Both regular ranks then reach their respective free inner ranks, excluding the traceless system.

In particular, suppose the bipartite graph of the formally nonzero positions of each matrix is a forest and each such entry evaluates to a nonzero monomial or binomial as above.
On any nonempty edge component choose a leaf and its incident edge.
The leaf row or column has only the selected pivot, so elimination changes no entries in the submatrix obtained by removing the two endpoints.
The pivot is a unit both over the free field and in \(U\).
Induction, discarding isolated vertices, gives the same rank in the two rings for each matrix and again excludes the system.
