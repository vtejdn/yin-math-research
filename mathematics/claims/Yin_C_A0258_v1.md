# Generation by adjacent labels with a common power at cyclic order twenty-four

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity, and let \(\chi:H\twoheadrightarrow C_{24}\) have marks \(3,4\).
Write \(F=\ker(\chi\bmod4)\), \(t_i=s^its^{-i}\), \(A=\langle t_0,t_1\rangle\), \(B=\langle t_2,t_3\rangle\), and \(J=A\cap B\).
Suppose a complex two-by-two four-site rectangle \(B_0+A_0t-s(D+Ct)\) has regular rank \(3/2\).
If the neighboring cyclic groups intersect nontrivially, let \(k\) be their positive intersection index, so
\[
           \langle t_0\rangle\cap\langle t_1\rangle
                 =\langle h\rangle,\qquad h=t_0^k=t_1^{\epsilon k},
                   \qquad \epsilon\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Then \(J=A=B=F\), with \(h\) central in \(F\).
In particular \(A_{\rm ab}\) is finite and surjects onto \(C_6\).
Combined with the [case of disjoint neighboring cyclic groups](Yin_C_A0256_v1.md#statement), finiteness of \(A_{\rm ab}\) is necessary without any condition on the intersection.

Moreover \(F/\langle h\rangle\) is a quotient of \(C_k*C_k\) that is not virtually free, and \(k\ge3\).
It follows in particular that a neighboring subgroup presented by \(\langle x,y\mid x^k=y^{\epsilon k}\rangle\) on \(x=t_0,y=t_1\) cannot support this half kernel.
The argument allows arbitrary unit-circle poles in the transfer.

## Central coefficients of the middle product

Use \(M(z)=(A_0z+B_0)(Cz+D)^{-1}\), \(u(z)=\det(A_0z+B_0)\), \(v(z)=\det(Cz+D)\).
The assembled block matrix is invertible, and both \(u,v\) are nonzero polynomials with unit-circle zeros by [the six-fiber exclusion](Yin_C_A0244_v1.md#statement).
The [normal power and middle cut](Yin_C_A0240_v1.md#the-common-rational-matrix-on-neighboring-cyclic-groups) gives the exponents above and puts \(h\) in \(J\) and in the center of \(F\).
Writing \(g=s^4\), one has \(F=\langle A,B\rangle\), \(g=ba\) with \(b\in B,\ a\in A\), and \(W a^{-1}\in{\rm GL}_2(U(J))\) for \(W=M(t_1)M(t_0)\).

Put \(\mathbb K=\mathbb C(h)\), a central subfield in \(U(F)\) since \(h\) has infinite order.
In each cyclic rational field \(\mathbb C(t_i)\), \(i=0,1\), the elements \(1,t_i,\ldots,t_i^{k-1}\) are a basis over \(\mathbb K=\mathbb C(t_i^k)\).
Expand uniquely
\[
       M(t_1)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}X_r t_1^r,\qquad
       M(t_0)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Y_j t_0^j,\qquad X_r,Y_j\in M_2(\mathbb K).
\]
The cosets \(t_1^r t_0^j\langle h\rangle\) are all distinct for the indicated indices.
Indeed coincidence for two pairs puts \(t_1^{r-r'}\in\langle t_0\rangle\), so \(k\mid r-r'\) by the intersection formula, and then \(k\mid j-j'\).
Multiply \(W a^{-1}\) by one nonzero Laurent polynomial in \(h\) that clears all coefficient denominators of the product.
The resulting polynomial matrix belongs to \(U(J)\) and has group-ring entries, hence has support in \(J\).
Since \(\langle h\rangle\) is central and belongs to \(J\), the distinct cosets yield
\[
                        X_rY_j\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                              t_1^r t_0^j\in J a.                 \tag{1}
\]

## A determinant excludes rotation over the central field

For independent complex variables \(z,w\), the graph of \(M(z)\) is represented by the two-column block \(\big(\begin{smallmatrix}A_0z+B_0\\Cz+D\end{smallmatrix}\big)\).
Putting the blocks for \(w,z\) next to one another gives the assembled coefficient matrix times the block matrix with columns \((wI,I)^{\mathsf t},(zI,I)^{\mathsf t}\).
Computing its determinant also after right multiplication by \(\operatorname{diag}((Cw+D)^{-1},(Cz+D)^{-1})\) gives, for a nonzero constant \(\kappa\),
\[
                     \det(M(w)-M(z))=\frac{\kappa(w-z)^2}{v(w)v(z)} .
\]
Thus for every nonidentity \(k\)-th root of unity \(\omega\), the rational matrix \(T(z)=M(z)^{-1}M(\omega z)\) satisfies
\[
                     \det(T(z)-I)=
                    \frac{\kappa(\omega-1)^2 z^2}{u(z)v(\omega z)} .       \tag{2}
\]
If \(T\) had entries in \(\mathbb C(z^k)\), then \(T(\omega z)=T(z)\).
Iteration of \(M(\omega z)=M(z)T(z)\) would give \(T^{\operatorname{ord}\omega}=I\).
Its eigenvalues over \(\mathbb C(z)\) would therefore be constants among the corresponding roots of unity.
Equation (2) makes \(\det(T-I)\) nonzero, so it would be a nonzero constant.
This forces \(u(z)v(\omega z)\) to be a scalar multiple of \(z^2\), contrary to the known unit-circle zero of \(u\).
Exactly the same exclusion holds for \(M^{\mathsf t}\): transposition preserves both the determinant difference and the determinant \(u/v\).

## The residues generate both neighboring cyclic groups

The subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/k\) generated by the differences \(r-r'\) with \(X_rY_j,X_{r'}Y_j\ne0\) for a common \(j\) is the whole cyclic group.
This is automatic for \(k=1\).
Otherwise, if the subgroup were proper, some prime \(d\mid k\) would divide every such difference.
For each column index \(j\), all its active residues \(r\) are then congruent modulo \(d\).
Take a primitive \(d\)-th root \(\omega\).
If all nonzero \(X_r\) have a single residue modulo \(d\), the field automorphism \(t_1\mapsto\omega t_1\) gives \(M(\omega t_1)=M(t_1)\omega^{r_0}I\), contradicting (2).

In the other case choose nonzero \(X_r,X_{r'}\) from distinct classes modulo \(d\).
For every \(j\), the \(\mathbb K\)-space \(\operatorname{im}Y_j\) is contained in the kernel of at least one of these two matrices.
The images of the \(Y_j\) span \(\mathbb K^2\), since a proper joint range would make \(M(t_0)\) singular over \(\mathbb C(t_0)\).
Thus the two kernels are distinct lines, and each occurs as \(\operatorname{im}Y_j\) for a nonzero \(Y_j\).
For each of the two lines \(V\), all residues \(b\) with \(X_bV\ne0\) lie in a single class \(r_V\bmod d\).
The automorphism \(t_1\mapsto\omega t_1\) fixes \(\mathbb K\), including when \(t_1^k=h^{-1}\).
Acting on a \(\mathbb K\)-basis drawn from the two lines gives
\(M(\omega t_1)=M(t_1)D_0\), where \(D_0\) has those basis vectors as eigenvectors of eigenvalues \(\omega^{r_V}\).
Thus \(M(t_1)^{-1}M(\omega t_1)\in M_2(\mathbb K)\), also forbidden by (2) since \(\mathbb K=\mathbb C(t_1^k)\).
This proves the residue assertion.

By (1), common-\(j\) differences yield \(t_1^{r-r'}\in J\).
Since \(t_1^k\in J\) and the differences generate modulo \(k\), we get \(t_1\in J\).
Apply the same residue argument to the transposed coefficient lists \(Y_j^{\mathsf t},X_r^{\mathsf t}\); their reversed products are nonzero exactly on the same pairs as in (1).
The differences \(j-j'\) among active columns with a common \(r\) therefore generate \(\mathbb Z/k\).
Their group quotients put \(t_1^r t_0^{j-j'}t_1^{-r}\) in \(J\), so the established \(t_1\in J\) and \(t_0^k\in J\) give \(t_0\in J\).
Now \(A=J\subseteq B\) and \(g=ba\in B\).
With \(\phi=\operatorname{Ad}(s^2)\) one has \(B=\phi(A)\subseteq\phi(B)=gAg^{-1}\subseteq B\), hence \(A=B=F\).
Finiteness of \(F_{\rm ab}\) and its marked quotient \(C_6\) are the [index-four subgroup calculation](Yin_C_A0252_v1.md#the-promislow-group-and-the-index-four-subgroup) and the definition of \(\chi/4\).

## A restriction on the central quotient

The generators \(t_0,t_1\) give a surjection \(C_k*C_k\twoheadrightarrow F/\langle h\rangle\).
If the quotient were virtually free, let \(E\le F\) be the inverse image of a finite-index free subgroup.
The central extension \(1\to\langle h\rangle\to E\to E/\langle h\rangle\to1\) splits by lifting a free basis.
Thus \(h\) has infinite order in \(E_{\rm ab}\).
On the other hand transfer from \(F_{\rm ab}\) to \(E_{\rm ab}\) sends \([h]\) to \([F:E][h]\).
Explicitly choose representatives \(r_i\) for \(E\backslash F\); writing \(r_i f=e_i(f)r_{\pi_f(i)}\), the sum of the classes of the \(e_i(f)\) is a homomorphism on \(F\) by permutation of the summands.
For central \(f=h\in E\), every \(e_i(h)=h\).
Since \(F_{\rm ab}\) is finite, this contradicts the infinite order found above.

For \(k=1\) the central quotient is trivial.
For \(k=2\) the quotient is generated by two involutions and has a cyclic subgroup of index at most two, generated by their product, so it is virtually free.
Finally the group presented solely by \(x^k=y^{\epsilon k}\) has central quotient by the common power exactly \(C_k*C_k\), which is virtually free: the kernel of its map to \(C_k\times C_k\) intersects both vertex factors and their conjugates trivially, and hence acts freely on the free product tree.
These observations give the stated exclusions.
