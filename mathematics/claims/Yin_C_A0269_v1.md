# Full reduced group algebra elements and Schur inputs with a closed row or column

## Statement

Put \(A=C^*_r(G)\), \(N=\mathcal N(G)\), and use the scalar inverse layers \(D_j(G)\) in the regular affiliated ring.
Let \(0\ne u\in D_j(G)\cap A\) be singular and suppose the closed two-sided ideal of \(A\) generated by \(u\) is \(A\).
Then some finite family of group translates of its kernel has intersection zero, and \(D_{j+1}(G)\) contains a bounded positive contraction with atoms at both zero and one, of the same positive mass.
There is already a Hermitian group-ring pencil in \(G\) with an affiliated-unit complementary block that is singular at both zero and one and injective at two.
In \(G\wr\mathbb Z\) a single fixed-support Hermitian pencil with one varying diagonal entry is generically injective and has strictly positive nullities tending to zero at the [dense cosine parameters](Yin_C_A0246_v1.md#isolated-intervals-in-the-regular-wreath-product).
In particular a group with simple reduced group algebra has these implications for every nonzero singular \(u\in D_j(G)\cap A\).

Let \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) be over \(\mathbb CG\), with \(a\) an affiliated unit and \(S=d-ca^{-1}b\) of rank strictly between zero and one.
If the first row or first column of \(M\) has closed range, a nonzero singular positive scalar \(T\in D_1(G)\cap A\) of exactly the rank of \(S\) is given below.
If this \(T\) generates \(A\) as a closed two-sided ideal, the equal-atom witness belongs to \(D_2(G)\).
This row or column condition allows \(a^{-1}\) to be unbounded.
For a simple reduced group algebra, [locality of \(D_\infty(G)\)](Yin_C_A0268_v1.md#locality-and-the-two-atoms) then excludes any such fractional Schur rank.

## Fullness gives a finite intersection

Since \(u\) generates \(A\) as a closed ideal, there are finite sums \(\sum_i b_i u c_i\) arbitrarily close to the identity in norm.
Indeed the closure of \(AuA\), with linear spans understood, is the closed two-sided ideal generated by \(u\): any closed two-sided ideal of a \(C^*\)-algebra is selfadjoint.
By norm density approximate the finitely many outside coefficients by group-ring polynomials, obtaining
\[
                    \left\|1-\sum_i B_i u C_i\right\|<1 .
\]
The sum has a bounded inverse by the Neumann series.
If \(p\) is the kernel projection of \(u\), a vector in every \(g^{-1}pg\) for \(g\) in the union of the supports of the \(C_i\) is killed by every \(u C_i\).
Invertibility of the sum makes the intersection zero.
The [minimal finite family argument](Yin_C_A0268_v1.md#finite-intersections-of-translated-kernels) produces the two equal atoms using one more scalar inverse in the original group.
Applying the polynomial realization to the resulting positive contraction gives the asserted original pencil; two lies outside its spectrum.
Its integer-shift lamp copies already give the independent weights required by the [isolated interval construction](Yin_C_A0246_v1.md#isolated-intervals-in-the-regular-wreath-product), with no free cyclic extension before the wreath product.

In particular every nonzero element of \(D_\infty(G)\cap A\) which generates \(A\) as a closed ideal also generates the unit ideal of \(D_\infty(G)\).
For this last claim the approximating sum belongs to the ring and is an affiliated unit, so its inverse is in the division closure.
Consequently the Jacobson radical of \(D_\infty(G)\) contains no such element, even if the division closure is not local.
When the reduced group algebra is simple this intersection of the radical with the bounded part lying in \(A\) is zero.

## The row and column Gram scalars

Write \(R=(a,b), V=(c,d)\).
The row \(R\) has dense range since \(a\) has dense range.
Under the closed-row hypothesis \(RR^*\) is a bounded positive unit with bounded inverse in \(A\) by spectral permanence, and its inverse is also in \(D_1\).
Thus \(P=I_2-R^*(RR^*)^{-1}R\) is the orthogonal kernel projection of the row with entries in \(D_1\cap A\).
Set \(T=VPV^*\).
This positive scalar has rank \(\operatorname{rk}(V|_{\ker R})=\operatorname{rk}M-1\) by projecting the kernel of \(M\) inside \(\ker R\) and applying rank-nullity.
Eliminating \(a\) in the affiliated algebra gives \(\operatorname{rk}M-1=\operatorname{rk} S\).

For the closed-column alternative use \(R=(a^*,c^*)\), \(V=(b^*,d^*)\), the rows of \(M^*\).
The first row has closed range by the closed range theorem for the first column of \(M\).
The same formula gives a positive first-layer scalar \(T\in A\) of rank \(\operatorname{rk}M^*-1=\operatorname{rk}S\).
The comparison preserves the numerical rank; the projections of these Gram scalars may differ from the original Schur projection.

For example if the first row is \(s-1,t-1\) for a freely generating pair \(s,t\), its row Gram is bounded below, even though \(s-1\) itself has no bounded regular inverse.
On the Cayley tree of the pair, a finite vertex set has edge boundary at least twice its size, as follows by counting internal edges in a forest.
For finitely supported \(f\) the layer-set formula for \(|f|^2\) gives \(\sum_{\{i,j\}}||f_i|^2-|f_j|^2|\ge2\sum_i|f_i|^2\).
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the left side by
\[
       \left(\sum_{\{i,j\}} |f_i-f_j|^2\right)^{1/2}
       \left(\sum_{\{i,j\}} (|f_i|+|f_j|)^2\right)^{1/2}
       \le \left(8\|f\|_2^2\sum_{\{i,j\}} |f_i-f_j|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The first energy is the form of \(RR^*\) and hence it is at least \(\|f\|_2^2/2\).
The same bound holds on every regular copy inside the original group, by coset decomposition.
This example allows the other row to contain arbitrary group elements.
