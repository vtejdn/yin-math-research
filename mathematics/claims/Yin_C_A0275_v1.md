# The next derived quotient of an order-24 half-rank candidate

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) satisfy the [normalized order-24 half-rank hypotheses](Yin_C_A0265_v1.md#statement), let \(F=\ker(\chi\bmod4)\), and write \(D=H''/H'''\).
If \(H'=H''\), then \(D=0\).
In the other marked cases put \(b=|H'/H''|\in\{5,13\}\).
Then \(F'=H''\), \(F_{\rm ab}=\langle\bar t\rangle\simeq C_{6b}\), and \(D\) is finite of order coprime to \(b\).
With \(T=\operatorname{Ad}_t\) on \(D\), \(D\) is a cyclic module over
\[
          \mathbb Z[X]\big/\big(1+X^6+\cdots+X^{6(b-1)}\big),\qquad X=T.
\]
Write \(S=\operatorname{Ad}_s\) on \(D\), and let \(u\) be the exponent of \(sts^{-1}\) relative to \(\bar t\) in \(F_{\rm ab}\).
One has \(u\equiv1\bmod6\), \(u\bmod b\) of exact order four, \(STS^{-1}=T^u\), and \(S^4=T^{3b}\).

For a prime \(q\mid |D|\) set \(m_q=\operatorname{lcm}(4,\operatorname{ord}_b(q))\).
For each \(j\ge1\), the dimension of \(q^{j-1}D_q/q^jD_q\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) is a multiple of \(m_q\) and at most \(6(b-1)\), where \(D_q\) is the \(q\)-primary subgroup.
In particular the multiplicity of every elementary divisor \(q^j\) is a multiple of \(m_q\).
The additive group \(D\) is a direct product of four isomorphic groups, and if nontrivial it has order at least 16 for \(b=5\) or 625 for \(b=13\).
The layer dimensions give necessary conditions and do not provide an upper bound on the exponents of \(D\).

## A single normal generator in the index-four subgroup

Use the [five remaining metabelian markings](Yin_C_A0265_v1.md#statement) and their [explicit generators](Yin_C_A0263_v1.md#statement).
For \(b=5,13\), the quotient \(H/H''\) is \(C_b\rtimes\langle\gamma\rangle\) with \(|\gamma|=24\), with \(\gamma\) acting by a square root \(i\) of \(-1\bmod b\).
In these coordinates \(s=\gamma^3\) and \(t=1\cdot\gamma^4\), where 1 denotes the additive generator of \(C_b\).
Thus \(F/H''=C_b\times\langle\gamma^4\rangle\) is cyclic of order \(6b\), generated by the image of \(t\).
It follows that \(F'\subseteq H''\); conversely \(H'\subseteq F\) implies \(H''\subseteq F'\).
In \(F_{\rm ab}\), the class of \(t_1=sts^{-1}\) has the form \(\bar t^u\), where \(u\equiv1\bmod6,\ u\equiv i^3\bmod b\).
The class of \(s^4=\gamma^{12}\) is the unique element of order two, equal to \(\bar t^{3b}\).

By the [adjacent generation result](Yin_C_A0272_v1.md#the-order-24-application), \(F=\langle t,t_1\rangle\) in the original group.
The element \(v=t_1t^{-u}\) belongs to \(F'\) and normally generates \(F'\) in \(F\): killing it makes the two-generator quotient cyclic, so the quotient kills \(F'\), and its normal closure was already contained in \(F'\).
Thus \(F'/F''=D\) is cyclic as a module for \(F/F'\), generated by the class of \(v\).
The conjugation identities for \(S,T\) follow because \(t_1t^{-u}\) and \(s^4t^{-3b}\) belong to \(F'\), which acts trivially on \(F'/F''\).

## Finiteness from the prime cyclic abelianization

Put \(K=H'\), so \(K'=H''=F'\), \(K'/K''=D\), and \(K/K'\) is cyclic of order \(b\), generated by the class of \(t^6\).
Indeed \(t^6\) has nonzero additive coordinate \(6\bmod b\) in that quotient.
Its conjugation on \(D\) is \(V=T^6\), with \(V^b=I\).
The endomorphism \(V-I\) is onto \(D\): after quotienting \(K/K''\) by \((V-I)D\), the image of \(D\) is central with cyclic quotient, hence the resulting group is abelian and its image of \(K'\) is trivial.
As \(H/H''\) is finite, its kernel \(H''\) is finitely generated and \(D\) is finitely generated abelian.
Thus \(V-I\) is an automorphism.
Multiplying \(I+V+\cdots+V^{b-1}\) by \(V-I\) shows that this sum annihilates \(D\), proving the module description.

On the free abelian quotient of \(D\), if the rank were positive, the rational characteristic polynomial of \(V\) would be a positive power of \(\Phi_b\): it divides a power of \(X^b-1\), has no root one, and \(b\) is prime.
Hence \(|\det(V-I)|\) there would be a positive power of \(b\), contradicting its being an integral automorphism.
Thus \(D\) is finite.
Modulo \(b\), \(V-I\) is both nilpotent and invertible, giving \(D/bD=0\).
This excludes \(b\) as a prime divisor of its order.

## Multiplicities of elementary divisors

Fix \(q\mid |D|\), with \(q\ne b\).
The endomorphism \(V-I\) remains invertible on each characteristic layer \(L=q^{j-1}D_q/q^jD_q\).
Over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_q\) the action of \(V\) is semisimple with nontrivial \(b\)-th roots of unity as eigenvalues.
Frobenius acts on their exponents in \((\mathbb Z/b)^\times\) by multiplication by \(q\), preserving the dimensions of the eigenspaces.
Since \(SVS^{-1}=V^u\), conjugation by \(S\) also permutes the eigenspaces through the subgroup generated by \(u\bmod b\), preserving their dimensions.
Together they give orbits of length \(m_q\), the order of \(\langle q,u\rangle\) in the cyclic group \((\mathbb Z/b)^\times\).
Dimension is unchanged under scalar extension, so \(m_q\mid\dim_{\mathbb F_q}L\).
Since \(D/qD\) is generated by one vector for \(T\) subject to the monic annihilator above of degree \(6(b-1)\), its dimension is at most that number.
Each \(L\) is a quotient of \(D_q/qD_q\simeq D/qD\) under multiplication by \(q^{j-1}\), proving the uniform upper bound.

The multiplicity of cyclic factors of order exactly \(q^j\) is the difference of two successive layer dimensions.
Each multiplicity is therefore divisible by \(m_q\), a multiple of four.
For \(b=5\) any nonzero layer has size at least \(2^4\).
For \(b=13\), the orders of 2 and 3 modulo 13 are twelve and three, respectively, so both \(m_2,m_3\) equal twelve.
If instead \(q\ge5\), size at least \(q^4\ge625\) suffices.
These estimates prove the stated lower bounds.
