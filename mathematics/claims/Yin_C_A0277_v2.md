# A strict gap from the Fourier coefficients of the two row projections

## Statement

Use the nine-monomial matrix in the [alternating subgroup test](Yin_C_A0277_v1.md#statement).
After scalar and group gauges its rows are \((1,1,1)\), \((1,\lambda u,\mu v)\), \((1,\sigma w,\theta z)\), with all four scalar moduli one.
Write \(A=\langle u,v\rangle\), \(B=\langle w,z\rangle\), each freely generated by the indicated pair, and \(\delta=\dim_G\ker H\) with unnormalized coordinate trace.
If \(A\cap B=\{1\}\), then
\[
                          \delta\le \frac12-\frac{3}{512}
                                 =\frac{253}{512}.
\]
No further relation between \(A\) and \(B\) is required for this estimate.

More generally suppose no product of \(2j\) alternating nonidentity elements from the two subgroups is the identity for \(1\le j\le n\).
For \(\ell=n+1\ge2\) one has
\[
                     \delta\le \frac1\ell-
                          \frac{3}{8\ell^2\,2^{6\ell}} .
\]
Thus the moment upper bounds \(1/(n+1)\) are all strict, including when mixed relations first occur beyond the stated syllable lengths.

## The row projection on the three-regular tree

We compute first at phases one for the row of \(A\).
Set \(s_1=1,\ s_2=u,\ s_3=v\).
Use two vertex sets \(L_h,R_h\), \(h\in A\), and edges \(e_{j,h}\) from \(L_h\) to \(R_{s_jh}\).
This is the tree covering the graph with two vertices and three parallel edges: it is connected since \(u,v\) generate, and the two based loops corresponding to a tree edge and each remaining edge give a free basis of its deck group.
In particular a nonbacktracking closed path would give a nontrivial relation on that free basis.
The incidence operator \(D\) sends an edge to its left endpoint minus its right endpoint.
Its kernel is the kernel of the two rows \((1,1,1)\) and \((1,u,v)\), identifying each edge type with a copy of the regular group space.

The vertex Laplacian \(\Delta=DD^*\) equals three times the identity minus the adjacency operator.
Its inverse has entry \((2/3)2^{-d(X,Y)}\), where \(d\) is vertex distance.
Indeed the adjacency norm is at most \(2\sqrt2\) by the weighted Schur test with positive weight \(2^{-d(X,o)/2}\): away from the root there are one inward and two outward neighbors, and at the root there are three outward neighbors.
The proposed inverse column is square summable by the growth of tree spheres.
At distance zero its Laplacian equals \(3(2/3)(1-1/2)=1\), and at positive distance it is harmonic by the same inward and outward count.
Thus \(P=I-D^*\Delta^{-1}D\) is the orthogonal projection onto the kernel.

Let \(W=\{c\in\mathbb C^3:\sum c_i=0\}\), and put \(p_i=e_i-\mathbf1/3\) for the constant coordinate vectors \(e_i\) and \(\mathbf1=(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\).
For \(h\ne1\) write \(d(h)=d(L_1,L_h)\), and let \(s(h),e(h)\) be the first and last edge colors of this geodesic, respectively.
The distance is positive and even.
On \(W\) the symmetry \(U=2P-I_W\) has identity Fourier coefficient zero and nonidentity coefficients
\[
       U_h=-3\,2^{-d(h)}\,\chi(h)\,p_{e(h)}p_{s(h)}^* ,
       \qquad \chi(u)=\lambda,\quad\chi(v)=\mu .
\]
Here \(\chi\) is the unitary character inserting the phases after the unweighted calculation.
To verify the formula, take the \((i,j)\) matrix entry from \(e_{j,1}\) to \(e_{i,h}\).
In the Green kernel its four-term incidence difference equals
\((2/3)2^{-d(h)}(1-t_i)(1-t'_j)\), where \(t_i\) is 2 for the last color and \(1/2\) otherwise, and \(t'_j\) uses the first color by the same rule.
Moving an endpoint along the geodesic reduces the distance by one, and any other move increases it by one.
This also gives the correct distance zero when the initial distance is two and both endpoints move inward.
As vectors \(1-t_i\) and \(1-t'_j\) are \(-3/2\) times the respective \(p\) vectors.
The off-identity sign from \(I-D^*\Delta^{-1}D\) and the factor two in \(U\) give the formula.
The identity coefficient follows from the [permutation symmetry of the free row](Yin_C_A0277_v1.md#the-scalar-coefficient-of-each-row-projection).
The same statements apply to \(V=2Q-I_W\) for the row of \(B\), with its own tree, colors and character, in the same constant space \(W\).

Set \(f_i=p_i/\|p_i\|\).
Each coefficient is a scalar of modulus \(2^{1-d(h)}\) times \(f_{e(h)}f_{s(h)}^*\).
The absolute inner product of two of these unit vectors is one for identical colors and \(1/2\) otherwise.
There are exactly six elements of distance two, namely \(s_i^{-1}s_j\) for \(i\ne j\), with end color \(i\) and start color \(j\).
Each color is the end of precisely two of them.

## A discrepancy on products of two short elements

Use \(\operatorname{Tr}\) with the unnormalized trace on \(W\) and \(\|X\|_{2,\operatorname{Tr}}^2=\operatorname{Tr}(X^*X)\).
Trivial intersection makes the factorization \(ab\) with \(a\in A,b\in B\) unique when it exists, and similarly in the opposite order.
Thus the Fourier coefficient of \(UV\) at \(ab\) for nonidentity factors is the single product \(U_aV_b\); the opposite coefficient comes from \(V_{b'}U_{a'}\) if \(ab=b'a'\) and is zero if there is no such expression.
This coefficient rule also follows by finite polynomial approximation as detailed below.

Let \(S\) be the set of 36 distinct products \(ab\) with both distances two.
Their coefficient matrices \(U_aV_b\) have ordinary Hilbert-Schmidt norms at least \(1/8\).
Fix such an \(a\).
Among its six products, at most two can be rewritten \(ab=b'a'\) with both factors of distance two and \(e(b')=e(a)\).
Indeed the assignment from \(b\) to \(b'\) is injective wherever defined for this \(a\): equal \(b'\) would give \(b_1^{-1}b_2\in A\cap B\).
Only two short elements of \(B\) have the specified end color.
Hence at least 24 products in \(S\) fail this condition.

For each failing product in \(S\cap S^{-1}\), the opposite coefficient has a different range line.
Orthogonal projection perpendicular to that line shows that the difference of the coefficients has Hilbert-Schmidt norm at least \(\sqrt3/16\).
For a failing product in \(S\setminus S^{-1}\), the opposite coefficient either vanishes or has a factor of distance at least four, giving it norm at most \((1/2)(1/8)=1/16\).
The difference therefore has norm at least \(1/16\).
Its inverse group element lies outside \(S\) and gives an equal squared norm since \(UV-VU\) is skew adjoint.
By Parseval each failing product thus contributes at least \(2/256\) to the lower bound, counting the inverse outside \(S\) in the latter case, and using \(3/256\) already on the product itself in the former case.
It follows that \(\|UV-VU\|_{2,\operatorname{Tr}}^2\ge3/16\).

Now \(\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=1/2\) by the [trivial-intersection trace calculation](Yin_C_A0277_v1.md#the-scalar-coefficient-of-each-row-projection).
Since \(PQP\) is a positive contraction and is the identity on its meet with \(Q\) inside \(P\), \(\operatorname{Tr}((PQP)^2)\ge\delta\).
Expansion gives
\[
 \|UV-VU\|_{2,\operatorname{Tr}}^2
 =32\big(\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)-\operatorname{Tr}((PQP)^2)\big)
 \le32(1/2-\delta),
\]
which gives the first bound.

## Alternating products at the next syllable length

Assume the hypothesis through length \(2n\) and write \(\ell=n+1\).
Every alternating product of \(\ell\) nonidentity syllables with prescribed starting subgroup has a unique expression of that form in \(G\).
Indeed two different expressions yield a nonidentity word in the abstract free product by its normal form.
In the product of the first and the inverse of the second, both end syllables of the original expressions can be merged at their respective ends after allowing cyclic conjugation.
The resulting cyclically reduced word has length at most \(2\ell-2\).
If it has length one it cannot vanish because both factors are actual subgroups; otherwise its nonzero even length is forbidden by hypothesis.

Let \(T_1,T_2\) be the alternating products of \(\ell\) operators starting respectively with \(U,V\).
Each Fourier coefficient is zero without an expression by \(\ell\) nonidentity syllables of its prescribed type; with such an expression it is the product of the individual coefficient matrices.
Indeed approximate each centered symmetry in its own subgroup algebra by uniformly bounded finite group-ring matrices with their identity coefficients subtracted, as in the [bounded moment approximation](Yin_C_A0277_v1.md#the-moments-up-to-the-prescribed-syllable-length).
Their products converge in matrix trace \(L^2\) by telescoping.
At each fixed group element the finite expansion contains at most one tuple by the uniqueness just proved; individual coefficients also converge, giving the assertion.
For an expression of total tree distance \(D\), the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of its coefficient lies between \(2^{1-D}\) and \(2^{\ell-D}\), by the rank-one formula and the \(\ell-1\) inner products.
The range line is always the one indexed by the end color of its first syllable.

Take distance-two elements \(a\in A,\ b'\in B\) with different end colors.
Form an alternating word \(a^{-1}b'c_3\cdots c_\ell\) with all \(c_i\) of distance two in the required subgroups.
Its \(T_1\) coefficient has norm at least \(2^{1-2\ell}\).
If its \(T_2\) coefficient has norm less than \(2^{-2\ell}\), then the discrepancy there is at least \(2^{-2\ell}\).
Otherwise it has an opposite expression \(t_1\cdots t_\ell\) whose total tree distance is at most \(3\ell\) by the upper coefficient bound.
Then
\[
              a t_1\cdots t_{\ell-1}
                    = b' c_3\cdots c_\ell t_\ell^{-1}.
\]
Both displays are alternating expressions of length \(\ell\), starting on opposite sides.
The left expression has total tree distance at most \(3\ell\), since replacing \(t_\ell\) by \(a\) adds no distance.
Its coefficient thus has norm at least \(2^{1-3\ell}\), and the two coefficients have different range lines by construction.
Projecting perpendicular to the right-hand line gives discrepancy at least \(\sqrt3\,2^{-3\ell}\).
The first case gives at least this much as well.
In particular \(\|T_1-T_2\|_{2,\operatorname{Tr}}^2\ge3\,2^{-6\ell}\).

Write \(S_0=UV\) and use the normalized two-square trace for its spectral probability measure.
The moments through order \(n\) vanish by the [alternating subgroup bound](Yin_C_A0277_v1.md#the-moments-up-to-the-prescribed-syllable-length).
Consequently the normalized trace of \(K_\ell(S_0)\) equals \(1/\ell\), where \(K_\ell(z)=|1+\cdots+z^{\ell-1}|^2/\ell^2\).
The spectral mass at \(1\) is at least \(\delta\) from the original and complementary meets.
On the circle \(|1-z^\ell|^2=\ell^2|1-z|^2 K_\ell(z)\), and the left side vanishes at \(1\).
As \(T_1T_2^*=(UV)^\ell\), integration gives
\[
        \tfrac12\|T_1-T_2\|_{2,\operatorname{Tr}}^2
                  \le 4\ell^2(1/\ell-\delta).
\]
Substitution proves the second estimate.
