# Cyclic intersections of two free faces in the nine-monomial matrix

## Statement

Over any discrete group \(G\), take the three rows of \(H\) to be
\[
 (1,1,1),\qquad (1,\lambda u,\mu v),\qquad (1,\sigma w,\theta z).
\]
Suppose \(A=\langle u,v\rangle\) and \(B=\langle w,z\rangle\) are freely generated by these pairs, and \(A\cap B=\langle c\rangle\) is infinite cyclic with generator \(c\).
Let \(d_A(g)\) denote the distance from \(L_1\) to \(L_g\) on the [three-edge covering tree](Yin_C_A0277_v2.md#the-row-projection-on-the-three-regular-tree) with \((s_1,s_2,s_3)=(1,u,v)\), and similarly for \(B\) with \((1,w,z)\).
This distance is twice word length in the six letters \(s_i^{-1}s_j,\ i\ne j\).
Write \(D=d_A(c)+d_B(c)\) and use unnormalized coordinate trace for \(\delta=\dim_G\ker H\).

If \(D\ge14\) and the four coefficient moduli are one, then
\[
                             \delta\le\tfrac12-\tfrac1{5120}.
\]
If \(D\ge14\) and the four moduli differ from one by at most \(2^{-17}\), then \(\delta\le 1/2-1/10240\).
Extra mixed relations in \(G\) are permitted throughout.
For generators of any length the formula below gives a separate test on the four phases using the two colored geodesics of \(c\) and its translation lengths.
These conclusions apply to any adjacent pair of [free faces](Yin_C_A0273_v1.md#the-two-row-and-two-column-subgroups) for which the stated normalization holds, including after taking the adjoint.

## The trace on the common cyclic subgroup

Assume unit moduli.
On \(W=\mathbf1^\perp\subset\mathbb C^3\), let \(P,Q\) be the projections onto the individual row kernels of the second and third rows, respectively, and set \(U=2P-I_W,\ V=2Q-I_W\).
The projections have trace one and the trace of their meet is \(\delta\).
For \(X=A,B\), denote by \(\ell_X\) the tree translation length of \(c\) and by \(h_X\) the distance of \(L_1\) from its axis.
A nonempty cyclically reduced loop conjugate to the loop for \(c\), repeated in both directions, lifts to the invariant geodesic.
Following the path to this geodesic, translating along it and returning on the translate of the initial path gives
\[
                 d_X(c^k)=2h_X+|k|\ell_X\qquad(k\ne0).
\]
Both translation lengths are positive and even since the deck action preserves the two vertex classes.
For positive \(k\) the first and last colors \(s_X(c^k),e_X(c^k)\) are constant by the same path description and color preservation, and they exchange for negative \(k\).

Put \(f_i=(e_i-\mathbf1/3)/\sqrt{2/3}\), with \(e_i\) the three coordinate vectors.
Write \(\chi_A(u)=\lambda,\ \chi_A(v)=\mu,\ \chi_B(w)=\sigma,\ \chi_B(z)=\theta\) for the free-group characters.
Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 L&=\ell_A+\ell_B,\quad \rho=2^{-L},\quad
 e^{i\phi}=\chi_A(c)\overline{\chi_B(c)},\\
 \kappa&=2^{-2(h_A+h_B)}
       \langle f_{s_A(c)},f_{s_B(c)}\rangle
       \langle f_{e_B(c)},f_{e_A(c)}\rangle .
 \end{split}
\]
Thus \(0<\rho\le1/16\).
The exact trace and resulting phase test are
\[
 \delta\ \le\ \operatorname{Tr}(PQ)
      =\tfrac12+2\kappa\,\frac{\rho(\cos\phi-\rho)}
                                {1-2\rho\cos\phi+\rho^2}.
\]
In particular \(\kappa(\cos\phi-\rho)<0\) excludes half nullity at every length.
For example, when precisely one corresponding endpoint color agrees and the character restrictions to \(\langle c\rangle\) agree, the inequality is strict.
The test uses the actual character restrictions, which can be forced to be trivial by the two words for \(c\).

Indeed the [Fourier coefficient matrices](Yin_C_A0277_v2.md#the-row-projection-on-the-three-regular-tree) give \(P_1=Q_1=I_W/2\) and
\[
 P_g=-2^{-d_A(g)}\chi_A(g) f_{e_A(g)}f_{s_A(g)}^*
 \qquad(1\ne g\in A),
\]
with the corresponding expression for \(Q\) on \(B\).
All nonidentity terms in the identity coefficient of \(PQ\) come from the labels \(c^k\).
Taking their coordinate traces and pairing \(k\) and \(-k\) gives \(2\kappa\sum_{k\ge1}\rho^k\cos(k\phi)\), yielding the displayed formula and
\[
                    |\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)-1/2|
                   \le \frac{2\,2^{-D}}{1-\rho}.
\]

## Grouping the coefficients of each ordered product

Assume \(D\ge14\).
For each \(g=ab,\ a\in A,\ b\in B\), its complete set of expressions in that order is \((ac^k)(c^{-k}b)\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\).
Thus the coefficient of \(UV\) at \(g\) is the sum of \(U_{ac^k}V_{c^{-k}b}\).
The product coefficient is the ordinary convolution by applying the bounded factors and using the square-summable coefficient vectors in each entry, with absolute convergence by Cauchy-Schwarz.
The same holds for \(VU\) in the opposite order.
Both symmetries have zero identity coefficient, and a term with nonidentity factors at tree distances \(d_1,d_2\) has Hilbert-Schmidt norm at most \(4\cdot2^{-(d_1+d_2)}\).

Call an element short if its distance is two in its indicated tree.
If \(a,b\) are both short, the \(k=0\) term has norm at least \(1/8\), since both factor norms are \(1/2\) and the connecting unit vectors have absolute inner product at least \(1/2\).
For \(k\ne0\), subadditivity gives
\[
 d_A(ac^k)+d_B(c^{-k}b)
       \ge D+(|k|-1)L-4 .
\]
The sum of all other term norms is therefore at most \(128\cdot2^{-D}/(1-\rho)\le1/120\).
This also bounds the remainder around any short pair in the opposite order.

We bound the entire opposite coefficient at \(g\) when it has no expression by two short factors.
If it has any expression with nonidentity factors, choose such an expression \(b'a'\) minimizing the total distance \(t\).
Index its expressions as \((b'c^k)(c^{-k}a')\), and denote the totals by \(t_k\), retaining only indices with nonidentity factors.
Thus \(t_0=t\ge6\) and the totals are even.
Subadditivity in both groups gives, for distinct retained indices \(j,k\),
\[
                     t_j+t_k \ge D+(|k-j|-1)L .
\]
Indeed the two factors on the \(B\) side of the expressions, after taking an inverse on the left, differ by the appropriate power of \(c\).
The two factors on the \(A\) side give its inverse power by taking an inverse on the right.

If \(t\le D/2\), retaining the minimal term and summing the others using \(j=0\) gives total norm at most
\[
          4\cdot2^{-t}+\frac{8\cdot 2^{t-D}}{1-\rho}.
\]
At \(t=6\) this is at most \(1/16+1/30=23/240\).
For \(t\ge8\) in this case \(D\ge16\), and the bound is at most \(1/64+1/30\).
If \(t\ge D/2\), the number of retained indices with \(t_k\le m\), for integer \(m\ge t\), is at most \(2+(2m-D)/L\) by applying the pairwise bound to their smallest and largest indices.
This also holds for a singleton.
Writing each \(2^{-t_k}\) as \(\sum_{m\ge t_k}2^{-m-1}\) bounds the norm sum by
\[
              4\cdot2^{-t}\big(2+(2t-D+2)/4\big)
                 \le 10\cdot2^{-D/2}\le 10/128.
\]
The first bound uses \(L\ge4\) and the second follows by writing \(t=D/2+j\), \(j\ge0\) integral, and using \(10+2j\le10\cdot2^j\).
Hence \(23/240\) uniformly bounds the reverse coefficient without a short pair, also when it vanishes.

## A strict bound from twenty-four different products

The 36 elements \(ab\) with \(a,b\) short are distinct.
Two different such expressions would produce a nonidentity \(c^k\) of distance at most four in each subgroup, while \(d_A(c^k)+d_B(c^k)\ge D>8\).
The same gives uniqueness of a short pair in the opposite order when it exists.
For fixed \(a\), at most two of the six \(b\)'s have a short reverse expression \(ab=b'a'\) with \(e_B(b')=e_A(a)\).
There are just two possible \(b'\)'s, and equality of this \(b'\) for two choices would imply \(b_1^{-1}b_2=(a'_1)^{-1}a'_2\), again a common element of distance at most four on each side.

At the resulting at least 24 distinct products, if there is no short reverse pair, the difference between the two ordered coefficients has norm at least
\[
                 1/8-1/120-23/240=1/48 .
\]
If there is a short reverse pair, its main term has a different range line from the main forward term.
Projection perpendicular to that reverse range bounds the norm difference of the main terms below by \(\sqrt3/16\), since their range unit vectors have absolute inner product \(1/2\).
The two remainders remove at most \(2/120\), leaving more than \(1/48\).
Parseval gives \(\|UV-VU\|_{2,\operatorname{Tr}}^2\ge24/48^2\).

Now \(\delta\le\operatorname{Tr}((PQP)^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)-\|UV-VU\|_{2,\operatorname{Tr}}^2/32\) by the [commutator square identity](Yin_C_A0277_v2.md#a-discrepancy-on-products-of-two-short-elements).
The trace excess over \(1/2\) is at most \(1/7680\), and \(24/(32\cdot48^2)-1/7680=1/5120\).
For the perturbed moduli the [free row spectral gap and four-factor estimate](Yin_C_A0281_v1.md#stability-under-changes-in-the-four-moduli) increase the bound on the four-factor trace by at most \(12\cdot 2^{-17}\).
Since \(1/5120-12\cdot2^{-17}>1/10240\), the near-unit-modulus conclusion follows.
