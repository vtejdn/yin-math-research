# A bounded common label of the two free faces in a half-kernel

## Statement

Let a three-square monomial matrix be normalized to have first row and first column equal to ones.
Write the remaining rows as \((1,\lambda u,\mu v)\) and \((1,\sigma w,\theta z)\).
Assume \(A=\langle u,v\rangle\) and \(B=\langle w,z\rangle\) are freely generated by their indicated pairs.
For \(A\) use the six-letter symmetric generating set \(\{s_i^{-1}s_j:i\ne j\}\), with \((s_1,s_2,s_3)=(1,u,v)\); for \(B\) use \((1,w,z)\) in its place.
Use the unnormalized matrix trace for the regular nullity \(\delta\).
If the four moduli differ from one by at most \(2^{-16}\) and
\[
                    \operatorname{Ball}_A(10)\cap\operatorname{Ball}_B(10)=\{1\}
\]
inside the original group, then \(\delta\le1/2-1/16384\).
Thus a half-kernel in this coefficient range requires a nonidentity group label expressible by at most ten letters on each side, even if the two subgroups have many relations with one another.
The conclusion applies separately to every choice of common row and to the adjoint whenever its normalization meets the stated conditions.
Under scalar group-ring injectivity the [six free faces](Yin_C_A0273_v1.md#the-two-row-and-two-column-subgroups) are necessary for any fractional-rank candidate.

## Truncation on the two trees

First replace each scalar by its phase and use the constant two-dimensional space \(W=\{(c_1,c_2,c_3):\sum c_i=0\}\).
Write \(P_0,Q_0\) for the two individual row-kernel projections there, \(U=2P_0-I,\ V=2Q_0-I\).
The [tree coefficient formula](Yin_C_A0277_v2.md#the-row-projection-on-the-three-regular-tree) gives zero identity coefficients and rank-one coefficient matrices of Hilbert-Schmidt norm \(2\cdot4^{-k}\) for every group label of length \(k\ge1\) in the indicated six-letter metric.
Indeed two steps between left vertices of the three-regular tree correspond to one such letter and the tree geodesic is shortest.
There are \(3\cdot 2^{2k-1}\) labels at length \(k\).
Write \(U_L,V_L\) for truncation through length \(L\), with zero identity coefficients.
For the unnormalized trace norm \(\|\cdot\|_2\) this gives
\[
                  \|U-U_L\|_2=\|V-V_L\|_2=\sqrt2\,2^{-L}.
\]

There is also the operator-norm bound \(\|U_L\|,\|V_L\|\le1+(2L+7)2^{-L}\).
For the tree adjacency \(T\), its selfadjoint spectrum lies in \([-2\sqrt2,2\sqrt2]\) by the weighted Schur test in the coefficient proof.
The operator summing over the sphere of radius \(n\) is \(b_n(T)\), with \(b_1=X,b_2=X^2-3,\ b_{n+1}=Xb_n-2b_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge2\).
For \(n\ge2\) and \(X=2\sqrt2\cos h\), the recurrence gives
\[
      2^{-n/2} b_n(X)
          =\frac12\,\frac{\sin((n+1)h)}{\sin h}+\cos(nh).
\]
Both sides extend by continuity at the endpoints, hence their absolute value is at most \((n+3)/2\).
The length-\(k\) shell of a symmetry applied to a vector is pointwise dominated in coordinate norm by \(2\cdot4^{-k}\) times the unweighted distance-\(2k\) sphere applied to its coordinate norms.
This uses the left vertices of the tree and applies on every right subgroup coset.
Its operator norm is at most \((2k+3)2^{-k}\).
Summing shells beyond \(L\) proves the operator-norm bound and identifies the norm sum with the Fourier tail by its \(L^2\) convergence.

## Local uniqueness in the two products

Set \(L=5\).
The ball hypothesis makes each expression \(ab\), \(a\in A,b\in B\) both of length at most \(L\), unique in those two balls.
Indeed a second expression would give a common element of lengths at most \(2L\) in the two subgroups.
The same holds in the reverse order.
We reproduce the [short-product discrepancy](Yin_C_A0277_v2.md#a-discrepancy-on-products-of-two-short-elements) for the finite sums \(U_L,V_L\).

Among the 36 distinct products \(ab\) with both lengths one, fix the first letter \(a\).
There are at most two choices of \(b\) for which a reversed representation \(b'a'\) uses two length-one factors with the last tree-edge color of \(b'\) equal to that of \(a\).
There are just two possible \(b'\) of that color and one such \(b'\) cannot occur for two \(b\)'s, since \(b_1^{-1}b_2=(a'_1)^{-1}a'_2\) would violate the ball hypothesis.
At the resulting at least 24 exceptional products the forward Fourier coefficient has norm at least \(1/8\).
In fact the two individual coefficients each have norm \(1/2\) and the inner product of their connecting unit vectors has absolute value one or one half.
If an exceptional product belongs to the inverse of the 36-element set, the two ranges have different last colors, so the difference has norm at least \(\sqrt3/16\) by projection perpendicular to the reversed range.
Otherwise the opposite coefficient is zero or involves a factor of length at least two, giving norm at most \(1/16\).
Its difference from the forward coefficient then has norm at least \(1/16\), and its inverse outside the set contributes an equal squared norm since the commutator is skew adjoint.
Parseval therefore gives
\[
    \|U_LV_L-V_LU_L\|_2^2\ge 24\cdot 2/256=3/16 .
\]
By the two tail bounds and the triangle inequality,
\[
   \|UV-VU\|_2\ \ge\
   \frac{\sqrt3}{4}-2\sqrt2\,2^{-L}\big(2+(2L+7)2^{-L}\big) >1/5 .
\]

The ball hypothesis also gives \(\operatorname{Tr}(U_{2L} V_{2L})=0\).
Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the two tails at \(2L\), using \(\|U\|_2=\|V\|_2=\sqrt2\), gives \(|\operatorname{Tr}(UV)|\le4\cdot2^{-2L}\).
Hence \(\operatorname{Tr}(P_0Q_0)\le 1/2+2^{-2L}\).
The exact [commutator square identity](Yin_C_A0277_v2.md#a-discrepancy-on-products-of-two-short-elements) now gives
\[
       \operatorname{Tr}(P_0Q_0P_0Q_0)
       \le 1/2+1/1024-1/800 .
\]

## Stability under changes in the four moduli

For one of the unit-modulus rows write \(b_0=(1,\lambda_0 u,\mu_0 v)|_W\), and put \(S=1+\lambda_0u+\mu_0v\).
Then \(b_0 b_0^*=3I-SS^*/3\).
The bipartite adjacency of the free three-edge cover has off-diagonal block \(S\) after removing the two phases by a free-group character.
The same adjacency bound therefore gives \(b_0b_0^*\ge I/3\).
If the corresponding actual row on \(W\) is \(b\), then \(\|b-b_0\|\le\sqrt2\,\epsilon\) for \(\epsilon=2^{-16}\), by the two changed entries.
Its adjoint is bounded below by \(1/\sqrt3-\sqrt2\,\epsilon>0\), so both row maps are onto.
With \(P\) the kernel projection of \(b\), restricting to the two kernels and using the respective lower bounds on their orthogonal complements yields
\[
 \|(I-P_0)P\|\le\sqrt6\,\epsilon,\qquad
 \|(I-P)P_0\|\le \sqrt6\,\epsilon/(1-\sqrt6\,\epsilon).
\]
The norm of a difference of projections is the maximum of these two cross norms, by squaring and splitting over \(P\) and \(I-P\).
Thus \(\|P-P_0\|\le3\epsilon\), and likewise \(\|Q-Q_0\|\le3\epsilon\).
All four projections have trace one by surjectivity.

The meet \(P\wedge Q\) is exactly the full kernel inside the constant first-row kernel.
Consequently \(\delta\le\operatorname{Tr}(PQPQ)\), since \((PQP)^2\) dominates the meet.
Telescoping the four factors in the trace changes its value by at most \(12\epsilon\): each changed factor has norm at most \(3\epsilon\), and the remaining product has trace norm at most one by the trace norm of a rank-one-in-trace projection and the operator norm bounds on the other factors.
Now \(1/800-1/1024-12/65536>1/16384\), proving the stated gap.
