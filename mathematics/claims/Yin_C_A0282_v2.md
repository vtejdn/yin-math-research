# A detectable drop in inner rank for the four phase translates in the original group

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity, and suppose \(H\) has an abelian quotient of order 24 and a two-by-two four-site rectangle of regular rank \(3/2\).
Use the [normalized marking](Yin_C_A0241_v1.md#statement) with \(\chi(s)=3,\ \chi(t)=4\) in \(C_{24}\).
For \(\omega^4=1\) put
\[
     K_\omega=B+At-\omega s(D+Ct),\qquad
     Y=\bigoplus_{\omega^4=1}K_\omega,\qquad q(t)=\det(Ct+D).
\]
The nonzero scalar \(q(t)\) is a unit in the regular affiliated algebra \(U(H)\).
The inner rank of \(Y\) over \(\mathbb CH\) is at least seven.
Over the subring generated by \(\mathbb CH\) and the single inverse \(q(t)^{-1}\), \(Y\) is equivalent to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_6,0_2)\) and has inner rank exactly six.
Its regular rank and its inner rank over \(D_1(H)\) are also six, where \(D_1(H)\) is the first scalar inverse layer.
Thus a half-nullity input forces an actual inner-rank drop after one scalar inverse in the original group.

The four summands are the character translates of one fixed input under \(s\mapsto\omega s,\ t\mapsto t\).
For every nonzero scalar pivot of each summand, the [first-layer scalar idempotent](Yin_C_A0282_v1.md#recovering-the-scalar-idempotent-from-the-schur-coordinate) has image the actual Schur kernel, and the orthogonal kernel projection belongs to \(D_2(H)\).
The [four projective classes](Yin_C_A0282_v1.md#the-phase-orbit-and-a-six-coordinate-factorization) sum to \(2[1]\).
The rank comparison here uses the four translated inputs together, and does not assume that their individual projective classes are equal.

## The base-ring lower bound

Write \(N(z)=Az+B,\ Q(z)=Cz+D\).
The [scalar character test for the four-step chain](Yin_C_A0247_v1.md#a-rank-of-at-least-seven-in-a-scalar-character) gives some \(\zeta\in\mu_6\) with
\[
                \sum_{\lambda^4=\zeta^3}
                       \dim_{\mathbb C}\ker(N(\zeta)-\lambda Q(\zeta))\le1 .
\]
This concerns only polynomial blocks and includes singular \(Q(\zeta)\).
Choose \(w\in\mu_{24}\) with \(w^4=\zeta\).
The character \(h\mapsto w^{\chi(h)}\) of \(H\) evaluates \(s\) at \(w^3\) and \(t\) at \(\zeta\).
Its evaluation on the summand \(K_\omega\) has \(\lambda=\omega w^3\), so the four distinct summands run through all four roots of \(\lambda^4=\zeta^3\).
The evaluation of \(Y\) at this single character has rank at least seven.
Any factorization over \(\mathbb CH\) specializes to a complex factorization through the same number of coordinates, giving the claimed lower bound without evaluating affiliated inverses.

## Six coordinates after one actual scalar inverse

Let \(R\subseteq U(H)\) be the subring generated by the group ring and \(q(t)^{-1}\).
The adjugate formula gives \(Q(t)^{-1}\in M_2(R)\), since its coefficients are in the cyclic group ring of \(t\).
Define \(V=(s^{-1}I_2)N(t)Q(t)^{-1}\).
The [four-phase transfer equality](Yin_C_A0240_v1.md#the-four-step-product-and-its-middle-factors) gives \(V^4=I_2\).
Left multiplication by \(s^{-1}I_2\) and right multiplication by \(Q(t)^{-1}\) change \(K_\omega\) to \(V-\omega I_2\).
Conjugation by a constant Fourier matrix on the four phase coordinates identifies the resulting direct sum with the block matrix of the four equations
\[
           Vx_0-x_1,\quad Vx_1-x_2,\quad Vx_2-x_3,\quad Vx_3-x_0 .
\]
Indeed the scalar cyclic shift has precisely the four eigenvalues \(\omega\).

Keep \(x_0\) and use \(y_i=x_i-Vx_{i-1}\), \(1\le i\le3\), as the other variables.
Both this triangular change and its inverse have entries in \(R\).
The first three equations become \(-y_i\), and the last becomes
\[
           (V^4-I_2)x_0+V^3y_1+V^2y_2+Vy_3.
\]
Row operations eliminate the three \(y_i\) terms and leave zero.
Permutations and signs give the asserted equivalence to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_6,0_2)\) over \(R\).
This proves the upper bound of six as well as regular rank six; the latter rules out a smaller factorization over any intermediate subring of \(U(H)\).
