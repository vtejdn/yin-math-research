# Every two-dimensional projective representation has cyclic image at the order-24 half-kernel

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular injectivity for nonzero complex group-ring scalars.
Suppose \(H\) has an abelian quotient of order 24 and some complex two-square rectangle \(B+At-s(D+Ct)\) has regular nullity \(1/2\).
For every field \(k\), every homomorphism \(H\to\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)\) factors through the cyclic abelianization \(C_{24}\).
In particular any noncommuting projective image of the generators rules out this nullity.
This includes all nonabelian finite subgroups of projective linear groups of degree two in any characteristic, and groups \(H\) in which a nonidentity commutator can be detected by a two-dimensional projective representation.

The same cyclic-image conclusion holds for ordinary homomorphisms into \(\operatorname{GL}_2(k)\).
Indeed once the projective image is cyclic, its inverse image in the linear image is generated by one lift and its scalar kernel and hence is abelian.
These conclusions concern this four-site matrix at the order-24 marking.

## An invariant of a double coset in two dimensions

The [solvable quotient theorem](Yin_C_A0278_v1.md#statement) gives \(H_{\rm ab}=C_{24}\), \(H'=H''\), and the normalized marking.
First consider a finite projective image \(J=\langle x,y\rangle\), and assume it is nonabelian.
It must be nonsolvable by that theorem.
Extend the coefficient field to its algebraic closure, an injective change for projective matrices.
A scalar \(y\) would give cyclic image.
If \(y\) has order two, the [adjacent generation theorem](Yin_C_A0272_v1.md#the-order-24-application) makes the image of the index-four normal subgroup generated by the two conjugate involutions \(y,xyx^{-1}\).
A group generated by two involutions has a cyclic subgroup of index at most two, so the full image, with cyclic quotient, would be solvable.
If \(y\) is nontrivial unipotent it is ruled out by the [unipotent row test](Yin_C_A0283_v1.md#a-determinant-along-a-full-unipotent-row).
Thus \(y\) is semisimple of order \(m\ge3\) and has two distinct eigenlines.
If \(\langle xyx^{-1}\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle\) is nontrivial, an element of this intersection has exactly the two eigenlines of each generator since every nonscalar power has distinct eigenvalues.
Then \(x,y\) preserve the same unordered pair of lines and generate a subgroup of the solvable monomial group.
The intersection must therefore be trivial.

Choose determinant-one lifts \(X,Y\) of \(x,y\) over the algebraic closure, with \(Y=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\lambda^{-1})\); the order of \(\lambda^2\) is \(m\).
The [two complete Fourier rows](Yin_C_A0283_v1.md#the-two-complete-rows-and-columns-of-finite-labels) give an \(i\bmod m\) such that for \(h=i,i+1\) and every \(j\bmod m\),
\[
     (xy^hx^{-1})y^j\in
        (x^2\langle y\rangle x^{-2})(x^3\langle y\rangle x^{-3}) x^4 .
\]
Indeed \(y_1^h=xy^h x^{-1}\); consecutive rows in the negative direction give the same unordered pair.
Left multiplying by \(x^{-2}\) and right multiplying by \(x^{-1}\) makes the target double coset \(\langle y\rangle x\langle y\rangle\).
Put \(Z=X^{-1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(K_h=ZY^hZ\).
The determinant-one matrix \(K_hY^jZ\) then equals \(\pm Y^v X Y^w\) for some \(v,w\), with the sign immaterial also in characteristic two.
The product of its upper left and lower right entries is the constant \(X_{11}X_{22}\): the two diagonal phases from each side cancel, as does the sign.

Write the entries of \(K_h\) as \(k_{rs}\).
As a function of \(j\), that diagonal product equals
\[
   (k_{11}a\,\lambda^j+k_{12}c\,\lambda^{-j})
   (k_{21}b\,\lambda^j+k_{22}d\,\lambda^{-j}).
\]
The characters \(1,\lambda^{2j},\lambda^{-2j}\) are linearly independent over the algebraic closure because the \(m\ge3\) powers of \(\lambda^2\) are distinct.
It follows at both values of \(h\) that
\[
                      ab\,k_{11}k_{21}=0,\qquad
                      cd\,k_{12}k_{22}=0.
\]

## The two consecutive rows force a common line or a common pair

If \(b=0\) or \(c=0\), the two generators preserve a common line and are triangular in a common basis, giving a solvable image.
If \(a=d=0\), they preserve the unordered eigenline pair, again giving a solvable image.
Suppose first that all four entries of \(Z\) are nonzero.
Each column of the invertible \(K_h\) must then have exactly one nonzero entry, so \(K_i,K_{i+1}\) are monomial.
The product \(K_{i+1}K_i^{-1}=ZYZ^{-1}\) is monomial as well.
An antidiagonal invertible matrix squares to a scalar and has projective order two.
As \(y\) has order at least three, \(ZYZ^{-1}\) is diagonal with distinct eigenlines.
Therefore \(Z\) preserves the unordered pair of coordinate lines and the image would be solvable.

There remain two patterns with exactly one zero on the diagonal of \(Z\).
When \(a=0\), the entry \(k_{12}=bd\lambda^{-h}\) is nonzero and \(cd\ne0\).
Thus \(k_{22}=cb\lambda^h+d^2\lambda^{-h}=0\), which specifies a single value of \(\lambda^{2h}\).
When \(d=0\), the entry \(k_{21}=ca\lambda^h\) is nonzero and \(ab\ne0\).
Then \(k_{11}=a^2\lambda^h+bc\lambda^{-h}=0\) also specifies a single value.
Neither pattern can occur at consecutive \(h\) since \(\lambda^2\ne1\).
This proves the assertion for every finite image.

## Preserving a noncommuting pair on specialization

Given any projective representation over \(k\), choose invertible representatives for its two generators and let \(R\subset k\) be the ring generated over the prime ring by their entries and their inverse determinants.
Every word in these matrices and their inverses has entries in \(R\) and invertible determinant there.
If the projective representatives do not commute, their group commutator as matrices is nonscalar.
One of its off-diagonal entries or its diagonal difference is a nonzero \(d\in R\).
Choose a maximal ideal of \(R[1/d]\); its residue field is finite.

For completeness a field finitely generated as a ring has this finiteness property.
In positive characteristic Zariski's lemma makes it a finite extension of its finite prime field.
In characteristic zero the field would also be finitely generated as a \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra and hence would be a number field by the same lemma.
All its ring generators lie in the algebraic integers with one integer \(N\) inverted, by clearing the denominators in their monic rational equations.
Such a ring cannot contain \(1/p\) for a prime \(p\nmid N\), since \(N^j/p\) is never a rational algebraic integer.
This contradicts its being a characteristic-zero field.

Reduction to the residue field preserves the projective relations of \(H\): each relation is a scalar matrix over \(R\) with unit determinant, so its scalar remains a unit.
The two images still do not commute projectively by the choice of \(d\).
They would therefore provide a finite nonabelian projective image, already excluded.
The original image is abelian and hence factors through \(C_{24}\), as claimed.
