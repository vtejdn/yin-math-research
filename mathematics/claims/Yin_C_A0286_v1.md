# Small rank of a marked generator in higher-dimensional projective images

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity and an abelian quotient of order 24.
Suppose the regular rank of a complex two-by-two rectangle \(B+At-s(D+Ct)\) is \(3/2\).
Use the [normalized ordered marks](Yin_C_A0241_v1.md#statement).
For a homomorphism \(H\to{\rm PGL}(V)\) on a finite-dimensional vector space over any field, choose lifts \(X,Y\in {\rm GL}(V)\) of the two marks and write \(d=\dim V\).
No finiteness of the image is assumed.

If after a scalar choice of lift \(r=\operatorname{rank}(Y-I)\le1\), the whole projective image is cyclic of order dividing 24.
This includes a transvection and a semisimple element with one possibly exceptional eigenvalue of multiplicity one.
The same conclusion holds if that scalar choice is possible after extending the field, since extension of scalars is injective on projective matrices.
In particular for \(d\ge3\) no \({\rm PSL}_d(\mathbb F_q)\) with a transvection as second mark can be a quotient, in any characteristic.

More generally, if \(r>0\) and the projective representation is irreducible over the chosen field, then \(d\le3r\).
If the chosen \(Y\) is unipotent, then \(d\le2r\).
Irreducibility here means that no nonzero proper linear subspace is invariant under both lifts.

## The subspaces preserved by consecutive conjugates

Let \(F\) be the [normal subgroup of index four generated by every adjacent pair](Yin_C_A0274_v1.md#statement).
Put \(Y_i=X^iYX^{-i}\), \(L_i=\operatorname{im}(Y_i-I)\) and \(U_i=L_i+L_{i+1}\).
Every \(U_i\) is preserved by the projective image of \(F\), since it is preserved by \(Y_i,Y_{i+1}\) and their projective classes generate that image.
Both act as the identity on \(V/U_i\), so the induced projective action of \(F\) there is trivial when this quotient is nonzero.
Moreover \(XL_i=L_{i+1}\) and \(XU_i=U_{i+1}\).

For an irreducible representation with \(r>0\), suppose \(U_0\ne V\).
The projective class of \(Y_2\) belongs to the group generated by the classes of \(Y_0,Y_1\).
Thus \(Y_2=cW\) for a nonzero scalar \(c\) and a word \(W\) in these two lifts and their inverses.
The linear action of \(Y_2\) on \(V/U_0\) is \(cI\), so \(\operatorname{rank}(Y_2-cI)\le\dim U_0\le2r\).
If \(c=1\) then \(L_2\subseteq U_0\), giving \(XU_0=U_1\subseteq U_0\) and hence equality by dimension.
This would make the nonzero proper \(U_0\) invariant under \(X,Y\).
Thus \(c\ne1\), and subtracting \(Y_2-cI\) from \(Y_2-I\) gives \(d\le r+2r\).
If \(Y\) is unipotent, its conjugate can induce only a unipotent operator on \(V/U_0\), forcing \(c=1\).
In that case \(U_0=V\) was necessary and \(d\le2r\); the same dimensional bound is immediate when \(U_0=V\) in the general argument.

## Rank one in a reducible or irreducible representation

If \(r=0\), the projective image is generated by \(X\) alone.
Suppose \(r=1\), so the \(L_i\) are lines.
The action on any projectively invariant space of dimension one or two has cyclic projective image by the [degree-two projective representation theorem](Yin_C_A0284_v1.md#statement).
In particular this already proves the assertion for \(d\le2\).

If \(U_0=U_1\), the subspace \(U_0\) is invariant under the entire group.
It has dimension at most two.
On \(V/U_0\) the second mark acts trivially, so this projective image is cyclic as well.

If \(U_0\ne U_1\), they are two distinct planes.
Indeed if \(L_0=L_1\), applying powers of \(X\) makes all the lines equal.
The two planes intersect in \(L_1\), so the image of \(F\) preserves \(L_1\) and, by normality and conjugation by \(X\), preserves each \(L_i\).
Let \(V_0\) be the span of all these lines, invariant under \(X,Y\).
A basis selected from the lines makes the action of \(F\) on \(V_0\) projectively diagonal and hence abelian.
The full projective action on \(V_0\) is solvable, since its quotient by the image of \(F\) is cyclic.
On \(V/V_0\) the projective image is cyclic whenever this quotient is nonzero, as \(Y-I\) has image in \(L_0\subseteq V_0\).

For any invariant subspace as above, if both its projective image and that on the quotient are solvable, then the full projective image is solvable.
Indeed the kernel of the two actions is represented in a block basis by matrices with scalar diagonal blocks.
Their diagonal scalar ratio gives a homomorphism to the multiplicative group of the field, with abelian kernel consisting projectively of additive upper off-diagonal blocks.
When the quotient space is zero only the action on the subspace is needed.
Thus the full image in both cases is solvable.
By the [solvable quotient theorem](Yin_C_A0278_v1.md#statement) it factors through \(C_{24}\).
The finite special linear examples are noncyclic, since the elementary matrices in positions \((1,2)\) and \((2,3)\) do not commute projectively.
