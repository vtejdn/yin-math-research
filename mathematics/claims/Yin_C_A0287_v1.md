# Two sparse Fourier supports forced by a nonabelian finite image

## Statement

Under the [order-24 half-kernel hypotheses](Yin_C_A0278_v1.md#statement), write \(M(z)=(Az+B)(Cz+D)^{-1}\).
If \(H\) has even one finite nonabelian image, both \(M\) and \(M^{-1}\) must satisfy the following coefficient test.
For the transfer being tested, denote its coefficients by \(A,B,C,D\), exchanging numerator and denominator for the inverse.
Exactly one of \(C,D\) has rank one and the other is invertible.
If necessary replace \(z\) by \(z^{-1}\) and interchange \(A,B\) and \(C,D\), so that \(D\) is invertible.
With
\[
            T=D^{-1}C,\qquad R=(A-BT)TD^{-1},
\]
one must have \(R^2=0\).
In particular, if either cyclic pencil has two invertible corners then every finite image is cyclic of order dividing 24.
For a residually finite \(H\), failure of either coefficient test therefore excludes the half-kernel.

In any finite nonabelian image, quotient by the intersection of the two neighboring cyclic subgroups.
The resulting image remains nonsolvable; write \(x,y\) for its two marks and \(m=|y|\).
There \(m\ge3\) and the two actual Fourier supports in the [middle transfer identity](Yin_C_A0280_v1.md#two-factors-in-the-actual-finite-regular-twist) are equal sets of exactly \(4m-4\) group elements.
Each consists of two full rows and two full columns of its \(m\)-by-\(m\) set of products of neighboring cyclic subgroups.
For each side the selected indices are \(0,1\) or \(0,-1\) according to the inversion of its parameter.
Thus the required equality is much more restrictive than a lower bound on the number of supported labels.

## Reducing the intersection and bounding the support

In a finite nonabelian image \(J=\langle x,y\rangle\), let \(y_i=x^iyx^{-i}\) and \(Y_i=\langle y_i\rangle\).
All \(Y_i\) have the same finite order.
Their adjacent intersection \(Z=Y_0\cap Y_1\) is the unique subgroup of its order in each cyclic group.
Hence \(xZx^{-1}=Z\) and \(y\) centralizes \(Z\), so \(Z\) is normal in \(J\).
As \(J\) is nonsolvable by the [solvable quotient exclusion](Yin_C_A0278_v1.md#statement), \(J/Z\) remains nonsolvable.
In this quotient the images of \(Y_0,Y_1\) have trivial intersection, and so do all conjugate adjacent pairs.
Retain the same notation in the quotient and put \(F\) for the image of the index-four subgroup of \(H\).
By [adjacent generation](Yin_C_A0272_v1.md#the-order-24-application) any two adjacent \(Y_i\) generate \(F\); the quotient by \(F\) is cyclic.
Orders \(m=1,2\) make \(F\) trivial or generated by two involutions, hence solvable, so \(m\ge3\).

In the middle identity the left support lies in \(S=Y_1Y_0\), the right support in \(S'=Y_2Y_3x^4\).
Both sets have size \(m^2\) by the cyclic intersections just checked.
On each side every label identifies its two indices uniquely, and the only factors in its coefficient are the two corresponding evaluated Fourier blocks, with an extra invertible group translation on the right side.
The equality of supports follows by comparing the finite-coordinate coefficients in the affiliated identity, as in the [two-factor comparison](Yin_C_A0280_v1.md#two-factors-in-the-actual-finite-regular-twist).

If either support has all \(m^2\) labels then \(S=S'\).
The set \(S\) is invariant on the left under \(Y_1\) from the first expression and under \(Y_2\) from the second.
Since it contains the identity and is contained in \(F\), adjacent generation gives \(F=S=Y_1Y_0\).
A group that is a product of two abelian subgroups is metabelian by [Itô's factorization theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01180647).
Thus \(J/Z\) would be solvable, a contradiction.
This use of the factorization theorem is solely about the actual finite image.

## The missing products and the numerical residue

For \(M\), and also for its inverse, the [regular pencil and assembled-block restrictions](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement) apply.
The two determinants have circle zeros and are nonzero, so each pair \(A,B\) and \(C,D\) has an invertible member and neither member can be zero.
The inverse transfer also has no constant point correspondence: an invertible rational transfer with an inverse carrying one constant line into another would carry the latter to the former.
These facts permit the [finite Fourier block calculation](Yin_C_A0280_v1.md#which-finite-frequencies-must-contribute) for either side.
Inverting the parameter if necessary, take \(D\) invertible.
If \(C\) is invertible as well, \(T=D^{-1}C\) is a unit.
All nonzero-index blocks are then units by their first nonzero Taylor coefficients.
For the zero index, multiplication on the right by \(D(I-(-zT)^m)\) gives exactly
\[
                          B+(-1)^{m-1} z^m A T^{m-1}.
\]
Indeed \((I+zT)^{-1}(I-(-zT)^m)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-zT)^j\) and the multiplier preserves the zero frequency.
The displayed polynomial is invertible over \(\mathbb C(z)\) by its constant or highest coefficient, since \(A\) or \(B\) is invertible.
Thus every block is a unit and the entire product set would occur, already excluded.

Suppose instead that \(C\) has rank one.
The matrix \(T\) has nonzero trace \(\alpha\), since otherwise \(\det(Cz+D)\) would be a constant.
The transfer then takes the form
\[
             M(z)=BD^{-1}+z(A-BT)D^{-1}-\frac{z^2}{1+\alpha z}R .
\]
The blocks of indices zero and one are units by the [rank-one calculation](Yin_C_A0280_v1.md#which-finite-frequencies-must-contribute).
Every other block is a nonzero scalar rational function times the same nonzero constant matrix \(R\).
At the actual infinite-order group labels these scalar functions are affiliated units, though functions of different group elements need not commute.
Since constant matrices commute with scalar operator matrices, a product of two blocks outside the selected indices is zero exactly when \(R^2=0\).
When \(R^2\ne0\) the support would again have size \(m^2\).
When \(R^2=0\) precisely the products with a selected index in at least one coordinate remain, giving \(m^2-(m-2)^2=4m-4\) labels.
Parameter inversion negates both selected indices.
This proves the simultaneous test for the transfer and its inverse.

Finally, if every finite image of \(H\) is cyclic, residual finiteness would kill \(H'\), giving \(H=H_{\rm ab}=C_{24}\), incompatible with torsion-freeness.
This yields the stated exclusion for residually finite label groups.
