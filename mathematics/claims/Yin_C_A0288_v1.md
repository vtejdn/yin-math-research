# A finite-order second mark forces cyclic image at the order-twenty-four half-kernel

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity and an abelian quotient of order 24.
Suppose a complex two-by-two rectangle \(B+At-s(D+Ct)\) has regular rank \(3/2\).
Use the [normalized ordered marks](Yin_C_A0241_v1.md#statement) with degrees \(3,4\) in \(H_{\rm ab}=C_{24}\).

Every homomorphism from \(H\) to an arbitrary group for which the image of \(t\) has finite order factors through \(C_{24}\).
For every integer \(m\ge1\), in this marking the abelianization map induces the isomorphism
\[
              H/\langle\!\langle t^m\rangle\!\rangle_H
                          \ \simeq\ C_{\gcd(24,4m)} .
\]
In particular the normal closure of \(t^{6m}\) in \(H\) is the full commutator subgroup \(H'\) for every \(m\ge1\).

Every finite image is cyclic, the profinite completion of \(H\) is \(C_{24}\), and every finite-index subgroup is the inverse image of a subgroup of \(C_{24}\).
The nontrivial perfect subgroup \(H'\) has no proper finite-index subgroup.
Thus no residually finite group can give the stated rectangle at abelian quotient order 24.
Every finite-dimensional linear or projective linear representation of \(H\) over every field also factors through \(C_{24}\).

## Comparing the two consecutive columns in an arbitrary image

Write \(J=\langle x,y\rangle\) for the image of \(H\), with \(x,y\) the images of \(s,t\) and \(y\) of finite order.
Suppose \(J\) is noncyclic, so it is nonsolvable by the [solvable quotient theorem](Yin_C_A0278_v1.md#statement).
Set \(y_j=x^jyx^{-j},\ Y_j=\langle y_j\rangle\).
The adjacent intersection \(Z=Y_0\cap Y_1\) is normal in \(J\).
Indeed its conjugate by \(x\) is \(Y_1\cap Y_2\), another subgroup of the same order in the finite cyclic \(Y_1\), so equals \(Z\); also \(y\) centralizes \(Z\).
Quotient by the cyclic normal subgroup \(Z\).
The resulting group remains nonsolvable and all its adjacent cyclic intersections are trivial.
Use the same symbols for the new images and write \(n=|y|\).
The [adjacent generation theorem](Yin_C_A0274_v1.md#statement) gives a normal subgroup \(F\) generated by any two adjacent \(Y_j\), with \(J/F\) cyclic of order dividing four and \(x^4\in F\).
If \(n=1\) then \(F\) is trivial; if \(n=2\) it is generated by two involutions and has a cyclic subgroup of index at most two.
Either case would make \(J\) solvable, so \(n\ge3\).

The [two consecutive columns of finite labels](Yin_C_A0283_v1.md#the-two-complete-rows-and-columns-of-finite-labels) apply here even though \(J\) itself need not be finite.
For detail embed \(H\) by \(h\mapsto(\pi(h),h)\) in \(J\times H\), where \(\pi\) is the current homomorphism.
Regular subgroup inclusion preserves affiliated identities by coset decomposition.
In the [two-factor transfer identity](Yin_C_A0280_v1.md#two-factors-in-the-actual-finite-regular-twist), only the individual cyclic groups \(Y_j\) are used to expand \(M(y_jt_j)\) and its inverse, where \(M(z)=(Az+B)(Cz+D)^{-1}\) and \(t_j=s^jts^{-j}\).
Their finite spectral decompositions give the same \(n\) rational Fourier blocks as there, evaluated at the infinite-order original translations \(t_j\).
The finitely many elements of \(J\) occurring in either product are independent over the second affiliated algebra: clear a common denominator on the right in \(\mathcal U(H)\), then take group Fourier coefficients on the disjoint \(J\)-coordinate fibers.
The \(Y_1Y_0\) products have unique indices since the adjacent intersection is trivial.
The two consecutive rational unit blocks and nonvanishing of every other block therefore give some \(i\bmod n\) for which
\[
                Y_1 y_0^e\ \subseteq\ P=Y_2Y_3 x^4
                           \qquad(e=i,i+1).
\]

The set \(P\) is invariant under left multiplication by \(Y_2\).
Thus \(Y_2Y_1y_0^e\subseteq P\) for both indices.
Both sides have cardinality \(n^2\), by triviality of the intersections of \(Y_2\) with each of \(Y_1,Y_3\).
Consequently
\[
                  Y_2Y_1 y_0^i=P=Y_2Y_1 y_0^{i+1}.
\]
It follows that \(Y_2Y_1\) is invariant on the right under \(y_0\), as well as under \(Y_1\).
These generate \(F\), while \(Y_2Y_1\subseteq F\) contains the identity.
Hence \(F=Y_2Y_1\) is a product of two finite cyclic subgroups.
It is metabelian by [Itô's factorization theorem](Yin_C_A0287_v1.md#reducing-the-intersection-and-bounding-the-support).
Its cyclic extension \(J\) would be solvable, contradicting the choice of quotient.
The original image must therefore factor through its abelianization as claimed.

## Normal closures and finite-index subgroups

In the quotient by the normal closure of \(t^m\) the second mark has finite order.
That quotient is therefore its own abelianization, canonically the quotient of \(\mathbb Z/24\) by the subgroup generated by \(4m\bmod24\) in the chosen marking.
This gives the displayed filling formula and the normal-closure assertion.

In every finite quotient \(t\) has finite order.
The intersection of the kernels of all finite quotients thus contains \(H'\), and the map onto \(C_{24}\) gives the reverse inclusion.
All finite quotients factor through this one, proving the completion assertion.
For any finite-index subgroup \(K\le H\), the action on its finitely many cosets has a kernel contained in \(K\), so \(H'\subseteq K\).
Conversely, the inverse images of all eight subgroups of \(C_{24}\) have finite index.
Any finite-index subgroup of \(H'\) would also have finite index in \(H\), so must equal \(H'\).
The commutator subgroup is perfect by the solvable quotient theorem and is nontrivial: if it vanished, the torsion-free \(H\) would itself be \(C_{24}\).
The nontrivial finite residual excludes residual finiteness.

## Representations over arbitrary fields

Suppose a finite-dimensional projective representation has noncommuting generator images.
Choose invertible matrix lifts, and over the prime ring form the subring generated by their entries and their inverse determinants.
Their commutator is nonscalar, so an off-diagonal entry or the difference of two diagonal entries is nonzero.
Invert that element in the ring and reduce at a maximal ideal, whose residue field is finite by the [finite-field specialization argument](Yin_C_A0284_v1.md#preserving-a-noncommuting-pair-on-specialization).
The reduced matrices remain invertible, the same commutator remains nonscalar, and all projective relations persist because each relation was a scalar matrix with unit determinant.
This gives a noncommuting finite image, already excluded.
Thus every such projective representation factors through \(C_{24}\).
For a linear representation the projective image is then cyclic; the linear image is generated by one lift of a projective generator and its central scalar kernel, so is abelian and again factors through \(C_{24}\).
