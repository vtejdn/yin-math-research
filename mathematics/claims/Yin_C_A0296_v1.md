# Adjacent conjugates generate the subgroup of index two at cyclic order twenty-six

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity and a quotient of order 26, assumed cyclic.
If a complex two-by-two four-site rectangle \(B+At-s(D+Ct)\) has regular rank \(3/2\), use the [normalized degrees](Yin_C_A0201_v1.md#statement) \(\chi(s)=1,\ \chi(t)=10\) in \(C_{26}=H_{\rm ab}\).
Write \(t_i=s^its^{-i}\).
Then
\[
                \langle t_i,t_{i+1}\rangle=\ker(\chi\bmod2)
                                       \qquad(i\in\mathbb Z).
\]
In any group image in which the second mark has order dividing two, the whole image is cyclic.
In particular
\[
                 H/\langle\!\langle t^2\rangle\!\rangle_H
                              \simeq C_2 .
\]

## The two-step transfer cannot have a second extremal window

The [assembled coefficient theorem](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement) gives the rationally invertible transfer \(M(z)=(Az+B)(Cz+D)^{-1}\).
Over the original affiliated algebra put \(U=s^{-1}M(t)\).
Its \(+1\) eigenspace has dimension \(1/2\), by multiplication with the invertible denominator and the rank hypothesis.
The sign character of \(H\) from \(\chi\bmod 2\) fixes \(t\) and negates \(s\); its trace-preserving twist gives the same dimension for the \(-1\) eigenspace.
Their algebraic direct sum is \(\ker(U^2-I)\), by the polynomial idempotents \((I\pm U)/2\) on that kernel.
Consequently this square has fixed-space dimension one.

Write \(A_0=\langle t_0,t_1\rangle\) and \(W=M(t_1)M(t_0)\).
The identity \(U^2=s^{-2}W\) holds in the actual multiplication order, with \(W\) affiliated over \(A_0\).
Suppose \(s^2\notin A_0\).
For the [two-position transfer window](Yin_C_A0183_v1.md#transfer-matrices-and-equality) with subgroup \(A_0\), shift \(s^2\) and matrix \(W\), the cosets \(A_0,A_0s^2\) are distinct.
Its bound for the fixed space of \(s^{-2}W\) is \(2/2=1\), just attained.
The equality theorem gives \((U^2)^2=I\) and gives dimension one to \(\ker(U^2+I)\).
This latter space is the algebraic direct sum of the \(+i\) and \(-i\) eigenspaces of \(U\), by interpolation on the two distinct roots.
The same sign twist interchanges them, so they each have dimension \(1/2\).

For the [short lift \((3,4)\)](Yin_C_A0201_v1.md#statement), the deformed rectangle at every nonzero parameter outside \(\mu_{26}\) has nullity zero.
At the parameter \(z=i\), the multiplier on \(t\) is \(i^4=1\), and the multiplier on \(s\) is \(i^3=-i\).
Thus its actual nullity is \(\dim_H\ker(U+iI)\), which would be \(1/2\), whereas \(i^{26}=-1\).
This contradiction proves \(s^2\in A_0\).

## The subgroup and the periodic filling

Conjugation by \(s\) sends \(t_0\) to \(t_1\) and \(t_1\) to \(s^2t_0s^{-2}\), both in \(A_0\).
The inverse conjugation sends \(t_0\) to \(s^{-2}t_1s^2\) and \(t_1\) to \(t_0\).
Thus \(A_0\) is normal, contains \(t\) and \(s^2\), and lies in \(\ker(\chi\bmod2)\).
The quotient is generated by \(sA_0\) with order dividing two and maps onto \(C_2\), proving equality.
Normality gives the claim for every translated pair.

In any image with second mark \(y\) of order at most two, the image of \(A_0\) is generated by \(y\) and its conjugate.
Two involutions generate a group with a cyclic subgroup of index at most two, generated by their product; the trivial case also gives a solvable group.
Since the quotient of the whole image by this normal subgroup is cyclic, the image is solvable.
By the [perfect commutator theorem](Yin_C_A0237_v1.md#statement) it factors through \(C_{26}\).
The quotient by the normal closure of \(t^2\) is consequently its own abelianization, the quotient of \(\mathbb Z/26\) by the subgroup generated by \(20\), which is \(C_2\).
