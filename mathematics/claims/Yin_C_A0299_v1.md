# Killing the two outer squares leaves at most a group of order two

## Statement

Assume the [normalized order-24 half-kernel hypotheses](Yin_C_A0288_v1.md#statement).
Use \(u_j=t_j^m\), \(L_m=\langle u_{-1},u_0,u_1,u_2\rangle\), \(g=s^4\), and the number \(r_m\) of right matches in the [ordinary power-subgroup identity](Yin_C_A0297_v1.md#the-coefficient-at-the-identity-coset-belongs-to-the-subgroup).
For any homomorphism \(\pi:L_m\to J\) killing \(u_{-1},u_2\), both \(\pi(u_0)\) and \(\pi(u_1)\) have finite order.
More precisely their two cyclic subgroups are contained in the same set
\[
                 S=\{\pi(t_2^c g t_{-1}^d):\
                    0\le c,d<m,\ t_2^c g t_{-1}^d\in L_m\},
                         \qquad |S|\le r_m.
\]
The target may be infinite and nonabelian.
In particular
\[
       [L_2:\langle\!\langle t_{-1}^2,t_2^2\rangle\!\rangle_{L_2}]
                     \ \le\ 2 .
\]
At \(m=3\) the corresponding quotient is cyclic of order at most three or generated by two involutions, hence a quotient of the infinite dihedral group.
These restrictions test normal closure inside a subgroup generated by four powers, even when that subgroup has infinite index in \(H\).

## Keeping the coefficients after clearing the two denominators

Put \(a=\pi(u_1),\ b=\pi(u_0)\), and take \(J=\pi(L_m)\).
Embed \(L_m\) diagonally by \(\ell\mapsto(\pi(\ell),\ell)\) into \(J\times F\).
Here \(F\) is the actual index-four subgroup.
A subgroup inclusion preserves affiliated identities by regular coset decomposition.
After this embedding the right side of the [identity-coset equation](Yin_C_A0297_v1.md#the-coefficient-at-the-identity-coset-belongs-to-the-subgroup) is a finite sum \(W=\sum_{k\in S} k W_k\) with coefficients in \(M_2(U(F))\), since its outer group powers \(u_2,u_{-1}\) were killed in \(J\).
Distinct first-coordinate labels are independent over \(U(F)\), by clearing a common bounded denominator in the second factor and separating the group Fourier supports.

Write \(F_h\) for the rational matrices defined by \(E_h(z)=z^h F_h(z^m)\).
Choose nonzero scalar polynomials \(P,Q\) such that \(P F_h\) and \(F_h Q\) are polynomial for every index.
Multiply the embedded equation for \(W\) on the left by \(P(a u_1)\) and on the right by \(Q(b u_0)\), where the suppressed coordinates in the two factors commute.
In the resulting left expression the term for \((h,j)\) has outer polynomial factors \((P F_h)(a u_1),(F_j Q)(b u_0)\), with intervening group element \((\pi(\ell_{hj}),\ell_{hj})\), \(\ell_{hj}=t_1^h t_0^j\in L_m\).
Every coefficient in the first group coordinate thus belongs entrywise to
\[
     \mathcal T=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
                  \{f(t_1)h(t_0): f,h\in\mathbb C(z)\}\subset U(F).
\]
This space is closed under multiplication on the left by \(\mathbb C(t_1)\) and on the right by \(\mathbb C(t_0)\).

Each \(W_k\) itself belongs entrywise to \(\mathcal T\).
To see this, if a finite sum \(D\) on \(J\) with second coefficients in \(U(F)\) has \(P(a u_1)D\) in \(\mathcal T[J]\), consider the coefficients modulo \(\mathcal T\).
For infinite-order \(a\), each orbit under left translation by \(a\) is a copy of the integer line.
On any such line with a nonzero surviving coefficient the first occupied position and the lowest nonzero term of \(P\) give a nonzero coefficient in the product, since left multiplication by a power of \(u_1\) is invertible on the quotient space.
For finite-order \(a\), invert \(P(a u_1)\) by the finite cyclic Fourier expansion, with coefficients \(P(\omega u_1)^{-1}\in\mathbb C(t_1)\) for the allowed roots of unity \(\omega\).
These preserve \(\mathcal T\) on the left.
Thus \(D\) has all coefficients in \(\mathcal T\).
Apply this to \(D=WQ(bu_0)\), then use the right-hand version on the right translation orbits of \(b\) to remove \(Q\).
The notation \(\mathcal T[J]\) here means finite coefficient lists and uses no internal ring multiplication of \(\mathcal T\).

## Separating two central variables while retaining the group labels

The [two-variable tensor map](Yin_C_A0291_v1.md#local-fourier-blocks-and-separation-of-repeated-endpoints) is injective for \(t_1,t_0\), because their cyclic subgroups intersect trivially.
Pull the coefficients of \(W\) back to matrices of rational functions in independent scalar variables \(z,z'\) by this linear isomorphism onto \(\mathcal T\).
Left multiplication by polynomials in \(u_1\) and right multiplication by polynomials in \(u_0\) become multiplication by the corresponding scalar polynomials in \(X=z^m,\ Y={z'}^m\).
Thus the cleared identity becomes an identity over the ordinary group algebra of \(J\) with two central rational variables, preserving the order of the first-coordinate group elements.

Average separately over the \(m\)-th root rotations of \(z,z'\).
The factors \(P(aX)\) and \(Q(bY)\) are invariant.
Every polynomial term on the other side contains \(z^h{z'}^j\) and functions of \(X,Y\), so exactly \((h,j)=(0,0)\) remains, with \(\ell_{00}=1\).
The resulting equation is
\[
          P(aX)\,W_{00}(X,Y)\,Q(bY)
                       =(P F_0)(aX)\,(F_0 Q)(bY),
\]
where \(W_{00}\) has support contained in \(S\) and coefficients in \(M_2(\mathbb C(X,Y))\).
The scalar polynomials \(P(aX),Q(bY)\) are invertible in the iterated formal Laurent series ring with coefficient ring \(\mathbb C J\), since their leading scalar-times-group coefficients are units.
Consequently \(W_{00}=F_0(aX)F_0(bY)\) there, with all coefficient supports still contained in \(S\).
One may expand at infinity in both variables instead by using their reciprocals.
This passage uses a polynomial identity in central variables and does not specialize an inverse in an abstract group quotient.

## Reading positive powers in the zero Fourier block

Take \(D\) invertible in the denominator pencil \(Cz+D\), using the inverse parameter if necessary.
Parameter inversion replaces the two group labels by their inverses in the expansions below and leaves their cyclic subgroups unchanged.
Write \(T=D^{-1}C,\ V=A-BT\).
For \(F_0(X)=\sum_{n\ge0}L_n X^n\) at zero the [zero-block formula](Yin_C_A0297_v1.md#bidegree-zero-retains-the-moving-poles) gives \(L_0=BD^{-1}\) and
\[
                         L_n=(-1)^{mn-1}VT^{mn-1}D^{-1}
                                      \qquad(n\ge1).
\]
The sign is scalar and \(V\) is invertible.
The \(X^nY^h\) coefficient of the product is \(L_n L_h\,a^n b^h\).
A nonzero numerical matrix coefficient therefore forces the single corresponding group element to belong to \(S\).

If \(T\) is invertible all products with \(n,h\ge1\) occur.
If \(T\) has rank one, its trace is nonzero by the nonmonomial denominator determinant.
Since \(m\ge2\), every \(L_n\) with \(n\ge1\) is a nonzero multiple of \(R=VTD^{-1}\).
For \(R^2\ne0\), again all positive mixed powers occur.
In either situation finiteness of \(S\) first forces \(a,b\) to have finite orders by holding one exponent fixed and varying the other.
Taking a positive multiple of that order for one exponent then puts every power of the other element in \(S\).

If \(R^2=0\), invertibility of \(F_0(X)=L_0+f(X)R\) forces \(L_0R\ne0\) and \(RL_0\ne0\), since otherwise this matrix annihilates the nonzero \(R\) on the right or left.
The coefficients with \(n=0,h\ge1\) and with \(h=0,n\ge1\) put all positive powers of both elements in \(S\) directly.
They have finite orders and their full cyclic subgroups lie in \(S\).

Finally take the universal quotient of \(L_m\) killing its two outer powers.
It is generated by \(a,b\).
The [degree count](Yin_C_A0297_v1.md#statement) gives \(r_2\le2\) and \(r_3\le3\).
Two cyclic subgroups contained in a single set of size two generate a cyclic group of order at most two.
With size at most three, a cyclic subgroup of order three already uses the entire set, and in its absence each generator has order at most two.
This proves both stated consequences.
