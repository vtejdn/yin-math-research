# The outer squares normally generate the subgroup of four squares

## Statement

Under the [order-24 half-kernel hypotheses](Yin_C_A0288_v1.md#statement) put \(u_j=t_j^m,\ -1\le j\le2\), \(L_m=\langle u_{-1},u_0,u_1,u_2\rangle\) and \(Q_m=L_m/\langle\!\langle u_{-1},u_2\rangle\!\rangle_{L_m}\).
Then
\[
                               Q_2=1,\qquad |Q_3|\le2 .
\]
In particular the two outer squares normally generate \(L_2\) inside itself.
For every \(m\ge2\), if \(L_m\) has finite index in \(F=\langle t_0,t_1\rangle\), then \(Q_m=1\) and \([F:L_m]=\gcd(m,6)\).
If \(L_3\) has infinite index and \(Q_3\ne1\), exactly two of the three possible degree-compatible right pairs in the [local orbit test](Yin_C_A0291_v1.md#four-powers-and-the-identity-coset) occur.

## Power fillings inside a finite-index subgroup

The [periodic-image theorem](Yin_C_A0288_v1.md#normal-closures-and-finite-index-subgroups) gives \(H'=\langle\!\langle t^{6k}\rangle\!\rangle_H\) for all \(k\ge1\) and puts \(H'\) in every finite-index subgroup of \(H\).
The following normal-generation observation also applies directly to \(H'\).
For every \(k\ge1\) the elements \(t_j^{6k}\), \(-1\le j\le2\), normally generate \(H'\) as a group in its own right.
Indeed any \(h\in H\) can be written \(h=n s^j t^l\) with \(n\in H'\) and \(j\) in the indicated four-element set.
In \(H_{\rm ab}=C_{24}\) the classes of \(s^j\) represent the four cosets of \(\langle\bar t\rangle\).
Conjugating \(t^{6k}\) by \(h\) thus gives exactly an \(H'\)-conjugate of one of the four displayed elements.
In particular \(H'\) has no nontrivial quotient that is a torsion group, regardless of whether that quotient has bounded exponent: use a common multiple of the orders of the four images of \(t_j^6\).

If \(L_m\) has finite index in \(F\), it also has finite index in \(H\).
It contains \(H'\), and \(F/H'=C_6\).
All four \(u_j\) have the same class \(m\bmod6\) there, so \([F:L_m]=\gcd(m,6)\).
The [two cyclic subgroups in a finite set](Yin_C_A0299_v1.md#statement) imply that all four \(u_j\) have finite order in \(Q_m\), including the two killed generators.
Choose \(k\) divisible by \(m\) and by \(m\) times each of those finite orders.
Then every \(t_j^{6k}\) belongs to the kernel of \(L_m\to Q_m\).
Since the kernel is normal in \(L_m\) and \(H'\subseteq L_m\), the preceding normal-generation observation puts \(H'\) in the kernel.
The remaining cyclic group \(L_m/H'\) is generated by the class of \(u_{-1}\), already killed.
This proves the finite-index assertion.

## A complete intersection of two marked orbits

Let \(m\) divide six, \(m\ge2\), and act on \(F/L_m\) with identity coset \(v\).
The orbit of \(v\) under \(t_2\) has size exactly \(m\): its length divides \(m\), and the homomorphism \(F\to C_6\to C_m\) vanishes on \(L_m\) and maps \(t_2\) to a generator.
For the same reason the \(t_{-1}\)-orbit of \(v\) has size \(m\), hence the \(t_3\)-orbit of \(gv\) also has size \(m\), where \(g=s^4\).
The number \(r_m\) in the [identity-coset test](Yin_C_A0297_v1.md#statement) is exactly the cardinality of the intersection of these two orbits.
Indeed the matching equation is \(t_2^{-c}v=t_3^d gv\), with each point on either side reached by a unique exponent modulo \(m\).

If \(r_m=m\), the two orbits coincide and are invariant under both \(t_2,t_3\).
They contain \(v\) and these two labels generate \(F\) by [adjacent generation](Yin_C_A0272_v1.md#the-order-24-application).
Thus the whole transitive action has size \(m\).
In the infinite-index case this proves \(1\le r_m<m\).
For \(m=2\) this gives \(r_2=1\); both remaining cyclic subgroups of \(Q_2\) lie in a common singleton, so both are trivial.
For \(m=3\) one has \(r_3\le2\), so the common set contains at most two elements and \(Q_3\) is cyclic of order at most two.
If that order is two, \(r_3=2\).
Combining with the finite-index case proves the claims.
