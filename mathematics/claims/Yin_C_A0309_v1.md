# Small triangle relations for the periodic marks at cyclic orders twenty-five and twenty-six

## Statement

Under the [critical order-25 or order-26 half-rank hypotheses](Yin_C_A0298_v1.md#statement), use the normalized ordered generators and put \(t_i=s^its^{-i}\).
Write \(x,y_i\) for the images of \(s,t_i\) in any image \(J\), and \(y=y_0\).
Use \(1/\infty=0\) for an element of infinite order.

* At order 25, if \(y^2=1\) and \(J\ne1\), then for \(m=|y_0y_1|\), \(k=|y_0y_2|\) one has \(2/m+1/k<1\).
  In particular the quotient by the normal closure of \(t^2,(t_0t_1)^3,(t_0t_2)^e\) is trivial for \(e=2\) and for \(e=3\).
* At order 26, if \(m=|y|\) is finite and \(J\) is noncyclic, then for every pair of integers \(i,j\) relatively prime to \(m\),
  \[
                         \frac2m+\frac1{|y_1^i y_0^j|}<1 .
  \]
  In particular for \(e\in\{2,3\}\) and \(\epsilon,\eta\in\{1,-1\}\) there is the canonical filling
  \[
      H/\langle\!\langle t^3,(t_1^\epsilon t_0^\eta)^e\rangle\!\rangle_H
                                  \simeq C_2 .
  \]
  This includes the commutator \(t_1t_0^{-1}=[s,t]\).

There is a strict bound in arbitrary matrix size under the generation test below.
The triangle conditions alone leave open larger product orders in images of an order-three mark.

## When two equal orders give a solvable group

Suppose \(a,b\) have the same order \(m\) and \(k=|ab|\).
If \(2/m+1/k\ge1\), the group \(P=\langle a,b\rangle\) is solvable.
For \(m=1\) or \(k=1\) it is cyclic, and for \(m=2\) the product generates a cyclic subgroup of index at most two.
The remaining possibilities are \((m,k)=(3,2),(3,3),(4,2)\).

For \((3,2)\) write \(c=ab\), so \(b=a^{-1}c\).
The three conjugates \(c_i=a^ica^{-i}\), \(i\bmod3\), are involutions and \(b^3=1\) gives \(c_2c_1c_0=1\).
Thus \(c_2c_1=c_0\) is an involution, so \(c_2,c_1\) commute and generate all three.
Their abelian subgroup is normal with cyclic quotient.
For \((3,3)\) put \(b=ca\) and define \(c_i\) in the same way.
The relations \(b^3=(ab)^3=1\) now give \(c_0c_1c_2=c_1c_0c_2=1\), so the \(c_i\) again generate an abelian normal subgroup with cyclic quotient.
For \((4,2)\) put \(b=ca\), using indices modulo four.
Then \(b^4=1\) gives \(c_0c_1c_2c_3=1\) and \((ab)^2=1\) gives \(c_1c_3=1\).
Its conjugate gives \(c_2c_0=1\); substitution shows that \(c_0,c_1\) commute and generate all \(c_i\).
This proves solvability in all cases, with no finiteness assumption on \(P\).

## The two marked images

At order 25 the [three adjacent conjugates](Yin_C_A0298_v1.md#statement) generate \(H\), so \(J=\langle y_0,y_1,y_2\rangle\).
Suppose \(y\) has order two.
Put \(a=y_0y_1,\ b=y_1y_2\).
They are conjugate by \(x\), and \(ab=y_0y_2\).
The subgroup \(P=\langle a,b\rangle\) is normal with index at most two, since conjugation by \(y_1\) inverts both generators and \(P,y_1\) generate \(J\).
If \(2/m+1/k\ge1\), then \(P\), and thus \(J\), is solvable.
Every [solvable image at order 25](Yin_C_A0229_v1.md#consequences-for-the-original-half-increment) factors through \(C_{25}\).
There the degree \(7\) of \(t\) generates the whole cyclic group; imposing order at most two makes its image trivial.
If \(y=1\) the three-generator assertion already gives \(J=1\).
This proves the first conclusion, including the filling where the two product orders divide \(3,e\).

At order 26 the image of the [normal subgroup of index two](Yin_C_A0296_v1.md#statement) is \(P=\langle y_1,y_0\rangle=\langle y_1^i,y_0^j\rangle\).
Both new generators have order \(m\).
If the weak reverse inequality held, \(P\) would be solvable by the two-generator calculation.
Its cyclic extension \(J\) would factor through \(C_{26}\) by [the perfect commutator theorem](Yin_C_A0237_v1.md#statement).
For the displayed filling the two orders divide \(3,e\); when the order of \(y\) is three the signs are units modulo three.
Thus this quotient is its own abelianization.
The added relators have degrees \(30\) and \(10(\epsilon+\eta)e\) in \(C_{26}\); their quotient is exactly \(C_2\) since \(\gcd(26,30)=2\) and both degrees are even.

## A strict window bound without scalar injectivity

Suppose only that \(H\) has the marked cyclic quotient above, that \(t\) has infinite order, and that all four complex \(h\)-square corners of the rectangle and their assembly are invertible.
Suppose some image \(J\) is nonsolvable.
At order 25 assume its \(y\) is an involution and the above product orders satisfy \(2/m+1/k\ge1\); at order 26 assume \(|y|=m<\infty\) and some pair of unit exponents gives this inequality.
Then the original regular nullity \(\delta\) is strictly less than \(h/4\).

At order 25 the subgroup generated by \(y_0,y_1,y_2\) would be solvable by the same computation with its even products.
If it contained one of \(x,x^2,x^3\), it would be normal with cyclic quotient by the [consecutive-label argument](Yin_C_A0298_v1.md#a-strict-window-bound-and-generation-at-order-twenty-five), contradicting nonsolvability.
Thus the four-position transfer window applies already in the original group and gives \(\delta\le h/4\).
Equality gives nullity \(h/4\) at each fourth root for \(s^{-1}M(t)\), where \(M(z)=(Az+B)(Cz+D)^{-1}\).
At order 26 the adjacent subgroup of \(J\) is solvable and cannot contain \(x^2\), by the two-position normality argument.
The [doubled transfer window](Yin_C_A0298_v1.md#the-two-position-window-at-order-twenty-six) gives \(2\delta\le h/2\), with equality forcing a \(-i\) eigenspace of dimension \(h/4\) for \(s^{-1}M(t)\) by the sign character.
Both window calculations use the [dimension-\(h\) equality theorem](Yin_C_A0183_v1.md#transfer-matrices-and-equality).

Now deform by \((3,-4)\) for \(n=25\) and \((3,4)\) for \(n=26\).
At equality the two disjoint phase sets \(\mu_n,i\mu_n\) would all have nullity \(h/4\), by the cyclic character twists.
After a scalar monomial shift the polynomial has degree seven and an injective endpoint, by the corner hypotheses.
The [degree budget](Yin_C_A0204_v1.md#the-bound-using-individual-column-widths) is \(7h\), whereas the required sum is \(nh/2>7h\).
This proves strictness without imposing scalar injectivity.
