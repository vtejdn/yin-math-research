# The metabelian quotient at the noncyclic order-twenty marking

## Statement

Suppose \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) satisfies the [noncyclic order-twenty half-kernel hypotheses](Yin_C_A0313_v1.md#statement).
Identify \(Q=H_{\rm ab}=C_5\times C_2^2\) so that the marked images are \(\sigma=(1,e_1)\) and \(\tau=(2,e_2)\).
Write \(M=H'/H''\) additively and let \(W\) act on \(M\) by conjugation by any lift of \(w=(1,0)\in Q\).
Then \(M\) is finite of order prime to five and
\[
                   W-I\in{\rm Aut}(M),\qquad I+W+W^2+W^3+W^4=0 .
\]
The order-five action is free on \(M\setminus\{0\}\), so \(|M|\equiv1\bmod5\).
The full metabelian quotient has the form \(H/H''=M\rtimes Q\).
One can choose its complement so that the actual marked elements in split coordinates are \((0,\sigma),(v,\tau)\), where \(v\) generates \(M\) as a \(\mathbb Z[Q]\)-module.
Thus \(M\) is a finite cyclic module over \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_5][C_2^2]\).
These conditions allow a nonzero finite \(M\).

## The sign action on a simple quotient

The subgroup \(H'\) has index twenty and hence is finitely generated, so \(M\) is finitely generated as an abelian group.
Conjugation on \(M\) factors through \(Q\).
Suppose \(M/(W-I)M\ne0\).
Choose a prime \(p\) for which reduction modulo \(p\) of this nonzero finitely generated group is nonzero, and take a simple quotient as an \(\mathbb F_p[C_2^2]\)-module.
In odd characteristic the commuting involutions simultaneously split into scalar signs, so this quotient is one-dimensional.
In characteristic two their differences from the identity are commuting square-zero operators.
The kernel of each is nonzero and stable under both, so simplicity makes both differences zero and again gives dimension one.
There is consequently a surjective equivariant homomorphism \(\phi:H'\to\mathbb F_p\) on which \(Q\) acts by a sign character.
A trivial sign, including characteristic two, would kill \([H,H']\) under \(\phi\), contrary to the [class-two obstruction](Yin_C_A0313_v1.md#nilpotent-and-dihedral-images).

For a nontrivial sign, \(p\) is odd.
In \(G=H/\ker\phi\) the derived subgroup is \(V=\mathbb F_p\), and the index-two subgroup \(E\) acting trivially on \(V\) centralizes it.
Take \(g\in G\setminus E\).
For \(h,k\in E\) the commutator \([h,k]\) belongs to \(V\).
Conjugation by \(g\) inverts it, but conjugating each of \(h,k\) merely multiplies that element by an element of \(V\), central in \(E\), and hence preserves their commutator.
Since \(p\) is odd, \(E\) is abelian.
Now \(d(h)=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\) defines a homomorphism \(E\to V\).
It restricts on \(V\) to multiplication by \(-2\), vanishes on \(g^2\), and satisfies \(d(ghg^{-1})=-d(h)\).
Sending \(h\in E\) to the rotation \(d(h)\) and \(g\) to a reflection therefore defines a surjection \(G\to V\rtimes C_2\): the conjugation and square relations hold, and \(E\) has index two.
This contradicts the [dihedral obstruction](Yin_C_A0313_v1.md#nilpotent-and-dihedral-images).

Thus \(W-I\) is a surjective endomorphism of a finitely generated abelian group, hence an automorphism.
Since \(W^5=I\), this gives \(\Phi_5(W)=0\).
On \(M/5M\) the operator \(W-I\) is both invertible and nilpotent, since its fifth power is zero.
Hence \(M/5M=0\), proving finiteness and coprimality.
Any element with orbit of length less than five is fixed by \(W\) and hence zero.

## Splitting with a prescribed lift of the first mark

Put \(\Gamma=H/H''\) and choose any lift \(g_5\in\Gamma\) of \(w\).
The element \(g_5^5\) belongs to \(M\) and is fixed by \(W\), so it is trivial.
The centralizer \(C_\Gamma(g_5)\) maps isomorphically onto \(Q\).
Its intersection with \(M\) is \(\ker(W-I)=0\), and for any \(b\in\Gamma\) one has \(b g_5 b^{-1}=m g_5\) for some \(m\in M\).
Then \(n b\) with \(n\in M\) centralizes \(g_5\) precisely when \((W-I)n=m\), which has a unique solution.
This gives the splitting.

Write \(S\) for the action of \(\sigma\) on \(M\).
Since \(S^2=W^2\), its square has trivial fixed subgroup: \(W^2\) generates the same action as \(W\).
Thus \(S-I\) is injective and hence invertible on the finite group \(M\).
In the splitting just constructed, the actual marked lift of \(s\) has the form \((m,\sigma)\).
Conjugating the complement by a suitable element \(n\in M\) places that lift in the new complement, since \(n(0,\sigma)n^{-1}=((I-S)n,\sigma)\).
The other mark then has the form \((v,\tau)\).
For the \(\mathbb Z[Q]\)-module \(M_0\) generated by \(v\), the subgroup \(M_0\rtimes Q\) contains the two actual marks, so equals \(\Gamma\).
This proves cyclic generation and, using \(\Phi_5(W)=0\), the assertion on the coefficient ring.
