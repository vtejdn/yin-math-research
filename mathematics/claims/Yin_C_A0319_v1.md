# Ten-channel bounds for the exceptional nontrivial signs at order twenty

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) map onto \(C_5\times C_2^2\) with marked images \((1,e_1),(2,e_2)\) for a binary basis.
Let \(p=3,11,41,61\) or \(101\), let \(\theta\) be a primitive fifth root in the finite field \(V=\mathbb F_p(\theta)\), and choose signs \((\epsilon_s,\epsilon_t)\ne(+,+)\).
Suppose \(H\) has an affine image on \(V\) generated by the marked transformations
\[
                s:u\mapsto\epsilon_s\theta u,\qquad
                t:u\mapsto v+\epsilon_t\theta^2 u,\qquad v\ne0 .
\]
For complex \(h\)-square matrices \(A,B,C,D\), each invertible and with \({\cal P}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B&A\\D&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) invertible, the regular nullity of \(K=B+At-s(D+Ct)\) satisfies
\[
                 \dim_H\ker K\le
                 \begin{cases}h/5,&\epsilon_s\ne\epsilon_t,\\
                         21h/110,&\epsilon_s=\epsilon_t=-1.
                 \end{cases}
\]
The trace is unnormalized on the \(h\) matrix coordinates.
The estimates use the actual affine and abelian quotients and hold without scalar group-ring injectivity.

In particular, in the [three remaining components](Yin_C_A0317_v1.md#excluding-the-binary-part-and-splitting-the-odd-components) at a noncyclic order-twenty half kernel, none of the indicated primes divides the order of any component.
Indeed any such prime gives a simple quotient in characteristic \(p\) of the finite cyclic \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]\)-module of that sign.
It has the form \(V\), a primitive fifth root acts as the five-primary generator, and the [marked splitting](Yin_C_A0313_v2.md#splitting-with-a-prescribed-lift-of-the-first-mark) gives exactly the affine images above, with nonzero image of the module generator.
The four corners and the assembly are then invertible. [Corner test](Yin_C_A0311_v1.md#the-two-ends-in-the-equality-case) [Assembly](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement)

## Normalizing the two steps

Changing the sign of either marked exponent means replacing a group label by its inverse, multiplying the rectangle on the left by \(s^{-1}\) or on the right by \(t^{-1}\), respectively, and rearranging the constant matrices.
Taking adjoints exchanges the roles of the two inverse labels.
Together these [rectangle symmetries](Yin_C_A0196_v1.md#all-surjections-onto-the-quotient) allow the following choices of ordered generators \((x,y)\) with the same nullity and corner hypotheses.

| Original signs | \((x,y)\) | linear action \(R\) of \(x\) | linear action of \(y\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \((-,+)\) | \((s,t)\) | \(-\theta\) | \(R^2\) |
| \((+,-)\) | \((t,s^{-1})\) | \(-\theta^2\) | \(R^2\) |
| \((-,-)\) | \((s,t^{-1})\) | \(-\theta\) | \(R^3\) |

In each row \(R\) has order ten.
Write \(k=2\) or \(3\) for the indicated second step.
Conjugate the affine image by a translation of \(V\) to move the unique fixed point of \(x\) to zero.
The translation part of \(y\) is nonzero in these coordinates: the original \(s\) has only zero as a fixed point and the original \(t\) moves zero, so the generated group has no common fixed point.
The additive span of the \(R\)-orbit of this nonzero field element is \(V\).
The marked character lattice is \(b\equiv ka\bmod5\) at exponent ten, since the order-five degree ratio in each displayed pair is \(k\bmod5\), and the binary images are always a basis.

On ten channels put \(Pe_i=e_{i+1}\) with indices in \(\mathbb Z/10\).
For any \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear functional \(\lambda:V\to\mathbb F_p\), set
\[
                  r=-R^{-1},\qquad e_i=(-1)^i\lambda(r^i).
\]
These exponents give genuine marked representations \(x\mapsto P,\ y\mapsto P^k\operatorname{diag}(w^{e_i})\) at every \(w^p=1\).
Indeed send translations by \(u\in V\) to diagonal entries \(w^{\lambda_0(R^{-i}u)}\) and multiplication by \(R\) to \(P\).
If the second affine map is \(u\mapsto v'+R^ku\), its phase on the column at \(i\) is \(w^{\lambda_0(R^{-i-k}v')}\).
Multiplication by \(R^{-k}v'\ne0\) permutes the choices of functional, giving the stated formula.
The five values of \(\lambda(r^i)\) sum to zero and \(e_{i+5}=-e_i\).
When \(p=3\) the possible lists of five values fill that sum-zero space: \(r\) has degree four, and evaluation on its powers injects the four-dimensional dual into a four-dimensional space.
For each indicated split prime they are \(c r^i\bmod p\), and all four choices of the primitive marked root \(r\) occur in the verification.

## Costs of the thirty-four marked representations

Use \(L=10p\) phases \(z^L=1\).
For integers \(a,b\) with \(b\equiv ka\bmod5\), tensor the preceding representation at \(w=z^{10}\) with the marked scalar character of values \(z^{pa},z^{pb}\).
Give the first edges \(i\to i+1\) the common cost \(pa\).
Give \(i\to i+k\) the centered representative \(b_i\) of \(pb+10 e_i\bmod L\) in \([-L/2,L/2)\), allowing a further addition of \(L\) at \(i=0\) when specified.
The original group translations remain attached to all edges.
Changing a lift at one site preserves all \(L\) representations by construction.
[Regular absorption](Yin_C_A0200_v1.md#the-degree-one-pencil-from-two-reflections) gives ten times the original regular nullity at each of these phases.

For a perfect matching, write \(m_i,n_i\in\{0,1\}\) for use of the first and second edges at \(i\).
Their necessary and sufficient conservation law is
\[
                         m_i-m_{i-1}=n_{i-k}-n_i .
\]
In fact inputs \(i\) and \(i-k\) supply \(1-n_i+n_{i-k}\) paths arriving at intermediate site \(i\), and \(1-m_{i-1}+m_i\) outputs are to be filled from this site.
Equal counts allow arbitrary local bijections, giving genuine matchings.
All matchings with the same two edge sets have cost \(pa\sum m_i+\sum b_i n_i\).
To recover the entire cost range one may test each of the \(2^{10}\) second-edge sets and both values of \(m_9\), using the recurrence and retaining exactly the binary periodic solutions.
There are 248 different pairs of edge sets for \(k=2\) and 216 for \(k=3\).

The [native integer verifier](../computations/programs/Yin_J_A0008/verify_ten_sign_channels.py) records \(a,b\), the orbit multiplier or ternary list, the extra period at index zero, and both extremal costs for each case.
For \(k=2,p=3\), for example, use \((a,b)=(0,-5)\), the five values \((1,1,1,2,1)\) and add \(L\) at zero.
The extremal costs are \(-15,45\).
For \(k=3,p=3\) use \((-1,2)\), the five values \((0,0,0,1,2)\) and no added period, giving \(-40,10\).
For each \(k\) the four split roots at each of four primes give another sixteen cases.
The following are the largest differences \(D\) of the two extremal costs for each characteristic.

| \(k\) | 3 | 11 | 41 | 61 | 101 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 60 | 220 | 680 | 910 | 1530 |
| 3 | 50 | 210 | 490 | 810 | 1090 |

Every minimizer and maximizer is unique as a pair of used edge sets.
The endpoint with these sets has full group rank by the [local matching blocks](Yin_C_A0293_v1.md#invertibility-when-the-extremum-has-unique-edge-sets), which use invertibility of all four corners and the assembly.
Thus the [matching spread estimate](Yin_C_A0219_v1.md#statement) gives \(10L\dim_H\ker K\le hD\).
The largest ratios \(D/(100p)\) are \(1/5\) and \(21/110\), respectively, as asserted.
