# A bounded column from two cyclic polynomial strips in the group ring

## Statement

Let \(s,t\) belong to a discrete group \(H\), and let \(f,g\in\mathbb C[z,z^{-1}]^2\) be arbitrary Laurent polynomial columns.
Form
\[
            L=f(s)+g(s)t,\qquad F(z)=[f(z),g(z)],\qquad d(z)=\det F(z).
\]
Suppose \(d\) is not identically zero.
If \(s,t^{-1}st\) freely generate a free group of rank two, then \(L\) is bounded below in the regular representation.
The bound below depends only on the two polynomial columns, uniformly over such marked groups and their other relations.

More generally, let \(n\ge1,\ |\zeta|=1\), and suppose the pair \(s^n,t^{-1}s^nt\) has a regular displacement bound \(\gamma>0\):
\[
             \max_{u=s^n,t^{-1}s^nt}\|(u-I)\xi\|
                       \ \ge\ \gamma\|\xi\|\qquad(\xi\in\ell^2(H)).
\]
For \(0<\epsilon<\min(\gamma,2)\), if every unit-circle zero \(z\) of \(d\) satisfies \(|z^n-\zeta|<\epsilon\), then
\[
       \|L\xi\|\ge
          \frac{(\gamma-\epsilon)m_\epsilon}{2A}\|\xi\|,\qquad
       m_\epsilon=\min_{\substack{|z|=1\\ |z^n-\zeta|\ge\epsilon}} |d(z)|>0,
       \qquad A=\max_{|z|=1}\|\operatorname{adj} F(z)\|>0 .
\]
In particular, if the \(n\)-th powers of all unit-circle roots equal the same phase, nonamenability of \(\langle s^n,t^{-1}s^nt\rangle\) suffices for strict positivity.
When there is no unit-circle root, the bounded inverse of \(F(s)\) gives a lower bound directly.

## Spectral localization on two translates

For the left regular translations one has \(L\xi=F(s)(\xi,t\xi)^{\mathsf T}\).
Multiplication by the adjugate bounds both \(\|d(s)\xi\|\) and \(\|d(s)t\xi\|\) by \(A\|L\xi\|\).
On the circle set \(|z^n-\zeta|<\epsilon\) the operator \(s^n-\zeta I\) has norm at most \(\epsilon\) on its spectral subspace.
On the complementary subspace \(d(s)\) is bounded below by \(m_\epsilon\).
Consequently for \(v=\xi,t\xi\) one has
\[
            \|(s^n-\zeta I)v\|
                 \le \epsilon\|\xi\|+2m_\epsilon^{-1}A\|L\xi\|.
\]
After multiplication of the second expression by \(t^{-1}\) this bounds the displacement of \(\xi\) at the same phase for both powered group elements.
The pointwise reverse triangle inequality preserves the lower bound on the maximum of the two displacements and gives
\(\|(u-\zeta I)\xi\|\ge\|(u-I)|\xi|\|\).
Applying the displacement hypothesis to \(|\xi|\) proves the displayed column bound.

For a nonamenable subgroup generated by the stated pair, some positive displacement constant exists.
Otherwise almost invariant unit vectors would give almost invariant probability densities by taking their squared moduli.
Decomposition into the subgroup's orbits on \(H\) and equivariant identification of each orbit with the subgroup transfer the summed densities to its regular action.
A weak-star accumulation point of their means would be invariant, a contradiction.

## A uniform test on a free pair

For a freely generating pair \(u,v\) the displacement bound can be taken as \(\gamma=\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}\).
In the subgroup the adjacency operator \(u+u^{-1}+v+v^{-1}\) on its four-regular tree has norm at most \(2\sqrt3\) by the weighted Schur test with vertex weight \(3^{-|h|/2}\), where \(|h|\) is free word length in this pair.
Away from the root the sum of the four neighboring weights equals \(2\sqrt3\) times the weight, and at the root it is smaller.
The same estimate holds on the ambient group by its coset decomposition.
Hence the sum of the squared displacements under \(u,v\) is at least \((4-2\sqrt3)\|\xi\|^2\), proving the maximum bound.
If \(s,t^{-1}st\) freely generate, the two \(n\)-th powers also freely generate, by free reduction, so this one \(\gamma\) works for all \(n\).

Let \(Z\) be the finite set of unit-circle zeros of \(d\).
There is \(1\le n\le64^{|Z|}\) with \(|z^n-1|<1/8\) for every \(z\in Z\).
Indeed put their arguments divided by \(2\pi\) into a single torus vector.
Two of its first \(64^{|Z|}+1\) multiples lie in a common box of side \(1/64\), giving a nonzero positive difference with each circular displacement at most \(2\pi/64\).
For the empty set take \(n=1\).
The preceding test now works with \(\zeta=1,\epsilon=1/4<\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}\).
Thus a polynomial determinant with any finite set of boundary roots is allowed, including roots with irrational angles.

## A six-site column and a second column of arbitrary support

For example, take arbitrary unit scalars \(\omega,\nu\) and the column
\[
                   L=\binom{(s^2-\omega)(1-t)}
                               {(s^3-\nu)(1+t)} .
\]
Its two coefficient columns over the \(s\)-variable have determinant \(2(z^2-\omega)(z^3-\nu)\).
Under the free-pair hypothesis the six sites \(1,s^2,s^3,t,s^2t,s^3t\) are distinct because a cross equality would give \(t\in\langle s\rangle\), and \(s\) has infinite order.
The first entry fails to be bounded below, since the regular translation \(t\) has unit approximate eigenvectors at one whether its order is finite or infinite.
If \(-1\) lies in the spectrum of \(t\), the second entry also has unit approximate kernel vectors through its right factor.
Otherwise \(1+t\) is boundedly invertible, and \(s^3-\nu\) fails to be bounded below since the infinite-order \(s\) has the whole unit circle as spectrum.
Multiplication on the right by that invertible factor preserves this failure.
The column nevertheless has the strictly positive bound just proved.

For this column, or any column in the theorem, one may adjoin **any** finite group-ring column \(R\) of the same height.
The Gram congruence for \(K=[L,R]\) then gives a positive scalar Schur complement with its sole denominator \(L^*L\) boundedly invertible in \(C_r^*(H)\).
It has the actual regular nullity of \(K\), and the [mutual recovery of kernel projections](Yin_C_A0323_v2.md#recovering-the-two-projections) applies also at nonclosed range.
If \(H\) satisfies the ball rapid decay bound, the [local spectral mass and finite moment tests](Yin_C_A0323_v3.md#statement) apply to this Schur scalar.
