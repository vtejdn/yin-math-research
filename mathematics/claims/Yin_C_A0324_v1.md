# Reversing the square of the other mark excludes fractional nullity at least one half

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be a torsion-free group with regular scalar group-ring injectivity.
If \(ts^2t^{-1}=s^{-2}\), then every complex two-square four-site matrix \(B+At-s(D+Ct)\) of noninteger regular nullity has nullity strictly below \(1/2\).
At each such marking there is a positive gap below \(1/2\) uniform over all coefficients and over a finite marked word neighborhood among torsion-free scalar-injective groups.
The same conclusions hold with the two labels exchanged or inverted.

More generally the strict pointwise bound holds if \(s\) has infinite order, some positive power \(s^m\) is inverted by \(t\), and \(\langle s,tst^{-1}\rangle\) is amenable.
For the reversed square \(m=2\) this amenability follows from the relation itself.

## A two-position endpoint after exchanging the marks

Put \(D_0=\langle s,tst^{-1}\rangle\).
In the general case \(\langle s^m\rangle\) is normal and its sign of conjugation yields a character on \(H\), positive on \(s\) and negative on \(t\).
For \(m=2\), \(s^2\) is central in \(D_0\), since it is also the inverse of \((tst^{-1})^2\).
The quotient by \(\langle s^2\rangle\) is generated by two elements of order at most two, so contains a cyclic subgroup of index at most two.
Its inverse image in \(D_0\) is abelian, being generated by a lift of a cyclic generator and the central kernel.
This proves the claimed amenability.

If \(s=1\) in the square case, \(H\) is cyclic and all ranks are integral.
Otherwise \(s\) has infinite order and the sign applies.
If \(t^2\in D_0\), conjugation by \(t\) and by \(t^{-1}\) preserve \(D_0\), using \(t^2st^{-2}\) and \(t^{-2}(tst^{-1})t^2\) for the two end generators.
Thus \(D_0\) is normal of index at most two, so \(H\) is amenable.
Scalar injectivity and [Ore localization](Yin_C_A0211_v1.md#right-fractions-for-commensurable-amenable-subgroups) then give integer ranks.

Assume \(t^2\notin D_0\) and suppose a rectangle has noninteger nullity \(\delta\ge1/2\).
With its original coefficients \(A,B,C,D\), left and right multiplication of its adjoint by \(t,s\) yield
\[
            tK^*s=(A^*s-C^*)-t(D^*-B^*s).
\]
In a finite algebra adjunction of a square matrix preserves nullity, and the outside translations are units.
Apply the [four-column reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement) on the exchanged labels.
The two pencils are regular, their assembly is invertible, and \(U=t^{-1}M(s)\), with \(M\) their rational transfer, has \(+1\) eigenspace of dimension \(\delta\).
The sign character sends \(U\) to \(-U\), so its \(-1\) eigenspace has the same dimension.
Interpolation gives dimension \(2\delta\) to \(\ker(U^2-I)\).
Now \(U^2=t^{-2}M(tst^{-1})M(s)\) is a transfer over \(D_0\) with shift \(t^2\).
The [two-position window](Yin_C_A0183_v1.md#transfer-matrices-and-equality) has two distinct cosets since \(t^2\notin D_0\).
Its bound is one, which forces \(\delta=1/2\), \(U^4=I\), and dimension one for \(\ker(U^2+I)\).
The latter two eigenspaces of \(U\) at \(i,-i\) have the same dimension by the sign character and sum directly to that kernel; each has dimension \(1/2\).

## The power filling and the neighborhood

The [periodic-image calculation](Yin_C_A0310_v1.md#two-consecutive-columns-in-an-arbitrary-periodic-image) now applies with shift \(t\) and second mark \(s\).
We detail its hypotheses because the four original cosets for these exchanged labels have not been assumed.
The preceding paragraph supplies the quartic identity and all four half-dimensional eigenspaces.
Consequently \(t^4\) lies in the subgroup generated by the first four conjugates of \(s\), by the Fourier support of the quartic product.
Neither pencil determinant can be a Laurent monomial, since that would make \(U\) or \(U^{-1}\) a group-ring matrix and its nonzero fixed-space interpolation idempotent would give a nonzero group-ring column in dimension \(1/2\), impossible by scalar injectivity.
The sign gives \(t\notin\langle s\rangle\), so the [constant point correspondence test](Yin_C_A0186_v1.md#a-constant-point-correspondence-in-a-two-dimensional-transfer) applies.
These are precisely the original transfer and coefficient data used in that periodic-image calculation.
In an image in which \(s\) has finite order, first quotient by the common finite subgroup of the neighboring cyclics.
If the reduced order is one the image is cyclic after this reduction and has order dividing four.
At reduced order at least two, two consecutive cyclic Fourier blocks in the diagonal embedding are units and all blocks are nonzero.
The two complete columns yield a product of two finite cyclic subgroups of index dividing four before reduction by the [counting and lifting argument](Yin_C_A0310_v1.md#counting-and-lifting-the-two-cyclic-subgroups).
Thus every such group image is finite, without any assumption about intersections of the cyclics in the original group.

In particular \(H/\langle s^m\rangle\) is finite, where the denominator is the actual normal cyclic subgroup.
This makes \(H\) virtually cyclic and hence amenable, contradicting the fractional rank by Ore localization.
This proves the pointwise assertion.

For the square case, if \(t^2\notin D_0\), then \(D_0,D_0t,D_0t^2\) are distinct: the sign character separates \(t\) from \(D_0\), and the remaining power is excluded by assumption.
By the [three-position attainment and upper semicontinuity theorem](Yin_C_A0307_v2.md#statement), the largest fractional nullity with zero adjoined on the exchanged marks is strictly below \(1/2\) and has a finite marked neighborhood with a uniform gap.
Exchanging the labels preserves the set of all rectangle nullities by the displayed adjoint formula.
If \(t^2\in D_0\) and \(s\ne1\), take a finite neighborhood preserving the square-reversing relation and an actual word for \(t^2\) in the two generators of \(D_0\).
The preceding virtual amenability argument applies throughout that neighborhood; a trivial \(s\) gives a cyclic group directly.
At \(s=1\) one can instead retain that single finite relation.
These cases prove the local assertion.
Inverting either label preserves the family of ranks by multiplying the rectangle on the corresponding outer side by a translation and permuting and signing its constant coefficients, as in the [rectangle symmetries](Yin_C_A0196_v1.md#all-surjections-onto-the-quotient).
