# Four cosets of the actual adjacent subgroup and a five-point action on two three-cycles

## Statement

Let \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) be torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity.
Put \(A_0=\langle t,sts^{-1}\rangle\), and suppose \(A_0,A_0s,A_0s^2\) are distinct right cosets.
For a two-square rectangle \(K=B+At-s(D+Ct)\) of noninteger regular nullity \(\delta\), the following bounds hold.

* If \([H:A_0]=3\), then \(\delta\le1/3\).
* If \([H:A_0]=4\), then \(\delta\le3/8\).
  When \(s\) acts as a four-cycle on the four cosets, the bound improves to \(1/3\).
* If \([H:A_0]=5\) and the permutation of the five cosets induced by \(s\) has order three, then \(\delta\le3/8\).

In particular a three-coset marking with a half kernel must have \([H:A_0]\ge5\), allowing infinite index.
At index five its shift cannot have order three in this action.
For each of the stated cases and each \(\epsilon>0\), the corresponding bound plus \(\epsilon\) holds in a finite marked word neighborhood among torsion-free scalar-injective groups by [three-position upper semicontinuity](Yin_C_A0307_v2.md#statement).
This local assertion does not require that nearby markings retain the same permutation quotient.

The two new finite representation tests used below apply more generally.
If the marked image is \(S_4\) generated by a transposition and a three-cycle, or \(A_5\) generated by two three-cycles, then
\[
                    \delta\le(2h-1)/8
\]
for \(h\)-square corners with invertible assembly \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B&A\\D&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), assuming \(0<\delta<1\).
For two-square corners the assembly and this range of \(\delta\) are automatic at a noninteger input by the [four-column reduction](Yin_C_A0184_v1.md#statement).
The proof uses actual unitary representations of the given marked image and no character of \(H\) of order three.

## Four phases from paired special-unitary matrices

We recall the algebra behind the [four compensated phases](Yin_C_A0223_v1.md#four-compensated-two-dimensional-phases).
Suppose a marked quotient has two pairs of projective unitary matrices with special-unitary lifts \((S_1,T_1),(S_2,T_2)\) of size two.
Assume the individual traces of the two first matrices agree, as do those of the two second matrices; each generator matrix is nonscalar, the generator matrices in each pair do not commute, and the two product traces \({\rm Tr}(S_jT_j)\) differ.
Suppose both pairs \((S_j\otimes\overline S_1,T_j\otimes\overline T_1)\) give genuine representations.
For \(j=1\) this only needs a projective representation, since its scalar on every relation cancels with the conjugate scalar.

The [two-dimensional normal form](Yin_C_A0194_v1.md#normal-form-for-a-pair-of-unitary-matrices) writes the first tensor factors, after a common unitary change of basis in that factor, as
\[
        S_j=aI+bJ,\qquad T_j=cI+dJ_z,\qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 J_z=\begin{pmatrix}0&z^{-1}\\ z&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The constants \(a,b,c,d\) depend on the two individual spectra, which agree between the pairs because the determinants are one; \(bd\ne0\).
The parameter is on the circle, with both \(z\) and \(z^{-1}\) available by swapping the first two coordinates.
Noncommutation rules out \(z=z^{-1}\), and \({\rm Tr}(S_jT_j)=2ac+bd(z+z^{-1})\) separates the two pairs.
Thus there are four distinct phases, closed under inversion.
The conjugate second factor stays fixed throughout.

For clarity the group-ring linear pencil from this construction has size \(4h\), since the fixed factor has dimension two.
For the fixed second factor use translations \(s\otimes\overline S_1,\ t\otimes\overline T_1\).
The normal-form calculation gives a linear pencil \(E_0+z E_1\) with both ends invertible over the group ring by the assembly; swapping the first coordinates interchanges \(E_0,E_1\).
Thus \(L=-E_1^{-1}E_0\) satisfies \(JLJ=L^{-1}\), with \(J\) now amplified.
At each phase the eigenspace has dimension \(4\delta\) by regular absorption.
It contains no nonzero group-ring column: the absorption unitary acts on each group vector by a fixed finite numerical matrix at that group element, so a finite-support kernel vector would untwist to a nonzero finite-support vector in one copy of the original kernel of dimension \(\delta<1\).
This is impossible by scalar injectivity.
A scalar polynomial annihilating \(L\) would yield a polynomial primary idempotent, followed by the highest surviving power of \(L\) minus an allowed eigenvalue, with nonzero column in that eigenspace.

For the monic product \(p\) of the four distinct allowed linear factors, \({\rm im}\,p(L)\) has dimension \(4h-16\delta\).
It is invariant under \(L,L^{-1},J\) by reciprocal symmetry.
Each nonzero part with \(J\)-sign \(+\) or \(-\) has dimension at least one because it is the image of a nonzero group-ring matrix \((I\pm J)p(L)/2\).
If at most one sign occurred, \(JLJ=L^{-1}\) would give \((L^2-I)p(L)=0\), again an excluded scalar polynomial identity, including the zero-image case.
Hence \(4h-16\delta\ge2\), which is the asserted estimate.

## The octahedral and icosahedral markings

For \(S_4\) order the marks as transposition \(x\) and three-cycle \(y\), exchanging labels by the adjoint rectangle when necessary.
Their product has order four since they act transitively on four points.
Use the faithful rotation representation of \(S_4\) on the real hyperplane of coordinate sum zero in \(\mathbb R^4\): multiply each permutation matrix on that hyperplane by its sign.
This makes determinant one and preserves faithfulness, since the only permutation acting as a scalar on this hyperplane is the identity (apply it to all differences of two basis vectors).
Lift the two rotations to \(S,T\in SU(2)\) by the unit-quaternion double cover of \(SO(3)\), given by conjugation on imaginary quaternions.
The lift of the half-turn has trace zero, and \(ST\) has nonzero trace since its projective order is four.
Take the pairs \((S,T)\) and \((-S,T)\).
Their product traces are distinct, and the second compensated representation is the first tensored with the permutation sign since \(x\) is odd and \(y\) even.
All the hypotheses of the four-phase proof hold.

For \(A_5\) start with the [two binary icosahedral matrices](Yin_C_A0223_v1.md#eight-phases-from-a-symmetric-square) for its standard order-two and order-three generators, choosing one of the two reciprocal phases for each \(j=1,3\) at \(r=5\).
They square and cube to \(-I\), respectively, and their product has fifth power \(-I\) and trace \(2\cos(j\pi/5)\).
Thus at both choices the projective pair represents \(A_5\), and the two lifts take each relator word to the same scalar, as shown by the [compensated representation](Yin_C_A0223_v1.md#four-compensated-two-dimensional-phases).
Both projective representations are faithful by simplicity of \(A_5\) and nontriviality.
Now evaluate fixed words representing the two new three-cycles at these two choices.
Each evaluated special-unitary matrix is nonscalar, and the cubes for a fixed word have the same sign at both choices, since the cube word is a relator.
A nonscalar special-unitary lift of a rotation of order three has trace one when its cube is \(-I\), and trace minus one when its cube is \(I\).
Thus individual traces agree.

The generating three-cycles overlap in exactly one letter, since they act transitively on five letters, so their product is a five-cycle.
Every five-cycle in \(A_5\) is conjugate in \(A_5\) to a nontrivial power of the standard product.
Indeed all five-cycles are conjugate in \(S_5\), and the normalizer of a five-cycle contains an odd permutation inducing the square map on its cyclic group (multiplication by two modulo five).
Lifting the word for the new product therefore gives, up to signs, the traces \(2\cos(k\pi/5)\) and \(2\cos(3k\pi/5)\) at the two choices for some \(1\le k\le4\).
Their absolute values differ for each such \(k\).
The two pairs of word lifts are noncommuting and have genuine compensated representations by restriction to these same words in the old pairs.
They again give four distinct phases and prove the \(A_5\) test.

## The actual adjacent subgroup on three to five points

Act by left multiplication on \(H/A_0\), with basepoint \(o=A_0\).
The distinct right cosets in the hypotheses give three distinct points \(o,s^{-1}o,s^{-2}o\) by inversion.
The image \(y\) of \(t\) fixes \(o\) and \(s^{-1}o\), since \(t,sts^{-1}\in A_0\).
The image \(x\) of \(s\) has basepoint cycle of length at least three.
The action is transitive since the marks generate the group.

On three points \(y\) fixes everything, so \(x\) gives a genuine cyclic quotient of order three killing \(t\); apply the [three-phase estimate](Yin_C_A0327_v1.md#statement).
On four points, if \(y\) is the identity, \(x\) is a four-cycle and gives the same configuration with a character of order four.
All four scalar eigenspace twists have the same dimension, and the [three-parameter bound](Yin_C_A0329_v1.md#statement) applied to any three gives \(3\delta\le1\).
If \(y\ne1\) it is a transposition.
For a four-cycle \(x\), the two fixed points are consecutive in its cyclic order and \(y\) interchanges the remaining two neighbors.
The four conjugates of that adjacent transposition generate \(S_4\).
The quotient by the normal Klein four subgroup is \(S_3\), where \(x,y\) become generating involutions and hence two reflections with product of order three.
The [two-reflection estimate](Yin_C_A0200_v1.md#statement) gives \(\delta\le1/3\).
For a three-cycle \(x\), transitivity requires the transposition to connect its support to the remaining point, giving \(S_4\) with the octahedral marking above.

On five points a three-cycle \(x\) has two fixed points outside the basepoint cycle.
The permutation \(y\) fixes two points inside that cycle and must connect the remaining point to both points outside.
It must therefore be a three-cycle on those three letters.
The subgroup of even permutations they generate has order divisible by three and five by the three-cycle and transitivity.
It is all of \(A_5\): an order-fifteen group would be cyclic by the Sylow counts and cannot embed in \(A_5\), and an order-thirty subgroup would be normal of index two, contradicting simplicity.
The two generating cycles give the icosahedral test just proved.
