# At most one exceptional trace parameter for fixed ordered marks

## Statement

Fix the ordered generators \(g,h\) in one normalized orientation of the [noncyclic order-twenty marking](Yin_C_A0336_v1.md#the-coefficient-lines).
Assume the group \(H\) is torsion-free with regular scalar group-ring injectivity.
Let \(s_0=\sigma\sqrt5\), \(q(y)=y^2-s_0y+2\), \(p(y)=2y^2-s_0y+1\).
Use the [traceless transfer prototypes](Yin_C_A0337_v1.md#statement) \(M(y)=N(y)/q(y)\), \(M^2=(p/q)I\), with three linearly independent numerator matrices and parameter \(d=\operatorname{tr}(M(0)M(\infty))\).
For any such prototype write \(\delta(d)=\dim_H\ker(M(h)-gI)\), with unnormalized coordinate trace.
This number depends only on \(d\) for the fixed ordered marks and is the same for either sign \(\sigma\).
It equals the nullity of the corresponding two-square rectangle by right multiplication by its cyclic denominator pencil.

For every finite set of distinct admissible complex parameters one has
\[
                              \sum_{d\in D}\delta(d)\le \tfrac12 .
\]
In particular at most one parameter of a half kernel is possible, and it must be real.
At every nonreal parameter, for example \(d=i\), conjugation symmetry gives \(\delta(d)\le1/4\).
Among any \(k\) distinct parameters chosen in the positive interval, at least one has \(\delta(d)\le1/(2k)\).
The [positive interval](Yin_C_A0337_v1.md#statement) \(2<d<5/2\) is excluded by [forward power boundedness](Yin_C_A0339_v1.md#statement).
For the two possible normalized orientations of one original marking, the union therefore contains at most two candidate values.
The counting estimate does not assume an approximation property of the group or use a quantitative gap from the positive interval.

## A linear pencil on eight coordinates

Let \(V=\mathbb C^3\) with basis \(e_0,e_1,e_2\), and put \(N(y)=\sum_{j=0}^2N_jy^j\).
For the trace pairing \(\operatorname{tr}(XY)/2\), the [numerator Gram matrix](Yin_C_A0337_v1.md#from-the-boundary-equation-to-three-traceless-matrices) on this basis is
\[
 G(d)=\begin{pmatrix}
  2&-3s_0/2&d\\
  -3s_0/2&10-2d&-3s_0/2\\
  d&-3s_0/2&2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Write \(\varepsilon_j\) for exterior multiplication by \(e_j\) and \(\iota_\ell\) for contraction by a linear functional \(\ell\) on \(V\).
On the eight-dimensional exterior algebra take
\[
 c_j(d)=\varepsilon_j+\iota_{G(d)(e_j,\,\cdot\,)},\qquad
 K(d)=\sum_{j=0}^2c_j(d)h^j-gq(h)I_8 .
\]
The defining identities of exterior multiplication and contraction give
\(c_ic_j+c_jc_i=2G_{ij}(d)I\).
These operators realize the left regular module of the complex Clifford algebra with this Gram matrix, also at a degenerate form.
Indeed the relations order every product into a span of eight increasing monomials, including the empty product.
Those monomials applied to the exterior vacuum have as leading terms the eight distinct exterior basis elements, with all other terms of lower degree.
Thus the vacuum map is a vector space isomorphism intertwining left multiplication with these operators.

At admissible \(d\), the two assignments of generators to \(N_j\) and to \(-N_j\) identify this algebra with \(M_2(\mathbb C)\oplus M_2(\mathbb C)\).
For detail, the three \(N_j\) span the traceless matrices, so each component map is onto.
Take a complex orthonormal basis of the nondegenerate form.
The product of its three generators commutes with all generators and has square \(-1\).
Its two matrix images are opposite nonzero scalar matrices, so its two interpolation idempotents separate the components.
Surjectivity onto the direct sum and equality of dimensions give the identification.
Left multiplication on each two-square matrix algebra gives two copies of its defining representation, one per column.
The sign character negating \(g\) and fixing \(h\) shows that the kernels of \(N(h)-gq(h)I\) and \(-N(h)-gq(h)I\) have the same trace.
Since \(q(h)\) is invertible, \(K(d)\) has nullity \(4\delta(d)\), which also proves independence from the particular numerator realizing \(d\).

The independent sign negating \(h\) and fixing \(g\) switches the two prototype signs by \(M(y)\mapsto M(-y)\), leaving its trace parameter unchanged.
Complex conjugation of the coefficient matrices induces an antiunitary equivalence on the regular space by coordinatewise conjugation in the group basis; it preserves the real polynomial coefficients of \(p,q\) and conjugates \(d\).
Hence \(\delta(\bar d)=\delta(d)\).

## Full rank at a degenerate Gram matrix

Take \(d_0=2\) or \(5/2\).
In either case \(G(d_0)\) has rank two by the [block Gram calculation](Yin_C_A0337_v1.md#from-the-boundary-equation-to-three-traceless-matrices).
Choose two nondegenerate orthogonal generators and a third nonzero radical generator \(u\).
In the Clifford algebra the two-sided ideal generated by \(u\) is spanned by the four monomials ending in \(u\), because \(u^2=0\) and it anticommutes with the other generators.
The quotient is the two-generator Clifford algebra with nondegenerate form, isomorphic to \(M_2(\mathbb C)\) by taking two anticommuting Pauli matrices after rescaling.
On the ideal, writing \(a u\) with \(a\) in this two-generator algebra identifies the left action with the same left regular quotient module, since multiplication by the radical generator kills the ideal.
Thus \(K(d_0)\) is block triangular with four two-square diagonal blocks, all given by the same quotient numerator \(N_*(h)-gq(h)I\), counted with multiplicity.

In this two-dimensional matrix representation the images of the three original generators span a space of dimension at most two and are traceless.
The rational function \(M_*=N_*/q\) still satisfies \(M_*^2=(p/q)I\) by the defining Gram relations.
Its entries have no poles on the circle and \(\det(M_*(y)-xI)=x^2-p(y)/q(y)\).
The [five-square calculation](Yin_C_A0336_v1.md#the-coefficient-lines) gives an actual character of \(H\) at which this last determinant is nonzero, since among the ten values of \(y\) only six are zeros after \(x^2=y^4\).

Normalize the span of its coefficients by one invertible value of \(M_*\) on the left.
The normalized span contains the identity, and an eigenvector of a second matrix (or any vector in the one-dimensional span case) yields a constant vector line sent to a constant line by \(M_*(y)\).
When these two lines coincide, constant triangularization gives two scalar diagonal entries of \(M_*(h)-gI\).
After right multiplication by \(q(h)\) these are group-ring scalars, each nonzero by evaluation at the chosen character.
They are both affiliated units by the scalar hypothesis.
When the lines differ, a constant basis consisting of their vectors puts \(M_*\) into the form \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&b\\a&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
Elimination of the pivot \(g\) in \(gI-M_*(h)\) gives the scalar \(g-c(h)-a(h)g^{-1}b(h)\).
Multiplying on the left and on the right by \(q(h)\) clears this to a group-ring scalar, since \(a,b,c\) have denominator \(q\).
Its character value is nonzero by the numerical two-square determinant, the nonzero pivot and \(q(y)\ne0\).
Scalar injectivity again gives full rank. [Constant point elimination](Yin_C_A0186_v1.md#a-constant-point-correspondence-in-a-two-dimensional-transfer)
Thus all diagonal blocks of \(K(d_0)\) are invertible over the affiliated algebra, and so is \(K(d_0)\).

## The generalized zero space at infinity

Fix \(d_0=2\) and put \(K_0=K(d_0)\), \(L=\partial K/\partial d\).
In exterior coordinates
\[
                  L=h^2\iota_{e_0^*}-2h\iota_{e_1^*}+\iota_{e_2^*}.
\]
Over the rational field of \(h\) this is contraction by a nonzero covector.
Its kernel equals its image, either by choosing a basis or by \(L\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_2 L=I\) and \(L^2=0\).
The dimensions of its kernel in exterior degrees \(0,1,2,3\) are \(1,2,1,0\), and those of the graded quotient by its image are \(0,1,2,1\).
These rational subspaces give the same dimensions after extending to \(U(H)\), since the basis change is invertible there.

Write \(C_0=\sum_j c_j(d_0)h^j\).
It anticommutes with \(L\): the contraction terms anticommute among themselves and the pairing with the wedge vector \((1,h,h^2)\) is \(h^2-2h^2+h^2=0\).
Thus \(C_0\) maps \(\ker L\) into \({\rm im}\,L\).
The map induced by \(K_0\) from \(\ker L\) to the quotient by \({\rm im}\,L\) consequently agrees with the map induced by \(-gq(h)I\), which preserves exterior degrees.
The two lists of dimensions bound its rank by \(0+1+1+0=2\).
It follows that at least two dimensions of vectors \(v\in\ker L\) satisfy \(K_0v\in{\rm im}\,L\).

Set \(X=K_0^{-1}L\) in the eight-square affiliated algebra.
The last submodule is \(\ker X\cap{\rm im}\,X\), and \(\dim\ker X=4\).
The map \(X:\ker X^2\to\ker X\cap{\rm im}\,X\) is onto with kernel \(\ker X\), giving \(\dim\ker X^2\ge6\).
For \(d\ne d_0\), the kernel of \(K(d)\) is the eigenspace of \(X\) at \(-1/(d-d_0)\).
For any finite set of distinct such parameters these nonzero eigenspaces sum directly with \(\ker X^2\) by polynomial interpolation at the distinct nonzero eigenvalues and the double root zero.
Their dimensions therefore sum to at most two.
Division by the multiplicity four proves the estimate.
A half kernel would exhaust this budget and, by conjugation symmetry, can only occur at a real parameter.

## A finite-dimensional check of the infinity bound

The grading estimate can be attained by numerical unitary marks when the original-group hypotheses are omitted.
On a two-point space let \(h=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) and let \(g\) be \(e^{\pi i/4}\) times the swap.
The two-square transfer on the two sites has kernel isomorphic to the \(i\)-eigenspace of \(M(1)M(-1)\), by solving for one site with the invertible transfer at the other.
This product has determinant one and trace \(2d-3\), from \(\langle N_0+N_2+N_1,N_0+N_2-N_1\rangle=4d-6\) and \(q(1)q(-1)=4\).
At \(d=3/2\) its eigenvalues are \(i,-i\), so that the kernel has numerical dimension one.
Negating \(g\) gives the same return product.
At \(d_0=2\) the return product has trace one and determinant one also in the two-generator quotient, hence no eigenvalue \(i\) and the degenerate matrix \(K_0\) is invertible here.
All four Clifford copies therefore contribute one finite kernel dimension among sixteen numerical coordinates.
The generalized zero space at infinity has dimension at least twelve by the grading estimate and at most twelve by this finite eigenspace.
In this example a further generalized chain cannot decrease the budget.
