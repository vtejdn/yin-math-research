# Scalar injectivity at each actual inverse layer is equivalent to atomlessness

## Statement

Let \(G\) be sofic and let \(D_0=\overline{\mathbb Q}G\).
Inside \(U(G)\) let \(D_{j+1}\) be the unital \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\)-algebra generated by \(D_j\) and the inverses of its elements that are affiliated units.
Let \(D_\infty=\bigcup_jD_j\).
For each fixed \(j\), including \(j=\infty\), the following are equivalent:

1. Every nonzero scalar in \(D_j\) is an affiliated unit.
2. Every \(Y\in D_j\) that is not a scalar multiple of the identity has atomless Brown measure.

Here \(Y\) can be unbounded: all the actual rational evaluations involved belong to the domain \(\mathcal M^\Delta\) of the unbounded Brown measure, characterized by \(\tau\log^+|Y|<\infty\).
The criterion for \(D_j\) concerns the actual elements of that same layer.
No implication from layer \(j\) to layer \(j+1\) is asserted.

For torsion-free sofic \(G\), condition 2 for \(D_\infty\) is equivalent to the complex-coefficient strong Atiyah conjecture on \(G\).
It suffices in this last criterion to test bounded, positive, nonconstant scalar rational contractions for an atom at one.
In fact a nonzero nonunit \(s\in D_j\) already gives the nonconstant positive contraction \((1+s^*s)^{-1}\in D_{j+1}\) with an atom of mass \(\dim_G\ker s\) at one.
This supplies a bounded detection at one additional inverse without a closed-range assumption.

## Logarithmic integrability for unbounded evaluations

Restrict to the subgroup generated by the finite support of the rational expression whenever a countable marked approximation is used.
The trace-preserving subgroup inclusion respects the actual affiliated operations, spectral projections and logarithmic integrals.
An algebraic group-ring auxiliary matrix \(a\) that is invertible in the affiliated algebra has finite integral of \(\log^-(t)\) against the singular measure; this follows from the [algebraic determinant estimate](Yin_C_A0347_v1.md#logarithmic-tails-for-roots-of-all-powers) at power one.
Therefore \(a^{-1}\in\mathcal M^\Delta\) at the corresponding matrix size.
Every fixed algebraic rational evaluation, bounded or otherwise, is a Schur expression \(d-ca^{-1}b\) by [actual linearization](Yin_C_A0347_v3.md#an-actual-linear-system-and-its-spectral-comparison).
Closure of \(\mathcal M^\Delta\) under sums and products puts the evaluation in this same class.
An affiliated-invertible rational evaluation also has its inverse in the class, since that inverse is another finite actual rational evaluation. [Haagerup and Schultz, Propositions 2.5 and 2.6 and Theorem 2.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0605251)
We use normalized traces on square matrices.

For an unbounded rational matrix \(Y\), an integer polynomial \(P\) of fixed degree at most \(n\) and coefficient height at most \(D\ge1\) satisfies
\[
    \int_{(0,\infty)}\log^-(t)\,d\mu_{|P(Y)|}(t)\le l\log D+C_n .
\]
Here \(\mu_{|P(Y)|}\) denotes normalized singular distribution and \(l\) is the degree of a fixed coefficient Galois field, independent of \(n\).
Use one copy of the [sparse polynomial system](Yin_C_A0347_v3.md#sparse-repeated-systems-with-a-fixed-number-of-coefficient-dependent-rows).
Its whole matrix is a bounded group-ring matrix even when its output \(P(Y)\) is unbounded.
The graph comparison is unchanged: restrict to \(\mathbf1_{[0,t]}(|P(Y)|)\) and to the bounded auxiliary graph cutoff before using the closed expression.
On these vectors the output is defined and has norm at most \(t\) times the input norm; the eliminated expression agrees with the closed affiliated output on the graph domain.
Affiliated elimination gives the exact equality of the two kernel dimensions.
The same finite-minor bound and integration over \((0,t]\) now give the inequality.
Only the one final output block contains the coefficients of \(P\), which keeps the slope \(l\) independent of its degree.

## An unbounded rational matrix with no algebraic eigenvalue

Suppose every algebraic scalar shift \(Y-\alpha I\) is injective.
Each such shift is an affiliated unit at its square size, and its inverse is an actual algebraic rational evaluation.
The positive and negative logarithmic integrals are both finite, so \(\log\Delta(Y-\alpha I)>-\infty\).
The unbounded Brown potential identity rules out an atom at \(\alpha\).
In fact \(\int\log^+|z|\,d\mu_Y(z)<\infty\), and an atom at \(\alpha\) would force the potential there to be \(-\infty\).

Every nonzero integer polynomial \(P\) then gives an injective \(P(Y)\) by factoring over the algebraic numbers.
The logarithmic bound in the preceding section gives
\(\log\Delta(P(Y))\ge-l\log D-C_n\).
The potential identity and multiplicativity under polynomial factorization give
\[
                    \log\Delta(P(Y))=\int\log|P(z)|\,d\mu_Y(z).
\]
For a transcendental point \(\lambda\), the [complex pigeonhole estimate](Yin_C_A0347_v1.md#transcendental-points-and-small-polynomial-values) gives for each large \(D\) a polynomial of degree at most \(n\), height at most \(D\), with \(0<|P(\lambda)|\le C_{n,\lambda}D^{1-(n+1)/2}\).
For all other points use \(\log^+|P(z)|\le\log D+\log(n+1)+n\log^+|z|\).
Thus an atom of mass \(\alpha>0\) at \(\lambda\) would give an upper bound \((1-\alpha(n+1)/2)\log D+O_{n,\lambda,Y}(1)\) on the potential of the polynomial.
Choosing \(n+1>2(l+1)/\alpha\) contradicts the lower bound as \(D\) grows.
This proves atomlessness for such an unbounded matrix without invoking radial convergence for powers of an unbounded operator.

## Actual eigenvectors and the layer criterion

For any \(Y\in\mathcal M^\Delta\) with kernel projection \(p\), its Brown mass at zero is at least the normalized trace of \(p\).
For \(0<\tau(p)<1\), decompose \(Y\) relative to \(p,1-p\).
Its first column is zero; write \(b=pY(1-p)\) and \(C=(1-p)Y(1-p)\).
All corner entries remain logarithmically integrable in the positive part.
For \(w\ne0\), right multiplication of \(Y-wI\) by \(I+b/w\) clears its upper off-diagonal entry.
This possibly unbounded shear has determinant one.
Indeed it and its inverse are in \(\mathcal M^\Delta\), and its square is similar to it by the bounded invertible diagonal \(2p+(1-p)\).
Multiplicativity and similarity invariance give a finite positive determinant equal to its square.
Consequently the logarithmic potential of \(Y\) agrees off zero with that of \(\tau(p)\delta_0+(1-\tau(p))\mu_C\), using the normalized trace on the second corner.
The potentials agree almost everywhere, hence have the same distributional Laplacian. This gives the mass inequality; the cases \(p=0,I\) are immediate.
Translation gives the same conclusion for kernels of arbitrary shifts. [Unbounded logarithmic potential and uniqueness](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0605251#page=10)

If every nonzero scalar in \(D_j\) is injective, then every algebraic shift of a nonconstant \(Y\in D_j\) is a nonzero element of that same layer and is injective.
The preceding argument proves condition 2.
Conversely a nonzero nonunit \(s\) has positive kernel trace and cannot be a constant, so its Brown measure has an atom at zero.
This proves the layer equivalence.
For the bounded test, the function \((1+t)^{-1}\) on the spectrum of \(s^*s\) takes the value one exactly at zero.
The mass there is positive and less than one for a nonzero nonunit, so the contraction is nonconstant.
Each \(D_j\) is closed under adjoints by induction on the actual inverse gates.

Finally \(D_\infty\) is the scalar division closure inside the affiliated algebra.
If all its nonzero scalars are units then it is a skew field.
Gaussian elimination gives integer regular ranks over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}G\); the sofic base-change theorem gives strong Atiyah integrality over \(\mathbb C\).
Conversely the strong Atiyah conjecture makes the division closure over \(\mathbb C\) a skew field, forcing condition 1 on each of its indicated subalgebras.
The bounded positive test detects every failure of condition 1 at the union by the contraction formula above. [Division-closure criterion](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#starting-references) [Jaikin-Zapirain, Theorem 1.1](https://matematicas.uam.es/~andrei.jaikin/preprints/sac.pdf#page=4)
