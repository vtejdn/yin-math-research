# The integral determinant bound gives equal ranks at number-field embeddings

## Statement

Let \(G\) be a discrete group satisfying the ordinary integral determinant bound: for every square matrix over \(\mathbb ZG\), the unnormalized logarithmic Fuglede--Kadison determinant with the kernel omitted is nonnegative. [Determinant convention](../audits/literature/Luc22/Yin_U001.md#luc22-kernel-removed-determinant).
For any number field \(K\), any rectangular \(A\) over \(KG\), and any two embeddings \(\sigma,\gamma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\), one has
\[
                       \operatorname{rk}_G(\sigma A)=\operatorname{rk}_G(\gamma A).
\]
The embeddings act on coefficients and leave the group labels fixed.
The result also applies to matrices with algebraic coefficients in a field of infinite algebraic degree, by restriction to the number field generated by the entries' coefficients.

Any finite rational matrix expression over \(KG\) defined in one of these embeddings is defined in all of them and has the same regular rank.
Its intermediate and final evaluations may be unbounded.
For a square output \(Y\), extend the embeddings to include an algebraic point \(\lambda\).
The mass at \(\sigma(\lambda)\) of the probability Brown measure of the \(\sigma\)-evaluation equals the corresponding mass at \(\gamma(\lambda)\) of the \(\gamma\)-evaluation.
In particular, for one fixed embedding of the coefficients, all \(e=[K(\lambda):K]\) conjugates of an atom over \(K\) occur with the same mass, which is at most \(1/e\).
The [all-points formula under the integral determinant bound](Yin_C_A0347_v7.md#statement) makes every atom algebraic.

This is a conclusion for algebraic coefficient fields under a hypothesis on integer matrices alone.
The [general field-embedding theorem for sofic groups](../audits/literature/JZ19/Yin_U001.md#base-change-proof) has a different group hypothesis and also treats embeddings of fields with transcendental elements.

## One lower bound for all scalar multiples

Pad a rectangular matrix with zero rows or columns to make it \(n\)-square, preserving rank.
Choose a primitive element \(\theta\) for \(K/\mathbb Q\) and multiply it by a positive integer if needed so that it is an algebraic integer.
Let \(l=[K:\mathbb Q]\) and \(O=\mathbb Z[\theta]\).
After multiplying \(A\) by one nonzero integer, all its coefficients belong to \(O\).
This operation preserves every rank.

For each nonzero \(u\in O\), replace each coefficient of \(uA\) by its multiplication matrix on the integral power basis \(1,\theta,\ldots,\theta^{l-1}\).
These are integer matrices because \(\theta\) satisfies a monic integral polynomial of degree \(l\).
The resulting matrix over \(\mathbb ZG\) is similar, by a fixed numerical matrix repeated on the group-ring coordinates, to \(\bigoplus_\sigma \sigma(u)\sigma(A)\), where the sum is over all \(l\) embeddings.
Indeed the evaluation matrix of the power basis is a nonsingular Vandermonde matrix, and each evaluation intertwines multiplication with the corresponding scalar.

Write \(\mathcal L_+(X)\) for the integral of \(\log s\) on strictly positive singular values with the unnormalized regular trace, including the empty integral zero.
A bounded similarity by a fixed invertible matrix changes this integral by at most the coordinate count times the logarithm of the matrix condition number.
This follows also from the [kernel and cokernel volume formula](Yin_C_A0347_v7.md#pseudodeterminants-under-a-change-of-metric).
In particular, the integer determinant hypothesis gives
\[
              \sum_\sigma\mathcal L_+\big(\sigma(u)\sigma(A)\big)\ge-C_A
                         \qquad(0\ne u\in O),
\]
with \(C_A\) independent of \(u\).
At \(u=1\) every summand is finite since each bounded matrix has finite positive-part logarithmic integral and the sum is bounded below.
Put \(r_\sigma=\operatorname{rk}_G(\sigma A)\).
Scalar multiplication adds \(r_\sigma\log|\sigma(u)|\) to the corresponding integral.
There is consequently a finite \(C'_A\) such that
\[
                         \sum_\sigma r_\sigma\log|\sigma(u)|\ge-C'_A
                                  \qquad(0\ne u\in O).
\]

## Varying the archimedean sizes

Let \(v\) run over the real embeddings and one representative of each conjugate pair of complex embeddings, and put \(n_v=1,2\) respectively.
Conjugation of all \(\ell^2(G)\) coefficients in the group basis gives the same rank for a pair of conjugate embeddings; write \(r_v\) for their common rank.
Under these evaluations \(O\) is a full lattice in the product of the real and complex coordinate spaces.
In fact the real-linear evaluation map on polynomials of degree less than \(l\) with real coefficients is injective there, since vanishing also at the conjugate points would give \(l\) distinct roots.
The real dimensions agree, and the integer coefficient lattice maps exactly onto the image of \(O\).

Take any real vector \((t_v)\) with \(\sum_v n_v t_v=0\).
For each positive integer \(m\), in the real coordinates take intervals centered at zero and in the complex coordinates take disks centered at zero, all of radii \(c\exp(mt_v)/2\).
Their product has volume independent of \(m\) and larger than the lattice covolume if \(c\) is sufficiently large.
Two of its points then differ by a nonzero lattice vector, by integrating the number of translates over a fundamental domain.
Thus there exists \(0\ne u_m\in O\) with \(|v(u_m)|\le c\exp(mt_v)\) for all \(v\).
Insert these bounds into the preceding inequality, using \(r_v\ge0\), divide by \(m\), and let \(m\to\infty\).
The result is \(\sum_v n_vr_v t_v\ge0\).
Using \(-t_v\) gives equality.
Orthogonality to this entire hyperplane forces all the \(r_v\) to be equal, proving the claimed embedding invariance.

## Actual rational gates and Brown masses

Proceed through the finite rational expression in the order of its gates.
Group-ring gates at every size have the rank invariance just proved.
For an already defined rational matrix, the [auxiliary system](Yin_C_A0347_v3.md#an-actual-linear-system-and-its-spectral-comparison) represents it by \(d-ca^{-1}b\), with \(a,b,c,d\) group-ring matrices and \(a\) an affiliated unit.
The full group-ring block has rank \(\operatorname{rk}_G(a)+\operatorname{rk}_G(d-ca^{-1}b)\).
Both the full block rank and the rank of the auxiliary matrix are embedding invariant.
Whenever an inverse of a square output is required, full rank in one embedding is therefore full rank in the other, which is exactly invertibility in the finite affiliated algebra.
The same auxiliary equations and their successive eliminations define the expression in all embeddings.
This proves both definedness and rank invariance for sums, products and actual inverses.
If coefficients of adjoint group-ring expressions occur, first write them as coefficients of the indicated words; an arbitrary field embedding need not commute with complex conjugation.

Apply the assertion over \(K(\lambda)\) to each power of \(Y-\lambda I\).
In all embeddings the dimensions of its kernels match at every positive power.
The [Brown generalized-kernel formula](Yin_C_A0347_v7.md#statement) passes this equality to the atom masses, with the same matrix-size normalization.
For embeddings fixing \(K\) these are atoms of the same probability measure at the distinct roots of the minimal polynomial of \(\lambda\).
Their total mass is at most one, giving the stated bound.
