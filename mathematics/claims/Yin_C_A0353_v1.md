# A Toeplitz positivity test for a two-column Schur kernel

## Statement

Let \(t\) have infinite order in a discrete group \(G\), let \(s\in G\), and suppose each nonzero element of \(\mathbb CG\) acts injectively in the regular representation.
For complex two-by-two Laurent polynomial matrices \(P,Q\), put \(K=P(t)-sQ(t)\).
By a common right multiplication by a power of \(t\), write both polynomials with powers \(0,\ldots,n\).
Write \(P(z)=\mathcal P Z(z)\), \(Q(z)=\mathcal Q Z(z)\), where \(Z(z)=(I,zI,\ldots,z^nI)^{\mathsf T}\) denotes a column of two-square blocks.
If \(K\) has noninteger regular nullity, there is a strictly positive Hermitian block Toeplitz matrix \(\Gamma\) of size \(2(n+1)\) satisfying
\[
               \mathcal P\Gamma\mathcal P^*=\mathcal Q\Gamma\mathcal Q^* .                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular an affine pencil gives a four-by-four test on its numerical coefficients.

Strict feasibility of (1) fails precisely when some constant Hermitian \(H\) gives a matrix
\[
        D_H(z)=P(z)^*H P(z)-Q(z)^*H Q(z)
\]
that is positive semidefinite everywhere on the unit circle and is nonzero somewhere.
Either condition excludes every fractional kernel dimension, including for pivots with nonclosed range.
If a chosen scalar pivot is an affiliated unit, its Schur complement is then zero or an affiliated unit.
The test is necessary for a fractional kernel and does not assert a converse from feasibility.

## Strict positivity from the kernel projection

Let \(p\) be the orthogonal kernel projection of \(K\) and set \(\Gamma=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)(Z(t)pZ(t)^*)\), with the group trace applied to each entry.
Its blocks depend only on the difference of the two powers by traciality.
The identity \(P(t)p=sQ(t)p\) gives (1) because the trace is invariant under conjugating each output entry by \(s\).
If a nonzero numerical row \(u\) annihilates this positive Gram form, faithfulness gives \(h(t)p=0\) for the polynomial row \(h(z)=uZ(z)\).
This row is nonzero because the coefficients at the distinct powers are the blocks of \(u\).
A nonzero Laurent polynomial in \(t\) is an affiliated unit by Fourier transform on its infinite cyclic subgroup and right-coset replication.
Hence the kernel of \(h(t)\) over the affiliated algebra is generated by the column \(v(t)=(-h_2(t),h_1(t))^{\mathsf T}\).
Indeed either nonzero coordinate of \(h\) can serve as pivot, and its inverse commutes with the other coordinate.
The column is injective over the affiliated algebra.
If \(K v(t)\) has a nonzero entry, scalar injectivity forces the original kernel to vanish.
If \(K v(t)=0\), the original kernel is this whole line and has dimension one.
These statements use the right modules cut out by the Hilbert kernel projections, so no Hilbert-space vector need be in the domain of a pivot inverse.
A fractional kernel therefore makes \(\Gamma\) strictly positive.
Over the affiliated algebra block elimination at a unit scalar pivot gives \(\dim\ker K=\dim\ker S\) for its scalar Schur complement \(S\).
A scalar of nullity zero is an affiliated unit, and nullity one forces it to be zero.

## The finite-dimensional alternative

Use the real trace inner product on Hermitian matrices and let \(\mathcal T\) be the real subspace of Hermitian block Toeplitz matrices.
Write \(L(T)=\mathcal P T\mathcal P^*-\mathcal Q T\mathcal Q^*\).
If \(\mathcal T\cap\ker L\) misses the open positive cone, finite-dimensional separation gives a nonzero positive semidefinite matrix \(B\) orthogonal to that subspace.
Indeed the separating functional vanishes on the linear space by scaling and is nonnegative on the closed positive cone by continuity.
The orthogonal complement is \(\mathcal T^\perp+\operatorname{im}L^*\), so \(B=N+\mathcal P^*H\mathcal P-\mathcal Q^*H\mathcal Q\) with \(N\perp\mathcal T\).
For each unit \(z\) and \(x\in\mathbb C^2\), \(Z(z)xx^*Z(z)^*\) is block Toeplitz.
Consequently \(Z(z)^* B Z(z)=D_H(z)\ge0\).
Its Haar-averaged trace is \(\operatorname{Tr} B>0\) by orthogonality of the monomials.

Conversely, suppose (1) holds for \(T>0\) in \(\mathcal T\).
Choose \(0<\epsilon<\lambda_{\min}(T)\) and factor \(T-\epsilon I\) as the Gram matrix \((V_i^*V_j)_{i,j=0}^n\) of maps from \(\mathbb C^2\) into a finite-dimensional Hilbert space.
Toeplitz equality shows \(V_i x\mapsto V_{i+1}x\) for \(0\le i<n\) defines an isometry on their span.
Extend it to a unitary on the common ambient space.
Its finite spectral resolution expresses \(T-\epsilon I\) as a sum of \(Z(z)W_z Z(z)^*\) with \(W_z\ge0\), using the conjugates of the unitary eigenvalues for \(z\).
For \(n=0\) use a single point \(z=1\).
The identity matrix itself is the Haar integral of \(Z(z)Z(z)^*\).
If \(D_H\) were positive semidefinite and nonzero somewhere, the continuous function \(\operatorname{Tr}D_H\) would have strictly positive Haar integral.
Pairing \(H\) with \(L(T)=0\) would give the sum of the nonnegative numbers \(\operatorname{Tr}(D_H(z)W_z)\) and \(\epsilon\) times that positive integral, a contradiction.

There is also a direct way to use a given \(H\).
The equality \(P(t)\xi=sQ(t)\xi\) implies \(\langle D_H(t)\xi,\xi\rangle=0\), since a numerical \(H\) commutes with \(sI\).
Positivity then gives \(D_H(t)\xi=0\).
Any nonzero polynomial row of \(D_H\) confines the kernel to the cyclic rational line in the preceding proof.
For a particular input it suffices to check one nonzero entry of \(Kv(t)\) is injective.

## A mixed pencil with no two-sided group-ring denominator

Put \(u(z)=1-z/2\), \(v(z)=z-1/2\), \(k=1/\sqrt2\), and take
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}0&kv\\v&-u/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\-v&u\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The constant and the linear coefficient of each matrix are invertible, as follows from \(\det P=-k v^2\), \(\det Q=u^2\) and their constant and leading coefficients.
For \(|z|=1\) one has \(|u|=|v|\), and
\[
 Q^*Q-P^*P=\tfrac14(2v,-u)^*(2v,-u).
\]
Thus \(H=-I\) gives a positive defect of rank exactly one and the surviving line is generated by \(w=(u,2v)^{\mathsf T}\).
The second component of \(K w(t)\) is \(-s u(t)v(t)\), a bounded unit because \(u\) and \(v\) have no unit-circle zeros.
This proves injectivity on every such marking without the group-ring scalar injectivity hypothesis.
The pivot \(a=-s u(t)\) is a unit, so the originally chosen Schur complement has full rank.

Now take \(G=F(s,t)\), \(A=\mathbb CF(s,t)\) and suppress \(t\) in the polynomial arguments.
The other entries are \(b=kv,\ c=(1+s)v,\ d=-u/2-su\), so
\[
       S=d+X u^{-1}Y,\qquad X=(1+s)v,\quad Y=k s^{-1}v .
\]
For arbitrary nonzero \(L,R\in A\) one has \(LSR\notin A\).
To see this, first take any \(X_0,Y_0\in A\) and write \(X_0=\sum_i r_i f_i(t)\), \(Y_0=\sum_j g_j(t)q_j\) using distinct left and distinct right cosets of \(C=\langle t\rangle\), respectively.
The free factor \(C\) is malnormal by reduced words.
Distinct sets \(r_i C q_j\) therefore meet in at most one point.
Indeed two distinct common points give a nonidentity element in \(r_i C r_i^{-1}\cap r_k C r_k^{-1}\), forcing \(i=k\), after which intersecting right cosets force \(j=l\).
The inverse of \(u\) has an absolutely summable geometric series in \(t\).
If \(X_0u^{-1}Y_0\in A\), the restriction of its coefficients to each such set outside the finitely many intersections shows that every Laurent fraction \(f_i g_j/u\) has finite Fourier support.
It is then a Laurent polynomial, so primality of \(u\) in the Laurent ring implies either \(X_0\in Au\) or \(Y_0\in uA\).

Apply this to \(X_0=LX,\ Y_0=YR\).
If \(LX\in Au\), coprimality of \(v,u\) on each left coset gives \(L(1+s)\in Au\cap A(1+s)\).
This intersection is zero: a finite relation between the two right-hand binomials would give a finitely supported weighted edge chain in the Cayley tree on \(s,t\), with nonzero contributions at both endpoints of each occupied edge.
The coefficient at a leaf cannot vanish.
If \(YR\in uA\), then \(vR\in vA\cap (s u s^{-1})A\).
This intersection also vanishes, using the left Cayley forest for the free pair \(t,sts^{-1}\) and the same leaf argument.
In the first case \(L(1+s)\ne0\) for \(L\ne0\) by infinite order of \(s\), and in the second \(vR\ne0\) for \(R\ne0\).
Removing the group-ring summand \(LdR\) proves the asserted impossibility.
Thus the [two-sided multiplication test](../ledger/Yin_L_A0081_v1.md#a-certificate-using-both-sides) can fail on the very scalar whose kernel the Hermitian test excludes.

For a limit of this test, take \(U=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(J(z)=\operatorname{diag}(z,1)\), \(P=UJ,\ Q=JU\).
Both are unitary on the circle, so \(\operatorname{Tr}D_H(z)=0\) for every constant \(H\).
No nonzero semidefinite defect exists.
Nevertheless, on \(\mathbb Z^2\) with its standard marks the kernel is zero: for fixed unit \(t\), \(\det(P(t)-sQ(t))\) has nonzero quadratic leading coefficient as a polynomial in \(s\), hence vanishes on a Haar-null set by Fubini.
The [rank-one commutator criterion](Yin_C_A0157_v1.md#separating-the-ranges-and-excluding-rank-one) describes the ambient question; the exclusions above apply to witnesses arising from the specified inputs.
