# Schur complements of conjugated roots in a central cyclic amalgam

## Statement

Let \(H_1,H_2\) be countable discrete groups with a common infinite cyclic group \(Z=\langle z\rangle\) embedded in the center of each, and let \(G=H_1*_{Z}H_2\).
Choose \(r_i\in H_i\) and \(m_i\ge2\) with \(r_i^{m_i}=z\).
Write \(U(H)\) for the affiliated algebra of the regular group von Neumann algebra of \(H\).
For \(f_i\in\mathbb C(w)\) and units \(k_i\in U(H_i)\), form the rows
\[
 (a,b)=(k_1,f_1(r_1)k_1),\qquad
 (c,d)=(k_2,f_2(r_2)k_2).
\]
The Schur complement \(S=d-ca^{-1}b\) vanishes exactly when a single \(F\in\mathbb C(w)\) satisfies
\[
                    f_1(w)=F(w^{m_1}),\qquad f_2(w)=F(w^{m_2}).
                    \tag{1}
\]
In every other case \(S\) is a unit in \(U(G)\).
This applies to two-by-two group-ring matrices whenever \(k_i\in\mathbb CH_i\) are affiliated units and the \(f_i\) are Laurent polynomials.
In this case the unnormalized regular nullity of the matrix is one in (1) and zero otherwise, by elimination at \(a\).
Thus these matrices cannot supply a rank-one commutator output through a proper nonzero Schur kernel. [Rank-one criterion](Yin_C_A0157_v1.md#separating-the-ranges-and-excluding-rank-one)

## The regular trace at a central character

Put \(Q=G/Z\) and \(Q_i=H_i/Z\).
The presentation of the amalgam after quotienting by \(Z\) gives \(Q=Q_1*Q_2\).
Choose a section \(s:Q\to G\) with \(s(e)=e\), taking \(Q_i\) into \(H_i\).
The identity \(s(x)s(y)=z^{b(x,y)}s(xy)\) defines an integer cocycle \(b\).
Fourier transformation in the exponent \(j\) of the normal form \(z^j s(x)\) identifies \(\ell^2(G)\) with the direct integral of \(\ell^2(Q)\) over Haar measure on \(\mathbb T\).
In the fiber at \(\zeta\), left multiplication by \(z\) is the scalar \(\zeta\), and \(s(x)\) sends \(\delta_y\) to \(\zeta^{b(x,y)}\delta_{xy}\).
The vector trace at \(\delta_e\) is the faithful normalized trace of the twisted group von Neumann algebra.
Integration of these traces gives the regular trace since the integrated coefficient of \(z^j s(x)\) at the identity is \(\mathbf1_{j=0,x=e}\).
The section unitaries together with the central functions generate the decomposable regular algebra with these fibers, since \(Q\) is countable and the section unitaries generate the fiber algebras.
The vertex algebras have the corresponding fibers generated by their \(Q_i\) unitaries.
In each fiber any alternating product of centered vertex group polynomials has trace zero, since every word in nonidentity alternating quotient syllables has nonidentity product in \(Q\).
Bounded strong approximation gives free independence of the two vertex von Neumann algebras for the fiber trace.

An affiliated vertex operator disintegrates by taking its bounded transform and recovering its measurable graph.
Its kernel projection and its adjoint kernel projection therefore disintegrate fiberwise, and the trace of either projection is the integral of its fiber traces.
In particular each \(k_i\) and its affiliated inverse act as mutually inverse affiliated operators on a common conull set of fibers.
All operators in the argument may be evaluated there using closed affiliated sums and products, as follows from their graphs or the arithmetic of measurable operators in a finite algebra.
Each \(r_i\) has infinite order, so its regular law is Haar and every nonzero polynomial denominator defining \(f_i(r_i)\) is an affiliated unit.

## The largest point kernels in the two fibers

In a fiber write \(R_i\) for the unitary representing \(r_i\).
Its \(m_i\)-th power equals \(\zeta\), and \(r_i^j\notin Z\) when \(0<j<m_i\), since \(r_i^j=z^k\) would give \(r_i^{j-m_i k}=e\).
It follows that the fiber trace of \(R_i^j\) is zero for \(0<j<m_i\).
For each \(w\) with \(w^{m_i}=\zeta\), the spectral projection \(m_i^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{m_i-1}(w^{-1}R_i)^j\) thus has trace \(1/m_i\).

Let \(L_i\) be the subgroup of \(m_i\)-th roots of unity \(\omega\) for which \(f_i(\omega w)=f_i(w)\) as rational functions.
For every \(\omega\notin L_i\), equality can hold at only finitely many \(w\) outside the poles.
Discard the images under \(w\mapsto w^{m_i}\) of these finitely many exceptions and of all poles on the unit circle, for the finitely many choices of \(i,\omega\).
In each remaining fiber the classes of roots giving an equal value of \(f_i\) are exactly the orbits of \(L_i\).
Consequently every point kernel of positive trace for \(f_i(R_i)\) has trace \(|L_i|/m_i\).
The fixed field of multiplication by all \(m_i\)-th roots of unity is \(\mathbb C(w^{m_i})\), since \(1,w,\ldots,w^{m_i-1}\) is a basis over that field and the generator acts on these basis vectors by distinct roots of unity.
Thus either \(f_i(w)=F_i(w^{m_i})\) and \(f_i(R_i)=F_i(\zeta)\), or every point kernel has trace at most \(1/2\).

Set \(X_i=k_i^{-1}f_i(r_i)k_i\), so \(S=k_2(X_2-X_1)\).
In each of the indicated fibers, multiplication by \(k_i\) gives an isomorphism of affiliated right kernel modules for \(X_i-\lambda\) and \(f_i(R_i)-\lambda\), for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\).
The associated trace dimensions agree, including for unbounded conjugators.
Write \(\nu_\zeta\) for the normalized fiber kernel dimension.
The [free-difference formula for arbitrary affiliated scalars](Yin_C_A0115_v1.md#two-bounded-graph-rows) gives
\[
 \nu_\zeta(X_2-X_1)=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb C}
       \bigl(\nu_\zeta(X_1-\lambda)+\nu_\zeta(X_2-\lambda)-1\bigr)_+ .
\]
The fiber freeness comes from the quotient normal forms, and the formula permits nonnormal \(X_i\).
If both rotation stabilizers are proper, the preceding upper bounds make the right side zero, including when both bounds are \(1/2\).
If exactly one function, say \(f_1\), descends to \(F_1\), the only point to test is \(\lambda=F_1(\zeta)\).
The nonzero rational function \(f_2(w)-F_1(w^{m_2})\) has only finitely many zeros away from its poles, so its roots exclude only finitely many central characters.
Interchanging the indices proves the other mixed case.
If both functions descend, then \(X_2-X_1=F_2(z)-F_1(z)\) by centrality.
This is zero precisely in (1) and an affiliated unit otherwise by the Haar law of \(z\).
Integration proves zero kernel trace in every remaining case.
Faithfulness and polar decomposition in the finite regular algebra then give the claimed affiliated inverse.

## A pivot with unbounded inverse and two quotients outside the group ring

For integers \(m,n\ge2\) take the two HNN extensions
\[
 H_1=\langle r,t\mid tr^mt^{-1}=r^m\rangle,\qquad
 H_2=\langle s,u\mid us^nu^{-1}=s^n\rangle
\]
and identify \(z=r^m=s^n\) in their amalgam.
Each HNN extension has infinite cyclic vertex group with the indicated subgroup embedded at both ends.
The normal form theorem embeds the vertex groups and gives \(z\) infinite order.
Any finite-order element acting without inversions on the Bass--Serre tree fixes a vertex, so the two HNN extensions and their amalgam are torsion-free.
The infinite-order elements \(t,u\) make \(k_1=1-t,\ k_2=1-u\) affiliated units, and the Haar spectral law of \(t\) gives \(1-t\) nonclosed range.
Taking \(f_1(w)=f_2(w)=w\) yields the injective scalar
\[
              s(1-u)-(1-u)(1-t)^{-1}r(1-t).
\]
If \(a^{-1}b\in\mathbb CG\), a finitely supported \(h\) would solve \((1-t)h=r-rt\).
The coefficients of the left side sum to zero on every orbit \(\langle t\rangle v\).
The right side meets two such orbits, since the reduced word for \(rt r^{-1}\) in \(Q=(C_m*\langle t\rangle)*(C_n*\langle u\rangle)\) lies outside \(\langle t\rangle\).
For \(ca^{-1}\in\mathbb CG\), the corresponding equation is \(h(1-t)=1-u\).
Its left side has zero coefficient sum on each orbit \(v\langle t\rangle\), whereas \(e,u\) lie in distinct orbits as seen in the same quotient.
Thus both group-ring quotient tests fail in this explicit matrix.
The nonsplit central root relations and independent affiliated similarities are the inputs beyond the [split central-parameter result](Yin_C_A0114_v1.md#two-rows-sharing-the-central-cyclic-subgroup) and the [cyclic-vertex scalar winding calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0019_v1.md#the-actual-group-and-the-winding-coordinate).
