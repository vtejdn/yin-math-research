## Statement

Let (M,tau), X=(X_1,...,X_d), H=L2(M,tau), Omega, P and bounded D_i satisfy the complete hypotheses of Yin_C_F0001.
Thus every X_i is normal, M=W*(X), and [D_i,lambda(X_j)]=delta_ij P.
Write U=U(M) for the unital algebra of closed densely defined affiliated operators and

\[
F_X=\operatorname{Div}_{U}(\mathbb C\langle X\rangle).
\]

Then for every f in M,

\[
\bigl(\operatorname{rank}[D_i,\lambda(f)]<\infty\ \text{for all }i\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f\in F_X.
\]

This does not assert membership in Div_M C<X>, nor an equality F_X intersect M = Div_M C<X>.

## External input and conventions

[MSY23 source interfaces](../audits/literature/MSY23/readme.md#accepted-inputs) checks the published version supplied as MSY23: Proposition 6.2, pp. 47-48 (arXiv v2 p. 35), says existence of a bounded dual system implies Delta(X)=d, with no selfadjointness assumption.
Its convention is [X_i,D_j]=delta_ij P; replacing our D_j by -D_j gives exactly that convention.
Theorem 1.1(i),(v), published pp. 3-4 (arXiv v2 p. 3), then identifies F_X with an embedded free skew field and in particular proves that F_X is a unital division subring of U.
The earlier arXiv interface remains recorded in [Miy26-U004](../audits/literature/Miy26/Yin_U004.md#miy26-u004).
The main free-field theorem is an accepted external input, not a newly audited full source proof.
The published ambient-algebra distinction is checked in the MSY23 source index.

The finite-tracial affiliated algebra U contains M and the canonical L2(M,tau) realization.
Multiplication of an L2 affiliated operator by a bounded element agrees with the left/right Hilbert-space actions.
Tensor products below are algebraic unless marked otherwise.
We use an inner product linear in the second variable.

Let rho(a) be right multiplication, rho(a) b-hat = (ba)-hat.
Thus rho is a linear anti-homomorphism, rho(a) commutes with lambda(M), and rho(a)=J lambda(a*) J.
For bounded a, rho(a)Omega=lambda(a)Omega and P rho(a)=P lambda(a), the latter by the trace identity tau(ba)=tau(ab).

## 1. Fundamental operator identity for every bounded f

Define the bounded operator

\[
K=\sum_{i=1}^d[\rho(X_i),D_i].
\]

Because rho(X_i) commutes with lambda(X_j), the Jacobi identity gives

\[
[K,\lambda(X_j)]
=\sum_i[\rho(X_i),[D_i,\lambda(X_j)]]
=[\rho(X_j),P]
=[\lambda(X_j),P].
\]

Therefore K+P commutes with each lambda(X_j).
Each lambda(X_j) is normal, so the exact Fuglede theorem implies K+P also commutes with lambda(X_j)*.
Hence it commutes with the von Neumann algebra generated by those operators, namely lambda(M).
For every f in M,

\[
\boxed{
[\lambda(f),P]
=[K,\lambda(f)]
=\sum_i[\rho(X_i),[D_i,\lambda(f)]].
}
\tag{1}
\]

The last equality again uses [rho(X_i),lambda(f)]=0.
This argument uses exact commutation in Fuglede, never a version modulo finite rank.
It avoids any assertion that C<X> is L2-dense or weakly dense in M.

### Exact normal commutation and constants

For completeness, the exact Fuglede fact used above has a short proof.
If a bounded T commutes with a bounded normal N, the operator-valued entire function

\[
F(z)=e^{zN^*}T e^{-zN^*}
=e^{zN^*-\bar zN}T e^{-zN^*+\bar zN}
\]

has norm at most ||T||: normality makes the exponents on the right skewadjoint.
The equality follows by inserting the commuting exponentials involving N on the two sides of T.
Every scalar matrix coefficient is bounded and entire, so Liouville's theorem makes F constant.
Differentiating at zero gives [N*,T]=0.
No finite-rank version of Fuglede is invoked.

Equation (1) also resolves the common-kernel issue: if [D_i,f]=0 for every i, then [f,P]=0, and evaluation at Omega gives f=tau(f)1.
Thus the maximal bounded derivatives determine a bounded primitive up to an additive scalar.
This does not produce a bounded rational representative.

## 2. Convert the finite-rank identity to finite tensors

Use the Yin_C_F0002 map

\[
\Theta(g\otimes h)\zeta=g\langle Jh,\zeta\rangle,
\qquad \Theta:H\odot H\longrightarrow\operatorname{FinRank}(H).
\]

This is a linear bijection; for bounded g,h it equals lambda(g) P lambda(h).
Every finite-rank commutator admits a finite representation

\[
[D_i,\lambda(f)]=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n_i}\Theta(g_{i\alpha}\otimes h_{i\alpha}),
\qquad g_{i\alpha},h_{i\alpha}\in L^2(M,\tau).
\]

For arbitrary L2 g,h and bounded x,

\[
[\rho(x),\Theta(g\otimes h)]
=\Theta(gx\otimes h-g\otimes xh).
\tag{2}
\]

To check the second term, rho(x)*Jh = rho(x*)Jh = J(xh); thus Theta(g tensor h) rho(x)=Theta(g tensor xh).
The first term follows from rho(x)g=gx.
All products in (2) remain in L2.

As [lambda(f),P]=Theta(f tensor 1-1 tensor f), injectivity of Theta and (1)-(2) yield the exact algebraic identity in H tensor_C H:

\[
f\otimes1-1\otimes f
=\sum_{i,\alpha}\bigl(g_{i\alpha}X_i\otimes h_{i\alpha}
-g_{i\alpha}\otimes X_i h_{i\alpha}\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]

The canonical injection H into U induces an injection H tensor_C H into U tensor_C U, since tensoring vector spaces over a field preserves injections.
Therefore (3) remains an identity in U tensor_C U.
No completed tensor product or tensor topology is used in this step.

## 3. Balanced tensor descent to the division subring

Map (3) under the canonical quotient

\[
U\otimes_{\mathbb C}U\longrightarrow U\otimes_{F_X}U.
\]

The first U is a right F_X-module and the second a left F_X-module.
Since X_i belongs to F_X, every summand on the right of (3) vanishes by the balancing relation (g X_i) tensor h = g tensor (X_i h).
Consequently,

\[
f\otimes_{F_X}1=1\otimes_{F_X}f.
\tag{4}
\]

We use the elementary equalizer fact: for a unital division subring F of a nonzero unital ring U, an element u in U satisfying u tensor_F 1 = 1 tensor_F u belongs to F.
Indeed, if u is not in F, the vectors 1,u are right-F-linearly independent: a+ub=0 with b nonzero would imply u=-ab^{-1} in F, and b=0 forces a=0.
Extend {1,u} to a right-F basis of U and choose a right-F-linear map phi:U to F with phi(1)=0 and phi(u)=1.
Applying phi tensor id to the asserted equality, and identifying F tensor_F U with U, gives 1=0, a contradiction.
The choice of a vector-space basis is purely algebraic; continuity is neither available nor required.

Apply this fact to F=F_X and u=f in (4).
It gives f in F_X, proving the statement.

## Exact remaining gap for Yin_C_F0001

The established target here is an affiliated free-field expression whose intermediate inverses may be unbounded.
A bounded final element f does not by itself supply an expression with every intermediate inverse bounded.
The remaining descent would need to prove, at least for the finite-rank class under consideration,

\[
f\in F_X\cap M\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f\in\operatorname{Div}_M\mathbb C\langle X\rangle,
\]

or supply another direct bounded representation.
This draft does not claim that descent or the reverse inclusion from all of F_X intersect M into the finite-rank class.
The full Yin_C_F0001 is therefore not closed by this argument.
