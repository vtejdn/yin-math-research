## Statement and Scope

Let B be a nonzero unital complex C*-algebra and let A be a unital complex subalgebra with the same identity.
Define

\[
R=\operatorname{Div}_B A
\]

using only finitely many algebraic operations and scalar inversions in B, as in [Yin_C_F0001](Yin_C_F0001_v1.md#yin_c_f0001).
No norm closure or affiliated inversion is used.
Assume

\[
A^*\subseteq R.
\tag{1}
\]

Then R is closed under adjoints, and for every n>=1 and every T in M_n(R),

\[
T\text{ invertible in }M_n(B)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T^{-1}\in M_n(R).
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, (1) holds whenever A is a unital *-subalgebra.
More generally, every unital *-subalgebra R of B that is closed under scalar inverses in B is closed under matrix inverses of every finite size.
The hypotheses do not require that B be a von Neumann algebra or finite, or that a trace, dual system, normal generators, or any density assumption be present.

The proof is constructive.
For an invertible n by n matrix T, it forms T* T and eliminates its positive Schur complements.
There are n scalar pivot inversions, each applied to a uniformly positive element already in R.
These are n additional pivot inversions after expressions for the entries of T and T* have been supplied.
No matrix inverse is used as an unexplained scalar-closure operation.

## 1. Closure under Adjoints

Every element of R admits a finite scalar rational expression whose leaves are in A and whose inverted subexpressions are invertible in B.
Conversely, such expressions form an inverse-closed unital algebra containing A, so this expression description agrees with the smallest-subalgebra definition of division closure.

Adjoint a given expression, reversing the order of each product.
Its leaves now belong to A*, hence to R by (1).
If an inverted subexpression evaluates to an invertible a in B, then its adjoint a* is also invertible and

\[
(a^{-1})^*=(a^*)^{-1}.
\tag{3}
\]

Induction through the expression shows that its adjoint evaluates to an element of R.
Thus R*=R.
This does not adjoin a new operation to the definition of division closure: each adjointed leaf already has a finite admissible expression in the original algebra by (1), and all subsequent operations and inverses are admissible there.

In particular, for an algebra A generated by a tuple X, the condition that every X_j* belong to R implies (1), by reversing polynomial words and conjugating their scalar coefficients.
Conversely, if R is closed under adjoints, then it contains every X_j*.
The generator condition and adjoint closure of R are therefore equivalent.

## 2. Positive Scalar Pivots

Assume henceforth that R is a unital *-subalgebra of B closed under scalar inversion in B.
We prove by induction on n that

\[
H\in M_n(R),\quad H\ge\varepsilon I_n,
\quad\varepsilon>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H^{-1}\in M_n(R),
\tag{4}
\]

with a finite scalar-inversion construction.
Positivity and order here are those of the ambient C*-algebra M_n(B).
A positive element bounded below by epsilon times the identity is invertible: its spectrum is contained in `[epsilon, ||H||]`.
This elementary continuous-functional-calculus fact is used only to justify the existence and boundedness of the inverses.
It is not used to put arbitrary continuous functions of H into R.

For n=1, the scalar inverse exists in B and belongs to R by definition.
For n>1, write

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b^*&C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a\in R,\quad b\in M_{1,n-1}(R),\quad C\in M_{n-1}(R).
\]

Compression to the first coordinate gives a>=epsilon 1.
Therefore a is a boundedly invertible scalar element of B and a^{-1} belongs to R.
Since a is positive, its inverse is selfadjoint.
Define

\[
S=C-b^*a^{-1}b\in M_{n-1}(R),
\qquad
V=\begin{pmatrix}-a^{-1}b\\I_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]

Direct multiplication in the indicated order gives

\[
HV=\begin{pmatrix}0\\S\end{pmatrix},
\qquad V^*HV=S.
\]

Compression by a rectangular matrix preserves positivity.
Consequently,

\[
S\ge\varepsilon V^*V
=\varepsilon\bigl(I_{n-1}+b^*a^{-2}b\bigr)
\ge\varepsilon I_{n-1}.
\tag{6}
\]

The last inequality follows because `b* a^{-2} b=(a^{-1}b)*(a^{-1}b)` is positive.
Thus the Schur complement retains the same lower bound epsilon.
It lies over R, so induction constructs S^{-1} entrywise in R using n-1 further bounded scalar pivots.

For completeness, the ordered factorization is

\[
H=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\b^*a^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&S\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&a^{-1}b\\0&I\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7}
\]

The two triangular factors have explicit inverses obtained by changing the signs of their off-diagonal blocks.
Multiplying the three inverse factors gives

\[
H^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^{-1}+a^{-1}bS^{-1}b^*a^{-1}&-a^{-1}bS^{-1}\\
-S^{-1}b^*a^{-1}&S^{-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{8}
\]

Every entry is in R.
Equations (5)–(8) are valid without commutativity of the entries.
They complete the induction.
Each scalar pivot is at least epsilon times the identity and hence has inverse norm at most epsilon^{-1}; no row permutation or selection of a merely nonzero pivot is needed.

## 3. Arbitrary Invertible Matrices and Rational Closure

Let T in M_n(R) be invertible in M_n(B).
Its Gram matrix H=T* T belongs to M_n(R), because R is closed under adjoints, and

\[
H\ge\varepsilon I_n,
\qquad \varepsilon=\|T^{-1}\|^{-2}>0.
\tag{9}
\]

This bound can be checked in a faithful Hilbert-space representation from `||T xi|| >= ||T^{-1}||^{-1} ||xi||`, or by the positive invertibility of T* T.
Section 2 constructs H^{-1} with entries in R.
Since

\[
T^{-1}=(T^*T)^{-1}T^*,
\tag{10}
\]

equation (2) follows.
The norm in (9) is used to prove that the algorithm's pivots exist.
The construction does not require prior knowledge of the value of this norm or access to the entries of T^{-1}.

The [MSY23-U003 definition and closure interface](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U003.md#msy23-u003) identifies rational closure with the smallest unital subalgebra closed under inverses of all square matrices in the same ambient algebra.
Equation (2) therefore gives the same-ambient equality

\[
\operatorname{Rat}_B A=\operatorname{Div}_B A
\quad\text{under (1).}
\tag{11}
\]

Indeed, rational closure contains the scalar division closure, while the matrix-inverse-closed algebra R contains all entries of every admissible matrix inverse over A.
This application does not assume that R is a division ring: a nonzero element of R can fail to be invertible in B.
The positive-pivot proof supplies the required stronger closure property directly.

## 4. Application to the Original Bounded Target

Return to Yin_C_F0001 and write R_X=Div_M C<X>.
If

\[
X_j^*\in R_X\qquad(1\le j\le d),
\tag{12}
\]

then R_X is closed under adjoints and Yin_C_F0008 applies.
Suppose a bounded scalar f has a representation

\[
f=p+uT^{-1}v,
\quad p\in R_X,
\quad u\in M_{1,n}(R_X),\quad v\in M_{n,1}(R_X),
\quad T\in M_n(R_X),
\tag{13}
\]

where T is invertible in M_n(M).
Then f belongs to R_X.
In particular, an affine polynomial pencil over the original X with bounded inverse and constant input/output vectors is sufficient.
Coefficients in arbitrary M are not covered by this conclusion.

Condition (12) holds for selfadjoint generators, and for unitary generators because X_j*=X_j^{-1} with a bounded inverse.
It also persists through any of the transformations in [Yin_C_F0006](Yin_C_F0006_v1.md#yin_c_f0006) whenever the initial closure has this property: Yin_C_F0006 proves equality of the original and transformed scalar division closures, so their common algebra remains closed under adjoints.
This uses equality of closures, not normality of the transformed tuple by itself.
The normality and other admissibility conditions in Yin_C_F0006 are still required when its transfer theorem for Yin_C_F0001 is invoked.

Normality alone does not supply (12).
The disk example in [Yin_C_F0003](Yin_C_F0003_v1.md#yin_c_f0003) satisfies the original normal and bounded-dual hypotheses but has an infinite-rank adjoint commutator.
The forward implication in [Yin_C_F0002](Yin_C_F0002_v1.md#yin_c_f0002) then excludes X* from the original R_X.
Replacing A_X by its *-algebra would change the target; Yin_C_F0008 does not make that replacement.

Thus Yin_C_F0008 resolves the scalar-elimination step once a bounded inverse representation has been constructed in the class (12).
It does not produce that representation from a bounded affiliated compression or from the L2 states of [Yin_C_F0007](Yin_C_F0007_v1.md#yin_c_f0007).
It also does not prove scalar elimination for every normal tuple or the cross-ambient equality `F_X intersect M=R_X`.
These remaining questions do not affect the complete bounded C*-algebra argument above.

## Checks, Dependencies, and Review Boundary

The proof was checked directly for n=1 and through the explicit noncommutative two-block factorization.
The n-step induction retains the lower bound epsilon, and every inverted object is a bounded scalar element in the current algebra.
A pivot need not be a unit merely because it is nonzero; uniform positivity supplies the exact stronger property used here.
No limiting operation contributes an element to R.

The direct theorem uses elementary C*-positivity, its spectral lower-bound criterion for scalar invertibility, and the reconstructed block identities.
No external free-field or minimal-realization theorem is needed.
The conventional rational-closure identification in (11) is the already audited same-ambient interface in MSY23-U003.
Local source comparison also inspected the [DR97 small-power scalar-elimination proof](../audits/literature/DR97/Yin_U004.md), which supplies a different sufficient condition without the adjoint hypothesis.
No new public search or source acquisition was needed for this direct positive-pivot proof, and no claim about its historical priority is made.

Prepared by another AI agent and independently reviewed by the coordinating agent in the linked audit.
Numerical computation, proof-assistant verification, external human review, and full new source auditing were not performed.
