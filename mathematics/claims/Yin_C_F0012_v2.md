# A one-sided product counterexample with bounded duals

This variant replaces the selfadjoint sandwich pair in [v1](Yin_C_F0012_v1.md#statement) by the pair \((h,bh)\).
It lowers the degree of the second generator and uses one affiliated inverse to recover the witness.
The second generator is not normal, and the polynomial algebra here does not include its adjoint.
The selfadjoint statement and its independent reviews remain at v1; they are not replaced by a normality assertion for this variant.
Provenance: project deduction from the standard tracial model of v1 and the argument below; no historical novelty claim is made.

## Statement of the variant

<a id="statement"></a>

Let \(h\) have law \(d\mu(t)=4t^3\,dt\) on \((0,1)\), and let \(b\) be a standard semicircular element, of variance one, free from \(h\).
Put
\[
 M=W^*(h,b),\qquad Y=bh,\qquad A=\mathbb C\langle h,Y\rangle.
\]
The notation \(A\) means the unital algebra of polynomials in the two displayed generators, without adjoining \(Y^*\).
For an ambient algebra \(B\), \(\operatorname{Rat}_B(A)\) denotes matrix rational closure: the smallest unital subalgebra containing \(A\) and closed under taking the entries of inverses of square matrices that are invertible over \(B\).

Then \(W^*(h,Y)=M\), and bounded operators \(D_h,D_Y\) on the whole standard tracial Hilbert space \(H=L^2(M,\tau)\) satisfy
\[
 [h,D_h]=[Y,D_Y]=P_\Omega,
 \qquad [h,D_Y]=[Y,D_h]=0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Nevertheless,
\[
 b=Yh^{-1}\in \operatorname{Rat}_{L^0(M)}(A)\cap M,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{dist}\bigl(b,\operatorname{Rat}_M(A)\bigr)\ge1.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the two rational closures in question differ even with a bounded dual system.
The construction is not a counterexample with normal generators or a statement about the polynomial *-algebra generated by this pair.

## Proof

### The standard tracial model and the inverse vector

Use the [explicit standard tracial Fock model of v1](Yin_C_F0012_v1.md#1-an-explicit-tracial-fock-model): with \(K=L^2((0,1),\mu)\),
\[
 H=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}K^{\bar\otimes(n+1)}.
\]
The vacuum is \(1\) in grade zero, left multiplication by \(h\) acts on the first factor, and \(b=\ell+\ell^*\), where \(\ell\) prepends \(1\).
On a positive grade, \(\ell^*\) contracts the first factor against \(1\); it vanishes on grade zero.
The model is the standard tracial representation, with bounded right multiplication \(\rho(c)\xi=\xi c\) for \(c\in M\).
In particular, \(\rho(b)\) commutes with every left multiplier.

The affiliated inverse \(q=h^{-1}\) exists and
\[
 \|q\|_2^2=\int_0^1 4t\,dt=2,
 \qquad qh=1.
 \tag{3}
\]
It is an unbounded affiliated operator, used below only as a vector defining a bounded functional.

### A bounded right annihilation operator

Define \(R_q\) to vanish on grade zero and, for \(n\ge1\), set
\[
 R_q(\xi_0\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_n)
 =\left(\int_0^1q(t)\xi_n(t)\,d\mu(t)\right)
     \xi_0\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives a bounded linear functional of norm \(\|q\|_2\) on the last factor.
Different input grades have orthogonal output grades, so \(R_q\) extends to all of \(H\) with norm \(\sqrt2\).
It commutes with left multiplication by \(h\), since it retains the first factor on every positive grade and is zero on grade zero.

The operators \(R_q\) and \(\ell\) commute on every positive grade.
On grade zero their commutator sends \(\xi\in K\) to \(\langle q,\xi\rangle\Omega\).
Also \([R_q,\ell^*]=0\): both compositions vanish on grades zero and one, and on higher grades they contract different factors.
Thus, with the inner product linear in the second argument,
\[
 [R_q,b]=|\Omega\rangle\langle q|,
 \qquad
 [R_q,Y]=|\Omega\rangle\langle q|h=P_\Omega.
 \tag{5}
\]
The last equality uses \(hq=\Omega\) in \(K\), not an unbounded composition defining a dual operator.

### The second bounded dual

The [scalar Hilbert-transform construction of v1](Yin_C_F0012_v1.md#3-a-bounded-dual-for-h-and-its-bounded-correction) gives a bounded \(D_0\) on \(K\) with
\[
 [D_0,h]=|1\rangle\langle1|,\qquad \|D_0\|\le4\pi.
\]
Explicitly its kernel is \(1/(t-s)\) against \(\mu\); boundedness follows by multiplying the unweighted finite-interval Hilbert transform on both sides by \(\sqrt{4t^3}\), after the unitary change of measure.
Only this accepted Hilbert-transform interface is used from the source argument.
Let \(T\) act by \(-D_0\) on the last factor of every grade.
First- and last-factor actions commute on positive grades, while the grade-zero scalar identity gives
\[
 [h,T]=P_\Omega,\qquad [b,T]=0,\qquad \|T\|\le4\pi.
 \tag{6}
\]
The second equality holds because prefix creation and first-factor contraction commute with the last-factor action, including grades zero and one.

Set
\[
 D_Y=-R_q,\qquad D_h=T+\rho(b)R_q.
 \tag{7}
\]
These are bounded on all of \(H\), with
\[
 \|D_Y\|\le\sqrt2,
 \qquad \|D_h\|\le4\pi+2\sqrt2.
\]
Equations (5)--(6) give \([h,D_h]=P_\Omega\), \([h,D_Y]=0\), and \([Y,D_Y]=P_\Omega\).
For the remaining commutator,
\[
 [Y,D_h]=bP_\Omega-\rho(b)P_\Omega=0,
\]
because \(\rho(b)\Omega=b\Omega\).
This proves (1).
It also gives the actual finite-rank identities
\[
 [b,D_Y]=|\Omega\rangle\langle q|,
 \qquad [b,D_h]=-|b\Omega\rangle\langle q|,
 \tag{8}
\]
both of rank one.

### Generation and affiliated rational membership

Let \(\pi_\varepsilon=1_{[\varepsilon,1]}(h)\).
The bounded function \(h^{-1}\pi_\varepsilon\) belongs to \(W^*(h,Y)\), and
\[
 Y(h^{-1}\pi_\varepsilon)=b\pi_\varepsilon\longrightarrow b
\]
strongly, with a uniform norm bound of two.
Thus \(W^*(h,Y)=M\).
Since \(h\) has zero kernel, its inverse is available in \(L^0(M)\), and the affiliated product \(Yh^{-1}\) equals the bounded element \(b\).
This proves the membership in (2).

### Exclusion from every bounded matrix rational expression

For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), put
\[
 e_\varepsilon=1_{(0,\varepsilon]}(h),
 \qquad \xi_\varepsilon=e_\varepsilon\Omega/\sqrt{\tau(e_\varepsilon)}.
\]
These are unit vectors, since \(\tau(e_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^4\).
They satisfy \(\|h\xi_\varepsilon\|\le\varepsilon\) and \(\|Y\xi_\varepsilon\|\le2\varepsilon\).
Every nonempty word in \(h,Y\) consequently tends to zero on \(\xi_\varepsilon\), by applying its rightmost letter first.

Consider the unital algebra
\[
 \mathcal B=\{a\in M:\text{there is }c\in\mathbb C
       \text{ with }\|(a-c)\xi_\varepsilon\|\to0\}.
\]
The scalar \(c\) is unique, and \(a\mapsto c\) is a unital algebra homomorphism, denoted \(\chi\).
For multiplication this follows from
\[
 (ab-\chi(a)\chi(b))\xi_\varepsilon
 =a(b-\chi(b))\xi_\varepsilon
   +\chi(b)(a-\chi(a))\xi_\varepsilon.
\]
We have \(A\subset\mathcal B\).

This algebra is closed under all matrix inversions available in \(M\).
Indeed, take \(Q\in M_n(\mathcal B)\) invertible in \(M_n(M)\), and write \(Q_0=(\chi(Q_{ij}))\).
If \(Q_0v=0\) for a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb C^n\), then
\[
 Q(v\otimes\xi_\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0,
\]
contradicting the uniform lower bound \(\|Q\eta\|\ge\|Q^{-1}\|^{-1}\|\eta\|\).
Hence \(Q_0\) is invertible.
For every \(v\in\mathbb C^n\),
\[
 Q^{-1}(v\otimes\xi_\varepsilon)
 -(Q_0^{-1}v)\otimes\xi_\varepsilon
 =Q^{-1}\bigl[v\otimes\xi_\varepsilon
          -Q((Q_0^{-1}v)\otimes\xi_\varepsilon)\bigr]
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Taking the coordinate vectors \(v\) shows that every entry of \(Q^{-1}\) belongs to \(\mathcal B\).
Therefore \(\operatorname{Rat}_M(A)\subset\mathcal B\).

Freeness and \(\tau(b)=0,\tau(b^2)=1\) give
\[
 \langle\xi_\varepsilon,b\xi_\varepsilon\rangle=0,
 \qquad \|b\xi_\varepsilon\|=1.
\]
For any \(a\in\mathcal B\), with \(c=\chi(a)\), it follows that
\[
 \|b-a\|\ge\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
                    \|(b-a)\xi_\varepsilon\|
 =\sqrt{1+|c|^2}\ge1.
\]
This proves the distance assertion in (2), including its matrix rational scope.

### Scope of the simplification

Conditional expectation onto \(W^*(h)\) gives
\[
 E_{W^*(h)}(Y^*Y)=h^2,
 \qquad E_{W^*(h)}(YY^*)=\tau(h^2)1.
\]
The two differ, so \(Y\) is not normal.
Moreover \(Y^*=hb\) is not among the generators of \(A\); adding it invalidates the small-vector argument, since \(\|hb\xi_\varepsilon\|_2^2=\tau(h^2)=2/3\).
No claim is made here about \(\operatorname{Rat}_M(\mathbb C\langle h,Y,Y^*\rangle)\), or about exclusion from \(C^*(h,Y)\).
For a counterexample with two selfadjoint generators, [v1](Yin_C_F0012_v1.md#statement) remains the established construction.
