### 1. Statement

Let $(M,\tau)$ be a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state, and let $a\in M$ be a nonzero R-diagonal element.
Put
\[
\sigma=\|a\|_2>0,\qquad
H_{\mathrm{hol}}=\overline{\mathbb C[a]}^{\,L^2(M)},\qquad
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{hol}}=\overline{\mathbb C[a]}^{\,\|\cdot\|_M},
\]
and define the scalar inverse closures
\[
F_a=\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal U(M)}\mathbb C[a],
\qquad
D_a=\operatorname{Div}_M\mathbb C[a].
\tag{1}
\]
These are closures under scalar inverses that exist in their indicated ambient algebras.
No assumption that every nonzero polynomial in $a$ is invertible in $\mathcal U(M)$ is made.
In particular, $F_a$ is not automatically identified with the whole abstract field $\mathbb C(z)$.

Regard L2 elements as their canonical affiliated operators.
Then
\[
F_a\cap H_{\mathrm{hol}}
=
D_a\cap H_{\mathrm{hol}}
=
D_a\cap\mathcal A_{\mathrm{hol}}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular,
\[
f\in F_a,\quad f\in H_{\mathrm{hol}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f\in D_a\cap\mathcal A_{\mathrm{hol}}\subseteq M.
\tag{3}
\]

The hypothesis uses the intrinsic closed subspace generated by the nonnegative powers of $a$.
It does not separately assume coefficient recognizability; the proof derives that property from scalar rationality.
The conclusion does not require that $a$ be normal, injective, or part of a tuple with bounded duals.

### 2. Regular denominators in the affiliated closure

Let
\[
S_a=\{q\in\mathbb C[z]:q(a)\text{ is invertible in }\mathcal U(M)\}.
\]
For bounded $q(a)$ in the finite von Neumann setting, this is the regular-denominator condition.
It excludes the zero polynomial and requires no bounded inverse in $M$.

The normalized monomials $(\sigma^{-n}a^n\Omega)_{n\ge0}$ are orthonormal by [Yin_C_F0017, Section 2](Yin_C_F0017_v1.md#yin_c_f0017).
Therefore polynomial evaluation at $a$ is injective: a polynomial which evaluates to zero has all coefficients zero.

All evaluated polynomials commute.
A polynomial which commutes with an invertible affiliated polynomial also commutes with its inverse, by multiplication in the unital algebra $\mathcal U(M)$.
Thus
\[
L_a=\{p(a)q(a)^{-1}:p\in\mathbb C[z],\ q\in S_a\}
\tag{4}
\]
is a commutative unital algebra containing $\mathbb C[a]$.
It is closed under every inverse that exists in $\mathcal U(M)$.
Indeed, if $x=p(a)q(a)^{-1}$ is invertible there, then $p(a)=xq(a)$ is invertible there, so $p\in S_a$ and
\[
x^{-1}=q(a)p(a)^{-1}\in L_a.
\]
Conversely every expression in (4) is an allowed scalar inverse expression.
Hence
\[
F_a=L_a.
\tag{5}
\]
This argument is in the affiliated algebra and does not use an everywhere-defined unbounded composition.

For a nonzero $f\in F_a$, choose (4) and cancel the usual polynomial greatest common divisor.
The reduced denominator remains regular.
To check this, if $q=rq_0$ and $q(a)$ is invertible, its commuting factors $r(a),q_0(a)$ are both invertible in $\mathcal U(M)$; for example an inverse of $q_0(a)$ is $q(a)^{-1}r(a)$.
Cancellation therefore gives
\[
f=p_0(a)q_0(a)^{-1},
\qquad p_0,q_0\in\mathbb C[z]\text{ coprime},\quad
q_0\in S_a,\quad p_0\ne0.
\tag{6}
\]

### 3. The positive-power subspace turns multiplication into a shift

Write
\[
e_n=\sigma^{-n}a^n\Omega,\qquad n\ge0.
\]
These vectors form an orthonormal basis of $H_{\mathrm{hol}}$.
On their finite linear span, left multiplication by $a$ satisfies
\[
ae_n=\sigma e_{n+1}.
\tag{7}
\]
Since $a$ is bounded, $H_{\mathrm{hol}}$ is invariant under its left action and (7) extends to the whole subspace.
Thus its restriction is exactly $\sigma$ times the unilateral shift.

Now assume $f\in F_a\cap H_{\mathrm{hol}}$ and $f\ne0$, and take (6).
Expand
\[
f\Omega=\sum_{n\ge0}\alpha_n e_n,
\qquad
\sum_{n\ge0}|\alpha_n|^2=\|f\|_2^2<\infty.
\tag{8}
\]
Because $q_0(a)$ is bounded, the affiliated identity
\[
q_0(a)f=p_0(a)
\]
is also an equality of L2 vectors under bounded left multiplication.
Formula (7) therefore identifies its coefficients with the formal power-series equation
\[
q_0(\sigma z)F(z)=p_0(\sigma z),
\qquad F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\alpha_n z^n.
\tag{9}
\]
Only multiplication by a polynomial is involved in deriving (9), so no convergence outside the Hilbert space or evaluation of an infinite series at an affiliated operator is being assumed.

If $q_0(0)=0$, coprimality implies $p_0(0)\ne0$.
The constant coefficient of (9) would instead give $0=p_0(0)$, a contradiction.
Thus $q_0(0)\ne0$, and (9) uniquely identifies $F$ as the rational formal series regular at zero
\[
F(z)=\frac{p_0(\sigma z)}{q_0(\sigma z)}.
\tag{10}
\]
Here the formal Taylor series is identified with the actual L2 coefficients by the invariant-subspace equation, not by regularity at zero alone.

### 4. Finite residual dimension and Yin_C_F0017

One can see finite residual dimension directly, without importing a further formal-series theorem.
Write
\[
q_0(\sigma z)=Q_0+Q_1z+\cdots+Q_rz^r,\qquad Q_0\ne0,
\quad P(z)=p_0(\sigma z).
\]
For $m\ge N:=\max\{1,r,\deg P+1\}$ and every $n\ge0$, equation (9) gives
\[
\alpha_{n+m}
=-Q_0^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{r}Q_j\alpha_{n+m-j}.
\tag{11}
\]
For $r=0$ the sum is empty and these coefficients vanish.
Hence the residual sequences
\[
R_m\alpha=(\alpha_{n+m})_{n\ge0}
\]
satisfy the same finite recurrence for $m\ge N$.
Their span is generated by $R_0\alpha,\ldots,R_{N-1}\alpha$ and is finite dimensional.

The coefficient sequence is square summable by (8).
A single nonzero bounded R-diagonal variable is the $d=1$ case of Yin_C_F0017, with its free-family assumptions vacuous.
Apply that theorem to the normalized monomials $e_n$.
It gives an absolutely convergent homogeneous operator-norm series with the same L2 vector, and places its sum in $D_a\cap\mathcal A_{\mathrm{hol}}$.
Uniqueness of the L2 representative identifies the sum with $f$.
The case $f=0$ is immediate.

Finally $D_a\subseteq F_a$, and operator-norm convergence implies L2 convergence, so $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{hol}}\subseteq H_{\mathrm{hol}}$.
These inclusions and (3) yield all equalities in (2).
QED.

### 5. Interpretation and sharp boundary

The positive subspace has substantive content.
For a Haar unitary $u$, it is the ordinary nonnegative Fourier-power Hardy subspace.
A bounded rational function of $u$ can lie outside it; the negative-power resolvent in Yin_C_F0017 gives an explicit example.
Thus (2) retains the intersection on its right-hand side and does not claim $D_a\subseteq H_{\mathrm{hol}}$.

More strongly, [Yin_C_F0019](Yin_C_F0019_v1.md#yin_c_f0019) supplies a bounded R-diagonal $a=uh$ with
\[
a^{-1}\in F_a\cap L^2(M)\setminus M.
\]
That inverse is orthogonal to $H_{\mathrm{hol}}$ by the gauge action, so it is excluded by the intrinsic hypothesis of this theorem.
Replacing $H_{\mathrm{hol}}$ in (2) by all of $L^2(M)$ is false.

For several noncommuting R-diagonal variables, the scalar fraction reduction (5) is unavailable in general.
Consequently this proof does not remove Yin_C_F0017's coefficient hypothesis for an arbitrary many-variable affiliated rational function.
That is a distinct remaining research question.

### 6. Verification boundary

The proof reduces scalar inverse expressions to regular polynomial denominators, verifies cancellation, identifies multiplication on the exact monomial subspace, and derives the constant recurrence from the L2 operator equation.
Yin_C_F0017 supplies its previously reviewed boundedness and scalar reconstruction conclusion.
