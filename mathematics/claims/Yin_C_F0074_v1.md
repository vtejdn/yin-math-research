# A circular canonical dual does not characterize holomorphic affiliated rationality

## Statement

There is a finite von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau)\), with faithful normal tracial state, generated by one bounded nonnormal operator \(c\), for which the complete ACSY24 Proposition 3.5 premise holds: on the represented star algebra \(A=\mathbb C\langle c,c^*\rangle\) there is an outer-bimodule derivation
\[
 \delta:A\longrightarrow L^2(M,\tau)\otimes_2L^2(M,\tau),
 \qquad
 \delta(c)=1\otimes1,\qquad
 1\otimes1\in\operatorname{Dom}\delta^* .                        \tag{1}
\]
The domain of this Hilbert-space adjoint is computed using the dense core \(A\Omega\), where \(\Omega=1\).

Its canonical right contraction
\[
 E_0(p\Omega)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta(p),\qquad p\in A,
\]
has a bounded closure \(E\) on all \(H=L^2(M,\tau)\).
The generator-first operator \(D=-E\) satisfies
\[
 [c,D]=P_\Omega,\qquad \operatorname{Dom}D
  =\operatorname{Dom}D^*=H.                                   \tag{2}
\]
Nevertheless \(u=c^*\) has
\[
 u\notin\operatorname{Div}_{L^0(M)}\mathbb C[c],                 \tag{3}
\]
while its fraction commutator is zero for every fraction pair.
Explicitly, if \(Q=(s,a,b,t)\in M^4\) satisfies
\[
 u=s^{-1}a=bt^{-1}
\]
with \(s,t\) invertible in the affiliated algebra \(L^0(M)\), then
\[
 \Gamma[Q,D]:=sDb-aDt=0\quad\hbox{on all }H.                    \tag{4}
\]

Thus the full ACSY24 premise, even together with boundedness of the specified canonical dual, does not imply the equivalence between holomorphic affiliated rationality and finite rank of the fraction commutator.
The counterexample fails that converse even with the quantifier "every fraction pair" and rank zero.

The associated boundary identity also fails.
For \(Q_0=(1,c^*,c^*,1)\),
\[
 [\rho(c),\Gamma[Q_0,D]]=0,\qquad
 \Gamma[Q_0,P_\Omega]\Omega=-c^*\Omega\ne0.                     \tag{5}
\]
Here \(\rho(c)\) is bounded right multiplication.

## Argument

### 1. Standard free Fock model and the bounded dual

Use the standard full Fock space
\[
 \mathcal H=\mathbb C\Omega\oplus
 \bigoplus_{n\ge1}(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}
\]
with orthonormal letters \(e_1,e_2\).
Let \(\ell_j,r_j\) be left and right creation.
Put
\[
 S_j=\ell_j+\ell_j^*,\qquad
 M=W^*(S_1,S_2).
\]
The standard tracial Fock identification gives a faithful normal vacuum trace on \(M\), identifies \(\mathcal H=L^2(M,\tau)\), and makes \(S_1,S_2\) a variance-one free semicircular pair.
This foundational model and normalization are the inspected [Miy22-U001](../audits/literature/Miy22/Yin_U001_v1.md#miy22-u001).
No statement about Brown measure is required.

On the word basis, right deletion commutes with left deletion, while its commutator with left creation is nonzero only on the vacuum.
Thus
\[
 [r_i^*,S_j]=\delta_{ij}P_\Omega.                               \tag{6}
\]
Define
\[
 c=\frac{S_1+\mathrm iS_2}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 E=\frac{r_1^*-\mathrm i r_2^*}{\sqrt2},\qquad D=-E.             \tag{7}
\]
These operators are bounded, \(E\Omega=0\), and (6) gives
\[
 [E,c]=P_\Omega,\qquad [E,c^*]=0,\qquad [c,D]=P_\Omega.          \tag{8}
\]
The two scalar combinations have respective coefficients \((1+(-\mathrm i)\mathrm i)/2=1\) and \((1+(-\mathrm i)(-\mathrm i))/2=0\).

The relations
\[
 S_1=(c+c^*)/\sqrt2,\qquad S_2=(c-c^*)/(\mathrm i\sqrt2)
\]
show \(W^*(c)=M\) and \(A=\mathbb C\langle c,c^*\rangle=\mathbb C\langle S_1,S_2\rangle\).
In particular \(A\Omega\) is dense in \(H\).

### 2. The actual starred derivation and its vacuum

Use the unitary coarse-tensor realization
\[
 \Theta:H\otimes_2H\longrightarrow S_2(H),\qquad
 \Theta(g\otimes h)=gP_\Omega h\quad(g,h\in M).
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
Its tracial convention is the inspected [Voi98-U005 Hilbert-Schmidt realization](../audits/literature/Voi98/Yin_U005_v1.md#voi98-u005).
It respects the outer bimodule actions.

For every \(p\in A\), expansion of the commutator using (6) gives a finite sum of operators \(gP_\Omega h\) with \(g,h\in A\).
Define on the represented algebra
\[
 \partial_jp=\Theta^{-1}([r_j^*,p]),\qquad
 \delta p=\frac{\partial_1p-\mathrm i\partial_2p}{\sqrt2}
          =\Theta^{-1}([E,p]).
                                                               \tag{10}
\]
This is well-defined on represented elements: a zero polynomial operator has zero commutator, and \(\Theta\) is injective.
The commutator product rule makes each map an outer-bimodule derivation.
Its values lie in the algebraic tensor core \(A\otimes_{\mathbb C}A\), hence also in the required Hilbert tensor product.
Equations (8) give
\[
 \delta(c)=1\otimes1,\qquad \delta(c^*)=0.                       \tag{11}
\]
The individual maps in (10) have the usual free difference quotient values \(\partial_jS_k=\delta_{jk}1\otimes1\).

The slice identity and \(E\Omega=0\) show for every \(p\in A\) that
\[
 (\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta(p)
 =[E,p]\Omega=E(p\Omega).                                     \tag{12}
\]
Moreover
\[
 \begin{split}
 \langle1\otimes1,\delta(p)\rangle
 &=\langle\Omega,[E,p]\Omega\rangle\\
 &=\langle E^*\Omega,p\Omega\rangle.
 \end{split}
\]
Since
\[
 E^*\Omega=\frac{e_1+\mathrm ie_2}{\sqrt2}=c\Omega,
\]
the representing vector is in \(H\), so the actual Hilbert adjoint satisfies
\[
 1\otimes1\in\operatorname{Dom}\delta^*,\qquad
 \delta^*(1\otimes1)=c\Omega.                                 \tag{13}
\]
In bilinear tracial notation this says \((\tau\otimes\tau)\delta(p)=\tau(c^*p)\).
The actual adjoint vector is \(c\), not \(c^*\).

Equations (11) and (13) verify the complete starred-algebra hypothesis of ACSY24 Proposition 3.5, with its exact interface recorded in [ACSY24-U001](../audits/literature/ACSY24/Yin_U001_v1.md#acsy24-u001).

Equation (12) identifies the canonical contraction \(E_0\) with \(E|_{A\Omega}\).
Since this is the restriction of a bounded operator to a dense domain, its Hilbert closure is exactly \(E\) on all \(H\).
Thus \(D=-E\) is precisely the generator-first canonical choice, not an unrelated extension.

### 3. Nonrationality from the affiliated centralizer

The operator \(c\) is nonnormal.
A direct calculation gives
\[
 cc^*-c^*c=\mathrm i(S_2S_1-S_1S_2),
\]
and hence
\[
 (cc^*-c^*c)\Omega
   =\mathrm i(e_2\otimes e_1-e_1\otimes e_2)\ne0.                \tag{14}
\]

Inside the associative affiliated algebra \(L^0(M)\), let
\[
 Z(c)=\{v\in L^0(M):vc=cv\}.
\]
It is a unital subalgebra containing \(\mathbb C[c]\).
If \(v\in Z(c)\) is invertible in \(L^0(M)\), multiplication of \(vc=cv\) by \(v^{-1}\) on both sides gives \(v^{-1}c=cv^{-1}\).
Thus \(Z(c)\) is division closed in the relevant scalar sense.
By the definition of division closure,
\[
 \operatorname{Div}_{L^0(M)}\mathbb C[c]\subseteq Z(c).
\]
Equation (14) excludes \(c^*\) from \(Z(c)\), proving (3).
This argument allows all inverses in the affiliated algebra and is not merely an exclusion from a bounded or norm-closed algebra.

### 4. Every fraction commutator vanishes, but the boundary does not

For \(u=c^*\), every fraction pair has \(b=ut\) and \(a=su\).
Since \(u,D,s,t\) are bounded, the defining expression is on all \(H\), and (8) gives
\[
 \Gamma[Q,D]
 =sDut-suDt=s[D,u]t=0.
                                                               \tag{15}
\]
This proves the assertion for every fraction pair, with no domain qualification or choice of closure.

For \(Q_0=(1,c^*,c^*,1)\), the commutator on the left of (5) is zero.
On the other hand,
\[
 \Gamma[Q_0,P_\Omega]=P_\Omega c^*-c^*P_\Omega.
\]
The circular element is centered, and \(c^*\Omega=(e_1-\mathrm ie_2)/\sqrt2\) is nonzero.
Therefore
\[
 \Gamma[Q_0,P_\Omega]\Omega=-c^*\Omega\ne0,
\]
proving (5).
The failure is already visible in bounded operators on a common full domain.

## Scope and self-check

The construction checks the entire ACSY premise and uses its specified canonical contraction.
Both the canonical operator and its adjoint are bounded.
Its witness is excluded from the holomorphic affiliated division closure, while all fraction commutators vanish.
It therefore disproves the proposed nonnormal rationality converse, not ACSY24's maximal-Delta conclusion.

[C_F0003](Yin_C_F0003_v1.md#counterexample-normal-multiplication-on-the-disk) is a different earlier counterexample: it uses a normal multiplication operator to test an adjoint-generated rational algebra.
That result is not a premise here.
