## 1. Outcome and Local Reuse

The proposed general implication from affiliated rational q-Gaussian evaluations to recognizable actual Wick coefficients is false, even for a bounded one-variable resolvent whose scalar rational expression is regular at zero.
Section 2 proves this exactly.
It does not refute either the rational L2-to-bounded assertion Q5 or the bounded division-closure assertion Q6: the example already belongs to the bounded scalar division closure.

For q-circular systems, holomorphic monomials do coincide with raw positive-letter Fock words.
Section 3 therefore proves a useful different interface: an actual holomorphic L2 solution of a finite linear pencil equation with invertible constant matrix has recognizable coefficients, and hence bounded scalar-rational entries by Yin_C_F0101.
The existence of such holomorphic state vectors is an additional premise, not a consequence of affiliated evaluation alone.

## 2. Yin_C_F0103: A Bounded Rational q-Gaussian Resolvent Has Nonrecognizable Wick Coefficients

Fix real q with 0<|q|<1 and a variance-one selfadjoint q-Gaussian s.
In the one-mode q-Fock space generated by a unit one-particle vector e, write
\[
e_n=e^{\otimes n},\quad e_0=\Omega,\quad
\|e_n\|_q^2=[n]_q!,\quad
s e_n=e_{n+1}+[n]_q e_{n-1},
\quad [n]_q=\frac{1-q^n}{1-q}.
\tag{1}
\]
The e_n are orthogonal, not normalized, in the q-inner product.
These are exactly the raw-word and q-Gaussian conventions in MS23-U006.

Choose zeta outside the spectrum of s; in particular |zeta|>||s|| suffices.
The element
\[
f=(\zeta-s)^{-1}\in\operatorname{Div}_M\mathbb C[s]
\]
is bounded.
Write its actual Wick expansion
\[
f\Omega=\sum_{n\ge0}\alpha_n e_n,
\qquad \sum_n|\alpha_n|^2[n]_q!<\infty.
\tag{2}
\]
Equivalently f has L2 expansion in the Wick polynomials W(e_n), with these same raw coefficients alpha_n.
They are not the coefficients in the power series in s and not the coefficients in the orthonormal q-Hermite basis.

Since s is bounded, apply it to the convergent partial sums in (2) and compare each fixed homogeneous degree.
From (zeta-s)fOmega=Omega and (1), with alpha_(-1)=0, one obtains
\[
\zeta\alpha_n-\alpha_{n-1}-[n+1]_q\alpha_{n+1}
=\delta_{n0}\qquad(n\ge0).
\tag{3}
\]
No infinite rearrangement is involved: each fixed output degree receives only its two neighboring input degrees, and the degree projection is continuous.

Let F(t)=sum_(n>=0) alpha_n t^n as an ordinary formal power series.
Multiplying (3) by t^n and summing coefficientwise gives the exact q-difference identity
\[
F(qt)=\bigl(1-(1-q)\zeta t+(1-q)t^2\bigr)F(t)+(1-q)t.
\tag{4}
\]
For example its coefficient of t is alpha_1=zeta alpha_0-1, agreeing with the vacuum equation in (3).
The constant terms cancel as required.

Suppose the raw coefficient sequence were recognizable on its one-letter alphabet.
It would have a finite scalar realization alpha_n=lambda A^n gamma, so
\[
F(t)=\lambda(I-tA)^{-1}\gamma
\]
would be a rational function regular at zero.
Equation (4), initially an identity of formal expansions at zero, would then be an identity of rational functions.

If F has a finite pole, choose one p with maximal absolute value.
Regularity at zero gives p nonzero.
The left side F(qt) has a pole at t=p/q.
Its modulus is strictly larger than that of every pole of F, because 0<|q|<1.
The right side of (4) is holomorphic there: multiplication by a polynomial and addition of a polynomial cannot introduce a new finite pole.
This is a contradiction.
Hence F has no finite poles, and a rational F must be a polynomial.

If this polynomial is nonzero of degree N>=0, the right side of (4) has degree N+2 with nonzero leading coefficient (1-q)alpha_N, whereas its left side has degree N.
The added term (1-q)t cannot cancel that leading term.
The zero polynomial also fails (4).
This final contradiction proves that F is not rational and that the actual raw Wick coefficients are not recognizable.

The argument works unchanged for negative q: only nonzero q and |q|<1 were used in the pole comparison, and 1-q is nonzero.
The undeformed case q=0 is excluded, since the left side would be F(0) rather than a rescaled rational function; no conclusion against the free semicircular result is drawn.

This also gives an obstruction in any finite q-Gaussian tuple by taking its first coordinate.
The vector (zeta-s_1)^(-1)Omega lies in the one-letter sub-Fock space.
Different letter-count subspaces are orthogonal because the symmetrizer only permutes letters.
Thus the full raw coefficient series is supported on words consisting only of that letter.
A recognizable full-alphabet series would restrict to a recognizable one-letter series, contradicting the proof above.

There is no contradiction with the recognizable formal Taylor series of the rational function r(x)=(zeta-x)^(-1), namely sum_(n>=0) zeta^(-n-1)x^n when zeta is nonzero.
Those coefficients evaluate in ordinary powers s^n.
The powers create lower Wick degrees through annihilation, so the actual coefficients alpha_n in (2) differ.
The Wick transformation destroys recognizability in this example.
Yin_C_F0097 had shown that the energy of some recognizable coefficients is not a constant-recurrence sequence; the present assertion is different and stronger as an obstruction to the evaluation interface: these actual coefficients themselves are not recognizable.

## 3. A Positive Actual-State Interface for Holomorphic q-Circular Pencils

Let c_1,...,c_d be the canonical q-circular system for real -1<q<1, in the convention
\[
c_i=\ell_q(e_i)+\ell_q(f_i)^*,
\]
where all e_i,f_i are orthonormal one-particle vectors.
Denote by H_+ the closed Fock subspace generated by the positive words e_w.
Each c_i restricts on H_+ to literal left creation: its annihilation term kills that subspace.
Raw words in one degree remain nonorthogonal when q is nonzero, but they are a linear basis of that finite-dimensional degree space.

Let B_0,...,B_d be N by N complex matrices with B_0 invertible, let Gamma be an N by m complex matrix, and suppose an actual matrix Y with entries in L2(M) satisfies
\[
\left(B_0\otimes1+\sum_{i=1}^d B_i\otimes c_i\right)Y=\Gamma\otimes1,
\qquad Y_{ab}\Omega\in H_+\quad\text{for every }a,b.
\tag{5}
\]
The equation is a genuine L2 equation; its left factor is bounded.
In particular it applies to an affiliated inverse pencil times Gamma if all those specified state entries are L2 and holomorphic.
It does not follow merely from one scalar compression of that inverse being L2.

Set A_i=-B_0^(-1)B_i and gamma=B_0^(-1)Gamma.
Expanding YOmega in the positive raw-word basis, write its N by m coefficient matrix as y_w.
Boundedness of the pencil and continuity of each finite-degree coordinate allow comparison of coefficients in (5).
Since left creation prefixes its letter, the resulting equations are
\[
y_\varnothing=\gamma,\qquad y_{iw}=A_i y_w.
\]
Induction therefore gives
\[
y_w=A_w\gamma.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus each entry of the actual expansion is recognizable, with an explicit finite realization.
Minimality is not assumed; Yin_C_F0101 reduces each scalar realization to its observable and reachable part when needed.
The actual L2 hypothesis in (5) supplies precisely the q-Gram summability required by that result, without any comparison with the ordinary coefficient norm.

Yin_C_F0101 now reconstructs each entry in operator norm as an element of
\[
\operatorname{Div}_M\mathbb C\langle c_1,\ldots,c_d\rangle.
\tag{7}
\]
Its vector equals the original Y_ab Omega by (6) and convergence.
Faithfulness of the tracial L2 realization identifies this bounded operator with the original affiliated Y_ab.
In particular any fixed complex row compression of Y has the same bounded scalar-rational conclusion.

This is an interface from actual linear-equation states to the accepted recognizable-series theorem.
The state vectors are not presumed equal to a formal Taylor expansion; equation (5) and their membership in H_+ prove that equality.
No bounded inverse of the entire pencil is asserted unless the hypotheses supply every column of its actual inverse.
A polynomial right-hand side can be included by finite state augmentation and the already accepted rational/recognizable equivalence, but that extension is not needed for (5)-(7).

The hypotheses distinguish two remaining difficulties.
First, an affiliated rational output need not initially supply L2 entries for its entire state column.
The existing Yin_C_F0007 interface supplies certain L2 left and right states under its stated bounded-dual, finite-commutator and minimal monic hypotheses; it does not supply every inverse entry or every more general input tuple.
Second, L2 state entries need not have been shown to lie in H_+.
Neither difficulty is resolved by minimal finite-dimensional realization theory alone.

## 4. Nonzero Matrix Centres Do Not Automatically Supply the Fock Branch

The primary search located Porat and Vinnikov, *Realizations of non-commutative rational functions around a matrix centre, I: synthesis, minimal realizations and evaluation on stably finite algebras*, [arXiv:1905.11304v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11304v2), dated 25 March 2021, 51 pages.
The introduction's Theorems 1-2 and surrounding explanation on pp. 2-5 were read.
They provide scalar-origin realizations for functions regular there and matrix-centred realizations for arbitrary noncommutative rational functions, with the stated evaluation compatibility over stably finite algebras.
This record treats that statement as a source lead and algebraic comparison; it does not newly audit the full realization proofs or import a Hilbert-space convergence theorem from them.
Volcic's [arXiv:1505.07472](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.07472) was also located as the matrix-coefficient realization lead; no additional result from it is consumed.

A concrete example, also singled out in Porat-Vinnikov's introduction, is
\[
r(x_1,x_2)=(x_1x_2-x_2x_1)^{-1}.
\tag{8}
\]
It has no scalar point of definition: every scalar commutator vanishes.
It does have a matrix point, for example
\[
Y_1=E_{12},\qquad Y_2=E_{21},\qquad
[Y_1,Y_2]=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).
\]
This directly verifies a legitimate expansion point for the displayed expression.
At that point one expands in the matrix increments Z_i=X_i-Y_i.
To evaluate at an amplified q-circular tuple, the increments instead become I_2 tensor c_i minus Y_i tensor 1.
They have degree-zero matrix terms -Y_i as well as creation terms.
A power of these increments is consequently not a single homogeneous positive-word tensor.
No degreewise convergence or identification with (6) follows from the mere existence of the matrix-centred formal expansion.

After expanding the finite linear maps of a matrix-centred pencil at this evaluation, one may obtain an ordinary matrix pencil in the c_i with a new constant matrix.
That new constant matrix is not automatically invertible.
If it is invertible and the actual solution states meet (5), Section 3 applies; if either premise fails, a new argument is needed.
Moving the formal expansion point does not by itself establish either premise.

There is an exact grading obstruction for the example (8).
The nonzero positive polynomial p=c_1c_2-c_2c_1 is homogeneous of degree two on H_+.
Therefore
\[
p H_+\subseteq\bigoplus_{n\ge2}H_n^+,
\]
which is orthogonal to Omega.
No xi in H_+ can satisfy p xi=Omega.
If the affiliated inverse of p exists and happens to be L2, its vacuum vector must thus lie outside H_+.
This is a conditional obstruction to the holomorphic-state premise, not an assertion that this inverse is L2 or a counterexample to Q5.
It demonstrates exactly why a matrix-centred rational expression cannot simply be fed into the positive-word conclusion of Yin_C_F0101.

Even the free circular holomorphic-invariance proof cannot be copied with ordinary negative-letter deletion.
Let d_f be literal deletion of a final f-letter in the one-coordinate model c=ell_q(e)+ell_q(f)*.
On the two-letter vector f tensor f,
\[
[d_f,c]\bigl(f\otimes f\bigr)=q\Omega.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed the creation terms cancel, while the two annihilation coefficients are [2]_q=1+q and 1.
The operator d_f is bounded by the already checked deletion estimate, but it fails to commute with c when q is nonzero.
The corresponding free identity used in Miy26's circular analysis is therefore unavailable in this form.
This calculation does not exclude a different bounded intertwiner or a different proof of holomorphic membership.

## 5. Exact Remaining Route and Handoff

For selfadjoint q-Gaussians, a universal recognizable-Wick hypothesis is ruled out by Section 2.
A viable replacement must accommodate the actual q-dependent recurrence, or a different graded expansion.
Equation (4) gives a concrete q-difference model already forced by the simplest bounded rational resolvent; it is a possible test for such a replacement, not a proof of a general multivariable decay theorem.

For holomorphic q-circular inputs, Yin_C_F0101 and Section 3 reduce a useful class to two explicit analytic obligations: find sufficient actual L2 realization states, and place them in H_+.
Origin regularity, matrix-centred regularity, and affiliated invertibility are separate algebraic facts and do not silently discharge those obligations.
A scalar or matrix state equation satisfying (5) does discharge the coefficient-identification step completely.

The boundedness and bounded scalar division-closure goals remain distinct for general affiliated rational evaluations.
In particular no bounded-output implies bounded-whole-inverse assertion is inferred from the new realization search.
The present round stops at the exact nonrecognizable-resolvent obstruction and the proved conditional state interface.
Author checks cover raw normalization, the vacuum recurrence, the formal q-difference identity, negative q, pole cancellation, degree-zero terms, coordinate order, actual L2 equality, and the matrix-centre example.
Independent review is pending.
