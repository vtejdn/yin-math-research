## 1. Precise statement and reuse boundary

Let \((\mathcal M,\tau)\) be a finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial probability state, generated by freely independent projections \(p_1,\ldots,p_d\).
Put
\[
 A=\mathbb C\langle p_1,\ldots,p_d\rangle.
\]
The candidate assertion is
\[
 \operatorname{Div}_{L^0(\mathcal M)}A\cap\mathcal M
       =\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal M}A.
 \tag{1}
\]
The closures in (1) use scalar inverses of the elements invertible in the indicated ambient algebra.
No claim that the left affiliated closure is a division ring is made.

A projection of trace zero or one is scalar by faithfulness and may be omitted.
Thus assume \(0<\alpha_i=\tau(p_i)<1\); an empty remaining family gives the scalar assertion immediately.
Define
\[
 x_i=\frac{p_i-\alpha_i1}{\sqrt{\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)}},
 \qquad
 \kappa_i=\frac{1-2\alpha_i}{\sqrt{\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)}}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Then \(x_i=x_i^*\), \(\tau(x_i)=0\), \(\tau(x_i^2)=1\), and
\[
 x_i^2=1+\kappa_i x_i.
 \tag{3}
\]
The two families generate exactly the same unital polynomial algebra \(A\).
Constants in estimates below may depend on the given trace parameters; no bound uniform as \(\alpha_i\) approaches zero or one is asserted.

## 2. Alternating words and bounded end-deletion operators

Write \(\mathcal W\) for the words \(w=i_1\cdots i_n\) in the alphabet \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\) with adjacent letters different, including the empty word \(\varnothing\).
For such a word let
\[
 x_w=x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_n},\quad
 e_w=x_w\Omega,\quad \Omega=1,\quad x_\varnothing=1.
 \tag{4}
\]
The reduced free-product decomposition gives the orthonormal basis
\[
 H=L^2(\mathcal M,\tau)
   =\mathbb C\Omega\oplus
     \bigoplus_{\substack{n\geq1\\i_1\ne i_2,\ldots,i_{n-1}\ne i_n}}
       \mathbb C e_{i_1\cdots i_n}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Here each centered factor Hilbert space is one dimensional.
Alternatively, orthogonality and normalization follow by successive centering of the middle factors in \(\tau(x_v^*x_w)\), using freeness and (3); different lengths or the first differing matching letter give zero.
The algebraic reduced-word span is \(A\), and is dense in \(H\).

Left multiplication is
\[
 x_i e_w=
 \begin{cases}
 e_{iw},& w=\varnothing\text{ or the first letter of }w\ne i,\\
 e_v+\kappa_i e_{iv},&w=iv.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{6}
\]
Define the bounded operator \(R_i\) by deleting a terminal \(i\):
\[
 R_i\Omega=0,\qquad
 R_i e_{wi}=e_w,\qquad
 R_i e_w=0\quad\text{if }w\ne\varnothing
                 \text{ has terminal letter different from }i.
 \tag{7}
\]
This maps an orthonormal subset injectively into an orthonormal subset, so \(\|R_i\|\leq1\).
It is an auxiliary Hilbert-space operator, not an element of \(\mathcal M\).

Let \(P=P_\Omega\).
Directly from (6)–(7), the two local operations commute on words of length at least two, and checking \(\Omega,e_1,\ldots,e_d\) gives
\[
 [R_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij}K_i,\qquad
 K_i=P+\kappa_i P x_i-x_i P x_i.
 \tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(K_i\Omega=\Omega\), \(K_i e_i=\kappa_i\Omega-e_i\), and \(K_i e_j=0\) for \(j\ne i\).
It vanishes on all longer words.
Thus its rank is at most two.
No normalized dual system is being claimed for variables with atoms.

## 3. The affiliated-to-bounded commutator implication

Apply Yin_C_F0034 with \(D_{i,0}=R_i\), \(\mathcal E=H\), and \(V_i=H\).
These are dense left \(\mathcal M\)-modules and the actual adjoints \(R_i^*\) have domain \(H\).
For every \(u\in L^0(\mathcal M)\), the lemma uses bounded fractions
\[
 u=s^{-1}a=bt^{-1},\qquad s,a,b,t\in\mathcal M,
 \quad s,t\text{ invertible in }L^0(\mathcal M).
\]
The corresponding defects \(sR_i b-aR_i t\) are everywhere bounded.
By Yin_C_F0034 their simultaneous finite-rank class is an inverse-closed unital subalgebra of \(L^0(\mathcal M)\).
Equation (8) puts every \(x_j\), and hence \(A\), in that class.

Consequently, for
\[
 f\in\operatorname{Div}_{L^0(\mathcal M)}A\cap\mathcal M,
\]
choose the bounded fractions \(s=t=1,\ a=b=f\) to obtain
\[
 [R_i,f]\in\mathcal F(H)\qquad(1\leq i\leq d).
 \tag{9}
\]
This is the only unbounded-operator step in the argument.
It does not differentiate an undefined composition with an unbounded rational inverse.

## 4. Selected commutator columns are all right residuals

Expand
\[
 f\Omega=\sum_{v\in\mathcal W}\beta_v e_v,\qquad
 \sum_{v\in\mathcal W}|\beta_v|^2<\infty.
 \tag{10}
\]
Extend the coefficient function to every word in the free monoid by setting \(\beta_v=0\) for nonalternating words.
For a word \(t\), its right residual is the formal series, or its square-summable coefficient vector,
\[
 \beta\mathbin{\triangleleft}t
       =(\beta_{ut})_{u}.
 \tag{11}
\]
All its nonzero coefficients have alternating indices \(u\), so the vector is naturally in \(H\).

The required formula is
\[
 [R_i,f]e_w=\sum_{u\in\mathcal W}\beta_{u i w^{\mathrm{rev}}}e_u
 \quad\text{if }w=\varnothing
          \text{ or the terminal letter of }w\ne i.
 \tag{12}
\]
The restriction on the terminal letter is essential; extra terms are present in the excluded columns.

First check (12) for \(f=x_v\), where \(v\) is alternating.
The Leibniz rule and (8) give
\[
 [R_i,x_v]=\sum_{v=u i q} x_u
       (P+\kappa_i P x_i-x_i P x_i)x_q.
 \tag{13}
\]
Every displayed cut has the last letter of \(u\), if present, different from \(i\), and the first letter of \(q\), if present, different from \(i\).
Hence \(x_i x_q=x_{iq}\) and \(x_u x_i=x_{ui}\) are reduced words.
Since \(x_q^*=x_{q^{\mathrm{rev}}}\), orthonormality in (5) yields
\[
 [R_i,x_v]e_w
 =\sum_{v=u i q}
   \left(
    \mathbf1_{\{w=q^{\mathrm{rev}}\}}e_u
    +\mathbf1_{\{w=q^{\mathrm{rev}}i\}}
                  (\kappa_i e_u-e_{ui})
   \right).
 \tag{14}
\]
For precisely the columns specified in (12), the second term vanishes.
The first retains the unique cut \(v=u i w^{\mathrm{rev}}\), when such a cut exists.
This proves the polynomial assertion.

For a bounded \(f\), take its finite Fourier truncations \(f_N\in A\), so \(f_N\to f\) in \(L^2\), without assuming operator-norm convergence.
For each fixed \(w\),
\[
 (f_N-f)e_w=(f_N-f)x_w\Omega\longrightarrow0
\]
in \(H\), since right multiplication by the bounded \(x_w\) is \(L^2\)-bounded.
The same holds with \(R_i e_w\), which is zero or another fixed word vector.
Boundedness of \(R_i\) therefore gives convergence of the left side of (12).
The residuals on the right converge in \(H\), because the coefficient map \(u\mapsto u i w^{\mathrm{rev}}\) is injective.
Thus (12) holds for \(f\).

Every nonempty alternating suffix is \(t=i q\) with \(q=\varnothing\) or initial letter different from \(i\).
Taking \(w=q^{\mathrm{rev}}\) in (12) gives its full right residual as a column of \([R_i,f]\).
A nonalternating suffix has zero residual.
It follows that
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\beta\mathbin{\triangleleft}t:t\text{ any word}\}
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{span}\{f\Omega\}
       +\sum_{i=1}^d\operatorname{Ran}[R_i,f],
 \tag{15}
\]
a finite-dimensional space by (9).

The accepted finite-residual theorem gives a minimal finite representation
\[
 \beta_w=\lambda\,\mu(w)\,\gamma,\qquad
 \mu(w)=\mu_{i_1}\cdots\mu_{i_n},\quad
 \mu_\varnothing=I_N,
 \tag{16}
\]
where \(\lambda\) is a complex row, \(\gamma\) a column, and the \(\mu_i\) are complex \(N\)-by-\(N\) matrices.
The zero element can be handled separately, so take \(N\geq1\).

There are two important consequences of minimality:

1. Reachable columns \(\mu(q)\gamma\) span \(\mathbb C^N\), and observable rows \(\lambda\mu(p)\) span its dual.
   Their pairings express each entry of \(\mu(w)\) as a fixed finite linear combination of \(\beta_{p w q}\).
   Therefore
   \[
    \sum_w\|\mu(w)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2<\infty.
    \tag{17}
   \]
   The injectivity of \(w\mapsto p w q\) proves each required scalar square-summability bound.
2. For every \(i,p,q\), \(\beta_{p i i q}=0\).
   The same spanning rows and columns imply
   \[
    \mu_i^2=0.
    \tag{18}
   \]
   Thus \(\mu(w)=0\) for every nonalternating word, not only after multiplication by \(\lambda\) and \(\gamma\).

The second fact is what will make the powers of an operator matrix genuinely homogeneous in the reduced-word basis.

## 5. A uniform homogeneous estimate in the given trace model

Set \(\kappa=\max_i|\kappa_i|\).
For finitely supported coefficients on alternating words of length \(n\), we prove
\[
 \left\|\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W\\|w|=n}}c_w x_w\right\|
 \leq (n+1+n\kappa)
       \left(\sum_{|w|=n}|c_w|^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \tag{19}
\]
This is the scalar specialization of the creation/diagonal/annihilation mechanism in the inspected Ricard–Xu upper-bound proof.
Here is a direct verification of the exact constant and its trace dependence.

Define the partial left creation operator
\[
 L_i e_w=
 \begin{cases}
 e_{iw},&w=\varnothing\text{ or its first letter differs from }i,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Let \(Q_i=L_iL_i^*\), the projection onto the words beginning with \(i\).
Equation (6) is the bounded-operator identity
\[
 x_i=L_i+L_i^*+\kappa_i Q_i.
 \tag{20}
\]
For adjacent distinct \(i,j\),
\[
 L_i^*L_j=L_i^*Q_j=Q_iL_j=Q_iQ_j=0.
 \tag{21}
\]
Consequently expansion of (20) along an alternating word \(w=i_1\cdots i_n\) leaves only the following \(2n+1\) terms:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x_w
 &=\sum_{k=0}^{n}
       L_{i_1}\cdots L_{i_k}
       L_{i_{k+1}}^*\cdots L_{i_n}^*\\
 &\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\kappa_{i_k}
       L_{i_1}\cdots L_{i_{k-1}} Q_{i_k}
       L_{i_{k+1}}^*\cdots L_{i_n}^* .
\end{aligned}
 \tag{22}
\]
Empty products are identities.
To see completeness of this expansion, any nonzero choice of terms from (20) must place all creation factors before all annihilation factors; it can contain at most one diagonal factor, located between them.
Every other order contains one of the adjacent zero products in (21).

For a reduced word \(u\), write \(L_u=L_{u_1}\cdots L_{u_m}\).
For fixed length \(m\), the \(L_u\) have mutually orthogonal ranges and norms at most one.
Thus the row operator
\[
 (h_u)_u\longmapsto\sum_{|u|=m}L_u h_u
 \tag{23}
\]
is a contraction.
The length-zero case is the identity.
The adjoint column operator is also a contraction.

Fix \(k\) in the first sum of (22).
Write \(w=u v^{\mathrm{rev}}\), with \(|u|=k\), \(|v|=n-k\), and define a coefficient matrix \(C\) by \(C_{u,v}=c_{u v^{\mathrm{rev}}}\), using zero if the concatenation is not alternating.
The sum at this cut is
\[
 \sum_{u,v}C_{u,v}L_u L_v^*.
 \tag{24}
\]
By the row and column contractions it has norm at most \(\|C\|\), hence at most its Hilbert–Schmidt norm, which is \((\sum_w|c_w|^2)^{1/2}\).

At a fixed diagonal cut \(k\), the corresponding expression is
\[
 \sum_{u,v,i}c_{u i v^{\mathrm{rev}}}\kappa_i L_u Q_i L_v^*,
 \qquad |u|=k-1,\quad |v|=n-k.
 \tag{25}
\]
Between the same contraction maps, the middle block matrix is \(\sum_i\kappa_i C_i\otimes Q_i\), where \((C_i)_{u,v}=c_{u i v^{\mathrm{rev}}}\).
The \(Q_i\) are mutually orthogonal projections, so this matrix has norm
\[
 \max_i|\kappa_i|\,\|C_i\|
 \leq \kappa\left(\sum_w|c_w|^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
where the displayed maximum is read as \(\max_i(|\kappa_i|\|C_i\|)\).
Summing the \(n+1\) bounds from (24) and the \(n\) bounds from (25) proves (19).
No factor exponential in the word length has been introduced.

## 6. State energy gives a bounded resolvent

The square-summability (17) has a useful direct quantitative consequence.
Define the positive map on \(M_N(\mathbb C)\)
\[
 \Phi(T)=\sum_{i=1}^d\mu_i T\mu_i^*.
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\Phi^n(I_N)
       =\sum_{|w|=n}\|\mu(w)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.
 \tag{26}
\]
By (17), the positive series
\[
 Q=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi^n(I_N)
 \tag{27}
\]
converges in finite-dimensional matrix norm, satisfies \(Q\geq I_N\), and obeys \(\Phi(Q)=Q-I_N\).

If \(Q=I_N\), positivity gives \(\mu_i=0\) for every \(i\), and the following construction is constant.
Otherwise put
\[
 c=1-\|Q\|^{-1}\in(0,1).
\]
Since \(I_N\geq Q/\|Q\|\), positivity implies
\[
 \Phi(Q)\leq cQ,\qquad
 \Phi^n(I_N)\leq c^nQ.
 \tag{28}
\]
Combining (26)–(28),
\[
 \sum_{|w|=n}\|\mu(w)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
       \leq c^n\operatorname{Tr}Q.
 \tag{29}
\]

Now form the finite matrix over the original polynomial algebra
\[
 V=\sum_{i=1}^d\mu_i\otimes x_i\in M_N(A).
 \tag{30}
\]
Its algebraic \(n\)-th power is
\[
 V^n=\sum_{|w|=n}\mu(w)\otimes x_w
     =\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W\\|w|=n}}\mu(w)\otimes x_w,
 \tag{31}
\]
because (18) kills every nonalternating word before its operator product is reduced.
Apply (19) to each of the finitely many scalar entries and then use (29).
For example,
\[
 \|V^n\|
   \leq N(n+1+n\kappa)
               \sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}Q}\,c^{n/2}.
 \tag{32}
\]
Thus \(\sum_{n\geq0}V^n\) converges in matrix operator norm and
\[
 B=I_N-V
 \quad\text{is invertible in }M_N(\mathcal M),\qquad
 B^{-1}=\sum_{n\geq0}V^n.
 \tag{33}
\]
This bounded inverse is a new representation of the particular \(f\); no boundedness is asserted for an arbitrary originally supplied affiliated linearization.

## 7. Explicit bounded scalar inverses and equality

It remains to pass from a bounded matrix inverse to bounded scalar division closure.
Here \(A\) is a unital star algebra, because the original projections are selfadjoint.
This permits a completely explicit finite scalar-pivot procedure.

Put
\[
 G=B^*B\in M_N(A),\qquad
 \delta=\|B^{-1}\|^{-2}>0.
\]
Then \(G\geq\delta I_N\).
For any positive block matrix \(G_0\geq\delta I\), write
\[
 G_0=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b^*&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S=D-b^*a^{-1}b.
 \tag{34}
\]
The scalar entry \(a\geq\delta1\) is boundedly invertible in \(\mathcal M\).
Moreover \(S\geq\delta I\): evaluate the quadratic form of \(G_0\) on \((-a^{-1}b\,y,y)\), for arbitrary \(y\in H^{N-1}\).
It equals \(\langle y,Sy\rangle\) and is at least \(\delta\|y\|^2\).
The inverse formula is
\[
 G_0^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a^{-1}+a^{-1}bS^{-1}b^*a^{-1}&-a^{-1}bS^{-1}\\
 -S^{-1}b^*a^{-1}&S^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{35}
\]
Apply (34) recursively to the successive positive Schur complements, ending with a scalar.
Each step uses one actual scalar inverse whose lower bound is at least \(\delta1\), and only finitely many additions and multiplications.
All entries stay in \(\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal M}A\); taking adjoints stays there because the generators are selfadjoint and \((a^{-1})^*=(a^*)^{-1}\).
Reconstructing upward with (35) therefore gives
\[
 G^{-1}\in M_N(\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal M}A),\qquad
 B^{-1}=G^{-1}B^*\in M_N(\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal M}A).
 \tag{36}
\]
Equations (34)–(36) specify the required bounded scalar inverse expressions.
They use at most \(N\) successive scalar pivots, with their values determined by the finite matrices in (16) and the generators (2).
The Neumann series in (33) establishes invertibility; it is not itself the rational expression.

Set
\[
 g=\lambda B^{-1}\gamma
       =\lambda G^{-1}B^*\gamma
          \in\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal M}A.
 \tag{37}
\]
For each finite cutoff, (16) and (31) give
\[
 \lambda\left(\sum_{n=0}^L V^n\right)\gamma
    =\sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal W\\|w|\leq L}}\beta_w x_w.
 \tag{38}
\]
The left side converges to \(g\) in norm by (33).
The right side, applied to \(\Omega\), converges to \(f\Omega\) by (10).
Thus \(g\Omega=f\Omega\).
Faithfulness of \(\tau\), equivalently that \(\Omega\) is separating for the left action of \(\mathcal M\), gives \(g=f\).

This proves the nontrivial inclusion in (1).
The reverse inclusion follows directly since a bounded inverse is also its affiliated inverse.
Every expression in (37) can be returned to the original \(p_i\) using their scalar affine relation (2).

The argument also proves, with the \(R_i\) fixed by (7), the bounded criterion
\[
 \operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal M}A
   =\{f\in\mathcal M:[R_i,f]\in\mathcal F(H)
                              \text{ for all }i\}.
 \tag{39}
\]
The forward implication in (39) is the bounded commutator product and inverse identity using (8); the reverse implication is Sections 4–7.
Equation (39) is not a normalized dual criterion for atomic variables.

## 8. What this resolves and what still needs work

The argument treats three freely independent projections at any prescribed traces in \((0,1)\), and in fact every finite number of them.
It requires no rationality of the traces, spectral gaps of the two-projection central parameter, absence of central atomic summands, or reduction to a group trace.
It therefore supplies the next finite-atomic case requested by Q7.

The proof does not prove general stability of (1) from the corresponding equalities in arbitrary freely independent factor algebras.
For an arbitrary diffuse coordinate, its centered Hilbert space is infinite dimensional; replacing that entire space by one end-deletion letter does not give the finite alphabet and the polynomial reduced-word basis used here.
A recurrence or rationality hypothesis must actually supply finite residuals and a controlled finite state realization.
Equality for a larger ambient semicircular or group algebra does not by itself descend to a smaller algebra generated by nonlinear coordinate functions.

For finite-dimensional factors with more than two atoms, the inspected Ricard–Xu decomposition already suggests an analogous homogeneous estimate, but the exact multi-letter cut identity and state reconstruction would still have to be written and checked.
This file claims only the projection-family result above; it does not silently promote that plausible next extension.
