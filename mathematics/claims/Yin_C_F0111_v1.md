Fix d circular pairs and m=2d real coordinates, real -1<q<1.
Use
\[
c_j=\ell_q(e_j)+\ell_q(f_j)^*,\qquad
c_j^*=\ell_q(f_j)+\ell_q(e_j)^*
\]
and the normalized bounded signed duals satisfying
\[
[D_i^+,c_j]=\delta_{ij}P,\qquad [D_i^+,c_j^*]=0,\qquad
[D_i^-,c_j]=0,\qquad [D_i^-,c_j^*]=\delta_{ij}P,\qquad
D_i^\pm\Omega=0.
\tag{1}
\]
The positive holomorphic Fock subspace is \(H_+\).
Write
\[
\mathcal F_+=\operatorname{Div}_{L^0(M)}
\mathbb C\langle c_1,\ldots,c_d\rangle .
\]
It is the positive-variable free subfield in the field generated by the full selfadjoint tuple.
In particular nonzero positive polynomial denominators are invertible in the affiliated algebra.

Let \(K_{\rm iter}\) be the common kernel of all \(D_k^-D_{j_s}^+\cdots D_{j_1}^+\), with s>=0.
Yin_C_F0107 proves
\[
\mathcal F_+\cap L^2(M,\tau)\subseteq K_{\rm iter}.
\tag{2}
\]
The space \(K_{\rm iter}\) is closed, invariant under every \(D_j^+\), and contains \(H_+\).

The main additional hypothesis used in Sections 3–5 is
\[
\bigcap_j\ker D_j^+\cap\bigcap_j\ker D_j^-=\mathbb C\Omega.
\tag{3}
\]
Yin_C_F0109 proves (3), and a quantitative lower bound, whenever
\[
|q|\le\frac1{1000m^2}=\frac1{4000d^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
We keep d as the number of circular pairs throughout.
Any future improvement of (3) would extend the corresponding conclusions below without changing their proofs.

## 2. The actual charge action and its dual covariance

On the complex one-particle space define
\[
V_\theta e_j=e^{i\theta}e_j,\qquad
V_\theta f_j=e^{-i\theta}f_j.
\]
This is the complexification of a real orthogonal rotation of each underlying pair.
On the algebraic Fock space let
\[
\mathcal U_\theta=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}V_\theta^{\otimes n}.
\]
Every tensor permutation commutes with \(V_\theta^{\otimes n}\), so the q-symmetrizer does too.
Thus \(\mathcal U_\theta\) extends to a unitary on the actual q-Fock Hilbert space, fixes \(\Omega\), and forms a strongly continuous circle representation.
The creation/annihilation formulas give
\[
\mathcal U_\theta c_j\mathcal U_\theta^*=e^{i\theta}c_j.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently conjugation defines a trace-preserving automorphism \(\alpha_\theta\) of M and its affiliated algebra.

A raw word with p positive letters and r negative letters has charge p-r.
Different charges are orthogonal: the q-symmetrizer permutes letters without changing their numbers.
Denote the closed charge-k subspace by \(H^{[k]}\), for k an integer.
Then
\[
H=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb Z}H^{[k]}.
\]
On polynomials, normalized differentiation with respect to \(c_j\) decreases charge by one, and differentiation with respect to \(c_j^*\) increases it by one.
Boundedness and density give on all H
\[
\mathcal U_\theta D_j^+\mathcal U_\theta^*=e^{-i\theta}D_j^+,\qquad
\mathcal U_\theta D_j^-\mathcal U_\theta^*=e^{i\theta}D_j^-.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular \(D_j^+H^{[k]}\subseteq H^{[k-1]}\), \(D_j^-H^{[k]}\subseteq H^{[k+1]}\), and \(K_{\rm iter}\) is preserved by the gauge action and by every charge projection.

A vector has charge k when it belongs to \(H^{[k]}\).
For an L2 affiliated element f this is equivalent to \(\alpha_\theta(f)=e^{ik\theta}f\).
Formal homogeneity of a positive rational expression, with variables assigned degree one and inversion negating degree, is a sufficient condition.

## 3. A finite positive-dual orbit is nilpotent on a fixed charge

For \(\eta\in H\), define its algebraic positive-dual orbit
\[
W_\eta=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
\{D_{j_s}^+\cdots D_{j_1}^+\eta:s\ge0\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Suppose \(\eta\in H^{[k]}\) and \(W_\eta\) is finite dimensional.
All orbit vectors obtained using s duals have charge k-s.
If one vector from a word of length N is nonzero, every intermediate vector along that word is nonzero.
These N+1 intermediate vectors have distinct charges and hence are linearly independent.
Therefore, writing \(r=\dim W_\eta\),
\[
D_{j_r}^+\cdots D_{j_1}^+\eta=0
\quad\text{for every word of length }r.
\tag{8}
\]
This is actual finite-dimensionality in H, not recognizability of raw Wick coefficients.
It uses no particle-degree truncation of \(\eta\).

Every \(f\in\mathcal F_+\cap H\) has finite \(W_{f\Omega}\).
Indeed, choose a minimal full affine representation \(f=uB(c)^{-1}v\).
Yin_C_F0107's state bootstrap puts all entries of \(Y=B(c)^{-1}\) in H.
Yin_C_F0108's same positive-coordinate contraction, with \(G=(\tau(Y_{ab}))\), gives
\[
D_j^+(uY)=uY A_jG.
\tag{9}
\]
The span of the n entries of the row \(uY\) is invariant under every \(D_j^+\) and contains f.
Thus \(\dim W_{f\Omega}\le n\).
The vectors in that row are not required to be homogeneous; only the orbit of the particular homogeneous f is graded as in the preceding paragraph.

## 4. Nilpotent iterated-kernel vectors are positive polynomials

Assume (3).
For any \(\eta\in K_{\rm iter}\), if all positive-dual words of length N kill \(\eta\), then \(\eta=p(c)\Omega\) for a positive polynomial p of degree at most N-1.

The proof is by induction on N>=1.
If N=1, both signs kill \(\eta\), so (3) gives \(\eta\in\mathbb C\Omega\).
For N>1, each \(D_j^+\eta\) lies in \(K_{\rm iter}\) and is killed by every positive word of length N-1.
Induction gives positive polynomials \(p_j\) of degree at most N-2 with \(D_j^+\eta=p_j(c)\Omega\).
Put
\[
p=\sum_{j=1}^d p_j(c)c_j.
\tag{10}
\]
On positive polynomial vectors, the normalized positive dual is exactly literal terminal-letter deletion.
Consequently \(D_i^+p\Omega=p_i(c)\Omega\), and every \(D_i^-\) kills \(p\Omega\).
Both signs therefore kill \(\eta-p\Omega\); by (3) this difference is scalar.
Adding the constant proves the induction.
The right append in (10), rather than a left append, is essential.

Combining Sections 3 and 4 proves: under (3), if
\[
f\in\mathcal F_+\cap H,\qquad
\alpha_\theta(f)=e^{ik\theta}f,
\]
then f is a positive polynomial of degree at most \(\dim W_{f\Omega}-1\).
Its charge further implies
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
k<0:& f=0,\\
k=0:& f\in\mathbb C1,\\
k>0:& f\text{ is a homogeneous positive polynomial of degree }k.
\end{array}}
\tag{11}
\]
The last conclusion uses independence of positive raw words and their charge, not a norm comparison between Fock models.
Faithfulness identifies the bounded polynomial vector with the original affiliated element.

The proof requires only the joint-kernel assertion (3), not the full small-q joint inverse or any all-H totality theorem.
In particular it is valid on the explicit interval (4).

## 5. Finite charge spectra and explicit denominators

The homogeneous conclusion also handles finite charge support.
Suppose \(f\in\mathcal F_+\cap H\) has only finitely many nonzero charge components, with support inside an integer interval [a,b].
Choose an integer N>b-a and put \(\theta_l=2\pi l/N\).
Each component, for \(a\le k\le b\), is the finite sum
\[
f_k=\frac1N\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}e^{-ik\theta_l}\alpha_{\theta_l}(f).
\tag{12}
\]
The finite-root average separates precisely these charges because their differences have absolute value less than N.
The automorphisms preserve \(\mathcal F_+\) and H, so \(f_k\in\mathcal F_+\cap H\).
Apply (11) to each and sum.
Thus finite-charge-spectrum positive rational L2 elements are positive polynomials under (3).

For d>=2 and (4), this excludes the following actual L2 candidates:
\[
(c_1c_2-c_2c_1)^{-1}\notin H,\qquad
c_1c_2^{-1}\notin H.
\tag{13}
\]
The first denominator is a nonzero positive polynomial and hence has an affiliated inverse.
Its inverse has charge -2 and is nonzero, contradicting (11) as soon as L2 is hypothesized.
The second element has charge zero; if it were L2, (11) would make it scalar, forcing \(c_1=\lambda c_2\), contrary to algebraic freeness.

More generally the inverse of every nonzero homogeneous positive polynomial of degree r>=1 has charge -r and is not L2 under (3).
These statements concern actual affiliated inverses, not a formal Taylor branch.
For two circular pairs, the currently certified interval is \(|q|\le1/16000\); it does not include q=1/2.

## 6. A separate tail criterion for general iterated-kernel vectors

The charge calculation also supplies a quantitative sufficient condition without rational membership.
Suppose constants \(\kappa,L>0\) satisfy
\[
\left(\sum_j\|D_j^+\zeta\|^2+\sum_j\|D_j^-\zeta\|^2\right)^{1/2}
\ge\kappa\|\zeta\|\quad(\zeta\perp\Omega),\qquad
\left(\sum_j\|D_j^+\zeta\|^2\right)^{1/2}\le L\|\zeta\|.
\tag{14}
\]
Yin_C_F0109 supplies such constants when its perturbation inequality holds; for example, in its notation,
\[
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{1-t}(1-\rho_m(t))}{\sqrt m},\qquad
L=c_t+\sqrt m\,\epsilon_m(t),\qquad t=|q|.
\tag{15}
\]
For the upper bound, the positive signed subtuple is bounded by the full tuple, and the full deletion and perturbation bounds are Yin_C_F0109, Equations (7) and (5).
These constants are conservative.

Let \(P_{\ge n}\) be the projection onto particle degrees at least n.
If \(\eta\in K_{\rm iter}\) and
\[
\|P_{\ge n}\eta\|\le C r^n\quad(n\ge0),\qquad
0<r<1,\qquad Lr^2<\kappa,
\tag{16}
\]
then \(\eta\in H_+\).

First suppose \(\eta\) has a negative charge k.
All positive-dual iterates remain in \(K_{\rm iter}\), have negative charge, and hence are orthogonal to \(\Omega\).
Applying (14) at each stage, with all negative-dual terms zero, gives
\[
\left(\sum_{|w|=s}\|D_w^+\eta\|^2\right)^{1/2}
\ge\kappa^s\|\eta\|.
\tag{17}
\]
The exact q-duals lower particle degree by at least one on every nonzero homogeneous component.
After s positive steps, the output has charge k-s, so any nonzero output has particle degree at least s-k.
It must therefore come from an input degree at least 2s-k.
Hence
\[
\left(\sum_{|w|=s}\|D_w^+\eta\|^2\right)^{1/2}
\le L^s\|P_{\ge2s-k}\eta\|
\le C r^{-k}(Lr^2)^s.
\tag{18}
\]
Comparison with (17) and (16) forces \(\eta=0\).

For charge k>=0, each positive-dual word of length k+1 sends \(\eta\) to charge -1.
These vectors retain an exponential tail of rate r: degree lowering gives, for a word w of length s,
\[
P_{\ge n}D_w^+\eta=P_{\ge n}D_w^+P_{\ge n+s}\eta,
\]
and its norm is bounded by \(L^s C r^{n+s}\).
The negative-charge argument makes all the length-k+1 outputs zero.
The reconstruction in Section 4 makes \(\eta\) a positive polynomial; its charge is k.
Finally, charge projections preserve \(K_{\rm iter}\), commute with particle projections, and preserve the tail bound in (16).
Apply the argument componentwise and use closedness of \(H_+\), proving the assertion for general \(\eta\).

This is a conditional decay criterion.
No such exponential estimate has been derived for an arbitrary vector of \(K_{\rm iter}\), or for every actual rational state, at the required rate.
Particle truncations are not known to preserve all the iterated tests.
Charge projections do preserve \(K_{\rm iter}\), but a fixed charge can contain arbitrarily high particle degrees.
Thus (16) is not being used as an unproved regularization principle to conclude full totality.

## 7. Exact remaining scope

The new unconditional consequences under the certified joint-kernel hypothesis are (11)–(13).
The general iterated-kernel conclusion in Section 6 requires the explicit tail premise (16).
Gauge homogeneity plus finite actual dual orbit is sufficient to force nilpotence and avoids that decay premise; an arbitrary element of \(K_{\rm iter}\) need not have a finite orbit.
An arbitrary rational element has finite orbit, but can have infinitely many charge components, so its successive images need not lie in distinct orthogonal charge sectors.
Neither missing condition is supplied by the grading alone.

Full small-q totality \(K_{\rm iter}=H_+\), the corresponding unrestricted holomorphic rational boundedness/division-closure assertions, and the d=2,q=1/2 question remain unresolved.
The earlier abstract shift perturbation example is not used here: the new proofs use the actual signed charge covariance, actual q-dual degree lowering, and the independently audited finite rational-state interface.
