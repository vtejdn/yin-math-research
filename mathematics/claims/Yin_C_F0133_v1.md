## Statement

There exist a finite von Neumann algebra (M,tau) with a faithful normal tracial probability state and a single bounded nonnormal generator X, with H=L2(M,tau), Omega=1, such that:

1. A bounded operator D on all H satisfies
   \[
   [X,D]=P_\Omega.
   \]
   In particular both actual domains Dom D and Dom D* equal H.
   Thus the strong full-domain condition S(X), and hence the bare full-domain condition F(X), hold.
2. There is no complex-linear outer-bimodule derivation
   \[
   \delta:\mathbb C\langle X,X^*\rangle\longrightarrow H\otimes_2H,
   \qquad \delta(X)=\Omega\otimes\Omega .
   \]
   Therefore the full ACSY24 condition A(X) fails for every possible witness, before considering its actual vacuum-adjoint requirement.

All these conditions refer to the represented starred algebra and the original one-coordinate tuple.
Inner products are linear in the second variable.
Outer actions are a dot (xi tensor eta) dot b=(a xi) tensor (eta b).

Consequently neither S(X) nor F(X) implies A(X) for arbitrary bounded generating tuples.
The example does not refute the corresponding known normal strong-domain implication, because X is nonnormal.
It also does not decide A(X) implies F(X).

## Argument

### 1. The finite tracial algebra and its single generator

Let
\[
 d\mu(s)=\frac1\pi\sqrt{4-s^2}\,1_{(0,2)}(s)\,ds,\qquad
 M=M_2(L^\infty((0,2),\mu)),\qquad
 \tau(a)=\frac12\int_0^2\operatorname{Tr}_2(a(s))\,d\mu(s).
\]
The measure mu is a probability measure, and tau is a faithful normal tracial probability state.
Put H=L2(M,tau), Omega=I2, and let s also denote the central coordinate function s I2.
Set
\[
 J_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 h=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\tfrac12\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 K=hJ_0h^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Y=sJ_0,\quad X=sK=hYh^{-1}.
 \tag{N1}
\]
The symbol J_0 is just this constant matrix, not tracial conjugation.
Both h and its inverse are bounded elements of M.
Directly,
\[
 K^2=I_2,\quad
 K^*K=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
 KK^*=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{N2}
\]
Thus X is bounded and nonnormal, while X squared=s squared I2 is central and positive.

The element X generates all M as a von Neumann algebra.
Indeed, functional calculus of X squared gives s I2 in W*(X).
The uniformly bounded products
\[
 1_{\{s\ge1/n\}}(s)\,s^{-1}X
   =1_{\{s\ge1/n\}}(s)K
\]
converge strongly to K, so K belongs to W*(X).
For q=(K+I2)/2 one has
\[
 e_{11}=\tfrac45qq^*,\qquad
 e_{12}=2(q-e_{11}),\qquad
 e_{21}=e_{12}^*,\qquad e_{22}=I_2-e_{11}.
\]
Hence all constant matrix units belong to W*(X).
Together with the central algebra generated by s, they generate M.

### 2. A bounded full-H dual, and therefore the strong antecedent

For every bounded Borel f,
\[
 \tau(f(Y))=\frac12\int_0^2(f(s)+f(-s))\,d\mu(s)
 =\int_{-2}^2 f(t)\,\frac{\sqrt{4-t^2}}{2\pi}\,dt.
 \tag{N3}
\]
So Y has the standard variance-one semicircular distribution.
Let B=W*(Y), and identify H_B=closure(B Omega) with the one-letter full Fock space by the semicircular polynomial basis.
On that space Y is ell+ell*, where ell is the unilateral creation shift.
The backward shift E_B=ell* is bounded and satisfies
\[
 [E_B,Y|_{H_B}]=P_\Omega .
 \tag{N4}
\]
This follows directly from ell* ell=I and ell ell*=I-P_Omega.

The subspace H_B reduces bounded left multiplication by the selfadjoint Y.
Extend E_B by zero on its orthogonal complement in H, obtaining a bounded operator E_Y on all H.
On H_B its commutator is (N4); on the orthogonal complement both terms vanish.
Therefore
\[
 [E_Y,Y]=P_\Omega\quad\hbox{on all }H,\qquad E_Y\Omega=0.
 \tag{N5}
\]
No claim that Y generates M is used in this step.

Define the bounded invertible Hilbert-space operator
\[
 W(a\Omega)=h a h^{-1}\Omega\qquad(a\in M).
\]
Left and right bounded multiplication make this a well-defined bounded invertible operator on H.
It intertwines left Y with left X.
Moreover W Omega=Omega and
\[
 P_\Omega W^{-1}(a\Omega)
   =\tau(h^{-1}ah)\Omega=\tau(a)\Omega.
\]
The equality extends from M Omega to H, so W P_Omega W^{-1}=P_Omega.
Put
\[
 E_X=W E_Y W^{-1},\qquad D=-E_X.
 \tag{N6}
\]
Then E_X and D are bounded on H, and
\[
 [E_X,X]=P_\Omega,\qquad [X,D]=P_\Omega.
 \tag{N7}
\]
In particular Dom D=Dom D*=H.
This verifies S(X), not merely F(X), for the generating tuple consisting of X alone.
All maps are complex linear.
The transport uses an ordinary bounded similarity, not an unbounded-domain extension.

### 3. Every normalized starred Hilbert derivation is impossible

Suppose that an outer-bimodule derivation
\[
 \delta:A=\mathbb C\langle X,X^*\rangle
   \longrightarrow H\otimes_2H,\qquad
 \delta(X)=I_2\otimes I_2
\]
exists.
No adjoint or vacuum premise is needed for the contradiction.
Write beta=delta(X*).
The product rule forces
\[
 \delta(X^2)=X\otimes I_2+I_2\otimes X.
 \tag{N8}
\]
The actual represented relation [X squared,X*]=0 holds because X squared=s squared I2 is central.
Differentiating it gives
\[
 ((X^2)\otimes I_2-I_2\otimes X^2)\,\beta
 =X^*\delta(X^2)-\delta(X^2)X^*.
 \tag{N9}
\]
All multiplications in (N9) are the bounded outer bimodule actions.

Identify the coarse space with
\[
 L^2((0,2)^2,\mu\otimes\mu;
        M_2(\mathbb C)\otimes_2M_2(\mathbb C)),
\]
using the normalized matrix trace on each factor.
With s and t denoting the two scalar coordinates, (N9) reads
\[
 (s^2-t^2)\beta(s,t)
 =s^2 K^*K\otimes I_2
   +st K^*\otimes K
   -st K\otimes K^*
   -t^2 I_2\otimes KK^* .
 \tag{N10}
\]
Extract the coefficient of e_21 tensor e_11.
Coefficient extraction is a bounded linear map on the finite-dimensional matrix tensor factor, so the scalar coefficient b(s,t) of an H-tensor vector must belong to L2(mu tensor mu).
Equations (N1)-(N2) instead force
\[
 (s^2-t^2)b(s,t)=s^2+st=s(s+t).
 \tag{N11}
\]
Since s,t>0 and mu is nonatomic, the diagonal s=t is product-null and (N11) implies
\[
 b(s,t)=\frac{s}{s-t}\qquad\text{almost everywhere off the diagonal}.
 \tag{N12}
\]
On, for example, [1/2,1]^2 the density of mu is bounded below by a positive constant and s is bounded below by 1/2.
Tonelli's theorem and the divergence of integral |s-t|^{-2} near t=s give
\[
 \int\!\!\int |b(s,t)|^2\,d\mu(s)d\mu(t)=\infty.
 \tag{N13}
\]
This contradicts beta in H tensor_2 H.
Therefore there is no normalized outer Hilbert-valued derivation on the full represented starred algebra.
In particular A(X) fails for every possible witness.


## Consequences and verification boundary

The bounded operator (N6) verifies the strongest stated antecedent with its actual full-H adjoint.
The contradiction (N8)-(N13) applies to every putative normalized Hilbert-valued derivation on the represented starred algebra.
Changing the supplied dual, choosing a different canonical construction, or changing a proposed vacuum cannot alter that contradiction.

By the accepted [full-domain maximality argument in C_F0024 Section 3](Yin_C_F0024_v1.md#3-maximal-delta-from-dual-identities-on-all-bounded-vectors), this X still has Delta(X)=1.
That conclusion uses neither normality nor a starred derivation, and so is compatible with the counterexample.
The difference between maximal Delta and the ACSY premise is essential here.

The construction resolves the nonimplication F(X) does not imply A(X) for general tuples, and strengthens it to S(X) does not imply A(X).
It does not decide the other general implication, any arbitrary-normal comparison, or an alternative nonnormal fraction-commutator rationality theorem.
