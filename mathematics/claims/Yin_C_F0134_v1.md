## Statement

Let (M,tau) be a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal probability trace, H=L2(M,tau), Omega=1, and P=P_Omega.
Let h be bounded, positive and regular, let S be a standard semicircular element free from N=W*(h), and assume M=W*(h,S).
Assume h has a scalar conjugate variable xi_h in L2(N).
Equivalently for this purpose, the selfadjoint pair (h,S) has the joint conjugate system (xi_h,S).
Put

\[
 Y=hS,\qquad X=(h,Y).
 \tag{1}
\]

For a bounded generating tuple Z, put A_Z=C<Z,Z*> and use the following existential conditions.

- \(\mathsf A(Z)\): there are outer derivations \(\delta_i:A_Z\to H\bar\otimes H\) with \(\delta_i(Z_j)=\delta_{ij}1\otimes1\) and actual \(1\otimes1\in\operatorname{Dom}\delta_i^*\).
- \(\mathsf F(Z)\): there are complex-linear maps \(D_i:M\Omega\to H\) with \([Z_j,D_i]=\delta_{ij}P\) on M Omega, without continuity or adjoint-domain assumptions.
- \(\mathsf S(Z)\): the same identities hold for densely defined operators with \(M\Omega\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}D_i\cap\operatorname{Dom}D_i^*\).

Then X generates M and

\[
 \boxed{\mathsf A(X)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathsf F(X)
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathsf S(X)
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ h^{-1}\in L^2(M).}
 \tag{2}
\]

When b=h^{-1} belongs to L2, the constructed derivations have explicit actual vacuum vectors

\[
 \delta_Y^*(1\otimes1)=bS,\qquad
 \delta_h^*(1\otimes1)=\xi_h-b(S^2-1).
 \tag{3}
\]

The canonical right contractions associated with these derivations have Hilbert closures whose domains and actual adjoint domains contain M Omega.
The inverse b need not be bounded.

The argument proves (2) only for the specified free semicircular coordinate change.
It does not establish a general nonnormal implication between the three existential conditions.

## Argument

### 1. Reference contractions and generation

Write A_0=C<h,S>.
We use the exact scalar-conjugate free-adjoining and selfadjoint contraction interfaces in [Dab10-U002, Sections 2-3](../audits/literature/Dab10/Yin_U002.md#dab10-u002).
They give represented free difference quotients partial_h, partial_S on A_0 and a closed operator

\[
 B=\overline{(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\partial_h}
\]

such that

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 M\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}B\cap\operatorname{Dom}B^*,\quad
 B1=0,\quad B^*1=\xi_h,\\
 [B,h]=P,\qquad [B,S]=0 \quad\hbox{on }M,\\
 \|Ba\|_2\le4\|\xi_h\|_2\|a\|,\qquad
 \|B^*a\|_2\le5\|\xi_h\|_2\|a\|,\qquad a\in M.
 \tag{4}
 \end{gathered}
\]

The commutator identities initially hold on A_0.
Uniformly bounded polynomial approximants and the bounded-set weak continuity of B from that source interface extend them to M: both B(p_n) and B(hp_n), or B(Sp_n), converge weakly to their respective closed-operator values.

For e_n=1_[1/n,infinity)(h), the bounded b_n=h^{-1}e_n belongs to W*(h).
Since b_nY=e_nS tends strongly to S, the von Neumann algebra generated by h and Y contains S.
Thus W*(X)=M and A_X=C<h,hS,Sh> is L2 dense.
This argument uses only regularity, before assuming b in L2.

All constructions below first use the operator-first convention [E,Z]=P.
Negating the constructed E gives the generator-first D in the statement.

### 2. Right creation and a free weighted estimate

The pointed free-product Hilbert space for N and W*(S) is the reduced-word model in [Voi98-U005, free products and the semicircular model](../audits/literature/Voi98/Yin_U005.md#voi98-u005).
On the semicircular factor use its orthonormal Chebyshev basis 1,u_1(S),u_2(S),..., with u_1(S)=S and S u_k=u_(k+1)+u_(k-1).
Let r be the right creation isometry: it increases the degree of a final semicircular block by one, and appends u_1(S) if the word ends in N or is the vacuum.
Directly on reduced words,

\[
 \rho(S)=r+r^*,\quad r^*1=0,\quad
 [r^*,S]=P,\quad [r^*,a]=[r,a]=0\quad(a\in N).
 \tag{5}
\]

Here rho(c) is right multiplication by c, while unmarked elements in commutators act on the left.
The source free-product interface supplies the end-action interpretation; (5) also follows by checking the vacuum and the final/initial block.
In particular r* agrees with (id tensor tau) partial_S on A_0, by the same generator commutators and value zero at 1.

For each bounded c in N and every xi in H,

\[
 \|\rho(c)r\xi\|_2=\|c\|_2\|\xi\|_2.
 \tag{6}
\]

Indeed the range of r consists of words ending in a nonvacuum semicircular block.
Decompose c=tau(c)1+c^circ.
On finite reduced vectors, scalar multiplication and appending the centered N-vector c^circ produce orthogonal sectors.
The latter operation is tensor concatenation, so its norm is ||c^circ||2 times the input norm.
Their squared norms add to ||c||2^2||xi||2^2.
Density proves (6).

For any c in L2(N), bounded approximants c_n converging in L2 make rho(c_n)r a Cauchy sequence in B(H), with norm difference exactly ||c_n-c_m||2.
Denote its limit by C_c.
In particular, if b=h^{-1} is in L2 and \(b_n=b\,1_{\{b\le n\}}\), then

\[
 C_b\in B(H),\qquad \|C_b\|=\|b\|_2,\qquad
 \rho(b)r=C_b\quad\hbox{on all }H.
 \tag{7}
\]

The last equality has an actual domain assertion.
Right multiplication by the positive affiliated b is a closed selfadjoint operator with spectral truncations rho(b_n).
Equation (6) implies that rho(b_n)r xi converges for every xi.
The spectral-domain criterion therefore puts r xi in Dom rho(b) and identifies its image with C_b xi.

### 3. The transported coordinate operator and its actual adjoint

Assume b in L2 and define the closed operator

\[
 K=\rho(b)r^*,\qquad
 \operatorname{Dom}K=\{\xi\in H:r^*\xi\in\operatorname{Dom}\rho(b)\}.
 \tag{8}
\]

It is closed because r* is bounded and rho(b) is closed.
Every a in M belongs to its domain: from rho(S)=r+r*,

\[
 r^*a=aS-ra.
\]

The vector aS is in M and hence in Dom rho(b); the vector ra is in that domain by (7).
Consequently

\[
 Ka=aSb-C_ba,\qquad
 \|Ka\|_2\le2\|b\|_2\|a\|,\qquad a\in M.
 \tag{9}
\]

Here ||Sb||2=||b||2 follows by freeness and tau(S^2)=1, first for bounded spectral cuts and then by L2 convergence.

For q in M, q is in Dom rho(b).
Pairing (8) with q on the entire domain of K gives the actual adjoint assertion

\[
 q\in\operatorname{Dom}K^*,\qquad K^*q=r(qb),\qquad
 \|K^*q\|_2\le\|b\|_2\|q\|.
 \tag{10}
\]

We use only this inclusion, not an unproved equality between complete adjoint-product domains.
In particular K*1=rb=bS: for bounded spectral cuts r b_n=b_n r1=b_nS by (5), and both sides converge in H.

Left multiplication by any element of M preserves Dom rho(b) and commutes with rho(b) there.
Equations (5), (8), and the fact that all tested inputs lie in M now give

\[
 [K,h]=0,\qquad [K,S]=bP,\qquad [K,hS]=P
 \quad\hbox{on }M.
 \tag{11}
\]

The map bP sends a to tau(a)b and is a bounded rank-one operator because b is in H.

### 4. A full dual system with both domains

On the dense domain M set

\[
 E_Y=K|_M,\qquad E_h=B|_M-K\rho(S)|_M.
 \tag{12}
\]

All products have actual domains because aS belongs to M.
Equations (4) and (11) imply

\[
 [E_Y,h]=0,\quad [E_Y,hS]=P,\qquad
 [E_h,h]=P,\quad [E_h,hS]=0.
 \tag{13}
\]

For the last identity, [B,hS]=PS, whereas [K rho(S),hS]=P rho(S)=PS.
The equality of the two rank-one maps uses traciality, tau(aS)=tau(Sa), and does not assert S P=P S.

For every q in M, the actual adjoints of the domain-M operators in (12) are defined and satisfy

\[
 E_Y^*q=K^*q,\qquad
 E_h^*q=B^*q-\rho(S)K^*q.
 \tag{14}
\]

This follows by testing all a in M and using (4), (10), and boundedness/selfadjointness of rho(S).
Thus both operators are closable, both actual adjoint domains contain M, and D_h=-E_h, D_Y=-E_Y prove S(X).
These witnesses need not be normalized at 1.

### 5. Represented starred derivations and their actual vacua

We prove A(X) directly rather than inferring it from a general S-to-A assertion.
For bounded polynomial tensor factors define the coefficient operations

\[
 I_{b\otimes1}(u\otimes v)=ub\otimes v,\qquad
 I_{1\otimes S}(\eta\otimes v)=\eta\otimes Sv.
 \tag{15}
\]

The first map is used only on finite sums of bounded polynomial tensors; no bounded extension to all H bar tensor H is claimed.
Both operations commute with the outer actions on their indicated domains.

Define on the represented algebra A_0

\[
 \delta_Y=I_{b\otimes1}\partial_S,\qquad
 \delta_h=\partial_h-I_{1\otimes S}\delta_Y.
 \tag{16}
\]

Their values belong to H bar tensor H, and the outer Leibniz rule follows from (15).
As hb=1, their generator values satisfy

\[
 \delta_Y(h)=0,\quad\delta_Y(hS)=1\otimes1,\qquad
 \delta_h(h)=1\otimes1,\quad\delta_h(hS)=0.
 \tag{17}
\]

Restrict (16) to A_X.
This is the actual represented starred algebra C<h,hS,Sh>, so all its relations and its starred values are respected by construction.

Let Q_Y=(id tensor tau)delta_Y and Q_h=(id tensor tau)delta_h.
On A_0,

\[
 Q_Y=K,\qquad Q_h=B-K\rho(S)+\rho(b).
 \tag{18}
\]

For the second equality, the Leibniz rule gives K(pS)=(id tensor tau)(delta_Y(p) dot S)+pb.
The trace of the second tensor leg is unchanged by moving S from its right to its left, which yields (18).

Equation (10) at 1 yields Q_Y*1=bS.
Similarly (14) at 1 and rho(b)*1=b give

\[
 Q_h^*1=\xi_h-\rho(S)(bS)+b
       =\xi_h-b(S^2-1).
 \tag{19}
\]

These are actual adjoint identities on the polynomial domains: pairing (18) with 1 is bounded by a fixed H vector.
Since <1 tensor 1,delta_i(p)>=<1,Q_i p>, the same vectors certify 1 tensor 1 in Dom delta_i*, with precisely (3).
This proves A(X).

For completeness the canonical contractions on A_X have both full bounded-vector domains as claimed.
Their domain-M extensions in (18) satisfy operator-norm-to-L2 estimates by (4), (9), and ||ab||2<=||a||||b||2.
The actual adjoints of those extensions contain M by (10), (14), and

\[
 \langle q,ab\rangle=\langle qb,a\rangle,\qquad a,q\in M,
\]

with b positive.
Thus Q_Y and Q_h are closable, and their core adjoints already contain M.
Apply Kaplansky approximation in A_X with uniformly bounded polynomial norms.
The images under Q_i are bounded in H; weak compactness and weak-closedness of the closed graph place each a in M in Dom overline{Q_i}.
Moreover (overline{Q_i})*=Q_i*, whose domain already contains M.
This conclusion does not identify the entire domain of overline{Q_Y} with Dom K.

### 6. Necessity for every bare full-domain witness

Suppose F(X) holds and let E=-D_Y be any Y-coordinate witness on M.
Its two commutator identities are

\[
 [E,h]=0,\qquad [E,hS]=P.
\]

The product expansion on M gives h[E,S]=P.
Evaluating at 1,

\[
 h\bigl(E(S)-S E(1)\bigr)=1.
 \tag{20}
\]

The bracket is an H vector.
Since h is positive and regular, the unique affiliated solution of hv=1 is h^{-1}; equivalently the spectral theorem applied to (20) gives tau(h^{-2})<infinity.
Therefore b belongs to H.
This argument assumes neither boundedness nor normalization of the supplied witness and excludes every possible bare witness when b is not in L2.

### 7. Necessity from the actual ACSY vacuum

Suppose A(X) holds, and take its Y-coordinate derivation with actual vacuum vector xi=delta_Y*(1 tensor 1).
For every scalar polynomial f,

\[
 \delta_Y(f(h)Y)=f(h)\otimes1.
\]

The adjoint identity and freeness therefore imply

\[
 |\tau(f(h))|
 \le\|\xi\|_2\,\|f(h)hS\|_2
 =\|\xi\|_2\,\|h f(h)\|_2.
 \tag{21}
\]

Let mu be the law of h, supported in [0,||h||] with no atom at zero.
The subspace t C[t] is dense in L2(mu), since polynomials are dense and multiplication by t has dense range.
Inequality (21) says that the functional

\[
 t f(t)\longmapsto\int f(t)\,d\mu(t)
\]

extends boundedly to L2(mu).
If w is its Riesz representative, then int (t overline{w(t)}-1) f(t) dmu(t)=0 for every polynomial f.
The coefficient defines a finite complex measure.
Polynomial density in the continuous functions of the compact interval gives t overline{w(t)}=1 almost everywhere.
Hence 1/t belongs to L2(mu), with ||h^{-1}||2<=||xi||2.

This step uses only polynomial inputs to the derivation.
No extension of an arbitrary supplied derivation to measurable or continuous functional calculus has been assumed.

Combining Sections 4-5, Sections 6-7, and the immediate S-to-F implication proves (2).

## Scope and unresolved direction

The decisive sufficient estimate is (6), which uses freeness and the semicircular right creation operator.
A bounded inverse is not required.
For a correlated selfadjoint reference pair, the formal candidate rho(h^{-1})B_S need not have the same estimate; deciding whether an appropriate weighted bound is forced remains a separate problem.
Neither that potential failure nor any failure of a chosen candidate alone refutes the existential implication A-to-F for general tuples.
