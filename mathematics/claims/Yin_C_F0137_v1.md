# C3 spectral graphs preserve bare duals and actual conjugate variables

## Current assessment
[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \((M,\tau)\) be a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal probability trace, \(H=L^2(M,\tau)\), \(\Omega=1\), and \(P=P_\Omega\).
Let \(S=(S_1,\ldots,S_d)\) be a bounded selfadjoint generating tuple.
For every \(i\), let \(I_i\) be a compact real interval containing \(\sigma(S_i)\), and let \(g_i\) be real-valued and \(C^3\) on an open real interval containing \(I_i\).
Set
\[
 X_i=S_i+\mathrm i g_i(S_i).
\]
Then \(X\) is normal and generates \(M\).
No condition on the joint law or free independence is imposed.

For a bounded generating tuple \(Y\), use the following exact conditions:
- \(\mathsf A(Y)\): complex-linear outer derivations
\[
 \delta_i:\mathbb C\langle Y,Y^*\rangle\subset H\longrightarrow H\otimes_2H
\]
satisfy \(\delta_i(Y_j)=\delta_{ij}1\otimes1\) and \(1\otimes1\in\operatorname{Dom}\delta_i^*\).
The domain is the represented full starred algebra, and the adjoint is an actual Hilbert-space adjoint.
- \(\mathsf F(Y)\): complex-linear maps \(D_i:M\Omega\to H\) satisfy \([Y_j,D_i]=\delta_{ij}P\) on all \(M\Omega\).
  No continuity, closability, or adjoint premise is imposed.
- \(\mathsf S(Y)\): densely defined operators \(D_i\) satisfy those commutators and \(M\Omega\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}D_i\cap\operatorname{Dom}D_i^*\).

The outer actions are \(a\cdot(u\otimes v)\cdot b=au\otimes vb\); inner products are linear in the second variable.
All conditions are existential.

The following conditions are equivalent:
\[
 \boxed{\mathsf A(X)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathsf A(S)\
 \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathsf F(S)\
 \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathsf F(X)\
 \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathsf S(X).}
 \tag{G1}
\]
When they hold, the canonical operators
\[
 -\overline{(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta_i},
 \qquad \operatorname{Dom}((\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta_i)
       =\mathbb C\langle X,X^*\rangle\Omega,
\]
and their actual adjoints have domains containing all \(M\Omega\).

The regularity threshold \(C^3\) is sufficient and is not asserted sharp.
The functions need not be analytic.
The statement does not concern arbitrary normal spectral sets, and does not assert equality of the unstarred rational fields generated by \(S\) and \(X\).

## Argument

The proof extends the inner-multiplier method of [C_F0040](Yin_C_F0040_v1.md#yin_c_f0040) and [C_F0041](Yin_C_F0041_v1.md#yin_c_f0041).
The new interface is weighted Fourier summability for \(C^3\) graphs, which supplies an actual multiplier-adjoint domain without a holomorphic extension.
The existing [C_F0042](Yin_C_F0042_v1.md#yin_c_f0042) has an embedded analytic-arc hypothesis and is not invoked for a nonanalytic graph.
The whole new argument is given below.

Since \(S_i=(X_i+X_i^*)/2\), \(W^*(X)=W^*(S)=M\), and \(\mathcal P_S=\mathbb C\langle S\rangle\) is a dense subalgebra of \(\mathcal P_X=\mathbb C\langle X,X^*\rangle\).
Equality of these polynomial algebras is not assumed.

### Fourier summability needed for the two changes of coordinates

Fix i and suppress its index.
Put
\[
 h(s,t)=g^{[1]}(s,t)=\int_0^1g'(\theta s+(1-\theta)t)\,d\theta,\quad
 J=1+\mathrm i h,\quad K=J^{-1}.
 \tag{G2}
\]
Since g is real on I, Re J=1 and J never vanishes on I squared.
Both J and K are C2 on a larger real rectangle.

Extend g with a C3 cutoff to a periodic C3 function on a larger real interval of period L, and put omega=2pi/L.
Its Fourier coefficients c_n satisfy
\[
 \sum_n(1+n^2)|c_n|<\infty .
 \tag{G3}
\]
Indeed Parseval for its third derivative gives sum n^6|c_n|^2<infinity, and Cauchy--Schwarz with sum_{n ne 0}n^{-2}<infinity gives sum n^2|c_n|<infinity; finitely many low modes cause no issue.
Thus the function and its first derivative have absolutely uniformly convergent Fourier series.
On I squared,
\[
 h(s,t)=\sum_n \mathrm i n\omega c_n
       \int_0^1e^{\mathrm i n\omega\theta s}
                   e^{\mathrm i n\omega(1-\theta)t}\,d\theta .
 \tag{G4}
\]
This is an absolutely convergent projective tensor expansion, with a Bochner integral of rank-one tensors.
In particular its projective norm is finite.
The factors are unitaries after evaluating at S, including when their frequency is not an integer.

Choose a C2 cutoff on a larger rectangle to extend K periodically to a C2 function on a two-dimensional torus.
Its Fourier coefficients d_mn satisfy
\[
 \sum_{m,n}|d_{mn}|<\infty.
 \tag{G5}
\]
Parseval for all derivatives of order at most two gives sum (1+m^2+n^2)^2 |d_mn|^2<infinity; Cauchy--Schwarz uses the convergent sum (1+m^2+n^2)^(-2).
The Fourier series is absolutely uniformly convergent and equals K, since it represents the same L2 function and both sides are continuous.
Thus K has an ordinary discrete projective expansion in unitary exponentials.
Cutoffs agree with the original functions on the full spectral intervals, not merely almost everywhere.

### A(X) implies A(S): actual adjoint of the J multiplier

Assume A(X), write delta_i for its actual starred derivation and xi_i=delta_i*(1 tensor 1).
The normal phase determination gives
\[
 \delta_i(S_j)=\delta_{ij}K_i.
 \tag{G6}
\]
The phase formula can be supplied by C_F0076 Part 1, or the original ACSY canonical-core and C_F0025 normal-phase interface.
Restrict delta_i to the dense P_S=C<S>, which is contained in the original starred X core.
Define theta_i=I_{J_i}delta_i on P_S.
The inner action commutes with all outer actions and (G6) gives theta_i(S_j)=delta_ij 1 tensor 1.

It remains to establish the actual vacuum.
For real alpha, polynomial approximation of exp(i alpha S_i), with uniform first derivative convergence, gives membership in the original closed delta_i domain and
\[
 \|\bar\delta_i(e^{\mathrm i\alpha S_i})\|_2\le|\alpha|.
 \tag{G7}
\]
Indeed its marginal kernel is the exponential divided difference times K_i, and |K_i|<=1.
These exponentials and their adjoints have graph approximants in the original core converging also in operator norm.

The actual tensor-adjoint calculation consequently gives, for a=e^{i alpha S_i}, b=e^{i beta S_i},
\[
 a\otimes b\in\operatorname{Dom}\delta_i^*,\qquad
 \|\delta_i^*(a\otimes b)\|_2
 \le \|\xi_i\|_2+|\alpha|+|\beta|.
 \tag{G8}
\]
To justify this at the original domain, first expand the vacuum pairing of a_n* p b_n* for polynomial approximants a_n,b_n.
The two differentiated-factor terms are bounded by ||delta_i(a_n*)||_2 ||b_n|| ||p||_2 and ||a_n|| ||delta_i(b_n*)||_2 ||p||_2.
The vacuum term is bounded by ||a_n|| ||b_n|| ||xi_i||_2 ||p||_2.
Uniform function and graph convergence makes the corresponding adjoint vectors Cauchy; closedness of delta_i* proves (G8).
The same argument proves graph continuity as alpha,beta vary on compact sets.

The adjoint applied to the n-th integral term of (G4), including its scalar coefficient, has norm at most
\[
 |n|\omega |c_n|\,(\|\xi_i\|_2+|n|\omega).
\]
This is summable by (G3).
Closedness of delta_i* and graph-continuous Bochner integration show that bar J_i=I_{J_i}^*(1 tensor 1) belongs to its actual domain.
Hence theta_i has actual vacuum
\[
 \zeta_i=\delta_i^*(\bar J_i).
 \tag{G9}
\]
This proves A(S), without presuming that an arbitrary bounded tensor multiplier preserves an adjoint domain.

### Projective correction of full-domain operators

For E:M Omega -> H bounded from the operator norm into H and a projective tensor z=sum a_l tensor b_l, set
\[
 Q_z(E)=\sum_l\rho(a_l)E\rho(b_l).
 \tag{G10}
\]
The series converges in B(M,H).
Formula (G4) is handled by the corresponding operator-norm Bochner integral; its integrands are continuous because exponential factors are operator-norm continuous.
All right multipliers commute with left M multiplication.
For any x for which [E,x]=Theta(v)|M,
\[
 [Q_z(E),x]=\Theta(I_zv)|M,
 \qquad\Theta(a\otimes b)=aPb.
 \tag{G11}
\]
This is first checked termwise and then passed through the absolutely convergent series.
The identities use the inner action I_{a tensor b}(u tensor v)=ua tensor bv.
No boundedness of E on H is assumed.

If E is an actual densely defined Hilbert operator with both M Omega contained in Dom E and Dom E*, and both restrictions are operator-norm-to-H bounded, the operator Q_z(E) with domain M Omega has actual adjoint containing M Omega.
Indeed its adjoint value there is the absolutely convergent series
\[
 \sum_l\rho(b_l^*)E^*\rho(a_l^*).
 \tag{G12}
\]
Pairing on M Omega proves this assertion; it is not just a formal adjoint notation.

### A(S) implies S(X)

Under A(S), the selfadjoint finite-Fisher bounds give the actual canonical E_i^S with both full M domains and both operator-norm-to-H bounds (Dab10-U002).
Their polynomial commutators extend to the original X_i:
\[
 [E_i^S,X_j]=\delta_{ij}\Theta(J_i)\quad\hbox{on }M\Omega .
 \tag{G13}
\]
For j=i, approximate the C1 function s+i g_i(s) by polynomials uniformly together with its derivative.
The divided differences converge uniformly to J_i, so the commutator kernels converge in Hilbert-Schmidt norm.
The full M-to-H bound for E_i^S makes E_i^S(p_n(S_i)b) converge for every bounded b.
This proves (G13).
For j different from i the same approximation preserves the zero commutator.

Apply (G10) to K_i and E_i^S.
Equations (G5), (G11), and I_Ki J_i=1 tensor 1 give
\[
 [Q_{K_i}(E_i^S),X_j]=\delta_{ij}P
 \quad\hbox{on all }M\Omega.
\]
Equation (G12) supplies all M Omega in the actual adjoint domain.
Therefore D_i=-Q_{K_i}(E_i^S), with its densely defined domain M Omega, realizes S(X).
It is closable because its adjoint domain is dense.
This argument constructs a strong system before invoking any canonical identification.

### F(X) implies F(S), and completion of the equivalence

Assume F(X).
C_F0069 supplies automatic operator-norm-to-H boundedness of the normalized positive dual E_i.
C_F0070 Part A gives the actual starred commutators, without an adjoint premise.
In particular
\[
 [E_i,S_j]=\delta_{ij}\Theta(K_i)\quad\hbox{on }M\Omega.
\]
The projective J_i of (G4), formula (G11), and I_Ji K_i=1 tensor 1 show that -Q_Ji(E_i) is a bare full-domain system for S.
Thus F(X) implies F(S).
C_F0071 Part B gives F(S) iff A(S).
The preceding paragraphs give A(X) -> A(S) -> S(X) -> F(X), while C_F0070 Part C gives S(X) -> A(X).
This proves (G1).

Finally C_F0075 Part 4 supplies the canonical compatibility: the normalized canonical X contractions and their adjoints are actually defined on all bounded vectors.
One must not identify an arbitrary supplied bare F system with these canonical full-domain operators; this conclusion follows after constructing or deriving S(X).

The C3 threshold is sufficient, not claimed sharp.
The graph can be nonanalytic, and the joint state is arbitrary.
No claim that S_i lies in the unstarred rational field of X is used or made.


## Proof interfaces, scope and self-review

The normal phase and normalization under bare full-domain maps use C_F0070 Part A, with its automatic boundedness input C_F0069.
Under the actual ACSY condition, the phase determination is C_F0076 Part 1; its later second-derivative results are not used.
The selfadjoint full-domain estimates are precisely Dab10-U002, and the bare selfadjoint converse is C_F0071 Part B.
The final canonical compatibility is C_F0075 Part 4 after a strong-domain system has actually been constructed.

The Fourier argument uses only the ordinary periodic Fourier coefficients, Parseval for derivatives, Cauchy--Schwarz and elementary smooth cutoffs.
The two summability estimates and their dimensions are proved above.
No theorem about arbitrary bounded tensor multipliers preserving adjoint domains, bounded Schur lifting, or smooth functions preserving noncommutative rational fields is invoked.

The author checked the weighted one-dimensional series for J, the unweighted two-dimensional series for K, actual exponential and tensor-adjoint domains, graph-continuous Bochner integration, the order of paired right multipliers, the actual adjoint series, the different polynomial cores and full-domain commutator passage.
The argument was developed under the authorized F0011 research in the assigned working material, then fixed here for independent review.
This is a project extension of the cited analytic graph mechanism, without a literature-priority assertion.
