## Statement

There is no finite numerical constant K such that every finite tracial realized partial liberation gamma of an abelian algebra N against an algebra B, with actual vacuum zeta=(i gamma)*(1 tensor 1) in L2(M), every finite bounded nonnegative real symmetric marginal tensor g, and every norm-one analytic-core unitary u satisfy

\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle u,(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)I_g\gamma(u)\rangle
 \ge-K\|(i\gamma)^*\sqrt g\|_2^2.
 \tag{1}
\]

The inner product is linear in its second variable.
Inner insertion is I_(x tensor y)(a tensor b)=a x tensor y b.
All square-root tensors in the counterexamples belong to the actual adjoint domain.

For every 0<t<1 the construction below has g=sqrt(g) a projection, bounded actual vacua, and a unitary u=a+i sqrt(1-a squared) with a selfadjoint core element of norm less than one, such that

\[
 \|(i\gamma)^*\sqrt g\|_2^2=9t^3/8,\qquad
 \operatorname{Re}\langle u,C_gu\rangle=t^2q(s),\quad
 q(s)=-s^2/8+O(s^3)<0.
 \tag{2}
\]

Here C_g=(id tensor tau)I_g gamma, s>0 is fixed sufficiently small independently of t, and all derivative values are actual analytic graph values.
The negative ratio therefore diverges as t decreases to zero.

This obstructs the arbitrary-weight estimate, not the normal ACSY comparison.
The displayed marginal is atomic, and g is not asserted to be the three-leg curvature kernel of a normal coordinate.

## Argument

Let \(0<t<1\), let \(N=\mathbb Cp\oplus\mathbb Cq\oplus\mathbb Cr\) have faithful trace masses \(t/2,t/2,1-t\), and let \(S\) be a standard semicircular variable free from N.
Work in their finite tracial free product.
Write \(p_1=p,p_2=q,p_3=r\), \(e=p+q\), and choose the symmetric entrywise positive coefficient matrix
\[
 a=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\1&2&1\\1&1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}p_iSp_j,\qquad
 R=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{-1}p_iSp_j.
 \tag{SC1}
\]
Here inverse entries are scalar reciprocals, not a matrix inverse.
In particular \(S=\sum a_{ij}^{-1}p_iTp_j\), so the represented cores generated by N,S and by N,T agree.
The coefficient substitution and its reciprocal are inverse algebraic substitutions over N.
Thus the represented partial liberation \(\gamma\), with \(\gamma n=n\otimes1-1\otimes n\) and \(\gamma T=0\), exists on that full core.

Let \(\gamma_0\) be liberation of N against the free S, whose actual vacuum is zero, and let \(\partial_S\) be the reference semicircular derivative.
Their actual vacuum assertions follow by testing reduced centered alternating words: a differentiated mixed word has a centered free factor on at least one trace leg; words within N have zero liberation trace, and words within the semicircular factor have conjugate vector S.
Hence gamma_0*1=0 and partial_S*1=S on the full reference core.
Solving the equation \(\gamma T=0\) gives
\[
 \gamma S=S\otimes1-1\otimes S
 -\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{-1}p_iT\otimes p_j
 +\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{-1}p_i\otimes Tp_j=:Q.
 \tag{SC2}
\]
Thus \(\gamma=\gamma_0+I_Q\partial_S\).
All coefficients are bounded reference-core elements.
The actual algebraic-tensor adjoint formula of [ACSY24-U003, equation (1)](../audits/literature/ACSY24/Yin_U003.md#acsy24-u003), with \(\partial_S^*1=S\), proves
\[
 \gamma^*1=RT-TR,\qquad
 \zeta=(i\gamma)^*1=-i[R,T].
 \tag{SC3}
\]
Indeed the contributions from \(S\otimes1-1\otimes S\) cancel.
Differentiating \(Tp_i\) and \(p_jT\) leaves scalar correction terms \(\sum_{i,j}(\tau(p_i)p_j-\tau(p_j)p_i)=0\); the remaining terms give the displayed commutator.
The vacuum is therefore an actual bounded vector.
This is a full represented liberation law, not a direct sum with a common central projection.

Take the finite bounded nonnegative symmetric weight
\[
 g=p\otimes q+q\otimes p=\sqrt g,
 \qquad \beta=i\gamma,\quad C_g=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)I_g\gamma.
\]
The actual finite-tensor adjoint formula, equivalently [C_F0077's finite symmetric marginal formula (S1)](Yin_C_F0077_v1.md#the-marginal-restriction-of-the-actual-liberation-adjoint), gives \(\eta=\beta^*g=S_g\zeta\).
For i not equal to j,
\[
 p_i[R,T]p_j=
 \sum_k\left(\frac{a_{kj}}{a_{ik}}-\frac{a_{ik}}{a_{kj}}\right)
 p_iSp_kSp_j.
 \tag{SC4}
\]
For (i,j)=(1,2), the three coefficients are \(-3/2,3/2,0\); they reverse for (2,1).
The free fourth-moment formula gives
\[
 \langle p_iSp_kSp_j,p_iSp_lSp_j\rangle
 =\delta_{kl}\tau(p_i)\tau(p_k)\tau(p_j)
 \quad(i\ne j).
\]
One can check this directly from \(E_N(SaSbScS)=\tau(a)\tau(c)b+\tau(b)\tau(ac)1\).
Consequently
\[
 \|\eta\|_2^2=\frac98t^3.
 \tag{SC5}
\]
The vanishing middle-r term is essential; it removes the extra outside noise contribution while preserving the full represented liberation.

For completeness, inserting a general finite marginal weight into (SC2) gives, for each ordered pair (u,v),
\[
 I_{p_u\otimes p_v}\gamma S
 =\sum_i\left(1-\frac{a_{iu}}{a_{iv}}\right)p_iSp_u\otimes p_v
 +\sum_j\left(\frac{a_{vj}}{a_{uj}}-1\right)p_u\otimes p_vSp_j.
 \tag{SC6}
\]
For u,v equal to 1,2 in either order, every term with i=3 or j=3 vanishes.
Also \(I_g\gamma e=0\).
Hence this weighted derivation, restricted to the core generated by p,q,eSe in eMe, is exactly the two-atom liberation for the compressed coefficients \(a_{11}=a_{22}=2,a_{12}=a_{21}=1\).

Use the normalized trace \(\tau_e=\tau/t\) and set \(V=eSe/\sqrt t\).
Then V is standard semicircular and free from \(\mathbb Cp\oplus\mathbb Cq\) in the corner.
The precise source available for the mixed-moment check is [NS06-U004](../audits/literature/NS06/Yin_U004.md#ns06-u004), equation (14.5), printed p.228/PDF p.245, directly read on 2026-10-15; its noncrossing-pairing formula with N coefficients, after inserting e and dividing each S by sqrt(t), gives the normalized-corner formula term by term.
The scalar normalization also follows from Theorem 14.10, printed p.230/PDF p.247, directly read.
The exact source statements and applicability are accepted; the general original combinatorial proof was not independently re-audited.
This does not apply Corollary 14.12 to p,q, since e is not free from them.
In particular \(\|V\|=2\), and the joint corner law is independent of t.

Write \(C^{(e)}\) for the ordinary two-atom liberation contraction with \(\tau_e\).
In this fixed corner model, \(T_e'=\sum_{i,j\le2}a_{ij}p_iVp_j\), and direct partial tracing of (SC2) gives
\[
 C^{(e)}V=V-\tfrac34T_e',\qquad
 \tau_e(VT_e')=\tfrac32,
 \operatorname{Re}\langle V,C^{(e)}V\rangle_e=-\tfrac18.
 \tag{SC7}
\]
For sufficiently small fixed \(s>0\), put \(u_s=sV+i\sqrt{e-s^2V^2}\) in the corner.
The differentiated binomial series converges in the actual liberation graph for \(2s<1\).
The vacuum trace of \(C^{(e)}V\) is zero.
Since gamma is anti-real, the real part of the vacuum trace of \(C^{(e)}V^2\) is zero.
Expanding the unitary and its adjoint therefore yields
\[
 q(s):=\operatorname{Re}\langle u_s,C^{(e)}u_s\rangle_e
 =-s^2/8+O(s^3)<0
 \tag{SC8}
\]
for all sufficiently small positive s.
The expansion and its error are in the fixed corner law and are independent of t.

The whole-algebra analytic unitary \(u=sV+i\sqrt{1-s^2V^2}=ir+u_s\) has norm one, and V is in the original N,T core by (SC1).
Its actual graph value follows from the same convergent binomial series.
Derivative tensor values in the corner have the same algebraic expression, but partial tracing with tau contributes t relative to tau_e.
The final scalar pairing contributes a second t.
Thus
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle u,C_gu\rangle=t^2q(s),\qquad
 \frac{-\operatorname{Re}\langle u,C_gu\rangle}{\|\beta^*\sqrt g\|_2^2}
 =\frac{8[-q(s)]}{9t}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{SC9}
\]
This disproves a numerical law-independent lower bound for all finite nonnegative symmetric weights under only an actual liberation vacuum.
All weights here are projections, all coefficients and vacua are bounded, and the tests are the specified analytic unitaries.
The obstruction is not a failure of actual domains.

The atomic marginal does not satisfy the normal ACSY coordinate hypothesis, and g is not the three-leg curvature kernel of such a coordinate.
The normal target remains open.
Curvature couples an edge weight to third spectral points, while the full normal vacuum costs the corresponding cross-edge commutator; these relations may rule out precisely the localization exploited in (SC5)-(SC9).
No normal separating example is claimed.
