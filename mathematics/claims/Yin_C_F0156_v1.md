## Statement

Let Z be bounded normal, N=W*(Z) abelian with faithful normal probability trace and diffuse compact spectral law mu, S standard semicircular free from N, and M=W*(N,S).
Let a be bounded measurable, real symmetric and strictly positive mu squared almost everywhere.
Write T=T_a for the bounded Gaussian element with first relative S-chaos vector a, characterized on finite kernels by T_(sum f tensor g)=sum f(Z)Sg(Z).
The proof below constructs it as a bounded strong limit, with ||T||<=2||a||infinity, and proves W*(Z,T)=M.

Assume the actual starred ACSY condition on the ORIGINAL target core A=C<Z,Z*,T>: represented outer Hilbert-tensor derivations delta_Z,delta_T have their prescribed unstarred coordinate values and actual vacuum vectors xi,psi in H=L2(M).
No reality, reference-domain extension, bounded inverse or regularity of a beyond these hypotheses is assumed.

Then both specified canonical contractions E_j=(id tensor tau)delta_j, with initial domain A Omega, are closable and
\[
 M\Omega\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}\overline E_j
                \cap\operatorname{Dom}E_j^*,\qquad j=Z,T.
\]
Their canonical closures satisfy the operator-first coordinate commutators on all bounded vectors.
Negating them gives the generator-first strong condition S and hence bare F.
These are the original target closures and actual adjoints, not chosen extensions.

The actual vacua are forced to have the following precise components.
With xi_N=E_N xi,
\[
 \psi=\Psi(1/a),\qquad \xi=\xi_N+\chi,
 \qquad
 \chi(z,w,v)=\frac{a(w,v)/a(z,w)-a(z,w)/a(w,v)}{\bar z-\bar v}.
\]
Here xi_N is the actual scalar marginal vacuum, Psi(1/a) belongs to relative S degree one, and chi belongs to degree two.
In particular 1/a is in L2(mu squared) and chi is in L2(mu cubed).
Put K0=sqrt(4/pi)(5+2 sqrt(27)) and K=sqrt(K0 squared+8).
The quantitative bounds on M are
\[
 \|\overline E_Zp\|_2\le K\|\xi\|_2\|p\|,
 \quad\|E_Z^*p\|_2\le(1+K)\|\xi\|_2\|p\|,
\]
\[
 \|\overline E_Tp\|_2\le2\|\psi\|_2\|p\|,
 \quad\|E_T^*p\|_2\le\|\psi\|_2\|p\|.
\]
The adjoint-reversed Z contraction has the same K||xi||2 bound.
Thus the actual curvature-weighted partial liberation contraction C_g satisfies Q_g(u)>=-(4K+2K squared)||xi||2 squared on analytic-core unitaries u=b+i sqrt(1-b squared), b=b* in A, ||b||<1.
Its definition is the actual normal mixed defect, not an assumption that the curvature kernel itself is a coarse Hilbert vector.

No equality between a maximal divided-difference domain and the original closure is claimed.
Polynomial tensors are not asserted to form a core for the whole adjoint.
The supplied target derivations are not applied to the hidden S before the new reference construction and canonical comparison.
The result concerns this Gaussian class, not arbitrary correlated normal tuples.

## Argument

### Actual target moment tests


The actual target vacuum is used before any attempt to differentiate the hidden S.
The following moment calculation forces the Hilbert coefficient of a new reference construction.

Use the hypotheses of the statement.
The normal relation forces delta_Z(Z*)=k and delta_T(Z*)=0, since outer left Z minus outer right Z is injective on the coarse Hilbert space: elementary outer spectral measures are absolutely continuous with respect to mu squared and its diagonal is null.
Thus delta_Z restricts to the usual scalar divided difference, and marginal compression gives its actual vacuum xi_N.
The standard tensor-adjoint formula makes the given derivation closable, so the actual smooth graph limits used below are legitimate.
Let f_0,f_2 be smooth marginal functions supported in positively separated disjoint sets, and let f_1 be a marginal polynomial.
Actual C1 graph approximation permits these endpoint functions in the delta_Z graph and preserves the vacuum pairing.
Set p=f_0 T f_1 T f_2.

Its conditional expectation is f_0 eta_a(f_1) f_2=0, where eta_a(f)(z)=integral a(z,w)^2 f(w)dmu(w).
Thus p is pure relative S degree two, with path coefficient f_0(z)a(z,w)f_1(w)a(w,v)f_2(v).
The middle Leibniz derivative contributes zero to the vacuum pairing because its two legs each contain one centered T.
The two endpoint derivatives give exactly
\[
 \langle\xi,p\rangle
 =\int f_0(z)f_1(w)f_2(v)
       \frac{a(w,v)^2-a(z,w)^2}{z-v}\,d\mu^3.
 \tag{M1}
\]
The terms containing f_0(v)f_2(v) or f_0(z)f_2(z) vanish by disjoint support.
Smooth separated-endpoint tensors and marginal polynomial middle factors determine L1 functions off the endpoint diagonal; diffuseness makes that diagonal null.
Since a(z,w)a(w,v)>0 almost everywhere, the H2 coefficient chi of xi is therefore
\[
 \chi(z,w,v)=\frac{a(w,v)/a(z,w)-a(z,w)/a(w,v)}{\bar z-\bar v}.
 \tag{M2}
\]
This is a distributional identification of an ALREADY EXISTING Hilbert vector, so chi is L2.
It does not assert a new target domain containing S.

Let delta_N denote the represented free reference derivation on C<Z,Z*,S>, equal to the scalar divided difference on C[Z,Z*] and zero on S; its actual vacuum is xi_N and its normalized contraction bounds are the C_F0045 free lift.
Define beta* by the two coefficient functions (r^-1-1)/(bar z-bar v) in H_0 tensor H_1 and (1-r)/(bar z-bar v) in H_1 tensor H_0, where r=a(z,w)/a(w,v).
These are the exact (G6) coefficients of [C_F0153](Yin_C_F0153_v1.md#argument).
The real numerators r^-1-1 and 1-r have the same sign.
Their squared norms add on the two orthogonal coefficient components, while the actual partial_S adjoint concatenates them onto the same H_2 path.
Hence ||beta||2<=||chi||2, exactly as in C_F0153 (G7)--(G8).
Their actual partial_S adjoint is chi by the arbitrary-base relative cut.
Hence the reference derivation delta'=delta_N+I_beta partial_S has actual vacuum xi_N+chi, and its contraction E' and actual adjoint have all bounded-vector domains and the estimates of that fixed proof.
This is a new reference construction, not yet identified with the supplied target derivative.

Because a is bounded, the formal first- and second-leg divided differences of a are L2: their corresponding degree-one coefficients are the outer multiplication of beta by a.
This gives a maximal divided-difference statement only; it does not establish that a belongs to the original closed tensor-leg graph.


### Weak algebraic-tensor integration by parts


Here is the complete missing scalar step.
Let D be the original normal scalar divided difference with actual vacuum xi_N, and suppose f is bounded with D_max f in L2(mu squared).
Put
\[
 K_\varepsilon(z-v)=\frac{\bar z-\bar v}{|z-v|^2+\varepsilon^2},
 \quad D_\varepsilon f=(f(z)-f(v))K_\varepsilon(z-v),
 \quad C_\varepsilon(z)=\int K_\varepsilon(z-v)d\mu(v).
 \tag{M4}
\]
C_F0055 Section 5 gives ||C_epsilon||2<=||xi_N||2.
For every starred polynomial p, antisymmetry yields
\[
 \langle2\overline{C_\varepsilon},p\rangle
 =\iint(p(z)-p(v))K_\varepsilon(z-v)d\mu^2
 \longrightarrow\langle\xi_N,p\rangle.
\]
The divided differences of p are bounded, so dominated convergence applies.
Density and the uniform L2 bound imply 2 conjugate(C_epsilon) tends weakly to xi_N in L2, without selecting a subsequence or asserting a principal value.

For marginal starred polynomials b,c, the bounded soft operator has the exact adjoint formula
\[
 D_\varepsilon^*(b\otimes c)
 =2bc\,\overline{C_\varepsilon}
       -b E^\#_\varepsilon c-c E^\#_\varepsilon b,
 \tag{M5}
\]
where E#_epsilon c(z)=integral (c(z)-c(v))conjugate(K_epsilon(z-v))dmu(v).
This follows by expanding the two terms f(z)-f(v), interchanging z,v in the second, and using antisymmetry.
The two difference terms converge strongly in L2 to E#c and E#b, since their quotient kernels are uniformly bounded and the soft scalar factors converge almost everywhere.
Therefore the right side of (M5) converges weakly to
\[
 bc\xi_N-bE^\#c-cE^\#b=D^*(b\otimes c).
\]
The final equality is the actual algebraic tensor adjoint identity, which can also be verified directly by testing the same limit against original polynomials.

Meanwhile D_epsilon f=|z-v| squared/(|z-v| squared+epsilon squared) times D_max f, so it converges STRONGLY in L2 to D_max f.
Pairing (M5) against fixed f consequently proves
\[
 \langle b\otimes c,D_{\max}f\rangle
       =\langle D^*(b\otimes c),f\rangle.
 \tag{M6}
\]
This is only against the specified algebraic tensor tests.
It does not identify their span as a core for D* or identify maximal and minimal derivative domains.
Tensoring an additional L2(mu) variable gives the same result for a bounded two-variable kernel with either formal leg derivative in L2: first prove it for product test vectors by (M5), then use their finite linear span.
Weak convergence of the adjoint test tensored with a fixed polynomial factor is sufficient for this assertion.

### Bounded Gaussian kernels and the original reference commutator

For completeness T_a is a bounded actual element for bounded Hermitian a.
In the relative Wick representation of C_F0153 (G1), its creation part maps
\[
 f(z_1,\ldots,z_{n+1})\longmapsto
 a(z_0,z_1)f(z_1,\ldots,z_{n+1}),
\]
with norm at most ||a||infinity; its annihilation is the adjoint, because a is Hermitian.
Thus ||T_a||<=2||a||infinity.
Finite separated marginal kernels approximate a almost everywhere with a common supremum bound.
Their creation and annihilation actions converge strongly by dominated convergence, so T_a lies in the original free product algebra and has first-chaos vector a.
Real symmetric smooth polynomial kernels a_n can be chosen with a_n tending to a in L2 and almost everywhere and ||a_n||infinity<=||a||infinity+1, by bounded continuous approximation, polynomial approximation and symmetrization.
No bound on their derivatives is needed.

For these a_n, T_n belongs to the original reference polynomial core, is uniformly bounded and converges strongly and in L2 to T_a.
The explicit derivative delta_N T_n has only the relative degree components H_0 tensor H_1 and H_1 tensor H_0.
Against reference polynomial tensor tests u tensor v, only the corresponding degree-zero and degree-one Wick projections of u and v contribute; they are finite sums with polynomial marginal factors.
Applying the parameterized identity (M6) to a shows
\[
 \langle u\otimes v,\delta_NT_n\rangle
 \longrightarrow\langle u\otimes v,G_a\rangle,
 \tag{M7}
\]
where G_a is its formal two-leg divided-difference tensor.
To see why this is a genuine limit despite possibly unbounded derivative norms, first move delta_N to the actual adjoint of the fixed algebraic test and use T_n tending to T_a in L2; (M6) identifies that limit with the displayed formal tensor.
For tensor tests of any other relative degree, the pairing is identically zero for every n and remains zero.
No second-derivative or maximal-domain assertion is used.

The tensor G_a exists in L2 because its two coefficients are a(z,v) times the corresponding coefficients of minus beta, with bounded a.
Moreover
\[
 I_\beta\partial_ST_n=J_{a_n}\beta\longrightarrow J_a\beta=-G_a
 \quad\hbox{strongly in the coarse Hilbert space}.
 \tag{M8}
\]
Here J_a is the OUTER multiplier by a at the two external endpoints.
The outer spectral measures of the two explicit degree-one components of beta are absolutely continuous with respect to mu squared, so bounded almost-everywhere convergence of a_n gives the strong convergence by dominated convergence in mu cubed.

Construct E' as the ORIGINAL closure of the contraction of delta_N+I_beta partial_S on the reference core.
Its actual vacuum and both contraction estimates give all bounded-vector domains by C_F0072; values on M equal the sum of the two already constructed full-domain contractions, by bounded strong-to-weak graph approximation.
The same reasoning gives its full bounded adjoint domain.
For reference-core u,v, the universal tensor identity
\[
 \langle u,[E',T_n]v\rangle
       =\langle u\otimes v^*,(\delta_N+I_\beta\partial_S)T_n\rangle
\]
now tends to zero by (M7)--(M8).
For bounded u,v, the actual full adjoint domain gives <u,[E',T_n]v>=<E'^*u,T_n v>-<T_n^*u,E'v>.
Uniform strong convergence of T_n therefore identifies the limit as <u,[E',T_a]v>.
Density first in u and then bounded core approximation in v prove [E',T_a]=0 on ALL bounded vectors.
The same original-core and full-adjoint argument gives [E',Z]=P and its normal starred commutator on M.

Thus the normalized restriction of E' to the target core agrees with the supplied canonical Z contraction: their commutators agree on Z,Z*,T and both kill 1, so induction on words proves equality.
Core bounds and the actual adjoint formula restore the original TARGET closure and adjoint by C_F0072; equality of the closures of a sum was never presumed.
The supplied vacuum must then equal xi_N+chi by density of the target core, so no unaccounted higher-chaos component remains.

### The other coordinate and generation

The actual T-coordinate target derivation kills Z and Z*: the normal commutation relation and diffuse outer spectral diagonal give the latter from the former.
Testing its vacuum psi on f(Z)T_a g(Z) gives
\[
 \langle\psi_{H_1}, f(z)a(z,v)g(v)\rangle
          =\tau(f)\tau(g).
\]
Density of polynomial tensor factors implies a conjugate(psi_H1)=1 almost everywhere.
For positive a this gives psi_H1=1/a and, in particular, 1/a in L2(mu squared).
If zero values of a had initially been allowed, the same identity would exclude a zero set of positive mu squared measure.

The reference weighted derivative I_(1/a)partial_S has total coefficient degree zero, hence actual vacuum and both full bounded domains by C_F0153 (G3).
Its commutators with Z,Z* vanish.
For T_n above, its derivative is a_n/a, converging to 1 in L2 by dominated convergence against the finite measure |1/a| squared dmu squared.
The same actual-adjoint matrix-element argument gives its commutator with T_a equal to P on all bounded vectors, and target-core uniqueness restores the two original T canonical domains.

Finally a nonzero bounded a makes W*(N,T_a) the whole reference algebra.
For bounded q_n=min(n,1/a), the bounded Gaussian kernel a q_n is approximated by products a q_(n,l) with finite bounded marginal tensor q_(n,l) tending to q_n almost everywhere and in L2.
The vectors T_(a q_(n,l)) are finite outer N combinations of T_a, hence lie in the target algebra; the common bound 2||a||infinity n and the creation/annihilation description give strong convergence to T_(a q_n).
As n increases, 0<a q_n<=1 and a q_n tends to 1 almost everywhere, so these latter operators tend strongly to S.
Therefore S belongs to W*(N,T_a).
This proves the generation assertion needed for the vacuum uniqueness and dense target-core step above.

This proves the stated original canonical-domain result.
The free Gaussian reference remains substantive; no corresponding coefficient decomposition or proof for an arbitrary correlated normal law is asserted.

Initially xi_N and chi are orthogonal projections of xi; after canonical identification they exhaust it.
The actual core adjoint p xi-F'p gives the stated target adjoint bound.

 \|E_Zu\|_2^2+\|F_Zu\|_2^2

   +2\operatorname{Re}\langle F_Zu,u\xi\rangle-Q_g(u).

Its actual phase tensor-leg and analytic first-derivative domains follow under the present normal ACSY hypotheses; no growth assumption is used for this identity.
The two K||xi||2 bounds give the claimed lower estimate directly.
