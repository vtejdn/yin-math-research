## Statement and conventions

Let (M,tau) be a finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial state.
Fix positive integers m and r, and let s_1,...,s_(2r) be freely independent standard semicircular elements, normalized by tau(s_j)=0 and tau(s_j s_l)=delta_(jl).
Set B=M_m(C), E=id_B tensor tau, and identify x in B with x tensor 1.
Define
\[
 c_k=\frac{s_{2k-1}+i s_{2k}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 T=\sum_{k=1}^r d_k\otimes c_k,
 \qquad d_1,\ldots,d_r\in M_m(\mathbb C).
\]
Then the following assertions hold.

1. The c_k are freely independent standard circular elements, with tau(c_k c_k*)=tau(c_k* c_k)=1 and tau(c_k^2)=0.
2. T is a centered semicircular pencil with coefficients
   \[
   a_{2k-1}=d_k/\sqrt2,\qquad a_{2k}=i d_k/\sqrt2.
   \]
   Its coefficient maps satisfy, for every x in B,
   \[
   \rho(x):=\sum_j a_jxa_j=0,\qquad
   \eta(x):=\sum_j a_jxa_j^*=\sum_kd_kxd_k^*,\qquad
   \eta^*(x)=\sum_kd_k^*xd_k.
   \]
   Here eta* denotes the adjoint of eta for the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product Tr(x* y).
3. With respect to E, the only possibly nonzero B-valued cumulants of (T,T*) are its two mixed second cumulants,
   \[
   \kappa_2^E(Tx,T^*)=E(TxT^*)=\eta(x),\qquad
   \kappa_2^E(T^*x,T)=E(T^*xT)=\eta^*(x).
   \]
   Thus T is a B-valued circular element, in the sense of [DG24, Theorem 2.9(b) and Definition 2.10](../audits/literature/DG24/Yin_U001.md#dg24-u001), arXiv v2, printed/PDF p. 6.
   Its B-valued *-distribution is invariant under T -> exp(i theta) T for every real theta.

The same cumulant and rotation conclusions in item 3 hold for any centered pencil T=sum_j a_j tensor s_j in free standard semicircular elements whenever rho(x)=sum_j a_j x a_j vanishes for every x in B.
This last assertion does not require a chosen circular coefficient pairing.

## Proof

### Circular normalization and coefficient maps

The definition c_k=(s_(2k-1)+i s_(2k))/sqrt(2) is the standard circular normalization.
The two centered semicircular elements in each pair have variance one and zero mixed second moments.
Expanding c_k c_k*, c_k* c_k and c_k^2 therefore gives the three scalar second moments in item 1.
The scalar cumulant argument below also verifies that these are the only nonzero scalar *-cumulants of each c_k.
The unital *-algebras generated by disjoint pairs of a free family are free, so the c_k are free.

Expanding T gives the displayed semicircular coefficients.
The complex scalar i commutes with all x in B, and hence
\[
 \rho(x)=\frac12\sum_k\bigl(d_kxd_k+(i d_k)x(i d_k)\bigr)=0.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 \eta(x)=\frac12\sum_k
       \bigl(d_kxd_k^*+(i d_k)x(i d_k)^*\bigr)
       =\sum_kd_kxd_k^*.
\]
For x,y in B, cyclicity of the ordinary matrix trace gives
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\eta(x)^*y)
 =\sum_k\operatorname{Tr}(x^*d_k^*yd_k)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(x^*\eta^*(y)),
\]
which proves the stated adjoint formula.
Also E(T)=0.

### Matrix-valued cumulants

Write u_j=1_m tensor s_j.
For q>=1, indices j_1,...,j_q and matrices A_1,...,A_q,x_1,...,x_(q-1) in B, the B-valued cumulant formula is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\kappa_q^E(A_1u_{j_1}x_1,\ldots,
          A_{q-1}u_{j_{q-1}}x_{q-1},A_qu_{j_q})\\
 &\qquad=
 A_1x_1A_2x_2\cdots A_{q-1}x_{q-1}A_q\,
 \kappa_q^\tau(s_{j_1},\ldots,s_{j_q}).                 \tag{1}
\end{aligned}
\]
For q=1 the matrix product on the right is A_1.
To verify (1), note that each u_j commutes with B, so the corresponding E-moment factors into the ordered matrix product on the right times the scalar tau-moment.
Substitute the proposed cumulants into the noncrossing moment--cumulant recursion: each nested block contracts its scalar cumulant and preserves the order of the inserted matrix factors.
The resulting sum is that matrix product times the scalar moment--cumulant sum, which is the required E-moment.
Uniqueness in this recursion proves (1).
The adopted B-valued recursion is DG24, Definition 2.5, printed/PDF pp. 4--5.

The scalar cumulants of a free standard semicircular family satisfy
\[
 \kappa_q^\tau(s_{j_1},\ldots,s_{j_q})=
 \begin{cases}
 \delta_{j_1j_2},&q=2,\\
 0,&q\ne2.
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{2}
\]
This is the usual semicircular/free-cumulant characterization; see [MS17, Chapter 2, Section 2, Exercise 9 and Theorem 14](../literature/pdf/MS17.pdf), printed pp. 34 and 37 / PDF pp. 46 and 49.
We use the scalar moment--cumulant characterization and the closure of freeness under grouping disjoint generated algebras as standard foundational inputs, rather than asserting a new proof of free probability's foundational equivalences.

For any centered pencil T=sum_j a_j u_j, expand each argument T or T* in its B-valued cumulants and use (1)--(2).
All orders other than two vanish.
Centering identifies the second cumulants with the four second moments
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E(TxT)&=\sum_j a_jxa_j=\rho(x),&
 E(T^*xT^*)&=\sum_j a_j^*xa_j^*=\rho(x^*)^*,\\
 E(TxT^*)&=\sum_j a_jxa_j^*=\eta(x),&
 E(T^*xT)&=\sum_j a_j^*xa_j=\eta^*(x).
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{3}
\]
The adjoint formula in the last expression follows from the same trace calculation as above.
Thus rho=0 leaves only the mixed second cumulants.
By the cumulant characterization in DG24, Theorem 2.9(b) and Definition 2.10, this is precisely the required B-valued circular property.
Applying the same expansion with scalar paired coefficients 1/sqrt(2) and i/sqrt(2) verifies the scalar circular cumulants asserted in item 1.

### Rotation invariance

Replacing T by exp(i theta)T multiplies a cumulant with p occurrences of T and q occurrences of T* by exp(i(p-q)theta).
Each potentially nonzero cumulant in (3) has p=q=1, while all other cumulants vanish.
Thus every B-valued cumulant remains the same.
The moment--cumulant recursion then shows that all B-valued *-moments, with arbitrary inserted matrices from B, remain the same.
This is the claimed invariance of the B-valued *-distribution.

## Application and review boundary

The construction has a_0=0 and rho=0, exactly the hypotheses of the radial argument in [C_F0170, Sections 1--4](Yin_C_F0170_v1.md).

The scalar case m=1 gives covariance eta(x)=(sum_k |d_k|^2)x, consistently with the normalization in item 1.
