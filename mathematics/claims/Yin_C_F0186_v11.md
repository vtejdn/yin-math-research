<a id="model"></a>
## Original complex fixed-law model

Fix \(d\ge1\).
For each \(j\), fix a complex law \(\xi_j\) with \(\mathbb E\xi_j=0\), \(\mathbb E|\xi_j|^2=1\), and a real law \(\zeta_j\) with \(\mathbb E\zeta_j=0\), \(\mathbb E\zeta_j^2<\infty\).
The real and imaginary parts of one \(\xi_j\) need not be independent.
For each \(N\), the Hermitian matrix \(W_j^{(N)}\) has independent strict upper entries of law \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), conjugate lower entries, and independent diagonal entries of law \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\).
Write \(\zeta_{j,k}\) for the raw diagonal variable of input \(j\) at physical coordinate \(k\).
All upper and diagonal families and all \(d\) matrices are independent within each dimension.
The laws are fixed independently of \(N\), may differ between inputs, and may be atomic.
All dimensions may have any coupling preserving these complete within-dimension laws.
No density, moment above two, or independence between dimensions is imposed.

Let \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal trace \(\tau\).
Write \(\tau_m=\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau\).
All coefficients, degrees, auxiliary sizes and factor counts below are fixed independently of \(N\).
Use ordinary determinants and FK determinants including zero values, with \(\log0=-\infty\).
Call an input purely imaginary when \(\operatorname{Re}\xi_j=0\) almost surely.
Let
\[
 \mathcal E=\{j:\mathbb P(\operatorname{Re}\xi_j\ne0)>0\}
\]
be the eligible exposed indices.
No condition is imposed on the other inputs beyond the stated complex Hermitian model.

## Exact determinant statements

<a id="scalar-affine-statement"></a>
### Arbitrary complex scalar affine coefficients

For \(a_0,\ldots,a_d\in\mathbb C\), put
\[
 Z_N=a_0I_N+\sum_{j=1}^d a_jW_j^{(N)},\qquad
 z=a_0+\sum_{j=1}^d a_js_j,\qquad
 \ell_N=N^{-1}\log|\det Z_N|.
\]
If all \(a_j\), \(j\ge1\), vanish, then \(\ell_N=\log|a_0|=\log\Delta_\tau(z)\) identically, including \(-\infty\) when \(a_0=0\).
Otherwise the free target \(c=\log\Delta_\tau(z)\) is finite and
\[
 \ell_N\longrightarrow c\quad\hbox{in probability through even }N,
 \qquad
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\ell_N=c
 \quad\hbox{almost surely under every allowed coupling}.
 \tag{Affine-even-and-limsup}
\]
An index \(j\) is active when \(a_j\ne0\).
The probability limit holds through all dimensions except in the following exact branch:
\[
 a_0=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Re}\xi_j=0\ \hbox{a.s.},\qquad
 \zeta_j=0\ \hbox{a.s.}
 \quad\hbox{for every active }j.
 \tag{Affine-skew-exception}
\]
In that branch every odd-dimensional determinant vanishes, while the even-dimensional probability limit still has the finite target \(c\).
Thus a full-sequence probability limit to \(c\) is impossible in that branch.

<a id="one-variable-matrix-statement"></a>
### Arbitrary matrix polynomials in one Hermitian variable

Let \(W_N\) be any single input of the model, and let \(s\) denote a corresponding variance-one semicircular element.
Fix \(m\ge1\) and \(H(t)=\sum_{r=0}^D B_rt^r\in M_m(\mathbb C[t])\), with arbitrary fixed complex matrix coefficients.
They need not commute and may be singular.
Set \(q(t)=\det_mH(t)\).
If \(q\equiv0\), then every finite \(H(W_N)\) is singular and \(\log\Delta_{\tau_m}(H(s))=-\infty\).
If \(q\not\equiv0\), the target \(u=\log\Delta_{\tau_m}(H(s))\) is finite and
\[
 \frac1{mN}\log|\det H(W_N)|
       \longrightarrow u\quad\hbox{in probability through even }N,
 \qquad
 \limsup_N\frac1{mN}\log|\det H(W_N)|=u
 \quad\hbox{almost surely under every allowed coupling}.
 \tag{One-variable-matrix}
\]
For \(q\not\equiv0\), convergence in probability holds through all dimensions unless the input is purely imaginary with zero diagonal law and \(q(0)=0\).
In that exceptional case every odd-dimensional determinant vanishes.
Nonzero constant \(q\) gives its normalized constant logarithm at every dimension.

The same conclusion applies to \(H(Y_N)\) for
\[
 Y_N=b_0I_N+\sum_j b_jW_j^{(N)},\qquad b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_d\in\mathbb R,
\]
by applying the one-input result to the normalized combined law described in the proof.
The probability classification is that for this combined law and the substituted polynomial, not a claim inferred separately from the original input laws.
The case \(\sum_jb_j^2=0\) is the direct constant matrix \(H(b_0)\otimes I_N\).
This includes arbitrary \(A_0+B\sum_jb_jW_j\), but does not include several unrelated coefficient directions of a general multi-input pencil.

<a id="carrier-statement"></a>
### Complex carriers with eligible exposed indices

Relabel an index of \(\mathcal E\) as \(1\).
Fix an arbitrary scalar polynomial \(Q(x_2,\ldots,x_d)\), a nonzero scalar \(c\), \(K\ge0\), and
\[
 T_\nu=x_{j_\nu}+Q_\nu((x_j)_{j\ne1,j_\nu}),
 \qquad j_\nu\in\mathcal E\setminus\{1\},
 \qquad
 H=cT_1\cdots T_K,\qquad R=(x_1+Q)H.
 \tag{Complex-carrier}
\]
Every \(Q_\nu\) is an arbitrary scalar polynomial in the displayed allowed variables.
Take \(H=c\) when \(K=0\).
Indices may repeat and factors may share inputs and be dependent.
No reversal symmetry is required.
Only the actual exposed indices \(1,j_1,\ldots,j_K\) must lie in \(\mathcal E\); all background variables may have arbitrary complex Hermitian laws of the model.

For every fixed \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 N^{-1}\log|\det(R(W)-\lambda I)|
   \longrightarrow \log\Delta_\tau(R(s)-\lambda)\in\mathbb R
       \quad\hbox{in probability through all }N,
\]
and the limsup equals that target almost surely under every allowed coupling.
The assertion includes \(\lambda=0\).
It also holds for \(\widetilde R=H_{\rm L}(x_1+Q)H_{\rm R}\) whenever \(H_{\rm L},H_{\rm R}\) are fixed scalar polynomials omitting \(x_1\) and \(H_{\rm R}H_{\rm L}\) has exactly the displayed certificate.
In particular separate right and left certificates concatenate in that order, with shared variables and repeated indices permitted.

For either carrier form, every fixed scalar polynomial \(g\) and parameter \(\lambda\) have the same probability and limsup conclusions for \(g(R)-\lambda\).
The target is finite if \(g(t)-\lambda\) is a nonzero polynomial.
If it vanishes identically, both finite and free logarithms are identically \(-\infty\).
The zero-shift conclusion also holds for every finite ordered product
\[
 S=\prod_{h=1}^q g_h(R_h),
\]
where the carriers may use different eligible exposed indices of the same original inputs and factors may share variables and fail to commute.
The target is finite when every \(g_h\) is nonzero; a zero factor makes both products zero; the empty product is the identity with target zero.
No nonzero-shift conclusion for a general ordered product follows solely from this zero-shift assertion.

The events may depend on the fixed coefficients, polynomials and parameters.
No common event for an uncountable parameter family, full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic limit, full Brown theorem or completion of the original general Tasks is asserted.

## Complete candidate argument

<a id="floor-interfaces"></a>
### Precisely selected probability interfaces

A centered variance-one complex law is either supported in a real line through zero or is not.
In the line case write \(\xi=c\eta\), where \(|c|=1\) and \(\eta\) is real centered variance one.
If the line is not the imaginary axis, \(\operatorname{Re}c\ne0\) and the accepted C_F0194_v6 main floor applies.
If the law is not supported in such a line, use the exact [C_F0194_v7 non-line candidate floor](Yin_C_F0194_v7.md#nonline-floor).
Thus every eligible input has a uniform polynomial least-value floor in probability under arbitrary deterministic polynomially bounded complex shifts, with its independent-random-shift extension.
This unification depends on the candidate v7 conclusion; it is not asserted to be already reviewed.
Centering rules out an affine support line not passing through zero.

For purely imaginary upper law \(i\eta\), use the [v7 candidate quantile-guard floor](Yin_C_F0194_v7.md#quantile-guard-floor).
In this one-input interface, let \(d_1,\ldots,d_N\) denote the raw diagonal copies of its fixed real law.
For an independent shift \(F_N\) with entry guard \(\Gamma_N\), this floor is uniform on
\[
 \Sigma_N(F_N,L_0)=
 \bigcap_i\left\{
 s_{k_N}\!\left(
    \operatorname{diag}(d_j/\sqrt N)_{j\ne i}
      +\operatorname{sym}(F_N)_i\right)\ge N^{-L_0}
 \right\},
 \qquad k_N=4\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor+1.
\]
Its unguarded failure probability adds \(\mathbb P(\Gamma_N^c\cup\Sigma_N^c)\).
The guard may involve the noise diagonal and is not conditioned on as an independent event.
For the zero-diagonal purely imaginary input and a complex skew shift, use the [v7 candidate even floor](Yin_C_F0194_v7.md#even-floor).
Only even dimensions are controlled by that conclusion.
These are precisely the new floor interfaces needed below; no arbitrary-shift purely imaginary least-value theorem is assumed.

<a id="norm-and-log-transfer"></a>
### Polynomial norm guards and logarithmic transfer

For any fixed scalar polynomial \(P\) of degree \(D\ge1\), C_F0186_v4's complex-input power tail gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|P(W)\|>t\}
 \le N\,\mathbb E\mu_{|P(W)|}((t,\infty))
 \le C_PNt^{-2/D}.
\]
Choosing a sufficiently large fixed power \(t=N^M\) supplies any requested polynomial failure exponent.
Constant polynomials are immediate.
This is the norm calculation used for every polynomial shift below, and uses no moment above two.

Here is the common probability-level logarithmic passage.
Let \(P\) be a fixed scalar polynomial, and let \(\mathcal N\) be either all dimensions or the even dimensions.
Suppose a fixed \(L\ge1\), \(c,C,\alpha>0\) give, on an event of probability tending to one along \(\mathcal N\),
\[
 a_1(P(W))\ge N^{-\alpha},\qquad
 a_k(P(W))\ge c(k/N)^L
       \quad(CN^{3/4}\le k\le N),
 \tag{Floor-and-middle}
\]
where \(a_1\le\cdots\le a_N\) are increasing singular values.
C_F0186_v4 supplies a deterministic limiting singular law \(\nu\) and weak convergence in probability, also along \(\mathcal N\).
The middle bound gives its finite-dimensional count below \(t\) at most \(CN^{3/4}+C'Nt^{1/L}\).
At a positive continuity point, a violation \(\nu([0,t])>C't^{1/L}\) would contradict convergence in probability of that count and the probability tending to one of the upper bound.
Decreasing continuity points to any \(t>0\) gives \(\nu([0,t])\le C't^{1/L}\).
It excludes a zero atom and gives finite negative free logarithm by integration against \(dt/t\).
The free operator is bounded, so the positive free logarithm is also finite.

On the good event the bottom \(O(N^{3/4})\) normalized negative logarithms are bounded by \(O(N^{-1/4}\log N)\).
The other values below \(t\) contribute at most \(C''t^{1/L}(1+|\log t|)+o(1)\), by summing the displayed middle estimate.
Thus negative logarithmic tails are negligible in probability as \(t\downarrow0\).
C_F0186_v4 controls the positive logarithmic tails.
Convergence for a continuous logarithm clipped at fixed lower and upper cutoffs, followed by removal of both tails, proves convergence in probability of \(N^{-1}\log|\det P(W)|\) to its finite free logarithm along \(\mathcal N\).
No expectation convergence of unregularized logarithms or first Borel--Cantelli step on nonsummable floor errors is used.

The fixed-regularizer argument in C_F0186_v4 supplies the full-sequence almost-sure upper bound for the same fixed polynomial under every allowed coupling.
Probability convergence along \(\mathcal N\) supplies the marginal lower-deviation criterion of C_F0190_v1.
Equivalently choose a deterministic subsequence inside \(\mathcal N\) whose failures at tolerances \(1/j\) have probabilities at most \(2^{-j}\), and apply first Borel--Cantelli there.
The full-sequence limsup is therefore the finite target almost surely under every coupling.
This argument applies along even dimensions without asserting a full-sequence probability limit.

<a id="scalar-affine-proof"></a>
### Full scalar-affine classification

The constant case is the exact finite and free determinant identity.
Otherwise choose an active index \(j_0\).
If any active index is eligible, choose such an index and write
\[
 Z_N/a_{j_0}=W_{j_0}^{(N)}+F_N,\qquad
 F_N=(a_0I+\sum_{j\ne j_0}a_jW_j^{(N)})/a_{j_0}.
\]
The shift is independent of the exposed input and has polynomial norm guards with arbitrary polynomial failure.
Condition on its realization, use the unified eligible floor, add the guard failure, and absorb the fixed scalar into the floor exponent.
This gives a least-value floor in probability through all dimensions.

Suppose all active off-diagonal laws are purely imaginary.
Then each has upper law \(i\eta_j\), and the off-diagonal part of each active input is transpose-skew.
With the same independent-shift decomposition,
\[
 \operatorname{sym}(Z_N/a_{j_0})
  =\operatorname{diag}\left(
     a_0/a_{j_0}+\nu_1/\sqrt N,\ldots,
     a_0/a_{j_0}+\nu_N/\sqrt N\right),
 \quad
 \nu_i=\sum_{j\ {\rm active}}(a_j/a_{j_0})\zeta_{j,i}.
\]
The \(\nu_i\) are iid copies of a fixed centered complex finite-second-moment law.
Their real and imaginary parts may be dependent.
If \(a_0\ne0\), then
\[
 \mathbb P\{\max_i|\nu_i|>
       |a_0/a_{j_0}|\sqrt N/2\}
 \le N\mathbb P\{|\nu|>|a_0/a_{j_0}|\sqrt N/2\}\to0,
\]
since \(t^2\mathbb P\{|\nu|>t\}
 \le\mathbb E[|\nu|^2{\bf1}_{\{|\nu|>t\}}]\to0\).
On the complement every principal symmetric part has least value at least \(|a_0/a_{j_0}|/2\), and the candidate quantile guard holds with \(L_0=1\) eventually.
If \(a_0=0\) and \(\nu\) is not identically zero, choose \(\delta,p>0\) with \(\mathbb P\{|\nu|\ge\delta\}\ge p\).
A Chernoff bound gives at least \(pN/2\) such coordinates outside probability \(Ce^{-cN}\).
Deleting one coordinate still leaves more than \(k_N\) values of modulus at least \(\delta/\sqrt N\ge N^{-1}\), eventually.
The same quantile guard therefore holds.
In both cases use the candidate quantile floor for the original pair \((W_{j_0},F_N)\) and add its norm and quantile failures.
No independence of the diagonal guard from the exposed input is needed.
The floor holds in probability through all dimensions.

If \(a_0=0\) and \(\nu=0\) almost surely, centered independent input diagonals give
\[
 0=\mathbb E|\nu|^2
  =\sum_{j\ {\rm active}}
      |a_j/a_{j_0}|^2\mathbb E\zeta_j^2.
\]
Thus every active diagonal law vanishes.
Both \(Z_N/a_{j_0}\) and \(F_N\) are complex transpose-skew.
The candidate even skew floor applies conditionally on the independent shift and its polynomial guard.
For odd dimensions, transposing the determinant gives its negative, so it is zero identically.
This is precisely the stated exception and gives the even-dimensional floor.

For the chosen active index, C_F0187_v2 applies directly to the original complex Hermitian input plus \(F_N\), without a shift-norm bound.
After multiplication by \(|a_{j_0}|\), it gives
\[
 a_k(Z_N)\ge c(k-1)/N
 \quad(k\ge\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil+2)
\]
outside summable failures.
The common logarithmic passage with \(L=1\) proves the finite target and the claimed probability and arbitrary-coupling limsup conclusions.
It also proves full-sequence probability convergence in every nonexceptional branch.

<a id="one-variable-matrix-proof"></a>
### Finite and free fiber identities remove the scalar premise

For every Hermitian \(W_N\), unitary diagonalization followed by tensor regrouping gives
\[
 \det_{mN}H(W_N)
  =\prod_{\ell=1}^N\det_mH(\lambda_\ell(W_N))
  =\det_N q(W_N).
 \tag{Finite-fiber}
\]
This uses no commutation among the \(B_r\).
For the bounded selfadjoint \(s\), spectral calculus in the abelian algebra generated by \(s\) gives
\[
 \log\Delta_{\tau_m}(H(s))
  =\frac1m\int_{-2}^2\log|q(t)|\,d\mu_{\rm sc}(t).
 \tag{Free-fiber}
\]
Indeed the fiber sum of logarithmic singular values is \(\log|\det_mH(t)|\), including \(-\infty\) on singular fibers.
Positive parts are bounded on the compact interval.
Taking decreasing lower regularizations, or monotone convergence of the negative part after subtracting a uniform upper bound, justifies the extended integral identity.
If \(q\equiv0\), every fiber and every finite block is singular and both sides have the claimed value \(-\infty\).

Otherwise factor \(q(t)=b\prod_{\ell=1}^r(t-\lambda_\ell)\) with \(b\ne0\), repeated roots and \(r=0\) allowed.
The scalar-affine assertion with \(d=1\), coefficient of \(W_N\) equal to one and constant \(-\lambda_\ell\), supplies every scalar root logarithm in probability through even dimensions.
There is no remaining even-scalar floor premise.
A finite union, not independence of the root statistics, gives the probability limit of their sum.
Equations (Finite-fiber) and (Free-fiber) identify that limit with the normalized matrix target.
Each root logarithm has a finite semicircular integral, so the target is finite.
If \(q\) is a nonzero constant, the finite normalized logarithm is its constant divided by \(m\) at every dimension.

The scalar-affine classification says each root limit holds through all dimensions except when the input is purely imaginary with zero diagonal and that root is zero.
Thus the matrix probability classification is exactly as stated.
If \(q(0)=0\) in that exceptional input model, the forced zero eigenvalue of every odd \(W_N\) makes (Finite-fiber) vanish.
For limsup, apply the universal scalar-polynomial upper bound to \(q(W_N)\), divide by \(m\), and use the even-dimensional probability limit and the marginal criterion.
It would be incorrect to infer this conclusion solely by adding the root limsups.

For the real-linear-combination extension put \(\sigma^2=\sum_jb_j^2\).
If \(\sigma>0\), the matrix
\[
 \widetilde W_N=(Y_N-b_0I)/\sigma
\]
has upper law \(\widetilde\xi=\sigma^{-1}\sum_jb_j\xi_j\) and diagonal law \(\widetilde\zeta=\sigma^{-1}\sum_jb_j\zeta_j\).
Independence and centering give \(\mathbb E|\widetilde\xi|^2=1\); the diagonal is real centered with finite second moment, and the coordinate and matrix independence in the original model gives exactly the required within-size Wigner independence.
The resulting cross-size coupling remains allowed.
Apply the one-input theorem to \(t\mapsto H(b_0+\sigma t)\) with these combined laws.
The corresponding free combination is semicircular with the matching mean and variance.
If \(\sigma=0\), evaluate the constant matrix \(H(b_0)\) directly, including singularity.
This argument does not assume that estimates for separate original inputs imply an estimate for their normalized combined law.

<a id="complex-carrier-proof"></a>
### Floors and middle values for the larger carrier class

Fix an arbitrary polynomial failure exponent \(b>2\).
For each \(T_\nu\), condition on all inputs except its eligible \(W_{j_\nu}\).
Its polynomial shift is independent of that input and has the norm guard already proved.
The unified eligible floor gives a fixed exponent \(b_\nu\) with \(\mathbb P\{s_{\min}(T_\nu(W))<N^{-b_\nu}\}\to0\).
A finite union and \(s_{\min}(AB)\ge s_{\min}(A)s_{\min}(B)\) yield a fixed \(b_H\) and an event \(J_N\), measurable in \(W_2,\ldots,W_d\), such that
\[
 \mathbb P(J_N^c)\to0,\qquad
 s_{\min}(H(W))\ge N^{-b_H},\qquad
 \|Q(W)\|\le N^{b_H}\quad\hbox{on }J_N.
\]
The scalar \(c\) is absorbed by exponent slack, and \(K=0\) has a deterministic inverse bound.
For fixed \(\lambda\), define \(F_N=Q(W)-\lambda H(W)^{-1}\) on \(J_N\), and zero elsewhere.
It remains independent of the eligible \(W_1\) and has a deterministic polynomial entry bound.
The same floor and exact identity
\[
 R(W)-\lambda I=(W_1+F_N)H(W)\quad\hbox{on }J_N
\]
give a polynomial output floor in probability.
The parameter order is the polynomial norm exponents, then the factor-floor exponents and \(b_H\), then the shift exponent for \(F_N\), and finally its eligible floor exponent.
No purely imaginary exposed input is inserted into this arbitrary-shift step.

Put \(L=K+1\), and apply C_F0187_v2 conditionally to each full complex Hermitian exposed input in \(T_\nu\) and \(W_1+F_N\).
Its marginal intermediate failures are summable and do not require independence between the resulting factors.
The deterministic product inequality gives
\[
 a_{r_1+\cdots+r_L-(L-1)}(M_1\cdots M_L)
       \ge\prod_{\nu=1}^La_{r_\nu}(M_\nu)
\]
when the left index is at most \(N\).
Distribute \(k+L-1\) among \(L\) positive indices differing by at most one.
For \(k\ge2L(\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil+1)\), each index lies in the intermediate range and its predecessor is at least \(k/(2L)\).
Consequently, including the scalar \(c\) in a constant,
\[
 a_k(R(W)-\lambda I)\ge c_0(k/N)^L
 \quad(2L(\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil+1)\le k\le N)
\]
on an event of probability tending to one.
The output event includes \(J_N\), whose failures need not be summable.
The common logarithmic passage now proves the finite target, full-sequence probability limit and every-coupling limsup for each fixed \(\lambda\).

<a id="cyclic-root-product-proof"></a>
### Two-sided forms, root factorization and ordered products

For the two-sided form put \(U=H_{\rm L}(W)\), \(V=(W_1+Q(W))H_{\rm R}(W)\).
Then \(UV=\widetilde R(W)\) and \(VU=(W_1+Q(W))H_{\rm R}(W)H_{\rm L}(W)\), which has the one-sided certificate.
The accepted finite cyclic identity gives \(\det(UV-\lambda I)=\det(VU-\lambda I)\) for every \(\lambda\), including zero and without inverses.
The bounded-free cyclic identity gives \(\Delta(uv-\lambda)=\Delta(vu-\lambda)\) for \(\lambda\ne0\), and ordinary FK multiplicativity gives it at zero.
Thus the probability limit and almost-sure upper-limit equality transfer exactly, without claiming equality of singular laws.

If \(g(t)-\lambda\) is a nonzero nonconstant polynomial, write \(g(t)-\lambda=b\prod_{\ell=1}^r(t-\alpha_\ell)\).
Finite determinant multiplicativity identifies the normalized logarithm with
\[
 \log|b|+\sum_{\ell=1}^r
      N^{-1}\log|\det(R(W)-\alpha_\ell I)|.
\]
A finite union gives convergence in probability of this sum to its finite targets.
Ordinary bounded FK multiplicativity identifies the sum with \(\log\Delta(g(R(s))-\lambda)\).
The universal fixed-polynomial upper bound and the marginal criterion yield the full-sequence limsup equality under every allowed coupling.
Repeated roots cause no change, a nonzero constant gives its deterministic logarithm, and an identically zero polynomial gives \(-\infty\) on both sides.

For an ordered product, apply these probability limits at zero to its finitely many factors and add their logarithms.
Finite determinant and bounded FK multiplicativity identify the sums with the ordered products, without commutation or independence among factors.
The fixed-polynomial upper bound and the same marginal criterion then give the limsup equality.
A zero factor and the empty product have the stated direct meanings.
Neither step infers a limsup of a sum just from the individual limsups.

<a id="boundary"></a>
## Actual scope and remaining boundary

The affine assertion covers every scalar coefficient tuple in the original complex fixed-law model, including zero constant term.
The matrix-polynomial assertion covers one Hermitian variable and the specified real-linear-combination reductions, including arbitrary singular constant matrix coefficients.
It is not a general multi-direction matrix-pencil theorem.
The carrier assertion permits arbitrary complex Hermitian backgrounds but only eligible exposed indices.
The pure-imaginary quantile guard is not assumed for an arbitrary carrier inverse shift, so those excluded exposed indices are not silently reinstated.
The [single-sandwich tensor boundary](Yin_C_F0186_v10.md#one-occurrence-boundary) remains applicable; this certificate does not cover every noncommutative polynomial with one distinguished occurrence.
The whole arbitrary noncommutative-polynomial and almost-sure Brown questions remain outside this candidate.
All probability arguments retain finite targets before applying the coupling criterion and preserve the ordinary determinant at every singular finite matrix.
