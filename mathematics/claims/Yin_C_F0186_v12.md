# Conditional Wigner carriers with transpose signs or nonconstant diagonals

This is a project deduction extending the carrier part of [C_F0186_v11](Yin_C_F0186_v11.md#carrier-statement), with no novelty or priority claim.
The earlier version's scalar-affine and one-variable matrix-polynomial results, proofs and assessments remain unchanged.
Two sufficient conditions are treated here: a fixed transpose sign for every input gives even-dimensional probability convergence, while an enlarged eligible set of exposed laws gives all-dimension probability convergence under arbitrary complex backgrounds.
Both give the original fixed-parameter almost-sure dimension-limsup equality under every allowed coupling, with the exact closures below.
The [current decision](#decision-carrier-extension-reviewed) adopts both statements after the [independent whole-proof finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v11.md#carrier-extension-whole-proof), with their exact hypotheses and limits preserved.
The second sufficient condition uses the adopted C_F0194_v8 diagonal floor at its new nonconstant-diagonal scope.
That input's review does not review the present carrier assembly, and the earlier C_F0186_v11 review is not inherited by this changed statement.

<a id="model"></a>
## Original fixed-law model and carrier certificate

Fix d>=1 and independent Hermitian Wigner matrices W_1^(N),...,W_d^(N) within each dimension.
For each j, the strict upper entries are independent copies of the fixed complex law xi_j/sqrt(N), with E xi_j=0 and E|xi_j|^2=1; lower entries are their conjugates.
The diagonal entries are independent copies of the fixed real law zeta_j/sqrt(N), with E zeta_j=0 and E zeta_j^2<infinity.
All upper and diagonal families and all input matrices are independent within a dimension, while the dimensions may have any coupling preserving those laws.
No density, moment above two, or independence between real and imaginary components of an entry is assumed.
Let s_1,...,s_d be free standard semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state tau.
Every coefficient, degree, factor count and parameter below is deterministic and fixed independently of N.
Ordinary determinants include zeros, with log 0=-infinity, and the free determinant is the ordinary bounded FK determinant.

Relabel the active input as 1, fix a nonnegative integer K, and take arbitrary scalar complex polynomials in the displayed variables:
\[
 \begin{split}
 T_\nu&=x_{j_\nu}+Q_\nu((x_j)_{j\ne1,j_\nu}),
          \qquad j_\nu\in\{2,\ldots,d\},\quad1\le\nu\le K,\\
 H&=cT_1\cdots T_K,\qquad c\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\},\\
 R&=(x_1+Q(x_2,\ldots,x_d))H.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Carrier}
\]
Set H=c for K=0.
Repeated exposure indices, shared inputs and dependent factors are permitted.
Each Q_nu must omit the active input and its own exposed input, so its shift is independent of that input and H is independent of W_1.
No reversal, transpose-symmetry or commutation condition is imposed on the polynomials.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two sufficient conditions and their exact conclusions

<a id="signed-carrier-statement"></a>
### Fixed transpose signs

Suppose there are deterministic signs sigma_j in {+1,-1} such that each positive-sign input has a real strict upper law and each negative-sign input has a purely imaginary strict upper law and identically zero diagonal law.
Thus
\[
 (W_j^{(N)})^T=\sigma_jW_j^{(N)}
       \quad\text{for every realization}.
 \tag{Signed-tuple}
\]
The real symmetric positive-sign inputs may have arbitrary diagonal laws satisfying the centered finite-second-moment model; they need not have zero diagonal.
Every active or factor-exposure index in (Carrier) may have either sign.
For every fixed lambda in C,
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det(R(W^{(N)})-\lambda I)|
   \longrightarrow \tau\log|R(s)-\lambda|\in\mathbb R
       \quad\text{in probability through even }N,
 \tag{Signed-carrier-log}
\]
and
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|\det(R(W^{(N)})-\lambda I)|
   =\tau\log|R(s)-\lambda|
       \quad\text{almost surely under every allowed coupling}.
 \tag{Carrier-limsup}
\]
This includes lambda=0 with a finite target.
All purely imaginary zero-diagonal tuples are included by taking every sign negative.

<a id="diagonal-exposure-statement"></a>
### Nonconstant diagonals enlarge the eligible exposure set

In the original model with arbitrary complex Hermitian backgrounds, put
\[
 \mathcal E_+=\{j:\mathbb P(\operatorname{Re}\xi_j\ne0)>0
                       \text{ or }\operatorname{Var}(\zeta_j)>0\}.
 \tag{Enlarged-exposure}
\]
Suppose every actual exposure index 1,j_1,...,j_K belongs to this set.
No transpose-sign condition is imposed on any input in this second sufficient condition.
Then for every fixed lambda the same finite-target probability convergence holds through all dimensions, and (Carrier-limsup) holds under every allowed coupling.
This branch uses the exact adopted C_F0194_v8 floor input specified above.
Its positive-variance diagonal condition cannot be replaced by merely a nonzero mean; the present centered model excludes a nonzero constant diagonal law in any event.

<a id="carrier-closures"></a>
### Exact closures, constants and scope

Both conclusions transfer to
\[
 \widetilde R=H_{\mathrm L}(x_1+Q)H_{\mathrm R}
\]
when H_L,H_R are fixed scalar polynomials omitting x_1 and H_R H_L has the required certificate for H, with the respective exposure conditions.
No separate certificate for the two polynomials is required; separate certificates, when available, concatenate in the order H_R H_L.

For either carrier form, every fixed scalar polynomial g and parameter lambda have the same probability and limsup conclusions for g(R)-lambda.
The target is finite when g(t)-lambda is a nonzero polynomial, including a nonzero constant.
If g(t)-lambda vanishes identically, both finite and free logarithms are identically -infinity.
Repeated roots are included.

The zero-shift conclusion also holds for every finite ordered product S=product_h g_h(R_h) of covered carriers, allowing different exposed indices, shared inputs and noncommuting factors.
The target is finite if all g_h are nonzero polynomials; a zero factor gives the zero polynomial and logarithms -infinity, and the empty product gives the identity and logarithm zero.
These closures have all-dimension probability convergence when every participating carrier satisfies (Enlarged-exposure), and otherwise have even-dimensional probability convergence; the every-coupling full-sequence limsup conclusion holds in either case.
No nonzero-shift assertion for a general ordered product follows solely from its zero-shift factorization.
Every assertion is for each fixed model, certificate, polynomial and parameter; no event uniform over an uncountable parameter family is asserted.
The common floor, middle-value and logarithmic deductions in the argument are included at their stated scopes.
Neither a general scalar noncommutative-polynomial theorem, a matrix-coefficient polynomial theorem, a full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic limit, a Brown-measure theorem nor completion of the original Tasks is asserted.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Complete argument

<a id="log-transfer-interface"></a>
### The reused probability-level logarithmic implication

The accepted [C_F0186_v11 transfer](Yin_C_F0186_v11.md#norm-and-log-transfer) has the following precise form.
Let P be a fixed scalar polynomial in the original model, and let the dimension set be either all positive integers or the even integers.
Suppose for fixed alpha,c,C>0 and L>=1 an event of probability tending to one along that set gives increasing singular values
\[
 a_1(P(W))\ge N^{-\alpha},\qquad
 a_k(P(W))\ge c(k/N)^L\quad(CN^{3/4}\le k\le N).
 \tag{Floor-and-middle}
\]
Together with the original fixed-polynomial weak and positive-log controls, these bounds imply a finite free logarithm and convergence in probability of N^(-1)log|det P(W)| to it along the chosen set.
Its proof bounds the bottom O(N^(3/4)) normalized negative logarithms by O(N^(-1/4)log N) and the remaining tail by C't^(1/L)(1+|log t|)+o(1), then removes the cutoffs after weak convergence.
The fixed-regularizer upper bound holds on the full sequence almost surely; a deterministic subsequence inside the chosen dimension set with summable deviation probabilities supplies the lower bound by first Borel--Cantelli.
Consequently the full-sequence limsup equals that same finite target under every allowed coupling.
This is the already reviewed implication used below, not an assumption of free negative-log integrability, expectation convergence, cross-size independence or summability of the new floors.

<a id="transpose-polynomial-dichotomy"></a>
### The transpose operation and nonvanishing after semicircular substitution

For the fixed signs in the signed-tuple statement, define the complex-linear involutive anti-automorphism \(\#_\sigma\) on \(\mathbb C\langle x_1,\ldots,x_d\rangle\) by
\[
 c^{\#_\sigma}=c,\qquad x_j^{\#_\sigma}=\sigma_jx_j,\qquad
 (UV)^{\#_\sigma}=V^{\#_\sigma}U^{\#_\sigma}.
\]
It reverses each word and multiplies it by the product of the signs of its letters, without conjugating its coefficient.
Thus \(P(W)^T=P^{\#_\sigma}(W)\) exactly, and
\[
 \mathfrak S_\sigma(P):=\frac{P+P^{\#_\sigma}}{2},\qquad
 \operatorname{sym}P(W)=\mathfrak S_\sigma(P)(W).
 \tag{Transpose-polynomial}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{sym}A=(A+A^T)/2\) is unrelated to the Hermitian part \((A+A^*)/2\).
The operation \(\#_\sigma\) is formal; no transpose operation on the ambient free von Neumann algebra is assumed.

A nonzero scalar free polynomial \(P\) cannot vanish at the free standard semicircular tuple.
The classical full Fock realization is described in [NS06, the retained 434-page edition](../audits/literature/NS06/readme.md#yin-edition), Definition 7.13 and Corollary 7.17, printed pp. 103 and 107 / PDF pp. 120 and 124.
Those passages, together with the intervening formulas and Propositions 7.15–7.16 on printed pp. 104–107, were read directly in the local original for this argument.
This is attribution for the established realization, not a claim of a whole-book proof audit or a new algebraic-freeness theorem.
The following vacuum-moment and highest-grade calculation supplies the complete nonvanishing argument actually used here; no unproved source result is added as a logical premise.
Let \(\mathcal F=\mathbb C\Omega\oplus\bigoplus_{r\ge1}(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes r}\), let \(\ell_j\) prepend the basis vector \(e_j\), and put \(S_j=\ell_j+\ell_j^*\).
Expanding a vacuum word shows that its nonzero terms are exactly the color-respecting noncrossing creation-annihilation pairings: an annihilation must remove the current leftmost tensor of the same color, and the word must end at the vacuum.
Each such term has value one.
These are exactly the joint moments of a free standard semicircular tuple, the same colored-pairing convention used in [C_F0186_v4's bounded moment argument](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#bounded-moments).
Consequently \(\tau(P(s)^*P(s))=\|P(S)\Omega\|^2\).
If the largest degree in \(P\) is \(r\), the grade-\(r\) component of \(P(S)\Omega\) is
\[
 \sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_r}
    p_{i_1\cdots i_r}\,e_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_r},
\]
where the coefficients are those of its degree-\(r\) words after equal words have been combined.
Only the all-creation term of each such word reaches that grade, so this vector is nonzero.
The degree-zero case is immediate.
This proves \(\tau(P(s)^*P(s))>0\), hence \(P(s)\ne0\), without an algebraic-freeness theorem being imported without proof.

It follows that if \(P\) is formally nonzero, there are fixed \(a>0\) and \(\delta>0\) such that, with probability tending to one,
\[
 s_{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}(P(W))\ge a.
 \tag{Polynomial-bulk}
\]
Indeed the nonzero positive operator \(|P(s)|\) has positive spectral mass above some positive threshold, by faithfulness of \(\tau\).
Choose a smaller threshold at a continuity point of its law, and then a smaller positive \(\delta\).
The fixed-polynomial Hermitized weak law in [C_F0186_v4](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration) makes the number of singular values above that threshold at least \(\delta N\), eventually almost surely under every allowed coupling.
This proves the displayed probability statement as well; no quantitative convergence rate or inverse of \(P(s)\) is required.
The same reasoning applies to a polynomial in any subtuple of the background variables.

Two elementary singular-value facts will be used.
For the principal compression \(A_i\) obtained by deleting one coordinate from an \(N\)-by-\(N\) matrix \(A\),
\[
 s_k(A_i)\ge s_{k+2}(A)\qquad(1\le k\le N-2).
 \tag{Compression-quantile}
\]
To verify this, let \(P_i\) be the coordinate projection and embed \(A_i\) as \(P_iAP_i\), with one extra zero singular value.
The difference \(A-P_iAP_i=(I-P_i)A+P_iA(I-P_i)\) has rank at most two.
Intersecting the singular-value min-max subspace with its kernel proves the displayed rank-two inequality.
Also
\[
 s_k(BAC)\le\|B\|\,\|C\|\,s_k(A),
 \tag{Bounded-multipliers}
\]
by applying \(B\) and \(C\) to a best rank-\((k-1)\) singular-value truncation of \(A\).
These statements require neither normality nor symmetry.

For every fixed polynomial \(P\) of degree \(D\ge1\), [C_F0186_v4's power tail](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#positive-tail) gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|P(W)\|>N^M\}\le C_PN^{1-2M/D}.
 \tag{Carrier-norm}
\]
Choose \(M\) sufficiently large to make any prescribed polynomial failure bound; constant polynomials are immediate.
All degrees and the finite lists of polynomials below are fixed.

<a id="signed-factor-floors"></a>
### Factor floors for the signed tuple

Fix a factor \(T_\nu\), and condition only on the input matrices other than \(W_{j_\nu}\).
Its shift \(Q_\nu(W)\) is independent of \(W_{j_\nu}\), and (Carrier-norm) supplies a polynomial entry guard with probability tending to one.
If \(\sigma_{j_\nu}=+1\), the exposed real upper law has variance one and is eligible in the adopted C_F0186_v11 floor interface.
Its uniform arbitrary-complex-shift floor, with the independent polynomial shift and its norm guard, gives a fixed polynomial least-value floor in probability through all dimensions.
This uses the original centered finite-second-moment real diagonal law, without requiring it to vanish.
For \(\sigma_{j_\nu}=-1\), put \(S_\nu=\mathfrak S_\sigma(Q_\nu)\) and use the following two branches.
If \(S_\nu\) is the zero polynomial, \(Q_\nu(W)\) is exactly complex transpose-skew in every dimension.
The adopted [C_F0194_v7 even skew floor](Yin_C_F0194_v7.md#even-floor), integrated over the independent shift on its entry guard, gives a fixed exponent \(b_\nu>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(T_\nu(W))<N^{-b_\nu}\}\longrightarrow0
       \quad\text{through even }N.
 \tag{Factor-even-floor}
\]
In this branch the error can in fact be any prescribed inverse power after increasing the exponent.

If \(S_\nu\) is formally nonzero, (Polynomial-bulk) gives at least \(\delta_\nu N\) singular values of \(\operatorname{sym}Q_\nu(W)\) above a fixed \(a_\nu>0\), with probability tending to one.
For \(k_N=4\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor+1\), eventually \(k_N+2\le\lfloor\delta_\nu N\rfloor\).
Because the exposed matrix has zero diagonal and is transpose-skew, the symmetric part of every actual principal minor of \(T_\nu(W)\) is exactly the corresponding compression of \(\operatorname{sym}Q_\nu(W)\).
By (Compression-quantile), all those minors simultaneously have their \(k_N\)-th singular value at least \(a_\nu\ge N^{-1}\), eventually on the bulk event.
Apply the adopted [C_F0194_v7 quantile floor](Yin_C_F0194_v7.md#quantile-guard-floor) with \(L_0=1\), then add the entry-guard and bulk-event failure probabilities.
This proves (Factor-even-floor), in this branch actually through all dimensions.
This application conditions on the independent shift alone, not on the intersection of the norm or spectral guards.
Equivalently the uniformly bounded conditional probability of the least-value failure intersected with the guards is integrated, and their unconditional failure probabilities are then added.

Each factor is covered by its positive-sign floor or by the preceding negative-sign dichotomy.
There are only finitely many factors, so a union bound and
\(s_{\min}(UV)\ge s_{\min}(U)s_{\min}(V)\) give a fixed \(b_H>0\) with
\[
 \mathbb P\{H(W)\text{ is invertible and }
          \|H(W)^{-1}\|\le N^{b_H}\}\longrightarrow1
       \quad\text{through even }N.
 \tag{H-even-inverse}
\]
The fixed nonzero factor \(c\) is absorbed by increasing \(b_H\); \(K=0\) is direct.
No independence between the factors, or conditional floor after conditioning on their common good event, is used.

<a id="signed-carrier-floor"></a>
### The inverse shift reduces to one fixed polynomial

Fix \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\).
Choose a fixed \(M>0\) so that \(\|H(W)\|,\|Q(W)\|\le N^M\) outside a probability tending to zero, and let
\[
 J_N=\{H(W)\text{ invertible},\ \|H(W)^{-1}\|\le N^{b_H},
                 \ \|H(W)\|,\|Q(W)\|\le N^M\}.
\]
This event depends only on the background inputs \(W_2,\ldots,W_d\), and \(\mathbb P(J_N^c)\to0\) through even dimensions by (H-even-inverse) and (Carrier-norm).
Define a background-measurable shift in every dimension and on the whole probability space by
\[
 F_N=
 \begin{cases}
 Q(W)-\lambda H(W)^{-1},&J_N,\\
 0,&J_N^c.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{Background-shift}
\]
It is independent of the whole exposed matrix \(W_1\), and for a sufficiently large fixed \(\gamma\), \(\|F_N\|\le N^\gamma\) deterministically for all sufficiently large \(N\).
The finite-dimensional inverse on the open set of invertible matrices is continuous, so this extension is measurable.
On \(J_N\),
\[
 R(W)-\lambda I=(W_1+F_N)H(W).
 \tag{Carrier-factorization}
\]

If \(\sigma_1=+1\), the adopted C_F0186_v11 eligible floor applies directly to this globally defined independent shift, giving the required polynomial floor for \(W_1+F_N\).
The event \(J_N\) is not conditioned on.
For the remaining case \(\sigma_1=-1\), introduce the fixed background polynomial
\[
 K_\lambda=H^{\#_\sigma}\mathfrak S_\sigma(Q)H-\lambda\mathfrak S_\sigma(H).
 \tag{Carrier-transpose-polynomial}
\]
For every invertible finite matrix \(H=H(W)\), the exact identity on \(J_N\) is
\[
 \begin{split}
 H^T\operatorname{sym}(F_N)H
 &=H^T\operatorname{sym}(Q(W))H
   -\frac\lambda2 H^T(H^{-1}+H^{-T})H\\
 &=H^T\operatorname{sym}(Q(W))H
   -\frac\lambda2(H^T+H)
 =K_\lambda(W).
 \end{split}
 \tag{Inverse-transpose-identity}
\]
The scalar is \(\lambda\), not \(\bar\lambda\), because ordinary transpose is complex-linear.
This identity does not use or assert an inverse of \(H(s)\).

If \(K_\lambda\) is formally zero, invertibility of \(H(W)\) makes \(\operatorname{sym}F_N=0\) on \(J_N\); the extension is also skew on \(J_N^c\).
The even skew floor therefore supplies a polynomial least-value floor for \(W_1+F_N\) through even dimensions.
It applies to its globally defined independent shift with the deterministic polynomial entry bound just verified.

If \(K_\lambda\) is formally nonzero, (Polynomial-bulk) supplies fixed \(a,\delta>0\) and an event \(B_N\), with \(\mathbb P(B_N^c)\to0\), on which
\(s_{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}(K_\lambda(W))\ge a\).
On \(J_N\cap B_N\), (Inverse-transpose-identity), (Bounded-multipliers), and \(\|H^T\|=\|H\|\) give
\[
 s_{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}(\operatorname{sym}F_N)
       \ge aN^{-2M}.
\]
Choose the fixed exponent \(L_0=2M+1\).
By (Compression-quantile) and \(k_N+2\le\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\) eventually, the symmetric part of every actual principal minor of \(W_1+F_N\) has its \(k_N\)-th singular value at least \(N^{-L_0}\) on \(J_N\cap B_N\).
Here the symmetric part of \(W_1\) is exactly zero, including its diagonal.
The quantile floor yields the desired least-value probability bound intersected with this guard.
Adding \(\mathbb P(J_N^c)+\mathbb P(B_N^c)\), which tend to zero along even dimensions, gives a fixed \(A_\lambda>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(W_1+F_N)<N^{-A_\lambda}\}\longrightarrow0
        \quad\text{through even }N.
 \tag{Inverse-shift-even-floor}
\]
This does not condition on \(J_N\), \(B_N\), or their intersection with the quantile guard; the random-shift theorem is applied by conditioning only on the complete background-measurable \(F_N\).
Its constants are uniform in that shift, and the guard failure is bounded by the displayed background failure probabilities.

For the negative-sign active input, the formal-zero and formal-nonzero alternatives exhaust all \(K_\lambda\), since the Fock argument excludes a formally nonzero polynomial with zero semicircular evaluation.
At either active sign, combining (Carrier-factorization), (H-even-inverse), and the just-proved inverse-shift floor gives a fixed \(b_\lambda>0\) with
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(R(W)-\lambda I)<N^{-b_\lambda}\}
       \longrightarrow0\quad\text{through even }N.
 \tag{Carrier-even-floor}
\]
This works for every certificate (Carrier) in the signed tuple, including repeated exposed background indices, \(K=0\), and \(\lambda=0\).
It requires no dichotomy or nonvanishing test on the random finite matrix \(K_\lambda(W)\) itself.

<a id="signed-middle-and-log"></a>
### Intermediate values and the logarithmic conclusion

Set \(q_N=\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil\), write singular values increasingly as \(a_r\), and put \(L=K+1\).
Apply the accepted [C_F0187_v2 intermediate theorem](Yin_C_F0187_v2.md#statement) to each \(T_\nu(W)\), conditioned only on all inputs other than its exposed \(W_{j_\nu}\), and to \(W_1+F_N\), conditioned only on the background.
The law of each exposed matrix is its original fixed real symmetric law with its prescribed diagonal, or its purely imaginary law with zero diagonal, according to its sign.
The theorem permits an arbitrary independent complex shift without any norm guard.
Its constants are uniform in each realization of that shift.
For the finite list of factors, a union bound gives, outside a summable probability \(CN^2e^{-cN^{1/4}}\),
\[
 a_r(T_\nu(W)),\ a_r(W_1+F_N)
       \ge c(r-1)/N\qquad(q_N+1\le r\le N),
 \tag{Carrier-factor-middle}
\]
where \(c>0\) may be decreased to accommodate the finitely many fixed laws.
The summability here does not make \(J_N^c\) or the quantile-floor errors summable.

For square matrices, the singular-value min-max inequality
\[
 a_{r+s-1}(UV)\ge a_r(U)a_s(V),\qquad r+s-1\le N,
\]
is proved at its singular-matrix scope in [C_F0186_v6](Yin_C_F0186_v6.md#argument).
Iterating it for the \(L\) factors, and taking \(r=\lfloor k/L\rfloor\), gives on \(J_N\) and (Carrier-factor-middle)
\[
 a_k(R(W)-\lambda I)\ge c_1(k/N)^L
       \qquad(2L(q_N+1)\le k\le N)
 \tag{Carrier-middle}
\]
for a fixed \(c_1>0\), absorbing \(|c|\).
Indeed the iterated index is \(L(r-1)+1\le k\), while \(r-1\ge k/(2L)\) throughout this range for all sufficiently large \(N\).
This is a product singular-value statement and does not require independent factors.
Together with (Carrier-even-floor), it supplies both bounds on an event of probability tending to one along even dimensions.

The common [probability-level logarithmic interface](#log-transfer-interface), with dimension set equal to the even integers and exponent \(L=K+1\), now gives the finite free target, even-dimensional logarithmic convergence in probability, and full-sequence almost-sure limsup under every allowed coupling.
Its hypotheses are (Carrier-even-floor), (Carrier-middle), and the accepted fixed-polynomial weak and logarithmic controls.
No almost-sure conclusion is inferred by summing the nonsummable quantile-floor failures.

<a id="diagonal-exposure-proof"></a>
### Verification of the enlarged all-dimension exposure interface

Fix one actual exposure index j in E_+.
If P(Re xi_j nonzero)>0, the adopted C_F0186_v11 eligible-law floor applies at its original scope.
Otherwise xi_j=i eta_j almost surely for a real centered variance-one eta_j, because the original upper law is centered with second moment one.
Membership in E_+ then gives Var(zeta_j)>0, which under finite second moment is equivalent to a nonconstant real diagonal law.

## Exact boundary of the extension

The signed-tuple proof uses \(P(W)^T=P^{\#_\sigma}(W)\) on every background polynomial, which fails for a general complex Hermitian input with neither transpose sign.
In particular it does not impose that identity on arbitrary complex backgrounds in the second sufficient condition.
That second condition instead relies on a uniform floor for every actually exposed input; a purely imaginary exposed input with identically zero diagonal remains outside E_+.
The signed condition covers this important exception when all backgrounds have fixed signs, but neither condition covers every possible background law and carrier certificate simultaneously.

Odd dimensions cannot in general be added to the signed-tuple probability conclusion.
The allowed carrier R=x_1 at lambda=0, with a negative-sign input, is transpose-skew and singular in every odd dimension, while its free logarithm is finite by the proved even floor-and-middle transfer.
This does not prevent the full-sequence almost-sure limsup conclusion.
The pure-imaginary quantile-floor errors used in the proof tend to zero and are not assumed summable; the signed statement therefore makes no general even-dimensional almost-sure logarithmic limit claim.
The scalar certificate itself remains a genuine restriction and does not establish the arbitrary polynomial, general matrix-pencil or Brown-measure Tasks.
