# Full-sequence Brown limits under positive mirror-pair covariance

This version enlarges the [elliptic additive theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v14.md#statement) by replacing its full EC certificate with a positive Hermitian covariance condition on the exposed mirror pair.
It keeps the original finite-second-moment Wigner model, independent additive exposure, arbitrary cross-size coupling, and complete logarithmic and planar conclusions.
The new floor selection and covariance reduction are justified below; the previous version's reviews do not cover this larger scope.
The unrestricted polynomial and matrix-coefficient Tasks remain distinct from this subclass statement.

## Direct proof inputs and their exact scope

The [positive-covariance mirror-pair floor](Yin_C_F0194_v11.md#statement), denoted (Pair-floor) below, is the necessary new least-singular-value input.
Its fixed-law statement permits arbitrary dependence within a centered unit-variance mirror pair with positive Hermitian covariance, arbitrary complex finite-second-moment diagonals, and every deterministic polynomial-entry complex shift.
Its arbitrary polynomial failure exponent, inclusion of exact singularity, and independent-random-shift conclusion are used at precisely that scope.
Its [applicability proof](Yin_C_F0194_v11.md#argument) reuses the corrected fixed local proof modules with a new covariance argument; the older elliptic theorem is not applied outside its statement.
The [independent whole-chain finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v14.md#positive-covariance-additive-whole-proof) checks this consuming assembly and its exact use of the independently reviewed floor; the selected assessment below adopts that evidence.

[C_F0187_v2](Yin_C_F0187_v2.md#decision-complex-intermediate-reviewed) supplies the adopted complex Hermitian Wigner intermediate singular-value bound, uniform over every independent complex shift, without a shift norm guard.
Its original-law hypotheses allow dependent real and imaginary parts, arbitrary permitted real diagonals, the range \(j\ge N^{3/4}\), and summable marginal failure probabilities.

[C_F0186_v4](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#decision-finite-variance-reviewed) supplies the adopted original-model Hermitized weak limits, positive-logarithmic tail control, local planar potential bounds, and pathwise eigenvalue tightness.

It is used only for the outer-polynomial root factorization and Brown polynomial pushforward, at its retained source and proof-inspection scope.

There is no rolling-draft proof dependency or inherited review of this new assembly.

## Original fixed-law complex Hermitian model

Fix \(d\ge1\).
For each \(j\), fix a complex law \(\xi_j\) with \(\mathbb E\xi_j=0\), \(\mathbb E|\xi_j|^2=1\), and a real law \(\zeta_j\) with \(\mathbb E\zeta_j=0\), \(\mathbb E\zeta_j^2<\infty\).
At each size \(N\), the upper off-diagonal entries of \(W_j^{(N)}\) are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), the lower entries are their conjugates, and the diagonals are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\).
All those families and input matrices are independent within a dimension.
The laws are fixed independently of \(N\), while all dimensions may have any coupling preserving these complete marginal laws.
No density, moment above two, or independence between the real and imaginary parts of one entry is imposed.
Let \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).
Every coefficient, polynomial, index set and degree below is fixed independently of \(N\).
Use ordinary determinants including zero values, \(\log0=-\infty\), algebraic eigenvalue multiplicities, and the ordinary bounded FK determinant \(\Delta_\tau\).

<a id="statement"></a>
## Full-sequence theorem

Fix a nonempty index set \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\), nonzero coefficients \(a_j\in\mathbb C\) for \(j\in I\), and a scalar polynomial \(Q\) in only the variables with indices outside \(I\).
Define
\[
 M_N=\sum_{j\in I}a_jW_j^{(N)},\qquad
 R(x)=\sum_{j\in I}a_jx_j+Q((x_j)_{j\notin I}).
 \tag{Additive}
\]
Zero coefficients can be omitted from \(I\).
Put
\[
 m_j=\mathbb E\xi_j^2,\qquad
 \sigma^2=\sum_{j\in I}|a_j|^2>0,\qquad
 h=\sum_{j\in I}|a_j|^2m_j.
\]
Here \(\sigma>0\) denotes the positive square root of the displayed variance.
Assume the strict covariance condition
\[
 |h|<\sigma^2.
 \tag{Covariance-condition}
\]
The phase of each coefficient \(a_j\) is unrestricted.
No EC identities for the real and imaginary components are assumed.

The following conclusions hold under every allowed cross-size coupling.
For every fixed \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det(R(W^{(N)})-zI_N)|
 \longrightarrow \log\Delta_\tau(R(s)-z)\in\mathbb R
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Fixed-log}
\]
There is one probability-one event on which
\[
 \mu_{R(W^{(N)})}\Longrightarrow\mu_{R(s)}^{\mathrm{Br}}
 \tag{Brown}
\]
against every bounded continuous real function on \(\mathbb C\).
For every fixed one-variable complex polynomial \(g\) and fixed \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det(g(R(W^{(N)}))-\lambda I_N)|
 \longrightarrow\log\Delta_\tau(g(R(s))-\lambda)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Outer-log}
\]
If \(g(t)-\lambda\) is not the zero polynomial, this target is finite.
If \(g\equiv\lambda\), both sides are identically \(-\infty\).
There is also one probability-one event for the full Brown weak convergence of \(g(R(W^{(N)}))\) against every bounded continuous real test function.
The conclusions include constant \(g\).
No selfadjointness, symmetry, degree bound beyond being fixed, or boundedness assumption is imposed on \(Q(W)\).

The event in (Fixed-log) may depend on \(z\).
The single event in (Brown) is obtained by the measurable planar argument below, not by an uncountable intersection of those fixed-parameter events.

## Complete deduction from the positive-covariance floor

<a id="pair-covariance"></a>
## Exact covariance of the original exposed combination

For each upper/lower pair set
\[
 U=\sigma^{-1}\sum_{j\in I}a_j\xi_j,
 \qquad
 V=\sigma^{-1}\sum_{j\in I}a_j\overline{\xi_j},
 \tag{Exposed-pair}
\]
where the \(\xi_j\) are independent with their original fixed laws.
Centering and independence make all terms with distinct indices vanish, so
\[
 \mathbb EU=\mathbb EV=0,\qquad
 \mathbb E|U|^2=\mathbb E|V|^2=1,\qquad
 \mathbb E(U\overline V)=\frac{h}{\sigma^2}.
\]
In the cross moment the coefficient product is \(a_j\overline{a_j}=|a_j|^2\), not \(a_j^2\).
Consequently the Hermitian covariance of \((U,V)\) is
\[
 \Gamma=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&h/\sigma^2\\\overline h/\sigma^2&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma)=1-|h|/\sigma^2>0.
 \tag{Exposed-Gram}
\]
Thus (Covariance-condition) is exactly the positive-pair hypothesis of C_F0194_v11.
There is no assumption that the two components of the pair are independent.
Distinct unordered pairs are independent, since they use disjoint original input coordinates.
The raw diagonal law of \(\sqrt N M_N/\sigma\) is
\(\sigma^{-1}\sum_{j\in I}a_j\zeta_j\), which is fixed, centered, complex-valued and of finite second moment.
These diagonals are independent of one another and of all mirror pairs by the original within-dimension independence.
Every law and covariance gap is fixed independently of \(N\).
This verifies the complete raw-matrix hypothesis of the floor input.

<a id="conditional-floor"></a>
## A summable floor for an arbitrary polynomial background

Fix \(z\), and put \(A_N=R(W^{(N)})-zI_N=M_N+Q(W)-zI_N\).
Condition on all background inputs, namely those outside \(I\).
The matrix
\[
 X_N=\frac{\sqrt N}{\sigma}M_N
\]
has exactly the raw pair model in (Pair-floor) and is independent of the conditioned background.
Its deterministic conditional shift is
\[
 F_N=\frac{\sqrt N}{\sigma}(Q(W)-zI_N).
 \tag{Raw-shift}
\]

For any original input, direct entry counting gives
\[
 \mathbb E\|W_j^{(N)}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
   =N-1+\mathbb E\zeta_j^2\le C_jN.
\]
Thus, for any \(K>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\max_{j\notin I}\|W_j^{(N)}\|>N^K\}
 \le C N^{1-2K}.
 \tag{Norm-guard}
\]
The finite list of coefficients and words of \(Q\) gives
\(\|Q(W)\|\le C_Q(1+\max_{j\notin I}\|W_j\|)^D\), where \(D=\max(1,\deg Q)\).
If there are no background inputs, the maximum is taken as zero and the guard is automatic.
Given any prescribed \(b>0\), choose \(K\) so that the failure in (Norm-guard) is at most \(C N^{-b-2}\).
On the guard, every entry of (Raw-shift) is at most \(N^\alpha\) for a fixed sufficiently large \(\alpha\), after increasing the dimension threshold to absorb \(z,\sigma,C_Q\).

Apply (Pair-floor) conditionally with exponent \(b+2\), then integrate over the background.
Since \(A_N=(\sigma/\sqrt N)(X_N+F_N)\), there are fixed \(L,C,N_0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(A_N)<N^{-L}\}\le C N^{-b-2}
 \quad(N>N_0).
 \tag{Additive-floor}
\]
The constant factor \(\sigma\) is absorbed by increasing \(L\).
First Borel--Cantelli therefore gives the polynomial floor eventually almost surely for this fixed \(z\), for every cross-size coupling.
Neither a bounded matrix norm nor summable errors at a smaller entry cutoff are assumed.

<a id="intermediate-and-log"></a>
## Intermediate values and the entire negative logarithmic tail

Choose \(j_0\in I\) with \(a_{j_0}\ne0\).
Condition on all inputs except \(W_{j_0}\), and write
\[
 A_N=a_{j_0}(W_{j_0}^{(N)}+H_N),
 \qquad
 H_N=a_{j_0}^{-1}
 \left(\sum_{j\in I\setminus\{j_0\}}a_jW_j^{(N)}+Q(W)-zI_N\right).
\]
The shift \(H_N\) is independent of \(W_{j_0}\).
It can be arbitrary complex and nonnormal, and needs no norm bound for this step.
The [accepted complex Hermitian intermediate theorem](Yin_C_F0187_v2.md#statement) gives fixed \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\exists j\in[\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil,N-1]:
       s_{N-j}(A_N)<c j/N\right\}
 \le C N^2 e^{-cN^{1/4}},
 \tag{Additive-middle}
\]
after absorbing the fixed scalar \(|a_{j_0}|\) into the constants.
The failure probabilities are summable.

Work on the probability-one event where (Additive-floor), (Additive-middle), and the [C_F0186_v4 weak singular-value and positive-logarithmic controls](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#statement) all hold for the fixed polynomial and parameter.
Let \(k_N=\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil\), and choose \(0<\varepsilon<\min(1,c)\).
The lowest \(k_N\) singular values contribute at most \(Lk_N\log N/N\) to the normalized negative logarithm, eventually.
For the remaining ones with value below \(\varepsilon\), (Additive-middle) forces \(j<\varepsilon N/c\) and bounds their contributions by \(\log(N/(cj))\).
Consequently
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{s_i(A_N)<\varepsilon}|\log s_i(A_N)|
 \le \frac{Lk_N\log N}{N}
       +\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N/c\rfloor}
              \log\frac{N}{cj}.
 \tag{Negative-tail}
\]
The decreasing positive function \(t\mapsto\log(1/(ct))\) bounds the last Riemann sum from above by its integral from zero to \(\varepsilon/c\).
Thus
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}
 \frac1N\sum_{s_i(A_N)<\varepsilon}|\log s_i(A_N)|
 \le \frac{\varepsilon}{c}(1+|\log\varepsilon|)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Negative-UI}
\]
The fixed polynomial floor also makes all sufficiently large \(A_N\) invertible on this event.

The singular-value weak limit, (Negative-UI), and the accepted positive-logarithmic tail bound imply convergence of the logarithmic integrals by clipping the logarithm between two fixed finite levels and then removing the cutoffs.
They also imply that the free singular law has integrable logarithm and no atom at zero; no separate atomlessness theorem is needed for this deduction.
This proves (Fixed-log), including finiteness of its deterministic target for each fixed \(z\).

<a id="planar-event"></a>
## Measurable planar passage and one event for every test

Put \(u_N(\omega,z)=N^{-1}\log|\det(R(W^{(N)}(\omega))-zI)|\) and \(u(z)=\log\Delta(R(s)-z)\).
Each \(u_N\) is jointly measurable because the determinant is a polynomial in the measurable matrix entries and \(z\), followed by the extended-real Borel logarithm.
The Brown potential \(u\) is a deterministic Borel function, and its finiteness at each fixed \(z\) was obtained above.
Hence
\[
 E=\{(\omega,z):u_N(\omega,z)\longrightarrow u(z)\}
\]
is measurable, using the usual countable limit criterion.
For each fixed \(z\), (Fixed-log) gives \(\mathbb P(E_z)=1\).
Tonelli on each integer-radius disk, followed by a countable intersection, gives one event on which \(u_N\to u\) for planar almost every \(z\).

Intersect that event with the [accepted planar local \(L^2\) bounds and tightness](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) for the fixed polynomial \(R\).
On each compact disk, these bounds imply uniform integrability of \(u_N-u\); the limit Brown potential has the corresponding local \(L^2\) bound.
Almost-everywhere convergence therefore gives \(u_N\to u\) in local planar \(L^1\) on this single event.
The distributional identities \(\Delta u_N=2\pi\mu_{R(W^{(N)})}\) and \(\Delta u=2\pi\mu_{R(s)}^{\mathrm{Br}}\) imply convergence against compactly supported smooth tests.
Pathwise tightness and approximation on compact disks extend this to every bounded continuous real test function, as a deterministic consequence on the same event.
This proves (Brown) without intersecting uncountably many test-dependent events.

<a id="outer-polynomial"></a>
## Outer-polynomial logarithms and Brown pushforward

Fix \(g\) and \(\lambda\).
If \(g-\lambda\) is a nonzero constant, (Outer-log) is the exact constant identity, and if \(g\equiv\lambda\), both logarithms are identically \(-\infty\).
Otherwise write
\[
 g(t)-\lambda=b\prod_{h=1}^{k}(t-z_h),\qquad b\ne0,
\]
with roots counted with multiplicity.
Ordinary determinant multiplicativity gives, in the extended real sense,
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det(g(R(W))-\lambda I)|
 =\log|b|+\sum_{h=1}^{k}\frac1N\log|\det(R(W)-z_hI)|.
\]
Intersect the finitely many probability-one events in (Fixed-log) for these roots.
Each limiting summand is finite, so the finite sum converges almost surely.
Bounded FK multiplicativity identifies that sum with \(\log\Delta_\tau(g(R(s))-\lambda)\), proving (Outer-log) and its finiteness in this case.

For any bounded element \(a\), the same root factorization and Brown logarithmic-potential formula yield
\[
 \log\Delta(g(a)-\lambda)
 =\int\log|g(w)-\lambda|\,d\mu_a^{\mathrm{Br}}(w).
\]
This identity permits \(-\infty\), since the positive logarithms are bounded on the compact Brown support and only finitely many factors are present.
Distributional Laplacians in \(\lambda\) prove \(\mu_{g(a)}^{\mathrm{Br}}=g_*\mu_a^{\mathrm{Br}}\).
The constant-polynomial case is the direct point-mass identity.
At finite size, the spectral measure is exactly the continuous pushforward by \(g\), with algebraic multiplicities.
Continuous pushforward of (Brown) therefore proves the full weak conclusion for \(g(R)\) on the same event as (Brown).
A bounded continuous test composed with \(g\) remains bounded and continuous, so no uniform-continuity or compact-support condition on that test is inserted.

<a id="covariance-boundary"></a>
## The exact common-phase-line boundary

For each active input, \(|m_j|\le\mathbb E|\xi_j|^2=1\).
The failure of (Covariance-condition) is precisely equality \(|h|=\sigma^2\), and this equality holds if and only if all the active off-diagonal laws lie on one common real line through the origin in \(\mathbb C\).
Here the common line concerns the original \(\xi_j\) laws, not the phases of their coefficients.

For the forward implication, write \(h=\sigma^2e^{i\phi}\).
Then
\[
 0=\sigma^2-\Re(e^{-i\phi}h)
   =\sum_{j\in I}|a_j|^2
       \bigl(1-\Re(e^{-i\phi}m_j)\bigr).
\]
Each summand is nonnegative and each weight is positive, so
\(\Re(e^{-i\phi}m_j)=1\) for every active \(j\).
Since \(|m_j|\le1\), this forces \(m_j=e^{i\phi}\).
Choose \(\theta\) with \(2\theta=\phi\) modulo \(2\pi\).
The identity
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl(\Im(e^{-i\theta}\xi_j)\bigr)^2
 =\tfrac12\bigl(1-\Re(e^{-2i\theta}m_j)\bigr)=0
\]
implies \(\xi_j\in e^{i\theta}\mathbb R\) almost surely for every \(j\in I\).
Conversely, on a common line write \(\xi_j=e^{i\theta}\eta_j\) with real \(\eta_j\) of second moment one.
Then \(m_j=e^{2i\theta}\) for all active indices and \(h=e^{2i\theta}\sigma^2\), giving equality.

In particular the strict condition holds if at least one active law is not supported on any such line.
If every active law is supported on a line, it holds exactly when those lines are not all the same, with opposite phase representatives defining the same real line.
This characterizes the boundary of the stated sufficient condition, not the boundary of possible Brown convergence or of every least-value method.

<a id="concrete-classes"></a>
## Concrete enlarged original-model classes

For a single exposed index \(j_0\) with \(|\mathbb E\xi_{j_0}^2|<1\), every nonzero coefficient \(a\) and independent background polynomial \(Q\) satisfy (Covariance-condition).
Thus the theorem applies to
\[
 g\bigl(a x_{j_0}+Q((x_j)_{j\ne j_0})\bigr)
\]
with arbitrary fixed one-variable \(g\), including constant polynomials.
The uniform law on \(\{1,-1,i,-i\}\) has \(m_{j_0}=0\) and gives a purely atomic example with dependent real and imaginary components.
There is no requirement to expose a second input or to arrange a special coefficient phase in this example.
More generally, when every active \(m_j=0\), the condition is automatic for every nonzero coefficient tuple; no restriction on \(\sum_j a_j^2\) is imposed.

For a real off-diagonal input \(\xi_1=\eta\) and a purely imaginary input \(\xi_2=i\nu\), with independent centered real variance-one \(\eta,\nu\), one has \(m_1=1\) and \(m_2=-1\).
For any nonzero complex coefficients \(a_1,a_2\),
\[
 |h|=\bigl||a_1|^2-|a_2|^2\bigr|
     <|a_1|^2+|a_2|^2=\sigma^2.
\]
Hence every \(g(a_1x_1+a_2x_2+Q(x_3,\ldots,x_d))\) is covered for this exact original input class.
The two diagonal laws remain arbitrary permitted real finite-second-moment laws.
This includes the earlier elliptic real/skew example and also coefficient choices not meeting its EC pattern.

Every exact EC certificate in C_F0186_v14 is contained in the current condition: its normalized pair has Hermitian cross covariance \((2\mu-1)\rho\), of modulus less than one.
The phase of the common scalar normalization cancels from this cross covariance, and its modulus is \(\sigma\) by the unit-variance condition, so (Exposed-Gram) gives the same strict inequality.
Two real-symmetric exposed inputs with coefficients \(1,i\), however, have \(m_1=m_2=1\) and \(h=\sigma^2\).
They remain outside the present sufficient condition even though the coefficients have different phases.

## Exact gain and remaining obligations

The positive-covariance floor supplies arbitrary-power marginal failures for the exposed combination, and one original Hermitian input supplies the summable intermediate singular-value bound.
The logarithmic and measurable planar assembly then yields the stated full-sequence conclusions under every allowed cross-size coupling.
The substantive extension is the exact weighted second-moment criterion and its fixed-law match to C_F0194_v11; the other ranges use the same accepted interfaces as the earlier elliptic assembly.
The new floor and consuming proof have fresh independent findings at their expanded scopes; no old independent finding is selected as coverage of this enlarged statement.

Independent additive exposure and strict positive mirror-pair covariance are genuine hypotheses.
General noncommutative polynomials without that exposure, a common phase-line exposed family, arbitrary auxiliary matrix coefficients, and unrestricted noncommutative products of these outputs are not covered by this statement.
Failure of this sufficient condition is not a counterexample to any of those conclusions.
The original unrestricted F0012 Brown contract and F0013/F0014 determinant contracts remain unresolved.
