# Full-sequence Brown limits for finite recursive Wigner carriers

This version extends the [positive-covariance additive theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v15.md#statement) to two-sided carriers with an additional independent polynomial background and finite recursive factor certificates.
The recursion propagates both arbitrary-power least-value bounds and a quantitative intermediate range, rather than assuming that a factor already has an unproved inverse bound.
It also permits the exact previously proved real symmetric-carrier class as a seed.
Earlier physical versions retain their own statements and assessments; the expanded class has a [new independent whole-chain finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v15.md#recursive-carrier-whole-proof) selected below.

## Exact proof inputs and their roles

The adopted [positive mirror-pair floor](Yin_C_F0194_v11.md#decision-positive-pair-reviewed) supplies every prescribed polynomial failure exponent for fixed centered complex mirror pairs with unit marginal variances and positive Hermitian covariance, complex finite-second-moment diagonals, and arbitrary independent polynomial-entry complex shifts.
Its exact singularity and independent-random-shift conclusions are included.
The [original additive covariance calculation](Yin_C_F0186_v15.md#pair-covariance) identifies the raw pair used at every new exposure; its strict weighted criterion, not the older full EC pattern, is used here.

The adopted [complex Hermitian intermediate theorem](Yin_C_F0187_v2.md#decision-complex-intermediate-reviewed) supplies the N^(3/4) range, arbitrary independent complex shifts without a norm guard, and summable marginal failures.
It is applied to one original Hermitian input at a time, including after the other members of an exposed combination have been conditioned.

The [real symmetric-carrier theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v7.md#decision-symmetric-carrier-reviewed), including its [complete floor and middle argument](Yin_C_F0186_v7.md#argument), supplies only the precisely restated seed class below.
Its proof gives arbitrary-power floor failures and a fixed product exponent K+1 in the intermediate range.
The probability-only carrier results in C_F0186_v10 and C_F0186_v12 are methodological predecessors, not inputs that can replace these summable bounds.

The [deterministic product min-max argument](Yin_C_F0186_v6.md#argument) supplies the increasing-singular-value product inequality for arbitrary square matrices, including singular factors.
The [finite-variance spectral controls](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#decision-finite-variance-reviewed) supply Hermitized weak convergence, positive logarithmic tails, local planar L2 bounds, the Brown-potential convention, and pathwise tightness for each fixed original polynomial.
No conditional rational expression is substituted into that polynomial weak-limit theorem.

[FK52-U001](../audits/literature/FK52/Yin_U001.md#bounded-multiplicativity-and-the-finite-tracial-adaptation) supplies ordinary bounded FK multiplicativity, including zero values, at its retained finite-tracial and original-proof inspection scope.
Only bounded free factors are used; no inverse of a free factor is assumed bounded or inserted into that theorem.
The elementary root, finite determinant, norm and probability calculations are supplied below.
No new external source theorem or inherited review of this expanded assembly is asserted.

<a id="model"></a>
## Original fixed-law complex Hermitian model

Fix \(d\ge1\).
For each \(j\), fix a complex law \(\xi_j\) with \(\mathbb E\xi_j=0\), \(\mathbb E|\xi_j|^2=1\), and a real law \(\zeta_j\) with \(\mathbb E\zeta_j=0\), \(\mathbb E\zeta_j^2<\infty\).
At each size \(N\), the upper off-diagonal entries of \(W_j^{(N)}\) are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), the lower entries are their conjugates, and the diagonals are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\).
All those families and input matrices are independent within a dimension.
The laws are fixed independently of \(N\), while all dimensions may have any coupling preserving these complete marginal laws.
No density, moment above two, or independence between the real and imaginary parts of one entry is imposed.
Let \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).
Every coefficient, polynomial, index set and degree below is fixed independently of \(N\).
Use ordinary determinants including zero values, \(\log0=-\infty\), algebraic eigenvalue multiplicities, and the ordinary bounded FK determinant \(\Delta_\tau\).

<a id="certificate"></a>
## Finite certificates with explicit independent exposures

A certificate is a finite ordered list of scalar noncommutative polynomials \(R_1,\ldots,R_t\), called carrier nodes.
Each node has a declared set \(S_\alpha\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\) containing every variable used in its displayed construction.
All certificate data, including its length and its polynomials, are fixed independently of \(N\).
Only an earlier node may be cited as a factor of a later node, so the certificate has no cycle.

A factor expression made from earlier nodes is
\[
 F=c\prod_{\nu=1}^{q}g_\nu(R_{\beta_\nu}),
 \qquad c\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\},\qquad
 g_\nu\in\mathbb C[t]\setminus\{0\}.
 \tag{Factor-expression}
\]
The displayed order is retained; nodes and indices may repeat, and factors may share variables and fail to commute.
The empty product is the nonzero constant \(c\).
Nonzero constant \(g_\nu\)'s can be absorbed into \(c\).
Requiring an expression to use only a set \(J\) means that every referenced node has \(S_{\beta_\nu}\subseteq J\), after constant factors have been removed.
This explicit condition is sufficient; no cancellation of an undeclared input is inferred.

There are two permitted forms of a carrier node.

### Positive-covariance carrier step

Choose a nonempty \(I_\alpha\subseteq S_\alpha\), nonzero coefficients \(a_{\alpha j}\in\mathbb C\), and put
\[
 M_\alpha=\sum_{j\in I_\alpha}a_{\alpha j}x_j,\qquad
 \sigma_\alpha^2=\sum_{j\in I_\alpha}|a_{\alpha j}|^2,\qquad
 h_\alpha=\sum_{j\in I_\alpha}|a_{\alpha j}|^2\mathbb E\xi_j^2.
\]
Require
\[
 |h_\alpha|<\sigma_\alpha^2.
 \tag{Node-covariance}
\]
Take factor expressions \(B_\alpha,C_\alpha\) made only from earlier nodes whose declared sets lie in \(S_\alpha\setminus I_\alpha\), and an arbitrary scalar polynomial \(Q_\alpha\) in that same background set.
Define
\[
 R_\alpha=B_\alpha M_\alpha C_\alpha+Q_\alpha.
 \tag{Recursive-carrier}
\]
Neither side factor nor the background is required to be selfadjoint, symmetric, or bounded uniformly in matrix size.
All inputs in \(I_\alpha\) remain independent of the entire background within each dimension.
Choosing both side factors constant gives the additive class without earlier nodes.

### Accepted real symmetric seed

Alternatively, the node may have the exact following certificate from C_F0186_v7.
All off-diagonal laws indexed by \(S_\alpha\) are real, and one distinguished index is \(i\in S_\alpha\).
Let \(\operatorname{rev}\) reverse words without conjugating their coefficients.
Choose \(c\ne0\), \(K\ge0\), and polynomials
\[
 Q\in\mathbb C\langle x_j:j\in S_\alpha\setminus\{i\}\rangle,
 \qquad \operatorname{rev}Q=Q,
\]
\[
 T_\nu=x_{j_\nu}+Q_\nu((x_j)_{j\in S_\alpha\setminus\{i,j_\nu\}}),
 \quad j_\nu\in S_\alpha\setminus\{i\},\quad
 \operatorname{rev}Q_\nu=Q_\nu\qquad(1\le\nu\le K),
\]
\[
 H=cT_1\cdots T_K,\qquad \operatorname{rev}H=H,
 \qquad R_\alpha=(x_i+Q)H.
 \tag{Symmetric-seed}
\]
For \(K=0\), put \(H=c\).
The original centered real diagonal laws may be arbitrary finite-second-moment laws.
Only this exact real-input seed is imported; arbitrary nonsymmetric or merely probability-level carriers are not seeds by default.

### Quantitative exponent attached to the certificate

Assign the nonnegative integer
\[
 \ell(F)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{q}\deg(g_\nu)\ell(R_{\beta_\nu})
 \tag{Factor-exponent}
\]
to a factor expression, with \(\ell(c)=0\).
For a positive-covariance step and a symmetric seed respectively, assign
\[
 \ell(R_\alpha)=\ell(B_\alpha)+1+\ell(C_\alpha),
 \qquad
 \ell(R_\alpha)=K+1.
 \tag{Node-exponent}
\]
Every node exponent is positive.
These exponents are determined from finite certificate data; their definition assumes no random singular-value bound.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Full-sequence conclusions and quantitative bounds

Write increasing singular values as \(a_1(A)\le\cdots\le a_N(A)\), and put \(q_N=\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil\).
For every node \(R_\alpha\), fixed \(z\in\mathbb C\), and prescribed \(b>0\), there are fixed \(A,c,D,C_b,N_0>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
  a_1(R_\alpha(W^{(N)})-zI)<N^{-A}
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \exists k\in[\lceil Dq_N\rceil,N]:
  a_k(R_\alpha(W^{(N)})-zI)<c(k/N)^{\ell(R_\alpha)}
                 \right\}
 \le C_bN^{-b}\qquad(N>N_0).
 \tag{Node-floor-and-middle}
\]
The constants may depend on the fixed certificate, laws, node, parameter and \(b\), but not on \(N\).
Every factor expression in the completed certificate has the same bound at zero shift with exponent \(\ell(F)\); a constant factor has its deterministic interpretation with exponent zero.
In particular these are all-dimension bounds that include exact singularity.

For every node and fixed \(z\),
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det(R_\alpha(W^{(N)})-zI)|
 \longrightarrow \log\Delta_\tau(R_\alpha(s)-z)\in\mathbb R
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Carrier-log}
\]
There is one probability-one event for the weak Brown convergence of each node against every bounded continuous real test function on \(\mathbb C\).
The finitely many node events can be intersected.
For each fixed one-variable polynomial \(g\), the same weak conclusion holds for \(g(R_\alpha)\), including constant \(g\).

For each fixed \(g\) and \(\lambda\),
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det(g(R_\alpha(W^{(N)}))-\lambda I)|
 \longrightarrow\log\Delta_\tau(g(R_\alpha(s))-\lambda)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Outer-log}
\]
The target is finite when \(g(t)-\lambda\) is a nonzero polynomial, and both sides are identically \(-\infty\) when it is the zero polynomial.
A factor expression \(F\) has a finite full-sequence logarithmic limit at zero and \(\Delta_\tau(F(s))>0\).
Its factors may be dependent and noncommuting.
A deliberately included identically zero factor instead gives the zero polynomial and logarithms \(-\infty\), but is not an admissible background factor certificate.
The empty product gives its nonzero scalar and its scalar logarithm.

Every conclusion holds under arbitrary cross-size coupling preserving the original marginals.
The logarithmic event may depend on the fixed parameter; no simultaneous event for all uncountably many parameters is claimed.
A general ordered factor expression has only the stated zero-shift conclusion unless it is itself a certified node or a covered outer-polynomial image.
No Brown conclusion for an arbitrary product follows merely from its zero-shift determinant factorization.

## Complete inductive proof

<a id="product-closure"></a>
### The deterministic product and root steps

For arbitrary square matrices and \(r+s-1\le N\), the accepted min-max inequality is
\[
 a_{r+s-1}(UV)\ge a_r(U)a_s(V).
 \tag{Product-minmax}
\]
It follows by intersecting a subspace where \(V\) has the required lower bound with the inverse image under \(V\) of a subspace where \(U\) has its lower bound.
The codimensions add, and the argument allows singular factors.
Iterating gives the product index \(\sum_{j=1}^{L}r_j-(L-1)\), whenever this index is at most \(N\).

Suppose the earlier nodes satisfy (Node-floor-and-middle) for every fixed parameter.
Factor each nonconstant \(g_\nu(t)=b_\nu\prod_h(t-z_{\nu h})\), with roots counted with multiplicity, and retain nonzero constants as scalar multipliers.
Then \(F(W)\) is exactly a nonzero scalar times a fixed ordered product of \(L\) matrices \(R_{\beta_\nu}(W)-z_{\nu h}I\).
No factors are commuted across different nodes.
A finite union and \(a_1(UV)\ge a_1(U)a_1(V)\) prove the asserted arbitrary-power floor for \(F\).
The scalar multiplier is absorbed by increasing the floor exponent for sufficiently large \(N\).

For the intermediate range, take \(r=\lfloor k/L\rfloor\) when \(L\ge1\).
The iterated product index \(L(r-1)+1\) is at most \(k\), while for \(k\ge2L(D_*+2)q_N\), one has \(r\ge D_*q_N\) and \(r\ge k/(2L)\), where \(D_*\) bounds the finitely many earlier thresholds.
Monotonicity and (Product-minmax) therefore give
\[
 a_k(F(W))\ge c_F(k/N)^{\sum_{\nu,h}\ell(R_{\beta_\nu})}
              =c_F(k/N)^{\ell(F)}.
 \tag{Factor-middle}
\]
All bounds hold simultaneously outside a finite sum of the earlier \(O(N^{-b})\) failures.
Independence between these factors is not used.
When \(L=0\), \(F\) is a fixed nonzero scalar, and both quantitative bounds are immediate.
This establishes factor closure without using a limiting free inverse or a logarithmic conclusion.

<a id="seed-interface"></a>
### The exact seed and fixed-polynomial norm interfaces

For a real symmetric seed, the proof of C_F0186_v7 gives a floor with every prescribed polynomial failure exponent and intermediate exponent \(K+1\), starting at \(2(K+1)(q_N+1)\).
Its conditional shifts are symmetric under precisely (Symmetric-seed), and its finitely many intermediate failures are bounded by \(CN^2e^{-cN^{1/4}}\).
Choosing its requested failure exponent larger than \(\max(b,2)\), and absorbing the exponential term for large \(N\), gives (Node-floor-and-middle).
Restriction to the declared real-input set preserves its complete marginal model and arbitrary cross-size coupling; unused complex inputs do not change that application.
No weaker probability floor from a nonsymmetric predecessor is upgraded to a summable one.

For every original input,
\[
 \mathbb E\|W_j^{(N)}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
       =N-1+\mathbb E\zeta_j^2\le C_jN.
\]
Markov and a finite union give \(\mathbb P\{\max_j\|W_j\|>N^K\}\le CN^{1-2K}\).
Any fixed polynomial \(Q\) therefore has a polynomial norm bound with failure \(O(N^{-b})\) for every prescribed \(b\), by first choosing \(K\) and then its polynomial exponent.
This uses only second moments and applies also to an arbitrary background polynomial with no factor certificate.

<a id="carrier-floor"></a>
### A summable floor for the two-sided carrier

Consider a positive-covariance node, suppress its index, and fix \(z\) and a prescribed failure exponent \(b\).
By factor closure for the earlier nodes and the norm bound just proved, choose a background-measurable event \(J_N\) and fixed exponents \(A_B,A_C,K_Q\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P(J_N^c)=O(N^{-b}),\qquad
 a_1(B(W))\ge N^{-A_B},\quad
 a_1(C(W))\ge N^{-A_C},\quad
 \|Q(W)\|\le N^{K_Q}\quad\text{on }J_N.
 \tag{Background-guard}
\]
Take \(J_N\) to be exactly the intersection of these matrix inequalities.
Every quantity depends only on inputs outside the exposed set \(I\), even if the factors share those inputs.

On \(J_N\), define
\[
 F_N=B(W)^{-1}(Q(W)-zI)C(W)^{-1},
 \tag{Inverse-shift}
\]
and set \(F_N=0\) off \(J_N\).
Put \(V_N=M(W)+F_N\) on the whole probability space.
Inversion is used only where the finite matrices are invertible; this definition is measurable.
The shift is independent of the entire exposed combination and has a deterministic polynomial norm bound on the whole probability space.
Its exponent is chosen from \(A_B,A_C,K_Q\) before applying the new floor.

Write \(\sigma>0\) for the square root of \(\sum_{j\in I}|a_j|^2\).
The raw upper/lower pair of \(\sqrt N M(W)/\sigma\) is
\[
 U=\sigma^{-1}\sum_{j\in I}a_j\xi_j,\qquad
 V=\sigma^{-1}\sum_{j\in I}a_j\overline{\xi_j}.
\]
The exact covariance calculation of C_F0186_v15 gives unit marginal variances and cross covariance \(h/\sigma^2\), so (Node-covariance) is the positive-pair condition.
The fixed raw diagonal law is \(\sigma^{-1}\sum_{j\in I}a_j\zeta_j\), complex with finite second moment and independent of all unordered pairs.
Apply C_F0194_v11 to the raw matrix and the conditional shift \(\sqrt N F_N/\sigma\), then divide singular values by \(\sqrt N/\sigma\).
For a fixed exponent \(A_V\), this gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{a_1(V_N)<N^{-A_V}\}=O(N^{-b}).
 \tag{Exposed-floor}
\]
The fixed scalar and normalization are absorbed by increasing \(A_V\).

On \(J_N\), the exact identity is
\[
 R(W)-zI=B(W)V_NC(W).
 \tag{Carrier-factorization}
\]
The three least-value bounds and their finite union yield the floor in (Node-floor-and-middle), with exponent at least \(A_B+A_V+A_C\).
There is no use of a free inverse, a symmetry of the inverse shift, or independence of the background factors.

<a id="carrier-middle"></a>
### The intermediate range uses one original input

Choose any \(j_0\in I\) and condition on all original inputs except \(W_{j_0}\).
Then
\[
 V_N=a_{j_0}(W_{j_0}+H_N),\qquad
 H_N=a_{j_0}^{-1}
       \left(\sum_{j\in I\setminus\{j_0\}}a_jW_j+F_N\right)
\]
has a shift independent of the whole original \(W_{j_0}\).
The zero extension of \(F_N\) preserves this independence because \(J_N\) uses only inputs outside \(I\).
C_F0187_v2 consequently gives
\[
 a_r(V_N)\ge c_V(r-1)/N\ge(c_V/2)r/N,
 \qquad q_N+1\le r\le N,
 \tag{Exposed-middle}
\]
outside \(CN^2e^{-cN^{1/4}}\), with no shift norm restriction.
The fixed nonzero coefficient is absorbed into \(c_V\).

Factor closure gives the middle bounds for \(B,C\) with exponents \(\ell(B),\ell(C)\), outside \(O(N^{-b})\) failures.
For sufficiently large fixed \(D\) and \(k\ge Dq_N\), choose \(r=\lfloor k/3\rfloor\).
Then \(r\) lies in all three middle ranges, \(r\ge k/6\), and \(3(r-1)+1\le k\).
On \(J_N\) and these three middle events, apply (Product-minmax) to (Carrier-factorization) to obtain
\[
 a_k(R(W)-zI)\ge c_R(k/N)^{\ell(B)+1+\ell(C)}.
 \tag{Carrier-middle}
\]
Constants have exponent zero and satisfy the required bound at every index.
The event complement is \(O(N^{-b})+CN^2e^{-cN^{1/4}}=O(N^{-b})\) for large \(N\).
This proves (Node-floor-and-middle) and completes the finite induction over all nodes and their factor expressions.

<a id="logarithmic-and-planar"></a>
### Complete logarithmic tails and one planar event

Choose \(b>1\) in the quantitative bound and apply first Borel--Cantelli under the actual coupling.
For a fixed node and fixed \(z\), its floor and middle bound hold eventually almost surely.
Put \(k_N=\lceil Dq_N\rceil\) and \(\ell=\ell(R)\).
The lowest \(k_N\) singular values contribute at most \(Ak_N\log N/N\to0\) to the normalized negative logarithm.
For \(k_N<k\le\varepsilon N\), the middle bound gives
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{k_N<k\le\varepsilon N}
       (-\log a_k(R(W)-zI))_+
 \le (-\log c)_+\varepsilon
        +\frac\ell N\sum_{k\le\varepsilon N}\log(N/k).
\]
The limsup is at most \((-\log c)_+\varepsilon+\ell\varepsilon(1-\log\varepsilon)\), which tends to zero as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
All indices \(k\ge\varepsilon N\) have \(a_k\ge c\varepsilon^\ell\) eventually.
Taking a singular-value cutoff below that level proves the entire negative logarithmic uniform integrability, and the floor excludes exact zeros eventually.
The same argument applies to a nonconstant factor expression; a nonzero constant is immediate.

C_F0186_v4 applies to the fixed original polynomial \(R-z\), and supplies its Hermitized weak limit and positive logarithmic tail control.
Clipping the logarithm between two finite levels, taking the weak limit and then removing the cutoffs proves (Carrier-log).
The same negative-tail bound proves integrability of the free negative logarithm and absence of a zero atom, while boundedness of the free polynomial controls its positive logarithm.
Thus the free target is finite without assuming bounded free inverses or a separate algebraic full-rank criterion.

For the Brown conclusion, \(u_N(\omega,z)=N^{-1}\log|\det(R(W(\omega))-zI)|\) is jointly measurable, and \(u(z)=\log\Delta(R(s)-z)\) is a deterministic Borel function finite at every fixed \(z\).
The convergence event is measurable by the countable limit criterion.
Tonelli on each integer-radius disk and a countable intersection give one event of planar almost-everywhere convergence.
Intersect it with the pathwise local planar L2 bounds and tightness for this fixed polynomial from C_F0186_v4.
On every compact disk the L2 bounds give uniform integrability, hence local planar L1 convergence.
The distributional identities \(\Delta u_N=2\pi\mu_{R(W)}\) and \(\Delta u=2\pi\mu_{R(s)}^{\mathrm{Br}}\) give convergence against smooth compactly supported tests.
Pathwise tightness and approximation on compact sets extend it to every bounded continuous real test on that same event.

<a id="outer-and-factor-log"></a>
### Outer images and the factor nonvanishing criterion

If \(g(t)-\lambda\) is a nonzero nonconstant polynomial, root factorization gives a nonzero scalar times finitely many \(R-z_h\) factors.
Intersect their fixed-parameter probability-one events.
Ordinary determinant multiplicativity and bounded FK multiplicativity then prove (Outer-log), since every limiting summand is finite.
A nonzero constant gives its scalar logarithm, and the identically zero branch gives \(-\infty\) directly.
The same factorization also gives the quantitative zero-shift bound with exponent \(\deg(g-\lambda)\ell(R)\) whenever \(g-\lambda\ne0\).

For every admissible factor expression, bounded FK multiplicativity gives the explicit finite formula
\[
 \log\Delta(F(s))=\log|c|+
       \sum_\nu\left(\log|b_\nu|
          +\sum_h\log\Delta(R_{\beta_\nu}(s)-z_{\nu h})\right)
       \in\mathbb R,
 \tag{Factor-free-log}
\]
where the roots and leading coefficients are those of the nonconstant \(g_\nu\), and constant factors are absorbed into \(c\).
Consequently \(\Delta(F(s))>0\), so the factor cannot vanish as a free operator.
This follows from the displayed certificate and proved logarithmic limits, not from an unsupported inference that a formally nonzero polynomial always has an adequate inverse bound.
The corresponding finite logarithms add in the prescribed order on a finite intersection of probability-one events, proving the factor's full-sequence zero-shift conclusion.
No commutation or independence of those factors is needed.

For a bounded free element, the same root factorization and Brown logarithmic-potential formula give \(\mu_{g(R(s))}^{\mathrm{Br}}=g_*\mu_{R(s)}^{\mathrm{Br}}\), including constant \(g\), as in the [fixed polynomial pushforward proof](Yin_C_F0186_v15.md#outer-polynomial).
Finite-dimensional spectral mapping uses algebraic multiplicities and gives the identical pushforward relation.
Continuous pushforward of the node's weak convergence proves the outer-polynomial Brown conclusion on its existing event.
This finishes the stated conclusions.

<a id="concrete-classes"></a>
## Concrete additions to the original-model subclasses

For a one-level example, take \(B,C\) to be nonzero scalars times finite ordered products of additive nodes \(T_\nu=\sum_{j\in I_\nu}b_{\nu j}x_j+Q_\nu\).
Require each such node's strict weighted covariance condition, and require that all of its variables omit the outer exposed set \(I\).
Its background \(Q_\nu\) also omits its own \(I_\nu\).
Then \(B(\sum_{j\in I}a_jx_j)C+Q\) has the theorem's complete full-sequence logarithmic and Brown conclusions when the outer combination satisfies (Node-covariance) and \(Q\) omits \(I\).
Repeated factor indices, shared background variables and noncommuting factors are permitted.
This includes an additional arbitrary background \(Q\) outside the two side factors, not only a factorized expression \((M+Q)H\).

If each of the distinct inputs \(x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_r}\) has \(|\mathbb E\xi_{i_j}^2|<1\), each \(R_j=x_{i_j}\) is an additive node.
The ordered product \(x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}\) is itself a new carrier: expose \(x_{i_1}\), take \(B=1\), take the remaining ordered product for \(C\), and set \(Q=0\).
For \(r=1\), use the original additive node.
Therefore this noncommutative ordered product has the full conclusion at every fixed spectral parameter, including zero, and its outer polynomial images have the full Brown conclusion.
No cross-size independence or equality of the different entry laws is required.

More generally, a word with an input occurring exactly once can be written \(B x_i C\).
If that input has \(|\mathbb E\xi_i^2|<1\), and every other input appearing in the word is either non-line complex or real, the side words have factor certificates from the corresponding additive nodes or K=0 real symmetric seeds.
The other variables may repeat arbitrarily within and between the two side words.
The theorem then proves the same full-sequence conclusions for this word and its outer polynomial images.
It does not assert that every word has such an exposure or such a representation after algebraic manipulation.

<a id="boundary"></a>
## Remaining restrictions

The certificate is a sufficient condition that must be displayed and checked; arbitrary noncommutative polynomials do not acquire one from being nonzero.
At each new carrier step the whole background, including every previously built side factor, must omit the newly exposed inputs.
A variable appearing once in several different summands does not automatically produce a single factorization \(BMC+Q\).
The finite recursive closure therefore leaves the general matrix-coefficient and scalar-polynomial Tasks unresolved.

A single phase-line input fails (Node-covariance); several such inputs can pass only when their weighted second moments satisfy its strict inequality, as characterized in C_F0186_v15.
A purely imaginary zero-diagonal input is deterministically singular at zero in odd dimensions, so it cannot be admitted as an all-dimension zero-shift factor by this certificate.
Its even-dimensional floors or the nonsummable estimates in earlier carrier versions do not supply the all-dimension bounds used in this proof.
The accepted real symmetric seeds retain their separate symmetric-shift restrictions, and a common phase-line failure is not itself a refutation of Brown convergence.

This is a new assembly and recursive range, not a claim of completion of F0012, F0013 or F0014.
The new statement, induction, seed interface and consuming chain have the independent fixed-proof coverage selected in the assessment below.
