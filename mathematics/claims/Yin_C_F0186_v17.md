# Least values and determinant limits for a bounded two-channel Wigner model

This theorem completes a bounded real two-channel submodel of the [matrix-coefficient F0013 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) and its specified quadratic scalar reduction.
A deterministic vanishing scalar translation makes every retained physical minor invertible almost surely.
The proof transfers the needed spectral and coefficient estimates by their actual deterministic arguments, realizes each bounded fresh row by independent two-copy pairs and fair signs, applies the exact conditional and physical averaging interfaces of the graph-energy bridge, and transfers the least-value bound back.
The original model's logarithmic conclusion then uses the fixed intermediate theorem with this polynomial floor.
The operator-gap and comparable-mode inputs have been adopted at [their exact decisions in the current C187 entrance](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#decision-operator-gap-reviewed), including [the comparable-mode adoption](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#decision-comparable-modes-reviewed).
The intermediate-logarithm input has its [exact reviewed adoption](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#decision-balanced-middle-reviewed).
The [independent whole-chain review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v16.md#bounded-two-channel-whole-chain) supports the [proved adoption](#decision-bounded-two-channel-reviewed) of this complete assembly.

## Exact bounded model and conclusions

For \(j=1,2\), let \(W_j^{(N)}\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
The upper off-diagonal entries of \(W_j^{(N)}\) are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where each \(\xi_j\) is fixed, real, centered, variance one and bounded.
For each \(j\), the diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\zeta_j\) is fixed, real, centered and bounded.
Zero diagonal laws are allowed.
All upper-triangular coordinates and the two inputs are independent within each size.
Across different sizes the coupling is arbitrary.
Fix \(L<\infty\) bounding the absolute values of all raw entry laws.

Put
\[
 \alpha:=\sqrt2,\qquad X_N:=W_1^{(N)}+W_2^{(N)},\qquad
 Y_N:=W_1^{(N)}+\alpha W_2^{(N)},
\]
\[
 L_N:=\begin{pmatrix}X_N&I_N\\I_N&Y_N\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Q_N:=Y_NX_N-I_N.
 \tag{Fixed-bounded-pencils}
\]
There is a fixed \(c>0\), depending only on these laws, such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)<cN^{-5}
                    \text{ or }s_{\min}(Q_N)<cN^{-5}\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Unconditional-bounded-floor}
\]
The estimate is for all dimensions tending to infinity, without an inverse guard, a remaining singular-minor term, or a summability claim.

Let \(s_1,s_2\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).
Define
\[
 \ell:=\begin{pmatrix}s_1+s_2&1\\1&s_1+\sqrt2s_2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad q_\infty:=(s_1+\sqrt2s_2)(s_1+s_2)-1,
 \qquad \tau_2:=\operatorname{tr}_2\otimes\tau.
\]
The ordinary bounded FK logarithms are finite and satisfy
\[
 d:=\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell)\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 \log\Delta_\tau(q_\infty)=2d.
\]
Using \(\log0=-\infty\), the normalized logarithms satisfy
\[
 \frac1{2N}\log|\det L_N|\longrightarrow d,\qquad
 \frac1N\log|\det Q_N|\longrightarrow2d
 \quad\text{in probability along the full sequence}.
 \tag{Bounded-log-probability}
\]
Under every allowed cross-size coupling,
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1{2N}\log|\det L_N|=d,\qquad
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|\det Q_N|=2d
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Bounded-log-limsup}
\]
These are the displayed fixed pencil and quadratic polynomial at their stated determinant parameter.
No full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic convergence or planar Brown-measure conclusion is asserted.

## Complete argument

<a id="deterministic-atom-avoiding-shift"></a>
### Deterministic translations remove singular physical minors

For \(N\ge3\), let \(M_{N,S}\) be the original principal two-channel pencil with \(|S|\le2\), including the empty deletion.
Every ordered eigenvalue is a real random variable.
A probability measure on the real line has at most countably many atoms: for each positive integer \(k\), only finitely many points can have mass at least \(1/k\), and every positive atom is in one of these sets.
There are finitely many eigenvalue laws and deletion sets at a fixed \(N\).
Thus their negative atoms form a countable deterministic set \(\mathcal A_N\).

Choose deterministically
\[
 0<\theta_N<N^{-10},\qquad \theta_N\notin\mathcal A_N.
 \tag{Atom-avoiding-shift}
\]
The complement of a countable set in this interval is nonempty.
The choice depends only on the size and prescribed marginal laws, never on the sampled entries.
It follows that
\[
 M_{N,S}^{\theta}:=M_{N,S}+\theta_NI_{2(N-|S|)}
 \quad\text{is invertible almost surely for every }|S|\le2.
 \tag{Shifted-minor-invertibility}
\]
The finite union at each size and countable union over sizes show that these assertions hold on one probability-one event under every cross-size coupling.
The finitely many smaller sizes do not affect any asymptotic conclusion.

Shift the entire parent:
\[
 L_N^\theta:=L_N+\theta_NI_{2N}
 =\begin{pmatrix}X_N+\theta_NI_N&I_N\\
                  I_N&Y_N+\theta_NI_N\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Shifted-parent}
\]
It is obtained by replacing the first input by \(W_1^{(N)}+\theta_NI_N\), leaving all original off-diagonal coordinates and boundary rows unchanged.
Its physical minors are exactly \(M_{N,S}^{\theta}\).
The diagonal law of the translated first input is not centered and fixed in \(N\), so its required regularity is established next from the original model.

<a id="translated-spectral-regularity"></a>
### Weighted mass, projectors and closest-mode leverage

Write \(M:=M_{N,S}\), \(M^\theta:=M+\theta_NI\), and \(n:=N-|S|\).
For this deletion set, write \(C:=W_1^{(N)}[S^c,S^c]\), \(D:=W_2^{(N)}[S^c,S^c]\), \(X:=C+D\), and \(Y:=C+\alpha D\).
For every fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), eventually \(|\theta_N|\le\delta/2\), and functional calculus gives
\[
 \mathbf1_{[-\delta/2,\delta/2]}(M)
 \le\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}(M^\theta)
 \le\mathbf1_{[-2\delta,2\delta]}(M).
 \tag{Translated-window-inclusions}
\]
These are inequalities between spectral projections of the same original matrix.
For the auxiliary-channel projections \(\Pi_1,\Pi_2\), positive-semidefinite order preserves the weighted trace.
The original weighted mass therefore gives, simultaneously for both channels and all \(|S|\le2\),
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\Pi_\ell\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}(M^\theta)
 \ge c\delta n
 \tag{Translated-channel-mass}
\]
outside a probability tending to zero.
No weighted interlacing assertion is used.

For \(|S|\le1\), let \(P_i\) be the rank-two projection onto retained physical coordinate \(i\).
Choose \(2\delta\) within the original projector theorem's range.
Its estimate and the second inclusion give
\[
 \max_{S,i}\|P_i\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}(M^\theta)P_i\|
 \le C\delta
 \tag{Translated-physical-projector}
\]
outside a probability tending to zero, with a constant independent of the fixed \(\delta\).
The dimension threshold may depend on \(\delta\).
The physical resolvent conclusion is stable as well: the resolvent identity bounds the difference between the original and translated compressed resolvents at \(i\delta\) by \(|\theta_N|/\delta^2=o(1)\) for fixed \(\delta\).
This uses the established original projector/resolvent events, not a new centered-law theorem.

The original fixed-window count supplies an eigenvalue near zero in every minor.
Consequently
\[
 \max_{|S|\le1}\min_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}M^\theta}|\lambda|
 \longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
 \tag{Translated-closest-eigenvalue}
\]
Indeed, a closest absolute eigenvalue after translation is at most the original closest absolute eigenvalue plus \(|\theta_N|\).
For every real unit eigenvector \(w=(u,v)\) closest to zero for \(M^\theta\), put \(\eta_i(w):=u_i^2+v_i^2\).
On the event that its shifted eigenvalue lies in \([-\delta,\delta]\), (Translated-physical-projector) gives \(\max_i\eta_i(w)\le C\delta\).
First fix \(\delta\), let \(N\) increase, and then let \(\delta\downarrow0\).
This gives uniform closest-mode leverage tending to zero.
The chosen vector need not have been a closest-to-zero mode of the original matrix.

<a id="translated-residual-control"></a>
### Residual inverse energy on the translated matrices

From this subsection through the complete coefficient estimates, restrict to \(|S|\le1\), the range of the physical projector input.
The preceding atom-avoidance and weighted-mass assertions also allowed two deletions, but no two-deletion physical residual estimate is used or asserted here.
The original norm event supplies a fixed \(\Lambda\ge1\), enlarged once, that bounds the norms of \(M^\theta\), its two diagonal blocks \(X^\theta,Y^\theta\), and the parent \(L_N^\theta\), uniformly outside \(Ce^{-cN}\).
Here \(X^\theta:=X+\theta_NI\) and \(Y^\theta:=Y+\theta_NI\).
This follows from the original bound and \(|\theta_N|\le1\).

For any real unit eigenmode \(w=(u,v)\) with \(M^\theta w=\lambda^\theta w\), define
\[
 Z:=(M^\theta)^{-1},\qquad q:=(\lambda^\theta)^{-1},\qquad
 E:=Z-qww^T,\qquad E_F:=\|E\|_{\rm F}.
\]
These quantities exist almost surely by (Shifted-minor-invertibility).
There are \(2n-1\) remaining inverse eigenvalues, each of modulus at least \(1/\Lambda\), so
\[
 E_F^2\ge(2n-1)/\Lambda^2.
 \tag{Translated-residual-basic-size}
\]
The original weighted count in \([-h,h]\), summed over channels, gives at least \(chn\) eigenvalues there for every fixed sufficiently small \(h>0\), uniformly over the minors.
For \(N\) large enough that \(|\theta_N|\le h\), those shifted eigenvalues lie in \([-2h,2h]\).
Removing any one mode deletes at most one of them, and none is zero on the almost-sure translated invertibility event.
Hence
\[
 E_F^2\ge\frac{chn-1}{4h^2}.
 \tag{Translated-residual-divergence}
\]
Taking \(N\to\infty\) at fixed \(h\), then \(h\downarrow0\), proves \(E_F^2/n\to\infty\) uniformly over minors and removed modes.
This proof remains valid when the original \(M\) was singular.

For fixed \(\delta>0\), split \(E=E_<+E_>\) using the shifted spectral projection onto \([-\delta,\delta]\) and its complement.
The residual commutes with \(M^\theta\), including removal of an arbitrary vector in a repeated eigenspace.
The low-energy projector satisfies (Translated-physical-projector), and \(\|E_>\|\le\delta^{-1}\).
Writing
\[
 d_i(E):=\|P_iE\|_{\rm F}^2,\qquad
 \mathcal D(E):=\sum_i\|P_iEP_i\|_{\rm F}^2,
\]
spectral orthogonality and the rank-two physical projections give
\[
 d_i(E)\le C\delta E_F^2+2\delta^{-2}.
 \tag{Translated-residual-row-bound}
\]
For the diagonal blocks, the low-energy estimate follows from
\[
 \|P_iE_<P_i\|_{\rm F}^2
 \le\|P_i\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}(M^\theta)\|^2
      \|E_<P_i\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
The high-energy block has squared norm at most \(2\delta^{-2}\).
Summing and using the two-term square inequality yields
\[
 \mathcal D(E)\le C\delta E_F^2+4n\delta^{-2}.
 \tag{Translated-residual-diagonal-bound}
\]
The basic size bound proves \(\max_i d_i(E)/E_F^2\to0\), and the stronger divergence proves \(\mathcal D(E)/E_F^2\to0\), by the same fixed-\(\delta\), then \(\delta\downarrow0\) order.
All these events are uniform over the stated minors and every removed mode.

For completeness, write
\[
 E=\begin{pmatrix}F&H_0\\H_0^T&J_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T_0:=(H_0+H_0^T)/2,\qquad
 \mathcal R^2:=\|\operatorname{off}F\|_{\rm F}^2+
       \|\operatorname{off}J_0\|_{\rm F}^2+
       \|\operatorname{off}T_0\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
The actual translated identity is
\[
 H_0=I-uu^T-FX^\theta.
\]
It follows from \(Z M^\theta=I\) and \(v=\lambda^\theta u-X^\theta u\).
The same direct norm estimate as in the fixed residual comparison gives
\[
 \mathcal R^2\ge
 \frac{E_F^2-4n}{1+4\Lambda^2}-\mathcal D(E).
\]
Thus \(\mathcal R^2\ge c E_F^2\) outside a uniform bad event tending to zero, and visible row loads are bounded by \(d_i(E)\).
This also verifies the translated visible residual interface.

Now choose \(w\) closest to zero for \(M^\theta\), and put
\[
 Q:=\|Z\|=|q|,\qquad A_Z:=\|Z\|_{\rm F},\qquad
 d_*:=\max_i\|P_iZ\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
Use \(\mathcal D(Z):=\sum_i\|P_iZP_i\|_{\rm F}^2\) for the same physical diagonal functional.
Since \(Ew=0\),
\[
 A_Z^2=q^2+E_F^2,\qquad
 \|P_iZ\|_{\rm F}^2=q^2\eta_i(w)+d_i(E),
\]
\[
 \mathcal D(Z)\le2\mathcal D(E)+2q^2\sum_i\eta_i(w)^2
                \le2\mathcal D(E)+2q^2\max_i\eta_i(w).
\]
Consequently
\[
 A_Z^2/n\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \mathcal D(Z)/A_Z^2\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 d_*/A_Z^2\longrightarrow0,\qquad Q\longrightarrow\infty,
 \tag{Translated-full-inverse-control}
\]
uniformly in probability.
The last assertion follows from \(Q^2\ge A_Z^2/(2n)\).
No continuity estimate for an original real inverse has been used.

<a id="translated-gap-gram-cross"></a>
### Joint gaps, unchanged auxiliary Gram matrices and cross energy

For real symmetric \(n\)-by-\(n\) matrices \(A,B\), define
\[
 \Gamma_R(A,B):=\inf_{\substack{z\in\mathbb R^n,\ \|z\|=1\\ a,b\in[-R,R]}}
       \max\{\|(A-aI)z\|,\|(B-bI)z\|\}.
\]
The translated diagonal blocks correspond exactly to
\[
 C^\theta:=C+\theta_NI,\qquad D^\theta:=D,\qquad
 X^\theta=C^\theta+D^\theta,\qquad
 Y^\theta=C^\theta+\alpha D^\theta.
\]
For each fixed \(R>0\) and \(|\theta_N|\le1\),
\[
 \Gamma_R(C^\theta,D^\theta)\ge\Gamma_{R+1}(C,D),
 \tag{Translated-joint-gap}
\]
because the first proposed eigenvalue center is replaced by \(a-\theta_N\), while the second center is unchanged.
The fixed original joint-gap theorem therefore supplies a positive translated gap on every required fixed center interval.

Every eigenspace of \(M^\theta\) is exactly an eigenspace of \(M\).
The original all-mode auxiliary Gram event is independent of the ordering by absolute eigenvalue and gives the same fixed \(g>0\) for every translated real unit eigenvector:
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\|u\|^2&\langle u,v\rangle\\
                 \langle u,v\rangle&\|v\|^2\end{pmatrix}\succeq gI_2.
 \tag{Translated-all-mode-Gram}
\]
This includes original zero and repeated eigenvalues.

For a shifted closest mode, (Translated-closest-eigenvalue) gives \(|\lambda^\theta|\le1\) with probability tending to one.
The deterministic cross-energy proof in C187v5 now applies with \(X^\theta,Y^\theta,\lambda^\theta,E\), the common fixed \(\Lambda,g\), and the original sign normalization.
Its exact residual identities hold for \(M^\theta\), and its conclusion is
\[
 \frac{N^4}{24}\sigma(s_4)^2
 \ge(c_{\Lambda,g}-C\eta_*)E_F^2
          -C_{\Lambda,g}n-C\mathcal D(E),
 \qquad \eta_*:=\max_i\eta_i(w).
\]
Here \(\Pi_4\) retains the degree-four multilinear Walsh layer after substituting independent formal signs and reducing their squares to one, and \(\sigma(f)^2\) is the sum of squared Walsh coefficients of \(f\).
Explicitly,
\[
 s_4:=\Pi_4\bigl[
 (u^Tx)^2(x^TJ_0x)+(v^Tx)^2(x^TFx)
 -2(u^Tx)(v^Tx)(x^TT_0x)\bigr],
 \qquad x_i=\varepsilon_i/\sqrt N.
\]
The translated leverage and residual conclusions just proved show, with one fixed \(c_{\rm cross}>0\), that
\[
 \sigma(s_4)^2\ge c_{\rm cross}E_F^2/N^4
 \tag{Translated-cross-energy}
\]
outside a uniform bad event tending to zero.
The constants may be chosen for the enlarged norm guard; no unchanged numerical value of an original probability theorem's constant is assumed.

<a id="translated-complete-fourth-layer"></a>
### Exhaustive whole-layer estimates by deterministic proof interfaces

Write the full translated inverse as \(Z=\begin{pmatrix}G&H\\H^T&J\end{pmatrix}\), set \(T:=(H+H^T)/2\), and put
\[
 p_4:=\Pi_4[(x^TGx)(x^TJx)-(x^TTx)^2],
 \qquad
 \sigma_4^2:=\sum_{|I|=4}|(p_4)_I|^2,
\]
\[
 L_i^{(4)}:=\sum_{\substack{|I|=4\\i\in I}}|(p_4)_I|^2,\qquad
 \tau_4:=\max_iL_i^{(4)}/\sigma_4^2
 \quad\text{when }\sigma_4>0.
\]
Let \(r_Z:=A_Z^2/Q^2\) and let \(m:=s_2^\downarrow(Z)\) be the second largest singular value of \(Z\).
For every closest mode, \(m=\|E\|\) and \(E_F^2=A_Z^2-Q^2\), independently of the choice in a tied eigenspace.

Choose a fixed center range
\[
 R_\Lambda:=2(\alpha+1)\Lambda/(\alpha-1).
\]
The original gap at \(R_\Lambda+1\) supplies one fixed \(\epsilon_0>0\) for \(\Gamma_{R_\Lambda}(C^\theta,D^\theta)\).
The exact linear conversion of residuals gives
\[
 \Gamma_\Lambda(X^\theta,Y^\theta)
 \ge\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}\Gamma_{R_\Lambda}(C^\theta,D^\theta).
\]
Choose \(\epsilon:=(\alpha-1)\epsilon_0/(\alpha+1)>0\), which is also no larger than the input-pair gap.
All constants in this subsection are fixed from \(\Lambda,\epsilon,g\) before \(N\) increases.

First apply the deterministic argument of C187v6.
Its hypotheses are the actual identity-block pencil, the norm and joint-gap bounds, \(A_Z^2/n\) sufficiently large, and \(\mathcal D(Z)/A_Z^2\) sufficiently small.
All have just been verified.
The six-Gram coercivity, diagonal removal and rank-sensitive four-cycle error give
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge N^{-4}
       (c_h A_Z^4-C_hQ^2A_Z^2).
\]
Choose a fixed \(r_h\ge\max\{2,2C_h/c_h\}\).
On \(r_Z\ge r_h\),
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge(c_h/2)A_Z^4/N^4,\qquad
 \tau_4\le C\,d_*/A_Z^2\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Translated-high-rank-branch}
\]
The load estimate is the direct coefficient bound \(L_i^{(4)}\le C A_Z^2d_i(Z)/N^4\) from the same fixed proof.
This is an application of its deterministic calculation, not of its centered-law probability statement.

Second use C187v7's dimension-independent mixed coefficient inequality.
With \(e_4:=\Pi_4[(x^TFx)(x^TJ_0x)-(x^TT_0x)^2]\) and \(p_4=q s_4+e_4\), it gives
\[
 |\langle s_4,e_4\rangle|\le C_m mE_F^2/N^4.
\]
Choose
\[
 \gamma:=\min\{1/2,c_{\rm cross}/(4C_m)\}.
\]
Then (Translated-cross-energy) implies on \(m\le\gamma Q\)
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge(c_{\rm cross}/2)Q^2E_F^2/N^4.
\]
Its deterministic load calculation gives
\[
 \tau_4\le C\left[\eta_*+
       \left(1+\frac{E_F^2}{Q^2}\right)
                      \frac{\max_i d_i(E)}{E_F^2}\right].
\]
On the part \(r_Z<r_h\), the parenthesis equals \(r_Z\) and is bounded by the fixed \(r_h\).
Thus the translated leverage and residual row estimates imply \(\tau_4\to0\) there.
No Frobenius dominance or upper bound on \(Q\) is used.

Finally, on \(r_Z<r_h\) and \(m>\gamma Q\), apply C187v8's deterministic comparable-mode raw separation with these fixed \(\Lambda,\epsilon,\gamma\).
Its lower threshold \(Q_0\) is eventually satisfied outside a uniform bad event by (Translated-full-inverse-control).
For the fully symmetrized tensor \(\mathscr S_Z\) of the raw fourth polynomial, the fixed raw lemma gives
\[
 \|\mathscr S_Z\|_{\rm F}\ge c_0Q^2.
\]
Its deterministic collision estimate gives
\[
 \frac{\|\mathscr S_Z^{\rm rep}\|_{\rm F}^2}{Q^4}
 \le C r_h^2\left(\frac{\mathcal D(Z)}{A_Z^2}
                         +\frac{d_*}{A_Z^2}\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here the superscript retains repeated physical indices.
The exact sign normalization and the coefficient load inequality therefore yield
\[
 \sigma_4^2=\frac{24}{N^4}\|\mathscr S_Z^{\rm dist}\|_{\rm F}^2
       \ge c_cQ^4/N^4,\qquad
 \tau_4\le C r_h^2d_*/A_Z^2\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Translated-comparable-branch}
\]
The raw lemma and collision bound require no centered entry-law hypothesis once their deterministic matrix and gap premises hold.

Define the positive minor-measurable scale
\[
 \mathfrak a(M^\theta):=
 \begin{cases}
 A_Z^2,&r_Z\ge r_h,\\
 QE_F,&r_Z<r_h,\ m\le\gamma Q,\\
 Q^2,&r_Z<r_h,\ m>\gamma Q.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{Translated-complete-scale}
\]
These three regimes cover every invertible translated minor.
Taking a minimum of their fixed positive energy constants and a union of their uniform bad events proves
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge c_a\mathfrak a(M^\theta)^2/N^4
 \quad\text{simultaneously for all }|S|\le1
 \tag{Translated-complete-energy}
\]
outside a probability tending to zero.
For every fixed \(\eta>0\), the probability that any such minor has a vanishing fourth layer or \(\tau_4>\eta\) also tends to zero.
There is no extra union factor in the number of minors, because the spectral and regularity events used throughout already control all of them.
The new common thresholds \(r_h,\gamma\) belong to this translated proof and do not alter any previously fixed theorem or branch.


<a id="bounded-two-copy-fourth-layer"></a>
### Two-copy weights preserve fourth energy and low coordinate load

Fix one exposed physical index \(i\).
Let \(\mathcal F_i\) contain the whole second input, the retained first-input principal minor and the exposed first diagonal.
The deterministic scalar shift adds no randomness to this conditioning.
The remaining first-input row has \(n=N-1\) independent raw coordinates with law \(\xi_1\).

Use the elementary two-copy representation from [C194v3](Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#two-copy-matching), here with a fourth-energy calculation.
On an enlarged probability space for this index, take independent copies \(\xi_j',\xi_j''\) of \(\xi_1\), independent fair signs \(\varepsilon_j\), and put
\[
 a_j:=(\xi_j'+\xi_j'')/2,\qquad
 b_j:=(\xi_j'-\xi_j'')/2,\qquad
 \xi_j:=a_j+b_j\varepsilon_j.
 \tag{Bounded-pair-representation}
\]
All pairs and signs are independent across \(j\) and independent of \(\mathcal F_i\).
Selecting one of two independent identically distributed copies preserves exactly the original independent-coordinate row law.
Moreover,
\[
 |b_j|\le L,\qquad \mathbb E b_j^2=\tfrac12,
 \qquad |a_j+b_j\varepsilon_j|\le L
 \quad\text{for every possible sign}.
\]
In particular this representation changes neither the matrix marginal law nor its support bound.

Let \(p_4=\sum_{|I|=4}c_I\varepsilon_I\) be the formal layer associated with the actual shifted retained minor, and write
\[
 S_4:=\sum_{|I|=4}c_I^2,\qquad
 \ell_j:=\sum_{\substack{|I|=4\\j\in I}}c_I^2,\qquad
 \tau:=\max_j\ell_j/S_4.
\]
Work on \(S_4>0\), as guaranteed by (Translated-complete-energy).
Each \(c_I\) already includes the original \(N^{-2}\) normalization.
Conditional on the pairs, the actual Schur determinant in the remaining signs has fourth coefficients
\[
 \widehat c_I:=c_I\prod_{j\in I}b_j.
 \tag{Bounded-pair-fourth-coefficients}
\]
Indeed, its degree in the unscaled first-input row is at most four.
Translation by \(a/\sqrt N\) changes only lower raw degrees of the homogeneous fourth part.
A raw degree-four monomial with a repeated coordinate has support at most three and cannot produce four distinct signs, while every lower-degree term also has Walsh degree at most three.
Thus no other Schur term contributes to the displayed fourth coefficients.

Define the conditional squared fourth energy
\[
 H:=\sum_{|I|=4}c_I^2\prod_{j\in I}b_j^2.
\]
Conditioning on \(\mathcal F_i\), the coefficients are fixed and the amplitudes are independent, so
\[
 \mathbb E[H\mid\mathcal F_i]=2^{-4}S_4.
\]
Products indexed by disjoint \(I,J\) are independent.
Every remaining covariance is bounded in absolute value by a constant depending only on \(L\).
Consequently
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Var}(H\mid\mathcal F_i)
 &\le C_L\sum_{I\cap J\ne\varnothing}c_I^2c_J^2\\
 &\le C_L\sum_j\ell_j^2
 \le4C_L\tau S_4^2.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Bounded-pair-variance}
\]
The last inequality uses \(\sum_j\ell_j=4S_4\).
Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{H<2^{-5}S_4\mid\mathcal F_i\}\le C_L'\tau.
 \tag{Bounded-pair-energy}
\]
On its complement the actual conditional coordinate load satisfies
\[
 \widehat\tau
 :=\frac{\max_j\sum_{I\ni j}\widehat c_I^2}{H}
 \le32L^8\tau.
 \tag{Bounded-pair-load}
\]
Zero or negative amplitudes cause no exception to these estimates.
This proof requires boundedness and variance one, not a deterministic positive lower bound on every amplitude.

<a id="shifted-parent-graph-bridge"></a>
### Enlarged conditioning and the shifted-parent matrix floor

Choose a deterministic cutoff \(v_N\to0\), with \(v_N\ge N^{-1/2}\), slowly enough that the uniform conclusions already proved hold with \(\tau_4\le v_N\) outside a probability tending to zero.
Such a sequence exists from uniform convergence in probability: choose successive deterministic size thresholds for errors \(1/k\) and failure probabilities \(1/k\), and then enlarge by \(N^{-1/2}\).
No quantitative convergence rate is asserted.

For each single-deletion minor \(M_i^\theta\), let \(\mathcal G_i^0\) be the following minor-measurable event:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 M_i^\theta\text{ is invertible},\qquad
 \|M_i^\theta\|\le\Lambda,\qquad Q_i\ge1,\qquad E_{F,i}\ge1,\\
 \sigma_{4,i}^2\ge c_a\mathfrak a(M_i^\theta)^2/N^4,\qquad
 \tau_{4,i}\le v_N.
 \end{gathered}
\]
All the scalars in this event are functions of the retained minor.
The closest-mode residual Frobenius norm is \(E_{F,i}=(\|Z_i\|_{\rm F}^2-\|Z_i\|^2)^{1/2}\), so it is independent of a choice inside a tied eigenspace.
The preceding uniform estimates, including residual divergence, imply
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P((\mathcal G_i^0)^c)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Shifted-formal-good-average}
\]
In fact their union probability tends to zero, but only this average is used.

After placing the exposed coordinates first, write the shifted parent as
\[
 L_N^\theta\simeq
 \begin{pmatrix}A_i^\theta&\mathcal B_i^T\\
                  \mathcal B_i&M_i^\theta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad A_i^\theta:=A_i+\theta_NI_2,
\]
and put
\[
 Z_i:=(M_i^\theta)^{-1},\qquad
 S_i:=A_i^\theta-\mathcal B_i^TZ_i\mathcal B_i,\qquad
 p_i:=\det S_i,\qquad
 \Gamma_i:=I_2+\mathcal B_i^TZ_i^2\mathcal B_i.
\]
The boundary \(\mathcal B_i\) is unchanged by the scalar shift.
Its norm is bounded by a fixed \(B_0\) for every allowed first row and every allowed second row, because every raw coordinate is bounded by \(L\) and the original normalization is \(N^{-1/2}\).
In the two-copy representation this bound holds uniformly over all remaining signs, including after the pairs have been fixed.
The formal fourth layer of \(p_i\) is exactly the inverse polynomial used above; the fixed second-input boundary and \(A_i^\theta\) affect only lower raw degrees.

The deterministic graph estimates in [C194v13](Yin_C_F0194_v13.md#pole-area) give
\[
 \sup_{\|\mathcal B_i\|\le B_0}\sqrt{\det\Gamma_i}
       \le1+B_0^2Q_i^2,
\]
and, on the separated-mode branch,
\[
 \sup_{\|\mathcal B_i\|\le B_0}\sqrt{\det\Gamma_i}
 \le C(1+Q_i)(1+\|E_i\|)
 \le C'Q_iE_{F,i}
\]
when \(Q_i,E_{F,i}\ge1\).
The first bound is at most a fixed constant times \(A_{Z_i}^2\) on the high-rank branch and times \(Q_i^2\) on the comparable branch.
Thus one fixed \(C_{\rm area}\) bounds the area by
\[
 a_i:=C_{\rm area}\mathfrak a(M_i^\theta)
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal G_i^0.
 \tag{Shifted-graph-scale}
\]
Define \(a_i:=1\) off that event to obtain a positive measurable scale everywhere.

For each index enlarge \(\mathcal F_i\) to \(\widehat{\mathcal F}_i\) by all its independent two-copy pairs.
Let
\[
 \mathcal G_i:=\mathcal G_i^0\cap\{H_i\ge2^{-5}\sigma_{4,i}^2\}.
\]
It is \(\widehat{\mathcal F}_i\)-measurable.
The weighted calculation proves
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
 \le\frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P((\mathcal G_i^0)^c)+C_L'v_N=o(1).
 \tag{Shifted-pair-good-average}
\]
On \(\mathcal G_i\), the independent remaining fair signs satisfy
\[
 \sup_\varepsilon\sqrt{\det\Gamma_i}\le a_i,\qquad
 \widehat\sigma_{4,i}\ge
 \kappa_0a_i/N^2,\qquad
 \widehat\tau_{4,i}\le t_N,
 \tag{Shifted-actual-graph-conditions}
\]
where
\[
 \kappa_0:=2^{-5/2}\sqrt{c_a}/C_{\rm area}>0,\qquad
 t_N:=\max\{1,32L^8\}v_N\longrightarrow0.
\]
In particular \(t_N\ge N^{-1/2}\).
The lower-degree coefficients may depend arbitrarily on the pairs and conditioned data.

Here is the exact use of the fixed bridge proof at this enlarged conditioning.
Set \(r_N:=\lfloor(16t_N)^{-1}\rfloor\), which tends to infinity.
The [conditional matching argument](Yin_C_F0194_v13.md#conditional-matching) only needs a real polynomial of independent fair signs and the displayed energy, load and support-uniform graph area.
It therefore gives, with fixed constants \(c_1,C>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{d_i\le c_1b_0^{-1}N^{-4},\
                    \|L_N^\theta\|\le b_0\}
 \le\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
     +C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/17}.
 \tag{Shifted-integrated-distance}
\]
Here \(b_0\ge1\) is a fixed parent norm guard and \(d_i\) is the least value of the two physical columns after projection orthogonal to all other columns.
The exact graph identity on the invertible-minor event is unchanged by the diagonal translation.
In particular the distance event implies \(|p_i|\le b_0d_i\sqrt{\det\Gamma_i}\); this is the same determinant-to-distance step of the fixed proof.
Conditioning does not include the parent norm event, which remains only inside the event being estimated.
The independent fair-sign small-ball estimate retains its accepted MNV16 source boundary in that fixed Claim.

The construction for different indices may use different enlarged probability spaces.
For each index it gives precisely the original parent marginal law after the pairs and signs are integrated.
Therefore (Shifted-integrated-distance) is an inequality for the original distance probability and can be averaged over indices.
No simultaneous good-pair event or union over the \(N\) pair representations is needed.

The [physical-block averaging argument](Yin_C_F0194_v13.md#block-averaging) applies to this original bounded first law.
Its rectangular input requires only a centered real variance-one off-diagonal law with finite second moment, arbitrary independent diagonals and an independent second input.
The deterministic scalar translation preserves these off-diagonal coordinates and does not change any rectangle between disjoint physical index sets.
Thus its exceptional rectangle probability is still \(Ce^{-cN}\).
It gives, for a sufficiently small fixed \(c_2>0\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{\|L_N^\theta\|\le b_0,\
               s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le c_2b_0^{-2}N^{-5}\}
 \le{}&Ce^{-cN}
 +\frac C N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)\\
 &+C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/17}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Shifted-matrix-reduction}
\]
This uses the conditional and averaging proof interfaces just checked, not an application of the fair-Wigner theorem statement to the original non-Rademacher law.
Adding the exponentially small parent norm exception and using (Shifted-pair-good-average) proves, for a fixed \(c_3>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le c_3N^{-5}\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Shifted-unconditional-floor}
\]
The retained-minor singular probability is exactly zero by construction.
There is no remaining bad-minor average in this conclusion.

<a id="return-to-original-model"></a>
### Returning to the original pencil and quadratic polynomial

For any two equally sized matrices, the variational definition of the least singular value gives
\[
 |s_{\min}(A)-s_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|.
\]
Here \(\|L_N^\theta-L_N\|=\theta_N<N^{-10}\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\), the event \(s_{\min}(L_N)<(c_3/2)N^{-5}\) therefore implies \(s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le c_3N^{-5}\).
The unshifted parent inherits the desired polynomial floor.

For completeness, swap the two auxiliary column blocks of the original \(L_N\) to obtain
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}I_N&X_N\\Y_N&I_N\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its Schur complement over the upper identity block is \(I_N-Y_NX_N=-Q_N\).
Thus \(L_N\) and \(Q_N\) are singular simultaneously.
On their invertible branch, the lower diagonal block of the inverse of the displayed matrix is \(-Q_N^{-1}\).
Compression cannot increase operator norm, and the column permutation is orthogonal, so
\[
 \|Q_N^{-1}\|\le\|L_N^{-1}\|,\qquad
 s_{\min}(Q_N)\ge s_{\min}(L_N).
\]
The least-value comparison also holds on the common singular branch, where both sides vanish.
This proves (Unconditional-bounded-floor) with a smaller fixed \(c\) if necessary.
No logarithmic comparison between a shifted and unshifted determinant is used.

<a id="original-logarithmic-conclusion"></a>
### Logarithmic limits of the original bounded model

The original unshifted input laws satisfy exactly the model of the reviewed [C187v9 theorem](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#statement).
The polynomial floor just proved supplies its additional hypothesis with exponent five.
Its intermediate estimate controls all but \(O(\log N)\) bottom singular values, with summable exceptions.
On the polynomial-floor event, those omitted values contribute at most \(O((\log N)^2/N)\) to the normalized negative logarithm.
The fixed theorem therefore gives the finite target \(d\), full-sequence convergence in probability for \(L_N\), and its almost-sure upper logarithmic bound under every allowed coupling.
The intermediate proof and its external inputs are exactly those of that fixed Claim.

The [marginal coupling criterion](Yin_C_F0190_v1.md#coupling-criterion) then gives the limsup equality.
Explicitly, for any coupling, convergence in probability permits a deterministic subsequence \(N_k\) on which the probability of a lower deviation by \(1/k\) is at most \(2^{-k}\).
The first Borel--Cantelli lemma gives \(\limsup_N(2N)^{-1}\log|\det L_N|\ge d\) almost surely, without cross-size independence.
Together with the established upper bound this is equality.

The [exact finite and free identities](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v5.md#model-and-determinants) give
\[
 \det L_N=\det Q_N,\qquad
 \log\Delta_\tau(q_\infty)=2\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell).
\]
The finite identity is also immediate from the column swap and Schur complement above: both sign factors are \((-1)^N\) and cancel.
The normalized finite logarithm for \(Q_N\) is therefore exactly twice that for \(L_N\), including singular outcomes under \(\log0=-\infty\).
The fixed free factorization supplies the same factor of two with the normalized auxiliary trace.
This proves both remaining displayed conclusions.

## Exact boundary of the conclusion

The theorem concerns these two bounded real input laws, this two-channel coefficient tuple and this fixed quadratic polynomial at the displayed determinant parameter.
It does not assume a positive one-step frame or an initially invertible physical minor.
The two-copy calculation does not extend the argument to unbounded finite-second-moment laws: its covariance and support-uniform graph controls use the fixed bound \(L\).
The polynomial-floor failure probability merely tends to zero and is not asserted to be summable.
The logarithmic conclusions are full-sequence convergence in probability and arbitrary-coupling almost-sure limsup equality, not full-sequence almost-sure convergence.
The original arbitrary-coefficient and finite-second-moment F0012--F0014 contracts, and the planar logarithmic/Brown-measure obligations, remain unresolved.

No new external theorem or source-proof inspection is claimed here.
The independent review retains the exact accepted-source boundaries stated in its finding.
