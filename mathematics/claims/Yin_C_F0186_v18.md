# Two-channel determinant limits with finite-variance diagonal laws

This theorem extends the [reviewed bounded two-channel theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#decision-bounded-two-channel-reviewed) to fixed real diagonal laws having only finite second moment.
The two off-diagonal laws remain bounded.
Small operator-norm diagonal perturbations transfer fixed-window spectral and joint-gap estimates, but do not preserve eigenvectors.
The all-mode auxiliary Gram estimate is rederived from the actual block equations.
The intermediate singular-value argument is used with a common vanishing norm exception rather than its former summable exception.
The almost-sure logarithmic upper bound comes separately from the complete finite-variance scalar regularized-potential theorem.
The [independent whole-chain finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v17.md#finite-diagonal-two-channel-whole-chain) supports its [proved adoption](#decision-finite-diagonal-two-channel-reviewed).

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

For \(j=1,2\), let \(W_j^{(N)}\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
Their independent upper off-diagonal entries have law \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where each \(\xi_j\) is fixed, real, centered, variance one and bounded by a common \(L<\infty\).
Their independent diagonal entries have law \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where each \(\zeta_j\) is fixed, real, centered and satisfies \(\mathbb E\zeta_j^2<\infty\).
No boundedness or moment above two is assumed for the diagonal laws, and zero diagonal laws are permitted.
All upper-triangular coordinates and the two inputs are independent within each size.
The coupling across different sizes is arbitrary.

Define
\[
 \alpha:=\sqrt2,\qquad
 X_N:=W_1^{(N)}+W_2^{(N)},\qquad
 Y_N:=W_1^{(N)}+\alpha W_2^{(N)},
\]
\[
 L_N:=\begin{pmatrix}X_N&I_N\\I_N&Y_N\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Q_N:=Y_NX_N-I_N.
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-model}
\]
There is a fixed \(c>0\), depending only on the prescribed laws, such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)<cN^{-5}
                 \text{ or }s_{\min}(Q_N)<cN^{-5}\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-floor}
\]
This is an unconditional all-dimension probability estimate, with no remaining singular-minor term.

Let \(s_1,s_2\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\), and put
\[
 \ell:=\begin{pmatrix}s_1+s_2&1\\1&s_1+\sqrt2s_2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad q_\infty:=(s_1+\sqrt2s_2)(s_1+s_2)-1,
 \qquad \tau_2:=\operatorname{tr}_2\otimes\tau.
\]
The ordinary FK logarithms are finite, with
\[
 d:=\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell)\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 \log\Delta_\tau(q_\infty)=2d.
\]
With \(\log0=-\infty\),
\[
 \frac1{2N}\log|\det L_N|\longrightarrow d,\qquad
 \frac1N\log|\det Q_N|\longrightarrow2d
 \quad\text{in probability along the full sequence},
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-log-probability}
\]
and under every allowed cross-size coupling,
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1{2N}\log|\det L_N|=d,\qquad
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|\det Q_N|=2d
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-log-limsup}
\]
No summable least-value failure, eventual fixed operator-norm bound, full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic convergence or planar Brown-measure conclusion is asserted.
The unrestricted [F0013 coefficient contract](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) and [F0014 polynomial contract](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) remain outside this restricted result.

## Complete argument

<a id="uniform-diagonal-perturbation"></a>
### One common small diagonal perturbation and norm event

Write
\[
 W_j^{0,(N)}:=W_j^{(N)}
             -\operatorname{diag}(W_j^{(N)}),\qquad
 h_N:=\max_{j=1,2;\ i\le N}|\zeta_{j,i}|/\sqrt N.
\]
For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), a union bound gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{h_N>\varepsilon\}
 &\le N\sum_{j=1}^2
           \mathbb P\{|\zeta_j|>\varepsilon\sqrt N\}\\
 &\le\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^2
       \mathbb E[\zeta_j^2\mathbf1_{\{|\zeta_j|>\varepsilon\sqrt N\}}]
 \longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Diagonal-smallness}
\]
The last limit is the finite-second-moment tail property.
Thus \(h_N=o_P(1)\), and its event controls every principal deletion simultaneously.

Let \(S\subset[N]\), \(n:=N-|S|\), and define
\[
 C_{N,S}:=W_1^{(N)}[S^c,S^c],\qquad
 D_{N,S}:=W_2^{(N)}[S^c,S^c],
\]
\[
 M_{N,S}:=
 \begin{pmatrix}C_{N,S}+D_{N,S}&I_n\\
 I_n&C_{N,S}+\alpha D_{N,S}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A superscript \(0\) denotes the same construction using the zero-diagonal inputs.
When a minor is fixed, suppress \((N,S)\) in \(C_{N,S}\) and \(D_{N,S}\).
For all \(S\) at once,
\[
 \|C_{N,S}-C_{N,S}^0\|\le h_N,\qquad
 \|D_{N,S}-D_{N,S}^0\|\le h_N,\qquad
 \|M_{N,S}-M_{N,S}^0\|\le(1+\alpha)h_N.
 \tag{All-minor-diagonal-difference}
\]
The original normalization is always \(\sqrt N\), including after a deletion.

Choose a fixed zero-diagonal norm constant \(B\) from C187v4.
Outside \(Ce^{-cN}\), both full zero-diagonal input norms are at most \(B\).
On the intersection with \(\{h_N\le1\}\), the actual full input norms are at most \(B+1\).
Their principal minors and all two-channel diagonal blocks and pencils therefore have one fixed norm bound \(\Lambda\), enlarged to allow an additional scalar translation of modulus at most one.
Put
\[
 p_N:=\mathbb P\{h_N>1\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The common norm event has failure at most \(p_N+Ce^{-cN}\).
This common event is used to bound probabilities of minor-norm failures; no distribution of a fresh row is conditioned on the full norm event.

<a id="finite-diagonal-atom-shift"></a>
### Atom avoidance for the actual parent and its minors

Apply the [countable-atom construction](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#deterministic-atom-avoiding-shift) to the actual finite-variance-diagonal eigenvalue laws, not to the zero-diagonal eigenvalue laws.
At each \(N\ge3\), the union of negative atoms of all ordered eigenvalue laws of \(M_{N,S}\), \(|S|\le2\), is countable.
Choose a deterministic \(\theta_N\in(0,N^{-10})\) outside that set.
Then
\[
 L_N^\theta:=L_N+\theta_NI_{2N},\qquad
 M_{N,S}^\theta:=M_{N,S}+\theta_NI_{2n}
 \tag{Actual-finite-diagonal-shift}
\]
are invertible almost surely for all these indices and sizes, under every allowed coupling.
The choice uses only the prescribed sizewise laws.
The fixed proof of atom avoidance has no boundedness premise.

Set
\[
 e_N:=(1+\alpha)h_N+\theta_N=o_P(1).
\]
Uniformly over all deletions under consideration,
\[
 \|M_{N,S}^\theta-M_{N,S}^0\|\le e_N.
 \tag{Shifted-diagonal-difference}
\]
The translated input blocks are \(C^\theta:=C+\theta_NI_n\) and \(D^\theta:=D\).
The resulting pencil's identity coupling is unchanged, and the input differences from \(C^0,D^0\) are at most \(h_N+\theta_N\).
Unlike the scalar shift alone, this total perturbation need not commute with the zero-diagonal matrix.

<a id="diagonal-mass-projector-transfer"></a>
### Weighted mass and physical projectors without commuting perturbations

Fix a sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), and choose a smooth function \(0\le f_\delta\le1\) that is one on \([-\delta/2,\delta/2]\) and vanishes outside \([-\delta,\delta]\).
Let \(\Pi_\ell\) be the projection onto auxiliary channel \(\ell\).
The zero-diagonal weighted mass input gives
\[
 n^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_\ell f_\delta(M_{N,S}^0)
 \ge c_0\delta
 \quad(\ell=1,2,\ |S|\le2)
\]
with probability tending to one, after reducing the fixed constant.
The bound is uniform over these deletions because the zero-diagonal theorem already supplies their common event.

The fixed smooth-functional estimate gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac1n|\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_\ell
       (f_\delta(M_{N,S}^\theta)-f_\delta(M_{N,S}^0))|
 &\le \frac{C_{f_\delta}}n
             \|M_{N,S}^\theta-M_{N,S}^0\|_1\\
 &\le2C_{f_\delta}e_N=o_P(1).
 \end{split}
 \tag{Perturbed-weighted-smooth-mass}
\]
Here trace norm is bounded by \(2n\) times operator norm.
No commutation with \(\Pi_\ell\) or weighted interlacing is required.
Because \(f_\delta\le\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}\), it follows that
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\Pi_\ell
      \mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}(M_{N,S}^\theta)
 \ge c_1\delta n
 \quad(\ell=1,2,\ |S|\le2)
 \tag{Perturbed-weighted-mass}
\]
outside a probability tending to zero for every fixed \(\delta\).

For the physical projector assertion restrict to \(|S|\le1\), and let \(P_i\) project onto retained physical coordinate \(i\) in both auxiliary channels.
The zero-diagonal physical-resolvent input gives
\[
 \max_{S,i}\|P_i(M_{N,S}^0-i\delta)^{-1}P_i\|\le C
\]
with probability tending to one.
Its constant is independent of sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\).
The resolvent identity and (Shifted-diagonal-difference) imply
\[
 \|P_i[(M_{N,S}^\theta-i\delta)^{-1}
             -(M_{N,S}^0-i\delta)^{-1}]P_i\|
 \le e_N/\delta^2.
\]
Spectral calculus for the actual matrix, followed by compression, therefore gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|P_i\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}(M_{N,S}^\theta)P_i\|
 &\le2\delta
       \|P_i\operatorname{Im}(M_{N,S}^\theta-i\delta)^{-1}P_i\|\\
 &\le2C\delta+2e_N/\delta.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Perturbed-physical-projector}
\]
For fixed \(\delta\), the event \(e_N\le\delta^2\) has probability tending to one.
Hence the right side is at most \(2(C+1)\delta\), with one constant valid for every sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\).
The limit order is fixed \(\delta\), then \(N\to\infty\), then \(\delta\downarrow0\).

In particular the mass bound gives a closest absolute eigenvalue tending to zero uniformly over \(|S|\le1\).
Every corresponding real unit eigenvector \(w=(u,v)\), including every choice in a repeated eigenspace, has
\[
 \eta_*(w):=\max_i(u_i^2+v_i^2)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{uniformly in probability}.
 \tag{Perturbed-closest-leverage}
\]
This follows by placing that eigenvector in the current low-energy spectral subspace and using the current physical projector bound.
No zero-diagonal eigenvector is transferred.

<a id="perturbed-joint-gap-and-gram"></a>
### Reestablishing the all-mode auxiliary Gram gap

For real symmetric \(n\)-by-\(n\) matrices \(A,B\), define
\[
 \Gamma_R(A,B):=
 \inf_{\substack{z\in\mathbb R^n,\ \|z\|=1\\a,b\in[-R,R]}}
 \max\{\|(A-aI)z\|,\|(B-bI)z\|\}.
\]
The triangle inequality implies
\[
 \Gamma_R(C^\theta,D^\theta)
 \ge\Gamma_R(C^0,D^0)-(h_N+\theta_N).
 \tag{Perturbed-joint-gap}
\]
For each fixed \(R\), the zero-diagonal joint-gap theorem supplies a fixed \(\epsilon_R^0>0\) uniformly for \(|S|\le1\) outside a probability tending to zero.
On \(h_N+\theta_N\le\epsilon_R^0/2\), the actual translated gap is at least \(\epsilon_R:=\epsilon_R^0/2\).

Here is the deterministic Gram argument at these new inputs.
Fix the common norm constant \(\Lambda\) above and put
\[
 L_*:=2\Lambda,\qquad b_*:=(1+L_*^2)^{-1/2}.
\]
For every real unit eigenpair
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}X^\theta&I\\I&Y^\theta\end{pmatrix}
       \binom uv=\lambda\binom uv,
 \qquad X^\theta:=C^\theta+D^\theta,\quad
        Y^\theta:=C^\theta+\alpha D^\theta,
\]
the block equations and \(|\lambda|\le\Lambda\) give \(\|u\|,\|v\|\ge b_*>0\).
Put
\[
 \Delta_w:=\|u\|^2\|v\|^2-\langle u,v\rangle^2,\qquad
 t:=\langle u,v\rangle/\|u\|^2,\qquad r:=v-tu.
\]
Then \(r\perp u\) and \(\|r\|^2=\Delta_w/\|u\|^2\).
If \(\Delta_w\le b_*^4/4\), then \(\|r\|\le b_*/2\) and \(b_*/2\le|t|\le1/b_*\).
Set \(x_0:=\lambda-t\) and \(y_0:=\lambda-t^{-1}\).
The exact block equations give
\[
 (X^\theta-x_0I)u=-r,\qquad
 (Y^\theta-y_0I)u=t^{-1}(\lambda I-Y^\theta)r.
\]
Using
\[
 C^\theta=(\alpha X^\theta-Y^\theta)/(\alpha-1),\qquad
 D^\theta=(Y^\theta-X^\theta)/(\alpha-1),
\]
the unit vector \(u/\|u\|\) has simultaneous input residuals at most \(K_*\|r\|\), where
\[
 K_*:=\frac{\alpha+2L_*/b_*}{(\alpha-1)b_*}.
\]
Their centers are
\[
 a_0:=(\alpha x_0-y_0)/(\alpha-1),\qquad
 b_0:=(y_0-x_0)/(\alpha-1),
\]
and lie in \([-R,R]\) for
\[
 R:=(\alpha+1)(\Lambda+2/b_*)/(\alpha-1).
\]
Choose the original joint-gap constant at this fixed \(R\), then use (Perturbed-joint-gap).
For any fixed
\[
 0<g<\min\{b_*^4/4,\ b_*^2\epsilon_R^2/(4K_*^2)\},
\]
the inequality \(\Delta_w<g\) would give both residuals smaller than \(\epsilon_R\), a contradiction.
The Gram matrix has trace one, so its smallest eigenvalue is at least its determinant.
Consequently
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\|u\|^2&\langle u,v\rangle\\
                 \langle u,v\rangle&\|v\|^2\end{pmatrix}\succeq gI_2
 \tag{Perturbed-all-mode-Gram}
\]
for every real unit eigenmode and every \(|S|\le1\), outside a probability tending to zero.
The constant order is \(\Lambda,b_*,R,\epsilon_R\), then \(g\), all before \(N\) increases.
This is the fixed C187v5 deterministic argument with verified perturbed premises, not an eigenvector-continuity assertion.


<a id="finite-diagonal-residual-and-fourth-layer"></a>
### Actual residual regularity and the complete fourth layer

Throughout this subsection restrict to \(|S|\le1\).
Write \(M:=M_{N,S}^\theta\), choose any closest-to-zero real unit eigenmode \(Mw=\lambda w\), and put
\[
 Z:=M^{-1},\quad q:=\lambda^{-1},\quad Q:=|q|=\|Z\|,
 \quad E:=Z-qww^T,\quad E_F:=\|E\|_{\rm F},\quad A:=\|Z\|_{\rm F}.
\]
These objects exist almost surely by the atom-avoiding construction.
For every matrix \(T\) on these physical coordinates define
\[
 d_i(T):=\|P_iT\|_{\rm F}^2,\qquad
 \mathcal D(T):=\sum_i\|P_iTP_i\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]

The [fixed residual proof](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#translated-residual-control) now applies to the actual \(M\), using the just established mass and projector bounds.
To identify all its premises, the norm event gives \(E_F^2\ge(2n-1)/\Lambda^2\).
For every fixed sufficiently small \(h>0\), (Perturbed-weighted-mass) gives at least \(chn\) eigenvalues in \([-h,h]\).
Deleting one eigenmode removes at most one from this count, and none of the remaining eigenvalues is zero.
Thus, even with the weaker denominator \(4h^2\),
\[
 E_F^2\ge(chn-1)/(4h^2),
 \qquad E_F^2/n\longrightarrow\infty
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-residual-divergence}
\]
uniformly in probability.
The order is first fixed \(h\), then \(N\to\infty\), then \(h\downarrow0\).

The residual commutes with the actual \(M\), even for an arbitrary vector in a repeated eigenspace.
Split it at the actual window \([-\delta,\delta]\).
The current projector bound and the high-energy inverse bound \(\delta^{-1}\) give, exactly as in the fixed spectral splitting,
\[
 d_i(E)\le C\delta E_F^2+2\delta^{-2},\qquad
 \mathcal D(E)\le C\delta E_F^2+4n\delta^{-2}.
\]
The basic size estimate, the divergence estimate and the fixed-\(\delta\) limit order imply
\[
 \max_i d_i(E)/E_F^2\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \mathcal D(E)/E_F^2\longrightarrow0
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-residual-spread}
\]
uniformly in probability.
These calculations use the actual matrix's spectral decomposition, not a difference of real inverses.

Since \(Ew=0\), the fixed exact identities give
\[
 A^2=Q^2+E_F^2,\qquad
 d_i(Z)=q^2\|P_iw\|^2+d_i(E),\qquad
 \mathcal D(Z)\le2\mathcal D(E)+2q^2\eta_*(w).
\]
Consequently
\[
 A^2/n\to\infty,\qquad
 \mathcal D(Z)/A^2\to0,\qquad
 d_*/A^2\to0,\qquad Q\to\infty,
 \qquad d_*:=\max_i d_i(Z),
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-full-inverse}
\]
uniformly in probability.
Here \(Q^2\ge A^2/(2n)\).
The actual inverse identities also give the visible-residual comparison from the fixed proof; no entry-distribution premise occurs in that calculation.

The closest eigenvalue tends to zero, so \(|\lambda|\le1\) uniformly outside a probability tending to zero.
The current norm and all-mode Gram constants, the current physical leverage, residual divergence and diagonal negligibility are precisely the hypotheses of the [deterministic cross-energy passage](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#translated-gap-gram-cross).
It gives a squared coefficient norm at least \(cE_F^2/N^4\) for that cross fourth layer, with one fixed \(c>0\).
The needed joint gap for the full-layer arguments is supplied by (Perturbed-joint-gap) at every required fixed center range.

For clarity, write
\[
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}G&H\\H^T&J\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T:=(H+H^T)/2,\qquad x_j:=\varepsilon_j/\sqrt N,
\]
\[
 p_4:=\Pi_4[(x^TGx)(x^TJx)-(x^TTx)^2],\qquad
 \sigma_4^2:=\sum_{|I|=4}|(p_4)_I|^2,
\]
\[
 \tau_4:=
 \frac{\max_j\sum_{I\ni j,\ |I|=4}|(p_4)_I|^2}{\sigma_4^2}
 \quad\text{when }\sigma_4>0.
\]
Here \(\Pi_4\) retains the degree-four multilinear Walsh layer after reducing squares of independent formal signs.
All physical coordinates still use the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.

Apply the [three deterministic coefficient estimates](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#translated-complete-fourth-layer) with the current fixed norm, joint-gap and Gram constants.
They choose new fixed thresholds \(r_h\ge2\) and \(\gamma\in(0,1/2]\); no preservation of old numerical thresholds is assumed.
Put
\[
 r:=A^2/Q^2,\qquad m:=s_2^\downarrow(Z)=\|E\|,\qquad
 \mathfrak a(M):=
 \begin{cases}
 A^2,&r\ge r_h,\\
 QE_F,&r<r_h,\ m\le\gamma Q,\\
 Q^2,&r<r_h,\ m>\gamma Q.
 \end{cases}
\]
The high-rank lower bound and physical load estimate use (Finite-diagonal-full-inverse).
The separated-mode lower bound uses the actual cross energy and the mixed-inner-product estimate; its load uses (Finite-diagonal-residual-spread) and (Perturbed-closest-leverage), with \(r<r_h\).
The comparable-mode raw estimate uses the current joint gap and \(Q\to\infty\); its repeated-index loss tends to zero by the same full-inverse physical controls.
Thus every deterministic premise of all three fixed estimates has been checked.
They give a fixed \(c_0>0\) such that, simultaneously for all \(|S|\le1\),
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge c_0\mathfrak a(M)^2/N^4,\qquad
 \max_{|S|\le1}\tau_4\longrightarrow0
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-fourth-layer}
\]
outside uniform bad events whose probabilities tend to zero.
The scale and the layer coefficients are functions of the actual minor.
A tied closest eigenspace causes no ambiguity in \(Q,E_F,m\), and the estimates hold for every closest-mode choice.

### The conditioned bounded row gives the unchanged matrix floor

Consider a single physical deletion \(i\).
Condition on the full second input, the retained first-input minor and the exposed first diagonal.
The unbounded exposed diagonals are now fixed.
The fresh first-input off-diagonal coordinates remain independent and bounded, as required by the [fixed two-copy proof](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#bounded-two-copy-fourth-layer).
The actual boundary matrix contains only off-diagonal row entries of the two inputs.
Its norm has a fixed support-uniform bound \(B_0\), including after conditioning on two-copy pairs.
The exposed diagonal values enter only the Schur polynomial's unrestricted lower-degree coefficients.

Choose a deterministic \(v_N\to0\), with \(v_N\ge N^{-1/2}\), slowly enough to control the formal loads in (Finite-diagonal-fourth-layer).
Let \(\mathcal G_i^0\) consist of actual minor invertibility, its fixed norm guard, \(Q_i,E_{F,i}\ge1\), the formal energy inequality and \(\tau_{4,i}\le v_N\).
These are minor-measurable conditions.
The preceding uniform estimates give
\[
 N^{-1}\sum_i\mathbb P((\mathcal G_i^0)^c)=o(1).
\]
The graph scales at the three branches are bounded by a common fixed multiple of \(\mathfrak a(M_i^\theta)\), by the deterministic area estimates retained in [C186v17](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#shifted-parent-graph-bridge).

The two-copy variance and load bounds give expanded-conditioning good events \(\mathcal G_i\) satisfying
\[
 N^{-1}\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
 \le N^{-1}\sum_i\mathbb P((\mathcal G_i^0)^c)+C_Lv_N=o(1).
\]
The conditional squared fourth coefficient norm is at least \(2^{-5}\) times its formal squared coefficient norm, and its relative load is at most \(32L^8v_N\).
These conclusions do not use a bound on the exposed diagonal.
The construction for each index has the original matrix marginal law after integration.
Only the displayed average is used, with no union over pair-good events.

The fixed conditional matching and physical averaging proof then gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\mathbb P\{\|L_N^\theta\|\le b_0,\
            s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le c b_0^{-2}N^{-5}\}\\
 &\qquad\le Ce^{-cN}
 +\frac C N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
 +C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/17},
 \qquad r_N:=\lfloor(16t_N)^{-1}\rfloor\to\infty,
 \end{split}
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-norm-guarded-floor}
\]
where \(t_N:=\max\{1,32L^8\}v_N\) and \(b_0\) is a fixed parent norm bound.
This is the exact conditioned-sign and averaging proof interface, not the previous bounded-diagonal theorem statement.
The averaging rectangles are unchanged by arbitrary diagonal entries and by the scalar shift.
Their fixed exceptional probability is exponential, and they only require the actual independent first off-diagonal law.
The graph-distance step retains the parent norm event inside its target event and never conditions the fresh signs on that event.

Add the single parent norm exception \(p_N+Ce^{-cN}\), which tends to zero.
The average good-event failure and the matching term also tend to zero.
Thus \(s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\ge c'N^{-5}\) with probability tending to one.
No multiplication of \(p_N\) by the number of indices occurs: minor good events have already been controlled by a common regularity event, and the matrix bridge averages their marginal failures.

The [least-value transfer and exact inverse-block comparison](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#return-to-original-model) use only \(\|L_N^\theta-L_N\|=\theta_N<N^{-10}\) and the identity-coupled block form.
They therefore give (Finite-diagonal-floor), with \(s_{\min}(Q_N)\ge s_{\min}(L_N)\), including their common singular branch.

### Intermediate singular values with a common nonsummable exception

The following probability estimate replaces the bounded-model summable theorem: for each fixed \(D>0\), there are fixed \(A_D,C_D,c_1>0\) such that eventually
\[
 \mathbb P\{\exists\,4\lceil A_D\log N\rceil\le j\le2N-1:
      s_{2N-j}(L_N)<c_1j/N\}
 \le p_N+C_DN^{-D}.
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-middle}
\]
Singular values in this display are listed in decreasing order.
Here is the precise change in the [C187v9 proof](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#argument).

For \(1\le k\le\delta N\), use its sets \(T_k:=\{N-k+1,\ldots,N\}\), \(V_i:=[N]\setminus\{i\}\), and \(U_{i,k}:=V_i\setminus T_k\), with \(i\notin T_k\).
The rectangles \(X_N[U_{i,k},T_k]\) and \(Y_N[U_{i,k},T_k]\) contain no diagonal entries.
They equal the rectangles of the zero-diagonal model.
The [thin-rectangle calculation](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#strong-tall-two-channel-rectangles) therefore supplies their simultaneous fixed lower bounds outside \(Ce^{-cN}\), for a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\).
It uses the bounded off-diagonal laws' second and fourth moments and a fixed net; no diagonal tail enters it.
The zero-diagonal full norm bound can be used for the rectangle net extension.

Let \(\mathcal H_N\) be the common actual full norm event from (Diagonal-smallness) and the zero-diagonal norm guard, so \(\mathbb P(\mathcal H_N^c)\le p_N+Ce^{-cN}\).
For a fixed \(i,k\), condition on both actual input principal minors on \(V_i\).
The condition that this retained pencil has norm at most the fixed bound and that its two indicated rectangles have the fixed lower bounds is measurable in these minors.
The common norm and rectangle events imply these conditions for every \(i,k\) simultaneously.

The [kernel-channel-balance argument](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#actual-kernel-channel-balance) is deterministic on those minor conditions.
Its equations
\[
 u_U+Y[U,V]v=0,\qquad v_U+X[U,V]u=0
\]
give a fixed positive fraction of squared norm in each channel for every vector in the retained kernel.
They make no bounded-entry assertion about the diagonals.

After deleting the current physical column, the projected current raw row is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{diag}(\xi_{1,\ell}+\xi_{2,\ell},
                    \xi_{1,\ell}+\alpha\xi_{2,\ell}),
 \qquad \ell\in V_i.
\]
Both current diagonal variables and the constant auxiliary identity entries have disappeared.
Conditional on the two principal minors, these original row variables are still independent and bounded.
The covariance, convex concentration, median estimate and real auxiliary-circle net in [C187v9](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#balanced-fresh-row-distance) therefore give its row-distance Gram bound outside conditional probability \(CN^2e^{-ck}\), uniformly on the stated minor conditions.
No full norm event is inserted into that conditioning.

Set \(k_{\min}:=\lceil A_D\log N\rceil\).
A union over the at most \(N^2\) choices of \(i,k\) costs \(CN^4e^{-ck_{\min}}\) only for these conditional concentration failures.
Failure of the common norm or rectangle events is added once.
The simultaneous Gram failure is consequently bounded by
\[
 p_N+Ce^{-cN}+CN^4e^{-ck_{\min}}
 \le p_N+C_DN^{-D}
\]
after choosing \(A_D\) large enough.
The exact [block negative-second-moment identity and index count](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#balanced-middle-index-count) are deterministic on this event and prove (Finite-diagonal-middle) with unchanged original normalization.
The \(p_N\) term is not claimed summable.

<a id="finite-diagonal-logarithmic-probability"></a>
### Probability convergence without an almost-sure middle estimate

The free pencil \(\ell\) is the same as in the zero-diagonal bounded model.
Its finite logarithm and the finite/free determinant identity are supplied by [C187v9](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#statement) and [L66v5](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v5.md#model-and-determinants).
This establishes the displayed finite targets \(d\) and \(2d\) without applying the bounded-diagonal probability theorem to the new model.

Let \(a_1\le\cdots\le a_{2N}\) be the actual singular values of \(L_N\), and choose
\[
 b_N:=4\lceil A_D\log N\rceil+2
\]
for one fixed \(D>1\).
On (Finite-diagonal-middle)'s good event, \(a_r\ge c_1(r-1)/N\) for all \(r\ge b_N\).
An elementary integral comparison, using \(\int_0^t|\log x|\,dx=t(1+|\log t|)\) for \(0<t<1\), gives for each sufficiently small fixed \(t>0\)
\[
 \frac1{2N}
 \sum_{\substack{r\ge b_N\\a_r<t}}(-\log a_r)
 \le C t(1+|\log t|)+C\frac{\log N}{N}.
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-middle-log}
\]
One obtains this bound by summing \(\log(N/(c_1(r-1)))\) only for \(r-1<Nt/c_1\).
Its failure probability tends to zero; no pathwise eventual statement is needed.

On the floor event, the remaining bottom contribution satisfies
\[
 \frac1{2N}
 \sum_{\substack{r<b_N\\a_r<t}}(-\log a_r)
 \le\frac{b_N}{2N}(5\log N+|\log c|)
 =O((\log N)^2/N).
\]
Together with the floor and middle failure bounds, this proves that for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
 \mathbb P\left\{\frac1{2N}
       \sum_{a_r<t}(-\log a_r)>\varepsilon\right\}=0.
 \tag{Finite-diagonal-negative-log-control}
\]

The actual model satisfies the full finite-second-moment hypotheses of [C186v4's Hermitized weak theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration).
Its matrix-polynomial scope gives the almost-sure weak singular law of \(L_N\), hence also convergence in probability.
For each fixed \(t>0\), choose a deterministic upper truncation \(R\) larger than both the fixed high-probability parent norm guard and \(\|\ell\|\).
The bounded continuous test \(x\mapsto\log\max(t,\min(x,R))\) then gives
\[
 \frac1{2N}\sum_{r=1}^{2N}\log\max(a_r,t)
 \longrightarrow
 d_t:=\int\log\max(x,t)\,d\nu_{|\ell|}(x)
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\]
The equality with the bounded test fails only on the parent norm event's \(o(1)\) complement.
This argument uses no eventual almost-sure norm bound.
Since \(d\) is finite, \(d_t\downarrow d\).
For \(t<1\), the difference between the truncated and untruncated normalized logarithms is nonnegative and bounded by the sum in (Finite-diagonal-negative-log-control).
Taking \(N\to\infty\) and then \(t\downarrow0\) proves the \(L_N\) probability limit.
The exact identity \(\det L_N=\det Q_N\), valid also on singular outcomes under \(\log0=-\infty\), proves the \(Q_N\) probability limit with the factor two.

<a id="finite-diagonal-almost-sure-upper"></a>
### The scalar finite-variance upper bound and arbitrary coupling

Use the fixed scalar polynomial
\[
 P(x,y):=(x+\sqrt2y)(x+y)-1.
\]
The entire model, including its unbounded diagonal laws, lies in the exact finite-second-moment scope of [C186v4's regularized potentials](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#regularized-potentials).
For every fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
 u_{N,\delta}:=\frac1{2N}
       \log\det(Q_N^*Q_N+\delta^2I_N)
 \longrightarrow
 u_\delta:=\frac12\tau\log(q_\infty^*q_\infty+\delta^2)
 \quad\text{almost surely}
\]
under every allowed cross-size coupling.
Pointwise, \(N^{-1}\log|\det Q_N|\le u_{N,\delta}\).
Intersect the probability-one events for a countable sequence \(\delta_k\downarrow0\).
Since the free logarithm is finite, monotone convergence of these decreasing regularized logarithms gives \(u_{\delta_k}\downarrow2d\).
Hence
\[
 \limsup_N N^{-1}\log|\det Q_N|\le2d
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
The finite determinant identity gives the corresponding upper bound \(d\) for \(L_N\).
No matrix-valued extension of the scalar regularized-potential statement is required.

The [arbitrary-coupling marginal criterion](Yin_C_F0190_v1.md#coupling-criterion), applied with the probability limits already proved and these almost-sure upper bounds, yields (Finite-diagonal-log-limsup).
Equivalently, for each coupling choose a deterministic subsequence on which lower-deviation probabilities at tolerance \(1/k\) are at most \(2^{-k}\), and apply first Borel--Cantelli.
Neither argument needs cross-size independence or an almost-sure middle estimate.

<a id="finite-diagonal-summability-boundary"></a>
## Probability boundary and remaining scope

The weaker norm-event probability is a real limitation of this proof interface.
For example, take the first diagonal law symmetric with \(\mathbb P\{|\zeta_1|>t\}=t^{-3}\) for \(t\ge1\), and take \(\zeta_2=0\).
This is a centered finite-variance model.
For every fixed \(A>0\) and all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\max_{i\le N}|\zeta_{1,i}|/\sqrt N>A\}
 =1-(1-A^{-3}N^{-3/2})^N
 \sim A^{-3}N^{-1/2}.
\]
These probabilities are not summable.
With independent sizes, the events occur infinitely often; the actual parent norm is at least this largest diagonal modulus.
Thus an eventual fixed operator-norm bound cannot be deduced from the present hypotheses.
This example does not refute the stated logarithmic conclusions or assert failure of every possible almost-sure middle theorem.

The two off-diagonal laws remain bounded throughout the coefficient, two-copy and intermediate row calculations.
Unbounded finite-second-moment off-diagonal laws, arbitrary complex laws, general auxiliary coefficients and general polynomial or planar parameters remain unresolved by this Claim.
The original Tasks retain their full contracts.
The [D10 transfer development](../drafts/Yin_D_F0010.md#finite-variance-diagonal-transfer) is provenance only; all necessary new arguments and exact fixed dependencies are included here.
No new external source or source-proof inspection is claimed.
