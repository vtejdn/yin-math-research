# A real parameter interval for two-channel quadratic determinants

This theorem extends the [finite-diagonal two-channel theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v18.md#decision-finite-diagonal-two-channel-reviewed) in both its real coefficient and its fixed real quadratic parameter.
The new free calculation gives an explicit interval, including negative quadratic parameters, rather than a continuity assertion about an unspecified neighborhood.
For the negative parameters the first noise coefficient is not the auxiliary identity.
Its actual fourth-degree Schur polynomial and every deterministic input conversion are checked below.
This is a project deduction, with no novelty claim or new original-source proof inspection.
The complete statement and assembly have received the [independent whole-chain review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v18.md#real-parameter-two-channel-whole-chain) selected below.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

Let \(W_1^{(N)},W_2^{(N)}\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices at each size.
Their strict upper entries are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where the two fixed real laws are centered, have variance one, and satisfy \(|\xi_j|\le L\) for some fixed finite \(L\).
Their diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where the fixed real laws are centered and have finite second moment.
All upper-triangular coordinates and the two inputs are independent within each size, and the coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
No boundedness or higher moment is assumed for the diagonal laws.

Fix \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\{1\}\), and put
\[
 c_\alpha:=\sqrt{2(1+\alpha^2)},\qquad \beta_\alpha:=1+\alpha,
 \qquad
 I_\alpha:=\left(-\frac{(c_\alpha-\beta_\alpha)^2}{c_\alpha},
                  \frac{(c_\alpha+\beta_\alpha)^2}{c_\alpha}\right).
 \tag{Real-parameter-interval}
\]
Fix \(t\in I_\alpha\), independently of dimension, and define
\[
 X_N:=W_1^{(N)}+W_2^{(N)},\qquad
 Y_N:=W_1^{(N)}+\alpha W_2^{(N)},\qquad
 Q_{N,t}:=Y_NX_N-tI_N.
\]
Let \(s_1,s_2\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\), and put
\[
 q_{\alpha,t}:=(s_1+\alpha s_2)(s_1+s_2)-t,
 \qquad u_{\alpha,t}:=\log\Delta_\tau(q_{\alpha,t}).
\]
Then \(u_{\alpha,t}\in\mathbb R\), and, with \(\log0=-\infty\),
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det Q_{N,t}|\longrightarrow u_{\alpha,t}
 \quad\text{in probability through all dimensions},
 \tag{Real-parameter-log-probability}
\]
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|\det Q_{N,t}|=u_{\alpha,t}
 \quad\text{almost surely under every allowed coupling}.
 \tag{Real-parameter-log-limsup}
\]

For \(t\ne0\), write \(\sigma:=\operatorname{sgn}t\), \(k:=\sqrt{|t|}>0\), and define the real symmetric pencil
\[
 M_{N,\sigma,k}:=
 \begin{pmatrix}X_N&kI_N\\kI_N&\sigma Y_N\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 m_{\alpha,\sigma,k}:=
 \begin{pmatrix}s_1+s_2&k\\k&\sigma(s_1+\alpha s_2)\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Signed-real-linearization}
\]
There is a fixed \(a>0\), depending on the prescribed laws and fixed parameters, such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(M_{N,\sigma,k})<aN^{-5}
                   \text{ or }s_{\min}(Q_{N,t})<aN^{-5}\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Real-parameter-floor}
\]
With \(\tau_2:=\operatorname{tr}_2\otimes\tau\),
\[
 \log\Delta_{\tau_2}(m_{\alpha,\sigma,k})=\tfrac12u_{\alpha,t},
\]
and the normalized pencil logarithms have the corresponding full-sequence probability limit and every-coupling almost-sure limsup.
No exponent-five least-value assertion is made here at \(t=0\).
All conclusions are for each fixed tuple; no uniformity as \(\alpha\to1\), \(t\to0\), or \(t\) approaches an endpoint is asserted.
No endpoint, exterior-parameter or nonreal-parameter finite-matrix approximation conclusion is included.
The arbitrary coefficients and laws in [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) and arbitrary polynomials and parameters in [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) remain unresolved by this restricted statement.

## Complete argument

<a id="signed-determinant-identities"></a>
### Exact signed finite and free identities

For \(t\ne0\), abbreviate \(M=M_{N,\sigma,k}\), and multiply on the right by the auxiliary-column swap \(P\).
Then
\[
 MP=\begin{pmatrix}kI&X_N\\\sigma Y_N&kI\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Eliminating with the invertible scalar block \(kI\) gives diagonal blocks \(kI\) and \(-\sigma Q_{N,t}/k\).
The two eliminating factors are unitriangular and \(\det P=(-1)^N\), so
\[
 \det M=\sigma^N\det Q_{N,t}.
 \tag{Signed-finite-determinant}
\]
The calculation does not invert \(X_N\) or \(Y_N\), and establishes simultaneous singularity.
On the invertible branch, the lower-right block of \((MP)^{-1}\) is \(-\sigma kQ_{N,t}^{-1}\), hence
\[
 s_{\min}(Q_{N,t})\ge k\,s_{\min}(M).
 \tag{Signed-inverse-compression}
\]
The same bounded block factorization in the free algebra has determinant-one unitriangular factors, a unitary swap, and cancelling scalar factors \(k\) and \(k^{-1}\).
The accepted bounded FK calculation from [L66v5](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v5.md#model-and-determinants), with these displayed scalar factors, gives
\[
 2\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(m_{\alpha,\sigma,k})
   =\log\Delta_\tau(q_{\alpha,t}),
 \tag{Signed-free-determinant}
\]
including zero determinants before finiteness is established.

<a id="explicit-free-channel-mass"></a>
### Explicit free channel mass for either sign

In this subsection fix any \(\alpha\ne1\), \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), and \(k>0\).
Write \(v:=1+\alpha^2\), \(c:=\sqrt{2v}\), \(b_\sigma:=\sigma(1+\alpha)\), and
\[
 A_1:=\operatorname{diag}(1,\sigma),\qquad
 A_2:=\operatorname{diag}(1,\sigma\alpha),\qquad
 J:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The free pencil is \(m=kJ+A_1s_1+A_2s_2\), with covariance map
\(\mathcal S(H)=A_1HA_1+A_2HA_2\).
Its Cauchy transform \(G(z):=(\operatorname{id}_2\otimes\tau)((z-m)^{-1})\) satisfies
\[
 G(z)^{-1}=zI-kJ-\mathcal S(G(z)).
 \tag{Signed-Dyson}
\]
This is the exact selfadjoint coefficient substitution in the fixed C119 identity.

Put
\[
 r_*:=\frac{\alpha-\sigma}{\alpha-1},\qquad
 s_*:=\frac{\sigma-1}{\alpha-1},\qquad
 \nu:=(r_*^2+s_*^2)^{-1}>0.
\]
Since \(r_*A_1+s_*A_2=I\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the two vectors \(V^{1/2}A_jz\) gives, for every positive matrix \(V\),
\[
 \mathcal S(V)\ge\nu V.
 \tag{Signed-covariance-domination}
\]
For \(V=-\operatorname{Im}G(z)>0\), the imaginary-part identity gives
\[
 V=G(z)^*(\operatorname{Im}z\,I+\mathcal S(V))G(z)
   \ge\nu G(z)^*VG(z).
\]
Thus \(\sqrt\nu V^{1/2}G(z)V^{-1/2}\) is a contraction, every eigenvalue of \(G(z)\) has modulus at most \(\nu^{-1/2}\), and
\[
 0<-\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{tr}_2G(z)\le\nu^{-1/2}.
\]
Integrating this Poisson bound over an interval and applying Fatou as in [L66v5](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v5.md#identity-direction-free-density) shows that the scalar free law has density at most \(1/(\pi\sqrt\nu)\).
Each channel measure has density at most \(2/(\pi\sqrt\nu)\), because their average is that scalar law.
In particular the ordinary free logarithm of \(m\) is finite for these fixed parameters, independently of the lower-mass condition below.

At \(z=i\eta\), uniqueness, transpose symmetry and the transformation \(G\mapsto-Q\overline GQ\), with \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), give
\[
 G(i\eta)=\begin{pmatrix}-ia_\eta&r_\eta\\r_\eta&-ib_\eta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad a_\eta,b_\eta>0,\quad r_\eta\in\mathbb R.
\]
The transformation preserves the Dyson equation because the two noise coefficients commute with \(Q\) and \(QJQ=-J\).
Direct inversion, with
\[
 D_\eta:=(\eta+2a_\eta)(\eta+vb_\eta)
                       +(k+b_\sigma r_\eta)^2,
\]
gives
\[
 a_\eta D_\eta=\eta+vb_\eta,\qquad
 b_\eta D_\eta=\eta+2a_\eta,\qquad
 r_\eta=-\frac{k}{D_\eta+b_\sigma}.
 \tag{Signed-positive-branch}
\]
The first two equalities imply
\[
 a_\eta(D_\eta^2-2v)=\eta(D_\eta+v)>0,
 \qquad D_\eta>c.
\]
Moreover \(c^2-b_\sigma^2=(\alpha-1)^2>0\), so \(D_\eta+b_\sigma\ge c+b_\sigma>0\).
The Poisson bound gives \(a_\eta+b_\eta\le2/\sqrt\nu\).
The displayed formula gives \(|r_\eta|\le k/(c-|b_\sigma|)\), and the definition of \(D_\eta\) then bounds it for \(0<\eta\le1\).
This uses the trace bound and explicit entries, not an operator-norm bound on \(G\) inferred from a nonunitary similarity.

Suppose
\[
 k^2<\frac{(c+b_\sigma)^2}{c}.
 \tag{Signed-bulk-condition}
\]
Consider any subsequential limit as \(\eta\downarrow0\).
If either limiting imaginary diagonal vanished, both would vanish by (Signed-positive-branch).
The determinant equation would then imply
\[
 k^2=f(D_0),\qquad f(D):=\frac{(D+b_\sigma)^2}{D},\qquad D_0\ge c.
\]
But \(f'(D)=1-b_\sigma^2/D^2>0\) for \(D\ge c\), contradicting (Signed-bulk-condition).
Both imaginary limits are therefore positive, and the first two equations force
\[
 D_0=c,\qquad \frac{a_0}{b_0}=\sqrt{v/2},\qquad
 r_0=-\frac{k}{c+b_\sigma},\qquad
 a_0b_0=\frac1c-\frac{k^2}{(c+b_\sigma)^2}>0.
 \tag{Signed-channel-limit}
\]
These conditions determine unique positive \(a_0,b_0\), proving full limits rather than merely subsequential positivity.

For either channel measure \(\mu_j\), the Poisson contribution from \(|x|>K\eta\) is at most \(4/(\pi\sqrt\nu K)\).
Choose a fixed \(K\) making this smaller than one quarter of \(\min(a_0,b_0)\).
For sufficiently small \(\eta\), the contribution from \([-K\eta,K\eta]\) is bounded below by a fixed positive constant, and the Poisson kernel there is at most \(1/\eta\).
Consequently there are fixed \(c_0,\delta_0>0\) such that
\[
 \mu_j([-\delta,\delta])\ge c_0\delta
 \quad(j=1,2,\ 0<\delta<\delta_0).
 \tag{Signed-channel-mass}
\]
For \(t>0\), condition (Signed-bulk-condition) is the upper inequality defining \(I_\alpha\); for \(t<0\), it is its lower inequality.
No continuity assertion in \(\alpha,k\) or at a real boundary point has entered the proof.

### Uniform physical regularity, finite diagonals and actual shifts

Fix from now on a nonzero \(t\in I_\alpha\), and normalize the parent by
\[
 \widetilde M_N:=k^{-1}M_{N,\sigma,k}
   =\begin{pmatrix}X_N/k&I_N\\I_N&\sigma Y_N/k\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(S\subset[N]\), let \(C_{N,S},D_{N,S}\) be the original input principal minors and let \(n=N-|S|\).
Let \(M_{N,S}\) be the principal pencil with diagonal blocks \(C_{N,S}+D_{N,S}\) and \(\sigma(C_{N,S}+\alpha D_{N,S})\), off-diagonal blocks \(kI_n\), and normalization \(\widetilde M_{N,S}:=M_{N,S}/k\).
Write \(\Pi_j\) for projection onto auxiliary channel \(j\), and \(P_i\) for projection onto retained physical coordinate \(i\) in both channels.
All retained entries still have normalization \(N^{-1/2}\).
Use a superscript zero for the corresponding inputs and pencils with their diagonals deleted.

The free mass and density bounds just proved also hold for \(m/k\), with changed fixed constants, by scaling spectral intervals by \(k\).
For the zero-diagonal random pencils, the weighted colored-moment proof in [C187v4](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#weighted-minor-mass) applies with the fixed selfadjoint matrices \(A_1/k,A_2/k\) and drift \(J\).
Indeed its insertion \(\Pi_j\) in the normalized word trace changes only the fixed auxiliary trace, while the same colored tree pairings give the operator-valued free limit.
The smooth-functional trace-norm estimate and independent row-block bounded differences likewise depend only on these fixed coefficient norms.
Thus, for every sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\), with probability tending to one simultaneously over \(|S|\le2\),
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\Pi_j\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}
                   (\widetilde M^0_{N,S})\ge c\delta n
 \quad(j=1,2).
 \tag{Parameterized-zero-mass}
\]
The fixed-window failure is bounded by \(C_\delta N^2e^{-c_\delta N}\) once the expected weighted convergence is accurate.

The covariance of the two raw fresh channel entries is
\[
 \Sigma_{\alpha,\sigma,k}:=k^{-2}
  \begin{pmatrix}2&\sigma(1+\alpha)\\
       \sigma(1+\alpha)&1+\alpha^2\end{pmatrix}>0,
 \qquad \det\Sigma_{\alpha,\sigma,k}=k^{-4}(\alpha-1)^2.
 \tag{Parameterized-row-covariance}
\]
For an auxiliary direction \(u\), its directional covariance is therefore bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \(\operatorname{diag}(|u_1|^2,|u_2|^2)\).
On (Parameterized-zero-mass), the conditional projected-row second moment is at least \(c\delta N\).
The norm of the projected raw row is a convex function of the original independent bounded coordinates with a fixed Lipschitz constant.
The same concentration, median and fixed-dimensional net calculation as in [C187v4](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#fresh-row-projector) gives the physical compressed resolvent bound
\[
 \max_{|S|\le1,\ i\notin S}
 \|P_i(\widetilde M^0_{N,S}-i\delta)^{-1}P_i\|\le C
 \tag{Parameterized-zero-resolvent}
\]
outside a probability tending to zero.
Here \(C\) is independent of the sufficiently small fixed window, although dimension thresholds may depend on it.
Deleting the physical column removes every mirrored occurrence of each fresh coordinate, so the required independence is unchanged.

Put \(h_N:=\max_{j,i}|\zeta_{j,i}|/\sqrt N\).
For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{h_N>\varepsilon\}
 \le\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^2
      \mathbb E[\zeta_j^2\mathbf1_{\{|\zeta_j|>\varepsilon\sqrt N\}}]
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Parameter-diagonal-smallness}
\]
The bounded zero-diagonal norm proof gives one fixed bound for both full zero-diagonal input norms outside \(Ce^{-cN}\).
On \(h_N\le1\), all actual parent and principal pencil norms are therefore bounded by one fixed \(\Lambda\), enlarged whenever necessary below.
This common norm event has failure at most \(p_N+Ce^{-cN}\), where \(p_N:=\mathbb P\{h_N>1\}\to0\).

At each size choose \(0<\theta_N<N^{-10}\) outside the countable union of negative eigenvalue atoms of the actual unnormalized parent and all its principal pencils with \(|S|\le2\).
The [atom argument](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#deterministic-atom-avoiding-shift) then makes all
\[
 M_{N,S}^\theta:=M_{N,S}+\theta_NI_{2n},\qquad
 \widetilde M_{N,S}^\theta:=k^{-1}M_{N,S}^\theta
\]
invertible almost surely, simultaneously over sizes under every allowed coupling.
In original input coordinates the shift is exactly
\[
 C_{N,S}^\theta=C_{N,S}+r_*\theta_NI_n,
 \qquad D_{N,S}^\theta=D_{N,S}+s_*\theta_NI_n.
 \tag{Signed-input-shift}
\]
The constants \(r_*,s_*\) are those defined in the free calculation.
They give \(C^\theta+D^\theta=X+\theta_NI\) and \(\sigma(C^\theta+\alpha D^\theta)=\sigma Y+\theta_NI\).
In particular, the negative-sign branch is not represented by shifting only the first input.

Uniformly over the required deletions,
\[
 \|\widetilde M_{N,S}^\theta-\widetilde M^0_{N,S}\|
 \le e_N:=k^{-1}\max\{2,1+|\alpha|\}h_N+\theta_N/k=o_P(1).
\]
The [noncommuting transfer calculation](Yin_C_F0186_v18.md#diagonal-mass-projector-transfer) now has exactly its verified premises.
For a fixed smooth lower-window function \(f_\delta\), its normalized weighted trace error is at most \(2C_{f_\delta}e_N\).
Thus the actual shifted weighted mass is at least \(c\delta n\) through two deletions, outside a probability tending to zero for each fixed window.
The resolvent identity gives compressed error at most \(e_N/\delta^2\), and spectral calculus gives
\[
 \max_{S,i}\|P_i\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}
          (\widetilde M_{N,S}^\theta)P_i\|
 \le2C\delta+2e_N/\delta\le2(C+1)\delta
 \tag{Parameter-physical-projector}
\]
on \(e_N\le\delta^2\), for \(|S|\le1\).
The limit order is fixed \(\delta\), then \(N\to\infty\), then \(\delta\downarrow0\).
Every actual closest eigenvalue tends to zero, and every unit vector in its eigenspace has vanishing maximal physical mass.
No zero-diagonal eigenvector is transferred.

<a id="virtual-input-gaps"></a>
### Actual gaps and the virtual inputs in the deterministic lemmas

For real symmetric \(A,B\), define
\[
 \Gamma_R(A,B):=
 \inf_{\substack{\|z\|=1,\ z\in\mathbb R^n\\a,b\in[-R,R]}}
       \max\{\|(A-aI)z\|,\|(B-bI)z\|\}.
\]
For every fixed \(R\), the original independent zero-diagonal inputs satisfy the [C187v5 gap](Yin_C_F0187_v5.md#joint-gap-proof) uniformly through one deletion outside a probability tending to zero.
The actual \(C^\theta,D^\theta\) differ in norm from those inputs by at most \(h_N+\max(|r_*|,|s_*|)\theta_N=o_P(1)\).
The triangle inequality therefore transfers a fixed positive gap at each such center range.

Suppress \((N,S)\), and set
\[
 X':=(C^\theta+D^\theta)/k,\qquad
 Y':=\sigma(C^\theta+\alpha D^\theta)/k.
\]
Their exact inverse conversion is
\[
 C^\theta=\frac{k(\alpha X'-\sigma Y')}{\alpha-1},\qquad
 D^\theta=\frac{k(\sigma Y'-X')}{\alpha-1}.
 \tag{Original-input-conversion}
\]
If \(B\) is the maximum absolute row sum of this two-by-two coefficient map, then for every fixed \(R\),
\[
 \Gamma_R(X',Y')\ge B^{-1}\Gamma_{BR}(C^\theta,D^\theta).
 \tag{Actual-channel-gap}
\]
This follows by mapping both proposed scalar centers and both residuals with the same coefficients.
All constants are finite and fixed because \(k>0\) and \(\alpha\ne1\).

To invoke the exact [C187v6 deterministic six-Gram statement](Yin_C_F0187_v6.md#actual-six-gram-coercivity), keep its numerical \(\alpha_0:=\sqrt2\) and define virtual matrices
\[
 C_*:=\frac{\alpha_0X'-Y'}{\alpha_0-1},\qquad
 D_*:=\frac{Y'-X'}{\alpha_0-1}.
 \tag{Virtual-six-Gram-inputs}
\]
They satisfy \(X'=C_*+D_*\) and \(Y'=C_*+\alpha_0D_*\), exactly as that deterministic lemma requires.
They need not be independent or Wigner matrices.
Their gap premise follows from
\[
 C^\theta=\frac{k[(\alpha-\sigma)C_*+(\alpha-\sigma\alpha_0)D_*]}{\alpha-1},
\]
\[
 D^\theta=\frac{k[(\sigma-1)C_*+(\sigma\alpha_0-1)D_*]}{\alpha-1}.
 \tag{Virtual-to-original-map}
\]
For the maximum absolute row sum \(B_*\) of this invertible map,
\(\Gamma_R(C_*,D_*)\ge B_*^{-1}\Gamma_{B_*R}(C^\theta,D^\theta)\).
In particular this supplies the lemma's exact center range \(2(\alpha_0+1)\Lambda/(\alpha_0-1)\).
The probabilistic independence input occurs only before these deterministic conversions, at the original zero-diagonal \(C,D\).

The actual all-mode auxiliary Gram estimate also follows from the [fixed deterministic conversion](Yin_C_F0187_v5.md#gram-gap-proof) with \(C_*,D_*\).
For clarity, the eigenmode equations are \(X'u+v=\lambda u\), \(u+Y'v=\lambda v\).
They bound both component norms away from zero on the fixed norm event.
Writing \(v=au+r\), \(r\perp u\), small Gram determinant bounds \(\|r\|\) and bounds \(|a|\) above and away from zero.
The exact residuals are
\[
 (X'-(\lambda-a)I)u=-r,\qquad
 (Y'-(\lambda-a^{-1})I)u=a^{-1}(\lambda I-Y')r.
\]
Their centers lie in a fixed interval, so (Actual-channel-gap), or the equivalent virtual-input conversion, contradicts sufficiently small Gram determinant.
Thus every actual real unit eigenmode has its two-by-two component Gram matrix bounded below by a fixed \(gI_2\), uniformly over the required minors.

### Residual controls and all three deterministic fourth-layer regimes

Throughout this subsection \(|S|\le1\).
Let \(\widetilde M=\widetilde M_{N,S}^\theta\), choose a real unit closest eigenmode \(w=(u,v)\), and write
\[
 Z:=\widetilde M^{-1}=qww^T+E
   =\begin{pmatrix}G&H\\H^T&J_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Ew=0,\quad Q:=|q|=\|Z\|,\quad E_F:=\|E\|_{\rm F},
\]
\[
 A_Z^2:=\|Z\|_{\rm F}^2=Q^2+E_F^2,\quad
 d_i(Z):=\|P_iZ\|_{\rm F}^2,\quad
 \mathcal D(Z):=\sum_i\|P_iZP_i\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
The actual weighted mass gives at least \(chn\) eigenvalues in a fixed small window of radius \(h\).
Deleting one mode and using invertibility gives
\(E_F^2\ge(chn-1)/(4h^2)\), so \(E_F^2/n\to\infty\) in the uniform probability sense used in [C186v18](Yin_C_F0186_v18.md#finite-diagonal-residual-and-fourth-layer).
Its actual spectral splitting, now justified by (Parameter-physical-projector), gives
\[
 d_i(E)\le C\delta E_F^2+2\delta^{-2},\qquad
 \mathcal D(E)\le C\delta E_F^2+4n\delta^{-2}.
\]
The fixed-window limit order, closest-mode leverage and spectral orthogonality consequently give
\[
 \frac{A_Z^2}{n}\to\infty,\quad Q\to\infty,\quad
 \frac{\mathcal D(Z)}{A_Z^2}\to0,\quad
 \frac{\max_i d_i(Z)}{A_Z^2}\to0,
 \tag{Parameter-full-inverse-controls}
\]
uniformly over \(|S|\le1\), together with the corresponding residual row and diagonal controls.
These calculations allow repeated closest eigenvalues and require no original unshifted inverse.

For the formal probe \(x_i=\varepsilon_i/\sqrt N\), put
\[
 p_Z(x):=(x^TGx)(x^TJ_0x)-(x^TT_0x)^2,
 \qquad T_0:=(H+H^T)/2.
\]
Let \(p_4:=\Pi_4p_Z\), let \(\sigma_4^2\) be its squared fourth Walsh coefficient norm, and let \(\tau_4\) be its maximum coordinate load divided by \(\sigma_4^2\).
