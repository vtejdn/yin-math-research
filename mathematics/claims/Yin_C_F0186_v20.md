# Finite-variance two-channel quadratic determinants

This theorem removes the off-diagonal boundedness assumption from the fixed two-channel case of [C186v17](Yin_C_F0186_v17.md#statement).
It uses the original finite-second-moment laws in every dimension.
One deterministic whole-parent translation removes singular minors, and all inverse-mode regimes then enter the finite-variance graph bridge at the same actual matrices.
The high-rank input is [accepted in C187v14](Yin_C_F0187_v14.md#decision-finite-variance-high-rank-reviewed), and the shifted low-rank input is [accepted in C187v15](Yin_C_F0187_v15.md#decision-shifted-finite-variance-low-rank-reviewed).
The complete new assembly has received [independent whole-chain review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v19.md#finite-variance-two-channel-whole-chain), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-finite-variance-two-channel-reviewed).
This is ordinary collaborative proof development for Yin, with no novelty claim or new original-source proof inspection.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact original model and conclusions

Let \(C_N,D_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices at each size.
The strict upper entries of input \(j\) are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where each \(\xi_j\) is a fixed real centered law of variance one.
Its diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where each \(\zeta_j\) is a fixed real centered law with finite second moment.
All upper-triangular coordinates and both inputs are independent within each size.
The coupling across sizes is arbitrary, and no boundedness, density or moment above two is assumed.
Set
\[
 \alpha:=\sqrt2,\qquad X_N:=C_N+D_N,\qquad
 Y_N:=C_N+\alpha D_N,\qquad
 L_N:=\begin{pmatrix}X_N&I_N\\I_N&Y_N\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Q_N:=Y_NX_N-I_N.
\]
Let \(s_1,s_2\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\), and put
\[
 x:=s_1+s_2,\qquad y:=s_1+\sqrt2s_2,\qquad
 \ell:=\begin{pmatrix}x&1\\1&y\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 q:=yx-1,\qquad \tau_2:=\operatorname{tr}_2\otimes\tau.
\]
The conclusions are as follows.
There is a fixed \(a>0\), depending on the two prescribed input laws, such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)<aN^{-6}
                  \ \text{or}\ s_{\min}(Q_N)<aN^{-6}\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Finite-variance-unconditional-floor}
\]
The free targets are finite and satisfy
\[
 u:=\log\Delta_\tau(q)
       =2\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell)\in\mathbb R.
 \tag{Finite-variance-free-target}
\]
With \(\log0=-\infty\), through all dimensions,
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det Q_N|\longrightarrow u,\qquad
 \frac1{2N}\log|\det L_N|\longrightarrow u/2
 \quad\text{in probability}.
 \tag{Finite-variance-log-probability}
\]
For every allowed cross-size coupling,
\[
 \limsup_N\frac1N\log|\det Q_N|=u,\qquad
 \limsup_N\frac1{2N}\log|\det L_N|=u/2
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Finite-variance-log-limsup}
\]
The statement fixes the coefficient \(\alpha=\sqrt2\) and quadratic parameter one.
It does not assert a full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic limit, arbitrary real parameters, general matrix coefficients, complex entry laws or Brown-measure convergence.
It therefore concerns a restricted instance of [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) and [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract), not completion of either Task or of [F0012](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract).

## Complete proof

<a id="one-whole-parent-shift"></a>
### One deterministic translation for every actual minor

For \(S\subset[N]\), \(|S|\le2\), let \(M^0_{N,S}\) be the physical principal minor of \(L_N\), with original normalization \(N^{-1/2}\).
At each size the finitely many ordered eigenvalue laws of these matrices have only countably many atoms.
Choose one deterministic
\[
 0<\theta_N<N^{-10}
\]
outside the union of their negative atom sets.
Then \(M^\theta_{N,S}:=M^0_{N,S}+\theta_NI\) is invertible almost surely, simultaneously over all these minors and, by a countable union, all dimensions \(N\ge3\).
The choice depends only on one-size marginal laws and works for every prescribed coupling.
In particular \(L_N^\theta=L_N+\theta_NI\) and all its actual single-deletion minors use this same shift.

The shifted parent is exactly
\[
 L_N^\theta
 =\begin{pmatrix}(C_N+\theta_NI)+D_N&I\\
                  I&(C_N+\theta_NI)+\alpha D_N\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus C194v14 applies with first input \(C_N+\theta_NI\), second input \(D_N\), and \(K=1\).
Adding a deterministic number to the independent first diagonals is allowed by that bridge's arbitrary-diagonal hypothesis.
It changes no first off-diagonal coordinate, second border or crossing rectangle.
C187v10 is used through its explicit deterministic-shift statement, not by replacing the original fixed diagonal laws with dimension-dependent laws in an unshifted theorem.

For every invertible full or single-deletion minor, abbreviate
\[
 Z=(M^\theta_{N,S})^{-1},\qquad
 A=\|Z\|_{\rm F},\quad Q=\|Z\|,\quad m=s_2^\downarrow(Z),\quad
 E_F=(A^2-Q^2)^{1/2},\quad r=A^2/Q^2.
\]
Removing any closest-to-zero eigenmode gives a residual of Frobenius norm \(E_F\) and operator norm \(m\), also in the presence of ties.
These four norms and \(r\) are minor-measurable and require no eigenvector choice.
The accepted shifted physical theorem gives uniformly
\[
 A^2/n\to\infty,\qquad E_F^2/n\to\infty,\qquad
 \frac{d_*(Z)}{A^2}\to0,\qquad
 \frac{\mathcal D(Z)}{A^2}\to0,
 \tag{Shifted-physical-inputs}
\]
almost surely under each coupling, where \(n=N-|S|\),
\(d_*(Z)=\max_j\|P_jZ\|_{\rm F}^2\) and
\(\mathcal D(Z)=\sum_j\|P_jZP_j\|_{\rm F}^2\).
Here \(P_j\) is the rank-two projection onto the two auxiliary coordinates at physical index \(j\).
Also
\[
 m^2\ge E_F^2/(2n-1)\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad Q\ge m.
 \tag{Shifted-two-mode-size}
\]
These conclusions remove any eventual size exception \(Q<1\) or \(m<1\) with probability tending to zero.

<a id="shifted-high-rank-coefficients"></a>
### The accepted approximate-mode event supplies shifted high rank

Take the constants \(\rho_0,g_0\) of C187v14's approximate-mode assertion at \(K_0=1\), and fix
\[
 0<\rho\le\min(\rho_0/2,\sqrt{g_0}/2).
\]
On its common event of probability tending to one, every unit vector \(w=(u,v)\) in
\(\operatorname{Ran}1_{[-\rho,\rho]}(M^\theta_{N,S})\) satisfies
\[
 \|(M^0_{N,S}-(-\theta_N)I)w\|
   =\|M^\theta_{N,S}w\|\le\rho\le\rho_0.
\]
Eventually \(|\theta_N|\le1\), so the unshifted approximate-mode assertion gives
\(\det\operatorname{Gram}(u,v)\ge g_0\) for every vector in this shifted low subspace, simultaneously over all required minors.
This uses the theorem's uniformity in the approximate eigenvalue \(\lambda\).
A statement only about individual exact eigenvectors would not suffice.

Apply C187v14's deterministic low-subspace lemma to
\[
 M^\theta=\begin{pmatrix}X+\theta_NI&I\\I&Y+\theta_NI\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Z_\rho=(M^\theta)^{-1}1_{[-\rho,\rho]}(M^\theta).
\]
Its hypotheses are real symmetry, identity coupling, the whole-subspace Gram bound and \(\rho<\sqrt{g_0}\).
It assumes no bound on the input operator norms.
Spectral calculus gives
\[
 \|Z-Z_\rho\|\le\rho^{-1},\qquad
 \|Z-Z_\rho\|_{\rm F}\le\sqrt{2n}/\rho=o(A).
\]
Consequently \(\|Z_\rho\|_{\rm F}/A\to1\) and
\(\|Z_\rho\|_{\rm F}^2/n\to\infty\) uniformly.
The fixed lemma's projection error tends to zero and gives
\[
 \|\mathscr Q(Z_\rho)\|_{\rm F}\ge d_0\|Z_\rho\|_{\rm F}^2
\]
with fixed \(d_0>0\), where
\[
 \mathscr Q(Z)=(G\otimes J+J\otimes G)/2-H_s\otimes H_s,\qquad
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}G&H\\H^T&J\end{pmatrix},\quad H_s=(H+H^T)/2.
\]
Bilinearity and Frobenius contraction of block extraction give
\[
 \|\mathscr Q(Z)-\mathscr Q(Z_\rho)\|_{\rm F}
 \le C(A+\|Z_\rho\|_{\rm F})\|Z-Z_\rho\|_{\rm F}=o(A^2).
\]
Hence \(\|\mathscr Q(Z)\|_{\rm F}\ge dA^2\) for a fixed \(d>0\), outside a uniform error tending to zero.

Let \(h_Z(x)=(x^TGx)(x^TJx)-(x^TH_sx)^2\), and write
\[
 \Pi_4 h_Z(\varepsilon/\sqrt N)
   =\sum_{|I|=4}c_I\varepsilon_I,\qquad
 \sigma_4^2=\sum_Ic_I^2,\qquad
 \tau_4=\frac{\max_j\sum_{I\ni j}c_I^2}{\sigma_4^2}.
\]
Here \(\Pi_4\) means substituting formal signs, reducing their squares to one, and retaining exactly four distinct coordinates.
It is a coefficient functional; the original first law need not be Rademacher.
As in the fixed deterministic high-rank calculation, deleting the physical block diagonals changes \(\mathscr Q(Z)\) by at most
\(4A\sqrt{\mathcal D(Z)}=o(A^2)\).
The four-cycle error and complete-layer coordinate-load formulas therefore yield fixed \(c_1,C>0\) such that
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge N^{-4}(c_1A^4-CQ^2A^2),\qquad
 \sum_{I\ni j}c_I^2\le CA^2\|P_jZ\|_{\rm F}^2/N^4.
 \tag{Shifted-high-rank-algebra}
\]
Choose a fixed \(K\ge1\) sufficiently large that \(C/K\le c_1/2\).
On \(r\ge K\), these imply
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge(c_1/2)A^4/N^4,\qquad
 \tau_4\le C' d_*(Z)/A^2\longrightarrow0
 \tag{Shifted-high-rank-conclusion}
\]
uniformly in probability.
All identities concern the actual shifted inverse.
No comparison of \(M^{-1}\) with \((M+\theta I)^{-1}\), unshifted invertibility assertion, fixed input norm or additional minor union is used.

<a id="all-regime-formal-coefficients"></a>
### Combining the two low-rank branches with high rank

Fix the preceding \(K\), before applying the low-rank input.
C187v15's stated shifted conclusion applies to this deterministic \(\theta_N\), the original input laws and every full or single-deletion minor.
At \(r\le K\), it supplies a fixed \(\gamma\in(0,1/2]\), \(c_K>0\), and uniform in-probability bounds
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge\frac{c_K}{N^4}
 \begin{cases}
 Q^2E_F^2,&m\le\gamma Q,\\
 Q^4,&m>\gamma Q,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \tau_4\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Shifted-low-rank-input}
\]
This is the separately proved low-rank input; physical spread alone does not supply it.
Its assertions are unconditional bad-event bounds intersected with the invertible low-rank branch, so no division by a branch probability occurs.
The thresholds \(r\ge K\), \(r<K,m\le\gamma Q\), and \(r<K,m>\gamma Q\) exhaust all actual inverses.
The original \(N^{-2}\) fourth-coefficient normalization is identical in the two inputs.

Since \(A^2\ge Qm\), \(QE_F\ge Qm\), and \(Q^2\ge Qm\), there is one fixed \(c_2>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\exists i:
       \sigma_{4,i}<c_2Q_im_i/N^2\right\}\to0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P\left\{\max_i\tau_{4,i}>\varepsilon\right\}\to0
 \quad(\varepsilon>0).
 \tag{All-regime-formal-input}
\]
Here \(i\) ranges over the actual single-deletion minors, and \(\tau_{4,i}=+\infty\) when \(\sigma_{4,i}=0\).
Null singular branches may be assigned arbitrary values.
Tied closest modes do not affect \(Q_i,m_i,E_{F,i}\), the branch tests or the complete formal coefficients.

Choose a deterministic \(t_N\to0\), with \(t_N\ge N^{-1/2}\), slowly enough that the second probability in (All-regime-formal-input) also tends to zero when \(\varepsilon=t_N\).
For example choose successive dimension thresholds at tolerances \(1/k\) with failure at most \(1/k\), and enlarge the resulting decreasing step sequence by \(N^{-1/2}\).
This is a qualitative choice; no rate for \(t_N\) is needed.

<a id="actual-second-border-and-conditioning"></a>
### Actual second borders and measurable graph events

For each \(i\), condition on the whole \(D_N\), the retained principal minor of \(C_N+\theta_NI_N\), and its exposed diagonal.
Call this sigma-field \(\mathcal F_i\).
The fresh first border \(x\) has its original independent-coordinate law \(\xi_1/\sqrt N\), independent of \(\mathcal F_i\).
The actual second border \(y_i\) and the shifted corner \(A_i^\theta\) are \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable.
After putting the exposed coordinates first, the boundary has columns
\[
 \mathcal B_i(x)
   =\bigl[(x+y_i,0)^T,\ (0,x+\alpha y_i)^T\bigr],
\]
and the Schur determinant is
\[
 p_i(x)=\det(A_i^\theta-\mathcal B_i(x)^TZ_i\mathcal B_i(x)).
\]
Its four-distinct-coordinate coefficients are exactly those of \(h_{Z_i}\): the corner and the fixed second border contribute only lower raw degrees.
Thus the formal coefficients in (All-regime-formal-input) are the ones required by C194v14.

For every fixed \(i\), the weak law gives \(\|y_i\|^2\to1\) in probability.
The identical within-size row laws imply
\[
 \overline e_N^{(2)}:=\frac1N\sum_i
           \mathbb P\{\|y_i\|>2\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Actual-second-row-average}
\]
No uniform bound over all actual second rows is asserted.
When \(\|y_i\|,\|x\|\le2\), the orthogonal auxiliary supports give
\[
 \|\mathcal B_i(x)\|\le B_0:=2(1+\sqrt2).
\]
For any real symmetric \(Z_i\) with largest singular values \(Q_i,m_i\),
\[
 \sup_{\|R\|\le B_0}\sqrt{\det(I_2+R^TZ_i^2R)}
   =a_i:=\sqrt{(1+B_0^2Q_i^2)(1+B_0^2m_i^2)}.
 \tag{Exact-two-mode-area}
\]
Indeed the largest singular value of \(Z_iR\) is at most \(B_0Q_i\).
A unit direction in the two-dimensional domain can be chosen with \(R\) of that direction perpendicular to a top eigenvector of \(Z_i^2\); its image under \(Z_iR\) has norm at most \(B_0m_i\), bounding the smaller singular value.
Taking the columns of \(R\) to be \(B_0\) times two corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors attains both bounds.
On \(Q_i,m_i\ge1\), \(a_i\le(1+B_0^2)Q_im_i\).

Define \(\mathcal G_i\) by invertibility, \(Q_i,m_i\ge1\),
\(\sigma_{4,i}\ge c_2Q_im_i/N^2\), \(\tau_{4,i}\le t_N\), and \(\|y_i\|\le2\).
All its defining numerical quantities are \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable.
Define \(a_i=1\) on a singular minor to make it positive everywhere.
The preceding uniform coefficient and size estimates and the averaged second-row estimate give
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Actual-good-conditioning-average}
\]
On \(\mathcal G_i\),
\[
 \sup_{\|x\|\le2}\sqrt{\det(I_2+\mathcal B_i(x)^TZ_i^2\mathcal B_i(x))}
 \le a_i,\qquad
 \sigma_{4,i}\ge\frac{\kappa a_i}{N^2},\qquad
 \tau_{4,i}\le t_N,\quad
 \kappa:=c_2/(1+B_0^2)>0.
 \tag{Actual-formal-graph-hypotheses}
\]
The global events used to prove these numerical bounds need not be \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable and are not put into \(\mathcal G_i\).
Neither the parent norm event nor an event involving the fresh first border is included in the conditioning.
The second border may be dependent on its retained minor; no independence of those two objects has been used.

<a id="finite-variance-matrix-floor"></a>
### A polynomial parent norm and the unconditional matrix floor

Fix \(C_0>5\).
The accepted Frobenius weak law gives probability tending to one to
\[
 \Omega_N=\{\|X_N\|_{\rm F}^2+\|Y_N\|_{\rm F}^2\le C_0N\}.
\]
Choose a fixed \(B>\sqrt{C_0+2}\).
On \(\Omega_N\), eventually
\[
 \|L_N^\theta\|\le\|L_N^\theta\|_{\rm F}
 \le\sqrt{(C_0+2)N}+\sqrt{2N}\theta_N\le B\sqrt N.
 \tag{Shifted-parent-norm}
\]
Use C194v14 with \(b_N=B\sqrt N\), \(\kappa\) and the actual \(\mathcal G_i\).
Let \(r_N=\lfloor(16t_N)^{-1}\rfloor\), and let \(\epsilon_N^{\rm pair}\) be its two-copy squared-row weak-law error.
Then \(r_N\to\infty\), \(\epsilon_N^{\rm pair}\to0\), and its exact reduction gives, with fixed constants,
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\mathbb P\{\|L_N^\theta\|\le B\sqrt N,\
               s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le cB^{-2}N^{-6}\}\\
 &\quad\le Ce^{-c'N}+\frac C N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
       +C\epsilon_N^{\rm pair}+Ce^{-c'r_N}
       +C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/17}\longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Shifted-matrix-floor}
\]
The bridge's distance scale is \(b_N^{-1}N^{-4}\), and physical averaging contributes the second factor \(b_N^{-1}N^{-1}\); hence the exponent is six at this norm guard.
Its original first-row two-copy representation is performed separately for each physical index and integrated before averaging.
There is no simultaneous pair-support event or union loss multiplying the pair and matching errors by \(N\).
The norm guard remains inside the estimated probability, and its common \(o(1)\) failure is added once.
The atom-avoiding shift leaves no singular-minor remainder.

Absorb \(B^{-2}\) into the fixed constant.
Since
\[
 |s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)-s_{\min}(L_N)|\le\theta_N
       <N^{-10}=o(N^{-6}),
\]
the original \(L_N\) has the same floor with a smaller fixed positive constant.
This uses the original single shift; no further translation is selected after conditioning.

<a id="quadratic-and-free-identities"></a>
### Returning to the quadratic matrix and the free target

Let \(P\) swap the two auxiliary column blocks.
Then
\[
 L_NP=\begin{pmatrix}I_N&X_N\\Y_N&I_N\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Unitriangular elimination gives diagonal blocks \(I_N\) and \(I_N-Y_NX_N=-Q_N\).
Including the swap determinant proves \(\det L_N=\det Q_N\), and the two matrices are singular simultaneously.
On their invertible branch the lower-right inverse block of \(L_NP\) is \(-Q_N^{-1}\).
Compression and orthogonal invariance of the norm therefore give
\[
 \|Q_N^{-1}\|\le\|L_N^{-1}\|,\qquad
 s_{\min}(Q_N)\ge s_{\min}(L_N).
\]
The latter inequality also holds on the common singular branch.
This proves the joint asserted floor.

The same bounded block factorization in the free algebra, with bounded unitriangular factors of determinant one and the unitary swap, gives
\[
 \log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell)=\tfrac12\log\Delta_\tau(q).
\]
This uses the fixed bounded FK interface, including zero determinants, and no affiliated inverse.
The free logarithm of \(\ell\) is finite by C187v11's small-singular-mass bound; consequently the scalar free target is finite as well.

<a id="finite-variance-logarithmic-conclusions"></a>
### Probability logarithms and every-coupling limsup

Apply C187v11's middle and logarithmic theorem directly to the original unshifted \(L_N\), using the floor just derived with exponent six.
It bounds the middle values by \(c(j/N)^{3/2}\) beyond \(O(\sqrt{N\log N})\) bottom indices, outside one common \(o(1)\) exception and an arbitrary inverse power.
Its middle negative-logarithmic tail is at most \(Ct^{2/3}(1+|\log t|)\).
The omitted indices contribute at most
\[
 O\bigl(N^{-1/2}(\log N)^{3/2}\bigr)\longrightarrow0
\]
on the polynomial-floor event.
Positive logarithmic tails are controlled by the same Frobenius event, since their average is bounded by
\[
 \frac{C_0+2}{2}\sup_{x>T}\frac{\log x}{x^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The accepted weak law applied to bounded doubly clipped logarithms, followed by these tail limits, proves
\((2N)^{-1}\log|\det L_N|\to\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell)\) in probability.
The exact determinant equality proves the scalar probability limit with factor two.
No shifted-to-unshifted logarithmic perturbation estimate is used.

For the almost-sure upper bound use the exact scalar polynomial
\(P(x,y)=(x+\sqrt2y)(x+y)-1\) at parameter zero.
For every fixed \(\delta>0\), C186v4 gives, under every allowed coupling,
\[
 \frac1{2N}\log\det(Q_N^*Q_N+\delta^2I)
 \longrightarrow\frac12\tau\log(q^*q+\delta^2)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
The raw scalar logarithm is bounded above by this regularization.
Intersecting countably many events along \(\delta\downarrow0\) and using the finite free target gives
\[
 \limsup_N N^{-1}\log|\det Q_N|\le u
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Probability convergence supplies a deterministic subsequence \(N_k\) whose lower-deviation probabilities at tolerance \(1/k\) are at most \(2^{-k}\).
First Borel--Cantelli on every prescribed coupling gives the opposite lower limsup, exactly the sufficiency direction of C190v1.
The determinant equality transfers it to the pencil.
None of the common physical, coefficient, row, Frobenius or floor exceptions has been asserted summable.
