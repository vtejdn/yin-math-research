# Positive-frame spectral-point determinants under finite variance

This theorem proves a polynomial least-singular-value floor and logarithmic determinant limits for real symmetric three-channel pencils with a positive coefficient frame and zero in the free spectral support.
It assembles the adopted complete coefficient theorem, original-law probability bridge and positive-frame middle theorem without changing the prescribed finite-second-moment laws.
The proof was developed for Yin through ordinary reasoning and authoring self-checks and is adopted with [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v21.md#positive-frame-spectral-point-assembly-whole-proof).
Its complete development in [D10](../drafts/Yin_D_F0010.md#positive-frame-spectral-point-log-assembly) is extraction provenance rather than a proof dependency.
The earlier [three-channel path theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v21.md#statement) remains fixed at its distinct coefficient scope.
No completion of the [arbitrary-coefficient F13 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) or [scalar-polynomial F14 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) is asserted.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

Fix \(d\ge1\) and real symmetric matrices \(A_0,\ldots,A_d\in M_3(\mathbb R)\).
For input \(j\), let \(\xi_j\) be a fixed real centered variance-one off-diagonal law and let \(\zeta_j\) be a fixed real centered finite-second-moment diagonal law.
The upper entries of \(W_j^{(N)}\) are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\) off the diagonal and \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\) on the diagonal; lower entries are symmetric copies.
All coordinates and inputs are independent within each size, while coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
There is no higher-moment, density, bounded-support or original-input operator-norm assumption.
Put
\[
 L_N=A_0\otimes I_N+\sum_{j=1}^d A_j\otimes W_j^{(N)},
 \qquad
 L=A_0\otimes1+\sum_{j=1}^d A_j\otimes s_j,
\]
where the \(s_j\) are free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).
Write \(\tau_3=\operatorname{tr}_3\otimes\tau\), and let \(\mu\) be the scalar spectral probability law of the selfadjoint \(L\).
Assume precisely
\[
 \chi_A:=\inf_{\substack{u,v\in\mathbb C^3\\\|u\|=\|v\|=1}}
               \sum_{j=1}^d|u^*A_jv|^2>0,
 \qquad 0\in\operatorname{supp}\mu.
 \tag{Frame-assembly-model}
\]
The first condition retains the complex-direction convention even though the coefficients and entries are real.
The second is the exact spectral-point branch of C187v26, including gapless boundaries; its free-energy characterization belongs to that source and is not a further premise here.
Neither condition is part of the unrestricted F13 or F14 contract.

There is a fixed \(a>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)<aN^{-17/2}\}\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{Frame-unshifted-floor}
\]
and, with ordinary FK determinants and \(\log0=-\infty\),
\[
 v:=\log\Delta_{\tau_3}(L)\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 X_N:=\frac1{3N}\log|\det L_N|\longrightarrow v
 \quad\text{in probability}.
 \tag{Frame-probability-log}
\]
Under every prescribed coupling,
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}X_N=v\qquad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Frame-every-coupling-limsup}
\]
The probability-one event may depend on the fixed coefficients, laws and coupling.
The matrix polynomial in these statements is the displayed degree-one matrix polynomial \(L_N\); no unmentioned scalar Schur polynomial is being substituted for it.

## Complete determinant assembly

<a id="common-atom-avoiding-shift"></a>
### One atom-avoiding shift for the parent and all required minors

For each \(N\), let \(M^0_{N,S}\) be the actual physical principal minor of \(L_N\), retaining all three auxiliary coordinates at every position outside \(S\), with \(|S|\le2\).
Empty minors require no eigenvalue condition.
There are finitely many such minors and finitely many ordered eigenvalues in each nonempty minor.
Each ordered real eigenvalue has a probability law with at most countably many atoms.
Let \(\mathcal A_N\) be the union of the negatives of these atoms over all those eigenvalues and minors.
This is a countable deterministic set depending only on the prescribed marginal input laws and coefficients.
Choose one deterministic
\[
 0<\theta_N<N^{-10},\qquad \theta_N\notin\mathcal A_N.
 \tag{Frame-common-shift}
\]
Then every
\[
 M^\theta_{N,S}=M^0_{N,S}+\theta_N I_{3(N-|S|)}
\]
is invertible almost surely.
A countable intersection over \(N\) makes this simultaneous under each allowed coupling.
The parent, every single-deletion minor used in C194v19, and the further single deletions appearing inside the fixed-deletion geometry are covered by the same shift.
All input normalizations remain \(N^{-1/2}\).

Set \(L_N^\theta=L_N+\theta_N I_{3N}\).
This changes only the deterministic constant coefficient to \(A_0+\theta_N I_3\).
It changes no entry law, independence relation, noise coefficient, coefficient frame or conditional fresh-row law.
In particular, after conditioning on the actual input minors and exposed diagonal variables at physical index \(i\), all \(d\) fresh borders still have the independent prescribed coordinates \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\).
No rare event is used as a conditioning event to enforce invertibility.
The shift tends to zero, so C187v27 and its incorporated C187v26 geometry apply with the same limiting free pencil \(L\) and with physical deletion bound one.
The additional deletion bound two above covers all actual objects needed in their proof interfaces without introducing unrelated matrices.

<a id="minor-good-events"></a>
### Minor-measurable good events and the unconditional average

Use exactly the sigma-field \(\mathcal F_i\), actual single-deletion inverse \(Z_i=(M^\theta_{N,\{i\}})^{-1}\), border map \(R_i\), complete sixth coefficient norm \(\sigma_i\), group-load ratio \(\tau_i\), and graph matrix \(\Gamma_i\) of C194v19.
Let \(q_1(Z_i)\ge q_2(Z_i)\ge q_3(Z_i)\) be the first three inverse singular values.
Put
\[
 \Lambda=\sum_j\|A_j\|^2,\qquad B_0=2\sqrt{d\Lambda},\qquad
 a_i=(1+B_0^2)^{3/2}q_1(Z_i)q_2(Z_i)q_3(Z_i).
 \tag{Frame-area-scale}
\]
For formal row vectors satisfying \(\max_j\|x_j\|\le2\), the actual map obeys \(\|R_i(x)\|\le B_0\).
The adopted coefficient theorem gives a fixed \(\kappa>0\) and, almost surely eventually uniformly in \(i\),
\[
 q_3(Z_i)\ge1,\qquad
 \sigma_i\ge\kappa a_iN^{-3},\qquad
 \sup_{\max_j\|x_j\|\le2}\sqrt{\det\Gamma_i(x)}\le a_i,
 \qquad \max_i\tau_i\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Frame-coefficient-events}
\]
Here \(q_3\to\infty\) and the deterministic area comparison are both the explicit graph-area interface of C187v27; that source derives the first from its adopted C187v26 fixed-two-mode residual energy.
The coefficient norm includes the original \(N^{-3}\) scale.
The exposed corner can change lower polynomial degrees but cannot change this complete sixth layer.

On null singular-minor branches set \(Z_i=0\), define its formal quantities by that extension, and set \(a_i=1\).
Set \(\tau_i=1\) whenever its denominator is zero or the minor is singular.
These extensions make all quantities measurable on the entire probability space and do not alter the eventual conclusions.
The convergence in probability of \(\max_i\tau_i\) yields a deterministic sequence
\[
 N^{-1/2}\le t_N\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \mathbb P\{\max_i\tau_i>t_N\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Frame-load-diagonal}
\]
For example, choose increasing deterministic \(N_k\) for which
\(\mathbb P\{\max_i\tau_i>1/k\}<1/k\) for all \(N\ge N_k\), and use the maximum of \(1/k\) and \(N^{-1/2}\) between successive thresholds.
This selection uses only probabilities under the prescribed size-\(N\) marginal laws, so it does not depend on how different sizes are coupled.

Define \(\mathcal G_i\) by invertibility, \(q_3\ge1\), the displayed coefficient lower bound and \(\tau_i\le t_N\).
Every condition is \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable, and the displayed area bound holds deterministically on \(\mathcal G_i\).
If \(\mathcal H_N\) denotes the common event consisting of invertibility, \(q_3\ge1\) and the coefficient lower bounds for all \(i\), then \(\mathbb P(\mathcal H_N^c)\to0\).
Consequently
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
 \le\mathbb P(\mathcal H_N^c)
     +\mathbb P\{\max_i\tau_i>t_N\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Frame-mean-bad-minor}
\]
This uses the already uniform fixed-deletion geometry and introduces no factor \(N\) in a marginal error estimate.
The common event \(\mathcal H_N\) is used only to bound unconditional probabilities.
It is not inserted into the fresh-row conditional law, and neither is a parent norm guard or a row-ball event.

<a id="frobenius-norm-guard"></a>
### A fixed Frobenius norm guard from the original finite-second-moment laws

At an off-diagonal physical position \(k<\ell\), the raw auxiliary block is
\(B_{k\ell}=\sum_j A_j\xi_{j,k\ell}\).
These blocks are independent and identically distributed over \(k<\ell\), with
\[
 \mathbb E\|B_{k\ell}\|_F^2=\sum_j\|A_j\|_F^2,
\]
because the original input variables are independent, centered and have variance one.
The weak law for the integrable random variable \(\|B_{k\ell}\|_F^2\) therefore gives
\[
 \frac2{N^2}\sum_{k<\ell}\|B_{k\ell}\|_F^2
 \longrightarrow\sum_j\|A_j\|_F^2
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\]
The random physical diagonal is \(N^{-1/2}\sum_jA_j\zeta_{j,k}\).
Its squared Frobenius contribution after division by \(N\) has expectation \(O(N^{-1})\).
Thus its Frobenius norm divided by \(\sqrt N\) vanishes in probability.
The deterministic diagonal \(A_0\otimes I_N\) has squared Frobenius norm \(N\|A_0\|_F^2\), and Cauchy--Schwarz makes its cross term with that random diagonal negligible after division by \(N\).
It follows that
\[
 \frac{\|L_N\|_F^2}{N}\longrightarrow
 Q_A:=\|A_0\|_F^2+\sum_j\|A_j\|_F^2
 \quad\text{in probability}.
 \tag{Frame-parent-Frobenius}
\]
The common translation changes the Frobenius norm by at most \(\sqrt{3N}\theta_N\).
Choose fixed \(B>\max(1,\sqrt{Q_A})\), and set \(b_N=B\sqrt N\).
Then
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|L_N^\theta\|>b_N\}
 \le\mathbb P\{\|L_N^\theta\|_F>B\sqrt N\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Frame-parent-norm-guard}
\]
This is a probability bound for a growing parent norm threshold, not a uniform operator-norm assertion about any original input.

<a id="unshifted-floor"></a>
### The original parent floor and quantitative return from the shift

All hypotheses of the adopted C194v19 bridge now hold with the just constructed \(\mathcal G_i,a_i,t_N,b_N\).
Its matching rank is \(\rho_N=\lfloor(24t_N)^{-1}\rfloor\to\infty\).
For each fixed original law, its pair-support error
\[
 e_{j,N}=\mathbb P\left\{N^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}
       [(\xi'_{j,k})^2+(\xi''_{j,k})^2]>4\right\}
 \longrightarrow0
\]
by the ordinary weak law, since the expectation limit is two.
There are only fixed \(d\) such errors.
The bridge and the parent norm guard yield
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le cB^{-3}N^{-17/2}\}
 &\le \mathbb P\{\|L_N^\theta\|>B\sqrt N\}
       +Ce^{-cN}+\frac C N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)\\
 &\quad+C\sum_j e_{j,N}
       +Ce^{-c\rho_N}
       +C\sqrt{\log\rho_N}\,\rho_N^{-1/25}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Frame-shifted-floor}
\]
The power is exactly \(b_N^{-3}N^{-7}=B^{-3}N^{-17/2}\).
Every error in this expression is unconditional and keeps the actual original within-size marginal law.
No independence between errors or summability of the final failure probabilities is needed.

The deterministic singular-value perturbation inequality gives
\[
 |s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)-s_{\min}(L_N)|
 \le\theta_N<N^{-10}=o(N^{-17/2}).
\]
For sufficiently large \(N\), the event
\(s_{\min}(L_N)<(cB^{-3}/2)N^{-17/2}\)
is contained in the event on the left of (Frame-shifted-floor).
Taking \(a=cB^{-3}/2\) proves (Frame-unshifted-floor).
The shift is removed from the matrix before any logarithm is taken.
There is no attempted continuity estimate for an unregularized shifted logarithmic determinant.

<a id="middle-and-free-target"></a>
### The original middle range and finite free target

Let \(a_{1,N}\le\cdots\le a_{3N,N}\) be the singular values of the original unshifted \(L_N\), and put
\[
 k_N=12\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil+2.
\]
The adopted C187v3 theorem applies because its complex-direction frame, finite-second-moment law, normalization and fixed coefficient hypotheses are exactly satisfied.
Its constants and good event refer to this original pencil; the preceding common shift is not needed in this step.
With summable failure \(CN^8e^{-cN^{1/4}}\), it gives, after decreasing a fixed \(c_1>0\),
\[
 a_{r,N}\ge c_1\,\frac r{3N}
 \qquad(k_N\le r\le3N).
 \tag{Frame-original-middle}
\]
The count and logarithmic conclusions of that theorem give
\[
 \nu_{|L|}([0,t])\le Ct,\qquad
 \int_{(0,t)}(-\log x)\,d\nu_{|L|}(x)
 \le Ct(1+|\log t|)
 \tag{Frame-free-small-tail}
\]
for small \(t>0\).
In particular \(\nu_{|L|}(\{0\})=0\).
The free pencil is bounded, so its positive logarithm is finite and \(v\in\mathbb R\).
Free bounded invertibility is neither needed nor available at the stipulated spectral point.

For completeness, the exact middle profile also bounds the finite middle contribution on its good event, simultaneously for sufficiently small \(t>0\):
\[
 \frac1{3N}\sum_{\substack{r\ge k_N\\a_{r,N}<t}}
              (-\log a_{r,N})
 \le Ct(1+|\log t|).
 \tag{Frame-middle-negative-tail}
\]
Indeed \(a_{r,N}<t\) forces \(r<3Nt/c_1\), and one sums
\(\log(3N/(c_1r))\) only up to that cutoff.
The elementary bound
\(\sum_{r=1}^{h}\log(A/r)\le\int_0^h\log(A/x)\,dx
=h(1+\log(A/h))\)
with \(A=3N/c_1\) gives the displayed uniform modulus.
This is the exact middle-logarithmic calculation already used in C187v3.

<a id="logarithmic-probability-limit"></a>
### Bottom logarithms, positive tails and the probability limit

On the original floor good event, the omitted bottom values satisfy
\[
 \frac1{3N}\sum_{r<k_N}(-\log a_{r,N})_+
 \le\frac{k_N}{3N}\left(\frac{17}{2}\log N+|\log a|\right)
 =O(N^{-1/4}\log N)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Frame-bottom-negative-tail}
\]
Combining the floor and middle events by a union bound, with (Frame-middle-negative-tail), gives
\[
 \forall\eta>0:\quad
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
 \mathbb P\left\{\frac1{3N}
       \sum_{a_{r,N}<t}(-\log a_{r,N})>\eta\right\}=0.
 \tag{Frame-negative-log-UI}
\]
Zero singular values contribute infinity and are excluded only on the floor good event.
The polynomial floor is a probability statement and is not upgraded to an eventual almost-sure statement.

Equation (Frame-parent-Frobenius) also supplies positive-logarithmic uniform integrability in probability.
For any fixed \(D>Q_A\), its good event gives, for \(T>1\),
\[
 \frac1{3N}\sum_{a_{r,N}>T}\log a_{r,N}
 \le\frac D3\sup_{x>T}\frac{\log x}{x^2}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(T\to\infty).
 \tag{Frame-positive-log-UI}
\]
The same conclusion follows from the adopted C186v4 matrix-polynomial positive-tail theorem, which also retains its stronger expected tail statement.
No almost-sure Frobenius convergence is inferred from the preceding weak law.

The adopted C186v4 weak theorem applies to the fixed selfadjoint matrix polynomial \(L_N\), followed by the continuous absolute-value map.
Thus its singular probability law \(\nu_N\) converges almost surely under each coupling to \(\nu_{|L|}\).
For fixed \(0<t<1<T\), the clipped logarithm
\(x\mapsto\log(\min(T,\max(t,x)))\)
is bounded and continuous.
Its integrals converge by the weak law.
Letting \(t\downarrow0\) and \(T\uparrow\infty\), using (Frame-negative-log-UI), (Frame-positive-log-UI) and (Frame-free-small-tail), proves
\[
 \int\log x\,d\nu_N(x)=X_N\longrightarrow v
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\]
This proves (Frame-probability-log) with the normalization \(1/(3N)\) of the actual matrix polynomial.

<a id="every-coupling-limsup"></a>
### The almost-sure upper bound and every-coupling limsup

Fix any allowed cross-size coupling.
For each fixed \(\delta>0\), apply the adopted C186v4 logarithmic-growth theorem to the fixed matrix polynomial \(L_N^2\) and
\(h_\delta(x)=\tfrac12\log(x+\delta^2)\).
It is continuous, nondecreasing, bounded below and of logarithmic growth.
The theorem's original-law positive-tail argument therefore gives
\[
 X_{N,\delta}:=\frac1{6N}\log\det(L_N^2+\delta^2 I_{3N})
 \longrightarrow
 v_\delta:=\tfrac12\tau_3\log(L^2+\delta^2)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Frame-regularized-upper}
\]
This is the matrix-coefficient instance of the [fixed-regularizer upper interface](../ledger/Yin_L_F0061_v2.md#upper-limit).
In particular it does not replace the unbounded original input norms by a deterministic constant.
Since \(X_N\le X_{N,\delta}\), intersecting the probability-one events for \(\delta=2^{-k}\) and using \(v_\delta\downarrow v\) yields
\[
 \limsup_NX_N\le v\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]

The finite-target probability limit provides deterministic \(N_1<N_2<\cdots\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{X_{N_k}<v-1/k\}\le2^{-k}.
\]
These are probabilities of the complete prescribed input marginals.
The sufficiency direction of the adopted C190v1 criterion, equivalently first Borel--Cantelli along this deterministic subsequence, gives
\(\limsup_NX_N\ge v\) almost surely under every coupling.
Together with the separately established upper bound this proves (Frame-every-coupling-limsup).
The favorable subsequence may depend on the fixed marginal model but not on cross-size dependence.
No full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic limit or expectation convergence of raw logarithms is asserted.

## Exact task boundary and continuation

The adopted theorem closes the displayed real symmetric \(m=3\), positive-frame, free-spectral-point pencil branch.
All direct mathematical inputs are adopted at their stated scopes, and the [whole-proof finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v21.md#positive-frame-spectral-point-assembly-whole-proof) checks the complete assembly and its interfaces.
The complementary free-gap branch with actual high inverse energy remains outside the argument.
No extension to complex input laws, nonselfadjoint coefficients, other auxiliary dimensions, or zero-frame tuples is supplied.

For F14, the [exact finite/free linearization identities](../ledger/Yin_L_F0061_v2.md#linearization-transfer) transfer a pencil determinant theorem only when the actual scalar polynomial's chosen pencil satisfies every hypothesis of that theorem.
They supply neither a real symmetric three-by-three positive frame nor the present spectral-point premise for arbitrary scalar polynomials.
Moreover a determinant identity alone does not transfer a least-singular-value floor without an actual inverse-block or comparable quantitative identity.
No such scalar certificate is assumed or manufactured here.
Thus this branch is partial progress for F13 and supplies a conditional application interface toward F14, without completing either original contract or asserting Brown-measure convergence.
