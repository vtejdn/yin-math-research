# Almost-sure Brown convergence for three independent real Wigner factors

This project deduction proves full-sequence Brown convergence for the product of three independent real finite-second-moment Wigner matrices.
The necessary [actual structured-pair estimate C194v21](Yin_C_F0194_v21.md#statement) now has a [proved assessment with independent whole-proof review](Yin_C_F0194_v21.md#decision-structured-companion-reviewed).
The [selected proved assessment](#decision-three-real-product-reviewed) adopts the conclusions below after a separate [independent whole-chain review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v22.md#three-real-product-whole-proof).
That review checks this argument's use of the upstream theorem and all later least-value, logarithmic and planar steps.

The argument retains the complete conditional numerator and replaces the nonsummable matching term in the [earlier real-carrier assembly](Yin_C_F0186_v10.md#floor-input) only for this actual three-factor shift.
The [isolated-minor reduction L66v10](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v10.md#isolated-background-upper-tail) and [inverse-quadratic reduction L66v12](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v12.md#inverse-quadratic-coupled-background-obligation) are extraction provenance.
Their necessary probability and algebraic deductions are supplied below in fixed C material.
The earlier physical versions and their distinct statements and assessments remain unchanged.

## Exact model and conclusions

For \(j=1,2,3\), fix a real law \(\xi_j\) with \(\mathbb E\xi_j=0\) and \(\mathbb E\xi_j^2=1\), and a real law \(\zeta_j\) with \(\mathbb E\zeta_j=0\) and \(\mathbb E\zeta_j^2<\infty\).
For every \(N\), the strict upper entries of \(W_j^{(N)}\) are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), its diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), and its lower entries are the symmetric copies of the upper entries.
All upper coordinates and all three inputs are independent within each dimension.
The complete tuples for different dimensions are defined on one probability space with arbitrary cross-size coupling.
The laws are fixed independently of \(N\), may differ between the three inputs and may be atomic or unbounded.
No density, moment above two or independence between dimensions is assumed.

Put
\[
 A_N=W_1^{(N)},\qquad B_N=W_2^{(N)},\qquad C_N=W_3^{(N)},
 \qquad P_N=A_NB_NC_N.
\]
Let \(s_1,s_2,s_3\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\), and put \(p=s_1s_2s_3\).
Use ordinary eigenvalue multiplicities, the Euclidean operator norm, the unnormalized Frobenius norm, and \(a_k(T)=s_{N-k+1}(T)\) for singular values in increasing order.
Algebraic transposition below has no complex conjugation.

The conclusions are the following.

1. For every fixed \(z\in(\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R)\cup\{0\}\) and every \(b>0\), there are \(a_{z,b},C_{z,b},N_{z,b}>0\) such that
   \[
    \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(P_N-zI_N)\le N^{-a_{z,b}}\}
       \le C_{z,b}N^{-b}\qquad(N\ge N_{z,b}).
    \tag{Three-factor-floor}
   \]
2. For every such fixed \(z\), the target \(\log\Delta_\tau(p-z)\) is finite and
   \[
    \frac1N\log|\det(P_N-zI_N)|
      \longrightarrow\log\Delta_\tau(p-z)
      \quad\hbox{almost surely}.
    \tag{Three-factor-log}
   \]
   The convention includes \(\log0=-\infty\) at any finite size.
   The probability-one event may depend on \(z\); no common event of pointwise logarithmic convergence for every complex parameter is asserted.
3. There is one event of probability one, for each fixed model and allowed coupling, on which
   \[
    \frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{\lambda_j(P_N)}
       \Longrightarrow\mu_p^{\mathrm{Br}}
       \quad\hbox{weakly on }\mathbb C.
    \tag{Three-factor-Brown}
   \]
   On that event convergence holds against every bounded continuous real test function.

The nonreal-parameter floor and the Brown conclusion use the exact adopted C194v21 input through the application below.
The zero-parameter floor and logarithmic argument are also given independently below using already accepted single-factor inputs.
No full-sequence logarithmic assertion at a nonzero real parameter is included.
This is the \(P=x_1x_2x_3\), three-real-input subclass of the [finite-variance Task](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract), not the full complex-law or arbitrary-polynomial contract.

## Complete argument

Fix a nonreal \(z\) until the separate zero-parameter section.
Fix a desired failure power \(b>0\), choose
\[
 \beta>\max\{b,1\},\qquad q=2\beta+6,\qquad b_{\rm iso}=2\beta+2.

### Original factor guards and the full shifted matrix

Apply C188v2 with zero shift to \(B_N\) and \(C_N\), requesting failure power \(q\).
Increasing one common exponent \(\ell\ge1\) if necessary gives the \(B,C\)-measurable event
\[
 \Lambda_N=\{s_{\min}(B_N)\ge N^{-\ell},
                  \ s_{\min}(C_N)\ge N^{-\ell}\},
 \qquad \mathbb P(\Lambda_N^c)=O(N^{-q}).
 \tag{Factor-guard}
\]
Define
\[
 F_N=-zC_N^{-1}B_N^{-1}\quad\hbox{on }\Lambda_N,
 \qquad F_N=0\quad\hbox{off }\Lambda_N,\qquad M_N=A_N+F_N.
\]
This measurable extension is independent of \(A_N\) within this dimension.
Choose a fixed integer \(\gamma>2\ell+1\); then \(\|F_N\|\le N^\gamma\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
On \(\Lambda_N\), the exact factorization is
\[
 P_N-zI_N=M_NB_NC_N.
 \tag{Actual-factorization}
\]
Only within-dimension conditioning on \(B_N,C_N\) will be used.

Choose an integer \(K>\gamma+2\) large enough that the finite-second-moment norm estimate and the [C194v3 norm calculation](Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#polynomial-shift-proof) give
\[
 \mathbb P\{\Lambda_N,\ \|M_N\|>N^K\}=O(N^{-q}).
\]
Indeed \(\mathbb E\|A_N\|_F^2=O(N)\) already gives arbitrary polynomial failure after choosing \(K\) large enough.
Choose the same \(K\) with enough slack for all actual principal minors to have the marginal conditional bound
\[
 \sup_i\mathbb P\{\|D_i\|>N^K\mid B_N,C_N\}
       =O(N^{-q})\quad\hbox{on }\Lambda_N.
 \tag{Minor-norm-guard}
\]
Multiplication by \(\sqrt N\) puts \(M_N\) into the raw C194v2 model with shift entry exponent at most \(\gamma+1\).
Its uniform random-shift corank estimate gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\Lambda_N,\ \operatorname{rank}M_N\le N-2\}=O(N^{-q}).
 \tag{Full-corank-guard}
\]
None of these events is used to condition the fresh border law.

### Actual minors, complete numerator and normalization

For each \(i\), with \(n=N-1\), put
\[
 X_i=(A_N)_{\widehat i,\widehat i},\quad
 F_i=(F_N)_{\widehat i,\widehat i},\quad D_i=X_i+F_i,\quad
 T_i=\operatorname{adj}D_i,\quad Q_i=(T_i+T_i^T)/2.
\]
On \(\Lambda_N\), \(F_i\) is the principal block of the full inverse product \(-zC_N^{-1}B_N^{-1}\), exactly as in C194v21.
It is never replaced by a product of inverses of principal blocks.
Let
\[
 \mathscr F_i=\sigma(X_i,(A_N)_{ii},B_N,C_N).
\]
When \(T_i\ne0\), define
\[
 Z_i=\frac{\det M_N}{\|T_i\|_F},\qquad
 \kappa_i(\tau)=\mathbb P\{|Z_i|\le\tau\mid\mathscr F_i\}.
\]
All uses of these ratios are restricted to \(T_i\ne0\).
Use the version of \(\kappa_i\) obtained by integrating the remaining original row against its fixed product law, a measurable function of the displayed background.

After a common permutation of rows and columns, write
\[
 M_N=\begin{pmatrix}\mu&(x+f)^T\\x+g&D_i\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad x=\xi/\sqrt N,
\]
where \(\xi\) has iid coordinates with the original fixed law \(\xi_1\).
Conditional on \(\mathscr F_i\), the quantities \(\mu,f,g,D_i\) are fixed and \(\xi\) retains that original law, with no cutoff or regularity conditioning.
Put
\[
 \mathsf A_i=\frac{Q_i}{\|T_i\|_F},\quad
 c_i=\frac{N(\mu\det D_i-f^TT_i g)}{\|T_i\|_F},\quad
 \ell_i=\frac{T_i g+T_i^T f}{\|T_i\|_F}.
\]
The bordered determinant identity gives
\[
 NZ_i=c_i-\xi^T\mathsf A_i\xi-\sqrt N\,\xi^T\ell_i.
 \tag{Complete-quadratic}
\]
Thus both the actual center and the actual linear coefficients remain present.

For later application of the raw isolated-minor theorem, the exact scaling is
\[
 t_j(\sqrt N D_i)=\sqrt N\,t_j(D_i),\qquad
 \frac{|\det(\sqrt N M_N)|}
      {\|\operatorname{adj}(\sqrt N D_i)\|_F}
       =N|Z_i|.
 \tag{Raw-ratio-scaling}
\]
The determinant has degree \(N\), whereas the minor adjugate has degree \(N-2\), so the ratio factor is \(N\), not \(\sqrt N\).

### Isolated minors, second values and the low-matching intersection

Apply C194v1 to \(\sqrt N M_N\) with raw shift exponent \(\gamma+1\) and failure exponent \(b_{\rm iso}\).
Let \(A_0,L_{\rm iso}\) be its determinant-ratio and isolation exponents.
Define the \(\mathscr F_i\)-measurable event
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal I_i=\{T_i\ne0\}\cap\bigl(&
       \{\operatorname{corank}D_i=1\}\\
       {}\cup{}&
       \{D_i\text{ invertible},\
       t_1(D_i)\le N^{-L_{\rm iso}-1/2},\
       t_1(D_i)/t_2(D_i)\le N^{-L_{\rm iso}}\}\bigr).
 \end{split}
\]
By (Raw-ratio-scaling), for every fixed \(\Theta\ge A_0+1\),
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\Lambda_N\cap\bigcup_i
       \bigl(\mathcal I_i\cap\{|Z_i|\le N^{-\Theta}\}\bigr)\right\}
       \le C N^{-b_{\rm iso}}.
 \tag{Isolated-joint}
\]
The theorem already includes the union over all principal indices.
Its internal raw-entry cutoffs do not change the conditional law defining \(\kappa_i\).

Use the arbitrary-power [second-value assertion of C194v5](Yin_C_F0194_v5.md#second-smallest-floor), with sufficiently large requested marginal power to absorb the \(N\) actual minors.
It gives a fixed \(L_2\ge1\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{\Lambda_N,\ \exists i:t_2(D_i)<N^{-L_2}\}
       =O(N^{-q}).
 \tag{All-second-values}
\]
Here \(X_i=\sqrt{n/N}\,W_n\), or equivalently its raw matrix is \(\sqrt N X_i\).
The bounded normalization multiplier and the replacement of \(N\) by \(n=N-1\) are absorbed in fixed exponent slack, exactly as in C194v5.

Choose the full-minor symmetric-part exponent \(L_0\ge1\) and internal symmetric-part exponent \(L_{\rm int}\) as in the [C194v5 complete guard assembly](Yin_C_F0194_v5.md#in-probability-floor), increasing the requested failure powers to absorb at most \(N^2\) internal and outer choices.
Their simultaneous exceptional probability on \(\Lambda_N\) is \(O(N^{-q})\).
In particular, with
\[
 S_i=(D_i+D_i^T)/2,\qquad K_i=(D_i-D_i^T)/2,
\]
the event \(s_{\min}(S_i)\ge N^{-L_0}\) fails conditionally on \(B,C\), on \(\Lambda_N\), with probability \(O(N^{-q})\), uniformly in \(i\).
This also follows directly from C188v2 applied to \(W_n+\sqrt{N/n}\operatorname{sym}F_i\).
The shift is genuinely complex symmetric and independent of that original principal Wigner block.

Choose successively
\[
 T_0>L_{\rm iso}+L_2+2,\qquad
 J>\max\{L_{\rm iso}+L_2+1,\ 2K+L_0+T_0+1\},
\]
\[
 H>\max\{J+L_{\rm int}+2K+1,\
                     2K+L_0+T_0+3/2\}.
 \tag{Coarse-exponent-order}
\]
Let \(\mathcal H_i\) be the event that \(T_i\ne0\) and the graph with edges \(j\ne k\) satisfying
\[
 |(Q_i)_{jk}|\ge N^{-H}\|T_i\|_F
\]
has a matching of at least \(r_N=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\).
The accepted C194v5 low-matching deduction, using \(r_N\log N=o(N)\), gives outside an additional \(O(N^{-q})+Ce^{-cN}\) exceptional event:
\[
 \|M_N\|\le N^K,\quad T_i\ne0,\quad\mathcal H_i^c
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_1(D_i)\le2N^{-T_0}.
 \tag{Low-matching-small-minor}
\]
The symmetric-part and second-value guards above are included in this event.
The chosen exponents then imply both raw isolation inequalities in \(\mathcal I_i\).
A singular \(D_i\) with nonzero adjugate has exactly corank one and is already its singular branch.
Consequently the low-matching intersection with a sufficiently small \(|Z_i|\) is bounded by (Isolated-joint) plus the displayed guard failures.
No estimate on the probability of the entire low-matching event is asserted or needed.

The same second-value guard and the choice \(J>L_{\rm iso}+L_2+1\) imply
\[
 T_i\ne0,\quad t_1(D_i)\le N^{-J}
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal I_i.
 \tag{Small-minor-isolation}
\]
For any \(\mathscr F_i\)-measurable \(\mathcal A_i\subseteq\{T_i\ne0\}\),
\[
 \mathbb E[1_{\mathcal A_i}\kappa_i(\tau)]
       =\mathbb P(\mathcal A_i,\ |Z_i|\le\tau).
 \tag{Conditional-numerator-identity}
\]
Apply this to \(\mathcal A_i=\Lambda_N\cap\mathcal I_i\cap\mathcal H_i\).
Markov's inequality, (Isolated-joint) and (All-second-values) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P\{&
 \Lambda_N,\mathcal H_i,\ t_1(D_i)\le N^{-J},\
              \kappa_i(N^{-\Theta})>N^{-\beta}\}\\
 &\le C N^{\beta-b_{\rm iso}}+O(N^{-q})
   =O(N^{-\beta-2}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{Isolated-background-tail}
\]
There is no additional index-union loss.
The simultaneous guards are used only to compare probabilities; they are not inserted as conditioning information in \(\kappa_i\).

### The remaining invertible branch produces an admissible structured pair

Work on
\[
 \Lambda_N,\qquad t_1(D_i)>N^{-J},\qquad
 \mathcal G_i^0:=\{\|D_i\|\le N^K,\ s_{\min}(S_i)\ge N^{-L_0}\}.
\]
The latter guard is \(\mathscr F_i\)-measurable, and its marginal conditional failure probability on \(\Lambda_N\) is \(O(N^{-q})\).
Write \(D,S,K_{\rm sk},T,\mathsf A\) for \(D_i,S_i,K_i,T_i,\mathsf A_i\) within this subsection, to distinguish the skew matrix \(K_{\rm sk}\) from the norm exponent \(K\).

The symmetry required by the quadratic inverse theorem is \(\mathsf A^T=\mathsf A\), not \(F_i^T=F_i\).
The complete source quadratic definition takes a supremum over all centers and linears.
Thus (Complete-quadratic) implies
\[
 \kappa_i(N^{-\Theta})>N^{-\beta}
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \rho_{N^{1-\Theta},\xi_1}(\mathsf A)>N^{-\beta}.
 \tag{Quadratic-source-match}
\]
Theorem 1.8 has no Frobenius-unit hypothesis on its symmetric coefficient array.
There is therefore no omitted division by \(\|\mathsf A\|_F\) in this implication.
A fixed variance-one real law is nondegenerate and satisfies the source's Condition 1.
The selector-symmetrized scalar law in its row-combination conclusion likewise satisfies that fixed condition.
No truncation of the remaining row and no higher moment is required in this application.

On the displayed invertible branch,
\[
 \mathsf A=dD^{-T}SD^{-1},\qquad
 d=\frac{\det D}{\|T\|_F},\qquad
 |d|=\frac1{\|D^{-1}\|_F}.
\]
Since \(\|D^{-1}\|_F\le\sqrt n\,N^J\), any fixed integer
\[
 P_0>L_0+2K+J+1
\]
gives
\[
 s_{\min}(\mathsf A)
 \ge\frac{s_{\min}(S)}{\|D\|^2\|D^{-1}\|_F}
 \ge N^{-P_0}
 \tag{Quadratic-coefficient-floor}
\]
eventually.
This is an exact inverse identity with the actual cofactor denominator.

Fix \(\epsilon=1/100\).
Apply Ngu12a Theorem 1.8 with exponent \(\beta+1\), absorbing \(N\) versus \(n=N-1\).
It supplies a bounded index set \(I_0\), a nonzero integer \(k\), polynomially bounded integers \(k_{hj}\), and at least \(n-2n^\epsilon\) admissible row indices.
For an admissible \(h\notin I_0\), let
\[
 c_h=ke_h+\sum_{j\in I_0}k_{hj}e_j,\qquad
 w_h=\mathsf A c_h.
\]
Because \(\mathsf A\) is symmetric, \(w_h^T\) is the source row combination.
Its norm is at least \(N^{-P_0}\), by \(\|c_h\|\ge1\) and (Quadratic-coefficient-floor).
The small-row-combination alternative is therefore excluded.
There is a fixed constant \(C_{\rm inv}\), independent of \(\Theta\), such that \(w_h\) has linear concentration at least \(N^{-C_{\rm inv}}\), for the source selector-symmetrized law, at radius at most \(N^{1-\Theta+C_{\rm inv}}\).

Normalize \(u_0=w_h/\|w_h\|\).
For an integer precision \(a\), choose
\[
 \Theta\ge a+C_{\rm inv}+P_0+5.
 \tag{Inverse-precision}
\]
The linear concentration radius is then no larger than the deterministic rational radius \(N^{-a-3}\).
Apply Theorem 1.3 at that larger radius and with \(n^\epsilon\) exceptions.
Use its rational-generator conclusion and the accepted rational rounding of exceptional coordinates.
Increasing a fixed structural integer \(C_0\), independently of \(a,\Theta\), gives a vector \(u\) with
\[
 \|u-u_0\|\le N^{-a},\qquad\tfrac12\le\|u\|\le2.
 \tag{Structured-approximation}
\]
All but \(C_0n^\epsilon\) coordinates lie in a symmetric GAP of rank at most \(C_0\) and displayed integer-box volume at most \(N^{C_0}\).
Every real and imaginary coordinate of \(u\) and its generators is rational with numerator and denominator bounded by \(N^{a+C_0}\).
The fixed rational radius, instead of a row-dependent irrational normalization radius, supplies the required deterministic rational description.
Coordinate errors \(O(N^{-a-3})\) have Euclidean norm \(O(\sqrt nN^{-a-3})\le N^{-a}\) eventually.
The polynomial heights and box exponents are precisely the accepted [C194v1 rounding class](Yin_C_F0194_v1.md#counting-class), with fixed exponent slack.

Let \(\mathcal U_N(a)\) be the entire deterministic set of vectors with this description and norm restriction, not the single vector selected from \(X_i\).
Its cardinality obeys
\[
 |\mathcal U_N(a)|
 \le n^{C_*n+O_a(n^\epsilon)+O_a(1)}
 \tag{Family-entropy}
\]
with \(C_*\) fixed before \(a\).
Indeed the bounded number of rational generators and box lengths have polynomially many choices with exponent \(O_a(1)\); each nonexceptional coordinate has at most \(N^{C_0}\) choices; the exceptional index set and its rational coordinates cost \(n^{O_a(n^\epsilon)}\).
Changing \(N\) to \(n=N-1\) increases only fixed slack in \(C_*\) and the displayed remainder.
The symmetric source GAP gives centered integer boxes, so uncontrolled translations of their endpoints are not counted.
For every fixed \(a\), the remainder is deterministic \(o(n)\), uniform in \(i,B,C\).
In particular this finite family is independent of \(X_i\) and satisfies C194v21's family requirement.

Set
\[
 \mathcal T=DS^{-1}D^T
       =(S+K_{\rm sk})S^{-1}(S-K_{\rm sk})
       =S-K_{\rm sk}S^{-1}K_{\rm sk}.
\]
The exact identity \(\mathcal T\mathsf A=dI\) gives
\[
 \mathcal T u_0=\frac d{\|w_h\|}c_h.
\]
Only \(E=I_0\cup\{h\}\), with \(|E|\le C_0\), can support this right side.
Since \(\|\mathcal T\|\le N^{2K+L_0}\), (Structured-approximation) yields
\[
 |(\mathcal T u)_j|\le N^{-a+2K+L_0}\qquad(j\notin E).
\]
Define the companion by the exact formula
\[
 v=S^{-1}K_{\rm sk}u.
\]
Then
\[
 Sv-K_{\rm sk}u=0\quad\hbox{on all }n\hbox{ rows},\qquad
 |(Su-K_{\rm sk}v)_j|\le N^{-a+2K+L_0}\quad(j\notin E).
 \tag{Actual-structured-pair}
\]
The \(O(n^\epsilon)\) exceptional progression coordinates and the \(O(1)\) omitted equations are different sets.
No independent sampling, coordinate structure or free net for \(v\) is introduced.

### Exact application of the structured-pair count and the mean bridge

On \(\Lambda_N\cap\mathcal G_i^0\), the actual objects match C194v21 as follows:
\[
 \gamma_F=\gamma,\qquad k_0=K,\qquad L=L_0,\qquad Q_0=2K+L_0,
 \qquad\mathcal U_{i,N}(a;B,C)=\mathcal U_N(a).
\]
The equality of the operator norms of a complex matrix and its transpose gives
\[
 \|S_i\|,\ \|K_i\|\le\|D_i\|\le N^K.
\]
Also \(B,C,S_i\) are invertible, \(\|F_i\|\le N^\gamma\), and \(\|S_i^{-1}\|\le N^{L_0}\).
Thus every guard in the actual C194v21 statement holds.
Its entropy constants \(C_0,C_*\) are fixed independently of \(a\), by (Family-entropy).
Its exact companion and bounded omission requirements are exactly (Actual-structured-pair).

Use the adopted count with requested power \(q\).
Choose a fixed integer \(a\ge a_*\) from that statement, and then choose \(\Theta\) by (Inverse-precision), also requiring \(\Theta\ge A_0+1\).
The implication from (Quadratic-source-match) to (Actual-structured-pair) is uniform in the actual exposed diagonal, since the source supremum includes every center and linear term.
Therefore it gives the necessary-event inclusion for every such diagonal value, and the marginal count may be applied without conditioning the count on that diagonal.
The consequence is
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sup_i\mathbb P\{&
 \Lambda_N,\mathcal G_i^0,\ t_1(D_i)>N^{-J},\
       \kappa_i(N^{-\Theta})>N^{-\beta}\}\\
 &\le C_qN^{-q}+Ce^{-cN}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Invertible-background-tail}
\]
This is a direct bound on the background upper-tail event.
There is no Markov factor \(N^\beta\) in this step.
The count supplies no guard-complement estimate; those were separately supplied by the original-law norm and symmetric-part floors above.

Include \(\mathcal H_i\), add the marginal failures of \(\mathcal G_i^0\), and combine (Invertible-background-tail) with (Isolated-background-tail).
Then
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i
 \mathbb P\{\Lambda_N,\mathcal H_i,\
              \kappa_i(N^{-\Theta})>N^{-\beta}\}
       =O(N^{-\beta-2}).
 \tag{Mean-background-tail}
\]
The exponential term has been absorbed into the displayed polynomial bound.
Because \(0\le\kappa_i\le1\), this implies
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i
 \mathbb E[1_{\Lambda_N\cap\mathcal H_i}\kappa_i(N^{-\Theta})]
 \le N^{-\beta}
   +\frac1N\sum_i
      \mathbb P\{\Lambda_N,\mathcal H_i,\kappa_i>N^{-\beta}\}
 =O(N^{-\beta}).
 \tag{Mean-full-numerator}
\]
This uses the actual complete numerator, not a supremum centered at an unrelated value, a quadratic energy or a bound for every background.
The [L66v11 rare conditional atom](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v11.md#scope-and-continuation) remains compatible with this averaged statement.

### Many-index averaging and the original three-factor floor

Choose the final exponent
\[
 \alpha>\max\{\Theta+2K+1,\ A_0+2K+2,\ H+2K+2,\ 1\},
 \qquad\varepsilon_N=N^{-\alpha}.
 \tag{Final-floor-exponent}
\]
For sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 C_1N^{2K+1}\varepsilon_N\le N^{-\Theta},\qquad
 C_1N^{2K+2}\varepsilon_N\le N^{-A_0},
\]
including the fixed constant in C194v3.
Let
\[
 G_i=\{T_i\ne0,\ |Z_i|\le C_1N^{2K+1}\varepsilon_N\}.
\]
Conditional on the independent full shift and then integrated on \(\Lambda_N\), the [C194v3 average](Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#spread-and-average) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{\Lambda_N,\|M_N\|\le N^K,\operatorname{rank}M_N\ge N-1,
                      s_{\min}(M_N)\le\varepsilon_N\}\\
 \le Ce^{-cN}+\frac{C}{N}\sum_i\mathbb P(\Lambda_N,G_i).
 \end{split}
 \tag{Many-index-application}
\]
The statement includes full corank one, where all usable determinant ratios are zero.
A zero \(T_i\) has no assigned ratio and does not enter \(G_i\); the theorem produces a positive proportion of nonzero usable adjugates on its stated full-rank guard.

For the high-matching intersection,
\[
 \mathbb P(\Lambda_N,G_i,\mathcal H_i)
 \le\mathbb E[1_{\Lambda_N\cap\mathcal H_i}
                    \kappa_i(N^{-\Theta})],
\]
whose average is \(O(N^{-\beta})\) by (Mean-full-numerator).
For the low-matching intersection, first remove the already bounded full norm failure \(\{\Lambda_N,\|M_N\|>N^K\}\), whose probability bounds its contribution to the average without an index factor.
Outside that failure and the simultaneous guard exceptions in the earlier section, (Low-matching-small-minor) puts every \(G_i\cap\mathcal H_i^c\) in the raw isolated-minor event.
The raw ratio is at most \(N^{-A_0}\) by (Final-floor-exponent).
The union already controlled in (Isolated-joint), plus those simultaneous exceptions, therefore bounds its average by
\[
 O(N^{-b_{\rm iso}})+O(N^{-q})+Ce^{-cN}.
\]
No union over \(i\) is applied to (Mean-full-numerator), and the averaging theorem contributes no factor \(N\).

Add (Factor-guard), the norm and corank failures, and the preceding two branches.
The result is
\[
 \mathbb P\{\Lambda_N^c
        \ \hbox{or}\ s_{\min}(M_N)\le N^{-\alpha}\}
       =O(N^{-\beta}).
 \tag{Shifted-floor}
\]
This replaces the earlier nonsummable \(\psi(\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor)\) term by the mean full-numerator bound; that earlier matching inequality is not used to claim summability.

By (Actual-factorization), on the good event,
\[
 s_{\min}(P_N-zI_N)
 \ge s_{\min}(M_N)s_{\min}(B_N)s_{\min}(C_N)
 >N^{-\alpha-2\ell}.
\]
Choose \(a_{z,b}>\alpha+2\ell\).
Since \(\beta>b\), this proves (Three-factor-floor) for fixed nonreal \(z\).
The full parameter order is (Probability-powers), factor and norm exponents, isolated and second-value exponents, symmetric-part guards, \(J,H\), \(P_0,C_{\rm inv},C_0,C_*\), the count precision \(a\), \(\Theta\), and finally \(\alpha,a_{z,b}\).
There is no dependence of a coarse entropy coefficient on the final precision.

<a id="summable-middle"></a>
### Summable middle singular values of the actual product

This step uses the original polynomial \(P_N-zI_N\), not an enlarged independent-block model.
Set \(m_N=\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil\).
Apply C187v2 to \(M_N=A_N+F_N\), conditionally on \(B_N,C_N\), and separately at zero shift to \(B_N,C_N\).
The constants are uniform in the shift, and each exceptional probability is at most \(CN^2e^{-cN^{1/4}}\).
No independence between these three good events is required.

For any square \(U,V\) and \(r+s-1\le N\), the deterministic inequality is
\[
 a_{r+s-1}(UV)\ge a_r(U)a_s(V).
\]
Choose subspaces with the corresponding norm lower bounds and codimensions \(r-1,s-1\).
Intersect the second with the inverse image under \(V\) of the first.
The codimension is at most \(r+s-2\), so singular-value min-max proves the inequality, also for singular matrices.
Iterating it for three factors and distributing \(k+2\) among three indices differing by at most one gives
\[
 a_k(P_N-zI_N)\ge c_0(k/N)^3,\qquad
 k_N:=6(m_N+1)\le k\le N,
 \tag{Three-factor-middle}
\]
on \(\Lambda_N\) and the three intermediate good events.
Each factor index is at least \(m_N+1\), and its predecessor is at least \(k/6\), which accounts for the fixed constant \(c_0>0\).
The factor-guard failure \(O(N^{-q})\) and the three intermediate failures are summable.
Thus (Three-factor-middle) holds eventually almost surely under every allowed cross-size coupling.
This is the \(L=3\) case of the [C186v10 product profile](Yin_C_F0186_v10.md#middle-and-free-log), now with a summable carrier guard because its two carrier factors are the individual real Wigner matrices.

<a id="fixed-parameter-log"></a>
### Bottom, middle and positive logarithmic tails

For this fixed nonreal \(z\), apply the floor with any \(b>1\).
First Borel–Cantelli gives \(s_{\min}(P_N-zI_N)>N^{-a_z}\) eventually almost surely, for a fixed \(a_z\).
Intersect this event with the eventual middle profile and the C186v4 almost-sure Hermitized weak law and positive-logarithm controls for the fixed polynomial \(x_1x_2x_3-z\).
No cross-size independence is used.

The bottom \(k_N=O(N^{3/4})\) terms satisfy
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{k\le k_N}(-\log a_k(P_N-zI_N))_+
 \le a_z\frac{k_N\log N}{N}
 =O(N^{-1/4}\log N)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Bottom-log}
\]
For \(k>k_N\) and \(a_k<t<1\), (Three-factor-middle) forces \(k\le C Nt^{1/3}\), and bounds the corresponding negative logarithm by \(-\log c_0+3\log(N/k)\).
Summation and the elementary integral bound for \(\log(N/x)\) give
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}
 \frac1N\sum_{\substack{k>k_N\\a_k<t}}(-\log a_k)
 \le Ct^{1/3}(1+|\log t|).
 \tag{Middle-log}
\]
Together these prove pathwise uniform integrability of the negative singular-value logarithm at zero.

For completeness, the free limiting singular law \(\nu_z=\mu_{|p-z|}\) has
\[
 \nu_z([0,t])\le Ct^{1/3}
\]
for small \(t>0\).
At a positive continuity point, this follows from the accepted weak limit and the eventual counting consequence \(N^{-1}\#\{k:a_k\le t\}\le k_N/N+Ct^{1/3}\).
Decreasing continuity points extend it to all such \(t\).
This excludes a zero atom and makes the free negative logarithm finite by integration of \(\nu_z([0,t])/t\).
The positive logarithm is finite because \(p-z\) is bounded.
This is the same finite target already identified in [C186v10](Yin_C_F0186_v10.md#middle-and-free-log), without a bounded free inverse assumption.

For fixed positive lower and finite upper cutoffs, the clipped logarithm converges by Hermitized weak convergence.
Remove the lower cutoff by (Bottom-log) and (Middle-log), and the upper cutoff by the [C186v4 pathwise positive-tail estimate](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics).
One uses that pathwise estimate, not merely expected uniform integrability.
The result is (Three-factor-log) for each fixed nonreal \(z\), including eventual nonsingularity at that parameter.

### Fubini, planar \(L^2\) and one event of weak Brown convergence

Define the eigenvalue potentials
\[
 u_N(z)=\frac1N\log|\det(P_N-zI_N)|
       =\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\log|z-\lambda_j(P_N)|,
 \qquad
 u(z)=\log\Delta_\tau(p-z).
\]
These are jointly measurable in the sample and parameter, with the finite-size value \(-\infty\) permitted at eigenvalues.
Each \(u_N\) is locally integrable on the plane, since its only singularities are finitely many logarithmic singularities.
The free potential has its usual local \(L^2\) realization.

For every fixed nonreal \(z\), (Three-factor-log) has probability one.
The real axis is a deterministic planar null set; no assertion that the Brown measure gives it zero mass is required.
Apply Tonelli to the indicator of failure of \(u_N(z)\to u(z)\) on each integer-radius disk, excluding that axis.
A countable intersection gives one probability-one event on which the convergence holds for planar-almost every \(z\).
This argument needs no compact-uniform floor exponent, no measurable choice of \(a_z\), and no intersection of uncountably many probability-one events.

On the same event, intersect the [C186v4 planar controls](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) for the original fixed polynomial \(x_1x_2x_3\).
For every integer-radius disk \(D_R\),
\[
 \sup_{N\ {\rm sufficiently\ large}}\|u_N\|_{L^2(D_R)}<\infty,
 \qquad u\in L^2(D_R).
\]
The finite eigenvalue singularities do not affect these Lebesgue norms.
Cauchy–Schwarz gives uniform integrability on each disk, so almost-everywhere convergence implies
\[
 u_N\longrightarrow u\quad\hbox{in }L^1(D_R)
\]
on that event.
Equivalently, one can integrate the clipped difference and use the uniform \(L^2\) bound on the remaining small-measure sets.

The distributional identities \(\Delta u_N=2\pi\mu_{P_N}\) and \(\Delta u=2\pi\mu_p^{\mathrm{Br}}\) therefore give convergence against every compactly supported smooth test.
The same C186v4 control supplies pathwise tightness, from the eventual bound
\[
 \sup_{N\ {\rm sufficiently\ large}}
 \int(\log^+|\lambda|)^2\,d\mu_{P_N}(\lambda)<\infty.
\]
A finite number of earlier measures causes no tightness difficulty.
On each resulting fixed path, smooth-test convergence and tightness imply weak convergence to the Brown probability measure, hence convergence for every bounded continuous real test.
This is a deterministic implication on that one path, not a further uncountable probabilistic intersection.
It completes the deduction of (Three-factor-Brown) from the adopted count and the stated direct inputs.

The [C186v4 defect equivalence](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#remaining-defect) is compatible with this route.
Indeed local \(L^1\) convergence of \(u_N\), locally uniform convergence of each fixed regularization, and \(u_\delta\downarrow u\) in local \(L^1\) give
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
       \int_{D_R}(u_{N,\delta}-u_N)\,d^2z=0
\]
on the same event.
No pointwise atom is discarded from the fixed-parameter logarithmic proof, and no low-rank stability assertion for nonnormal eigenvalues is used.
