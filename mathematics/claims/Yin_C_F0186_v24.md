## Exact two-class statement

For each \(N\), let \(W_1^{(N)},\ldots,W_d^{(N)}\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
For each color the upper off-diagonal coordinates are iid copies of a fixed centered variance-one real law divided by \(\sqrt N\), and the diagonal coordinates are iid copies of a fixed centered finite-second-moment real law divided by \(\sqrt N\), independent of the off-diagonal coordinates.
Different colors may have different fixed laws.
The joint coupling over all dimensions is arbitrary.
Let \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\) be free variance-one semicircular elements in a finite tracial von Neumann algebra with faithful trace \(\tau\).

Fix one of the following scalar noncommutative polynomials:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{additive class:}&
 P=x_1+Q(x_2,\ldots,x_d),\quad
 Q\in\mathbb C\langle x_2,\ldots,x_d\rangle;\\
 \text{ordered-product class:}&
 P=x_1x_2\cdots x_d,\quad d\ge1.
 \end{array}
 \tag{Two-classes}
\]
The additive class permits a constant \(Q\), including when there are no background variables, and arbitrary repeated occurrences of background variables.
Its entire background omits the distinguished first variable.
For the ordered-product class each displayed factor is one of the mutually independent original inputs.
Put \(P_N=P(W_1^{(N)},\ldots,W_d^{(N)})\) and \(p=P(s_1,\ldots,s_d)\).

The following conclusions use the adopted count and floor input just identified.

For every fixed \(z\in\mathbb C\) and \(b>0\), there are \(A_{z,b},C_{z,b},N_{z,b}>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(P_N-zI_N)\le N^{-A_{z,b}}\}
       \le C_{z,b}N^{-b}\qquad(N\ge N_{z,b}).
 \tag{Two-class-floor}
\]
No nonreal-parameter restriction is imposed.
For every fixed \(z\in\mathbb C\), the free target is finite and
\[
 \frac1N\log|\det(P_N-zI_N)|
       \longrightarrow \tau\log|p-z|
       =\log\Delta_\tau(p-z)\in\mathbb R
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Two-class-log}
\]
The fixed-parameter event may depend on \(z\).
There is also one probability-one event, for each fixed model and polynomial \(P\), on which
\[
 \mu_{P_N}\Longrightarrow\mu_p^{\rm Br}
 \tag{Two-class-Brown}
\]
as probability measures on \(\mathbb C\).
Thus on that one event convergence holds against every bounded continuous test.

In fact the limiting singular law \(\nu_z=\mu_{|p-z|}\) obeys, for sufficiently small \(t>0\),
\[
 \nu_z([0,t])\le C_z t^{1/h},\qquad
 h=1\ \text{in the additive class},\quad h=d\ \text{in the product class}.
 \tag{Two-class-free-small-values}
\]
This free integrability conclusion is proved from accepted intermediate-value and weak-limit inputs independently of the new least-value floor.
No compact-uniform floor, simultaneous convergence at every uncountable parameter, expectation convergence of unregularized logarithms, outer-polynomial closure, general complex entry law or arbitrary noncommutative polynomial is asserted.

\[

 \tag{OP-factorization}
\]

\[
 s_{\min}(W_j)\ge N^{-\ell},\quad

 \tag{OP-factor-guard}
\]

\[
 \|R_N\|\le N^{(d-1)B},\qquad
 \|R_N^{-1}\|\le N^{(d-1)\ell},\qquad
 \|F_N\|\le N^\gamma
 \tag{OP-shift-norm}
\]
eventually, for any fixed \(\gamma>(d-1)\ell+1\), enlarged to an integer if necessary.

The exact algebraic-transpose identity is
\[
 F_N-F_N^T
   =-zR_N^{-1}(R_N^T-R_N)(R_N^T)^{-1}.
 \tag{OP-skew-identity}
\]
It is valid for complex \(z\); no conjugation of that scalar occurs.
Set
\[
 T=x_d\cdots x_2-x_2\cdots x_d.
\]

Thus \(\tau(T(s)^*T(s))>0\).

Choose \(a_0>0\) and a bounded continuous bounded-variation test \(0\le f\le1\), zero on \([0,a_0^2]\), with \(m_0=\tau f(T(s)^*T(s))>0\).

\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\#\{j:s_j(R_N^T-R_N)>a_0\}<m_0N/2\right\}
       \le Ce^{-cN}
 \tag{OP-uninverted-skew-bulk}

for all sufficiently large \(N\).

The finite word degree affects only fixed rank-change constants.
No untruncated finite-\(N\) high trace moment is asserted.

For invertible \(A,C\), min-max gives \(s_j(ABC)\ge s_{\min}(A)s_j(B)s_{\min}(C)\).

On (OP-factor-guard), the full skew matrix \(K_N=(F_N-F_N^T)/2\) therefore has at least \(m_0N/2\) singular values at least

 \#\{j:s_j(K_i)\ge N^{-L_K}\}\ge\eta n
 \tag{OP-principal-skew-bulk}

\[

 \tag{OP-background-failure}

 G_i=\{T_i\ne0,\ |Z_i|\le C_{\rm av}N^{2K_{\rm op}+1-\alpha}\}.
\]
Here \(C_{\rm av}\) is the fixed constant in the many-index estimate.

\[
 \begin{split}
 &\mathbb P\{\Lambda_N,\|M_N\|\le N^{K_{\rm op}},
       \operatorname{rank}M_N\ge N-1,\ s_{\min}(M_N)\le N^{-\alpha}\}\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\le Ce^{-cN}+\frac C N\sum_i\mathbb P(\Lambda_N,G_i).
 \end{split}
 \tag{OP-cofactor-average}
\]
Its usable indices have nonzero adjugate, including when the full matrix has exact corank one.
Thus no ratio is assigned at \(T_i=0\).
Full corank at least two was already charged separately.

For high matching, the final exponent order and (OP-background-tail) give

 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P(\Lambda_N,G_i,\mathcal H_i)
 \le\frac1N\sum_i

For low matching, first remove the full norm failure in (OP-full-norm), whose single probability bounds its contribution to the average.
Remove the simultaneous second-value and symmetric-part failures and the additional event \(\Lambda_N\cap\mathcal E_{\rm low,N}\) just bounded above.

There is no union over \(i\) in (OP-background-tail) or (OP-cofactor-average).

 \mathbb P\{\Lambda_N^c\ \text{or}\ s_{\min}(M_N)\le N^{-\alpha}\}

\]
The actual factorization and the individual background floors give

on the remaining good event.

The full exponent order is the requested probability powers, factor/shift/bulk exponents, full norm and subsidiary exponents, \(T_0,J,H\), the source and entropy constants, \(a\), \(\Theta\), \(\alpha\), then the final product exponent.

### Intermediate singular values with summable failure

This section uses only accepted inputs, independently of C194v22.
Fix \(z\in\mathbb C\) and set \(m_N=\lceil N^{3/4}\rceil\).
In the additive class, \(F_N=Q(W_2,\ldots,W_d)-zI\) is independent of \(W_1\).
C187v2, conditionally on the complete background, gives
\[
 a_k(P_N-zI)\ge c(k-1)/N\qquad(m_N+1\le k\le N)
 \tag{Add-middle}
\]
outside probability \(CN^2e^{-cN^{1/4}}\).
There is no norm premise on this shift, so no polynomial cutoff or \(Q\)-inverse is required for this application.

In the product class at \(z=0\), apply C187v2 at zero shift to all \(d\) original factors.
For \(d=1\) and arbitrary \(z\), apply it directly with deterministic shift \(-zI\).
For \(d\ge2,z\ne0\), retain the original single-factor invertibility/floor event for \(W_2,\ldots,W_d\), at any fixed failure power \(q_0>1\).
Define \(F_N=-z(W_2\cdots W_d)^{-1}\) whenever the carrier is invertible and by zero otherwise.
This is a background-measurable shift independent of \(W_1\).
C187v2 applies conditionally to \(W_1+F_N\) without a shift bound, and separately to \(W_2,\ldots,W_d\) at zero shift.
On the carrier event the actual factorization (OP-factorization) holds.
A finite union of these middle-value exceptions has probability \(CN^2e^{-cN^{1/4}}\); independence of the good events is not required.

For square \(U,V\) and \(r+s-1\le N\),
\[
 a_{r+s-1}(UV)\ge a_r(U)a_s(V).
 \tag{SV-product}
\]
Choose a subspace of codimension \(s-1\) on which \(V\) has the displayed lower norm bound, and intersect it with the inverse image under \(V\) of a subspace of codimension \(r-1\) for \(U\).
The intersection has codimension at most \(r+s-2\).
Min-max proves (SV-product), also when one of the matrices is singular.
Iteration for \(d\) factors gives the corresponding bound at index \(\sum_j r_j-(d-1)\).

For
\[
 k_N=2d(m_N+1)\le k\le N,
\]
distribute \(k+d-1\) among \(d\) integers \(r_j\) differing by at most one.
Then \(r_j\ge m_N+1\) and \(r_j-1\ge k/(2d)\) for sufficiently large \(N\).
Applying the middle estimate to each factor gives
\[
 a_k(P_N-zI)\ge c_0(k/N)^d\qquad(k_N\le k\le N)
 \tag{Product-middle}
\]
on the retained carrier and middle events.
The total exception probability is
\[
 O(N^{-q_0})+CN^2e^{-cN^{1/4}},
 \tag{Middle-failure}
\]
which is summable; in the zero-parameter or one-factor cases the first term is unnecessary.
This is the general fixed-\(d\) version of the accepted [three-factor middle argument](Yin_C_F0186_v23.md#summable-middle), with the actual original carriers and explicit indices.

Combining (Add-middle) and (Product-middle), there are fixed \(c_0>0\) and \(k_N=O(N^{3/4})\) such that eventually almost surely
\[
 a_k(P_N-zI)\ge c_0(k/N)^h\qquad(k_N\le k\le N),
 \quad
 h=1\ \text{or}\ d
 \tag{Common-middle}
\]
according to the class.
For the additive class take \(k_N=2(m_N+1)\).
First Borel--Cantelli uses only summability of the marginal failures and applies under every allowed cross-size coupling.

<a id="finite-free-target"></a>
### Finite free target at every fixed parameter before the new floor

Fix \(z\) and intersect the accepted eventual event (Common-middle) with C186v4's almost-sure Hermitized weak limit for the fixed polynomial \(P-z\).
The singular empirical laws then converge weakly to \(\nu_z=\mu_{|p-z|}\).
The middle profile implies, for small \(t>0\),
\[
 \frac1N\#\{k:a_k(P_N-zI)\le t\}
       \le \frac{k_N}{N}+C t^{1/h}.
\]
At each positive continuity point of the limiting distribution function, weak convergence gives \(\nu_z([0,t])\le Ct^{1/h}\).
Decrease continuity points to an arbitrary \(t\ge0\) to obtain (Two-class-free-small-values), including \(\nu_z(\{0\})=0\).
Consequently
\[
 \int_0^1(-\log x)\,d\nu_z(x)
       =\int_0^1\frac{\nu_z([0,t])}{t}\,dt<\infty.
\]
The bounded free operator \(p-z\) has finite positive logarithm.
Thus \(\tau\log|p-z|\in\mathbb R\) for every fixed \(z\), including the real axis and zero.

This conclusion is deterministic about the free law.
For each fixed parameter it follows from accepted weak and middle inputs with no use of (Two-class-floor).
It requires no bounded free inverse and no product formula for a potentially undefined logarithm.
The real and zero parameter branches therefore have exactly the finite target needed by the following full-sequence argument.

### Bottom, middle and positive logarithmic tails

For a fixed \(z\), use (Two-class-floor) with any \(b>1\).
First Borel--Cantelli gives a fixed \(A_z\) with
\[
 s_{\min}(P_N-zI)>N^{-A_z}
\]
eventually almost surely.
Intersect this with the accepted middle event, Hermitized weak convergence and C186v4's pathwise positive-logarithm tail event for the fixed polynomial \(P-z\).
No independence across \(N\) is used.
In the accepted one-/two-factor and zero-product branches, the floor input itself is already accepted, so this subsection has no unresolved counting dependency at those parameters.

For the bottom \(k_N=O(N^{3/4})\) singular values,
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{k\le k_N}(-\log a_k(P_N-zI))_+
       \le A_z\frac{k_N\log N}{N}
       =O(N^{-1/4}\log N)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Bottom-log}
\]
For \(k>k_N\) with \(a_k<t<1\), (Common-middle) implies \(k\le CNt^{1/h}\) and
\[
 (-\log a_k)_+\le(-\log c_0)_++h\log(N/k).
\]
Summation, or comparison with the integral of \(\log(N/x)\), gives
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}
 \frac1N\sum_{\substack{k>k_N\\a_k<t}}(-\log a_k)
       \le C t^{1/h}(1+|\log t|).
 \tag{Middle-log}
\]
Together (Bottom-log) and (Middle-log) give pathwise uniform integrability of the negative singular-value logarithm at zero.

For fixed \(0<\delta<1<R\), the continuous clipped logarithm \(\min\{\log R,\max\{\log\delta,\log x\}\}\) converges in empirical integral by Hermitized weak convergence.
Remove the lower cutoff using the preceding negative-tail bounds and the finite free target.
Remove the upper cutoff using the [C186v4 pathwise positive-tail conclusion](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics).
Expected uniform integrability alone is not substituted for that pathwise input.
It follows that
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{k=1}^N\log a_k(P_N-zI)
       \longrightarrow \int\log x\,d\nu_z(x)=\log\Delta_\tau(p-z).
\]
The finite determinant identity identifies the left side with \(N^{-1}\log|\det(P_N-zI)|\), eventually finite by the floor.
This proves (Two-class-log) for every fixed complex parameter, with no exception on the real axis.

<a id="planar-fubini-brown"></a>
### One event of planar convergence and the Brown limit

Define
\[
 u_N(\omega,z)=\frac1N\log|\det(P_N(\omega)-zI_N)|,\qquad
 u(z)=\log\Delta_\tau(p-z).
\]
These functions are jointly measurable in sample and parameter, with the finite-\(N\) value \(-\infty\) allowed at eigenvalues.
For every sample, \(u_N\) is locally integrable in \(z\), since a characteristic polynomial has only finitely many logarithmic singularities and is not identically zero.
The free potential has its usual locally integrable representative, and the preceding argument identifies its finite value at every fixed parameter.

For each fixed \(z\), the fixed-parameter logarithmic limit has probability one.
Apply Tonelli to the failure indicator on each disk \(D_R=\{|z|<R\}\), with integer \(R\ge1\).
A countable intersection gives one probability-one event on which \(u_N(z)\to u(z)\) for planar-almost every \(z\).
This is not an uncountable intersection of fixed-parameter events.
There is no need for a measurable choice of the exponent \(A_z\), a compact-uniform least-value bound, or deletion of the real axis.

On that event intersect the accepted [C186v4 planar controls](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) for this fixed original scalar polynomial.
For every integer-radius disk,
\[
 \sup_{N\ {\rm sufficiently\ large}}\|u_N\|_{L^2(D_R)}<\infty,
 \qquad u\in L^2(D_R).
\]
Finite eigenvalue singularities do not affect these Lebesgue norms.
For every measurable \(E\subset D_R\), Cauchy--Schwarz bounds \(\int_E|u_N|\) by a pathwise constant times \(|E|^{1/2}\).
Thus the eventual sequence is uniformly integrable on the disk.
Almost-everywhere convergence then gives
\[
 u_N\longrightarrow u\quad\text{in }L^1(D_R)
 \tag{Planar-L1}
\]
on that same event.

The distributional identities
\[
 \Delta u_N=2\pi\mu_{P_N},\qquad \Delta u=2\pi\mu_p^{\rm Br}
\]
yield convergence against every compactly supported smooth test on each resulting path.
C186v4 also gives the eventual pathwise bound
\[
 \sup_{N\ {\rm sufficiently\ large}}
 \int(\log^+|\lambda|)^2\,d\mu_{P_N}(\lambda)<\infty.
\]
It implies tightness; finitely many initial measures do not affect tightness of the sequence.
The free Brown measure is a probability measure.
On each fixed path, smooth-test convergence and tightness imply weak convergence, hence convergence for every bounded continuous test.
This final implication is deterministic and introduces no further uncountable probability intersection.
It proves (Two-class-Brown).

The [C186v4 planar-defect equivalence](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#remaining-defect) is consistent with this assembly.
On the same countable control event, each fixed regularization converges locally uniformly, (Planar-L1) controls the unregularized potentials, and the free regularizations decrease to \(u\) in local \(L^1\).
Consequently
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
 \int_{D_R}(u_{N,\delta}-u_N)\,d^2z=0.
\]
No nonnormal eigenvalue stability under a low-rank perturbation is used.
