# Brown convergence from filled support of zero area and positivity at the free limit

This F0012 deduction for Yin on 2026-11-09 fixes the planar support argument developed in [the disk-average discussion](../drafts/Yin_D_F0009.md#thin-support) and applies it to products whose second factor is semidefinite only at the free tuple.
The full proof is given here with exact accepted inputs.
The earlier [two real-input classes and their adopted assessment](Yin_C_F0186_v24.md#decision-two-real-classes-reviewed) remain at their fixed version.
The present theorem uses the original complex-input finite-variance model and has the [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v24.md#free-semidefinite-whole-proof) selected below.
The general [F0012 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract) remains unresolved.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact statement

Use independent Hermitian Wigner inputs under the fixed-law finite-second-moment model of F0012.
Their off-diagonal laws may be real or complex, their real and imaginary parts need not be independent, their diagonal laws have finite variance, and the coupling across dimensions is arbitrary.
Let \(s=(s_1,\ldots,s_d)\) be free variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).
For any fixed scalar noncommutative polynomial \(P\), set \(P_N=P(W^{(N)})\), \(p=P(s)\), \(\mu_N=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{\lambda_j(P_N)}\), and \(\mu=\mu_p^{\mathrm{Br}}\).
Let \(G\) be the unbounded connected component of \(\mathbb C\setminus\operatorname{supp}\mu\), and set \(H=\mathbb C\setminus G\).

On one probability-one event for the fixed model and polynomial, the logarithmic potentials converge in \(L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(G)\).
Every subsequential weak limit \(\nu\) of \(\mu_N\) is supported in \(H\) and satisfies
\[
 U_\nu\le U_\mu\quad\text{almost everywhere on }\mathbb C,
 \qquad U_\nu=U_\mu\quad\text{on }G,
 \qquad
 U_\rho(z)=\int\log|z-w|\,d\rho(w).
 \tag{Support-order}
\]
If \(H\) has planar Lebesgue area zero, then on this same event
\[
 \mu_N\Longrightarrow\mu,
 \qquad
 U_{\mu_N}\longrightarrow U_\mu
 \text{ in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb C).
 \tag{Thin-limit}
\]
The weak convergence holds against every bounded continuous test function on that one event.

In particular, fix scalar noncommutative polynomials \(A=A^*\) and \(B=B^*\), allowing arbitrary shared variables and repetitions.
Assume only that \(b=B(s)\) is positive semidefinite or negative semidefinite in the free algebra.
Then for every fixed one-variable complex polynomial \(g\),
\[
 \mu_{g(A(W^{(N)})B(W^{(N)}))}
 \Longrightarrow \mu_{g(A(s)B(s))}^{\mathrm{Br}}
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{Free-sign-limit}
\]
The same assertion holds with the order of \(A\) and \(B\) reversed.
The semidefinite factor may be zero or have a kernel.
There is no requirement that \(B(W^{(N)})\) have the same sign, or that its operator norm remain bounded.
The statement concerns weak Brown convergence and local planar potentials, with no fixed-parameter unregularized determinant conclusion asserted.

The free regularizations decrease to \(u=U_\mu\) in local planar \(L^1\).
On one event, \(\mu_N\) are tight and their positive logarithmic second moments obey an eventual pathwise bound
\[
 \sup_{N\ge N_0(\omega)}\int(\log^+|w|)^2\,d\mu_N(w)
 \le C(\omega)<\infty.
 \tag{Log-tail}
\]
The same accepted version supplies almost-sure weak spectral convergence for every fixed self-adjoint matrix polynomial.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Planar argument

Fix a sample in the countable intersection of the accepted control events just stated.
All subsequent compactness and potential arguments are deterministic on that sample.
For a bounded disk \(D\), put \(p_{N,D}=\int_D(u_N-u)_+\,d^2z\).
Since \(u_N\le u_{N,\delta_k}\) and \(u_{\delta_k}\ge u\),
\[
 \limsup_N p_{N,D}
 \le \int_D(u_{\delta_k}-u)\,d^2z.
\]
Sending \(k\) to infinity gives \(p_{N,D}\to0\) for every bounded disk.
It is enough to take integer-radius disks, whose conclusions dominate all smaller compact sets.

<a id="exterior-annuli"></a>
### Exterior annuli

Choose \(R>\max_{\operatorname{supp}\mu}|w|\).
The elementary circle-mean identity gives
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|re^{i\theta}-w|\,d\theta
 =\log\max(r,|w|).
\]
For every \(r>R\), therefore,
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}(u_N-u)(re^{i\theta})\,d\theta
 =\int\log^+(|w|/r)\,d\mu_N(w)\ge0.
\]
The logarithmic singularity on a circle is integrable, and the finite positive logarithmic moments justify these identities and radial integration.
For any annulus \(D_{r_2}\setminus\overline{D_{r_1}}\) with \(R<r_1<r_2\), the signed area integral of \(u_N-u\) is nonnegative.
Using \(|t|=2t_+-t\) gives
\[
 \int_{r_1<|z|<r_2}|u_N-u|\,d^2z
 \le 2p_{N,D_{r_2}}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Exterior-L1}
\]

<a id="subsequence-potentials"></a>
### Potentials of subsequential limits

Take any subsequence of dimensions.
Tightness supplies a further subsequence, still indexed by \(N\), on which \(\mu_N\Rightarrow\nu\) for a probability measure \(\nu\).
By lower semicontinuity and (Log-tail),
\[
 \int(\log^+|w|)^2\,d\nu(w)\le C(\omega).
\]
We claim that \(u_N\to U_\nu\) locally in planar \(L^1\) on this subsequence.
Fix a compact disk \(K\), a continuous cutoff \(0\le\chi_L\le1\) equal to one on \(D_L\) and zero outside \(D_{2L}\), and a lower cutoff \(M>0\).
For fixed \(L,M\),
\[
 k_{L,M}(z,w)=\chi_L(w)\max(\log|z-w|,-M)
\]
is bounded and continuous in \(w\) for every \(z\), with a common bound for \(z\in K\).
Weak convergence and dominated convergence in \(z\) give local \(L^1\) convergence of the corresponding truncated potentials.
For \(L\) sufficiently large compared with \(K\), the removed far part is bounded, for either \(\rho=\mu_N\) or \(\rho=\nu\), by
\[
 \int_{|w|>L}\int_K|\log|z-w||\,d^2z\,d\rho(w)
 \le C_K\int_{|w|>L}(1+\log|w|)\,d\rho(w)
 \le \frac{C_{K,\omega}}{\log L}.
\]
The error from the lower cutoff is uniform in \(w\), because
\[
 \int_K\bigl(\max(\log|z-w|,-M)-\log|z-w|\bigr)\,d^2z
 \le 2\pi\int_0^{e^{-M}}r\log(e^{-M}/r)\,dr
 =\frac\pi2e^{-2M}.
\]
It retains this bound after integration against either probability measure.
First let \(N\to\infty\), and then \(L,M\to\infty\).
This proves the claimed \(L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}\) convergence to \(U_\nu\).
The positive-part control implies \(U_\nu\le u\) almost everywhere.
By (Exterior-L1), the two potentials agree almost everywhere for \(|z|>R\).

<a id="unbounded-component"></a>
### The unbounded component

The potential \(U_\nu\) is subharmonic and locally integrable on the plane.
For completeness, its far kernel is locally continuous by the finite positive logarithmic moment, and the near logarithmic kernel gives the submean inequality and upper semicontinuity by truncation from above of its negative singularity.
The potential \(u\) is harmonic on \(G\), because \(\mu\) has no mass there.
Consequently \(v=U_\nu-u\) is subharmonic on \(G\).
Its almost-everywhere nonpositivity implies \(v\le0\) everywhere by its area submean inequality on each disk compactly contained in \(G\).
On the exterior region its almost-everywhere vanishing and upper semicontinuity also imply \(v=0\) everywhere.
The maximum principle therefore gives \(v=0\) throughout the connected set \(G\).
One can see this directly at a zero of \(v\), where its area submean inequality forces \(v=0\) almost everywhere in each sufficiently small disk, and upper semicontinuity and nonpositivity make it zero everywhere in that disk.
The zero set is both open and closed in \(G\), and is nonempty by the exterior calculation.

Thus \(U_\nu=u\) on \(G\).
The distributional identity \(\Delta U_\nu=2\pi\nu\) and harmonicity of \(u\) show that \(\nu(G)=0\), proving the support assertion.
Every original subsequence has a further subsequence of potentials converging to \(u\) in local \(L^1(G)\), which proves the full-sequence convergence there.
If \(H\) has area zero, \(U_\nu=u\) almost everywhere on the whole plane.
Taking distributional Laplacians gives \(\nu=\mu\).
Tightness and uniqueness of every subsequential weak limit imply \(\mu_N\Rightarrow\mu\).
The same truncation argument then proves local planar \(L^1\) convergence for the full sequence.
These pathwise arguments establish one event for all bounded continuous tests, proving (Thin-limit).

<a id="free-semidefinite-factor"></a>
## Semidefiniteness only at the free tuple

Write \(a=A(s)=a^*\) and first suppose \(b=B(s)\ge0\).
The positive square root \(c=b^{1/2}\) is bounded, even when \(b\) is singular.
Apply the accepted bounded cyclic Brown identity to \(u=ac\), \(v=c\).
It gives
\[
 \mu_{ab}^{\mathrm{Br}}=\mu_{cac}^{\mathrm{Br}}.
 \tag{Free-cyclic}
\]
The element \(cac\) is self-adjoint, so its Brown measure is its spectral probability measure and is compactly supported on the real axis.
The complement in the plane of a compact subset of the real axis is connected, so its filled support is that same compact set and has planar area zero.
Apply (Thin-limit) to the original scalar polynomial \(AB\).
This proves its almost-sure Brown convergence, with no sign requirement on its finite-dimensional factor \(B(W^{(N)})\).
If \(b\le0\), apply this argument to \(-a\) and \(-b\), whose product is again \(ab\).
It includes \(b=0\).
Finite and bounded-free cyclic identities also identify the eigenvalue measures and Brown measures when the order is reversed.

For a fixed one-variable complex polynomial \(g\), finite-dimensional spectral mapping gives \(\mu_{g(P_N)}=g_*\mu_{P_N}\).
The accepted bounded-free polynomial mapping gives \(\mu_{g(p)}^{\mathrm{Br}}=g_*\mu_p^{\mathrm{Br}}\), including constant \(g\).
If \(f\) is bounded and continuous on the plane, then \(f\circ g\) is bounded and continuous.
The established base weak convergence therefore proves (Free-sign-limit).
This argument does not require the filled support of the image under \(g\) to have zero area.

<a id="repeated-variable-example"></a>
## A repeated-variable example with an explicit Brown second moment

Take
\[
 A(x,y)=xy+yx,\qquad B(x,y)=5-x^2,
 \qquad P(x,y)=(xy+yx)(5-x^2).
\]
For free standard semicircular \(x,y\), \(\|x\|=2\), so \(b=5-x^2\ge1\).
The theorem proves Brown convergence for \(P\), and for every outer polynomial \(g(P)\), under every allowed real or complex finite-variance input model and every cross-size coupling.
Both factors can depend on the same input, and the displayed polynomial has no fresh additive input in the sense of C186v24.
The earlier scalar certificate \(AC^*C\) is sufficient in its own class, while here the specified sign test is on the free evaluation alone.

The Brown second moment has the exact value
\[
 m=\int|z|^2\,d\mu_{P(s)}^{\mathrm{Br}}(z)
 =\tau(abab)=24,
 \qquad a=xy+yx,\quad b=5-x^2.
 \tag{Example-moment}
\]
Indeed (Free-cyclic) identifies this moment with \(\tau((b^{1/2}ab^{1/2})^2)=\tau(abab)\).
Freeness and the variance-one semicircular law of \(y\) give
\[
 \tau(f_0(x)y f_1(x)y f_2(x))
 =\tau(f_1(x))\tau(f_0(x)f_2(x))
\]
for polynomials \(f_0,f_1,f_2\).
Expanding the two copies of \(a\), the terms containing \(\tau(xb)\) vanish by symmetry and the other two give
\[
 \tau(abab)=2\tau(b)\tau(x^2b)
 =2(5-1)(5-2)=24.
\]
The same calculation gives
\[
 \tau((ab)^*(ab))
 =\tau(a^2b^2)
 =\tau(x^2b^2)+\tau(b^2)
 =(25-20+5)+(25-10+2)=27.
\]
Here the standard semicircular moments used are \(\tau(x^2)=1\), \(\tau(x^4)=2\), and \(\tau(x^6)=5\).
The difference between these two moments proves that \(ab\) is nonnormal, since a normal element's Brown measure is its spectral measure and its radial second moment equals \(\tau(p^*p)\).
For an elementary finite-dimensional witness, take \(X=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\) and \(Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\).
Then \(P(X,Y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&4\\5&0\end{pmatrix}\) is nonnormal.
The bound \(\|ab\|\le40\) follows from \(\|a\|\le8\), \(\|b\|\le5\).
Consequently for every fixed \(R>40\), weak convergence also gives
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\min(|\lambda_j(P_N)|^2,R^2)
 \longrightarrow24\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
This last assertion uses a bounded continuous test and assumes no convergence of high untruncated finite-matrix moments.

<a id="finite-factor-indefinite"></a>
## The finite-dimensional factor can be eventually indefinite

The free sign condition can hold even when the finite-dimensional factor is indefinite for all sufficiently large dimensions almost surely.
For the first Wigner input, let \(R_0\) have the Pareto law \(\mathbb P\{R_0>t\}=t^{-3}\) for \(t\ge1\), let \(\epsilon\) be an independent symmetric sign, and set \(\xi=\epsilon R_0/\sqrt3\).
Then \(\mathbb E\xi=0\) and \(\mathbb E\xi^2=1\), since \(\mathbb E R_0^2=3\).
Choose zero diagonal entries, and use any independent allowed second Wigner input.
For the \(N(N-1)/2\) independent off-diagonal coordinates,
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\max_{i<j}|W_{1,ij}^{(N)}|\le\sqrt5\right\}
 =\left(1-(15N)^{-3/2}\right)^{N(N-1)/2}
 \le \exp(-c\sqrt N)
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\) and a fixed \(c>0\).
This upper bound is summable, so first Borel--Cantelli applies for every permitted coupling of the matrix sizes.
Eventually \(\|W_1^{(N)}\|>\sqrt5\), hence \(5I-(W_1^{(N)})^2\) has a negative eigenvalue.
The accepted almost-sure semicircle convergence gives eigenvalues of \(W_1^{(N)}\) in \((-1,1)\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), so the same factor has a positive eigenvalue.
It is therefore eventually indefinite, while \(5-s_1^2\ge1\) and the asserted Brown convergence for the displayed polynomial still follows.
