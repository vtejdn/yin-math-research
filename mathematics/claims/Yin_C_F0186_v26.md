# Brown convergence in probability for a real fourth-moment two-sided Wigner product

This theorem completes the direct Hermitization assembly for \(A_NB_N+2B_NA_N\) with real finite-fourth-moment off-diagonal laws and finite-second-moment diagonals.
It combines the adopted probability floor and shrinking squared-singular-value counts with the finite-variance logarithmic and planar controls.
The proof establishes pointwise potential convergence in probability, expected local planar \(L^1\) convergence and weak Brown-measure convergence in probability.
It also gives an almost-sure deterministic-subsequence conclusion, with its quantifiers stated separately from convergence of the full sequence.
The [v25 result](Yin_C_F0186_v25.md#statement) remains fixed at its different semidefinite and thin-support scope.
The complete statement and argument are adopted with the [independent whole-proof finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v25.md#real-two-sided-brown-probability-whole-proof).

## Exact model and conclusions

For each \(N\), let \(A_N,B_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
Their strict upper entries are independent copies of \(\xi_A/\sqrt N,\xi_B/\sqrt N\), respectively, where the fixed real laws are centered, have variance one and have finite fourth moments.
Their real diagonal coordinates are independent copies of fixed centered finite-second-moment laws divided by \(\sqrt N\).
All upper pairs, diagonal coordinates and input colors are independent within each size.
The coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
Set
\[
 T_N=A_NB_N+2B_NA_N,\qquad T=s_1s_2+2s_2s_1,
\]
where \(s_1,s_2\) are standard free semicircular operators in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).
Let
\[
 \mu_N=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{\lambda_j(T_N)},\qquad
 \mu=\mu_T^{\mathrm{Br}},
\]
where finite eigenvalues are counted with algebraic multiplicity.
Define the unregularized potentials and squared-singular laws by
\[
 u_N(z)=\frac1N\log|\det(T_N-zI)|
       =\frac12\int_{[0,\infty)}\log x\,d\rho_{N,z}(x),
\]
\[
 \rho_{N,z}=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{s_j(T_N-zI)^2},\qquad
 \rho_z=\mu_{(T-z)^*(T-z)},\qquad
 u(z)=\tau\log|T-z|=\frac12\int\log x\,d\rho_z(x).
\]
The finite potential may equal \(-\infty\) at an eigenvalue, and planar norms refer to its locally integrable equivalence class.
The free potential is the Brown logarithmic potential \(U_\mu\).
It is finite for every \(z\ne0\), as follows from the adopted free-log/count input and the check below.

There is a deterministic planar set \(Z_*\subset\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R\) of full Lebesgue measure such that for every fixed \(z\in Z_*\),
\[
 u_N(z)\longrightarrow u(z)\quad\text{in probability}.
 \tag{Pointwise-potential-probability}
\]
For every compact planar set \(K\),
\[
 \mathbb E\|u_N-u\|_{L^1(K,dA)}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Expected-planar-L1}
\]
In particular, the local planar potentials converge in probability in \(L^1\).
No expectation convergence of an unregularized logarithmic determinant at an individual fixed parameter is asserted.

<a id="bounded-lipschitz-definition"></a>
**Definition (bounded-Lipschitz metric).**
For probability measures on \(\mathbb C\) with its Euclidean distance, define
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\nu,\mu)=
 \sup_{\substack{f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb R\,,\ \|f\|_\infty\le1\\
                  |f(z)-f(w)|\le|z-w|}}
       \left|\int f\,d\nu-\int f\,d\mu\right|.
\]
Then
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_N,\mu)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability},\qquad
 \mathbb E d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_N,\mu)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Brown-probability}
\]
Equivalently, the empirical eigenvalue probability measures converge weakly in probability to the Brown measure of \(T\).
In particular, for every fixed bounded continuous \(f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\),
\(\int f\,d\mu_N\to\int f\,d\mu\) in probability.

There is also the following precise subsequence consequence.
For every deterministic subsequence \((N_j)\), there exists a further deterministic subsequence \((N_{j_k})\) and one probability-one event, allowed to depend on this chosen subsequence, on which simultaneously
\[
 u_{N_{j_k}}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb C),
 \qquad \mu_{N_{j_k}}\Longrightarrow\mu.
 \tag{Deterministic-subsequence-AS}
\]
On that event weak convergence holds against every bounded continuous test function.
The further indices do not depend on the sample.
This is not an almost-sure convergence claim for the full sequence.
The theorem concerns this real fourth-moment subcase; it does not establish the general complex finite-variance Task.

## Complete proof

<a id="fixed-parameter-count"></a>
### Squared counts and the free logarithm

Choose \(Z_*\) to be the full-area set in C194v30, already disjoint from the real axis.
In particular, every \(z\in Z_*\) is nonzero, so C195v1 applies to the fixed nonconstant polynomial \(P(a,b)=ab+2ba\), for which \(P(0,0)=0\).
For every fixed \(z\ne0\), that count theorem gives constants \(a_z,\gamma_z,C_z>0\) such that, after reducing \(\gamma_z\) if necessary,
\[
 \mathbb E\rho_{N,z}([0,t])\le C_zt^{a_z},\qquad
 N^{-\gamma_z}\le t\le1,\qquad 0<\gamma_z<14.
 \tag{Squared-shrinking-count}
\]
Here \(a_z\) is the theorem's \(\beta_z/2\); reducing its original squared-scale exponent only shrinks the range in which the bound is required.
The constants are allowed to depend on this fixed \(z\).
No uniform choice of them over planar compact sets will be used.

The adopted weak theorem gives \(\rho_{N,z}\Rightarrow\rho_z\) almost surely and also convergence of the expected measures for this fixed parameter.
For \(0<t<1/2\), choose a continuous cutoff equal to one on \([0,t]\), between zero and one, and zero above \(2t\).
Expected weak convergence and (Squared-shrinking-count) imply
\[
 \rho_z([0,t])\le C_z(2t)^{a_z}.
 \tag{Free-small-mass}
\]
The free measure has compact support because \(T-z\) is bounded.
The bound excludes an atom at zero and, by layer integration, makes its negative logarithm integrable.
It follows that \(u(z)\) is finite for every \(z\ne0\), consistently with the free-log input already retained in C195v1.
Moreover,
\[
 \int_{[0,\varepsilon)}|\log x|\,d\rho_z(x)
 \le C_z'\varepsilon^{a_z}(1+|\log\varepsilon|)
 \longrightarrow0\quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
 \tag{Free-negative-tail}
\]
This verification uses the count only at fixed positive scales when taking the weak limit.

<a id="negative-log-in-probability"></a>
### Negative logarithms are controlled on the floor event

Fix \(z\in Z_*\), suppress it from the notation, and put
\[
 t_N=N^{-\gamma_z},\qquad
 \mathcal A_N=\{s_{\min}(T_N-zI)>N^{-7}\}.
\]
C194v30 gives \(\mathbb P(\mathcal A_N^c)\to0\).
On \(\mathcal A_N\), every point of the squared-singular law is greater than \(N^{-14}\).
For \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), set
\[
 L_N(\varepsilon)=\int_{[0,\varepsilon)}|\log x|\,d\rho_N(x),
\]
with the value \(+\infty\) permitted if zero has positive mass.
For all sufficiently large \(N\), \(t_N<\varepsilon\).
On \(\mathcal A_N\), the contribution below \(t_N\) is at most
\[
 14(\log N)\rho_N([0,t_N]).
\]
For the remaining interval, layer integration gives the unconditional deterministic bound
\[
 \int_{[t_N,\varepsilon)}|\log x|\,d\rho_N(x)
 \le |\log\varepsilon|\rho_N([0,\varepsilon])
       +\int_{t_N}^{\varepsilon}\rho_N([0,t])\,\frac{dt}{t}.
\]
These endpoint conventions only enlarge the right side.
Applying (Squared-shrinking-count) yields
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl[\mathbf1_{\mathcal A_N}L_N(\varepsilon)\bigr]
 \le14C_z(\log N)N^{-\gamma_za_z}
      +C_z'\varepsilon^{a_z}(1+|\log\varepsilon|).
 \tag{Floor-restricted-negative-bound}
\]
The random variable inside this expectation is defined to be zero off \(\mathcal A_N\); this convention does not integrate an infinite logarithm on a singular or extremely ill-conditioned exceptional event.
The expectation of the unregularized logarithm without this restriction is not used.
For every \(\eta>0\), Markov's inequality on the good event now gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{L_N(\varepsilon)>\eta\}
 \le\mathbb P(\mathcal A_N^c)
 +\eta^{-1}\left[14C_z(\log N)N^{-\gamma_za_z}
       +C_z'\varepsilon^{a_z}(1+|\log\varepsilon|)\right].
\]
Therefore
\[
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
       \mathbb P\{L_N(\varepsilon)>\eta\}=0.
 \tag{Negative-tail-in-probability}
\]
This is negative-log uniform integrability in probability at this fixed parameter, not an expectation bound on the full negative tail.

<a id="pointwise-proof"></a>
### Clipped weak limits give pointwise potential convergence

For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), define the lower-clipped potential
\[
 V_N(\varepsilon)=\frac12\int\log\max(x,\varepsilon)\,d\rho_{N,z}(x),
 \qquad V(\varepsilon)=\frac12\int\log\max(x,\varepsilon)\,d\rho_z(x).
\]
The double clipping
\(\tfrac12\log\max(\varepsilon,\min(x,R))\), with \(R>\max(1,\varepsilon)\), is bounded and continuous.
The fixed Hermitized weak law gives convergence of its integrals almost surely and in expectation.
The removed upper part is bounded by \(\frac12\int_{x>R}\log x\,d\rho_{N,z}(x)\).
Its expectation tends to zero uniformly in \(N\) as \(R\to\infty\), by C186v4's positive logarithmic tail bound, with the change from singular values to their squares included in this factor one half.
The free squared-singular law has compact support.
It follows that \(V_N(\varepsilon)\to V(\varepsilon)\) in probability, and also in expectation, for every fixed lower clipping level.

The free negative-tail estimate implies \(V(\varepsilon)\downarrow u(z)\) as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the finite lower-clipping error satisfies, in the extended-real sense,
\[
 0\le V_N(\varepsilon)-u_N(z)
 =\frac12\int_{[0,\varepsilon)}\log(\varepsilon/x)\,d\rho_{N,z}(x)
 \le\frac12L_N(\varepsilon).
\]
Combine the fixed-clipping convergence with (Negative-tail-in-probability), first taking \(N\to\infty\) and then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
This proves (Pointwise-potential-probability) for every fixed \(z\in Z_*\).
No passage through \(\mathbb E u_N(z)\) has occurred.

<a id="planar-null-sets"></a>
### Planar square bounds and measurable finite versions

For every sample at every fixed size, the characteristic polynomial of \(T_N\) is monic up to sign and has finitely many zeros.
The determinant expression makes \((\omega,z)\mapsto u_N(\omega,z)\) jointly measurable as an extended-real function.
Its set of infinite negative values is exactly that finite eigenvalue set in each planar fiber.
Tonelli's theorem applied to its indicator shows that this set has \(\mathbb P\otimes dA\) measure zero on every bounded disk.
A countable union over \(N\) and integer disks remains a product null set.
The free Brown potential is measurable and locally square integrable, so the set where it is not finite is a deterministic planar null set.

For product-space calculations, set \(\widetilde u_N=0\) on its zero-determinant set and equal to \(u_N\) elsewhere, and similarly replace the nonfinite values of \(u\) by zero to obtain \(\widetilde u\).
These are jointly measurable finite versions outside a product null set, and they represent the same local planar \(L^1\) and \(L^2\) classes.
For almost every fixed \(z\), the replacement changes none of the random variables outside a probability-zero event.
Thus the established pointwise convergence in probability holds for these versions at almost every planar parameter.

The precise C186v4 input is
\[
 \sup_N\mathbb E\int_K|\widetilde u_N(z)|^2\,dA(z)<\infty,
 \qquad \int_K|\widetilde u(z)|^2\,dA(z)<\infty
 \tag{Expected-local-square-control}
\]
for every compact \(K\).
Its kernel calculation is compatible with the present infinite values: for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 \int_K|\log|z-\lambda||^2\,dA(z)
 \le C_K\bigl(1+(\log^+|\lambda|)^2\bigr),
\]
and the pole is integrable since \(\int_0^1r|\log r|^2dr<\infty\).
Jensen's inequality for the finite eigenvalue potential, together with the positive logarithmic eigenvalue bound proved from Cauchy--Binet and singular tails in C186v4, gives the first estimate in (Expected-local-square-control).
The free estimate follows from compact support of the Brown measure and the same kernel bound.
The proof uses positive logarithmic moments, not a second eigenvalue modulus-moment lower bound.
In particular, the expected planar square bound applies across the floor-failure events that were deliberately excluded from the fixed-parameter negative-log expectation.

<a id="expected-planar-proof"></a>
### Product-measure convergence upgrades to expected local L1

Fix a compact \(K\) and \(\eta>0\), and define
\[
 \mathcal E_{N,\eta}=
 \{(\omega,z):z\in K,\ |\widetilde u_N(\omega,z)-\widetilde u(z)|>\eta\}.
\]
Its sections are measurable.
Pointwise convergence in probability at almost every \(z\), followed by dominated convergence in \(z\), gives
\[
 (\mathbb P\otimes dA)(\mathcal E_{N,\eta})
 =\int_K\mathbb P\{|\widetilde u_N(z)-\widetilde u(z)|>\eta\}\,dA(z)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
The constants in the preceding fixed-parameter count argument need not be uniform or chosen measurably in \(z\) for this step; the bounded probability integrand is what is integrated.
From (Expected-local-square-control),
\[
 \sup_N\mathbb E\int_K|\widetilde u_N-\widetilde u|^2\,dA\le C_K<\infty.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz on the product measure space therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\int_K|\widetilde u_N-\widetilde u|\,dA
 &\le\eta\,|K|
   +C_K^{1/2}(\mathbb P\otimes dA)(\mathcal E_{N,\eta})^{1/2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
First let \(N\to\infty\), then let \(\eta\downarrow0\).
The finite versions have the same planar norms as the original locally integrable potentials, proving (Expected-planar-L1).
Markov's inequality also yields local planar \(L^1\) convergence in probability.
This argument concerns the product-space norm and does not assert pointwise unregularized expectation convergence at a specified parameter.

<a id="distributional-tests"></a>
### Distributional Laplacians and a countable determining class

For a finite matrix, the determinant/eigenvalue identity gives
\[
 u_N(z)=\int\log|z-w|\,d\mu_N(w)
\]
as a locally integrable planar function, including nonnormal matrices and algebraic multiplicities.
The distributional identity \(\Delta\log|z-w|=2\pi\delta_w\) and the Brown potential convention therefore give
\[
 \mu_N=\frac1{2\pi}\Delta u_N,\qquad
 \mu=\frac1{2\pi}\Delta u.
\]
For every fixed \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb C)\),
\[
 \left|\int\phi\,d\mu_N-\int\phi\,d\mu\right|
 \le\frac{\|\Delta\phi\|_\infty}{2\pi}
       \|u_N-u\|_{L^1(\operatorname{supp}\phi)}.
\]
Taking expectations proves convergence in \(L^1\), hence in probability, against every such fixed test.
One may fix a countable uniformly dense family of smooth compactly supported tests on successive disks, allowing a slightly larger disk for each support.
Such a family determines finite Radon measures through uniform approximation of compactly supported continuous functions.
It supplies a countable determining class for the distributional limits.
To obtain weak convergence of probability measures, rather than only vague convergence, retain the one-event pathwise tightness of \(\mu_N\) proved in C186v4.
The expected-planar argument does not discard that existing control event.

<a id="deterministic-subsequence-proof"></a>
### Every deterministic subsequence has an almost-sure further subsequence

Let \((N_j)\) be any prescribed deterministic subsequence and let \(D_k=\{z:|z|\le k\}\).
By (Expected-planar-L1), choose increasing deterministic indices \(j_k\) such that
\[
 \mathbb E\|u_{N_{j_k}}-u\|_{L^1(D_k)}\le2^{-2k}.
\]
These choices use only deterministic expectations, not observed samples.
Markov's inequality bounds the probability that this norm exceeds \(2^{-k}\) by \(2^{-k}\).
First Borel--Cantelli gives one probability-one event on which the inequalities
\(\|u_{N_{j_k}}-u\|_{L^1(D_k)}\le2^{-k}\) hold eventually.
For each fixed \(m\), the norm on \(D_m\) is no larger once \(k\ge m\), so all integer disks, and consequently all compact sets, have local \(L^1\) convergence on this same event.

Intersect it with the C186v4 probability-one event of tightness for the full sequence \(\mu_N\).
Fix a sample in that intersection.
The Laplacian estimate gives convergence against all smooth compactly supported tests on this sample, without a test-dependent exceptional set.
Every weak cluster point of the tight sequence \(\mu_{N_{j_k}}\) must therefore equal \(\mu\), because the countable determining class, or equivalently all smooth tests, determines the measure.
Tightness and uniqueness of all cluster points imply weak convergence of the whole selected further sequence to \(\mu\).
Thus the convergence holds against every bounded continuous test on that one sample event, proving (Deterministic-subsequence-AS).
No independence between sizes was used in this extraction or in Borel--Cantelli.

<a id="bounded-lipschitz-proof"></a>
### Weak convergence in probability in a measurable metric

The bounded-Lipschitz class in the statement is separable in the topology of uniform convergence on compact disks.
Indeed its restrictions to each disk are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous, and a diagonal compactness argument over integer disks gives a compact metrizable family in that topology.
Choose a countable dense subclass once and for all.
For any two probability measures, the integral difference of a sequence from this class converging uniformly on compacts to a member of the full class converges to that member's integral difference: the functions are uniformly bounded by one and converge pointwise, so bounded convergence applies.
Consequently the supremum defining \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\) equals its supremum over this countable subclass and is a measurable random variable when evaluated at \(\mu_N\).
It is bounded by two.

On \(\mathbb C\), this metric metrizes weak convergence of probability measures.
The direction used here can be seen directly: if a sequence converges weakly, its tails and the limit's tails are uniformly small outside a sufficiently large disk, while the restrictions of the bounded-Lipschitz class to that disk have a finite uniform net.
Convergence for the finitely many net functions controls the supremum, and then the disk-tail error and net mesh are sent to zero.
Conversely, bounded-Lipschitz cutoff functions give tightness from metric convergence to a probability measure, and compact-set approximation extends convergence to bounded continuous tests.
Thus the almost-sure weak convergence on each extracted further subsequence also gives almost-sure convergence of its \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\) distance to zero.

If (Brown-probability) failed in probability, there would be \(\varepsilon,\eta>0\) and a deterministic subsequence with
\(\mathbb P\{d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_{N_j},\mu)>\varepsilon\}\ge\eta\) throughout.
Apply the preceding deterministic extraction to that subsequence.
On its further subsequence the metric tends to zero almost surely, so bounded convergence of the event indicators makes those probabilities tend to zero, a contradiction.
Hence \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_N,\mu)\to0\) in probability.
Finally,
\[
 \mathbb E d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_N,\mu)
 \le\varepsilon+2\mathbb P\{d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_N,\mu)>\varepsilon\}
\]
proves its expectation convergence after \(N\to\infty\) and \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
Weak-topology continuity at the deterministic limit measure gives convergence in probability against every fixed bounded continuous test.

<a id="limits"></a>
## Limits of the completed assembly

The theorem proves Brown convergence in probability for the stated real fourth-moment model, together with expected local planar potential convergence and the exact deterministic-subsequence consequence.
The adopted floor currently supplies a vanishing probability, without a summable bound.
The proof never assumes that an exceptional fixed-parameter unregularized logarithm is integrable, and it makes no such expectation assertion as a conclusion.
The planar expected norm is justified separately by logarithmic kernel integrability and the full product-measure square bound.
Neither the subsequence extraction nor the existing almost-sure weak Hermitized laws upgrade the full unregularized Brown sequence to almost-sure convergence here.
That upgrade remains a separate question requiring additional control.
