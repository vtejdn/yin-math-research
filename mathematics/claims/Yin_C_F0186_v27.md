# Global deep clipping and the exact remaining almost-sure Brown defect

This theorem proves an explicit almost-sure approximation to the Brown logarithmic potential under third off-diagonal and second diagonal moments.
It isolates the remaining almost-sure Brown question in the normalized logarithmic loss from singular values below one deterministic global cutoff.
For the real fourth-moment polynomial \(AB+2BA\), it also identifies summable intermediate errors and proves that the remaining planar loss already tends to zero in expectation.
The adopted [Brown convergence in probability](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#statement) remains fixed; full-sequence almost-sure Brown convergence is not asserted here.
The complete statement and argument are adopted with the [independent whole-proof finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v26.md#global-clipping-defect-whole-proof).

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

Fix \(d\ge1\).
For each input color \(j\), let the strict upper entries of \(W_j^{(N)}\) be independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where the fixed complex law obeys
\[
 \mathbb E\xi_j=0,\qquad \mathbb E|\xi_j|^2=1,\qquad
 \mathbb E|\xi_j|^3<\infty.
\]
The lower entries are their conjugate copies.
The real diagonal entries are independent copies of a fixed centered real law \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\) with \(\mathbb E\zeta_j^2<\infty\).
All upper pairs, diagonal entries and input colors are independent within each size; coupling of the whole tuples across sizes is arbitrary.
Real laws and dependence between the real and imaginary parts of one off-diagonal entry are allowed.

Let \(P\) be any fixed nonconstant scalar noncommutative polynomial with complex coefficients in these selfadjoint inputs.
Put \(T_N=P(W^{(N)})\), \(T=P(s_1,\ldots,s_d)\), where the \(s_j\) are standard free semicircular operators in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).
Use
\[
 \mu_N=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{\lambda_j(T_N)},\quad
 \mu=\mu_T^{\mathrm{Br}},\quad
 \rho_{N,z}=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{s_j(T_N-zI)^2},
\]
\[
 u_N(z)=N^{-1}\log|\det(T_N-zI)|,\qquad
 u(z)=\tau\log|T-z|=U_\mu(z).
\]
Algebraic multiplicities are used for the eigenvalues.
Finite potentials are understood as locally integrable planar functions, allowing the value \(-\infty\) on the finite eigenvalue set.

<a id="clipping-definition"></a>
**Definition (clipped potential and excess logarithmic loss).**
Fix any positive deterministic sequence \(L_N\to\infty\) with
\(\log L_N/\log N\to0\).
One explicit choice is \(L_N=\exp\sqrt{\log(N+1)}\).
Define
\[
 V_N(z)=\frac12\int\log\max(x,e^{-2L_N})\,d\rho_{N,z}(x),
 \qquad
 D_N(z)=\frac1N\sum_{s_j(T_N-zI)<e^{-L_N}}
                  \log\frac{e^{-L_N}}{s_j(T_N-zI)}.
 \tag{Global-clipped-potential-and-defect}
\]
In planar integrals set \(D_N=0\) on the finite eigenvalue set; elsewhere \(D_N=V_N-u_N\ge0\).
This convention is measurable and changes no integral.

The following conclusions hold under the displayed model.

1. For every fixed \(z\ne P(0)\), \(V_N(z)\to u(z)\) almost surely and \(\mathbb EV_N(z)\to u(z)\).
   There is one probability-one event on which \(V_N\to u\) in \(L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb C)\).
   For every compact planar set \(K\),
   \[
    \mathbb E\|V_N-u\|_{L^1(K)}\longrightarrow0.
   \]
   No least-singular-value estimate or operator-norm guard is an input.
2. On the finite-variance control event of C186v4 and the preceding clipping event,
   \[
    \mu_N\Longrightarrow\mu
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \int_{\{|z|<R\}}D_N(z)\,dA(z)\longrightarrow0
       \quad\text{for every }R\in\mathbb N.
   \]
   For the fixed sequence of within-size marginal laws, almost-sure weak Brown convergence under every allowed cross-size coupling is equivalent to
   \[
    \sum_N\mathbb P\left\{\int_{\{|z|<R\}}D_N(z)\,dA(z)>b\right\}<\infty
       \quad(R\in\mathbb N,\ b\in\mathbb Q_{>0}).
    \tag{Exact-every-coupling-defect-target}
   \]
   The series is an exact remaining condition, not an established estimate.
3. At a fixed \(z\), let \(a_z,\gamma_z>0\) be any constants for which
   \(\mathbb E\rho_{N,z}([0,t])\le C_zt^{a_z}\) for \(N^{-\gamma_z}\le t\le1\), as supplied by C195.
   The pointwise almost-sure and expectation conclusions in item 1 also hold with the faster height \(L_N=N^\eta\) whenever
   \(0<\eta<\min(\gamma_za_z,1/2)\).
   This is a fixed-parameter assertion and does not choose a common positive \(\eta\) on the plane.

There are these additional conclusions in the separate real fourth-moment subcase \(d=2\), \(P(x,y)=xy+2yx\).
The two off-diagonal laws are real, centered, variance one and have finite fourth moments; diagonals and all independence/coupling conventions remain as above.

4. For the global subpolynomial height,
   \[
    \mathbb E\int_KD_N(z)\,dA(z)\longrightarrow0
   \]
   for every compact planar set \(K\), by the adopted C186v26 expected planar convergence.
   This does not imply the summability in item 2.
5. Fix \(z\in Z_0\cap\sigma(s_1s_2+2s_2s_1)\setminus\mathbb R\), a fixed norm bound \(K\), a fixed bound on physical deletions, and \(L<\infty\).
   Here \(Z_0\) is the deterministic full-area finite-invertibility set of C197.
   Use \(F_2,\Delta\) from the exact [C194v30 minor data](Yin_C_F0194_v30.md#conditional-statement), with the original ambient normalization and a fixed measurable singular-vector convention.
   Let \(B_N(a,L)\) mean that at least one permitted minor on its own norm guard is singular, has \(F_2<L\sqrt N\), or has \(\Delta>a\).
   Then
   \[
    \sum_N\mathbb P(B_N(a,L))<\infty\qquad(a>0),
   \]
   and some deterministic nonincreasing \(\alpha_N\downarrow0\), \(\alpha_N\ge N^{-1}\), obeys
   \(\sum_N\mathbb P(B_N(\alpha_N,L))<\infty\).
   The paired row-norm error \(e_N\) in C194v30 also satisfies \(\sum_Ne_N<\infty\).
   No decay rate for \(\alpha_N\) is claimed.
6. There are admissible real fourth-moment off-diagonal laws with zero diagonals for which
   \(\sum_N\mathbb P\{\|W^{(N)}\|>K\}=\infty\) for every fixed \(K>0\).
   Thus an eventual fixed-norm argument cannot remove C194v30's guard under its every-coupling hypothesis.
   The example does not contradict Brown convergence.
   The supplied matching bound, its lack of automatic many-index amplification, and the precise arithmetic limitations are retained in the complete argument.

## Complete argument

The proof below retains every deduction needed for the statement.
Rolling draft links identify earlier comparisons only; no mathematical premise is left in those drafts.

<a id="complete-convergence-geometry"></a>
### The existing guarded geometry has summable marginal failures

The argument reuses the every-coupling criterion already retained in [the earlier D9 comparison](../drafts/Yin_D_F0009.md#wigner-lower-bound); its application to the actual guarded geometry is the additional deduction here.
Let \(X_N\) be the whole input tuple at size \(N\), with its prescribed within-size law \(\mathcal P_N\).
For any measurable events \(B_N=B_N(X_N)\), the following implication holds:
if \(B_N\) occurs only finitely often almost surely under every coupling of the \(\mathcal P_N\), then
\[
 \sum_N\mathcal P_N(B_N)<\infty.
 \tag{Every-coupling-complete-convergence}
\]
Indeed, independent sampling of the whole tuples across sizes is an allowed coupling.
If the series diverged, independence and the second Borel--Cantelli lemma would give infinitely many \(B_N\) almost surely under that coupling, a contradiction.
Conversely, the displayed series implies eventual avoidance under every coupling by the first Borel--Cantelli lemma.
Only this elementary marginal argument is used; it does not introduce cross-size independence into the final model.

Fix \(z\in Z_0\cap\sigma(s_1s_2+2s_2s_1)\setminus\mathbb R\), a fixed norm bound \(K\), and a fixed bound on the number of physical deletions.
Use \(F_2\) and \(\Delta\) from [C194v30's exact good-minor data](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v30.md#conditional-statement), with original normalization retained.
For \(a>0\) and \(L<\infty\), let \(B_N(a,L)\) mean that some permitted minor is on its own norm guard but is singular, has \(F_2<L\sqrt N\), or has \(\Delta>a\).
Tied singular vectors can be chosen by any fixed measurable convention; the existing estimates are uniform in these choices.
Finite invertibility on \(Z_0\), the guarded uniform divergence \(F_2^2/N\to\infty\), and the guarded uniform convergence \(\Delta\to0\) in C197v2 imply eventual avoidance of \(B_N(a,L)\) under every allowed coupling.
Consequently
\[
 \sum_N\mathbb P(B_N(a,L))<\infty
 \qquad(a>0, L<\infty).
 \tag{Fixed-tolerance-geometry-sum}
\]
This concerns guarded failures and asserts nothing about the frequency of the norm guards themselves.

For the fixed all-profile constant \(L\), one may choose a deterministic nonincreasing \(\alpha_N\downarrow0\), with \(\alpha_N\ge N^{-1}\), such that
\[
 \sum_N\mathbb P(B_N(\alpha_N,L))<\infty.
 \tag{Slow-geometry-sum}
\]
Choose increasing integers \(N_j\ge j\) so that
\(\sum_{N\ge N_j}\mathbb P(B_N(1/j,L))\le2^{-j}\).
Set \(\alpha_N=1/j\) for \(N_j\le N<N_{j+1}\), and set it to one before \(N_1\).
It has all the claimed monotonicity and lower-bound properties, and summing separately over the blocks proves the assertion.
No polynomial decay rate of \(\alpha_N\) follows from this argument.
In particular the resulting matching size \(r_N\asymp\alpha_N^{-1}\) may grow arbitrarily slowly.

<a id="paired-norm-complete-convergence"></a>
### Fourth moments make the paired row-norm exception summable

For the exact paired-support error \(e_N\) in C194v30,
\[
 \sum_N e_N<\infty.
 \tag{Paired-row-norm-sum}
\]
This is an application of the classical Hsu--Robbins complete-convergence theorem to squared entries.
The inspected primary passage and precise sufficiency statement are retained in [Erd49-U001](../audits/literature/Erd49/Yin_U001.md#erd49-u001), with the source's attribution to HR47 and its inspection boundary.
Here is a self-contained truncation proof of the precise sufficiency used here.
For one input law set \(Y=(\xi')^2+(\xi'')^2\), where the copies are independent.
Then \(\mathbb EY=2\) and \(Z=Y-2\) has mean zero and finite second moment.
It suffices to prove complete convergence of the averages of any fixed centered real finite-variance law \(Z\).

For each size write independent copies \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_N\), and put
\[
 T_{i,N}=Z_i\mathbf1_{\{|Z_i|\le N\}},
 \qquad \mu_N=\mathbb ET_{i,N}.
\]
The mean \(\mu_N\to0\).
For any fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), once \(|\mu_N|\le\varepsilon/2\), truncation and fourth-moment Markov give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^NZ_i\right|>\varepsilon N\right\}
 &\le N\mathbb P\{|Z|>N\}
   +\frac{C_\varepsilon}{N^4}
      \mathbb E\left|\sum_{i=1}^N(T_{i,N}-\mu_N)\right|^4\\
 &\le N\mathbb P\{|Z|>N\}
   +\frac{C_\varepsilon}{N^3}\mathbb E[|Z|^4\mathbf1_{\{|Z|\le N\}}]
   +\frac{C_\varepsilon(\mathbb EZ^2)^2}{N^2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Truncated-fourth-complete-bound}
\]
The last line follows by expanding the fourth power of independent centered real variables: only one repeated index or two paired indices survive.
Centering costs only a universal factor in the fourth moment, and the variance is at most \(\mathbb EZ^2\).
Tonelli gives
\[
 \sum_{N\ge1}N\mathbb P\{|Z|>N\}\le C\mathbb EZ^2,
\]
\[
 \sum_{N\ge1}N^{-3}\mathbb E[|Z|^4\mathbf1_{\{|Z|\le N\}}]
 =\mathbb E\left[|Z|^4\sum_{N\ge\max(1,\lceil|Z|\rceil)}N^{-3}\right]
 \le C\mathbb EZ^2.
\]
The third term is summable as well, proving complete convergence.
Apply this with \(\varepsilon=2\) to the two input laws and sum their errors.
This is a marginal statement, so it holds with arbitrary coupling between sizes.

Together with (Slow-geometry-sum), this removes the qualitative good-minor and paired-norm exceptions as independent obstacles on a fixed norm guard.
It does not make the remaining matching error summable.

<a id="stretched-clipped-potential"></a>
### One explicit global clipping converges almost surely without a floor

Fix a nonconstant scalar polynomial \(P\) and use the finite-moment Wigner scope of C195 and C186v4.
In particular, the real fourth-moment \(AB+2BA\) model is included.
Write
\[
 \rho_{N,z}=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{s_j(P(W^{(N)})-zI)^2},
 \qquad u(z)=\tau\log|P(s)-z|.
\]
For every fixed \(z\ne P(0)\), C195 supplies constants \(a_z,\gamma_z,C_z>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb E\rho_{N,z}([0,t])\le C_zt^{a_z},
 \qquad N^{-\gamma_z}\le t\le1.
 \tag{Count-for-deep-clipping}
\]
Use one deterministic height sequence on the entire plane, for example
\[
 L_N=\exp\sqrt{\log(N+1)},\qquad
 V_N(z)=\frac12\int\log\max(x,e^{-2L_N})\,d\rho_{N,z}(x).
 \tag{Global-clipping-height}
\]
More generally, any deterministic \(L_N\to\infty\) satisfying \(L_N=N^{o(1)}\) has all the conclusions below.
For every fixed \(z\ne P(0)\),
\[
 V_N(z)\longrightarrow u(z)
 \quad\hbox{almost surely under every allowed cross-size coupling},
 \qquad \mathbb EV_N(z)\longrightarrow u(z).
 \tag{Deep-clipped-pointwise}
\]
There is also one probability-one event on which
\[
 V_N\longrightarrow u\quad\hbox{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb C).
 \tag{Deep-clipped-planar}
\]
No least-singular-value floor or operator-norm guard is assumed.

Here is one proof that also permits faster clipping at a fixed parameter.
Fix \(z\), suppress its subscripts on \(a,\gamma,C\), and for now assume
\[
 L_N\to\infty,\qquad L_NN^{-\gamma a}\to0,\qquad
 \sum_N\exp\left[-\frac{cN}{(L_N+\log N)^2}\right]<\infty
 \quad\hbox{for every }c>0.
 \tag{Admissible-clipping-height}
\]
If \(e^{-2L_N}\ge N^{-\gamma}\), the count estimate directly controls the entire lower integral below.
Otherwise split that integral at \(N^{-\gamma}\).
For fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1\), layer integration and monotonicity of the count give, in both cases,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0&\le\mathbb EV_N^{\max}(\varepsilon,z)-\mathbb EV_N(z)\\
 &=\frac12\int_{e^{-2L_N}}^\varepsilon
             \frac{\mathbb E\rho_{N,z}([0,t])}{t}\,dt\\
 &\le C L_NN^{-\gamma a}+\frac{C}{2a}\varepsilon^a
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Deep-clipped-mean-comparison}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\), where
\(V_N^{\max}(\varepsilon,z)=\frac12\int\log\max(x,\varepsilon)\,d\rho_{N,z}(x)\).
The interval from \(e^{-2L_N}\) to \(N^{-\gamma}\), when nonempty, has logarithmic length at most \(2L_N\); on it use the bound at \(N^{-\gamma}\).
On the remaining interval use (Count-for-deep-clipping).
Weak Hermitized convergence and expected positive-logarithm uniform integrability in C186v4 imply
\(\mathbb EV_N^{\max}(\varepsilon,z)\to u_\varepsilon^{\max}(z)\).
The free count obtained by the weak limit makes the negative free logarithm integrable and gives \(u_\varepsilon^{\max}(z)\downarrow u(z)\).
The same bound with liminf and limsup, then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), proves \(\mathbb EV_N(z)\to u(z)\).

For concentration fix \(K_0>0\) and put
\[
 h_N(x)=\frac12\log\max\{e^{-2L_N},\min(x,N^{K_0})\}.
\]
Its total variation is \(L_N+(K_0/2)\log N\).
The C186v4 row-block inequality therefore gives, for each fixed \(b>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\left|\int h_N\,d\rho_{N,z}
          -\mathbb E\int h_N\,d\rho_{N,z}\right|>b\right\}
 \le2\exp\left[-\frac{c_bN}{(L_N+\log N)^2}\right].
 \tag{Deep-clipped-concentration}
\]
The right side is summable by (Admissible-clipping-height).
Intersect first Borel--Cantelli over rational \(b>0\).
The difference from \(V_N\) is nonnegative and at most
\(\frac12\int_{x>N^{K_0}}\log x\,d\rho_{N,z}(x)\).
The expected and pathwise positive-logarithm tail controls in C186v4 make this difference tend to zero in mean and almost surely.
Thus \(V_N-\mathbb EV_N\to0\) almost surely, proving the pointwise assertion under (Admissible-clipping-height).

Every subpolynomial height \(L_N=N^{o(1)}\) tending to infinity obeys (Admissible-clipping-height) for every fixed \(z\).
Indeed \(L_NN^{-\gamma_za_z}\to0\), and eventually \(L_N+\log N\le N^{1/4}\), so the concentration majorant is at most \(\exp(-cN^{1/2})\).
This gives a single height sequence for all parameters even though the count exponents depend on \(z\).
At a fixed parameter one may instead take \(L_N=N^\eta\) whenever
\[
 0<\eta<\min(\gamma_za_z,1/2).
 \tag{Faster-pointwise-clipping}
\]
The same proof gives this stronger fixed-parameter clipping, with summable concentration \(\exp(-cN^{1-2\eta})\).
No common positive \(\eta\) on the entire plane is asserted.

For the global planar conclusion, the clipped potentials are jointly measurable in the inputs and \(z\); this follows directly from continuity of singular values and the finite positive clipping level.
Apply Fubini to (Deep-clipped-pointwise), then intersect over integer-radius disks.
On one probability-one event, \(V_N(z)\to u(z)\) for planar-almost every \(z\).
There is also the pointwise deterministic sandwich, for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 u_N(z)\le V_N(z)\le u_{N,1}(z),
 \qquad
 u_{N,1}(z)=\frac12\int\log(x+1)\,d\rho_{N,z}(x).
 \tag{Clipped-potential-sandwich}
\]
It follows from \(x\le\max(x,e^{-2L_N})\le x+1\).
At the finitely many eigenvalues the left side is \(-\infty\); these points do not affect planar integration.
C186v4 gives an eventual pathwise uniform local \(L^2\) bound for \(u_N\), and locally uniform almost-sure convergence of \(u_{N,1}\) gives the same bound for that upper endpoint.
The sandwich implies \(|V_N|\le|u_N|+|u_{N,1}|\) almost everywhere, hence an eventual uniform local \(L^2\) bound for \(V_N\).
Uniform integrability on each disk and almost-everywhere convergence prove (Deep-clipped-planar).

The same sandwich gives a uniform expected local \(L^2\) bound: C186v4 supplies it for \(u_N\), while \(u_{N,1}\ge0\) is bounded in every fixed expected logarithmic moment by its positive-logarithm controls, uniformly on a compact \(z\)-set.
The deterministic limiting potential is locally square-integrable.
Convergence in product measure and Cauchy--Schwarz therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E\int_K|V_N(z)-u(z)|\,dA(z)\longrightarrow0
 \tag{Deep-clipped-mean-planar}
\]
for each compact disk \(K\).
This expectation assertion also needs no least-value floor.

<a id="extreme-planar-defect"></a>
### The remaining defect involves only singular values below the global cutoff

For the global height (Global-clipping-height), define almost everywhere in \(z\)
\[
 D_N(z):=V_N(z)-u_N(z)
   =\frac1N\sum_{s_j<e^{-L_N}}
                \log\frac{e^{-L_N}}{s_j}\ge0.
 \tag{Extreme-bottom-log-defect}
\]
For each realized matrix, its finite eigenvalue set has planar measure zero.
One can set \(D_N=0\) there when discussing planar integrals; this does not change any integral and leaves a measurable version.
For each fixed parameter in the deterministic finite-invertibility set, the displayed identity holds almost surely at every finite \(N\) without such a modification.

On the C186v4 control event and the event of (Deep-clipped-planar),
\[
 \mu_{P(W^{(N)})}\Longrightarrow\mu_{P(s)}^{\mathrm{Br}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \int_KD_N(z)\,dA(z)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{for every compact disk }K.
 \tag{Extreme-defect-Brown-equivalence}
\]
For sufficiency use
\[
 \|u_N-u\|_{L^1(K)}
 \le\|V_N-u\|_{L^1(K)}+\int_KD_N\,dA,
\]
then the Laplacian and tightness passage of C186v4.
For necessity, its [complete potential-continuity argument](../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#remaining-defect) shows that weak Brown convergence on the control event implies \(u_N\to u\) locally in \(L^1\).
The reverse triangle bound
\(\int_KD_N\le\|V_N-u\|_{L^1(K)}+\|u_N-u\|_{L^1(K)}\)
then proves the required defect limit.
Integer-radius disks suffice.

For the fixed marginal model, the almost-sure Brown conclusion under every allowed coupling is therefore equivalent to the exact marginal requirement
\[
 \sum_N\mathbb P\left\{\int_{\{|z|<R\}}D_N(z)\,dA(z)>b\right\}<\infty
 \quad(R\in\mathbb N,\ b\in\mathbb Q_{>0}).
 \tag{Extreme-planar-complete-convergence}
\]
The forward implication applies (Every-coupling-complete-convergence) to these measurable nonnegative within-size statistics, using the established equivalence under the independent-size coupling.
The reverse implication uses first Borel--Cantelli for the countable pairs \(R,b\) and (Extreme-defect-Brown-equivalence).
This is an explicit extreme-singular-value form of the existing every-coupling criterion in [the earlier D9 comparison](../drafts/Yin_D_F0009.md#wigner-lower-bound), not a proof that the displayed series converges.

For real fourth-moment \(AB+2BA\), the adopted [C186v26 statement](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#statement), with its [expected planar proof](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#expected-planar-proof), gives expected local \(L^1\) convergence of \(u_N\).
Together with (Deep-clipped-mean-planar), it implies the already vanishing first-moment defect
\[
 \mathbb E\int_KD_N(z)\,dA(z)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Extreme-planar-mean-zero}
\]
Thus the remaining almost-sure task is a complete-convergence estimate for a nonnegative planar defect whose expectation already tends to zero.
Markov's inequality at this qualitative mean convergence does not give summability.

At a fixed \(z\), a sufficient stronger statement is a summable floor at \(e^{-L_N}\), which makes \(D_N(z)=0\) eventually.
For the faster fixed-parameter height \(L_N=N^\eta\), the same assertion only requires a summable floor at \(e^{-N^\eta}\), rather than at a polynomial scale.
Alternatively one may allow arbitrarily small singular values and prove summable fixed-deviation tails for their total normalized excess logarithm.
Neither pointwise floor condition is claimed necessary for the planar Brown conclusion.

<a id="matching-and-many-indices"></a>
### Why the present conditional matching bound does not amplify automatically

After the two summable improvements above, C194v30 still supplies only
\[
 \psi(r_N)=Ce^{-cr_N}+C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/25}
\]
for each usable conditioned border.
The matching packing limit is \(r_N\le N/6\), so even inserting a linear matching size into this upper-bound formula does not make its displayed majorant summable.
For sufficiently large \(r_N\), the exponential term is absorbed by the displayed power term; it is not a separate rate obstacle.
Shrinking the distance threshold from a polynomial to a stretched exponential does not improve this particular source bound, which has no interval-length factor once the interval is below the coefficient threshold.
A nonsummable upper bound proves nothing about nonsummability of the actual probability.

The [earlier unit-energy flat quartic comparison](../drafts/Yin_D_F0011.md#summable-arithmetic-probe) already shows why coefficient energy, vanishing loads and large matching alone cannot give a generic summable estimate.
For completeness, take six independent groups of \(m\) fair signs \(\epsilon_{a,j}\), four groups assigned to \(x\) and two to \(y\), and set
\[
 F_m=m^{-3}\prod_{a=1}^6\left(\sum_{j=1}^m\epsilon_{a,j}\right).
\]
It has bidegree \((4,2)\) and \(m^6\) distinct monomials of coefficient \(m^{-3}\), hence coefficient energy one.
Each coordinate belongs to \(m^5\) monomials, so its squared coefficient load is \(m^5m^{-6}=1/m\).
The \(m\) edges using one common index \(j\) across all six groups form a matching, and no matching can have more than \(m\) edges because each edge uses a variable of the first group.
For even \(m\), Stirling's formula gives
\[
 q_m=2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}
   =\sqrt{\frac2{\pi m}}(1+O(m^{-1})).
\]
Independence of the groups therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb P(F_m=0)=1-(1-q_m)^6\asymp m^{-1/2},
\]
a nonsummable atom even for arbitrarily small positive windows.
This generic polynomial is not claimed to be the actual Schur numerator.
In particular, almost-everywhere finite invertibility already excludes an exact zero atom at each finite \(N\) for the actual problem's parameters.
The construction diagnoses a missing arithmetic or structural input, not a counterexample to the actual floor or Brown convergence.

Many-index averaging does not turn the existing conditional estimate into its high power.
As an exact probability illustration, let \(U_1,\ldots,U_m\) be independent uniform variables on \([0,1]\), fix \(0<b_m<1\), and define
\[
 A_m=\{(U_1+\cdots+U_m)\bmod1\le b_m\},\qquad B_i=A_m\quad(1\le i\le m).
\]
For every \(i\), conditioning on all other coordinates gives \(\mathbb P(B_i\mid U_{j\ne i})=b_m\).
Nevertheless all \(m\) bad events occur together and have probability \(b_m\), not \(b_m^m\).
This uses fixed independent coordinate laws, so the failure is not an artifact of changing the one-coordinate distribution with size.
For actual square matrices, a common determinant-zero event likewise forces every usable one-column Schur determinant to vanish, illustrating the algebraic source of strong dependence.
The example does not settle the actual nonsingular distance events; it shows that a new joint-exposure theorem, beyond the one-border conditional bound and a deterministic many-index implication, is logically necessary for such amplification.

The adopted [three-factor proof](../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v23.md#many-index-floor) obtains arbitrary polynomial failures through its separate structured-pair count and mean full-numerator theorem.
It explicitly replaces the earlier nonsummable matching term; it does not improve that term merely by averaging over indices.
No corresponding structured-pair theorem is presently identified for the complete \(AB+2BA\) Schur polynomial.

<a id="norm-guard-obstruction"></a>
### A fixed norm guard cannot hold eventually for every allowed coupling

There are allowed fixed off-diagonal laws for which
\[
 \sum_N\mathbb P\{\|A_N\|>K\}=\infty
 \qquad\text{for every fixed }K>0.
 \tag{Fixed-norm-guard-nonsummability}
\]
Take a symmetric centered Pareto law with tail \(\mathbb P(|\xi|>t)=c t^{-5}\) for all sufficiently large \(t\), rescaled to variance one, and zero diagonals.
Its fourth moment is finite.
If any of the \(N(N-1)/2\) independent raw upper entries exceeds \(K\sqrt N\) in absolute value, then an entry of \(A_N\), and hence its operator norm, exceeds \(K\).
Writing \(p_N\asymp_K N^{-5/2}\), independence gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|A_N\|>K\}\ge1-(1-p_N)^{N(N-1)/2}
 \ge c_KN^{-1/2}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\), because the product of the number of entries and \(p_N\) tends to zero.
Under independent tuples across sizes, these norm violations occur infinitely often almost surely.
Thus the fixed norm guard in C194v30 cannot be removed by an eventual-norm argument under its exact hypotheses.

The diagonal assumption has a separate version of this obstruction.
Use bounded off-diagonal laws and a centered symmetric diagonal law with tail \(\mathbb P(|\zeta|>t)=c t^{-3}\), which has finite second moment.
The \(N\) diagonal coordinates give the same lower bound \(c_KN^{-1/2}\) for exceeding a fixed norm threshold.
Neither example shows a macroscopic eigenvalue-measure deviation; rare large entries or diagonals may still have negligible Brown-measure effect.
They prevent only the stated guard-removal shortcut.

Using a growing polynomial norm guard has summable failure by crude second-moment bounds, but the all-profile raw theorem currently has nonquantitative constants depending on its fixed \(K\).
Substituting \(K=K_N\) therefore requires quantitative dependence of the raw, geometry, coefficient and graph estimates; it is not justified by their fixed-\(K\) statements.
Alternatively one needs a direct normalized logarithmic estimate that tolerates large entries or a proved truncation transfer for the negative logarithmic defect.
Low rank of the removed part alone does not transfer nonnormal eigenvalue measures, as already explained in C186v4.

<a id="remaining-targets"></a>
### Concrete remaining targets

The geometric and paired-norm contributions can be made summable on each fixed norm guard, with no stronger moment assumption.
The explicit global clipping converges almost surely without any least-value input and isolates (Extreme-bottom-log-defect) as a smaller unresolved remainder than the original polynomial floor target.
The current probability proof does not control this remainder almost surely: its matching term lacks a summable arithmetic estimate, and its fixed norm guard has the explicit every-coupling limitation above.
Either an actual-numerator arithmetic estimate together with guard removal, or direct summable macroscopic/planar logarithmic-defect bounds, would advance the almost-sure goal.
The [row-replacement fourth-moment route in L67v1](../ledger/Yin_L_F0067_v1.md#mean-and-influences) remains another sufficient interface, but its required typical influences have not been supplied by the probability floor.
