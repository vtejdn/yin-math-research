# Almost-sure Brown convergence and planar concentration under log-concave laws

This theorem strengthens the distributional assumptions in the adopted [real fourth-moment probability theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#statement).
It proves summable deviations of the exact planar defect from [C186v27](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#clipping-definition), and hence full-sequence almost-sure Brown convergence, for log-concave input laws.
It does not assert that conclusion for arbitrary finite-fourth-moment laws.
The new input is a dimension-free logarithmic oscillation estimate for conditional row polynomials; its failure for an atomic law is recorded below.
The complete statement and argument are adopted with the [independent whole-proof finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v27.md#log-concave-planar-brown-whole-proof).

<a id="model"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

For each \(N\), let \(A_N,B_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
For each of the two colors, its strict upper entries are independent copies of a fixed real law \(\xi/\sqrt N\), its lower entries are their symmetric copies, and its diagonal entries are independent copies of a fixed real law \(\zeta/\sqrt N\).
The two strict-upper-entry laws are centered, have variance one and finite fourth moment.
The two diagonal laws are centered and have finite second moment.
Assume additionally that each of these four real laws is log-concave, allowing a deterministic zero diagonal law.
Thus every nondeterministic law has a log-concave density on an interval, while a deterministic coordinate can be removed from the coordinate space.
All independent upper pairs, diagonal entries and input colors are independent within each size.
The coupling of the entire tuples across different sizes is arbitrary.

Put \(T_N=A_NB_N+2B_NA_N\) and \(T=s_1s_2+2s_2s_1\), where \(s_1,s_2\) are standard free semicircular operators in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).
Write
\[
 \mu_N=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{\lambda_j(T_N)},\qquad
 \mu=\mu_T^{\mathrm{Br}},\qquad
 \rho_{N,z}=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{s_j(T_N-zI)^2},
\]
\[
 u_N(z)=N^{-1}\log|\det(T_N-zI)|,\qquad
 u(z)=\tau\log|T-z|=U_\mu(z).
\]
Eigenvalues are counted with algebraic multiplicity, and finite potentials are locally integrable planar functions with possible logarithmic singularities at their finite eigenvalue sets.
Use the deterministic global height \(L_N\to\infty\), \(L_N=N^{o(1)}\), and the functions \(V_N,D_N\) from C186v27.
For \(R>0\), write
\[
 U_N(R)=\int_{|z|<R}u_N(z)\,dA(z),\qquad
 J_N(R)=\int_{|z|<R}D_N(z)\,dA(z).
\]

The conclusions are as follows.

1. For every bounded real Borel function \(g\) with compact support, the real random variable
   \[
    Z_N(g)=\int g(z)u_N(z)\,dA(z)
   \]
   satisfies, for \(t>0\),
   \[
    \mathbb P\{|Z_N(g)-\mathbb EZ_N(g)|>t\}
       \le 2\exp\!\left[-\frac{Nt^2}{8a_g^2+4a_gt}\right],
       \qquad a_g=C\|g\|_{L^1(dA)}.
    \tag{Planar-centered-concentration}
   \]
   The case \(g=0\) is interpreted directly.
   The constant \(C\) is absolute for this fixed polynomial and does not depend on the laws, the dimension, a norm guard or a minor inverse.
2. For every fixed \(R,\varepsilon>0\), there are constants \(c_{R,\varepsilon}>0,C_R<\infty\) and a deterministic \(N_0\), possibly depending on the laws, \(R,\varepsilon\) and the convergence of the means, such that
   \[
    \mathbb P\{J_N(R)>\varepsilon\}
       \le C_RN^{-3}
          +2\exp\!\left[-\frac{c_{R,\varepsilon}N}
                                   {(L_N+\log N)^2}\right]
          +2e^{-c_{R,\varepsilon}N}
       \qquad(N\ge N_0).
    \tag{Summable-planar-defect}
   \]
   Consequently \(\sum_N\mathbb P\{J_N(R)>\varepsilon\}<\infty\).
3. On one event of probability one, \(u_N\to u\) in \(L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb C)\) and \(\mu_N\Rightarrow\mu\).
   These conclusions hold under every allowed cross-size coupling.
   For a deterministic full-area set of fixed parameters \(z\), there is also pointwise almost-sure convergence \(u_N(z)\to u(z)\) and convergence \(\mathbb Eu_N(z)\to u(z)\).

<a id="argument"></a>
## Complete argument

The proof below uses the adopted probability and clipping statements only at their stated scopes.
It supplies the new summable estimate directly for the original log-concave row laws.
The conditional logarithmic estimate does not follow from fourth moments alone.

<a id="source-input"></a>
### Accepted source and the local logarithmic deduction

The [Carbery--Wright source unit](../audits/literature/CW01/Yin_U001.md#cw01-u001) retains the inspected author version, Theorem 8 on printed and PDF p. 13, and the source's affine-support definition of log-concavity immediately below it.
Its Banach-valued statement applies to complex-valued polynomials by taking the real Banach space \(\mathbb C\) with its modulus.
Only its \(q=1\) case is used.
The established source theorem is accepted at this checked statement and applicability scope; its original proof has not been re-audited.

Let \(p\) be a complex polynomial of degree at most a fixed integer \(d\ge1\), on a finite-dimensional real space with log-concave probability law \(\nu\).
Suppose \(p\ne0\) almost surely and \(\mathbb E|p|^{1/d}<\infty\).
Set
\[
 m_p=\left(\mathbb E|p|^{1/d}\right)^d>0.
\]
The source theorem and Markov's inequality give, for \(t\ge0\),
\[
 \nu\{\log|p|-\log m_p\le-t\}\le C_0e^{-t/d},
 \qquad
 \nu\{\log|p|-\log m_p\ge t\}\le e^{-t/d}.
 \tag{Two-log-tails}
\]
Indeed the source's \(q=1\) bound is
\(\mathbb E|p|^{1/d}\,\alpha^{-1/d}\nu\{|p|\le\alpha\}\le C_0\);
take \(\alpha=m_pe^{-t}\).
The upper tail follows by applying Markov to \(|p|^{1/d}\).
Tail integration yields
\[
 \mathbb E|\log|p|-\log m_p|^k
       \le (C_0+1)d^k k!\qquad(k\in\mathbb N).
\]
For two independent samples \(X,X'\) from \(\nu\), the deterministic center cancels, so
\[
 \mathbb E|\log|p(X)|-\log|p(X')||^k
       \le k!C_d^k \qquad(k\in\mathbb N)
 \tag{Log-copy-moments}
\]
after enlarging a constant depending only on \(d\).
Constant nonzero polynomials obey the same conclusion.
The result is uniform over the coefficients, the ambient dimension and the log-concave law.
For fixed coefficients, finite first moments of the input coordinates suffice for \(\mathbb E|p|^{1/d}<\infty\), because \(|p(x)|^{1/d}\le C_p(1+\|x\|)\).
Thus the proof does not silently impose moments of order proportional to the matrix dimension.

<a id="original-row-degree"></a>
### The actual independent row block has degree at most six

For physical index \(i\), group the original independent variables
\[
 X_i=\bigl((A_{ij})_{j\ge i},(B_{ij})_{j\ge i}\bigr),
\]
including the two diagonal variables and reflecting the strict upper entries to the lower triangle after each replacement.
There are \(N\) independent blocks.
A full displayed matrix row is not used as an independent block.
The law of \(X_i\), after removing deterministic coordinates, is log-concave: its density is a product of log-concave densities, so the negative logarithm is a sum of convex functions.
The normalization by \(N^{-1/2}\) preserves this property.

Consider the affine companion
\[
 \mathcal L_N(z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  -zI&A_N&2B_N\\
  B_N&-I&0\\
  A_N&0&-I
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The Schur determinant identity with the two constant auxiliary blocks gives
\[
 \det\mathcal L_N(z)=\det(T_N-zI).
\]
When the other \(X_j\)'s are fixed, all entries of \(\mathcal L_N\) that depend on \(X_i\) are affine functions of its coordinates.
They lie in the union of the three rows and three columns indexed by \(i,N+i,2N+i\).
In one determinant-permutation term, at most three chosen entries lie in the specified rows, and at most three additional entries can lie in the specified columns.
Thus the complete conditional determinant has total degree at most six in \(X_i\).
This reasoning includes its diagonal coordinates and does not require any principal minor to be invertible.
The degree bound concerns the entire determinant, including all cancellations.

<a id="null-sets-integrability"></a>
### Measurability, planar exceptional sets and integrability

For every realized finite matrix, its characteristic polynomial is nonzero and has only finitely many zeros.
Tonelli applied on every integer disk, followed by a countable intersection over \(N\), gives a deterministic full-area Borel set \(Z_{\mathrm{inv}}\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{\det(T_N-zI)\ne0\}=1
       \qquad(z\in Z_{\mathrm{inv}},\ N\in\mathbb N).
\]
For fixed \(N,i,z\) in this set, conditioning on all blocks except \(X_i\) shows that the conditional degree-six polynomial is nonzero almost surely under the original row law, for almost every background.
The resampled tuple has the same marginal law, so the same statement holds for its determinant.
No full-minor invertibility assertion is substituted for this argument.

At such a fixed \(z\), the determinant as a polynomial of the entire original independent tuple has degree at most \(2N\).
The whole tuple also has a log-concave law.
The preceding logarithmic argument with \(d=2N\) therefore proves \(\mathbb E|u_N(z)|<\infty\), and indeed finite moments of every fixed order.
Here constants may depend on \(N,z\); only the separate degree-six replacement estimate below has a uniform constant.

For planar integration, the [expected local \(L^2\) control](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) gives
\(\mathbb E\int_K|u_N(z)|\,dA(z)<\infty\) for every compact \(K\).
Every sample potential is locally integrable, including its finite logarithmic singularities.
Potentials may be assigned any finite value at their finite eigenvalue sets when using measurable planar integrands.
This changes neither an integral nor any assertion at a fixed \(z\in Z_{\mathrm{inv}}\).
Fubini converts the fixed-\(z\), almost-every-background conditional moment statements to almost-every-background, almost-every-\(z\) statements on each disk.
This justifies the conditional integrations used next; the null set need not be uniform over all backgrounds.

<a id="replacement-and-mgf"></a>
### Conditional row replacement and its elementary exponential conversion

Let \(X_i'\) be an independent copy of the whole original block \(X_i\), and let \(u_N^{(i)}\) be the potential after this replacement.
By the degree-six result and the logarithmic copy estimate, for every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
   |u_N(z)-u_N^{(i)}(z)|^k\mid (X_j)_{j\ne i}
 \right]\le k!\left(\frac{C_6}{N}\right)^k
 \tag{Conditional-pointwise-influence}
\]
for almost every background and \(z\in Z_{\mathrm{inv}}\).
Conditional Minkowski, using the planar measurability argument above, gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
  |Z_N(g)-Z_N^{(i)}(g)|^k\mid (X_j)_{j\ne i}
 \right]\le k!\left(\frac{a_g}{N}\right)^k,
 \qquad a_g=C_6\|g\|_1 .
 \tag{Conditional-planar-influence}
\]
In particular, the direct row-influence target of the earlier [resampling exploration](../ledger/Yin_L_F0067_v1.md) is satisfied under these stronger laws.
For \(g=(2\pi)^{-1}\Delta f\), \(f\in C_c^2(\mathbb C;\mathbb R)\), the distributional logarithmic-potential identity identifies \(Z_N(g)\) with \(\int f\,d\mu_N\).
The absolute value in Minkowski is applied to the replacement difference, while the statistic itself retains the signed Laplacian.

Here is a direct exponential argument, retaining more than the fourth-moment specialization of the [accepted BBLM05 resampling inequality](../audits/literature/BBLM05/Yin_U001.md#fourth-moment-resampling-bound).
For an integrable real statistic \(Z=F(X_1,\ldots,X_N)\), let \(\mathcal F_i=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_i)\) and
\[
 H_i=\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal F_i]
           -\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}].
\]
With the future blocks and \(X_i'\) integrated out,
\[
 H_i=\mathbb E[Z-Z^{(i)}\mid\mathcal F_i].
\]
Conditional Jensen followed by averaging the uniform all-other-blocks bound yields
\[
 \mathbb E[|H_i|^k\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
       \le k!b^k,\qquad b=a_g/N.
\]
The fact that the previous blocks appear among the conditioned backgrounds makes this bound valid conditionally on the past, not just unconditionally.
Since \(\mathbb E[H_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]=0\), the absolutely convergent exponential series gives, for \(|\lambda|b<1\),
\[
 \mathbb E[e^{\lambda H_i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
 \le 1+\frac{\lambda^2b^2}{1-|\lambda|b}
 \le\exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2b^2}{1-|\lambda|b}\right).
\]
Iteration and exponential Markov imply
\[
 \mathbb P\{Z-\mathbb EZ>t\}
 \le \exp\!\left[-\lambda t+
                 \frac{N\lambda^2b^2}{1-\lambda b}\right].
\]
Take \(\lambda=t/(4Nb^2+2bt)\).
Then \(\lambda b\le1/2\) and \(2Nb^2\lambda\le t/2\), so the exponent is at most
\(-t^2/(8Nb^2+4bt)\).
The same argument for \(-Z\) proves the stated planar centered concentration.
This conversion is proved here directly; no concentration theorem with unstated bounded-difference assumptions is invoked.
Exactly the same reasoning applies pointwise to \(u_N(z)\), with \(a_g\) replaced by \(C_6\), for every \(z\in Z_{\mathrm{inv}}\).

<a id="clipped-planar-defect"></a>
### Summable concentration of the exact extreme planar loss

Fix \(R>0\), and set
\[
 \widehat V_N(z)=\frac12\int
    \log\min\{\max(x,e^{-2L_N}),N^{10}\}\,d\rho_{N,z}(x),
 \qquad
 \widehat B_N(R)=\int_{|z|<R}\widehat V_N(z)\,dA(z).
\]
For \(N\ge2\), its scalar test has total variation at most \(L_N+5\log N\).
Replacing one grouped original block changes \(A_N\) and \(B_N\) by matrices of rank at most two each.
Writing the output difference as
\[
 (A-A')B+A'(B-B')+2(B-B')A+2B'(A-A')
\]
shows that its rank is at most eight.
The difference of the squared-singular matrices consequently has rank at most sixteen.
The rank inequality for their empirical distribution functions bounds the change in \(\widehat V_N(z)\) by
\(16(L_N+5\log N)/N\).
Integration over the disk therefore gives a deterministic bounded difference of at most
\[
 c_R(L_N+\log N)/N
\]
for \(\widehat B_N(R)\).
The [independent-row bounded-difference argument](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration), applied to these \(N\) grouped blocks, yields
\[
 \mathbb P\{|\widehat B_N(R)-\mathbb E\widehat B_N(R)|>t\}
 \le 2\exp\!\left[-\frac{c_R'Nt^2}{(L_N+\log N)^2}\right].
 \tag{Capped-planar-concentration}
\]

Only second moments are needed to remove this upper cap with a summable error.
For either input,
\[
 \mathbb E\|W_N\|_{\mathrm F}^2
       =N-1+\mathbb E\zeta^2\le C N.
\]
Thus the event
\(\mathcal G_N=\{\|A_N\|\le N^2,\ \|B_N\|\le N^2\}\)
has \(\mathbb P(\mathcal G_N^c)\le C N^{-3}\).
On \(\mathcal G_N\), \(\|T_N-zI\|\le3N^4+R\) for \(|z|<R\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\), its squared singular values are at most \(N^{10}\), simultaneously on that disk.
Consequently
\[
 J_N(R)=\widehat B_N(R)-U_N(R)
       \quad\hbox{on }\mathcal G_N.
\]
The finite eigenvalue sets do not affect this equality of integrals.

The [expected positive logarithmic controls](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#positive-tail) give a uniform bound, on compact \(z\)-sets, for
\(\mathbb E\int(\log^+x)^2\,d\rho_{N,z}(x)\).
Hence
\[
 \mathbb E\int_{|z|<R}(V_N-\widehat V_N)\,dA
 \le \frac{C_R}{\log N}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed the pointwise difference is at most
\(\tfrac12\int_{\{x>N^{10}\}}\log^+x\,d\rho_{N,z}\),
and \(\log^+x\le(\log^+x)^2/(10\log N)\) there.
The expected planar conclusion in [C186v27](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#statement) therefore implies
\[
 \mathbb E\widehat B_N(R)\longrightarrow\int_{|z|<R}u(z)\,dA(z).
\]
The adopted [expected planar proof of C186v26](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#expected-planar-proof) gives the same limit for \(\mathbb EU_N(R)\).
Their deterministic difference tends to zero without a quantitative rate assumption.

For \(N\) large enough that this difference has absolute value at most \(\varepsilon/3\), the event \(J_N(R)>\varepsilon\) is contained in
\[
 \mathcal G_N^c
 \ \cup\
 \{\widehat B_N(R)-\mathbb E\widehat B_N(R)>\varepsilon/3\}
 \ \cup\
 \{\mathbb EU_N(R)-U_N(R)>\varepsilon/3\}.
\]
The capped concentration bound and the direct planar bound with \(g=1_{\{|z|<R\}}\) prove the claimed explicit summable estimate.
Since \(L_N=N^{o(1)}\), its middle exponent is at least \(cN^{1/2}\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), after adjusting constants.
This proves complete convergence of the actual planar defect, with no independence assumption across sizes.

<a id="almost-sure-passage"></a>
### Full-sequence and pointwise consequences

Borel--Cantelli on integer \(R\) and positive rational \(\varepsilon\) gives one event on which \(J_N(R)\to0\) for every integer disk.
The exact defect equivalence and the almost-sure clipped-potential theorem in [C186v27](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#statement) then give \(u_N\to u\) locally in \(L^1\) and \(\mu_N\Rightarrow\mu\) on this event.
All probability bounds concerned only the within-size laws, so this conclusion holds for every cross-size coupling.

For the additional pointwise assertion, intersect \(Z_{\mathrm{inv}}\) with the deterministic full-area parameter set of C186v26.
The pointwise exponential estimate proves \(u_N(z)-\mathbb Eu_N(z)\to0\) almost surely and in probability.
C186v26 already proves \(u_N(z)\to u(z)\) in probability.
Subtracting these two convergence statements shows that the deterministic numbers \(\mathbb Eu_N(z)\) converge to \(u(z)\).
Thus \(u_N(z)\to u(z)\) almost surely at each such fixed \(z\).
Fubini gives almost-every-\(z\) convergence on one sample event; the existing pathwise local \(L^2\) control also supplies a separate direct route from this pointwise conclusion to local \(L^1\).
No uncountable intersection of pointwise probability-one events is used.

<a id="atomic-boundary"></a>
### Exact limitation of this route for the original fourth-moment question

The log-concavity assumption applies to all random coordinates, including random diagonal entries.
Conditioning on arbitrary finite-second-moment diagonals would only concentrate around a diagonal-dependent conditional mean.
The proof above supplies no estimate for the fluctuations of that mean, so it does not justify removing the diagonal assumption.

Even a fixed bounded centered variance-one atomic law does not satisfy the coefficient-uniform logarithmic copy estimate for all nonvanishing linear polynomials.
Let \(\xi\) be symmetric Rademacher and
\[
 p_\delta(\xi)=\frac{1+\xi}{2}+\delta,\qquad 0<\delta<1.
\]
It is nonzero everywhere on the law's support.
For an independent copy \(\xi'\),
\[
 \mathbb E\left|\log|p_\delta(\xi)|
                  -\log|p_\delta(\xi')|\right|^4
   =\frac12\left|\log\frac{1+\delta}{\delta}\right|^4
   \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus degree control, finite fourth moments and nonvanishing of the conditional determinant do not imply the essential uniform row-logarithm bound.
This example does not assert that actual \(AB+2BA\) conditional determinants attain these arbitrary coefficients on typical Wigner backgrounds.
Nor does it disprove almost-sure Brown convergence for atomic or general finite-fourth-moment laws.
That original question still requires quantitative information about the actual background, or another mechanism for its planar defect.
