# Almost-sure Brown convergence under an integrable logarithmic concentration function

<a id="statement"></a>
## Conditional determinant and Brown conclusions

For a nonconstant real law \(\sigma\) define \(Q_\sigma(r)=\sup_{b\in\mathbb R}\sigma([b-r,b+r])\).
The additional marginal assumption in this theorem is
\[
 \int_1^\infty v^3 Q_\sigma(e^{-v})\,dv<\infty .
 \tag{Logarithmic-concentration}
\]
It holds whenever \(Q_\sigma(r)\le Cr^\alpha\) for some \(\alpha>0\) on \(0<r<1\), and also when \(Q_\sigma(e^{-v})\le C v^{-4-\epsilon}\) for some \(\epsilon>0\) and all sufficiently large \(v\).
It permits disconnected support and singular continuous laws.
For example a variable with equiprobable independent ternary digits \(0,2\) has \(Q_\sigma(r)\le Cr^{\log 2/\log 3}\), since an interval of length comparable to \(3^{-j}\) meets a bounded number of basic level-\(j\) intervals, each of mass \(2^{-j}\).
Centering and scaling this bounded variable to variance one preserves the condition.
An atom in a nonconstant law violates it.

Use independent real symmetric Wigner matrices \(A,B\) with the original [finite fourth off-diagonal and second diagonal moments](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#statement), deterministic raw laws and arbitrary cross-size couplings.
Put \(M=AB+2BA\), \(u_N(z)=N^{-1}\log|\det(M-zI)|\), and let \(\mu\) denote the Brown measure of \(s_1s_2+2s_2s_1\) for free variance-one semicirculars.

1. For the following conditional conclusion impose (Logarithmic-concentration) only on the strict upper raw law of \(A\).
   For a prescribed set \(I\) of size \(k\), expose \(B\), the exterior entries of \(A\) and all the diagonals, giving \(\mathcal E\).
   Let \(U=A_{II}\), \(\mathcal U=\operatorname{diag}(U,U)\) and \(W(z)\) be the [actual D42 target](../drafts/Yin_D_F0042.md#repeated-interior) on the exterior event that \(B_{I^c I}\) has full column rank.
   For a bounded real Borel function \(g\) supported in a bounded disk, put
   \[
    Z_W=\int g(z)\log|\det(\mathcal U-W(z))|\,dA(z).
   \]
   This random variable is conditionally integrable there.
   If \(Z_W^{(i)}\) replaces the strict upper variables in the \(i\)-th row of \(U\) together with their reflected entries in both copies, then
   \[
    \mathbb E[|Z_W-Z_W^{(i)}|^4\mid\mathcal E,\text{ other upper rows of }U]
      \le C\|g\|_1^4\log^4(2+k),
   \]
   almost surely on that event, and
   \[
    \mathbb E[|Z_W-\mathbb E[Z_W\mid\mathcal E]|^4\mid\mathcal E]
      \le C\|g\|_1^4 k^2\log^4(2+k).
    \tag{Conditional-interior-oscillation}
   \]
   Constants may depend on the law.
   In particular the conditional probability of a centered fluctuation larger than \(vN\) is at most \(C_{g,v} k^2\log^4(2+k)/N^4\), summable for \(k\le N\).
   For prescribed \(k\le\delta N\) from [C196v4](Yin_C_F0196_v4.md#statement) the separate failure of the rank event has summable probability.
   These estimates apply to the actual shared-block determinant and allow minimizing directions selected from \(U\).
   The exterior conditional mean can still fluctuate.

2. Suppose in addition that each of the two raw off-diagonal laws and each nondeterministic raw diagonal law satisfies (Logarithmic-concentration).
   A deterministic zero diagonal is permitted.
   For \(Z_N(g)=\int g(z)u_N(z)\,dA(z)\),
   \[
    \mathbb E|Z_N(g)-\mathbb E Z_N(g)|^4
       \le C\|g\|_1^4\frac{\log^4(2+N)}{N^2}.
    \tag{Original-planar-oscillation}
   \]
   Let \(D_N\), \(V_N\) and a deterministic height \(L_N\to\infty,\ L_N=N^{o(1)}\) be as in [C186v27](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#clipping-definition).
   For fixed \(R,v>0\), eventually
   \[
    \Pr\!\left\{\int_{|z|<R}D_N(z)\,dA(z)>v\right\}
      \le C_R N^{-3}
       +2\exp[-c_{R,v}N/(L_N+\log N)^2]
       +C_{R,v}\log^4(2+N)/N^2 .
    \tag{Log-concentration-planar-deviation}
   \]
   In particular this holds on the further parameter restriction \(F_N(z)^c\) of [C186v29](Yin_C_F0186_v29.md#statement), for its specified height.
   On one event of probability one, \(u_N\) converges locally in \(L^1(dA)\) to the logarithmic potential of \(\mu\), and the empirical eigenvalue measures of \(M\) converge weakly to \(\mu\) along the full sequence under every allowed coupling.

The [log-concave theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v28.md#statement) gives exponential original-planar concentration under its log-concavity hypotheses.
Here the coefficient estimate for a row polynomial has a logarithmic dimension cost and uses only a one-dimensional concentration function.
The original fourth-moment problem still includes nonconstant atomic laws.

## Logarithm of a fixed-degree polynomial of independent scalars

Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be independent real scalars from a fixed finite collection of laws with finite first absolute moment and (Logarithmic-concentration).
Write \(Q\) for the maximum of their concentration functions and \(J=\max(1,\max_i\mathbb E|X_i|)\).
For a nonzero complex polynomial \(f\) of total degree at most \(d\) in these scalars, let \(M_f\) be the maximum modulus of its monomial coefficients.
There is a constant \(C\) depending only on the laws and \(d\), for which
\[
 \left\|\log|f(X)|-\log M_f\right\|_4\le C\log(2+n).
 \tag{Coefficient-log-oscillation}
\]
The tail bound (Product-sublevel) below holds for arbitrary coordinate laws with first absolute moments at most \(J\), using their common upper concentration function \(Q\), even if (Logarithmic-concentration) fails.
Only its subsequent fourth-moment integration uses the additional concentration assumption.
The norm statement includes a constant polynomial and an empty family of random coordinates, when the left side is zero.
All logarithmic norms here are taken with respect to the actual product law.

For a nonzero one-variable polynomial \(a\) of degree at most \(d\ge1\) with maximum coefficient modulus \(M_a\), fix \(u\ge1\) and put \(T=\exp(u/(2d))\).
Inside \([-T,T]\) the set \(\{|a|\le M_a e^{-u}\}\) is a union of at most \(2d+1\) intervals, possibly degenerate.
For a nonconstant polynomial this follows from the real polynomial \(|a(x)|^2-M_a^2e^{-2u}\).
A constant polynomial has empty sublevel set.
If one interval has length \(\ell>0\), interpolate \(a\) on \(d+1\) equally spaced points of its closure.
Each denominator of the Lagrange basis has modulus at least \((\ell/d)^d\) and its numerator has every coefficient bounded by \((1+T)^d\).
It follows that \(\ell\le C_d(1+T)e^{-u/d}\).
The same upper bound on covering interval length applies to an isolated point.
Using \(\Pr(|X|>T)\le J/T\), we obtain
\[
 \Pr\{|a(X)|\le M_a e^{-u}\}
   \le C_d\{Q(C_de^{-u/(2d)})+J e^{-u/(2d)}\}.
 \tag{Interval-sublevel}
\]

We next show for every fixed integer \(d\ge1\), with positive constants \(C_d,c_d\) depending also on \(J\), that for \(u\ge C_d\log(2+n)\)
\[
 \Pr\{|f(X)|\le M_f e^{-u}\}
 \le C_d\{Q(C_d e^{-c_d u})+n e^{-c_d u}\}.
 \tag{Product-sublevel}
\]
The degree-zero case has zero lower tail.
Normalize \(M_f=1\).
If some nonconstant coefficient has modulus at least \(e^{-u/4}\), choose a variable \(X_j\) appearing in that monomial with exponent \(h\ge1\).
The coefficient polynomial \(b=[X_j^h]f\) in the other variables has degree at most \(d-1\) and maximum coefficient at least \(e^{-u/4}\).
By induction, the event \(|b|\le e^{-u/2}\) has probability bounded by the right side of (Product-sublevel) with changed constants, on applying the previous degree bound at \(u/4\).
For \(b\) constant this bad event is empty.
Constants at degree \(d\) may be enlarged to meet all previous dimension thresholds.
Off this event the maximum one-variable coefficient of the conditional polynomial is at least \(e^{-u/2}\).
Independence and (Interval-sublevel) at \(u/2\) give the asserted bound after another change of constants.

In the other case the constant coefficient has modulus one.
On \(\max_j|X_j|\le e^{u/(8d)}\), the sum of the moduli of the nonconstant terms is at most \(C_d(1+n)^d e^{-u/8}\) since there are at most \(C_d(1+n)^d\) terms.
This is less than \(1/2\) when \(u\ge C_d'\log(2+n)\) with \(C_d'\) sufficiently large, so the specified sublevel event can only occur outside that box.
A union bound gives probability at most \(nJ e^{-u/(8d)}\).
This finishes the induction.
For the upper tail with \(M_f=1\), the same box bounds \(|f|\) by \(C_d(1+n)^d e^{u/8}<e^u\) at a sufficiently large logarithmic threshold.
Enlarging constants, (Product-sublevel) therefore bounds both tails of \(\log(|f|/M_f)\).
Under (Logarithmic-concentration), \(Q(r)\) tends to zero as \(r\) decreases to zero, so a nonzero polynomial vanishes with probability zero.

Integrate the two-sided bound against \(4u^3\,du\).
The part below \(C_d\log(2+n)\) is \(O_d(\log^4(2+n))\).
Above this cutoff \(n e^{-c_du}\le e^{-c_du/2}\) after increasing its prefactor in the cutoff.
The integral of \(u^3 Q(C_d e^{-c_du})\) from this cutoff to infinity is uniformly bounded, by a linear change of the logarithmic variable and (Logarithmic-concentration) for the finitely many laws.
This proves (Coefficient-log-oscillation).
For an independent copy \(X'\), cancellation of the deterministic \(\log M_f\) and Minkowski give
\[
 \|\log|f(X)|-\log|f(X')|\|_4\le 2C\log(2+n).
 \tag{Independent-copy-oscillation}
\]
No coefficient size, shift or uniform operator norm enters the bound.
When only some original coordinates are deterministic, substitute these values before computing \(M_f\).

## Conditional row replacement in the repeated block

Restrict to an exterior section where \(B_{I^c I}\) has full column rank.
Use independent blocks consisting of the raw strict upper entries of \(U\) in each row of its upper triangle.
Changing one complete block and reflecting it in both copies changes entries only in the two rows with this physical index and the corresponding two columns of \(\mathcal U-W(z)\).
In a determinant permutation at most two chosen positions are in these rows and at most two more are in these columns.
Each position is affine in the original independent scalars including the \(N^{-1/2}\) factor.
The conditional row polynomial thus has total degree at most four, uniformly in the target and the values of the other rows.

For each full sample with the cross block of full rank, the [conditional determinant identity](../drafts/Yin_D_F0042.md#repeated-interior) expresses this determinant in modulus as the quotient of the characteristic determinant by \(2^k\det(B_{I^c I}^*B_{I^c I})|\det(C_H-zI_H)|\).
The denominator has finitely many spectral zeros in the plane and the characteristic numerator is monic up to sign.
Fubini gives, for almost every exterior and other-row section, a full-area set of \(z\) at which the row polynomial at an original draw is nonzero almost surely.
It is thus a nonzero polynomial after deterministic substitutions.
The exterior section also has \(\mathbb E[|Z_W|\mid\mathcal E]<\infty\) almost surely.
Indeed its exterior denominator and nonzero scalar have locally integrable logarithms, and \(\mathbb E\int_{D_R}|u_N(z)|\,dA<\infty\) follows from the [base planar integrability bound](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar).
Logarithms at the finite sample spectra are assigned arbitrary finite values for planar integration.

For each such fixed \(z\) and other-row section apply (Independent-copy-oscillation) at degree four with \(n\le k\).
Conditional Minkowski after integrating against \(|g|\) yields the first bound in item 1.
Under fixed exterior data the rows of the strict upper triangle are independent.
For the bounded clipping of \(Z_W\) at levels \(\pm L\), apply the [fourth-moment resampling inequality](../audits/literature/BBLM05/Yin_U001.md#fourth-moment-resampling-bound).
The clipping is 1-Lipschitz and the bound on the unclipped replacement differences gives for the clipped centered fourth moment at most \(C k^2\|g\|_1^4\log^4(2+k)\).
As \(L\to\infty\), the conditional means converge by conditional integrability.
Fatou gives the asserted inequality for \(Z_W\).
The same conditioning and Markov's inequality give the stated \(vN\)-scale probability, accounting separately for the summable rank failures when the indicated range of \(k\) applies.
Finite sections of both copies always contain the same \(U\), including after each replacement.

## The true two-color determinant and the planar defect

Assume each nondeterministic original marginal has (Logarithmic-concentration).
For physical site \(i\) use one independent block of raw upper entries in row \(i\) from both colors, including the two diagonals.
In the [three-channel affine companion](Yin_C_F0186_v28.md#original-row-degree) each block affects only three rows and three columns.
The original characteristic determinant is thus a polynomial of total degree at most six in at most \(2N\) independent scalar coordinates of the block.
Every realized matrix has a monic characteristic polynomial up to sign.
Fubini therefore gives the same full-area, almost-every-background nonvanishing of the conditional polynomial as above.
Planar integrability gives \(Z_N(g)\in L^1\).
Applying (Independent-copy-oscillation) with \(d=6\), conditional Minkowski and the normalization \(1/N\) bounds the fourth moment of each independent replacement difference by \(C\|g\|_1^4\log^4(2+N)/N^4\).
The same bounded clipping, fourth-moment resampling inequality on the \(N\) blocks, and Fatou now give (Original-planar-oscillation).

For \(g=\mathbf1_{D_R}\), (Original-planar-oscillation) and Markov's inequality give the last term of (Log-concentration-planar-deviation) for downward fluctuations of the true potential statistic at any fixed positive threshold.
Use the [capped planar comparison](Yin_C_F0186_v28.md#clipped-planar-defect), whose upper cap at squared singular value \(N^{10}\) and rank bounds use only the original second moments and row independence.
The capped clipped integral equals \(\int_{D_R}V_N\) outside the \(O_R(N^{-3})\) input-norm exception.
Its upper deviations at any fixed positive threshold are bounded by \(2\exp[-c_{R,v}N/(L_N+\log N)^2]\).
Its mean and the mean of \(Z_N(\mathbf1_{D_R})\) tend to the same limit by the [base clipped convergence](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#statement), the cap-removal comparison there in C186v28, and the [expected planar logarithmic convergence](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#expected-planar-proof).
For large \(N\) their mean difference is less than \(v/3\).
On the norm event the difference of those integrals is exactly \(\int_{D_R}D_N\).
Their respective upward and downward deviations at threshold \(v/3\) and the norm exception give the claimed bound.

All three resulting probability sequences are summable for the indicated heights.
First Borel--Cantelli on the countable family of integer disks and positive rational thresholds, together with the [exact planar criterion and clipped potential convergence](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#statement), give the full-sequence conclusion.
Both computations use only the original distributions within each size.
The conditional estimate in item 1 controls oscillations around its own exterior mean when only one law has the additional regularity.
Item 2 controls the fluctuations of the full two-color statistic by imposing the stated scalar concentration condition on every random coordinate.
