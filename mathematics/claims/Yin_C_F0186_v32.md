# Complete convergence of the planar Wigner determinant under a second logarithmic moment

<a id="statement"></a>
## Model and planar conclusions

In the [original real two-color model](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#statement) put \(M=AB+2BA\).
For each nonconstant real raw law \(\sigma\) let \(Q_\sigma(r)=\sup_x\sigma([x-r,x+r])\).
The extra hypothesis on a specified law is
\[
 \int_1^\infty t Q_\sigma(e^{-t})\,dt<\infty .
 \tag{Second-log-concentration}
\]
This includes \(Q_\sigma(e^{-t})=O(t^{-2-\eta})\) with any \(\eta>0\) and permits unbounded laws satisfying both the concentration hypothesis and the original ordinary moment assumptions.
It excludes any nonconstant law having an atom.
Deterministic coordinates can be substituted before conditioning.

1. Suppose just the strict upper law of \(A\) satisfies (Second-log-concentration).
   For each size prescribe \(I\) of size \(k\le\delta N\) as in [C196v4](Yin_C_F0196_v4.md#statement) and expose the entire \(B\), the exterior of \(A_{II}\) and all its diagonals, giving \(\mathcal E\).
   On the cross-block rank event define \(Z_W(g)=\int g(z)\log|\det(\operatorname{diag}(U,U)-W(z))|\,dA(z)\) for the [D42 field](../drafts/Yin_D_F0042.md#repeated-interior), \(U=A_{II}\), and any fixed bounded real Borel \(g\) with compact support.
   The centered quantity uses its mean in the conditional law at the given exterior, which is finite for almost every exterior on the rank event.
   For each \(v>0\) the probabilities of
   \[
     \{|Z_W(g)-\mathbb E_{\mathcal E} Z_W(g)|>vN\}
   \]
   restricted to the rank event sum to a finite number.
   In fact the conditional probabilities there have a deterministic summable upper bound uniform in the exterior and the prescribed sizes \(k\le\delta N\), up to null sections.
   The rank failures themselves are summable.
2. Suppose both strict upper laws and every nondeterministic diagonal law satisfy (Second-log-concentration).
   For \(Z_N(g)=N^{-1}\int g(z)\log|\det(M-zI)|\,dA(z)\) and each \(v>0\),
   \[
     \sum_N\Pr\{|Z_N(g)-\mathbb E Z_N(g)|>v\}<\infty .
   \]
   For every prescribed \(R,v>0\) and \(L_N\to\infty\) with \(L_N=N^{o(1)}\), the [extreme singular-value loss](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#clipping-definition) satisfies
   \[
     \sum_N\Pr\!\left\{\int_{|z|<R}D_N(z)\,dA(z)>v\right\}<\infty .
     \tag{Second-log-planar}
   \]
   The bound includes the restriction to \(F_N(z)^c\) at the [height of C186v29](Yin_C_F0186_v29.md#statement).
   Under every cross-size coupling, on one event of probability one the logarithmic potentials converge locally in planar \(L^1\) and the empirical eigenvalue measures converge weakly to the Brown measure of \(s_1s_2+2s_2s_1\).
   Here \(s_1,s_2\) are free centered semicirculars of variance one.

The exponent two concerns the logarithm of a scalar concentration radius.
The result retains the fourth off-diagonal and second diagonal ordinary moment assumptions.
It establishes a sufficient condition for complete convergence without asserting necessity.

## From the one-row sublevel bound to integrated martingale differences

Use the [coefficient-normalized polynomial sublevel estimate](Yin_C_F0186_v31.md#product-polynomial-sublevel) at its stated first-absolute-moment scope.
Its proof applies at any fixed degree \(d\) before imposing a fourth logarithmic moment.
Write \(Q\) for the maximum concentration function of the finitely many random scalar laws to which the current assertion applies.
After deleting deterministic coordinates, two copies of the logarithm of the same nonzero conditional row polynomial differ by a variable \(D\) satisfying
\[
 \Pr(|D|>t\mid\text{other rows})
 \le C\{Q(C e^{-ct})+e^{-ct}\}=:q(t)
 \qquad (t\ge C_0\log(2+N)).
\]
Indeed subtract their common log maximum coefficient, use the two-sided bound twice at \(t/2\), and absorb the dimension prefactor of its exponential term by increasing \(C_0\) and decreasing \(c\).
The constants depend on the raw laws and the degree.
Use \(q_N(t)=1\) below that threshold and \(\min(1,q(t))\) above it.
By a change of logarithmic variable, \(\int_1^\infty t q(t)\,dt<\infty\).
In particular \(\int_0^\infty 2t q_N(t)\,dt\le C\log^2(2+N)\).

For the interior problem use \(m=k\) independent strict upper row groups of \(U\) after conditioning on \(\mathcal E\).
For the full problem use \(m=N\) independent raw upper row groups with both colors and both diagonals.
The degrees are at most four and six, respectively, as shown in the [two row exposures](Yin_C_F0186_v31.md#argument).
The characteristic determinant is monic up to sign in \(z\).
For the interior the D42 quotient differs from it by a fixed exterior multiplier with a logarithm locally integrable in \(z\) on each full-rank section.
The [base expected planar integrability](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) gives integrability of the full integrated logarithm and conditional integrability of the interior integral on almost every full-rank section.
Consequently the row polynomials are nonzero after substitution at almost every \(z\) and almost every other-row section, by Fubini as in C186v31.
These assertions need only the original within-size laws.
In the interior problem all following calculations take place on such a fixed exterior section.

If \(\|g\|_1=0\) the conclusions about this statistic are immediate.
Otherwise let \(Y\) be the corresponding unnormalized integrated logarithm divided by \(\|g\|_1\).
Expose the \(m\) rows successively, use the filtration \(\mathcal F_i\) and let \(H_i=\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F_i]-\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\).
Integrating over the fresh replacement \(i\)-th row and the future rows gives \(H_i=\mathbb E[Y-Y^{(i)}\mid\mathcal F_i]\).
For either of the even convex functions \(\phi(x)=x^2\) and \(\phi(x)=(|x|-a)_+\), conditional Jensen and Jensen for \(|g|\,dA/\|g\|_1\) give
\[
 \mathbb E[\phi(H_i)\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
 \le \int\mathbb E[\phi(D_i(z))\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
                 |g(z)|\,dA(z)/\|g\|_1.
\]
Here \(D_i(z)\) is the log difference at the two draws with the other rows fixed, and evenness absorbs the sign of \(g\).
Conditioning first on all the other rows in the right side is valid because these include the past.
The row tail and layer integration therefore imply almost surely
\[
 \mathbb E[H_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le V=C\log^2(2+N),\qquad
 \Pr\{|H_i|>b\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}\}\le
             \frac{2}{b}\int_{b/2}^\infty q_N(t)\,dt .
 \tag{Increment-bounds}
\]
The second inequality uses \(\mathbf1_{\{|x|>b\}}\le 2(|x|-b/2)_+/b\).

## Truncation and complete convergence at linear displacement

Fix \(x>0\), set \(b=xN/40\), and truncate each increment before centering it again,
\[
 K_i= H_i\mathbf1_{\{|H_i|\le b\}}-
          \mathbb E[H_i\mathbf1_{\{|H_i|\le b\}}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}].
\]
Then \(|K_i|\le y=2b\), their conditional means vanish and each conditional variance is at most \(V\).
If all \(|H_i|\le b\), the difference between their sum and \(\sum_i K_i\) has modulus at most \(m V/b\), using centering and \(\mathbb E[|H_i|\mathbf1_{\{|H_i|>b\}}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le V/b\).
This is \(O_x(\log^2(2+N))< xN/2\) eventually.
For the excluded event, the second bound in (Increment-bounds) and \(m\le N\) give
\[
 \Pr\{\max_i |H_i|>b\}\le C_x\int_{xN/80}^\infty q(t)\,dt
\]
for large \(N\).
Summing in \(N\) is legitimate since the count of \(N\) below \(80t/x\) is at most \(80t/x\) and \(\int_1^\infty t q(t)\,dt<\infty\).

For \(\lambda>0\) and \(|v|\le y\), the exponential series gives
\[
 e^{\lambda v}\le 1+\lambda v+
            v^2 (e^{\lambda y}-1-\lambda y)/y^2 .
\]
Applying this to each \(K_i\) and iterating the conditional expectations gives a bound \(\exp(mV(e^{\lambda y}-1-\lambda y)/y^2)\) on the moment generating function of their sum.
Use the same bound for \(-K_i\).
Take \(a=xN/2\), \(m\ge1\) and \(\lambda=y^{-1}\log(1+ay/(mV))\).
Exponential Markov and \(a/y=10\) then yield
\[
 \Pr\{|\textstyle\sum_i K_i|>a\}
 \le 2e^{a/y}(1+ay/(mV))^{-a/y}
 \le C_x\left(\frac{\log^2(2+N)}{N}\right)^{10}.
\]
These estimates give a deterministic summable bound for \(\Pr(|Y-\mathbb EY|>xN)\).
Applying them with \(x=v/\|g\|_1\) gives both centered assertions, with the conditional expectation in the first one.
No independence between sizes was used.

For the whole disk take \(g=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<R\}}\).
The [expected planar limit of the true potential](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#expected-planar-proof) and the [clipped mean comparison](Yin_C_F0186_v28.md#clipped-planar-defect) identify the same limiting expectation.
The latter proof bounds fixed-threshold upper deviations of the capped clipped disk integral by \(2\exp[-c_{R,v}N/(L_N+\log N)^2]\), and outside an event of probability \(O_R(N^{-3})\) the cap leaves the original clipped potential unchanged.
These inputs only require the base moment hypotheses.
Subtract the lower fluctuation of the integrated true potential, absorb the mean difference into \(v/3\) for sufficiently large \(N\), and apply the preceding complete convergence to obtain (Second-log-planar).
Positivity allows the further \(F_N^c\) restriction.
The [global clipping theorem and its exact planar criterion](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#statement), with first Borel--Cantelli on integer disks and positive rational thresholds, give the local potential and Brown conclusions.
The estimate compensates an interior-dependent near-mode through the realized characteristic determinant, including every such direction when the marginal hypothesis of item 2 holds.
