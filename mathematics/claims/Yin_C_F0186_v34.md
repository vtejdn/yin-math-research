# Logarithmic determinants with a positive imaginary part on an auxiliary complement

<a id="statement"></a>
## Determinants with semidefinite imaginary part

Use the [fixed complex finite-second-moment Wigner model](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#input-model).
All polynomial coefficients, matrix dimensions and spectral parameters below are deterministic and fixed.
Let \(H(x)=H_0+\sum_{j=1}^d H_jx_j\) with \(H_j=H_j^*\in M_m(\mathbb C)\), let \(Q\in M_m(\mathbb C)\) be positive semidefinite, and put \(L(x)=H(x)+iQ\).
Write \(L_N=L(W^{(N)})\), \(L_\infty=L(s)\), and \(\tau_m=\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau\).
If \(\dim\ker Q\le1\), then
\[
 \frac1{mN}\log|\det L_N|\ \longrightarrow\ \log\Delta_{\tau_m}(L_\infty)
 \quad\text{in probability along the even dimensions},
 \qquad
 \limsup_N\frac1{mN}\log|\det L_N|
   =\log\Delta_{\tau_m}(L_\infty)\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
The almost-sure assertion holds under every cross-size coupling permitted by F0013, on an event for the fixed model and coefficients.
The singular case with an identically zero direct summand has both logarithms equal to \(-\infty\).
All other cases covered by this corank condition have a finite free logarithm.
A fixed scalar rotation of \(L\) gives the same assertion.

More generally, decompose the auxiliary space as \(\ker Q\oplus(\ker Q)^\perp\), of dimensions \(k,r\), and write
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix} h&b^*\\ b&D\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad D=d+iQ_+,\quad h=h^*,\quad d=d^*,\quad Q_+\ge q I_r,\quad q>0.
\]
For \(1\le k\le r\), the same finite-target conclusion holds if a fixed contraction \(E:\mathbb C^r\to\mathbb C^k\) gives a square affine block \(B=Eb\) with finite free logarithm and convergence of its ordinary normalized logarithm in probability to that value through even dimensions.
The same argument works along any specified deterministic subsequence on which this witness has the stated probability convergence.
There is no independence requirement between \(B,h,b,d\).

<a id="scalar-domination"></a>
### Scalar polynomial application

Fix scalar noncommutative polynomials \(H=H^*,R_1,\dots,R_t\) and a nonzero scalar affine polynomial \(B\), allowing all of them to share input variables.
For
\[
 P=H+i\big(B^*B+\sum_{\nu=1}^t R_\nu^*R_\nu\big)
\]
and every fixed \(z\in\mathbb R\), \(N^{-1}\log|\det(P(W^{(N)})-zI)|\) converges in probability through even dimensions to the finite value \(\log\Delta_\tau(P(s)-z)\), and its almost-sure upper limit is that value under every allowed coupling.
If \(\operatorname{Im} z<0\), convergence holds almost surely through the full sequence.
In particular, the shared-variable choice \(B=x_1\) requires only the two input moments in the Task.
The boundary assertion also holds for any square scalar polynomial witness \(B\) having the same finite-target even-dimensional probability convergence.

## Proof

### Logarithmic tails of products

Write \(s_j(M)\) for singular values in decreasing order, pad by zeros when a rectangular compression is viewed in a larger space, and set
\[
 F_l(M)=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\log^+s_j(M),\qquad \log^+0=0 .
\]
Equivalently, \(F_l\) is the maximum of \(\log\|\bigwedge^j M\|\) over \(0\le j\le l\), with the zeroth exterior power equal to the identity.
Hence \(F_l(M_1M_2)\le F_l(M_1)+F_l(M_2)\), including for products through rectangular spaces.
Coordinate restrictions have \(F_l=0\).

For the proof, a family \(M_N\) of dimensions bounded by a fixed multiple of \(N\) has negligible top logarithmic sums if, for every \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
 \mathbb P\!\left\{F_{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}(M_N)>\eta N\right\}=0 .
 \tag{1}
\]
Use the selected even dimensions or subsequence throughout the probability estimates.
A statement on events of probability tending to one has the same force in (1), and inverses below are used on their invertibility events.
All fixed affine or polynomial blocks have (1) by the [singular-value positive-logarithm control](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#positive-tail): for any fixed \(T>0\), split \(\log^+s_j\) at height \(T\), paying at most \(T\delta N\) below it.
Rectangular blocks may be embedded in a fixed larger square polynomial.
Absolute constants and fixed changes to the proportional dimension do not affect (1).

If a square polynomial block \(B_N\) has a finite limiting free logarithm and its normalized log determinant converges in probability to that value, it is invertible with probability tending to one and \(B_N^{-1}\) has (1).
Indeed, by [weak singular-value convergence and the positive tails](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#statement), the integrals of \(\max(\log t,-T)\) against its singular measures converge to their free counterparts for fixed \(T\).
Subtract the convergent log determinant and then let \(T\to\infty\).
The normalized sum of \((\log^-s_j(B_N)-T)_+\) tends to zero in this order in probability, where \(\log^-t=\max(0,-\log t)\).
Splitting \(F_l(B_N^{-1})\) into the part below height \(T\) and that excess proves (1).

Conversely, for a fixed square polynomial \(M_N\) with invertibility probability tending to one, (1) for \(M_N^{-1}\) suffices for finite-target log convergence in probability.
For each desired probability and error tolerance, choose \(l=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\) so that \(F_l(M_N^{-1})/N\) is below the error tolerance with the desired probability.
On this event all but the largest \(l\) inverse log singular values are at most \(F_l(M_N^{-1})/l\), bounded by a fixed multiple of \(1/\delta\).
The sum of negative-log excesses above that fixed height is therefore at most \(F_l(M_N^{-1})\).
Consequently negative-log truncation has vanishing error in probability in the successive limits \(N\to\infty\), \(T\to\infty\).
At each fixed cutoff the integral converges to its deterministic free counterpart by the same weak convergence and positive tails.
For any two sufficiently large cutoffs their finite-matrix integrals differ by at most the larger truncation error with high probability, so their deterministic limits form a Cauchy family.
The limit is the finite free logarithm by monotone truncation, and the original normalized logarithms converge to it in probability.

### Schur complement on the kernel of the imaginary part

The bound \(\operatorname{Im}D_N\ge qI\) implies \(s_{\min}(D_N)\ge q\), hence \(\|D_N^{-1}\|\le q^{-1}\).
Suppress evaluation and size from the notation.
The Schur complement \(S=h-b^*D^{-1}b\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Im}S=T^*(\operatorname{Im}D)T\ \ge q T^*T,\qquad T=D^{-1}b .
\]
Indeed, \(-\operatorname{Im}D^{-1}=D^{-*}(\operatorname{Im}D)D^{-1}\).
For unit \(v\), \(\|Sv\|\ge|\langle Sv,v\rangle|\ge q\langle T^*Tv,v\rangle\).
Applying the max-min formula on the span of the first \(j\) right singular vectors of \(T\) yields \(s_j(S)\ge q\,s_j(T)^2\).

On the event that \(B=Eb\) is invertible, \(T\) has a left inverse \(K=B^{-1}ED\), where \(E\) includes the physical identity factor.
Its Moore--Penrose inverse is \(T^+=K\pi_{\operatorname{ran}T}\), so \(F_l(T^+)\le F_l(B^{-1})+F_l(D)\).
Reversing singular-value order in the preceding inequality gives
\[
 F_l(S^{-1})\le l\log^+(q^{-1})+2F_l(T^+).
\]
Thus \(S^{-1}\) has negligible top logarithmic sums.

The finite-size factorization
\[
 L=U\,\operatorname{diag}(S,D)\,V,\qquad
 U=\begin{pmatrix}I&b^*D^{-1}\\0&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 V=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\D^{-1}b&I\end{pmatrix}
\]
also gives \(\det L=\det S\det D\).
The off-diagonal blocks of \(U^{-1}-I,V^{-1}-I\) have negligible top logarithmic sums by the product bound and \(\|D^{-1}\|\le q^{-1}\).
The min-max inequality \(s_j(I+C)\le1+s_j(C)\) gives \(F_l(I+C)\le l\log2+F_l(C)\).
It follows that \(U^{-1}\) and \(V^{-1}\) satisfy (1).
For a direct sum the top sum is bounded by the sum of the top sums of the two summands.
Using \(L^{-1}=V^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(S^{-1},D^{-1})U^{-1}\) proves (1) for the inverse of the original polynomial \(L_N\).
The truncation argument now gives the probability limit with the \(1/(mN)\) normalization.
At the free level the same bounded Schur factors with triangular determinant one give
\[
 m\log\Delta_{\tau_m}(L_\infty)
 =k\log\Delta_{\tau_k}(S_\infty)+r\log\Delta_{\tau_r}(D_\infty)
\]
by [bounded FK multiplicativity and the triangular factors](Yin_C_F0186_v3.md#linearization-transfer).
The probability proof above uses weak convergence only for polynomial blocks.
Finally the [pathwise upper bound and marginal subsequence criterion](Yin_C_F0190_v1.md#statement) turn the finite-target probability limit along these sizes into the almost-sure upper-limit equality through all dimensions.

If \(k=0\), the deterministic singular-value floor directly gives full-sequence almost-sure log convergence from the pathwise weak and positive-tail controls.
For \(k=1\) and \(b\) not identically zero, choose one nonzero scalar component \(B\).
The [scalar affine theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v11.md#scalar-affine-statement) supplies its witness hypotheses along all even dimensions, including the constant nonzero case.
If \(b\equiv0\), the direct sum has the weighted sum of the scalar logarithm of \(h\) and the gapped logarithm of \(D\).
A zero \(h\) makes the full pencil singular, while every nonzero scalar affine \(h\) again has the preceding scalar result.
For \(m=k=1\) only this scalar block remains.

For the [scalar polynomial application](#scalar-domination), \(\operatorname{Im}(P_N-zI)\ge B_N^* B_N\) at real \(z\).
The same max-min inequality gives \(s_j(P_N-zI)\ge s_j(B_N)^2\), so (1) for its inverse follows from (1) for \(B_N^{-1}\).
The scalar conclusion and its coupling quantifier follow by the same truncation and subsequence argument.
For \(\operatorname{Im}z<0\) all singular values have the uniform floor \(-\operatorname{Im}z\), which yields the asserted full-sequence limit on the almost-sure polynomial control event.

## An irreducible two-dimensional example

Take \(A_0=iE_{22}\), \(A_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\), and \(A_2=E_{12}+E_{21}\).
The two variable coefficients generate \(M_2(\mathbb C)\), since the spectral projections of \(A_1\) are \(E_{11},E_{22}\) and sandwiching \(A_2\) gives each off-diagonal matrix unit.
For \(v=(\sqrt2,1)^t\), \(u=(-\sqrt2,1)^t\), both \(u^*A_1v\) and \(u^*A_2v\) vanish.
Thus the [bilinear coefficient frame](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#statement) is zero even for real test vectors.
The Schur complement is \(X-Y(2X+iI)^{-1}Y\), with \(B=Y\) as scalar witness.
Both within-size Wigner inputs may have arbitrary fixed complex finite-second-moment laws as in the Task.

For a scalar example at a free spectral point, set \(P=x+xyxyx+ix^2\) and \(z=0\).
Here \(H=x+xyxyx\) is selfadjoint and \(B=x\) witnesses the imaginary part.
The factorization \(P=(1+xyxy+ix)x\) gives approximate null vectors at the free evaluation, since the semicircular element \(x=s_1\) has zero in its spectrum and the other factor is bounded.
Thus the free determinant is positive by the scalar conclusion even though the free operator has no bounded inverse.
If the first input has pure imaginary off-diagonal entries and zero diagonal, its odd-dimensional matrices are skew-symmetric under transposition and singular.
The same factorization makes every odd-dimensional \(P(W)\) singular, while the even-dimensional logarithms converge in probability to the finite target.

At \(Q=0\) with \(m>1\), this method has no strictly positive pivot on an auxiliary complement.
The general [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) and [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) questions still require small-value control beyond the present hypotheses.
