# Logarithmic determinants from an observability matrix and tridiagonal Wigner pencils

<a id="statement"></a>
## Determinant convergence from a square observation

Let \(W^{(N)}=(W_1^{(N)},\ldots,W_d^{(N)})\) be the [fixed finite-second-moment Hermitian Wigner model](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#input-model), and let \(s=(s_1,\ldots,s_d)\) be its free semicircular limit.
All coefficients, auxiliary dimensions, numbers of inputs and polynomial degrees below are fixed independently of \(N\).
Fix a selfadjoint \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrix polynomial \(H=H^*\) and an \(r\)-by-\(m\) matrix polynomial \(J\) in the same selfadjoint variables, with arbitrary complex coefficients.
Form the square polynomial \(L=H+iJ^*J\) and the rectangular observability matrix
\[
 O=\begin{pmatrix}J\\ JH\\ \vdots\\ JH^a\end{pmatrix},\qquad a\ge0 .
\]
Suppose there is an \(m\)-by-\((a+1)r\) matrix polynomial \(E\) for which \(B=EO\) satisfies the following condition along a fixed deterministic infinite subsequence \(\mathcal N\):
\[
 \frac1{mN}\log|\det B(W^{(N)})|
 \ \xrightarrow[N\in\mathcal N]{\mathbb P}\
 \log\Delta_{\tau_m}(B(s))\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 \tau_m=\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau .
 \tag{1}
\]
Then \(\log\Delta_{\tau_m}(L(s))\) is finite and
\[
 \frac1{mN}\log|\det L(W^{(N)})|
 \ \xrightarrow[N\in\mathcal N]{\mathbb P}\
 \log\Delta_{\tau_m}(L(s)),\qquad
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1{mN}\log|\det L(W^{(N)})|
 =\log\Delta_{\tau_m}(L(s))\quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The latter conclusion holds under every allowed cross-dimensional coupling, on an event for the fixed polynomial data.
When \(H\) is affine and \(J\) is constant, (2) concerns exactly the ordinary determinant in [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract).
For \(m=1\) it applies to the polynomial in [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) at zero, or at a fixed shift upon writing the shifted polynomial in the displayed form.

<a id="tridiagonal-path"></a>
### An affine pencil with a single observed endpoint

For any \(m\ge2\), take \(H\) Hermitian tridiagonal with arbitrary selfadjoint scalar affine diagonal entries and nonzero scalar complex affine lower off-diagonal entries \(b_j=H_{j+1,j}\) for \(1\le j<m\).
The upper off-diagonal entries are their formal adjoints.
These affine entries may use any of the same independent Wigner inputs, with repeated occurrences allowed.
For \(J=e_m^*\), conclusion (2) holds through every even dimension in its probability assertion.
In this family \(J^*J=E_{mm}\) has corank \(m-1\).
When \(m>2\), the one-step square coupling condition for the \(m-1\) dimensional kernel in [the Schur-complement result](Yin_C_F0186_v34.md#statement) cannot hold with a one-dimensional positive complement.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Proof

For a matrix \(C\), write its singular values in decreasing order and set
\[
 F_l(C)=\sum_{j=1}^l\log^+s_j(C),\qquad \log^+0=0,
\]
padding by zeros beyond the rank.
The [logarithmic tail lemma](Yin_C_F0186_v34.md#logarithmic-tails-of-products) proves that \(F_l(CD)\le F_l(C)+F_l(D)\) for compatible rectangular matrices.
For equal-size rectangular \(C,D\), factor
\(C+D=[I\ I]\operatorname{diag}(C,D)[I\ I]^*\), where the first and last identities act on the appropriate output and input spaces.
These outer factors have norm \(\sqrt 2\), and \(F_l(\operatorname{diag}(C,D))\le F_l(C)+F_l(D)\).
Hence
\[
 F_l(C+D)\le l\log2+F_l(C)+F_l(D).
 \tag{3}
\]
Say an inverse has negligible top logarithmic sums along \(\mathcal N\) when its invertibility event has probability tending to one and, on that event, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\in\mathcal N}}
 \mathbb P\!\left(F_{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}(C_N^{-1})>\epsilon N,\
                  C_N\text{ invertible}\right)=0 .
 \tag{4}
\]
For any fixed polynomial evaluation the same top-sum bound on \(C_N\) itself, with no invertibility restriction, follows from the [positive-logarithm estimates](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#positive-tail).
This includes rectangular polynomials by embedding them in a square block matrix.

Fix a size in \(\mathcal N\) and suppress evaluation at \(W^{(N)}\).
By (1) and the cited tail lemma, \(B^{-1}\) has negligible top logarithmic sums.
On \(B\)'s invertibility event, \(K=B^{-1}E\) satisfies \(KO=I\).
If \(Lv=0\), the imaginary part of its quadratic form gives \(Jv=0\), and then \(Hv=0\) gives \(Ov=0\).
Thus \(v=0\), so \(L\) is invertible.
Write \(Z=L^{-1}\) and \(Y=JZ\).
We have
\[
 HZ=I-iJ^*Y,\qquad Y^*Y=-\operatorname{Im} Z .
 \tag{5}
\]
The second equality follows from \(Z-Z^*=Z^*(L^*-L)Z\).
For every unit \(v\), \(\|Zv\|\ge|\langle Zv,v\rangle|\ge\langle Y^*Yv,v\rangle\).
Max-min on the top \(j\) eigenspace of \(Y^*Y\) yields \(s_j(Y)^2\le s_j(Z)\).
Consequently
\[
 F_l(Y)\le\tfrac12 F_l(Z).
 \tag{6}
\]

For every \(n\ge1\), (5) gives
\(JH^nZ=JH^{n-1}-iJH^{n-1}J^*Y\).
Include the block \(JZ=Y\) at \(n=0\) and stack these identities as
\[
 OZ=F+GY ,
\]
where \(F\) has first row block zero and subsequent blocks \(JH^{n-1}\), and \(G\) has first row block \(I_r\) and subsequent blocks \(-iJH^{n-1}J^*\), for \(1\le n\le a\).
Both \(F\) and \(G\) are fixed rectangular matrix polynomials.
Applying \(K\), (3) and (6) gives the deterministic estimate
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_l(Z)&\le F_l(K)+l\log2+F_l(F)+F_l(G)+F_l(Y),\\
 F_l(Z)&\le 2\big(F_l(B^{-1})+F_l(E)+l\log2+F_l(F)+F_l(G)\big).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
Every term on the right obeys the bound required in (4).
Therefore \(L^{-1}\) has negligible top logarithmic sums.
Apply the converse in the [same tail lemma](Yin_C_F0186_v34.md#logarithmic-tails-of-products) to the fixed polynomial \(L\), whose singular-value laws converge and whose positive logarithms have the required control by [C186v4](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#statement).
It gives the finite free logarithm and probability limit along \(\mathcal N\).
The pathwise upper bound follows by truncating the logarithm from below for this polynomial, using its almost-sure singular-law and positive-tail controls.
Together with the probability subsequence it gives the claimed limsup by the [coupling criterion](Yin_C_F0190_v1.md#statement).

<a id="path-proof"></a>
### Computing the observation for the path

Set \(a=m-1\) and \(E=I_m\), so that \(B=O\) is square.
In the column order \(m,m-1,\ldots,1\) and row order \(e_m^* H^p\), \(0\le p\le m-1\), this matrix is lower block triangular.
Indeed reaching column \(m-p\) in \(p\) tridiagonal steps requires every step to decrease the index by one.
No column of smaller index is reachable.
Its diagonal blocks are \(1\) for \(p=0\) and \(b_{m-1}b_{m-2}\cdots b_{m-p}\) for \(p\ge1\).
Absolute finite determinants are invariant under the column permutation and multiplicative on triangular blocks.
Each \(b_j\) occurs for exactly \(j\) values of \(p\).
Thus
\[
 \frac{1}{mN}\log|\det O_N|=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{j}{N}\log|\det b_j(W^{(N)})| .
 \tag{8}
\]
For every \(j\), the [scalar affine theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v11.md#scalar-affine-statement) gives convergence in probability along even dimensions to the finite value \(\log\Delta_\tau(b_j(s))\).
There are finitely many factors, so the sum converges jointly in this sense, even when the factors share variables.

To verify the free target of (8) without taking inverses in the free algebra, one can return to the same top-sum lemma.
On the event where all diagonal blocks are invertible, denote the reordered triangular observation matrix by \(D_0+N_0\), with \(D_0\) block diagonal and \(N_0\) strictly lower block triangular.
Each block of \(D_0^{-1}\) is a product of at most \(m-1\) inverse affine factors, each satisfying (4).
The direct-sum and product bounds give (4) for \(D_0^{-1}\).
The strictly triangular matrix \(D_0^{-1}N_0\) has \(m\)-th power zero, giving the exact inverse
\[
 (D_0+N_0)^{-1}
  =\left(\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}(-D_0^{-1}N_0)^h\right)D_0^{-1}.
\]
The polynomial \(N_0\) has negligible top logarithmic sums, so (3) and the product inequality imply (4) for the full inverse.
The converse of the tail lemma for the polynomial \(O\) gives convergence to its finite free logarithm.
In particular \(\log\Delta_{\tau_m}(O(s))=m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j\log\Delta_\tau(b_j(s))\), verifying (1) and finishing the path proof.

<a id="irreducible-path"></a>
### Irreducible coefficients with a zero bilinear frame in every size \(m\ge3\)

Take \(H_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,m)\), \(H_2=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(E_{j,j+1}+E_{j+1,j})\), \(H=H_1x_1+H_2x_2\), and \(J=e_m^*\).
The spectral projections of \(H_1\) give each \(E_{jj}\), and sandwiching \(H_2\) between adjacent projections gives the corresponding oriented matrix units.
Products along the path give all \(E_{ij}\), hence the variable coefficients generate \(M_m(\mathbb C)\).
The real test vectors \(e_1,e_m\) kill both bilinear coefficients for \(m\ge3\), giving zero for the [coefficient frame](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#statement).
All edges equal \(x_2\), so the path proof applies for the original finite-variance laws.
