# Almost-sure planar convergence after removing the smallest singular values of a two-sided Wigner product

<a id="statement"></a>
## Model and conclusions

Let \(A_N,B_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices with the [fixed fourth off-diagonal and second diagonal moments](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#statement).
Their off-diagonal entries are centered and have variance \(1/N\).
All upper coordinates and both colors are independent at each dimension, with arbitrary coupling across dimensions.
Write \(P_N=A_NB_N+2B_NA_N\), \(P=s_1s_2+2s_2s_1\) for free standard semicircular inputs, and \(u(z)=\tau\log|P-z|\).
Let \(q_N=\lceil\log(N+3)\log\log(N+3)\rceil\), using only dimensions large enough that \(q_N<N\).
For singular values in decreasing order write \(p_j(Z)=\prod_{i=1}^j s_i(Z)\), with \(p_0=1\).
For the \(3N\)-dimensional companion \(L_N(z)\) displayed below, almost surely under each allowed coupling,
\[
 \frac1N\log p_{3N-q_N}(L_N(\cdot))\longrightarrow u,\qquad
 \frac1N\log p_{N-q_N}(P_N-\cdot I)\longrightarrow u
 \quad\text{in }L^1_{\rm loc}(\mathbb C).
 \tag{Trimmed}
\]
On each compact subset of \(\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R\) the same conclusion holds with \(q_N=\lceil C_K\log(N+3)\rceil\) for a sufficiently large constant depending on that compact set and the laws.
The normalization is by the original physical dimension \(N\) in both displays.

Consequently full-sequence weak convergence of the eigenvalue law to the Brown measure, almost surely under every allowed coupling, is equivalent to
\[
 \sum_N\Pr\!\left\{\frac{1}{N}
  \int_{|z|<R}\sum_{j>N-q_N}\log^-s_j(P_N-zI)\,dA(z)>b\right\}<\infty
 \quad(R\in\mathbb N,\ b\in\mathbb Q_{>0}).
 \tag{Terminal}
\]
Here \(\log^-x=\max(0,-\log x)\), and values on a finite eigenvalue set can be assigned arbitrarily in the planar integrals.
This equivalence leaves the displayed terminal estimates as the remaining probabilistic condition for this model.

## One step with a guarded minor

Use the [real symmetric model and companion](Yin_C_F0194_v30.md#statement) and fix \(z\) with \(\operatorname{Im}z\ne0\).
For a fixed nested order of physical sites in a size \(N\) sample, let \(L=L_n(z)\) be its principal companion at \(n\) sites and let \(L'=L_{n+1}(z)\) add the next site, keeping the original \(N^{-1/2}\) scaling.
Write \(A,B\) for the old \(n\)-site inputs and \(q=3n-r\) for the deficit of a deterministic index \(r\), with \(12\le q\le 3n\).
In physical block order,
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\ B&-I&0\\ A&0&-I\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad L'=\begin{pmatrix}L&U\\ V^*&D\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If \(a,b\) denote the two real, normalized fresh columns above their corner diagonals, then
\[
 Uw=(w_2a+2w_3b,\;w_1b,\;w_1a),\qquad
 Vw=(w_2b+w_3a,\;w_1a,\;2w_1b).
\]
Condition only on the old minor and fix its singular vector bases measurably.
On \(\max(\|A\|,\|B\|)\le K\) there are \(c,C>0\) depending only on the laws, \(z,K\), for which
\[
 p_{r+6}(L')\ge c\,p_r(L)(q/N)^3
 \tag{Paired-step}
\]
outside a conditional failure of probability at most \(CN^{12}e^{-cq}+CN^{-3}\).
Here the last probability comes from the fresh-row Frobenius guard used to extend a directional estimate over a net.
The conclusion is uniform in the corner diagonals.
For a prescribed chain of at most \(N\) steps the total failure probability is summable over \(N\) if the deficits are all at least \(C_0\log N\) for a sufficiently large fixed \(C_0\), with the norm guards included in the events of failure.

<a id="covariance"></a>
## Coercivity at a nonreal parameter

Fix a small \(\tau>0\) depending only on \(K,z\).
For a unit left singular vector, or any unit vector \(\ell=(h,g,j)\) satisfying \(\|L^*\ell\|\le\tau\), the second and third auxiliary equations give
\[
 g=Ah+e,\qquad j=2Bh+f,\qquad \|e\|+\|f\|\le 2\tau .
\]
Hence \(\|h\|\ge c_K>0\) for small enough \(\tau\), and \(\|(BA+2AB-\bar z I)h\|\le (1+2K)\tau\).
Writing a right near-null vector as \((h,g,j)\) instead gives \(g=Bh+e,\ j=Ah+f\) and \(\|(AB+2BA-zI)h\|\le (1+3K)\tau\).
For each of these two scalar polynomials and the corresponding parameter, the [individual-input affine gap](Yin_C_F0187_v30.md#individual-input-gap) bounds \(\|(\alpha A+\beta)h\|\) and \(\|(\alpha B+\beta)h\|\) below by \(c_{K,z}(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)^{1/2}\), after decreasing \(\tau\).
This uses the errors above before rescaling the coefficient pair, and includes complex \(\alpha,\beta\).

For fixed \(w\) with \(\|w\|=1\), independence, real coordinates and unit raw variance give
\[
 N\mathbb E|\ell^*Uw|^2
 =\|\bar w_2 h+\bar w_1 j\|^2+
   \|2\bar w_3 h+\bar w_1 g\|^2 .
\]
Insert the left graph formulas and apply the affine gap to \((2\bar w_1,\bar w_2)\) for \(B\) and \((\bar w_1,2\bar w_3)\) for \(A\).
The sum of the squared coefficient norms is at least one, and the combined error has norm at most \(2\tau\).
Thus the displayed conditional covariance is at least a fixed positive constant.
On a right near-null unit vector \(v=(h,g,j)\) the corresponding expression for \(Vw\) is
\[
 N\mathbb E|v^*Vw|^2
 =\|\bar w_3 h+\bar w_1 g\|^2+
   \|\bar w_2 h+2\bar w_1 j\|^2 .
\]
The right graph formulas give the same uniform positive lower bound.
Both bounds extend to every unit vector in the respective spectral subspaces at singular values at most \(\tau\).

<a id="concentration"></a>
## Projected borders and the alternative when few values are small

Let \(Q_L\) remove the first \(r+3\) left singular vectors of \(L\), and \(Q_R\) its first \(r\) right singular vectors.
If fewer than \(q/2\) singular values are at most \(\tau\), then \(s_{r+6}(L)\ge\tau\) because \(q\ge12\).
Singular-value monotonicity under compression gives \(p_{r+6}(L')\ge p_r(L)\tau^6\), sufficient for (Paired-step) because \(q/N\le3\).
Otherwise both projection spaces contain subspaces of dimension at least \(q/2-3\ge q/4\) where the respective graph residuals are at most \(\tau\).
Summing the preceding lower bounds over orthonormal bases gives
\[
 N\mathbb E\|Q_L Uw\|^2\ge c q,\qquad
 N\mathbb E\|Q_R Vw\|^2\ge c q
\]
for every unit \(w\), conditionally on the old minor.

Apply the [weighted fixed-cutoff argument](Yin_C_F0187_v10.md#finite-variance-projected-row) to each of these two maps.
For clarity, the raw-column maps \(\sqrt N Q_LUw,\sqrt N Q_RVw\) acting on the \(2n\) independent unscaled real input coordinates have operator norms at most \(3\), since each physical site uses the same \(3\)-by-\(2\) block of Frobenius norm at most \(3\) before projection.
The sums of squared column norms are at least \(cq\), and each squared norm is at most \(9\).
A fixed cutoff preserving a positive conditional variance selects at least a fixed proportion of this sum except with probability \(Ce^{-cq}\), by exponential Markov for the independent indicators.
Reveal the rejected coordinates and all the indicators.
Conditional on them, the norm of the projected raw row is convex and \(3\)-Lipschitz in independent bounded real coordinates, including the fixed shift from rejected variables.
On the retained-weight event its conditional second moment is at least \(c'q\).
The bounded-product concentration inequality used in the cited argument gives a median at least \(c''\sqrt q\) for sufficiently large \(q\) and lower-tail failure \(Ce^{-cq}\).
Enlarging \(C\) covers smaller \(q\).

Each unprojected raw border has squared Frobenius norm with conditional expectation at most \(CN\).
Markov gives norm at most \(N^2\) for both except with conditional probability \(CN^{-3}\).
A complex \(3\)-sphere net of mesh \(c'\sqrt q/N^2\) has cardinality at most \(CN^{12}\).
Thus both normalized projected borders have least singular value at least \(c\sqrt{q/N}\) with the stated failure bound.
The union over the two borders uses their actual shared coordinates.
Their column volumes are each at least \(c'(q/N)^{3/2}\).
The paired-block estimate with auxiliary size three gives (Paired-step).


<a id="rank-loss"></a>
## Fourth-moment truncation with a logarithmic deficit

In the old minor, suppose \(L_0\) is the companion of an old-minor-measurable pair of inputs with norms at most \(K\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(L-L_0)\le q/8\), with \(q\ge24\).
In the near-null branch of the preceding argument intersect the left subspace with \(\ker(L-L_0)^*\) and the right subspace with \(\ker(L-L_0)\).
Each resulting dimension is at least \(q/2-3-q/8\ge q/4\).
On these subspaces the corresponding residuals for \(L_0\) are bounded by \(\tau\).
The covariance formulas for the fresh borders are unchanged, so the conditional paired estimate follows with the bounded-input constants.
If the old minor has too few near-null directions the compression argument already applies directly.

For each full input first truncate the unscaled upper coordinates at \(b_N=\sqrt{N/\log N}\) and reflect off-diagonal pairs.
The [expected norm and bounded-product concentration proof](Yin_C_F0198_v1.md#sparse-truncation) applies at this threshold as follows.
The centered strict upper triangle has row and column variance sums bounded by one and the total of its normalized fourth moments is bounded, so the Latała expectation bound is uniform in \(N\).
Restoring the symmetric mean costs at most \(\sqrt N\,\mathbb E|\xi|^4/b_N^3=O((\log N)^{3/2}/N)\).
The truncated normalized diagonal has norm at most \(1/\sqrt{\log N}\).
The median of the full truncated norm is therefore bounded.
Its convex Lipschitz concentration for independent normalized coordinates of support diameter at most \(2/\sqrt{\log N}\) has tail \(C\exp(-c t^2\log N)\) at displacement \(t\) from the median.
A sufficiently large fixed displacement makes the norm exception summable.

Now restore coordinates with raw absolute value in \((b_N,\sqrt N]\).
For each color a prescribed off-diagonal entry exceeds \(b_N\) with probability at most \(C(\log N)^2/N^2\).
Independence at three distinct edges and a union over a site and three neighbors bound the probability that the medium-entry graph has degree at least three somewhere by \(C(\log N)^6/N^2\).
On its complement the normalized symmetric off-diagonal correction has absolute row sums at most two.
The restored diagonal has norm at most one.
Thus the pair truncated at \(\sqrt N\) has a fixed norm bound outside summable events.
The count \(M_N\) of all remaining large independent coordinates has bounded expectation by fourth-moment Markov for the two off-diagonal laws and second-moment Markov for the two diagonal laws.
Independence gives \(\Pr(M_N>h)\le(eC/h)^h\) for \(h>C\).
With \(h=\lceil 5\log N/\log\log N\rceil\) this is summable.
The companion differences on all nested principal minors have rank at most \(4M_N\), since each altered off-diagonal pair has rank at most two and each color occurs in two blocks.
The bounded comparison pair for each old minor is obtained just by truncating that minor and is old-minor-measurable.

It follows that the conditional rank and norm guards hold simultaneously for a prescribed nested chain with \(q\ge C_0\log N\), outside summable events for all sufficiently large \(N\).
The conditional probabilities of failures with these old-minor guards are summed before adding the exception for the full truncation event.
For \(z\) in a fixed compact set separated from the real axis the bounds \(c,C,\tau\) above can be chosen uniformly: the [affine-gap proof](Yin_C_F0187_v30.md#individual-input-gap) uses a lower bound on \(|\operatorname{Im} z|\), and the other deterministic bounds use only \(K\) and an upper bound on \(|z|\).
By increasing \(C_0\) on this parameter set, the bound \(N(CN^{12}e^{-cq}+CN^{-3})\) is summable.
This assertion applies to each deterministic sequence of parameters in the set, and does not assert simultaneous success for all its points.

<a id="initial-product"></a>
## The initial singular product

Let \(m=\lfloor N/\log^3(N+3)\rfloor\), \(n=N-m\), \(t=\sqrt{n/N}\), \(q_0=3m+q_N\), and \(r_0=3n-q_0\).
Fix a nonreal \(w\), and use the parameter \(z_N=t^2 w\) for the entire chain from size \(n\) to size \(N\).
The initial \(n\)-site old inputs have \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Let \(L_n^{\rm can}(w)\) denote the companion of those two matrices multiplied by \(1/t\), which have the canonical \(n^{-1/2}\) Wigner marginals.
Block multiplication gives
\[
 L_n(z_N)=S_tL_n^{\rm can}(w)S_t,\qquad S_t=\operatorname{diag}(tI_n,I_n,I_n).
\]
Each singular value lies between \(t^2\) and \(1\) times its canonically normalized counterpart, by the singular-value variational principle applied also with \(S_t^{-1}\).
The resulting change to \(N^{-1}\log p_{r_0}\) is bounded by \(2r_0|\log t|/N=o(1)\) whenever the product is positive.

Consider the canonical companion at fixed \(w\), with normalized singular law \(\nu_n\).
Its free logarithm under \(\operatorname{tr}_3\otimes\tau\) equals \(u(w)/3>-\infty\), by eliminating the two constant diagonal auxiliary blocks, FK multiplicativity and the [free finite-log input](Yin_C_F0186_v26.md#statement).
The [affine shrinking count](Yin_C_F0195_v1.md#statement) supplies fixed \(a,\gamma>0\) with
\(\mathbb E\nu_n([0,s])\le C s^a\) for \(n^{-\gamma}\le s\le1\).
The expected count at \(n^{-\gamma}\) is \(O(n^{1-a\gamma})=o(q_0)\).
Resampling one independent upper row block changes the companion and its Hermitian dilation by bounded rank.
The [bounded-variation row inequality](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration) for the threshold indicator gives an exceptional probability at most \(2\exp(-c q_0^2/n)\) for the count to exceed \(q_0\).
This is summable over \(N\).

With probability one,
\[
 \int\log\max(s,n^{-\gamma})\,d\nu_n(s)\ \longrightarrow\ u(w)/3 .
 \tag{Initial-clipping}
\]
Indeed for its bounded negative part, row concentration with variation at most \(C\log n\) gives summable deviations at each fixed tolerance.
For the expectations the tail below a fixed \(0<d<1\), relative to a clipping at \(d\), is at most
\(\int_{n^{-\gamma}}^d C s^{a-1}\,ds\le C d^a/a\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
For the fixed bounded clip use [expected weak convergence](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#finite-variance-weak) of the selfadjoint Hermitian dilation.
The positive part converges by the [pathwise positive logarithmic tail estimates](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics) and weak convergence.
These fixed-canonical-dimension statements also apply along the present old blocks: eventually \(n=N-m\) is nondecreasing and each value occurs at most twice, because the derivative of \(x/\log^3(x+3)\) lies between zero and \(1/2\) for large \(x\).
Partition into the two deterministic subsequences of first and second occurrences, extending missing canonical dimensions on an enlarged space if necessary.
The cited every-coupling statements therefore apply to each subsequence.
This also gives uniform integrability of the positive logarithms on these paths.

Eventually at most \(q_0\) singular values are at or below the cutoff, so the \(r_0\) retained ones have the true logarithm in (Initial-clipping).
The total positive clipped logarithm among the \(q_0\) omitted terms is \(o(n)\) by positive logarithmic uniform integrability and \(q_0/n\to0\).
Thus \(\liminf_N N^{-1}\log p_{r_0}(L_n(z_N))\ge u(w)\).

## Propagation to the final size and planar convergence

After \(j\) steps, use \(r_j=r_0+6j\) in dimension \(3(n+j)\).
Its deficit \(q_j=q_0-3j\) ends at \(q_m=q_N\).
For fixed nonreal \(w\), the parameters \(t^2 w\) eventually lie in a compact set separated from the real axis.
Both for \(q_N/\log N\to\infty\) and for a sufficiently large fixed logarithmic coefficient on a specified compact set, the preceding summable bounds apply to the chain.
The sum of the negative logarithms of its \(m\) lower factors \(c(q_j/N)^3\) is bounded above by \(O(m\log N)=o(N)\).
The initial lower bound consequently gives \(\liminf_N N^{-1}\log p_{3N-q_N}(L_N(t^2w))\ge u(w)\) almost surely.

For the upper bound, replace \(\log s\) by \(\log\max(s,d)\) for fixed \(0<d<1\) in the full companion.
Removing \(q_N\) terms can increase this upper bound by at most \(q_N|\log d|\).
The full fixed-\(d\) statistic at \(t^2w\) differs from that at \(w\) by at most \(3d^{-1}|w-t^2w|\) after division by \(N\), since ordered singular values are Lipschitz in the perturbation norm.
Its fixed-\(w\) limit follows from weak convergence of the dilation and positive logarithmic uniform integrability.
Letting \(d\downarrow0\) gives \(\limsup\le u(w)\).
Fubini applied to the measurable products gives almost-sure convergence for area-almost every \(w\) simultaneously.

Write \(B_N(z)=N^{-1}\log p_{3N-q_N}(L_N(z))\) and \(u_N(z)=N^{-1}\log|\det(P_N-zI)|\).
For almost every \(z\), \(\det L_N(z)=\det(P_N-zI)\).
On each compact disk \(K\) there is an eventually finite pathwise constant \(C_{K,\omega}\) such that
\[
 u_N(z)-C_{K,\omega}\le B_N(z)\le C_{K,\omega}.
\]
Indeed \(\sum_i\log^+s_i(L_N(z))/N\) bounds both the right side and the total removed positive logarithms on the left.
It is uniformly bounded there by the positive tail control at \(z=0\) and the perturbation bound \(s_i(L_N(z))\le s_i(L_N(0))+\sup_K |z|\).
The [uniform local planar square bound for \(u_N\)](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) therefore also holds for \(B_N(t^2\cdot)\) by changing variables; \(t\to1\).
Uniform integrability and the pointwise conclusion imply \(B_N(t^2\cdot)\to u\) in local planar \(L^1\).
Changing variables back preserves this convergence because fixed locally integrable functions are continuous in \(L^1_{\rm loc}\) under dilations tending to the identity.
For the fixed-coefficient logarithmic trim on a given compact set away from the real axis, apply the same reasoning on a slightly larger compact neighborhood before changing variables.

Finally eliminate the last two diagonal blocks in \(L_N(z)\).
With \(F=(A_N,2B_N)\) and \(H=(B_N,A_N)^{\mathsf T}\) denoting a block column (the two blocks are stacked), the factors are
\[
 L_N(z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}I&-F\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
 \operatorname{diag}(P_N-zI,-I_{2N})
 \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\-H&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The factors and their inverses have norms at most \(C(1+\|A_N\|+\|B_N\|)\), which are polynomial in \(N\) almost surely eventually by Frobenius Markov at \(N^2\) with summable exceptions.
Consequently the ordered singular values of the companion and of the middle block are within factors \(N^{\pm C}\).
Their full determinant moduli agree, so for almost every \(z\) their retained logarithmic sums differ by at most \(C q_N\log N\), by comparing the omitted bottom terms.
Inserting \(2N\) unit singular values changes the sum of the bottom \(q_N\) scalar logarithms only through omitted positive logarithms, of total size \(O_{K,\omega}(q_N\log N)\) on a compact disk by the same norm bounds for \(P_N-zI\).
Dividing by \(N\) proves the scalar assertion in (Trimmed).

The scalar trimmed statistic minus \(u_N\) is its terminal sum of negative logarithms divided by \(N\), up to the terminal positive logarithms just bounded by \(o(1)\) uniformly on each disk.
Thus the nonnegative terminal integral vanishes precisely when \(u_N\to u\) in local planar \(L^1\) on the control event.
The [finite-variance potential comparison in both directions](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#remaining-defect) equates this with weak Brown convergence there.
For every-coupling almost-sure convergence, first Borel--Cantelli gives sufficiency of (Terminal), and independent dimensions with second Borel--Cantelli give necessity.
