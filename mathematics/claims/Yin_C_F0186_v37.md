# Logarithmic determinants of free elliptic pencils under finite-variance Wigner evaluation

<a id="statement"></a>
## Universal Hermitian full rank

Let \(L(x)=A_0+\sum_{j=1}^g A_jx_j\) be a fixed complex \(m\)-by-\(m\) affine pencil and let \(W^{(N)},s\) be the Hermitian Wigner inputs and free semicircular family of [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#input-model).
Suppose that \(L(X)\) is invertible at every Hermitian tuple of every finite size.
It suffices to test this assumption on Hermitian tuples of sizes \(1\) through \((g+1)m^2\).
Under each allowed coupling across dimensions, the finite ordinary logarithms satisfy
\[
 \frac1{mN}\log|\det L(W^{(N)})|
 \ \longrightarrow\
 \log\Delta_{\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau}(L(s))\in\mathbb R
 \qquad\text{almost surely and in }L^p\ (1\le p<\infty).
 \tag{1}
\]
The tuple in the rank test may have arbitrarily large norm.
Neither the hypothesis nor the proof imposes a common positive singular-value lower bound over those tuples.

<a id="elliptic-definition"></a>
For matrices over \(\mathbb C\), use the following free ellipticity criterion of [Klep, Pascoe and Volčič, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.6](https://igorklep.github.io/files/regular_fun_23dec16.pdf).
For a \(d\)-by-\(e\) pencil with \(d\ge e\), choose \(D\in M_{e,d}(\mathbb C)\) such that
\[
 C=\operatorname{Re}(DA_0)\succeq0,\quad C\ne0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Re}(DA_j)=0\quad(1\le j\le g),\qquad
 \operatorname{Re}M=(M+M^*)/2 .
\]
When \(C>0\) the recursion terminates successfully.
Otherwise let \(V\) have orthonormal columns spanning \(\ker C\), and repeat the test with the narrower pencil \(LV\).
Existence of such a terminating chain defines free ellipticity.
Definition 2.1 allows any basis of the kernel, which yields the same test by changing the coordinates in the next multiplier by congruence.
Theorem 2.6 identifies this property with full column rank at every Hermitian tuple, equivalently at every tuple of size at most \((g+1)e^2\).
This exact rank characterization is the external input.
The estimates below concern the size of the inverse on Wigner evaluations even when the strongly free elliptic lower bound from Proposition 2.4 fails.

<a id="partial-chain"></a>
A partially completed chain also gives a determinant transfer for a square starting pencil.
Let \(T\) be the tall pencil that remains after any number of permitted kernel restrictions.
On a fixed deterministic subsequence, suppose \(T(W^{(N)})\) has full column rank with probability tending to one, and that the sum of the largest \(\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\) positive logarithms of its pseudoinverse singular values, divided by \(N\), vanishes in probability as \(N\to\infty\) on that event and then \(\delta\downarrow0\), as in [the inverse-log criterion](Yin_C_F0186_v35.md#argument).
Then the normalized logarithms of the starting square pencil converge in probability along this same subsequence to the finite free logarithm.
Their full-dimensional upper limit equals that target almost surely under every allowed coupling.
A square compression \(B=ET\) suffices if its normalized logarithmic determinants converge in probability on this subsequence to its own finite free logarithmic determinant, where \(E\) is a fixed matrix polynomial of the appropriate dimensions.
This alternative admits pencils that are singular at some Hermitian tuples.

<a id="argument"></a>
## A dimension-independent bound after one kernel restriction

For a rectangular matrix \(M\) let \(M^\dagger\) be the Moore--Penrose inverse and set
\[
 F_l(M)=\sum_{j=1}^l\log^+s_j(M)
\]
with singular values in decreasing order and zero padding.
Use \(\log^+0=0\).
The [exterior-power inequalities](Yin_C_F0186_v35.md#argument) give \(F_l(AB)\le F_l(A)+F_l(B)\) and \(F_l(A+B)\le l\log2+F_l(A)+F_l(B)\).

Evaluate a step \(D,C,V\) of the chain at an arbitrary Hermitian tuple \(X\) of size \(N\), suppressing tensor products with the identity on constant factors.
Assume \(LV\) has full column rank and let \(G=(LV)^\dagger\).
Then \(L=L(X)\) itself has full column rank: if \(Lv=0\), the identity \(\operatorname{Re}(DL)=C\) gives \(C^{1/2}v=0\), whence \(v=VV^*v\), and \(LV\) is injective.
For \(Z=L^\dagger\) one has \(LZ=\pi_{\operatorname{ran}L}\).
Put \(Y=C^{1/2}Z\) and \(T_C=(C^{1/2})^\dagger\).
We have
\[
 Y^*Y=\operatorname{Re}(Z^*DLZ),\qquad
 Z=VG LZ+(I-VG L)T_CY .
 \tag{2}
\]
For the second identity, decompose \(Z=VV^*Z+T_CY\), apply \(G L\), and use \(GLV=I\).
If \(\operatorname{Re}M\succeq0\), the inequality
\(\|Mv\|\ge|\langle Mv,v\rangle|\ge\langle(\operatorname{Re}M)v,v\rangle\) for unit vectors and the max-min principle give \(s_j(\operatorname{Re}M)\le s_j(M)\) for every \(j\).
Apply this to (2), recalling that \(LZ\) is an orthogonal projection:
\[
 2F_l(Y)\le F_l(Z)+l\log^+\|D\|.
\]
Since \(V,LZ\) are contractions and \(T_C\) is a fixed amplified constant, (2) and the sum and product inequalities yield
\[
 F_l(Z)\le c l+ 2 F_l(G)+F_l(L)+\tfrac12 F_l(Z)
\]
with \(c\) depending only on the fixed step.
Thus \(F_l(L^\dagger)\le 2c l+4 F_l((LV)^\dagger)+2 F_l(L)\).
At a final positive-definite step, \(\|D\|\|Lv\|\|v\|\ge\langle Cv,v\rangle\) bounds \(\|L^\dagger\|\) by a fixed constant at every size and tuple.

At each level \(P=P_0+\sum P_jx_j\), the same inequalities imply
\[
 F_l(P(X))\le c' l+c'\sum_{j=1}^g F_l(X_j).
\]
Indeed, the singular values of \(P_j\otimes X_j\) are the pairwise singular-value products, so each singular value of \(X_j\) is used at most \(\operatorname{rank}P_j\) times.
It follows by finite induction that any tall free elliptic pencil satisfies
\[
 F_l(L(X)^\dagger)\le K l+K\sum_{j=1}^g F_l(X_j)
 \tag{3}
\]
for all l, sizes and Hermitian tuples.
Along a partial chain the same bound holds with the extra term \(K F_l(T(X)^\dagger)\) on the full-column-rank event of its terminal restriction.

<a id="probability"></a>
## Logarithmic limits

For each allowed coupling, the [positive-log tail law](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics) for the input matrices gives
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_N N^{-1}F_{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}(W_j^{(N)})=0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Indeed first split the logarithm at any fixed large height, with cost at most that height times \(\delta\) on the lower portion, and use the pathwise uniform integrability of the positive logs.
Equation (3) gives the same assertion for \(L(W)^{-1}\), with finite inverses at every dimension.

For clarity, at \(l=mN\) inequality (3) first bounds the total normalized negative logarithms pathwise eventually, since the inputs have bounded mean positive logarithms there.
If that eventual bound is \(M\), for fixed \(\delta\) every inverse logarithm above height \(2M/\delta\) lies among the first \(\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\) indices for all sufficiently large \(N\), taking the total bound normalized by \(N\).
Hence (3) at small \(l\) gives uniform integrability of the negative logs of \(L(W)\) along the path.
Apply the [fixed matrix-polynomial weak law](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#finite-variance-weak) to \(L^*L\), and the [positive tail estimate](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics) to \(L\).
The truncated log integrals converge to the corresponding free truncated logs.
The two-sided tail bounds make these limits Cauchy as the cutoffs are removed, proving (1) almost surely with finite free target.

For the moment assertion, the sum and product bounds on \(L(W)\) itself and (3) at \(l=mN\) bound the absolute normalized log determinant by a constant times \(1+\sum_j N^{-1}\sum_i\log^+s_i(W_j)\).
For any fixed \(h\ge1\), Jensen's inequality and the [expected singular-value power bound](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#positive-tail) give a bounded \(h\)-th moment of this upper bound, uniformly in dimension.
Taking \(h>p\) and using almost-sure convergence proves (1) in \(L^p\).

For a partial chain, the last assertion after (3) transfers the terminal probabilistic rank and inverse-log bounds to the square starting pencil.
The [probabilistic truncation lemma](Yin_C_F0186_v34.md#logarithmic-tails-of-products) then gives its finite logarithmic probability limit.
Fixed-parameter regularization gives the almost-sure upper bound, and the [marginal criterion](Yin_C_F0190_v1.md#statement) yields the every-coupling upper-limit equality.
If using the compression \(B=ET\), its inverse tail bound follows from the same truncation lemma.
On its invertibility event, \(B^{-1}E\) is a left inverse of \(T\), and \(T^\dagger=B^{-1}E\pi_{\operatorname{ran}T}\) transfers the bound by the product inequality and the positive-log control of the polynomial \(E\).

<a id="scalar-application"></a>
## Zero-free values of a scalar polynomial

Fix a scalar noncommutative polynomial \(P\) and \(z\in\mathbb C\).
Use the [word-chain linearization](../ledger/Yin_L_F0061_v2.md#linearization-transfer) with auxiliary size \(m\).
Its unit upper triangular auxiliary pivot is invertible on every Hermitian tuple, including singular evaluations of the original polynomial.
Thus its full pencil \(L_z\) is free elliptic precisely when \(P(X)-zI\) is invertible on every finite Hermitian tuple.
By Theorem 2.6 this can equivalently be tested up to size \((g+1)m^2\).
Whenever this holds, (1) and the exact finite and free ordinary determinant identities give
\[
 N^{-1}\log|\det(P(W^{(N)})-zI)|
 \longrightarrow \log\Delta_\tau(P(s)-z)
 \quad\text{almost surely and in every finite }L^p,\quad p\ge1 .
\]
The scalar logarithms equal \(m\) times the normalized linearized logarithms on both sides.
For a partial chain, the terminal inverse-log hypothesis instead gives the every-coupling limsup through the same identities.

<a id="limits"></a>
## Constant multipliers and an obstruction with irreducible coefficients

Invertible constant left and right changes of basis \(L'=SLT\) preserve universal full rank.
They change every normalized square log determinant by the same constant \(m^{-1}\log|\det S\det T|\) in finite and free dimensions.
For a multiplier at the first step of \(L'\), congruence by \(T^{-1}\) shows that \(D=T^{-*}D'S\) is a first-step multiplier of \(L\), since \(D L=T^{-*}(D' L')T^{-1}\).
This gives an explicit obstruction for pencils with both \(I_m\) and \(iI_m\) among their variable coefficients: simultaneous vanishing of \(\operatorname{Re}D\) and \(\operatorname{Re}(iD)\) forces \(D=0\).

For every \(m\ge3\), one may add the variable coefficients \(\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,m)\) and \(\sum_{j<m}(E_{j,j+1}+E_{j+1,j})\).
Their adjacent matrix units and diagonal spectral projections generate \(M_m(\mathbb C)\), and \(e_1,e_m\) annihilate all four bilinear tests in the [coefficient frame](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#statement).
Choose a scalar constant coefficient \(c I_m\) with \(c>2\sum_{j=1}^4\|A_j\|\).
The free evaluation is boundedly invertible by a Neumann series, but the scalar Hermitian input \(X_1=-c,\ X_2=X_3=X_4=0\) is a singular witness and no nonzero first-step multiplier exists.
Thus the recursive certificate alone cannot establish [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) for general fixed coefficients.

There are free elliptic pencils for which an invertible first multiplier is unavailable.
For \(L=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\x&1+iy\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), writing \(D=(d_{ij})\), the variable constraints \(\operatorname{Re}(DE_{21})=0=\operatorname{Re}(iDE_{22})\) give \(d_{22}=d_{12}=0\).
The first multiplier \(D=E_{11}\) and final multiplier \((0,1)\) on the restricted column certify free ellipticity.
At \(x\to\infty,y=0\) the smallest singular value tends to zero even at scalar size.
This example illustrates the role of singular multipliers and logarithmic estimates rather than a uniform numerical gap.
