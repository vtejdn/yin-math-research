# Brown measures and ordinary determinants for Wigner laws with a translation modulus

<a id="statement"></a>
## Models and conclusions

Use the fixed Wigner laws, normalizations, within-size independence and arbitrary cross-dimensional couplings of [F0012](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract), [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) and [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract).
For each color choose either real symmetric matrices with real raw off-diagonal entries or Hermitian matrices with complex raw off-diagonal entries.
The raw diagonal laws are real in both cases.
Assume that each of these finitely many raw laws \(\mu\) satisfies a total-variation translation bound in its declared coordinate space \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb R^2\):
\[
 \|\mu(\cdot-h)-\mu\|_{\rm TV}\le C |h|^\alpha,\qquad |h|\le1,\quad \alpha>0.
 \tag{T}
\]
Here total variation is the supremum of absolute differences on Borel sets, and \(|h|\) is the Euclidean norm.
Take one common \(C,\alpha\) by reducing the exponent if necessary.
Assume the centered variance-one off-diagonal laws have a \(2+\delta\) absolute moment for some \(0<\delta\le1\), and the centered diagonal laws have finite second moments.
Real and complex colors may occur in the same tuple.
Real and imaginary parts within a complex entry can be dependent.
Examples include centered uniform interval laws in the real coordinates and planar uniform rectangle laws in the complex coordinates, with variances chosen as in the model.
For these examples the measure of the translated symmetric difference is \(O(|h|)\).

Fix a square affine pencil \(L=b+\sum_{j=1}^g A_j x_j\) of coefficient size \(m\), and put
\[
 U_N=(mN)^{-1}\log|\det L(W^{(N)})|,\qquad
 u=\log\Delta_{\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau}(L(s)).
\]
Write \(\mathcal G\) for the dimensions in which the mixed deterministic evaluation space has some tuple with invertible \(L\).
This space uses real symmetric inputs for the real colors and arbitrary Hermitian inputs for the complex colors.

If \(u\) is finite, \(U_N\to u\) almost surely and in every finite \(L^p\), \(p\ge1\), along \(\mathcal G\).
Every sufficiently large even dimension belongs to \(\mathcal G\); for an all-complex tuple every sufficiently large dimension does.
For a mixed or real tuple, existence of a single odd witness is necessary and sufficient for full-sequence convergence at a finite target.
In all dimensions outside \(\mathcal G\), \(U_N=-\infty\) identically.
If \(u=-\infty\), then \(U_N\to-\infty\) almost surely through all dimensions.
In particular the [F0013 upper-limit equality](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) holds for every fixed pencil under these laws.

The [exact scalar linearization](../ledger/Yin_L_F0061_v2.md#linearization-transfer) gives the [F0014 upper-limit equality](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) for every fixed scalar polynomial \(P\) and every fixed complex \(z\), including \(z=P(0)\).
For nonconstant \(P\), the free target is finite and the same finite-target conclusions on the good dimensions hold for the scalar logarithms.
In addition, for each fixed \(P\) the empirical eigenvalue measures of \(P(W^{(N)})\) converge weakly almost surely to the Brown measure of \(P(s)\) on one event under each allowed coupling.

<a id="floor-statement"></a>
Under (T) alone and finite second moments, the following quantitative input holds without the \(2+\delta\) assumption.
For each fixed pencil there exist \(b_*,c,C>0\), depending on its coefficients and the input laws, with
\[
 \Pr\{s_{\min}(L(W^{(N)}))\le\varepsilon\}
 \le C N^{5/2}\varepsilon^c
 \quad (N\in\mathcal G,\ N\text{ sufficiently large},\ 0<\varepsilon\le N^{-b_*}).
 \tag{1}
\]
The good-dimension least-value deviations below a suitable inverse power thus have summable probabilities, and the exponent can be enlarged to obtain any prescribed inverse-power probability.
The moment above two is used below in removing the intermediate singular-value cutoff.

<a id="argument"></a>
## A translated Gaussian tube under entry-law perturbations

For either coordinate dimension increase \(C\) so that (T) also holds for \(|h|>1\), since total variation is at most one.
If \(g\) is a centered Gaussian vector with fixed nonsingular covariance on that coordinate space, convex mixing and (T) show that the total variation distance from the law of \(X\) to that of \(X+\sigma g\) is at most \(C\sigma^\alpha\mathbb E|g|^\alpha\).
Independence and telescoping the product laws give
\[
 \|\operatorname{law}(W)-\operatorname{law}(W+\sigma G)\|_{\rm TV}
       \le C N^2 \sigma^\alpha .
 \tag{2}
\]
Here \(G\) is independent of \(W\) and consists of independent normalized GOE or GUE matrices in the declared color spaces, with off-diagonal variances one before division by \(N\) and diagonal raw variances two for GOE and one for GUE.
Under both conventions the map from independent standard real Gaussian coordinates to Gaussian matrices multiplies the Euclidean metric by a factor between \(N^{-1/2}\) and \(\sqrt{2/N}\) in the product Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
The map is a real linear isomorphism onto the mixed evaluation space.

We detail the deterministic repair on this space.
Given an actual mixed tuple \(X\) and a complex unit vector \(v=(v_1,\ldots,v_m)\) with \(\|L(X)v\|\le\varepsilon\), let the columns of the real isometry \(U\) span the real and imaginary parts of all \(v_i\).
Thus \(v_i=Uw_i\) and \(k=\dim\operatorname{ran}U\le 2m\).
Use a real isometry \(V\) onto the span of the real and imaginary parts of every column of \((I-UU^t)X_jU\).
This gives \(\ell\le2gk\); its columns are orthogonal to \(U\).
Write \(X_jU=UC_j+VE_j\).
Here \(C_j\) is symmetric for a real color and Hermitian for a complex color, and \(E_j\) is real or complex accordingly.
The two residuals are
\[
 (b\otimes I_k+\textstyle\sum_j A_j\otimes C_j)w,\qquad
 (\textstyle\sum_j A_j\otimes E_j)w.
\]
Their real and imaginary parts together with \(\|w\|^2-1\) define a quadratic real polynomial map in a number of real coordinates depending only on \(g,m,k,\ell\).
There are finitely many such types.
At the actual compressed point its norm is at most \(\varepsilon\) and the squared coordinate norm is bounded by \(C(1+M^2)\) on \(\max_j\|X_j\|\le M\).

For maps with nonempty zero sets, the [global polynomial error bound](Yin_C_F0186_v1.md#error-bound) and the [growth calculation](Yin_C_F0186_v3.md#global-repair-growth) give a zero at distance at most \(C(1+M^2)\varepsilon^a\) for a common \(a>0\).
Exactly corrected coefficients lift to mixed matrices by adding
\[
 U\,\Delta C_j\,U^t+V\,\Delta E_j\,U^t+U\,\Delta E_j^*\,V^t .
\]
The total Hilbert--Schmidt norm is at most a constant times that same bound.
The lifted matrices obey the required transpose or adjoint identities, and the corrected \(w\) gives a nonzero exact kernel vector.
For a type with empty zero set the auxiliary polynomial map \((x,y)\mapsto(yF(x),y(y-1))\) in [the same global calculation](Yin_C_F0186_v3.md#global-repair-growth) gives the lower residual bound \(c_0(2+|x|^2)^{-E}\).
The finite set of types thus allows a common \(\theta>0\) so small that \(2\theta<a\) and \(2E\theta<1\) for every empty type, omitting the respective restriction when there is no such type.
Taking \(M=\varepsilon^{-\theta}\) excludes the empty types for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).
For every remaining realization with these norm and least-value guards the distance to the singular-input set is at most \(C\varepsilon^{a-2\theta}\).
If there are no nonempty types, the event on the norm guard is itself empty for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).

Take \(0<\lambda<a-2\theta\) and \(\sigma=\varepsilon^\lambda\) in (2).
For the original tuple, the probability outside the norm guard is at most \(CN\varepsilon^{2\theta}\) by the expected squared Frobenius norms and Markov's inequality.
Transfer only the joint norm and least-value event by (2) to the perturbed tuple.
Conditioned on the original matrices as centers, its singular set in standard Gaussian coordinates is the zero set of a nonzero nonnegative real polynomial, the squared modulus of the determinant after translation, scaling and the invertible Gaussian coordinate parametrization.
The polynomial has degree at most \(2mN\) and is nonzero exactly because the dimension is good.
The deterministic repair puts the joint event inside its tube of Euclidean radius \(C\sqrt N\,\sigma^{-1}\varepsilon^{a-2\theta}\), uniformly in the centers.
The [coefficient-uniform Gaussian tube bound without additive error](Yin_C_F0186_v3.md#tube-without-floor) thus bounds its conditional probability by
\[
 C N^{5/2}\varepsilon^{a-2\theta-\lambda}
 \exp\!\big(C N^{5/2}\varepsilon^{a-2\theta-\lambda}\big),
\]
using ambient real dimension \(O(N^2)\) and degree \(O(N)\).
For a constant nonzero determinant the singular set is empty and the tube argument uses its empty case.
Choosing \(b_*\) sufficiently large bounds the exponential uniformly on the range of (1).
Combining the three costs proves (1) with \(c=\min(2\theta,\lambda\alpha,a-2\theta-\lambda)>0\).
In the no-nonempty-type case choose any \(a>2\theta\) and use only the original norm exception.
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) in the same bounds at fixed \(N\in\mathcal G\) gives nonsingularity almost surely there as well.

## Intermediate singular values with \(2+\delta\) moments

For the signed Hermitian dilation of a fixed finite-free-log pencil, let \(\eta\) be its resolvent parameter and \(h\) a complex raw off-diagonal entry treated as a two-dimensional real vector (one dimension in a real color).
After subtracting the order-two Taylor polynomial at a zero replacement coordinate, the [pair derivative bounds](Yin_C_F0195_v1.md#derivative-bound) bound the remainder both by \(C N^{-5/2}|h|^3\eta^{-4}\) and by \(C N^{-2}|h|^2\eta^{-3}\).
The second bound uses the first-order Taylor remainder plus the absolute value of the subtracted Hessian term.
Taking the product of these bounds with exponents \(\delta\) and \(1-\delta\), respectively, gives \(C N^{-2-\delta/2}|h|^{2+\delta}\eta^{-3-\delta}\).
With a Gaussian replacement of the same real covariance matrix, the subtracted Taylor polynomials have equal expectations on both sides.
Summing over \(O(N^2)\) pairs costs at most \(C N^{-\delta/2}\eta^{-3-\delta}\).
Use the [finite-second-moment diagonal replacement](Yin_C_F0195_v1.md#diagonal-removal), the [matched-to-GOE interpolation](Yin_C_F0195_v1.md#covariance-interpolation), and [the fixed GOE comparison](Yin_C_F0195_v1.md#count-proof) unchanged.
The free smoothed mass is at most \(Ct^\beta\) for some \(0<\beta\le1\) by the same fixed-pencil count proof, since finite free logarithm implies no zero atom.
With \(\nu_N\) the singular probability measure of the original pencil, multiplication of the resolvent errors by \(t\) yields
\[
 \mathbb E\nu_N([0,t])\le C\big(t^\beta+
       N^{-\delta/2}t^{-2-\delta}+N^{-1/2}t^{-3}+N^{-1}t^{-6}\big),
 \qquad 0<t\le1 .
 \tag{3}
\]
Take \(\gamma>0\) smaller than \(\delta/(2(2+\delta+\beta))\), \(1/(6+2\beta)\), and \(1/(6+\beta)\).
Then \(\mathbb E\nu_N([0,t])\le C't^\beta\) on \(N^{-\gamma}\le t\le1\).
At \(t=N^{-\gamma}\) the normalized small count exceeds \(C''N^{-\gamma\beta}\) only with summable probability, by the [bounded-rank row concentration](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration) with failure at most \(2e^{-c'N^{1-2\gamma\beta}}\).

<a id="log-assembly"></a>
## Ordinary logarithms along good dimensions

Put \(a_N=N^{-\gamma}\).
For fixed \(d\in(0,1)\) the logarithm clipped below at \(d\) converges almost surely after normalized integration against \(\nu_N\), by the [weak singular law](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#finite-variance-weak) and the [pathwise positive-log control](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics).
The expected negative log excess in replacing \(d\) by \(a_N\) is bounded by \(\int_{a_N}^d C't^{\beta-1}\,dt\le C'd^\beta/\beta\).
The bounded negative part of the log clipped at \(a_N\) concentrates around its expectation with summable deviations by the same bounded-rank row estimate, since its variation is at most \(O(\log N)\).
The fixed-\(d\) negative part has bounded continuous test and its expectation converges to the free one.
Sending \(d\downarrow0\) and using the positive-log law therefore gives almost surely the finite target \(u\) for the normalized \(a_N\)-clipped logarithms.
This argument uses a countable sequence of tolerances and the Borel--Cantelli lemma under the actual coupling.

Choose \(B>\max(b_*,\gamma)\) arbitrarily large.
The excess of the negative logarithms below \(a_N\), after normalization, is at most
\[
 B\log N\,\nu_N([0,a_N])+\big(\log^-s_{\min}(L_N)-B\log N\big)_+ .
\]
Through \(\mathcal G\) the second term vanishes eventually almost surely for \(Bc>7/2\) by (1).
The first tends to zero almost surely by the summable count bound after (3).
This proves the almost-sure limit along the good dimensions.

For each fixed \(p\ge1\), the normalized clipped logarithms are bounded in \(L^{2p}\).
The positive part has the required bound by Jensen's inequality and the [expected positive singular-value tails](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#positive-tail).
For the negative part apply Jensen and integrate \(2p|\log t|^{2p-1}\mathbb E\nu_N([0,t])/t\) from \(a_N\) to \(1\), bounded uniformly by (3) on that range.
The first excess term above tends to zero in \(L^p\) because \(\nu_N([0,a_N])^p\le\nu_N([0,a_N])\) and its expectation is \(O(N^{-\gamma\beta})\).
By (1), the probability that the second term is above \(s\ge0\) is \(O(N^{5/2-Bc}e^{-cs})\).
Layer integration shows its \(p\)-th moment tends to zero along \(\mathcal G\).
The clipped convergence and uniform integrability, followed by these two excess estimates, give the asserted \(L^p\) convergence.

At an infinite negative free target, fixed-\(d\) clipping bounds the original logarithm above in every dimension.
The fixed-\(d\) limits decrease to \(-\infty\) by definition, so the full sequence converges to \(-\infty\) almost surely.
For finite target the [generic-rank and all-even witness theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v9.md#all-even-witness) gives real symmetric witnesses in every sufficiently large even size and Hermitian witnesses in all sufficiently large sizes.
The real witnesses are legal for every mixed evaluation space.
Direct sums of witnesses preserve the allowed color types and produce a witness in the sum dimension.
One odd witness therefore gives all sufficiently large odd dimensions by adding sufficiently large even sizes.
Conversely without an odd witness each odd logarithm is identically \(-\infty\), as asserted.

## Scalar determinants and Brown measures

For each nonconstant scalar polynomial \(P\) and any fixed \(z\), including \(P(0)\), the [scalar SS15 atomlessness input](../audits/literature/SS15/Yin_U001.md#ss15-u001) applies to the nonconstant selfadjoint \((P-z)^*(P-z)\).
Its top degree term \(P_D^*P_D\) is nonzero.
The [free scalar algebraic-log argument](Yin_C_F0186_v8.md#goe-scalar-brown) gives a finite free logarithm for \(P(s)-z\) from this absence of an atom, with no condition on the constant coefficient for that algebraic-log step.
The [exact word-chain construction](../ledger/Yin_L_F0061_v2.md#linearization-transfer) has unit upper triangular auxiliary pivot.
Thus the ordinary good-dimension sets of the scalar and linearized expressions coincide, and their finite and free normalized logarithms differ by the same factor equal to the auxiliary dimension.
This proves the fixed-parameter assertions, including the parity criterion.
If \(P\) is constant, its eigenvalue law and both determinants are immediate, with the usual \(-\infty\) convention when \(P=z\).

If \(z\ne P(0)\), evaluation at the all-zero tuple witnesses invertibility in every size.
Thus at every fixed such \(z\), the original logarithmic potential converges along the entire sequence almost surely.
Fubini's theorem gives a single event of area-almost-everywhere convergence on the whole plane for each fixed \(P\) and coupling.
On this event the [pathwise local planar square bounds and tightness](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) imply convergence in local planar \(L^1\).
Taking distributional Laplacians yields vague convergence of the empirical eigenvalue probabilities to the Brown measure, and tightness gives the stated weak convergence.

The transfer (2) controls the entire joint law after adding independent coordinate noise.
A nonzero atom in a raw law prevents (T), even when the rest of that law has a smooth component.
In the quadratic two-color problem the [regular-component theorem](Yin_C_F0351_v2.md#statement) admits such residual atoms under its own hypotheses.
The present argument treats every fixed polynomial for the joint-law class (T) and gives the corresponding terminal summability for \(AB+2BA\) when the [fourth-moment trimmed criterion](Yin_C_F0186_v36.md#statement) also applies.
