# Logarithmic moment bounds and favorable dimensions for Wigner determinants

<a id="statement"></a>
## Determinants under two tail conditions

Use the fixed real or complex Wigner laws of [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#input-model), allowing mixed colors and arbitrary cross-size coupling.
Use the translation modulus (T), the affine pencil \(L=b+\sum A_jx_j\) of size \(m\), its ordinary normalized logarithm \(U_N\), its free target \(u\) and its good dimensions \(\mathcal G\) from the [translation-regular theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#statement).
All diagonal laws need only their stated second moments and translation modulus.
At \(u=-\infty\) the fixed-clip upper bound of that theorem already gives almost-sure convergence to \(-\infty\).
Suppose below that \(u\) is finite.

1. Let \(0<\beta\le1\) be a free smoothed-mass exponent for this pencil, so that \(\int t^2/(s^2+t^2)\,d\nu_{|L(s_1,\ldots,s_g)|}(s)\le Ct^\beta\) for \(0<t\le1\).
   If each raw strict upper entry \(\xi\) satisfies \(\mathbb E|\xi|^2(\log(2+|\xi|))^{1+2/\beta}<\infty\), then \(U_N\to u\) almost surely and in every finite \(L^p\), \(p\ge1\), through \(\mathcal G\).
2. Suppose instead that a deterministic sequence of even dimensions tending to infinity satisfies
   \[
    N\log N\,p_N\longrightarrow0,\qquad
    p_N=\sum_j\Pr\{|\xi_j|>N^{1/4}\}.
    \tag{1}
   \]
   With only finite second moments in addition to (T) and (1), \(U_N\to u\) almost surely and in every finite \(L^p\) on this sequence.
   If (1) holds through all dimensions the conclusion applies through all good dimensions.
   The sequence in (1) is common to all fixed coefficients and auxiliary sizes.
   The event in a convergence assertion may depend on the pencil.

Each alternative gives the dimension-limsup equality of F0013 for its stated pencils and the equality of [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) for scalar polynomials whose exact linearizations satisfy the same condition.
In particular (1) gives both equalities for every fixed pencil and scalar polynomial, including the fixed shift \(P(0)\).
Condition (1) is an additional tail hypothesis.
It admits laws with no finite positive logarithmic improvement of their second moment.

## Comparison with the second-moment remainder retained

Write \(\nu_N\) for the normalized singular measure and \(\ell=\log(N+2)\).
For all strict upper laws and their centered real-covariance-matched Gaussian replacements, sum
\(H(R)=\sum_\xi\mathbb E[|\xi|^2\min(1,|\xi|/R)]\).
The two [Taylor remainders](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#fractional-count) for a raw vector \(h\) are bounded by \(CN^{-2}|h|^2t^{-3}\min(1,|h|/(\sqrt N t))\).
The terms through degree two cancel in expectation.
Summing over pairs and multiplying by \(t\), using the same diagonal removal, pseudocovariance interpolation and GOE comparison as in that proof, gives
\[
 \mathbb E\nu_N([0,t])\le C\{t^\beta+t^{-2}H(\sqrt N t)
                         +N^{-1/2}t^{-3}+N^{-1}t^{-6}\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The bound uses a smoothed free mass, which exists with some positive exponent by the [fixed-pencil count argument](Yin_C_F0195_v1.md#count-proof).

Let \(D=1+2/\beta\) and \(e_N=\sum_\xi\mathbb E[|\xi|^2{\bf1}_{|\xi|>N^{1/4}}]\), again including the Gaussians.
The moment hypothesis gives \(d_N=\ell^D e_N\to0\) by dominated convergence on these tails.
Splitting at \(N^{1/4}\) gives \(H(\sqrt N t)\le e_N+C N^{-1/4}/t\).
Take \(w_N=\min(\log(\ell+2),(d_N+N^{-1})^{-1/4})\) and \(a_N=\ell^{-1/\beta}/w_N\).
Then \(w_N\to\infty\) and (2) gives \(\mathbb E\nu_N([0,t])\le Ct^\beta+\delta_N\) on \(a_N\le t\le1\), with \(\ell\delta_N\to0\).
Indeed \(\ell e_Na_N^{-2}=d_Nw_N^2\to0\), and the other errors have a negative power of \(N\) times powers of logarithms.
Also \(\ell a_N^\beta=w_N^{-\beta}\to0\).

## Moving truncations with their actual variances

For (1), truncate each strict upper coordinate to \(\xi{\bf1}_{|\xi|\le N^{1/4}}\).
The expected normalized rank of the resulting change of the pencil is at most \(CNp_N\), because each changed physical pair has uniformly bounded rank.
Write \(\mu_{j,N}\) and \(v_{j,N}^2\) for the complex mean and the variance about that mean in each truncated law.
Then \(v_{j,N}\to1\) and \(|\mu_{j,N}|\le N^{-1/4}\mathbb E|\xi_j|^2\).
After removing these means above and below the diagonal, the deterministic difference \(D_N\) of the pencils satisfies \(\|D_N\|_{\rm HS}^2/(mN)\le C N^{-1/2}\), by summing squared entries.
This includes complex means with conjugate entries below the diagonal.
Discarding the singular directions of \(D_N\) above \(t\) costs rank at most \(\|D_N\|_{\rm HS}^2/t^2\) and leaves operator norm at most \(t\).
Consequently the original expected count at \(t\) is bounded by the centered core count at \(2t\) plus \(C(Np_N+N^{-1/2}t^{-2})\).
Here and below counts are normalized by the respective dimension.

Divide each centered core color by \(v_{j,N}\), including its original diagonal in this division.
For all large \(N\) the resulting independent-coordinate triangular model \(X_{j,N}\) has variance-one strict upper entries, uniformly bounded diagonal second moments, and raw third absolute upper moments at most \(CN^{1/4}\).
Only the centered core uses this normalization.
Its pencil is \(L_v(X)=b+\sum v_j A_jX_j\), with \(v=v_N\) eventually in the compact box \(V=[1/2,2]^g\).

Use the auxiliary affine pencil
\[
 K_v(x)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 b&A_1x_1&\cdots&A_gx_g\\
 -v_1I&I& &0\\
 \vdots&&\ddots&\\
 -v_gI&0&&I
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its random coefficients are fixed and its constant coefficients vary in a compact semialgebraic set.
For any subspace on which \(L_v(X)\) has norm at most \(t\) on unit vectors, the graph \(h\mapsto(h,v_1h,\ldots,v_gh)\) has the same dimension, has norm at least \(\|h\|\), and is mapped by \(K_v(X)\) to \((L_v(X)h,0,\ldots,0)\).
Thus the normalized count for \(L_v\) is bounded by \(g+1\) times that of \(K_v\) at the same threshold.

The free graph kernel of \(K_v\) is zero for every \(v\in V\).
Indeed \(u>-\infty\) implies that \(L(s)\) has zero kernel.
By [the semicircular analytic-rank and free-field theorem](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U001.md#exact-introductory-interfaces), applied to a semicircular system of maximal \(\Delta\) as in [the source audit](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U006.md), \(L\) has full polynomial inner rank.
Each substitution \(x_j\mapsto v_jx_j\) is an automorphism of the free algebra, preserving this rank.
The same theorem gives zero free kernel for \(L_v\), and the graph identity identifies the kernel of \(K_v\).
The [uniform free count on compact semialgebraic drifts](Yin_C_F0195_v2.md#argument) therefore gives a common \(Ct^\theta\) for some \(0<\theta\le1\).

In the [finite-size comparison](Yin_C_F0195_v1.md#derivative-bound) the third-order replacement error is linear in the sum of the absolute third moments of a raw pair and its matched Gaussian, with constants depending on the fixed random coefficients.
The diagonal cost uses only a uniform second moment.
The [covariance interpolation](Yin_C_F0195_v1.md#covariance-interpolation) is uniform for variance-one raw pairs and the [fixed-coefficient GOE bound](Yin_C_F0195_v2.md#finite-count) is uniform over these drifts.
The proof is within each \(N\) and applies to the above triangular laws.
The third moment bound \(CN^{1/4}\) changes the count error \(CN^{-1/2}t^{-3}\) to \(CN^{-1/4}t^{-3}\).
Combining these bounds, enlarging constants for \(2t>1\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E\nu_N([0,t])\le C(t^\theta+N^{-1/4}t^{-3}
              +N^{-1}t^{-6}+N^{-1/2}t^{-2}+Np_N).
 \tag{3}
\]
Taking \(a_N=N^{-1/32}\) leaves an error \(\delta_N=o(1/\ell)\) in (3) uniformly on \([a_N,1]\) along (1), and \(\ell a_N^\theta\to0\).
No rate for \(v_{j,N}\to1\) enters this estimate.

## Removing the cutoff and transferring the limit

In either argument write \(\theta\) for the applicable exponent.
For fixed \(0<d<1\), the expected negative-log excess between \(d\) and \(a_N\) is at most \(C d^\theta/\theta+\delta_N\log(1/a_N)\).
The second term tends to zero, and \(\ell\,\mathbb E\nu_N([0,a_N])\to0\).
The [bounded-rank row estimate and fixed-clip limits](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration) apply to the original independent row groups.
The negative log clipped at \(a_N\) has variation \(O(\ell)\), and the count at \(a_N\) has bounded variation.
Thus the centered clipped negative log and \(\ell\) times the centered count have tails bounded by \(2\exp(-cNx^2/\ell^2)\) at deviation \(x>0\), with a change of constants for signed dilation.
These differences tend to zero almost surely under every coupling and in all finite \(L^p\) by tail integration.
The bounded negative-log test clipped at \(d\) converges in expectation.
Sending \(d\downarrow0\) proves convergence of the \(a_N\)-clipped negative logarithm to its free value.
For the excess below \(a_N\) use \(B\ell\,\nu_N([0,a_N])+(\log^-s_{\min}-B\ell)_+\) with a fixed sufficiently large \(B\).
On good dimensions the [finite-variance floor under (T)](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#floor-statement) gives a summable bound \(CN^{5/2-Bc}\) for positive excess of the second term and the same bound times \(e^{-cx}\) for an extra depth \(x\).
Both terms tend to zero almost surely and in every finite \(L^p\).
The positive log has almost-sure convergence and uniform bounds in higher log moments by [the pathwise and expected tail estimates](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics), yielding the asserted limits.

All sufficiently large even dimensions are good at a finite target by [the all-even witness theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v9.md#all-even-witness).
The [fixed-clip upper bound](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics) holds in every dimension.
The subsequence limit thus proves the ordinary dimension-limsup equality.
For a scalar polynomial use [the unit-triangular Schur linearization](../ledger/Yin_L_F0061_v2.md#linearization-transfer).
The normalized finite and free logarithms of its pencil must both be multiplied by the same auxiliary dimension.
The witness argument and the identities also apply at \(z=P(0)\); constants follow directly.
If the endpoint condition holds for every positive logarithmic power, the first conclusion applies to every fixed linearization.
For \(z\ne P(0)\) zero inputs witness every dimension, so the pointwise full limit in that case gives almost-sure weak Brown convergence for each fixed polynomial by [Fubini and local uniform integrability](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#polynomial-consequences).

## How much (1) requires of a finite-variance law

In the product of the centered variance-one strict upper law spaces with the quadratic Wasserstein metric, the tuples satisfying (1) form a dense \(G_\delta\) set if the tail in (1) is taken with \(\ge\).
More precisely this description concerns existence of an even subsequence for the closed-tail condition.
For each integer \(k\), take the union over even \(N\ge k+2\) of the sets where \(N\log N\sum_j\Pr(|\xi_j|\ge N^{1/4})<1/k\), then intersect over \(k\).
Each union is open by upper semicontinuity of closed-set probabilities and contains every tuple of bounded laws.
Such tuples are dense by truncation, centering and rescaling in quadratic mean, and they themselves belong to the intersection.
These assertions concern the tail condition in the finite-variance law space; the determinant application separately requires (T).

For a concrete real example let \(b_1\ge 10,\ b_{j+1}=\exp(b_j^2)\), and assign symmetric mass of total size \(1/(j^2 b_j^2)\) at \(\pm b_j\) for each \(j\), putting the unused mass at zero.
Add an independent centered uniform variable on \([-1,1]\) and divide by the resulting standard deviation.
Translation of the uniform component gives (T) with exponent one.
The law has finite second moment, while disjoint levels have contributions bounded below by \(c j^{-2}(\log b_j)^s\) to the second moment weighted by the \(s\)-th log power for every \(s>0\) and all large \(j\).
For even \(N_j=2\lceil b_j^8/2\rceil\) its threshold \(N_j^{1/4}\) exceeds every normalized level through \(j\) including the added noise.
The remaining probability is \(O(b_{j+1}^{-2})\), so (1) holds.
Finitely many colors with this law have the same favorable dimensions.

For comparison a real symmetric smooth density equal to a positive constant times \(x^{-3}/(\log x\,(\log\log x)^2)\) for large \(|x|\), with \(x\) replaced by \(|x|\) there, can be normalized to variance one.
It has integrable derivative, finite second moment, and (T).
Integrating on \([r,2r]\) gives \(\Pr(|\xi|>r)\ge c r^{-2}/(\log r\,(\log\log r)^2)\) for large \(r\).
Consequently it has no subsequence satisfying (1), and its second moment with any positive log power is infinite.
The determinant limits under arbitrary second-moment tails and (T) remain unresolved by these two arguments.
