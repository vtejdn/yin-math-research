# Ordinary determinants of affine Wigner pencils with a translation modulus and finite second moments

<a id="statement"></a>
## Matrix and scalar determinants

Use the fixed real or complex Hermitian Wigner model of [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#input-model), with centered variance-one strict upper entries, centered finite-second-moment diagonal entries, and the stated independence within each dimension.
The laws and the coefficient matrices are fixed; the colors may mix real symmetric and complex Hermitian inputs.
Let \(L=b+\sum_{j=1}^g A_j x_j\) be a square affine pencil of size \(m\), and put
\[
 \nu_N=\nu_{|L(W^{(N)})|},\qquad U_N=(mN)^{-1}\log|\det L(W^{(N)})|,\qquad
 u=\log\Delta_{\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau}(L(s)).
\]
Here \(\nu_N\) is the probability measure counting all singular values, including zeros.
Fix a pencil with \(u>-\infty\).
There are \(C,\beta,\gamma>0\) such that throughout all sufficiently large dimensions
\[
 \mathbb E\nu_N([0,t])\le Ct^\beta,\qquad N^{-\gamma}\le t\le1. \tag{1}
\]
For every fixed \(B>0\) and deterministic \(a_N\downarrow0\) with \(N^{-B}\le a_N\le1\) eventually, the negative-log contribution between \(N^{-B}\) and \(a_N\) tends to zero in every finite \(L^r,\ r\ge1\), and its positive deviation probabilities at each fixed tolerance are summable.
These two conclusions use only the stated second moments.

Suppose in addition that every raw law has the [total-variation translation modulus (T)](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#statement) in its declared coordinate space.
Let \(\mathcal G\) be the dimensions admitting an invertible deterministic evaluation in those mixed spaces.
On \(\mathcal G\), \(U_N\to u\) almost surely under every cross-size coupling and in every finite \(L^r,\ r\ge1\).
At a finite target every sufficiently large even dimension is good, and a full-sequence limit holds if and only if an odd dimension has a witness.
Throughout the all-complex model every sufficiently large dimension is good.
If \(u=-\infty\), then \(U_N\to-\infty\) almost surely through all dimensions by the fixed-clip upper bound.
Consequently for every fixed affine pencil in this translation-regular finite-variance model,
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}U_N=u\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Applying exact linearization gives the ordinary dimension-limsup equality of [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) at every prescribed \(z\), including \(P(0)\), and the good-dimension convergence at every finite scalar target.
For each fixed scalar polynomial under these hypotheses the empirical eigenvalue measures converge weakly almost surely to the free Brown measure under every coupling.
The raw-law translation assumption enters when removing the polynomial cutoff on good dimensions.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Uniform power bound at polynomial scales

The finite free logarithm implies that \(L(s)\) has zero kernel, hence \(L\) is full over the free polynomial algebra by the [analytic-rank theorem for a semicircular system](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U001.md#exact-introductory-interfaces).
Equivalently, any polynomial factorization through fewer than \(m\) columns would already force a positive free kernel by the trace rank bound after evaluation.
Apply [the conditional bounded-entry comparison](Yin_C_F0195_v4.md#bounded-core) to the signed Hermitian dilation of this pencil.
For clarity, an upper entry is active when it lies in a fixed bounded set of positive mass with nonzero conditional variance.
Expose the active sets, their complementary entries and the diagonals, placing the active conditional means in the background.
The background has uniformly bounded expected squared normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
Clipping each of its matrices at operator norm \(M\ge1\) thus costs expected normalized rank \(O(M^{-2})\).
Replacement of the remaining independent bounded variables, [covariance interpolation for the actual masks](Yin_C_F0195_v4.md#covariance-mask), and [Gaussian resolvent comparison with arbitrary drift](Yin_C_F0195_v4.md#free-background) give
\[
 \mathbb E\nu_N([0,t])\le C\left(M^{-2}+N^{-1/2}t^{-3}+N^{-1}t^{-4}+
        \mathbb E f_{D,M}(t)\right). \tag{2}
\]
Conditionally on the frozen matrices, \(f_{D,M}(t)\) is the normalized free smoothed singular mass of \(L(D_1^{(M)}+c_1s_1,\ldots,D_g^{(M)}+c_gs_g)\), for a free standard semicircular family free from the deterministic matrix algebra with its normalized trace.
The constants \(c_j>0\) are fixed by the original raw laws.
The arguments to (2) use arbitrary affine coefficients, before the scalar application in its source.

Since \(\|D_j^{(M)}\|_2\le M\), the [free power estimate for full affine pencils](Yin_C_F0352_v1.md#statement) gives \(f_{D,M}(t)\le C(1+M)^h t^{b_m}\) with \(b_m>0\).
This is uniform in the realization and physical dimension.
Take \(M=t^{-\delta}\) with \(\delta=b_m/(2(h+1))\), and \(\beta=\min(2\delta,b_m/2)\).
Choose \(\gamma>0\) so that \(\gamma(3+\beta)<1/2\) and \(\gamma(4+\beta)<1\).
Then each term in (2) is \(O(t^\beta)\) on the window in (1).

<a id="band"></a>
## The logarithmic integral between the two cutoffs

Write \(F_N(t)=\nu_N([0,t])\), \(c_N=N^{-B}\), and define the normalized loss
\[
 Y_N=\int\log\frac{\max(s,a_N)}{\max(s,c_N)}\,d\nu_N(s)
     =\int_{c_N}^{a_N} F_N(t)\frac{dt}{t}.
\]
Split the interval at \(N^{-\gamma}\), omitting empty pieces.
By monotonicity and (1),
\[
 0\le \mathbb E Y_N
 \le C a_N^\beta/\beta+C B\log N\,N^{-\gamma\beta}. \tag{3}
\]
The original row groups are independent in each dimension.
The test defining \(Y_N\) has total variation at most \(B\log N\) on the positive half-line, so the [bounded-rank row concentration inequality](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration) gives
\[
 \Pr\{|Y_N-\mathbb EY_N|>x\}\le 2\exp[-cNx^2/(B\log N)^2]\quad(x>0).
\]
This proves the summable deviations and \(L^r\) convergence by integration.
The same conclusions hold for the literal unshifted closed-band integral \(J_N=\int_{[c_N,a_N]}\log^-s\,d\nu_N(s)\).
Indeed layer integration with both endpoint atoms included gives \(J_N=Y_N+|\log a_N|F_N(a_N)-|\log c_N|F_N(c_N-)\).
For \(a_N\ge N^{-\gamma}\) the additional positive term in expectation is at most \(C|\log a_N|a_N^\beta\).
For smaller \(a_N\) it is at most \(CB\log N\,N^{-\gamma\beta}\).
Both tend to zero, and the test for \(J_N\) has variation at most \(2B\log N\), giving the same concentration and moment argument.
In particular it applies on the entire sequence to the old [finite-variance clipping depth](Yin_C_F0195_v3.md#statement), \(a_N=\exp[-b(\log(N+2))^{\theta/(\theta+2)}]\) for every fixed \(b>0\).
The same layer calculation with a fixed upper clip \(d\in(0,1)\) bounds the expected error down to \(c_N\) by \(C d^\beta/\beta+o(1)\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Ordinary logarithms and good dimensions

At fixed \(d\) the bounded negative log clipped at \(d\) converges in expectation by the [weak singular law](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#finite-variance-weak).
Let \(d\downarrow0\) in the last bound and use \(u>-\infty\).
The expected \(c_N\)-clipped negative log converges to its free counterpart.
Its centered fluctuations vanish almost surely and in every finite \(L^r\) by the same row concentration.
The positive log converges almost surely and is uniformly integrable to every fixed log power by the [pathwise and expected positive-log estimates](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics).

On good dimensions the excess below \(c_N\) is bounded by \((\log c_N-\log s_{\min})_+\).
For \(B\) sufficiently large, the [original-law floor under (T)](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#floor-statement) bounds its tail at extra depth \(x\ge0\) by \(CN^{5/2-B\kappa}e^{-\kappa x}\), where \(\kappa>0\) depends on the pencil and laws.
Choose \(B\) above the floor threshold and \(B\kappa>7/2\).
This error vanishes eventually almost surely and tends to zero in every finite \(L^r\).
Consequently \(U_N\) has the asserted good-dimension limits.
On each other dimension its value is identically \(-\infty\).
The [witness and parity argument](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#log-assembly) gives all sufficiently large even sizes, all sufficiently large sizes for all-complex inputs, and eventual full coverage in the mixed model whenever there is a single odd witness.
If \(u=-\infty\), fixed clipping from below bounds the limsup above by free clipped values decreasing to \(-\infty\) almost surely.
This proves the claimed dimension-limsup equality with the ordinary determinant convention.

<a id="scalar"></a>
## Scalar linearization

For a fixed nonconstant \(P\) and any fixed \(z\), the [unit-triangular auxiliary pivot](../ledger/Yin_L_F0061_v2.md#linearization-transfer) identifies good dimensions before and after word-chain linearization.
At finite \(N\) the normalized scalar log is exactly \(m U_N\) for the corresponding auxiliary pencil of size \(m\), and the free normalized logarithms have the same factor by the Schur identity and Fuglede--Kadison multiplicativity.
The free scalar target is finite at every \(z\) by the [scalar atomlessness and logarithmic integrability argument](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#polynomial-consequences).
For \(z\ne P(0)\) the zero tuple is a witness in every size.
The resulting full-sequence fixed-shift limits give an event of planar almost-everywhere convergence by Fubini for each fixed polynomial and coupling.
The [local planar square bounds and tightness](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) then give local \(L^1\) convergence and the weak Brown limit by the distributional Laplacian.
Constant polynomials are deterministic.
The [scalar conditional-comparison theorem](Yin_C_F0195_v4.md#scalar-fisher) also proves the word-chain case using scalar free Fisher information.
