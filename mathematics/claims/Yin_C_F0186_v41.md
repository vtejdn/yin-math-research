# Finite-variance trimmed potentials for the real two-sided Wigner product

<a id="statement"></a>
## Trims at fixed, moving and planar parameters

Let \(A_N,B_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices of [F0012](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract), with fixed centered variance-one strict upper laws and centered finite-second-moment diagonal laws, all in the \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Write \(P_N=A_NB_N+2B_NA_N,\ p=ab+2ba,\ u(z)=\tau\log|p-z|\) for free standard semicircular \(a,b\).
Let \(p_j(T)\) be the product of the largest \(j\) singular values of \(T\), \(p_0=1\), and set \(q_N=\lceil\log(N+3)\log\log(N+3)\rceil\).
Put
\[
 L_N(z)=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A_N&2B_N\\B_N&-I&0\\A_N&0&-I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Under every allowed cross-size coupling,
\[
 N^{-1}\log p_{3N-q_N}(L_N(\cdot)),\qquad
 N^{-1}\log p_{N-q_N}(P_N-\cdot I)\ \longrightarrow\ u
 \quad\hbox{in local planar }L^1\text{ almost surely}.
\]
For each deterministic \(z_N\to z\notin\mathbb R\), both quantities evaluated at \(z_N\) converge to \(u(z)\) almost surely.
The event may depend on the sequence.
The moving-shift assertion also holds with \(q_N=\lceil C_K\log(N+3)\rceil\) when the parameters are eventually in a fixed compact set \(K\) off the real axis and \(C_K\) is sufficiently large.
The rescaled principal-minor two-trim comparison and the equivalence of the terminal deviation series with every-coupling weak Brown convergence in [the conclusion below](#terminal) hold with these finite second moments.
The theorem gives trimmed potentials; obtaining an untrimmed limit requires control of the indicated bottom terms.

## The input projection of a fresh border

Use the physical border of the [three-channel determinant comparison](Yin_C_F0186_v36.md#one-step) on a prescribed nested sequence of sites, keeping the \(N^{-1/2}\) scaling.
Denote the old companion on \(l\) sites by \(L\), its extension by \(L'\), their upper and lower fresh blocks by \(U,V^*\), and the deterministic deficit by \(q=3l-r\).
Assume \(24\le q\le3l\), and \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K_N=N^2\) for the old inputs.
This guard holds simultaneously on the nested minors except on an event of probability \(O(N^{-3})\), by Frobenius Markov on the full inputs and norm contraction under principal compression.
Fix a compact parameter region with \(|\Im z|\ge\eta>0\).
We prove that
\[
 p_{r+6}(L')\ge N^{-55}p_r(L)
 \tag{1}
\]
except for conditional probability \(CN^{66}e^{-cq}+CN^{-3}\) on the old guard, for all sufficiently large \(N\).
The constants may depend on the laws and the region, and the corner diagonals do not enter the proof.

Take \(\tau_N=N^{-9}\).
For each left unit vector \(\ell=(h,d,e)\) with \(\|L^*\ell\|\le\tau_N\), the auxiliary rows give \(d=Ah+d',\ e=2Bh+e'\), where \(\|d'\|+\|e'\|\le2\tau_N\).
Thus \(\|h\|\ge c/(1+K_N)\) and \(\|(BA+2AB-\bar z)h\|\le(1+2K_N)\tau_N\).
The right unit vectors with \(\|L\ell\|\le\tau_N\) satisfy \(d=Bh+d',\ e=Ah+e'\) with the same error bound and \(\|(AB+2BA-z)h\|\le(1+3K_N)\tau_N\).
Both scalar residuals are at most \(\eta\|h\|/2\) for large \(N\).
For \(C=A,B\) and either residual, the [imaginary-part estimate](Yin_C_F0187_v30.md#individual-input-gap) gives
\(\|(C-vI)h\|\ge\eta\|h\|/(2K_N)\) for every complex \(v\).
If \(|\alpha|^2+|\delta|^2=1\) and \(|\alpha|\ge1/(4(1+K_N))\), multiply this gap at \(v=-\delta/\alpha\) by \(|\alpha|\).
For smaller \(|\alpha|\) use \(\|(\alpha C+\delta I)h\|\ge(|\delta|-K_N|\alpha|)\|h\|\).
Hence \(\|(\alpha C+\delta I)h\|\ge c_\eta(1+K_N)^{-2}\|h\|\).

Let \(Q_L,Q_R\) project off respectively the first \(r+3\) left and \(r\) right old singular vectors in paired bases.
For a unit \(w\in\mathbb C^3\) put \(Z=\sqrt N Q_LUw=T_w g\), where \(g\in\mathbb R^{2l}\) stacks the two independent fresh raw columns and \(T_w\) is the complex-linear extension of its coefficient matrix conditional on the minor.
Before projection the squared norm of the adjoint acting on a left \(\ell\) is
\[
 \|\bar w_2h+\bar w_1e\|^2+\|2\bar w_3h+\bar w_1d\|^2 .
\]
For \(Z=\sqrt N Q_RVw\) the two right-vector terms are \(\|\bar w_3h+\bar w_1d\|^2+\|\bar w_2h+2\bar w_1e\|^2\).
In each case the graph substitution yields two affine tests for \(A,B\), with sum of squared coefficient norms at least one.
The combined graph error is \(O(\tau_N)\).
The preceding affine gap gives \(\|T_w^*\ell\|\ge c_\eta(1+K_N)^{-3}-O(\tau_N)\ge N^{-7}\) on every unit vector in the corresponding projected near-null subspace.

If fewer than \(q/2\) old singular values are at most \(\tau_N\), compression monotonicity gives \(p_{r+6}(L')\ge p_r(L)\tau_N^6\).
Otherwise each of the two projections contains at least \(q/2-3\ge q/4\) left or right low singular directions satisfying its graph bound on their whole span.
By min--max, each corresponding \(T_w\) has at least \(\lfloor q/4\rfloor\) singular values at least \(N^{-7}\).
For the projection \(E_w\) onto their right input singular directions,
\[
  \|T_w g\|\ge N^{-7}\|E_w g\|,\qquad \|E_w\|\le1,\qquad
  \|E_w\|_{\rm HS}^2\ge\lfloor q/4\rfloor .
\]
Here the complex projection acts on a real random vector in the complexified input space.
The independent real entries have unit variance, giving the weight \(\|E_w e_i\|^2\) in the sum of the coordinate variances.

For these projected inputs the [weighted fixed-cutoff estimate](Yin_C_F0187_v10.md#finite-variance-projected-row) yields \(\Pr\{\|E_wg\|<c\sqrt q\mid L,A,B\}\le C e^{-c'q}\).
In detail, condition on which independent raw coordinates lie in fixed bounded sets of positive probability and positive conditional variance, and expose rejected values.
Each weight is at most one; the independent indicators retain at least a positive multiple of \(q\) weight outside probability \(Ce^{-c'q}\) by exponential Markov.
The resulting norm is a convex \(1\)-Lipschitz function of independent bounded coordinates with fixed shift and conditional second moment at least \(cq\), regardless of conditional means.
The two-sided bounded-product concentration about its median yields a median at least \(c'\sqrt q\) and exponential lower-tail control at half that value, adjusting constants for small \(q\).
The factor \(N^{-7}\) outside the input projection affects only the logarithm incurred at a successful step.

The raw pair of columns has norm at most \(N^2\) except with conditional probability \(O(N^{-3})\) by its second moment.
On that guard \(w\mapsto T_w g\) is \(CN^2\)-Lipschitz for each border.
A mesh \(N^{-11}\) of the complex unit sphere of dimension three has at most \(CN^{66}\) elements.
Consequently both normalized projected blocks have least singular value at least \(N^{-8}\) with the stated conditional success probability.
The [paired-block inequality](Yin_C_F0347_v1.md#statement) gives (1) by multiplying both three-column volumes, including their fixed geometric constant.
The few-small-values alternative gives (1) since \(\tau_N^6=N^{-54}\).
Only old-measurable guards are used when applying the conditional bounds.

## Initialization on the original finite-variance minor

For \(m=\lfloor N/\log^3(N+3)\rfloor,\ n=N-m,\ a_N=n/N\), start with \(q_0=3m+q_N,\ r_0=3n-q_0\) and end with deficit \(q_N\) after \(m\) steps.
For any deterministic \(z_N\to z\notin\mathbb R\) the initial companion is \(S L_n^{\rm can}(w_N) S\), with \(S=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{a_N}I,I,I)\) and \(w_N=z_N/a_N\to z\).
The canonical minor uses normalization \(n^{-1/2}\).
By this [scaling identity](Yin_C_F0186_v36.md#initial-product), the cost in its normalized retained product is \(O(|\log a_N|)\).

The finite-variance [affine count down to polynomial scales](Yin_C_F0186_v40.md#argument) applies with common positive \(C,\beta,\gamma\) to these canonical companions at \(w_N\).
Indeed their coefficient tuples for \(w\) in a compact neighborhood of \(z\) form a compact family of full pencils.
The free companion has a unit triangular Schur elimination with remaining scalar \(ab+2ba-w\), a nonzero polynomial over the free field at every \(w\), so it is full.
The [free shifted estimate](Yin_C_F0352_v1.md#statement) is uniform on this compact family.
The [bounded-entry comparison](Yin_C_F0195_v4.md#free-background) in the proof of the affine theorem has fixed variable coefficients and allows arbitrary deterministic drift, leaving its constants unchanged under this spectral parameter.
At \(n^{-\gamma}\) the expected number of smaller values is \(O(n^{1-\beta\gamma})=o(q_0)\).
Original-row bounded differences give exception \(2\exp(-c q_0^2/n)\) for that count to exceed \(q_0\).

The canonical log clipped below at \(n^{-\gamma}\), normalized by \(3n\), converges almost surely to \(u(z)/3\).
For its bounded negative part use the same count at every \(s\in[n^{-\gamma},1]\): replacing a fixed clip \(d<1\) by the moving clip costs expected negative-log excess at most \(Cd^\beta/\beta\).
Its centered deviation at fixed tolerance is summable by [original-row concentration](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration), since the test has variation \(O(\log n)\).
At fixed \(d\) weak convergence and the singular-value perturbation bound for \(w_N-z\) give the clipped negative expectation.
For the positive part use the [pathwise positive-log control](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#log-statistics) at \(z\), and the fact that \(\log^+s\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz on \([0,\infty)\).
This also yields uniform integrability of the positive logs on the path, including the shifted sequence.
Each canonical \(n=N-m\) occurs at most twice for large \(N\), so the every-coupling input applies on two injective subsequences as in the [original trim proof](Yin_C_F0186_v36.md#initial-product).
The free normalized log of the companion equals \(u(z)/3\) by the exact Schur determinant, and is finite.
Except on summable events all retained terms lie above the clip.
The omitted positive terms contribute \(o(n)\) by uniform integrability and \(q_0=o(n)\); removal of negative terms can only increase the retained logarithm.
Thus \(\liminf N^{-1}\log p_{r_0}(L_n(z_N))\ge u(z)\).

## Growth to a logarithmic deficit

At step \(j\) the deficit is \(q_0-3j\) and the retained index grows by six per step.
Equation (1) fails on some guarded step with probability at most \(CN^{67}\exp(-c q_N)+CN^{-2}\).
This is summable for the stated \(q_N\), or with a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(\log(N+3)\) on the prescribed compact region.
The full norm guard fails with summable probability.
The cost \(55m\log N/N\to0\) gives the lower limit at \(z_N\).
For the upper limit replace all logarithms below fixed \(d\) by \(\log d\); omitting \(q_N\) terms raises the normalized upper bound by at most \(q_N|\log d|/N\).
The full fixed clip at \(z_N\) differs from the one at \(z\) by \(O(|z_N-z|/d)\) after normalization and converges almost surely to its free counterpart.
Let \(d\downarrow0\) to obtain the moving-shift limit.

For planar convergence take constant nonreal shifts and use Fubini.
The positive-log control gives an eventual pathwise bound \(C_{D,\omega}\) on every disk \(D\) for the companion's sum of positive logs divided by \(N\), by shifting from zero.
Consequently the normalized trimmed log is bounded above by \(C_{D,\omega}\) and below by the original scalar characteristic log divided by \(N\) minus \(C_{D,\omega}\), away from the finite spectrum.
The [pathwise local planar square bound](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) therefore gives uniform integrability and local \(L^1\) convergence.
For both pointwise and planar transfer to the scalar product, the [triangular Schur factors](Yin_C_F0186_v36.md#final-product) have polynomial norms and inverse norms under the above Frobenius guard.
Comparing omitted singular values and removing inserted unit singular values costs \(O_D(q_N\log N/N)=o(1)\) almost surely; the full absolute determinants agree.
At a prescribed root one can compare retained products directly: the ratios of the two triangularly equivalent matrices' retained products are bounded by the condition bounds to the power \(q_N\) even in the singular case, by continuity from regular shifts.
Indeed the full determinant ratio is exactly one off the spectra, the omitted terms compare there, and the inequalities between the resulting products extend continuously.
These bounds use polynomial positive singular-value norms uniformly on \(D\).

## Two old-minor trims and the terminal equivalence

In the [old-minor comparison](Yin_C_F0351_v1.md#minor-trims) let \(k=2\lfloor\log^3(N+3)/2\rfloor,\ n=N-k,\ h=q_n\), and let \(t_i(z)\) be the increasing singular values of \(A_{JJ}B_{JJ}+2B_{JJ}A_{JJ}-zI\) with the size-\(N\) scaling and \(|J|=n\).
Writing \(b_j=N^{-1}\sum_{i>j}\log t_i\), we have \(b_h,b_{2h}\to u\) in local planar \(L^1\) almost surely, and \(\int_D(h/N)\log^-t_{2h+1}\to0\) on every fixed disk.
Indeed \(b_h\) is the rescaled canonical trimmed potential at \(z/(n/N)\), plus the vanishing scale logarithm.
Each \(n\) occurs at most three times and dilation of a fixed locally integrable function is continuous in local \(L^1\).
The same global logarithmic norm guard bounds the positive parts of the omitted \(2h\) terms by \(O_D(h\log N/N)\) after normalization.
The fixed regularized upper bound for \(b_{2h}\) in the cited comparison uses only the [finite-variance regularized potentials](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#regularized-potentials).
Sending the regularizer to zero proves the second trimmed limit, since \(b_{2h}\) is at least \(b_h-o(1)\).
Finally \(h\log^-t_{2h+1}\le N(b_{2h}-b_h)+\sum_{i=h+1}^{2h}\log^+t_i\), proving the integral estimate.

Subtracting the full scalar potential from its \(q_N\)-trimmed version gives the sum of the bottom \(q_N\) negative logarithms divided by \(N\), up to \(o(1)\) uniformly on each disk from their positive parts.
The [finite-variance planar-potential comparison](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#remaining-defect) thus equates almost-sure weak Brown convergence under every coupling with summability at each positive tolerance of the disk-integrated normalized terminal negative logarithms.
For necessity apply the second Borel--Cantelli lemma under independent dimensions; the first Borel--Cantelli lemma proves sufficiency for every coupling.
The conditional border estimate in this theorem does not itself bound the final untrimmed loss.
