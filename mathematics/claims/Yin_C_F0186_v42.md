# Finite-variance trims and planar initial overlaps for real quadratic polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## Quadratic polynomials and the initial flag

Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_g\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices with fixed centered strict upper laws of variance one, fixed centered diagonal laws with finite second moments, independent upper triangular coordinates and \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Fix a real polynomial of degree exactly two,
\[
 P(x)=c+\sum_i \ell_i x_i+\sum_{i,j}Q_{ij}x_ix_j,\qquad Q\ne0,
 \qquad u(z)=\tau\log|P(s)-z|,
\]
where \(s\) is a standard free semicircular tuple.
Write \(p_j(T)\) for the product of the \(j\) largest singular values, and \(h_n=\lceil\log(n+3)\log\log(n+3)\rceil\).
Let \(n=N-o(N)\) be deterministic, \(n\le N\).
On a prescribed set of \(n\) sites form the polynomial \(M=P(X_1|_{[n]},\ldots,X_g|_{[n]})\) *after* restricting each input, keeping the \(N^{-1/2}\) scaling.
Almost surely under every cross-size coupling,
\[
 N^{-1}\log p_{n-j}(M-\cdot I)\to u\quad\text{in }L^1_{\rm loc}(\mathbb C),
 \qquad j=h_n,\ 2h_n .
\]
Both limits also hold at every deterministic \(z_N\to z\notin\mathbb R\), on an event for the prescribed sequence.
The same assertions hold for the companion in the proof, with its full size in the retained index and the same normalization \(1/N\).
For the increasing singular values \(t_i(w)\) of \(M-wI\), the normalized \((h_n/N)\log^-t_{2h_n+1}\) tends to zero in disk integral and at those moving nonreal parameters.
Consequently the original [terminal series criterion](Yin_C_F0186_v41.md#terminal) with \(q_N=h_N\) holds for this \(P\): under every coupling almost-sure weak convergence to the free Brown measure is equivalent to summability of the fixed positive deviations of the disk-integrated normalized bottom-\(h_N\) negative logarithms.

There is a planar estimate for the first adaptive flag columns which applies to every such \(Q\).
On each side provide \(2h_n\) fresh real vectors in \(\mathbb R^n\), independent of the old inputs, with independence of successive left-right pairs.
On each side assume the entries of a vector are iid copies of a fixed nonconstant centered finite-variance real law of positive variance.
The laws of the two sides may differ, and their vectors within a pair may be dependent or identical.
In particular they may be fixed nondegenerate linear combinations of the fresh cross colors.
For constants \(A,B>0\) specified below, and a nonreal \(w\), select the largest \(1\le r\le2h_n\) such that \(t_r(w)<N^{-Ah_n}t_{r+1}(w)\), or zero if none exists.
Let \(F_R,F_L\) be orthonormal frames of its bottom right and left singular subspaces.
Let \(V_R,V_L\) span the first \(r\) respective raw columns, in orthonormal coordinates.
Declare a failure when either set of columns is dependent or when either \(|\det(F_\bullet^*V_\bullet)|<N^{-(B+2)r}\); rank zero incurs no failure.
Then for every disk and every positive area tolerance, the probabilities of exceeding the tolerance in failure area are summable over \(N\).
The assertion concerns the area of the bad-parameter set; the two omitted tails and the subsequent full restoration depend on the polynomial.

## A directional estimate for the companion of any real quadratic

Choose a real rank factorization \(Q=UV\), with \(U\) of full column rank \(d\) and \(V\) of full row rank \(d\).
Write \(X\) for the vector of inputs and use the companion
\[
 L(z)=\begin{pmatrix}c+\ell\cdot X-z&X^{\mathsf T}U\\ VX&-I_d\end{pmatrix}
\]
in the tensor convention, with \(p=1+d\) auxiliary channels.
Its scalar Schur complement is \(M-z\); the finite and free absolute determinants agree with the physical normalizations and the two unit triangular factors and their inverses have polynomial norms in the input norms.
Work on the old-measurable guard \(\max_i\|X_i\|\le K=N^2\), simultaneously available on all nested minors outside \(O(N^{-3})\) by Frobenius Markov.
Fix a compact region \(|z|\le R,\ |\Im z|\ge\eta>0\).
For a left unit vector \((v,y)\) of residual \(\|L(z)^*(v,y)\|\le\tau=N^{-10}\), we have \(y=U^{\mathsf T}Xv+O(\tau)\), \(\|v\|\ge c_0/K\), and \(\|(M^{\mathsf T}-\bar z)v\|\le CK\tau\).
All error bounds are Euclidean and constants may depend on the fixed polynomial and compact region.

Let \(a=(a_0,a')\) be a unit direction for the fresh physical border and denote by \(Z_i=\sum_j Q_{ji}X_j\) the selfadjoint column combinations.
Before projection the adjoint of the raw upper border map in direction \(a\) applied to \((v,y)\) has color components
\[
 t_i=\overline{(\ell_i a_0+(Ua')_i)}v+\bar a_0(V^{\mathsf T}y)_i .
\]
These formulas follow by collecting the independent raw columns for the entries of each \(X_i\).
If \(|a_0|\le c_1/K\) for sufficiently small \(c_1\), injectivity of \(U\) and \(\|y\|\le CK\|v\|+O(\tau)\) give \(\|(t_i)\|\ge c_2\|v\|\).
Otherwise project each component off the line of \(v\), using the projection \(E_v\) onto its orthogonal complement.
Since the terms \(c,\ell_i\) are real, the imaginary part of the normalized quadratic expectation is bounded using just the off-line components:
\[
 |\Im\langle v,Mv\rangle|
  \le \Big(\sum_i\|E_v Z_i v\|^2\Big)^{1/2}
       \Big(\sum_i\|X_i v\|^2\Big)^{1/2}.
\]
Indeed each collinear coefficient \(\langle v,Z_iv\rangle/\|v\|^2\) is real, and so is \(\langle v,X_iv\rangle\).
The left residual implies the left side is at least \(\eta\|v\|^2/2\).
As \(V^{\mathsf T}y=(Z_i v)_i+O(\tau)\), the projected color norm is bounded below by \(c_\eta\|v\|/K^2-C\tau\).
Combining the two alternatives gives \(\|(t_i)\|\ge N^{-7}\) for large \(N\).
The right calculation is the identical left calculation for the transposed companion, with the factorization \(V^{\mathsf T}U^{\mathsf T}\) and \(P^{\rm rev}\).
This estimate holds on every unit vector in each corresponding near-null subspace, uniformly in \(a\).

For retained index \(j\) at an old \(l\)-site minor, let \(q=pl-j\ge 8p\).
The [paired volume inequality](Yin_C_F0347_v1.md#statement) uses the two tail projections removing the first \(j+p\) left and first \(j\) right singular vectors.
If fewer than \(q/2\) singular values are at most \(\tau\), then compression monotonicity bounds the new \(p_{j+2p}\) below by the old \(p_j\tau^{2p}\).
Otherwise each projected near-null space has dimension at least \(q/4\), giving at least \(\lfloor q/4\rfloor\) singular values \(\ge N^{-7}\) of the corresponding raw border map by min--max.
The [input projection calculation](Yin_C_F0186_v41.md#flattening) now applies: the orthogonal right singular projection has that rank for iid-across-sites real scalar coordinates after stacking the independent colors, and the weighted fixed-cutoff concentration uses their fixed positive variances.
The number of colors is fixed, so distinct color laws only change the constants.
Both projected border volumes are at least \(N^{-C_p}\) except with conditional probability \(C N^{C_p}e^{-cq}+CN^{-3}\).
Indeed the unscaled fresh vector norm is at most \(N^2\) outside \(O(N^{-3})\) by the second moments, and the resulting Lipschitz bound permits an inverse-polynomial mesh of the fixed-dimensional unit sphere of \(a\).
Both border successes are intersected by a union bound, allowing their actual dependence.
The net, root-\(N\) normalization and finitely many auxiliary singular values cost only a fixed power of \(N\).
Thus a step increases the retained index by \(2p\) with logarithmic cost \(O_P(\log N)\), uniformly in the corner diagonal.

## Initialization and local planar comparison

At target \(n=N-o(N)\) let \(l=n-m,\ m=\lfloor n/\log^3(n+3)\rfloor\), and start at deficit \(q_0=pm+j\) for \(j=h_n\) or \(2h_n\).
At each of \(m\) physical steps the deficit decreases by \(p\), so the total normalized cost is \(O(m\log N/N)=o(1)\) with summable exceptions by the preceding bounds.
The initial companion equals its canonical \(l\)-dimensional evaluation with all variable coefficients scaled by \(\sqrt{l/N}\to1\).
Its drift at \(z_N\to z\) also converges.
The initial normalized expected singular count is bounded by \(Ct^\beta\) on \(l^{-\gamma}\le t\le1\) for some fixed \(\beta,\gamma>0\).
For uniformity, use the proof of the [affine finite-variance count](Yin_C_F0186_v40.md#argument) on the compact family \(L_z(sX)\) with \(s\in[1/2,2]\) and \(z\) in a fixed compact set.
Each pencil is full because its Schur complement \(P(sx)-z\) is a nonzero free polynomial.
The [free estimate](Yin_C_F0352_v1.md#statement) is uniform on this family; the [conditional bounded-core comparison](Yin_C_F0195_v4.md#free-background) has constants bounded by fixed upper bounds on the coefficient norms, with bounds uniform in \(s,z\) as seen from the finitely many derivative and covariance terms.
This parameter \(s\) is a common physical scale, not a free random input.

The [original row rank concentration](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration) at \(t=l^{-\gamma}\) gives normalized count \(o(1/\log^3 N)\) outside summable probabilities, by taking a deviation \(1/\log^4 N\).
Hence all retained values lie above that clip.
The expected clipped negative log converges to the free negative integral by layer integration of the polynomial count from the clip to a fixed \(d_0>0\), then \(d_0\downarrow0\).
At a fixed clip the varying coefficients have the same expected limit as the limiting tuple, because the expected normalized squared Hilbert--Schmidt perturbation tends to zero, and bounded Lipschitz singular tests are continuous under this perturbation by the singular-value Hoffman--Wielandt inequality.
The canonical tuple has the [finite-variance weak limit](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#finite-variance-weak).
The expected positive log tails are uniformly integrable by the uniform bound on expected normalized squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm of the affine pencils.
Clipping the positive log at \(N^4\) in singular-value scale is eventually immaterial on the summable norm guard.
Both clipped log tests concentrate with summable deviations by original row ranks and variation \(O(\log N)\).
Fixed positive tail tests clipped above likewise concentrate, so omission of \(q_0=o(l)\) of the smallest values costs \(o(N)\) in positive logs.
Equivalently those omitted positive logs are bounded by \(q_0\) times the bottom median's positive log, eventually bounded by the fixed-scale weak law.
The initial retained normalized logarithm consequently has lower limit at least \(u(z)\), since the factor from normalized companion trace to physical trace is \(p\).
For the upper bound at the final \(n\) use any fixed lower clip and the same fixed-clip limit, with \(O(j|\log d_0|/N)\) for an omitted negative clip, then send \(d_0\downarrow0\).
These concentration bounds sum directly over \(N\) even if a particular canonical minor size is repeated.

Unit triangular Schur elimination compares the retained products with those of \(\operatorname{diag}(M-z,-I)\) at cost \(O_P(j\log N)\), by [the omitted-factor comparison](Yin_C_F0186_v41.md#assembly), including a singular shift by continuity of the products.
Appending identity singular values changes the trimmed logarithms by the same order on the norm guard.
This proves the moving pointwise results for both trims.
For planar \(L^1_{\rm loc}\), apply Fubini at fixed nonreal parameters.
On each disk the positive log sums of the affine companion divided by \(N\) are eventually bounded almost surely by the positive-log estimates above at zero and the scalar shift bound.
Thus the companion trimmed log is bounded below by the original scalar characteristic log divided by \(N\) minus a uniformly bounded positive-log sum, and is bounded above by a uniformly bounded positive-log sum.
The negative part of the characteristic log has a deterministic uniform disk \(L^2\) bound by Jensen for the translated kernels \(\log^-|z-\lambda|\), since \(n/N\le1\).
This gives local uniform integrability and the asserted planar limits.
The adjacent log follows from the difference of the two trims and the \(o(N)\) total positive logs of \(2h_n\) omitted values.
The same trimmed difference versus the full scalar characteristic log gives the [finite-variance terminal equivalence](Yin_C_F0186_v41.md#terminal) by the original [potential criterion](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#remaining-defect), including independent dimensions for necessity.

## Real sparse vectors and compensated polynomial words

Fix a degree \(d_0\ge1\), a finite list of scalar-coefficient real polynomials \(H\) of that degree, or a bounded family with a common highest-degree monomial of coefficient bounded away from zero.
For any prescribed member of such a family we prove the following estimate, with constants uniform over the family.
Use the old \(n\)-site Wigner matrices with \(n/N\to1\).
Write \(K=C_J\log^3(N+2)\), with \(J\) an arbitrarily large fixed exception exponent.
Outside probability \(O(N^{-J})\), every real unit \(x\) with \(\|H(X)x\|\le c\) has distance at least \(K^{-C}\) from all real vectors supported on at most \(c'n/\log K\) coordinates.
All constants below may depend on the degree, the number of colors, a bound on coefficients, and the chosen positive lower bound on the highest coefficient.
We give the details because this assertion applies to high degrees during an initial flag comparison.

Truncate each raw coordinate at \(\sqrt N\log^2(N+2)\), replacing entries of larger modulus by zero.
By the [finite-variance truncation and walk bounds](Yin_C_F0196_v6.md#truncated-core), the set \(E\) of all endpoints of marked pairs in any color has at most \(C N/\log^3 N\) sites and all resulting core matrices \(X_i^c\) have norm at most \(K\), outside \(O(N^{-J})\).
The constants are uniform for \(n/N\to1\).
Expose the indicators and marked values; all remaining coordinates are still independent with their appropriate conditional laws.
The conditional strict upper variances are uniformly bounded below and their centered squares uniformly integrable for large \(N\).
The [independent-rectangle row estimate](Yin_C_F0196_v6.md#argument) thus maps each fixed real input, on a rectangle with a fixed positive fraction of \(n\) rows, to a vector of norm \(\ge c\) times its input norm except with \(Ce^{-cn}\).
The same holds for independent differences of conditional draws; the variance and uniform integrability argument there applies to those draws.
This rectangle estimate and all nets are used before adding the overall norm failure from the original, unconditioned law.

Let \(\mathcal W\) contain all words of length at most \(d_0-1\), including the empty word, and define
\[
 \Psi((v_w))=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W} w(X^c)v_w,\qquad v_w\in\mathbb R^E.
\]
Its range contains the range of \(H(X)-H(X^c)\) by telescoping each word with the core on the left of the first difference.
On the core norm guard, with additional conditional failure \(Ce^{-cn}\) for each admissible mark pattern, its least singular value is at least \(K^{-C_1}\) when the domain is nonempty.
Here is a quantitative proof.
For a fixed unit tuple of coefficients choose the largest degree \(j\) at which some coefficient vector has norm at least \(|\mathcal W|^{-1/2}K^{-j(d_0+1)}\).
Such a degree exists; the contribution of all higher words on the norm guard is at most \(C K^{-1}|\mathcal W|^{-1/2}K^{-j(d_0+1)}\), with \(C\) fixed.
For \(j=0\) the truncated polynomial-vector is the empty-word vector itself.
For \(j>0\) select a word realizing this lower norm at degree \(j\).
Starting at \(E\), take \(j\) subsequent disjoint panels of size \(\lfloor n/(2d_0+2)\rfloor\) outside \(E\).
Test only the last panel, and leave fresh precisely the rectangles joining consecutive panels in the sequence of colors of the chosen word, read from right to left.
Disjoint edges give independent rectangles even when a color repeats.
A \(j\)-fold alternating rectangular difference kills every term of degree less than \(j\).
To contribute in degree \(j\), a walk beginning at the initial support and ending at the last panel must traverse each of these \(j\) rectangles once in forward order.
Thus the only surviving term is the product of the \(j\) difference rectangles applied to the chosen coefficient vector.
Sequential independent row estimates give it norm at least \(c_j\) times the chosen norm with failure \(Ce^{-cn}\).
By \(j\) Cartesian Cauchy--Schwarz steps, a small-norm event for the degree-truncated polynomial-vector of probability \(p_0\) holds at all \(2^j\) resamplings with probability at least \(p_0^{2^j}\).
On this event its alternating difference has norm at most \(2^j\) times the tested width.
A width less than \(2^{-j} c_j\) times the chosen norm therefore has exponentially small probability for the degree-truncated test, with adjusted constants.
Only after this bound do we compare to the higher terms on the original norm guard.
A fine inverse-power-of-\(K\) net of the unit tuple sphere suffices on that guard, since the map norm is at most \(C K^{d_0}\).
Its log size is \(O_{g,d_0}(|E|\log K)=o(n)\).
This proves the claimed lower bound.

If \(\|H(X)x\|\le c\) for a real unit vector \(x\), there is now a compensating tuple with \(\|v\|\le K^{C_2}\) and
\(\|H(X^c)x+\Psi(v)\|\le c\): use the range inclusion and \(\|H(X^c)x\|\le C K^{d_0}\).
To exclude sparse \(x\), fix first a sparse real unit vector \(x_0\) and a tuple bounded by \(K^{C_2}\).
Repeat the \(d_0\)-rectangle test above starting at \(E\cup\operatorname{supp}(x_0)\), using the nonzero top word of \(H\).
All compensator terms have smaller degree, so the isolated difference maps \(x_0\) with that top coefficient.
The last-panel output fails its fixed positive lower bound with probability \(Ce^{-cn}\) for each tested pair.
On the norm guard the full test is Lipschitz in the vectors with a fixed-power-of-\(K\) constant.
The compensator nets again cost \(\exp(o(n))\); for the sparse unit vectors the log cost, including the supports, is bounded by \(C s n(\log K+\log(e/s))\) when the support size is at most \(s n\).
Choosing \(s=c'/\log K\) with small \(c'>0\) absorbs this cost.
Thus a unit near-mode within \(K^{-C}\) of a sparse vector contradicts the test, reducing \(c\) and enlarging \(C\) as needed.
One may normalize a nearby nonzero sparse vector to unit length at a cost at most twice its original distance.
The constants are independent of a prescribed member of the parameter family because all lower-degree coefficients stay bounded and the identified top coefficient stays bounded away from zero.

## Several near-eigenvectors tested against a common fresh vector

Remove a fixed thin strip at the real axis from a bounded disk and work first in the upper half-plane.
Fix \(D=12\) parameters \(w_1,\ldots,w_D\) in the resulting compact region with a prescribed positive minimum spacing.
On the finite-variance Frobenius norm guard \(\|M\|\le N^5\) outside \(O(N^{-3})\).
For the estimates take \(B=20D+10\) and \(A=10(B+2)\).
All exceptional estimates below are uniform for **each prescribed tuple** in the compact separated set.
The constants may depend on the spectral strip and spacing; the powers \(A,B\) do not.

For any subset of \(d\le D\) shifts consider unit right near-eigenvectors \(v_j\) of \(M\) with \(\|(M-w_j)v_j\|\le (N^5+R)N^{-Ah_n}\).
The same proof for left vectors uses \(M^{\mathsf T}\) and the conjugate shifts.
Apply the sparse-vector estimate to
\[
 H(X)=\prod_{j=1}^d (M-w_j)(M-\bar w_j)
\]
or to the same expression for \(M^{\mathsf T}\).
Its coefficients are real and uniformly bounded; its homogeneous term of degree \(4d\) has a nonzero word coefficient \(Q_{ij}^{2d}\) from repeating any fixed nonzero quadratic word.
Pay the sparse-vector exception only once per subset and side, with finitely many subsets.
For real \((t_j)\) of norm one put \(x=\sum_j t_j\operatorname{Re}v_j\).
The interpolation polynomial in \(M\) which is one at one of the \(2d\) nodes \(w_j,\bar w_j\) and zero at all the others has degree \(2d-1\), bounded coefficients and norm at most \(C N^{5(2d-1)}\).
Acting on a near-eigenvector differs from its nodal value times that vector by at most \(C N^{5(2d-2)}(N^5+R)N^{-Ah_n}\), by factoring each monomial difference.
Choose a coordinate with \(|t_j|\ge1/\sqrt d\) and apply interpolation to \(\frac12\sum_j t_j(v_j+\bar v_j)\).
It follows that \(\|x\|\ge c N^{-5(2d-1)}\).
Also \(\|H(X)x\|\le CN^{5(2d-1)}(N^5+R)N^{-Ah_n}\) by the residuals and the same factorization.
The normalized \(x\) is thus among the real near-modes just considered.
After any deletion of at most \(c'n/\log K\) coordinates, the real matrix with columns \(\operatorname{Re}v_j\) retains least singular value at least \(cN^{-5(2d-1)}K^{-C}\).

The maximum-volume extraction in the [disjoint-basis calculation](Yin_C_F0351_v5.md#sign-flag-area) now gives at least \(c_D n/\log K\) disjoint bases of \(d\) rows from this matrix, each with least singular value at least \(cN^{-5(2d-1)}K^{-C}/\sqrt{dn}\).
Indeed on each remainder a maximum-volume basis expresses every row with coefficients bounded by one, and one may successively delete its \(d\) rows while the preceding lower bound holds.
After multiplication by \(N^{B-1}\), every resulting basis has least singular value at least one for large \(N\).
For any common fresh raw vector \(g\) on this side the [basis Fourier estimate for general iid real entries](Yin_C_F0351_v6.md#border-tests) bounds the probability that all \(|v_j^*g|\le N^{-B}\) by \(C_D(n/\log K)^{-d/2}\).
It suffices to test their real parts, whose rescaled box eventually lies inside the unit ball.
This works conditionally for any such vectors fixed before the current draw.

At the first \(r\) Gram--Schmidt stages of either projected singular frame, choose a unit vector in the frame orthogonal, in frame coordinates, to the previously projected raw columns.
If all the resulting scalar tests exceed \(N^{-B}\) the projected raw volume is at least \(N^{-Br}\).
The actual raw volume is at most \(N^{2r}\) when all its columns have norm at most \(N^2\), outside a single probability \(O(h_nN^{-3})\).
Thus any initial flag failure on this guard selects a side and a stage with a small scalar test.
If a projected column history is singular the same measurable direction choice is still possible, and a first vanishing volume implies a failure at or before that stage.
Over all separated parameters assign each failure to one of \(2h_n\) stages and one of two sides.
At a stage assigned \(s\) failures test only the more frequent side, with \(d\ge s/2\).
Its common fresh vector is independent of the entire previous history even if the two current vectors are dependent.
The sparse-spread guard depends only on the original minor for this prescribed tuple.
Iteration over chronological stages gives a total failure probability at most
\[
 C_D(4h_n)^D(n/\log K)^{-D/4}+O(N^{-3}+h_nN^{-3})
\]
for all \(D\) prescribed shifts to fail simultaneously; choose \(J>4\).
The series is summable for \(D=12\).
For a fixed disk and a positive tolerance, discard a thin real strip, reflect by complex conjugation to the upper half-plane and enclose the result in a compact set.
The area of failures is invariant under reflection, since the bottom spaces at conjugate shifts are conjugate and the raw columns real.
An exceptional area above tolerance gives a product set of separated failing \(D\)-tuples of measure bounded below, by recursively removing disks of sufficiently small fixed radius.
Fubini and the uniform joint bound prove the area assertion in the statement.

With \(r>0\), the [compound estimate](Yin_C_F0351_v1.md#adaptive-gap) applies also to distinct left and right deleted flags: its leading term pairs the complementary left and right wedges with the corresponding physical volume vectors.
The product of the two overlap bounds dominates the error \(N^{-Ah_n}\) because \(r\le2h_n\) and \(A>4(B+2)\).
The remaining adjacent ratios cost \(O_A(h_n^3\log N)\) in the undivided logarithm.
The result controls the first deletions from the old polynomial minor; the characteristic factors during the remaining deletions and the reconstruction of the full matrix still require estimates at their actual paired spaces.
