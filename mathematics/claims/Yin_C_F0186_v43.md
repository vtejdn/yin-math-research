# A product-measure tube bound for affine Wigner determinants

<a id="statement"></a>
## Matrix determinants and polynomial images

Use the fixed independent Wigner colors, normalizations and arbitrary cross-size couplings of the [matrix determinant Task](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract).
Declare each upper-entry law on the real line or on the complex plane viewed as two real coordinates, and each diagonal law on the real line.
Retain the finite second moments of the contract.
Assume that each real raw law, including each diagonal law, has constants \(C,\alpha>0\) with
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}\Pr\{|X-x|\le h\}\le C h^\alpha,\qquad 0<h\le1 .
 \tag{1}
\]
For each planar raw law assume either a bounded joint density with respect to planar Lebesgue measure or independent coordinates satisfying (1) after some fixed orthogonal change of real coordinates.
Different colors may use different laws, and coordinates in the bounded-density case may depend on one another.
One may also declare any color to have purely imaginary upper entries \(iX\) with \(X\) satisfying (1) and identically zero diagonal.
For this color the evaluation space below is \(i\mathfrak{so}(N,\mathbb R)\), the purely imaginary Hermitian matrices with real skew-symmetric imaginary part.

For every fixed square affine pencil \(L(x)=A_0+\sum_j A_jx_j\) of auxiliary size \(m\), write \(u=\log\Delta_{\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau}(L(s))\) and \(U_N=(mN)^{-1}\log|\det L(W^{(N)})|\), including \(\log0=-\infty\).
Let \(\mathcal G\) consist of the dimensions admitting an invertible evaluation on the declared input spaces.
At finite \(u\), \(U_N\to u\) almost surely under each coupling and in every finite \(L^r\), \(r\ge1\), along \(\mathcal G\).
At finite \(u\), all sufficiently large even dimensions belong to \(\mathcal G\), as do all sufficiently large dimensions when every color is planar or there is a single odd witness.
The determinant is identically zero outside \(\mathcal G\), and the dimension-limsup equals \(u\) almost surely for every pencil.
At \(u=-\infty\) the full sequence tends to \(-\infty\) almost surely.
The constant coefficient may be singular or zero.

For every fixed scalar noncommutative polynomial \(P\) and every prescribed \(z\in\mathbb C\), the [scalar determinant limsup](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) also holds under these entry hypotheses.
For each fixed \(P\) its empirical eigenvalue measure converges weakly almost surely to the Brown measure of \(P(s)\) under every permitted coupling, as required in [the spectral Task](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract).

<a id="one-block"></a>
## Negative moments on one raw block

Fix a positive integer \(b\).
For a real or planar raw block \(X\), write \(M(f)\) for the largest absolute monomial coefficient of a complex polynomial \(f\) of total degree at most \(b\) in its one or two real coordinates.
There exist \(a,C>0\), uniform over the finitely many laws and all such polynomials with \(M(f)=1\), such that
\[
 \Pr\{|f(X)|\le v\}\le C v^a \quad (0<v\le1).
 \tag{2}
\]
To see this in one coordinate, any subset of \([-1,1]\) of positive length \(\ell\) contains \(b+1\) points separated by at least \(\ell/(4(b+1))\).
Choose points successively, removing an interval of twice that radius each time.
Lagrange interpolation at these points shows that every coefficient of a degree-\(b\) polynomial of modulus at most \(v\) there is bounded by \(C_b v\ell^{-b}\).
Its \(v\)-sublevel set in \([-1,1]\) therefore has length at most \(C_b(v/M(f))^{1/b}\).
For \(R\ge1\), the rescaled polynomial \(f(R\,\cdot)\) has coefficient maximum at least \(M(f)\).
With \(M(f)=1\), the sublevel set on \([-R,R]\) consequently has length at most \(C_b Rv^{1/b}\).
It consists of at most \(2b+1\) intervals or points by the polynomial inequality \(|f|^2-v^2\le0\), with the constant case satisfying the same component bound.
Applying (1) on these intervals, enlarging its constant to cover lengths greater than one, and using the variance bound outside \([-R,R]\), gives \(C((Rv^{1/b})^\alpha+R^{-2})\).
Choosing \(R=v^{-1/(2b)}\) gives a positive power in (2).

For two coordinates, rescale a square \([-R,R]^2\) to \([-1,1]^2\) and expand the rescaled polynomial in its first coordinate.
One coefficient polynomial in the second coordinate has coefficient maximum at least one.
Outside a subset of second coordinates of length \(C_b v^{1/(2b)}\), its modulus exceeds \(\sqrt v\) by the one-variable length bound.
Each remaining first-coordinate slice has sublevel length at most \(C_b v^{1/(2b)}\) by the same bound.
The joint bounded density and second moment thus give probability at most \(C(R^2 v^{1/(2b)}+R^{-2})\).
Choose \(R=v^{-1/(8b)}\) to obtain (2) in the plane.
For independent planar coordinates satisfying (1), first use the stated orthogonal coordinates, in which coefficient maximum remains comparable by norm equivalence on the fixed finite-dimensional polynomial space.
Normalize the new coefficient maximum to one, then choose a first-coordinate coefficient whose polynomial in the second coordinate has coefficient maximum one.
The one-coordinate probability estimate bounds the chance that this coefficient has modulus at most \(\sqrt v\) by \(C v^{a_1/2}\).
At each other second coordinate, independence permits the same one-coordinate estimate in the first coordinate, at relative level at most \(\sqrt v\), giving \(C v^{a_1/2}\) after taking the smaller of the two one-coordinate exponents \(a_1\).
Undoing the fixed coefficient normalization changes only the constant.
Scaling and integrating (2) gives \(\mathbb E|f(X)|^{-r}\le C_r M(f)^{-r}\) for all nonzero \(f\) and \(0<r<a\).

<a id="tube"></a>
## Taylor coefficients and the tube estimate

Let \(F\) be any nonzero complex polynomial on \(K\) independent raw blocks with total degree at most \(b\) separately in each block.
For a joint multi-index \(\gamma\), put \(a_\gamma(X)=\partial^\gamma F(X)/\gamma!\) and let \(S\) be its set of touched blocks, with \(s=|S|\).
Fix \(0<p\le1\) with \(2p<a\) and \(2pb\le2\).
There is \(H\), independent of \(K,F,\gamma\), with
\[
 \mathbb E\left|a_\gamma(X)/F(X)\right|^p\le H^s .
 \tag{3}
\]
First, for a polynomial \(G\) on \(s\) blocks with \(M(G)=1\), integration of the one-block estimate successively gives \(\mathbb E|G|^{-2p}\le C_{2p}^s\).
Indeed fix a monomial with coefficient of modulus one and expand \(G\) in the first block.
The coefficient polynomial \(g\) of its chosen first-block monomial still has \(M(g)=1\) in the remaining blocks.
Conditionally the one-block inverse-moment bound is at most \(C_{2p}|g|^{-2p}\), and induction from the zero-block constant gives the assertion, including almost-sure nonvanishing of \(g\).
Applied on all \(K\) blocks this also gives \(F(X)\ne0\) almost surely after normalization.

Freeze all blocks outside \(S\).
Almost every resulting polynomial in \(S\) is nonzero by the preceding conclusion and Fubini.
Divide it by its coefficient maximum, which cancels in the ratio in (3).
In these \(s\) blocks each coefficient now has modulus at most one.
Expanding the binomials of the Taylor formula around the sampled coordinates bounds the numerator by \(C_b^s\prod_{i\in S}(1+|X_i|)^b\), since each block has at most two coordinates and bounded degree.
The conditional \(2p\)-moment of this bound is at most a constant to the \(s\)th power by independence and the second moments.
Cauchy–Schwarz with the preceding inverse \(2p\)-moment proves (3).

If \(F\) vanishes at a point within coordinate sup distance \(\rho\le1\) of \(X\), the finite joint Taylor formula gives
\[
 1\le\sum_{\gamma\ne0} \rho^{p|\gamma|}|a_\gamma(X)/F(X)|^p
\]
by subadditivity of the \(p\)th power.
For each chosen subset of \(s\) blocks there are at most \(D_b^s\) admissible nonzero block multi-indices, where \(D_b\) bounds the number of monomials of degree at most \(b\) in two coordinates.
Their total degree is at least \(s\).
Consequently the probability that the sample is within \(\rho\) of the real zero set of \(F\) is at most
\[
 (1+D_b H\rho^p)^K-1\le C K\rho^p
 \qquad (K\rho^p\le1).
 \tag{4}
\]
Every constant here depends only on the one-block laws and degree bound.

<a id="application"></a>
## From repair of the kernel to ordinary logarithms

For \(N\in\mathcal G\), the determinant \(F\) in the original raw variables is a nonzero polynomial, in \(K=O(N^2)\) independent blocks and of total degree at most \(2m\) in each block.
The off-diagonal block perturbs a subspace of output dimension at most \(2m\), and the diagonal block a subspace of dimension at most \(m\).
Apply the [deterministic structured repair on the mixed evaluation space](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#argument) before its Gaussian perturbation step.
It provides fixed \(\theta,d,C>0\) such that if \(s_{\min}(L(W))\le\varepsilon\) and \(\max_j\|W_j\|\le\varepsilon^{-\theta}\), the input is within Hilbert–Schmidt distance \(C\varepsilon^d\) of an exact singular tuple in that same space, for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).
An empty repair type contributes no point on this event.
Each raw coordinate is \(\sqrt N\) times the corresponding normalized matrix entry or its real or imaginary part.
Thus (4) applies with \(\rho=C\sqrt N\,\varepsilon^d\).
The omitted norm event has probability \(O(N\varepsilon^{2\theta})\) by the expected squared Hilbert–Schmidt norms.
Choosing \(B_0\) sufficiently large to ensure \(K\rho^p\le1\) gives constants \(k,c>0\) such that
\[
 \Pr\{s_{\min}(L(W))\le\varepsilon\}\le C N^k\varepsilon^c
 \qquad (N\in\mathcal G,\ 0<\varepsilon\le N^{-B_0})
 \tag{5}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).
One can take \(k=2+p/2\) and \(c=\min(2\theta,dp)\).

At finite \(u\), the [finite-variance band and clipped logarithm proof](Yin_C_F0186_v40.md#determinant) applies to every fixed polynomial cutoff \(N^{-B}\).
Choose \(B\ge B_0\) with \(Bc>k+2\).
For the excess below the cutoff on a good dimension, the probability of a positive least-value log loss exceeding \(t\ge0\) is at most \(C N^{k-Bc}e^{-ct}\) by (5).
The normalized sum of all excess logarithms is bounded by that single largest loss.
Integration and first Borel–Cantelli give its finite \(L^r\) and almost-sure convergence to zero under every permitted coupling.
The [good-dimension witnesses and parity](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#log-assembly) depend only on the declared real symmetric and Hermitian evaluation spaces.
The clipped upper bound at \(u=-\infty\) applies on every dimension by [the same finite-variance argument](Yin_C_F0186_v40.md#determinant).

The [scalar linearization and planar argument](Yin_C_F0186_v40.md#scalar) now apply using (5) at every prescribed scalar shift on its good dimensions.
The finite and free scalar logarithms carry the same auxiliary-dimension factor.
For nonconstant \(P\) the free target is finite at every shift, and the zero input is a witness in every dimension when \(z\ne P(0)\).
Hence for each coupling Fubini gives convergence at almost every spectral point on one probability-one event, and the local planar integral bounds give convergence of the eigenvalue measures there.
For \(z=P(0)\) the fixed-shift limsup still follows from the good even dimensions, and constant polynomials are deterministic.

<a id="skew"></a>
## Purely imaginary upper entries and zero diagonal

To include a skew color, restrict the [real compression and repair](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#argument) to the corresponding real linear spaces before applying the polynomial error bound.
With \(U,V\) the mutually orthogonal real frames of that argument, \(C_j=U^t W_jU\) is purely imaginary Hermitian and \(E_j=V^t W_jU\) is purely imaginary rectangular.
Parameterize \(C_j\) by the independent entries of its real skew-symmetric imaginary part and \(E_j\) by its real imaginary part.
Use these real parameters for all variables of this color in the inside and outside residual equations and in their zero sets.
The number of variables and the degrees remain bounded independently of \(N\), with finitely many choices of frame dimensions.
The same global polynomial error bound on each of these fixed real polynomial maps, and the same empty-zero-set estimate, therefore give positive repair and norm-guard exponents for this list of color spaces.
The correction \(U\Delta C_jU^t+V\Delta E_jU^t+U\Delta E_j^*V^t\) is purely imaginary Hermitian, so its diagonal vanishes exactly.
It realizes the corrected inside and outside residuals on \(U\) with no additional physical directions, with the same Hilbert–Schmidt norm comparison.
Only the independent strict upper imaginary coordinates enter (4), each with block degree at most \(2m\).
Thus (5) holds on the declared good dimensions.

For a finite free target the [generic-rank theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v2.md#generic-rank) gives a nonzero determinant polynomial \(f_q\) on unrestricted Hermitian tuples in every sufficiently large size \(q\).
Set \(\sigma_j=-1\) on the skew colors and \(+1\) on the others.
Both \(H\mapsto f_q(H)\) and \(H\mapsto f_q((\sigma_j\overline H_j)_j)\) are nonzero polynomials on that real coordinate space, since the latter substitution is a real-linear bijection.
Choose a Hermitian tuple where their product is nonzero.
Write \(\mathcal R(B)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\Re B&-\Im B\\ \Im B&\Re B\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) for realification.
Use \(\mathcal R(H_j)\) in every ordinary color and \(i\mathcal R(-iH_j)\) in every skew color.
The latter is purely imaginary Hermitian with zero diagonal because \(-iH_j\) is skew-Hermitian and its realification is real skew-symmetric.
One fixed unitary on the doubled physical space splits these inputs into the two tuples \(H_j\) and \(\sigma_j\overline H_j\).
Both pencils are invertible, giving every sufficiently large even size, and direct sums give every sufficiently large odd size if one odd witness exists.
The unrestricted finite-variance clipped estimates allow arbitrary dependence between the real and imaginary parts of each upper entry, including a vanished real part and deterministic zero diagonal.
The [ordinary logarithm and scalar arguments](#application) now apply, including the zero-tuple witness at every \(z\ne P(0)\).

<a id="examples"></a>
## Entry laws reached by the estimate

The centered and variance-normalized middle-thirds Cantor law obeys (1) with \(\alpha=\log 2/\log 3\).
Before this affine normalization, an interval of length at most \(3^{-j}\) meets at most two level-\(j\) construction intervals, each of mass \(2^{-j}\).
This singular law falls outside the [translation hypothesis](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#statement), since total-variation continuity under translation implies absolute continuity by convolution with approximate identities and convergence in total variation.

A bounded real density can also have no positive power translation modulus.
For \(j\ge3\) put \(I_j=[2^{-j},2^{-j}+2^{-j-2}]\), \(h_j=2^{-j^3}\).
On each \(I_j\) occupy alternate consecutive intervals of length \(h_j\), and normalize the indicator of the union of the occupied intervals to obtain a density.
The intervals \(I_j\) are disjoint, and a translation by \(h_j\) exchanges occupied and vacant intervals inside \(I_j\) except within distance \(h_j\) of the endpoints.
The resulting total variation distance is at least a constant times \(2^{-j}\), whereas \(h_j^\alpha=2^{-\alpha j^3}\) for every \(\alpha>0\).
Centering and dividing by the positive standard deviation preserve this failure as well as bounded density and bounded support.
