# Affine Wigner determinants from continuous upper entries and arbitrary diagonals

<a id="statement"></a>
## The three limits

Use the fixed-law Hermitian Wigner model and arbitrary couplings across dimensions of the [matrix determinant Task](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract).
Assume each strict upper law has a density either on the real line for a real symmetric color or jointly on the plane for a complex Hermitian color.
Only the second moments in the contract are required, and the centered real diagonal laws are arbitrary.
For every fixed affine pencil \(L=A_0+\sum_{j=1}^g A_jx_j\) of size \(m\), the almost-sure dimension-limsup of \(U_N=(mN)^{-1}\log|\det L(W^{(N)})|\) equals \(u=\log\Delta_{\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau}L(s)\).
At finite \(u\), for every \(h\) admitting an invertible evaluation \(L(S)\) on the declared real symmetric and Hermitian \(h\)-dimensional inputs, \(U_N\to u\) almost surely along the multiples \(N=hn\).
Such an \(h\) exists by the [finite-target witness theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v38.md#log-assembly).
In particular an invertible \(A_0\) gives full-sequence convergence.
At \(u=-\infty\) the full sequence tends to \(-\infty\) almost surely.
For each fixed scalar polynomial \(P\) and each prescribed shift, including \(P(0)\), its [ordinary determinant limsup](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) equals the free logarithm almost surely.
For each fixed \(P\), its empirical eigenvalue measure converges weakly almost surely to the Brown measure of \(P(s)\), as in [the spectral Task](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract).

For an individual finite-target pencil and a selected witness, the proof also permits each strict upper law to have a singular part of sufficiently small mass depending on \(g,m,h\).
The explicit mass condition is given [below](#marks).
The estimates for compact bounded-density components may depend on the pencil, witness and component laws.

<a id="witness"></a>
## Grouping sites at an invertible witness

Fix \(h,S\) as above and put \(B=A_0\otimes I_h+\sum_j A_j\otimes S_j\).
For \(N=hn\), partition the sites into \(n\) consecutive groups of \(h\) sites and arrange the physical blocks with auxiliary index in \(\mathbb C^m\otimes\mathbb C^h\).
Left-normalize \(L(W)\) by \(B^{-1}\) in this index to form \(T\).
Write \(T=I+V_n\), using \(W_j-I_n\otimes S_j\) for the random parts in physical group ordering.
The deterministic subtraction affects only the group diagonals.
For \(1\le a,b\le h\) define \(C_{jab}=h^{-1/2}B^{-1}(A_j\otimes E_{ab})\).
At a strict upper group edge, one original real coordinate in color \(j\), position \(ab\), multiplies \(C_{jab}\) in the forward block and \(C_{jba}\) in the reverse block, with a common factor \(n^{-1/2}\).
The imaginary coordinate of a planar color multiplies \(iC_{jab}\) forward and \(-iC_{jba}\) in reverse.
Thus reversing the edge permutes the coordinate coefficient pairs, with a sign change on imaginary coordinates.
The independent raw whole atoms on one edge have a fixed real dimension depending only on \(g,h\).
Different strict upper group edges are independent and use disjoint original upper entries.
The group diagonals can be arbitrary for the conditional frame argument below.
Their actual contribution includes the within-group original off-diagonals, original diagonals and the deterministic shifts.

Choose an orthogonal splitting adapted to a composition series of the unital algebra generated by the \(C_{jab}\).
Every block of \(V_n\), including the group diagonal, is upper triangular in this splitting.
Write \(C_{jab}\) also for the induced coefficients on one irreducible diagonal factor.
If all its coefficients vanish it is an identity factor.
Consider any nonzero factor, of auxiliary dimension \(d_0\le mh\).
Allow a real scalar \(s\in[1/2,3/2]\) in its constant term, writing \(T_s=sI+V_n^{\rm fac}\).
The estimates will be uniform in \(s\).

<a id="algebra"></a>
## Doubled blocks and off-diagonal observations

For each forward/reverse coefficient pair \((C_f,C_b)\) above, set \(D_\ell=\operatorname{diag}(C_f,C_b^{\mathsf T})\).
The reversed generators \(\operatorname{diag}(C_b,C_f^{\mathsf T})\) already belong to this list up to signs and permutation.
Put \(\mathcal B=\operatorname{alg}_{\mathbb C}(I,D_\ell)\) and take \(V=\mathcal B^*\) as a real Hilbert space with the real Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.
Here \(\mathcal B^*\) denotes the space of adjoints, and \(p=\dim_{\mathbb R} V\le4(mh)^2\).
All blocks of \(T_s\oplus T_s^{\mathsf T}\) in the doubled physical order belong to \(\mathcal B\).
For a group diagonal its color block comes from an \(h\)-by-\(h\) real symmetric or Hermitian matrix \(G\), including the witness subtraction.
Summing the real and available imaginary generators weighted by the entries of \(G\) produces upper block \(\sum G_{ab} C_{jab}\) and lower block its transpose, since \(G_{ba}=\overline{G}_{ab}\).
The inverse blocks on the invertible event likewise belong to \(\mathcal B\) by Cayley–Hamilton.

Write \(H_\ell v=D_\ell^*v\), \(\mathcal O(v)=(\operatorname{Tr}(v^*D_\ell))_\ell\), and \(\mathcal D=\ker\mathcal O\).
The \(H_\ell\) have trivial common kernel, and no nonzero real subspace of \(\mathcal D\) is invariant under all \(H_\ell\).
Indeed the first coordinate representation is irreducible, and the second is irreducible since its generators are all transposes of the first family up to a signed permutation.
Both are nonzero, so the spans of the ranges of their respective generators are the whole spaces.
This gives the common-kernel assertion for the adjoint multipliers.
The faithful direct sum of these two simple modules also makes \(\mathcal B\) semisimple with nondegenerate ambient bilinear trace form.
If a real invariant subspace of \(\mathcal D\) were nonzero, the adjoints of its complex span would form a nonzero right ideal of \(\mathcal B\) orthogonal under this trace to every generator.
They are consequently trace-orthogonal to all nonempty words, by cyclicity and the right ideal property.
The span of nonempty words is a two-sided ideal equal to \(\mathcal B\), since neither of the two faithful-sum constituents is annihilated by all generators and every simple summand occurs in at least one constituent.
This contradicts trace nondegeneracy. [Han26b, Lemmas 2.3–2.5 and Section 6.3](../audits/literature/Han26b/Yin_U001.md#doubled-algebra)

<a id="marks"></a>
## Exposed components and the punctured row

Set \(P_*=4(mh)^2\), \(\eta_0=\kappa=1/(16P_*)\).
For every original strict upper law retain a compact bounded-density component whose omitted probability is smaller than \(1/(1024gh^2P_*^2)\).
An absolutely continuous law admits such a restriction by cutting off both the spatial radius and its density.
More generally use any mixture decomposition with this bound on the omitted mass, introducing independent membership marks for individual whole original atoms.
Call a group edge good when all \(gh^2\) atoms on it have retained marks.
We never use the group diagonals for observations.
Except on an event of probability \(Ce^{-cn}\), two properties of the marks hold.
For every row \(a\) at most \(\eta_0n\) sites are erased, counting its diagonal and its bad incident edges.
For every anchor set \(I\) of at most \(2P_*\) group indices, at most \(\kappa n\) outside indices \(k\) have any bad edge to \(I\).
Indeed a group edge fails with probability at most \(1/(1024P_*^2)\), and the failure probability at one such outside index is at most \(1/(512P_*)\).
The trials for fixed anchors use disjoint edges as \(k\) varies.
Chernoff bounds and a polynomial union over anchor sets and rows prove both assertions for sufficiently large \(n\).

Fix the full membership pattern satisfying these two properties.
The whole original atoms remain independent with their conditional laws on strict upper group edges.
Let \(G_n\) be the event \(\max_j\|W_j\|_{\rm HS}\le N^2\), whose complement has probability \(O(N^{-3})\) on the original law by the finite second moments.
On \(G_n\) the squared sum of all normalized real coordinate entries after the group-diagonal subtraction is at most \(C n^4\), including the deterministic \(S_j\) terms.
For a row \(a\) let \(\mathcal A_a v\) be the row equations for the doubled pencil with output \(a\) omitted.
For \(k\ne a\) the output is
\[
 (\mathcal A_a v)_k=s v_k+\sum_{i,\ell} w_{ik\ell} H_\ell v_i .
 \tag{1}
\]
Here the real \(w_{ik\ell}\) include the normalization \(n^{-1/2}\) and the orientation permutation and signs, with the diagonal coordinates just described.
Consequently \(\|\mathcal A_a\|\le C n^2\) on \(G_n\).
For each \(k\) the sum of squares of its coordinates is likewise at most \(C n^4\).
Fix the erased set \(J\) of this particular row, which includes \(a\).
The domain to be tested has \(v_i\in\mathcal D\) for \(i\notin J\), \(v_i\in V\) for \(i\in J\).
We prove that for all rows its least norm on unit vectors of this domain exceeds \(n^{-E_*}\) except on an event of original probability \(Ce^{-cn}+O(n^{-3})\).

<a id="frame"></a>
## Two Gram operators for one punctured row

For profiles separated into sites outside and inside \(J\), respectively, let \(\Gamma_g,\Gamma_b\) be the separate sums of \(v_i\otimes_{\mathbb R} v_i\).
Let \(E_g\subset\mathcal D\), \(E_b\subset V\) be their respective spans and define
\[
 \Psi((x_\ell),(y_\ell))=\sum_\ell H_\ell(x_\ell+y_\ell),
 \qquad x_\ell\in E_g,\quad y_\ell\in E_b .
\]
The two input copies are orthogonal in this domain even when their images or physical coefficient subspaces intersect.
Write \(Y_\sigma\) for the left singular subspace of \(\Psi\) at singular values at least \(\sigma\).
For fixed small \(\tau,\sigma_0>0\), every pair with positive total rank and every \(0<\sigma\le\sigma_0\) obeys
\[
 y=\dim Y_\sigma\ge1+c,\qquad
 c=\#\{\text{singular values of }P_{Y_\sigma^\perp}|_{E_g}\text{ at most }\tau\}. \tag{2}
\]
Only the directions of \(E_g\) are charged in \(c\).
For a proof by compactness, a nonzero subspace in either group gives a uniformly positive operator norm of \(\Psi\), using the trivial common kernel and compactness of the nonempty unit sphere of \(V\).
If the surplus fails at arbitrarily small cutoffs, take convergent subsequences of fixed-rank subspaces as \(\tau,\sigma\) tend to zero.
The near-kernel in \(E_g\) has limiting space inside the limiting \(Y\cap E_g\), and \(H_\ell E_g\subset Y\) since the inactive singular values tend to zero.
Thus failure forces the nonzero \(Y\) inside \(E_g\), giving \(H_\ell E_g\subset E_g\) for every \(\ell\), in contradiction to the algebra result.
If \(E_g=0\) the same argument gives \(y\ge1\) directly from \(E_b\ne0\).
This proves the uniform assertion, also when \(E_b=0\).

First fix a deterministic profile with \(\|v\|_2\le2\), positive total rank and \(\Gamma_j\succeq\gamma P_{E_j}\) for \(j=g,b\), \(0<\gamma\le1\).
The frame operator of its columns in either nonempty group has determinant at least \(\gamma^{d_j}\) on the \(d_j\)-dimensional span.
Cauchy–Binet and \(\|v\|_2\le2\) select \(d_j\le p\) sites in that group with Gram operator at least \(c_p\gamma^{d_j}n^{-d_j}P_{E_j}\).
Indeed some squared minor is at least \(\gamma^{d_j}/\binom n{d_j}\), and every other singular value of that minor is at most two.
Use the union \(I\) of these two disjoint anchor sets, omitting an empty group.
There are at least \((1-\kappa)n-2p-1\) outputs \(k\notin I\cup\{a\}\) whose edges to every anchor are good.
For this fixed profile expose all values except the anchor edges to these retained outputs.
Each retained output has an independent set of unexposed compact bounded-density blocks and a fixed translation in (1).
Orientation merely permutes coordinate maps and signs, so the Euclidean Gram on \(Y_\sigma\) of the linear map from its unexposed raw coordinates dominates
\[
 c_p\gamma^p n^{-p-1} P_{Y_\sigma}\Psi\Psi^* P_{Y_\sigma}
 \succeq c_p\gamma^p n^{-p-1}\sigma^2 I_{Y_\sigma}.
\]
Indeed summing over anchors and coordinates gives \(\sum_{\ell} H_\ell(\Gamma_{g,I}+\Gamma_{b,I})H_\ell^*/n\) before projection.
This is a deterministic Euclidean Gram identity, without a within-block distributional uncorrelatedness requirement.
Each output uses only boundedly many real dimensions, with conditional joint density and support radius bounded by constants independent of \(n\).
Integration along the orthogonal kernel of its projected linear map bounds its probability in a ball of radius \(C\delta\) by \((C\delta\, n^{(p+1)/2}\gamma^{-p/2}\sigma^{-1})^y\).
Multiply these bounds over the retained outputs and integrate the exposure conditionally on the fixed marks.
This is the anchor argument in [Han26b, Lemmas 3.4–3.6 and 6.1](../audits/literature/Han26b/Yin_U001.md#real-profiles), with an independent set of columns for each of the two Grams.

For clarity the following scales make the passage to all profiles uniform.
Restrict first to pairs for which the singular values of \(\Psi\) lie in \([0,\lambda]\cup[\sigma,\infty)\), with \(C n^2\lambda\le\delta\) and \(0<\delta\le c_0\gamma\) for a sufficiently small \(c_0\).
Net the pair of Grassmannians in their gap family at projection distance \(\theta=c_1\delta n^{-2}\), using at most \((C/\theta)^{2p^2}\) centers in the family.
At a center project each group only onto its own approximating subspace.
This preserves exactly the trace constraint at every good site, costs at most \(2\theta\) in the profile norm, and preserves each Gram lower bound up to the factor \(1-\theta^2\).
On \(G_n\) its image error is at most \(C n^2\theta\).
For a projected profile of image norm at most \(2\delta\), the random sum at each output \(k\ne a\) is \(\Psi t_k\) with \(\|t_k\|\le C n^2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz separately in each group.
This includes all the fixed group-diagonal contributions.
As \(|s|\ge1/2\), every good projected coordinate therefore lies within \(C\delta\) of the active subspace of that center.

In \(E_g\), split into the \(c\) small right singular directions in (2) and their orthogonal complement.
For each good coordinate the first component has bounded norm, and the second has norm at most \(C\delta/\tau\).
On erased sites cover a full ball in \(E_b\).
Per-coordinate mesh \(c_2\delta n^{-5/2}\) gives total profile error at most \(c_2\delta n^{-2}\), hence image error at most \(C c_2\delta\) on \(G_n\).
The product covering at this center costs at most
\[
 (C n^{5/2})^{2pn}\delta^{-c n-p|J|}.
\]
Discard net points with norm greater than two or a nonzero-span Gram lower bound below \(\gamma/2\).
Starting from norm at most one, all needed rounded points remain after this deletion since \(\|\Gamma(v)-\Gamma(w)\|\le(\|v\|+\|w\|)\|v-w\|\) in each group.
The deterministic net inclusion is taken only on \(G_n\), after which the fixed-profile probability bound is applied without conditioning on \(G_n\).
The net and the fixed-profile probabilities together leave at least
\[
 y((1-\kappa)n-2p-1)-cn-p|J|-2p^2
 \ \ge\ (1-p\kappa-p\eta_0)n-O_p(1)
\]
powers of \(\delta\).
Thus for a fixed sufficiently large constant \(J_0\) the conditional probability of this gap-family event intersected with \(G_n\) is at most
\[
 C^n n^{J_0 n}\gamma^{-J_0 n}\sigma^{-J_0 n}\delta^{n/2-J_0}.
 \tag{3}
\]
All constants are uniform in the admissible marks, the deleted output and \(s\in[1/2,3/2]\).

With \(\gamma=n^{-D}\), \(D\ge1\), take \(\delta(t)=t^{4J_0+4}n^{-4[J_0(1+D)+5]}\).
Set \(t_0=\sigma_0\le1\), \(t_{r+1}=n^{-4}\delta(t_r)\) for \(0\le r<p\).
There is at least one singular value of \(\Psi\) at or above \(t_0\) by (2), so one of the \(p\) gaps \([t_{r+1},t_r)\) contains no singular value.
The choices \(\sigma=t_r,\lambda=t_{r+1},\delta=\delta(t_r)\) meet the inactive bound.
Since \(n/2-J_0\ge n/4\) eventually, (3) is at most \(e^{-2n\log n}\) at each scale.
All these thresholds are inverse powers up to fixed constants, so one can choose a fixed integer \(e(D)\) with \(n^{-e(D)}\le\min_r\delta(t_r)\) eventually.
This controls every prescribed pair of ranks with both Gram lower bounds \(n^{-D}\), unioning over the finitely many gap families.

For an arbitrary unit profile diagonalize its two Gram operators separately and work down through their total rank \(d\le2p\).
Choose \(D_1=1,\ D_{j+1}\ge\max(D_j+1,2e(D_j)+10)\).
At dimension \(d\), if the smallest positive eigenvalue of the two Grams is below \(n^{-D_d}\), discard that one eigendirection in its own group.
The other Gram is unaffected.
The process stops with positive rank because the two initial traces sum to one and at most \(2p\) eigenvalues below \(n^{-1}\) can be discarded.
At stopping rank \(d\), the total projection error is at most \(\sqrt{2p}\,n^{-D_{d+1}/2}\) if anything has been discarded, so its image error on \(G_n\) is at most \(\tfrac12 n^{-e(D_d)}\) for large \(n\).
The retained profile has both Gram lower bounds \(n^{-D_d}\) on their actual spans.
Rank zero for either group costs no anchor or directions.
Taking \(E_*>\max_{1\le d\le2p} e(D_d)+1\) proves the asserted bound on unit profiles, after a union over ranks and rows and integration over admissible marks.

## The observed gradient and a fixed ordinary floor

At a value of \(s\) where \(T_s\) is invertible, put \(F_s=\det T_s\) and \(R=T_s^{-1}\).
For each row \(a\), the profile \(\Phi_i=\operatorname{diag}(R_{ai},R_{ia}^{\mathsf T})^*\) satisfies \(\mathcal A_a\Phi=0\) by the off-diagonal equations for the doubled inverse.
On good sites split \(\Phi_i\) into its \(\mathcal D\) and \(\mathcal D^\perp\) components.
Place the entire profile at erased sites into the first part, yielding \(\Phi=v+w\) with \(v\) in the tested domain and \(w\) supported on good sites in \(\mathcal D^\perp\).
The punctured-row bound gives \(\|v\|\le n^{E_*}\|\mathcal A_a v\|= n^{E_*}\|\mathcal A_a w\|\le C n^{E_*+2}\|w\|\).
Since \(\mathcal O\) is bounded below on its fixed orthogonal complement to the kernel, \(\|\Phi\|^2\le C n^{2E_*+4}\sum_{i\notin J}|\mathcal O(\Phi_i)|^2\).

For the raw coordinate of a strict upper group edge \(i<k\) with coefficient pair \((C_f,C_b)\), Jacobi's formula on this irreducible factor gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,\partial_\ell F_s/F_s
   =\operatorname{Tr}(R_{ki}C_f)+\operatorname{Tr}(R_{ik}C_b).
\]
This is exactly the trace of the appropriate doubled inverse block against \(D_\ell\).
At the opposite orientation the signed permutation of the whole list supplies the same set of derivatives up to signs.
The good edges are symmetric in their two endpoints, so summing row by row counts each square modulus of a retained derivative twice, and every doubled inverse square contributes twice in total to \(\|R\|_{\rm HS}^2\).
There is no diagonal derivative.
Thus on the frame and norm events, uniformly in this scalar drift, \(\|R\|\le C n^{E_*+5/2}\|\nabla_{\rm good} F_s\|/|F_s|\). [Paired Wigner observations](../audits/literature/Han26b/Yin_U001.md#wigner-edges)

For each \(n\), fix the marks and all atom values on the group diagonals and on bad group edges.
The good edges retain their independent bounded compact density components in each original whole raw atom.
Each real coordinate has determinant degree at most \(2d_0\) since it varies only two group blocks of this factor.
For a nonzero slice polynomial, the relative gradient estimate in [Han26b, Section 4.3 and Lemma 6.2](../audits/literature/Han26b/Yin_U001.md#determinant-gradient) gives
\[
 \Pr\{|F_s|\le t\|\nabla_{\rm good} F_s\|\mid\text{frozen data}\}
 \le C n^3 t^{1/2},\qquad 0<t\le1 .
\]
Indeed for a real univariate slice of degree at most \(b\), \(|f|\le v|f'|\) off its zeros forces its argument within \(bv\) of one of at most \(b\) roots.
On a compactly supported planar block apply this one-coordinate length estimate and integrate the other coordinate with the joint density, keeping the whole block independent of the others.
Union over \(O(n^2)\) real coordinates, each tested at \(v=O(nt)\), even gives \(C n^3 t\) for these compact components.
Nonzero polynomial slices have null zero sets under their joint densities.
The bound is applied on the whole conditional good-edge product law, before adding the exception probability for the frame.

To handle a slice that is identically zero at the requested drift, regard \(F_s\) as a monic polynomial in \(s\) for every fixed complete realization.
For Lebesgue-almost every \(s\in[1/2,3/2]\), \(F_s\ne0\) almost surely simultaneously at every \(n\) and every nonzero quotient factor, by Fubini.
For such an \(s\) the conditional polynomial is nonzero for almost all frozen data.
The fixed-profile estimates gave constants independent of \(s\), with an exceptional event allowed to depend on \(s\).
Combining the last two estimates gives constants \(K,C,c>0\), uniform in these \(s\), such that
\[
 \Pr\{s_{\min}(T_s)\le t\}\le C n^K t^{1/2}+Ce^{-cn}+C n^{-3}
 \qquad(0<t\le1)
 \tag{4}
\]
for each nonzero quotient factor.
If the relative radius exceeds one the enlarged first term suffices.
For fixed \(n,t\), take an admissible \(s\) with \(|1-s|<t\); the least singular value changes by at most \(t\).
Applying (4) at \(2t\), or the trivial bound for \(2t>1\), gives (4) at \(s=1\) with a changed constant.
No vanishing-slice assumption remains at that drift.

At \(s=1\) choose \(b_0\) with \(K-b_0/2<-2\).
Outside summable probabilities all quotient factors then have least singular value greater than \(n^{-b_0}\).
The strictly upper triangular part of \(T\) in the composition series has norm at most \(C n^2\) on \(G_n\).
Factoring out the block diagonal uses its inverse of norm at most \(n^{b_0}\) and a finite Neumann sum of length at most \(mh\), as in [Han26b, Section 5](../audits/literature/Han26b/Yin_U001.md#determinant-gradient).
Consequently a sufficiently large fixed power \(N^{-b_1}\) is a least-value floor for \(T\) and for \(L(W)\) outside summable probabilities along \(N=hn\), since the witness normalizer and its inverse are fixed.

## Removing the cutoff

At finite \(u\) the [finite-second-moment clipping argument](Yin_C_F0186_v40.md#determinant) gives almost-sure convergence of the \(N^{-b_1}\)-clipped logarithm to \(u\) under every coupling.
The floor makes this the ordinary logarithm eventually along \(hn\), by first Borel–Cantelli.
The fixed-clip upper bound also gives \(\limsup_N U_N\le u\) through every dimension, including at \(u=-\infty\).
The witness implication at finite \(u\) therefore proves the full dimension-limsup assertion while allowing a singular original drift.
For scalar polynomials apply the [finite and free word-chain determinant identities](Yin_C_F0186_v40.md#scalar), including the exceptional fixed shift \(P(0)\).
For nonconstant \(P\) the free logarithm is finite at every shift, and its linearization has an invertible constant term at each \(z\ne P(0)\).
At these shifts \(h=1\) gives full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic convergence.
For a fixed polynomial and coupling, Fubini gives almost-everywhere convergence in \(z\) on one probability-one event.
The [local planar square bounds and mass-one limit](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#noncompact-planar) then give the weak Brown limit there.
Constants in the regularity and small-ball tests may depend on the fixed shift, and the same component mass bound for \(h=1\) works throughout the nonexceptional shifts of a fixed word-chain size.
Constant polynomials give their deterministic eigenvalue and determinant measures directly.
