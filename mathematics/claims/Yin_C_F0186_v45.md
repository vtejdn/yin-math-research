# Affine Wigner determinants with a positive continuous component in each upper law

<a id="statement"></a>
## Conditional mixture hypothesis and conclusions

Use the fixed-law Hermitian Wigner model of the [matrix-limsup contract](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract).
For every color assume that its strict upper law has an absolutely continuous component of positive mass with respect to Lebesgue measure on the real line for a real symmetric color or on the plane for a complex Hermitian color.
The rest of each upper law and the real diagonal laws may be arbitrary subject to the centering, variance and independence conditions of the contract.
Only finite second moments are required.
For a fixed affine pencil the almost-sure dimension-limsup of its ordinary normalized log determinant equals the free logarithm under every permitted cross-size coupling.
At a finite target there is almost-sure convergence on the multiples of every size admitting a deterministic invertible evaluation on the declared input spaces.
An invertible constant term gives convergence through all dimensions, and a free logarithm of minus infinity also gives full-sequence convergence.
For every fixed scalar polynomial and every prescribed shift, the [ordinary scalar determinant limsup](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) equals the free logarithm almost surely.
For every fixed scalar polynomial, the [full-sequence eigenvalue measures](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract) converge weakly almost surely to the Brown measure.

Use [grouping at an invertible witness](Yin_C_F0186_v44.md#witness) of size \(h\), with \(n=N/h\) physical groups, and the [real doubled-block algebra](Yin_C_F0186_v44.md#algebra) of one of the nontrivial irreducible factors.
The argument below replaces the previous high component mass requirement in its punctured inverse-row estimate.
It supplies a polynomial floor for each fixed witness and does not give quantitative uniformity as any component mass tends to zero.

<a id="marks"></a>
## Uniform incidence for several disjoint groups of anchors

From each continuous component select a positive-mass compact bounded-density restriction.
Use independent marks for membership of whole atoms in these restrictions, representing the original laws without changing any one-size marginal.
A strict group edge is good when all its \(gh^2\) constituent marks are good.
Its probability \(\rho>0\) is fixed, and different strict group edges have independent marks.
Freeze the pattern of marks, leaving the raw whole atoms independent with their conditional laws.
The finite real block space \(V\), the trace kernel \(\mathcal D\), and the real multiplication maps \(H_\ell\) are those of the doubled-block calculation.
Write \(p=\dim_{\mathbb R}V\).
For a row \(a\) its good sites are its strict good neighbors and its other sites are erased.
At a good site the profile coordinate is restricted to \(\mathcal D\), while at an erased site it may range in \(V\).
Let \(\kappa=\rho/(4p)\) and choose a fixed integer \(K\) with \((1-\rho^{2p})^K<\kappa/2\).
Outside an event of probability \(Ce^{-cn}\), the marks have both following properties.
Each site has at least \(\rho n/2\) strict good neighbors.
For each family of \(K\) disjoint nonempty sets \(I_j\), each of size at most \(2p\), at most \(\kappa n\) sites outside their union have no \(I_j\) to which every edge is good.
For each fixed family the success trials for a single exterior site use disjoint groups of edges, and different exterior sites use disjoint trials.
Thus the failure probability per exterior site is below \(\kappa/2\).
Chernoff's inequality and a union over at most \(n^{2pK+O(1)}\) families and rows give the assertions for all sufficiently large \(n\).
In what follows the probability estimates conditional on any such admissible pattern are uniform.

For each \(s\in[1/2,3/2]\) use the [punctured row operator](Yin_C_F0186_v44.md#marks)
\[
 (\mathcal A_a v)_k=s v_k+\sum_{i,\ell} w_{ik\ell}H_\ell v_i,\qquad k\ne a .
\]
On the original norm event \(G_n\) in that argument, \(\|\mathcal A_a\|\le Cn^2\), and the sum of squares of the normalized coordinate coefficients at each output is at most \(Cn^4\).
The complement of \(G_n\) has probability \(O(n^{-3})\) on the original law.
All coverings below use this norm bound only in deterministic implications and do not condition the independent small-ball tests on \(G_n\).

<a id="robust"></a>
## Residual spans with several bases

Allow a set \(Z\) of at most \(S_0=1+4p^2K\) exceptional sites, including \(a\).
Outside \(Z\) fix a pair of subspaces \(E_g\subset\mathcal D\), \(E_b\subset V\) for the good and erased coordinates, with ranks \(d_g,d_b\), total rank \(d>0\).
In each of \(K\) disjoint anchor sets assume that the columns of each nonempty class form a basis of its corresponding \(E_j\) and that their Gram dominates \(lP_{E_j}\), where \(l=c_p\gamma^p n^{-p}\), \(\gamma=n^{-D}\) and \(D\ge1\) is fixed.
All these anchor sites lie outside \(Z\).
We first bound the probability that a profile of norm at most two with these properties has small image, for fixed ranks, \(Z\), anchor indices and their class assignments.
The estimates are uniform over these choices and include the choices of subspaces.

Use the map \(\Psi((x_\ell),(y_\ell))=\sum_\ell H_\ell(x_\ell+y_\ell)\) on copies of \(E_g,E_b\) and its active left space \(Y\) at singular values at least \(\sigma\).
The [finite-dimensional surplus](Yin_C_F0186_v44.md#frame) supplies fixed \(0<\tau<1,\sigma_0\le1\) such that for \(\sigma\le\sigma_0\) we have \(y=\dim Y\ge1\) and \(c_g\le y-1\).
Here \(c_j\) counts singular values at most \(\tau\) of \(P_{Y^\perp}|_{E_j}\) on its domain, including its kernel.
Also \(c_b\le y\), since \(P_Y\) is injective on this right singular subspace when \(\tau<1\).
No upper bound on the erased-site fraction is used.

Fix \(0<\lambda<\sigma\le\sigma_0\) and \(\delta\) with \(Cn^2\lambda\le\delta\) and \(\delta\le c l\).
For pairs having no singular values strictly between \(\lambda\) and \(\sigma\), net the pair of Grassmannians by centers within this family at projection mesh \(c\delta n^{-2}\), using at most \((Cn^2/\delta)^{C_p}\) centers.
The good space at every center stays inside \(\mathcal D\).
Project the residual coordinates onto the corresponding centers, keeping \(Z\) apart, and grid all coordinates of \(v_Z\) at mesh \(c\delta n^{-5/2}\).
The error in total norm is at most \(C c\delta n^{-2}\).
The projection preserves the Gram on each prescribed anchor set up to the multiplicative factor \(1-O(\delta^2n^{-4})\), by the least singular value of the projection between the two nearby subspaces.

For each fixed grid choice on \(Z\), expose all values on edges incident to \(Z\) and all group diagonals in \(Z\).
For \(k\notin Z\) the contribution \(b_k=\sum_{i\in Z,\ell}w_{ik\ell}H_\ell v_i^{\rm grid}\) is now fixed.
For each projected residual profile of image norm at most \(2\delta\) on \(G_n\), the remaining random sum at \(k\) lies within \(Cn^2\lambda\) of \(Y\) at that Grassmannian center.
Indeed it equals \(\Psi t_k\) with \(\|t_k\|\le Cn^2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz in the two classes.
It follows that \(\|P_{Y^\perp}(s v_k+b_k)\|\le C\delta\) for every residual coordinate.
In the right singular decomposition of \(P_{Y^\perp}|_{E_j}\), project this constraint to the left singular directions with values greater than \(\tau\).
The corresponding high singular component of \(v_k\) lies within \(C\delta/\tau\) of a fixed center depending only on \(b_k,s\) and the subspaces, while its remaining \(c_j\) coordinates lie in a fixed-radius ball.
This estimate also bounds a translated tube intersected with the norm-two ball when the center has large norm.
Per-coordinate mesh \(c\delta n^{-5/2}\) gives a residual net of cardinality at most
\[
 (Cn^{5/2})^{C_p n}\delta^{-c_g n_g-c_b n_b},
\]
where \(n_g,n_b\) count the two residual classes.
The residual centers depend only on the marks, the fixed Grassmannian center, the gridded exceptional coordinates and the exposed edges incident to \(Z\).
Together with the exceptional grid they have image error \(O(\delta)\) on \(G_n\).
Retain only centers of norm at most three and anchor Grams at least \(l/2\) on every prescribed class and set.
These include the required approximations because the Gram perturbation on each set is at most the sum of the two profile norms times the total rounding error.

For each resulting fixed center, omit \(Z\) and every anchor from the output set.
All but at most \(\kappa n\) of the remaining outputs have good edges to at least one whole anchor set.
For each successful output select one such set using only the marks.
Expose the other raw atoms, leaving just those selected anchor-to-output edges unexposed.
They avoid \(Z\), and their reverse occurrences belong to omitted outputs.
The retained outputs have disjoint independent sets of compact bounded-density inputs and fixed translations, conditionally on these exposures.
As in the [projected continuous atom estimate](Yin_C_F0186_v44.md#frame), the Euclidean Gram from unscaled real coordinates to \(Y\) on each retained output is at least \((l/2n)\sigma^2 I_Y\).
This follows by summing the anchor Grams on the two subspaces against each multiplier, with the signed coordinate permutation for the reversed orientation.
Therefore its probability of norm at most \(C\delta\) is at most \((C\delta\sqrt{n/l}/\sigma)^y\), by integration along the kernel in a bounded-dimensional compact set.
Multiply over the retained outputs and integrate these last exposures before taking the union over the residual net conditional on the exceptional edges.
The estimates are uniform over those exceptional values.

Ignoring a bounded number of sites, the net pays at most \(y(n_g+n_b)-n_g\) powers of \(1/\delta\), while the small balls gain at least \(y(n_g+n_b-\kappa n-O(1))\) powers of \(\delta\).
As \(n_g\ge\rho n/2-|Z|\), the difference is at least \(\rho n/4-O(1)\).
The exceptional grid and Grassmannian nets cost only bounded additional powers of \(n/\delta\).
Union over the bounded-size exceptional and anchor index sets costs only a further polynomial in \(n\).
Consequently for a fixed sufficiently large \(J\), the probability conditional on the marks of any such near-kernel profile on \(G_n\) is at most
\[
 C^n n^{Jn}\gamma^{-Jn}\sigma^{-Jn}\delta^{\rho n/4-J}. \tag{1}
\]
Here the near-kernel threshold may be \(\delta\), since all projection and rounding errors increase the fixed-profile threshold by only a constant factor.
There is no union over realizations of the exceptional edges.

For fixed \(D\) choose \(L>p+2\) with \(L\rho/8>J+4\), and put \(\delta(t)=c(t\gamma/n)^L\) for a sufficiently small fixed \(c>0\).
Let \(t_0=\sigma_0,\ t_{j+1}=n^{-3}\delta(t_j)\) for \(0\le j<p\).
At least one singular value of \(\Psi\) is at or above \(t_0\), so at least one of the \(p\) intervals \([t_{j+1},t_j)\) is empty.
Its endpoints give \(\lambda,\sigma\), and \(\delta=\delta(\sigma)\le c' l\) satisfies the required inequalities for large \(n\).
The estimate (1) at each such scale is at most \(e^{-2n\log n}\) for large \(n\).
Choose a fixed integer \(e(D)\ge1\) large enough that \(n^{-e(D)}\le\min_{j<p}\delta(t_j)/4\) eventually.
This bounds the probability that any residual profile of this class has image at most \(n^{-e(D)}\) on \(G_n\), after a finite sum over scales and ranks.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## The terminal profile supported on finitely many sites

Suppose a profile of norm between \(1/2\) and two is supported on \(Z\), where \(|Z|\le S_0\).
Grid those coordinates at mesh \(n^{-6}\) in the fixed space \(V\), ignoring the trace restrictions for this upper bound.
At each relevant grid point some \(z\in Z\) has \(\|v_z\|\ge1/(3\sqrt{S_0})\).
The common kernel of the finitely many \(H_\ell\) is trivial, so \(\max_\ell\|H_\ell v_z\|\ge c>0\).
Keep outputs on good edges from \(z\) outside \(Z\) and omit all other outputs, obtaining at least \(\rho n/2-S_0\) independent trials.
For this test expose everything except those particular edges, including all other edges incident to \(Z\).
In each trial a one-dimensional projection along one of the indicated images has Euclidean coefficient norm at least \(c n^{-1/2}\).
A whole good group edge has compact support and bounded joint density in its real coordinates, so the probability of a ball of radius \(2n^{-2}\) at any translation is at most \(C n^{-3/2}\).
On \(G_n\) the gridding image error is \(O(n^{-4})\).
Taking the product and a polynomial union over grid points, \(Z,z,a\) gives an \(e^{-c n\log n}\) bound on the near-kernel event with image at most \(n^{-2}\).

<a id="peel"></a>
## Extracting bases and removing a weak direction

Set \(e_0=2\).
Recursively for \(d=1,\ldots,2p\) choose fixed integers \(D_d\) strictly increasing with \(D_d/2>4+\max_{0\le j<d} e_j\), and set \(e_d=e(D_d)\) from (1).
Start with any unit profile in the required domain, put \(Z=\{a\}\), and let \(E_g,E_b\) be the actual spans of its remaining coordinates in each class.
At total rank \(d>0\), try to extract successively \(K\) disjoint unions of two bases.
For each new union first test the Gram on the not-yet-selected sites of each nonempty class against \(\gamma I_{E_j}\) on its current span \(E_j\), with \(\gamma=n^{-D_d}\).
If both tests pass, Cauchy–Binet gives \(d_j\) columns in each nonempty class whose Gram on \(E_j\) is at least \(c_p\gamma^p n^{-p} I_{E_j}\).
Indeed some squared minor is at least \(\gamma^{d_j}/\binom n{d_j}\), and all upper singular values are bounded by the total profile norm.
Set these columns aside and continue testing in the same unchanged subspaces.
If all \(K\) stages succeed, the current profile belongs to the class just estimated at rank \(d\).

If a test fails, retain all anchors set aside during this attempt as additional members of \(Z\), keeping their actual current profile values.
On the remaining sites of one failing class choose a unit Gram eigenvector with eigenvalue less than \(\gamma\) on the tested \(E_j\), allowing eigenvalue zero.
Project precisely those coordinates onto its orthogonal complement inside \(E_j\).
This changes the total profile in norm by at most \(n^{-D_d/2}\), preserves all trace restrictions, and lowers the total actual residual rank by at least one.
Recompute the actual remaining spans and start a new attempt at the resulting lower rank.
At most \(2p\) rank drops occur, and each adds at most \(2pK\) exceptional sites, as allowed in \(S_0\).
The process ends either at positive rank with \(K\) bases or at rank zero with only the exceptional support.
All profile changes occur at rank drops, and the final norm is at least \(1-2p n^{-D_1/2}>1/2\).
If the terminal rank is \(j\), the sum of all approximation errors is at most \(2p n^{-D_{j+1}/2}\) whenever there was any rank drop.
Its image on \(G_n\) is at most \(\tfrac14 n^{-e_j}\) for large \(n\).
Thus a unit vector with image at most \(n^{-E}\), for fixed \(E>1+\max_{0\le j\le2p}e_j\), produces a terminal vector with image at most \(n^{-e_j}\).
The measured rank, directions and exceptional sites may depend on the full random matrix, since all terminal events were bounded simultaneously by the preceding coverings and index unions.
After integrating over the marks and adding the exceptions for the marks and \(G_n\), all rows obey the required frame inequality outside original probability \(Ce^{-cn}+O(n^{-3})\).
The constants are uniform in the scalar drift \(s\) on the chosen interval for each fixed input law and normalized factor.

<a id="limits"></a>
## Returning to the original laws

Apply the [observed-gradient and drift-approximation argument](Yin_C_F0186_v44.md#floor) with this frame and the actual good component marks.
Conditional on the marks and values of the bad edges and group diagonals, good edges still have independent compact bounded-density whole atoms.
The bound for the relative gradient is applied to their full conditional product law before adding the frame exception.
Monicity in the scalar drift permits passage from almost every drift to the requested drift at fixed matrix size and singular-value radius.
The finite triangular reconstruction therefore yields a polynomial least-singular-value floor outside summable original-law probabilities along multiples of the witness size.
The [clipping, scalar linearization and planar arguments](Yin_C_F0186_v44.md#limits) give the three conclusions at the stated scopes.
An upper law with zero continuous mass in its declared coordinate space does not satisfy the positive-frequency input of the proof.
