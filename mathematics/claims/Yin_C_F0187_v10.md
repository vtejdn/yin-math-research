# Almost-sure physical spectral regularity for finite-variance two-channel pencils

This theorem extends the physical spectral and full-residual controls of [C187v4](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#statement) to the original real finite-second-moment laws.
Weighted resolvent traces have bounded row-replacement differences even when entries are unbounded.
A weighted truncation of each fresh row then retains its projected variance, and a polynomial support guard permits simultaneous auxiliary-direction nets with summable failures.
No fixed operator-norm bound is assumed.
The visible-residual comparison and full fourth-layer coercivity are separate unresolved steps in this larger model.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

Let \(W_1^{(N)},W_2^{(N)}\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
Their upper off-diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where each \(\xi_j\) is fixed, real, centered and has variance one.
Their diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where each \(\zeta_j\) is fixed, real, centered and has finite second moment.
All upper-triangular coordinates and both inputs are independent within each size.
There is no moment hypothesis above two, and the cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
Fix any deterministic real sequence \(\theta_N\to0\), and put
\[
 \alpha:=\sqrt2,\qquad
 M_{N,S}^{\theta}:=
 \begin{pmatrix}C_{N,S}+D_{N,S}&I_n\\
 I_n&C_{N,S}+\alpha D_{N,S}\end{pmatrix}
 +\theta_N I_{2n},\qquad n:=N-|S|,
\]
where \(C_{N,S},D_{N,S}\) are the input principal minors on \(S^c\), with the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Let \(\Pi_1,\Pi_2\) be the two auxiliary-channel projections, and \(P_i\) the rank-two physical coordinate projections.
Let \(\mu_1,\mu_2\) be the two diagonal spectral probability measures of
\[
 \ell:=\begin{pmatrix}s_1+s_2&1\\1&s_1+\sqrt2s_2\end{pmatrix}
\]
under the auxiliary conditional trace, for free variance-one semicircular \(s_1,s_2\).

On one probability-one event, for every bounded continuous real function \(f\),
\[
 \max_{\substack{|S|\le2\\a=1,2}}
 \left|\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_a f(M_{N,S}^{\theta})
                   -\int f\,d\mu_a\right|\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Weighted-weak-AS}
\]
In particular there are fixed \(c_0,\eta_0>0\) such that for every fixed \(0<\eta<\eta_0\), almost surely eventually,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\Pi_a
     \mathbf1_{[-\eta,\eta]}(M_{N,S}^{\theta})
 \ge c_0\eta n
 \quad(a=1,2,\ |S|\le2).
 \tag{Finite-variance-channel-mass}
\]

There is a fixed \(C<\infty\), depending only on the laws, such that for every fixed \(0<\eta<\eta_0\), almost surely eventually,
\[
 \max_{\substack{|S|\le1\\i\notin S}}
 \|P_i(M_{N,S}^{\theta}-i\eta)^{-1}P_i\|\le C,\qquad
 \max_{\substack{|S|\le1\\i\notin S}}
 \|P_i\mathbf1_{[-\eta,\eta]}(M_{N,S}^{\theta})P_i\|
 \le2C\eta.
 \tag{Finite-variance-physical-AS}
\]
The constants are independent of the fixed small \(\eta\); its eventual dimension may depend on \(\eta\).
Consequently the maximum physical mass of every real unit closest-to-zero eigenvector tends to zero almost surely, uniformly over \(|S|\le1\), including zero and repeated eigenvalues.

On each invertible minor with \(|S|\le1\), remove any real unit eigenmode \(Mw=\lambda w\), where \(M:=M_{N,S}^{\theta}\), and define
\[
 Z:=M^{-1},\qquad q:=\lambda^{-1},\qquad E_w:=Z-qww^T,
\]
\[
 d_i(E):=\|P_iE\|_{\rm F}^2,\qquad
 \mathcal D(E):=\sum_i\|P_iEP_i\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
Almost surely, uniformly over those invertible minors and all removed modes,
\[
 \|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2/n\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \frac{\max_i d_i(E_w)}{\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \frac{\mathcal D(E_w)}{\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Finite-variance-full-residual-AS}
\]
Uniform divergence means that the infimum of the displayed energy ratio tends to infinity; an empty set has infimum \(+\infty\), and the corresponding suprema of ratios are zero.
On the same invertible branches, with \(A_Z:=\|Z\|_{\rm F}\), one also has almost surely uniformly
\[
 A_Z^2/n\to\infty,\quad \|Z\|\to\infty,\quad
 \frac{\max_i d_i(Z)}{A_Z^2}\to0,\quad
 \frac{\mathcal D(Z)}{A_Z^2}\to0.
 \tag{Finite-variance-full-inverse-AS}
\]
There exists a deterministic choice \(0<\theta_N<N^{-10}\) for which every \(|S|\le2\) minor is invertible almost surely simultaneously over all \(N\ge3\).
For that choice the preceding conclusions apply without an invertibility exception.

These assertions do not supply a visible-residual comparison, an auxiliary Gram lower bound, full fourth-layer energy, a least-value floor or a determinant limit in the unbounded off-diagonal model.
The [original F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) and [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) conclusions remain unresolved in that range.

## Complete proof

<a id="finite-variance-weighted-resolvents"></a>
### Expected weighted resolvents by a fixed cutoff

For sufficiently large fixed \(R\), define
\[
 m_{j,R}:=\mathbb E[\xi_j\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_j|\le R\}}],\qquad
 v_{j,R}:=\operatorname{Var}(\xi_j\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_j|\le R\}}),
\]
\[
 \xi_{j,R}:=
 \frac{\xi_j\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_j|\le R\}}-m_{j,R}}{\sqrt{v_{j,R}}}.
\]
Then \(v_{j,R}\to1\), and \(\xi_{j,R}\to\xi_j\) in \(L^2\).
Let \(W_{j,R}^{(N)}\) use these coupled bounded coordinates and zero diagonals, and let \(M_{N,S}^{R}\) be its unshifted two-channel pencil.
Put \(e_R:=\sum_j\mathbb E|\xi_j-\xi_{j,R}|^2\to0\).
The squared Frobenius estimate for the full matrices is
\[
 \mathbb E\|M_{N,\varnothing}^{\theta}
                    -M_{N,\varnothing}^{R}\|_{\rm F}^2
 \le C N(e_R+\theta_N^2)+C\sum_j\mathbb E\zeta_j^2.
 \tag{Cutoff-Frobenius}
\]
It follows by summing the squared normalized entries and using the fixed two-channel coefficients.
Every principal deletion has no larger Frobenius difference.

For \(z\) in the upper half-plane, with \(y:=\operatorname{Im}z>0\), write
\[
 F_{N,S,a}(z):=\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}
                   \Pi_a(M_{N,S}^{\theta}-z)^{-1}.
\]
The resolvent identity and Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz give for equally sized Hermitian \(A,B\)
\[
 \frac1n|\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_a[(A-z)^{-1}-(B-z)^{-1}]|
 \le\frac{\|A-B\|_{\rm F}}{\sqrt n\,y^2}.
 \tag{Weighted-resolvent-HS}
\]
For every fixed \(R,z\), the bounded weighted expectation theorem gives convergence for \(M^R\) to
\[
 m_a(z):=\int(t-z)^{-1}\,d\mu_a(t),
\]
uniformly over the three possible deletion sizes.
The distribution within each deletion size is independent of its particular indices.
Taking expectations in (Weighted-resolvent-HS), using (Cutoff-Frobenius), then taking \(N\to\infty\) and \(R\to\infty\), proves
\[
 \max_{|S|\le2,a}|\mathbb EF_{N,S,a}(z)-m_a(z)|\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Weighted-resolvent-mean}
\]
No comparison of real inverses or fixed operator-norm event is used.

<a id="weighted-rank-concentration"></a>
### Rank-bounded replacement gives almost-sure weighted weak limits

Group the raw coordinates by their first upper-triangular physical index, with both inputs and diagonals in that group.
These \(N\) groups are independent.
Replacing one group changes \(M_{N,S}^{\theta}\) only in one physical block row and column, hence by rank at most four.
The resolvent difference has rank at most four by the resolvent identity, and norm at most \(2/y\) by the triangle inequality.
Consequently
\[
 |F_{N,S,a}(z)-F'_{N,S,a}(z)|\le\frac8{ny}.
 \tag{Weighted-rank-replacement}
\]
The trace estimate follows from trace norm and \(\|\Pi_a\|=1\), without any bound on the changed entries.
Apply the accepted bounded-differences inequality separately to real and imaginary parts.
For every fixed \(z,t>0\), a union over channels and \(|S|\le2\) gives, eventually,
\[
 \mathbb P\{\max_{S,a}
 |F_{N,S,a}(z)-\mathbb EF_{N,S,a}(z)|>t\}
 \le C N^2\exp(-cNy^2t^2).
 \tag{Weighted-resolvent-concentration}
\]
Thus, by first Borel--Cantelli and (Weighted-resolvent-mean), all these weighted Cauchy transforms converge uniformly over \(S,a\), almost surely, simultaneously on a countable dense set of upper-half-plane points.
Include all \(z=iT\) with positive integer \(T\).

Here are the tightness and uniqueness details needed for the uniform weak assertion.
The positive weighted spectral measures
\[
 \nu_{N,S,a}(E):=\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}
                      \Pi_a\mathbf1_E(M_{N,S}^{\theta})
\]
are probability measures.
For any such measure \(\nu\),
\[
 \nu(\{|t|>T\})\le
 2\left(1-\int\frac{T^2}{t^2+T^2}\,d\nu(t)\right).
\]
The integral is \(T\operatorname{Im}F(iT)\).
Uniform Cauchy convergence at each integer \(T\), followed by \(T\to\infty\), therefore gives uniform tightness, since each free \(\mu_a\) has mass one.
If uniform weak convergence failed for a bounded continuous test, choose offending minors and a fixed channel along a subsequence.
Tightness gives a weakly convergent further subsequence.
Its Cauchy transform equals \(m_a\) on the dense set and hence everywhere in the upper half-plane, by the bound
\[
 |(t-z)^{-1}-(t-w)^{-1}|
 \le |z-w|/(\operatorname{Im}z\,\operatorname{Im}w).
\]
These transforms determine the measure: their imaginary parts are Poisson transforms, and integration against a continuous compactly supported test followed by decreasing the imaginary part recovers its integral.
The subsequential limit is therefore \(\mu_a\), a contradiction.
This establishes (Weighted-weak-AS) on the stated one event for every bounded continuous test.

The fixed free channel-mass estimate gives
\(\mu_a([-\eta/2,\eta/2])\ge c\eta\) for every sufficiently small \(\eta>0\).
Use a continuous function between zero and one, equal to one there and supported in \((-\eta,\eta)\).
Uniform weighted weak convergence yields (Finite-variance-channel-mass), with one positive constant independent of \(\eta\).
Its validity is pathwise eventually at each fixed window; no summable rate of expectation convergence was needed.

<a id="finite-variance-projected-row"></a>
### Fixed truncation retains the projected fresh-row energy

Fix \(|S|\le1\), \(i\notin S\), and let \(n':=N-|S|-1\).
Condition only on the actual fresh-deleted minor \(M_{N,S\cup\{i\}}^\theta\), and let
\[
 P:=\mathbf1_{[-\eta,\eta]}(M_{N,S\cup\{i\}}^\theta).
\]
Assume its two channel masses are at least \(c_0\eta n'\).
The raw border \(\widehat B_i:=\sqrt N B_i\) uses the two original fresh off-diagonal rows, independently of this conditioning.
For a fixed unit \(u=(u_1,u_2)\in\mathbb C^2\), write
\[
 \widehat B_i u
 =\sum_{j=1}^{n'}(g_{1j}(u)\xi_{1j}+g_{2j}(u)\xi_{2j}),
\]
\[
 g_{1j}(u):=u_1e_{1j}+u_2e_{2j},\qquad
 g_{2j}(u):=u_1e_{1j}+\alpha u_2e_{2j},\qquad
 w_{aj}:=\|Pg_{aj}(u)\|^2.
\]
Here \(e_{aj}\) are the auxiliary physical coordinate vectors.
One has \(0\le w_{aj}\le W:=\max(1,\alpha^2)=2\).
The matrix
\[
 \Sigma:=\begin{pmatrix}2&1+\alpha\\1+\alpha&1+\alpha^2\end{pmatrix}
\]
has positive least eigenvalue \(\gamma\), because its determinant is \((\alpha-1)^2\).
Therefore
\[
 H_u:=\sum_{a,j}w_{aj}
 \ge\gamma\sum_{\ell=1}^2|u_\ell|^2
                       \operatorname{Tr}(P\Pi_\ell)
 \ge\gamma c_0\eta n'.
 \tag{Fresh-projected-energy}
\]

Choose one fixed cutoff \(R\), independent of \(\eta\), such that for both laws
\[
 p_a:=\mathbb P\{|\xi_a|\le R\}\ge\tfrac12,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi_a\mid|\xi_a|\le R)\ge\tfrac12.
\]
Such a choice follows from their fixed centered variance-one finite-second-moment hypotheses.
Let \(I_{aj}:=\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_{aj}|\le R\}}\) and \(p_*:=\min_a p_a\).
For the independent indicators, the inequality
\(1-e^{-w/W}\ge(1-e^{-1})w/W\), \(0\le w\le W\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\left(-W^{-1}\sum_{a,j}I_{aj}w_{aj}\right)
 \le \exp[-p_*(1-e^{-1})H_u/W].
\]
Exponential Markov consequently implies
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\sum_{a,j}I_{aj}w_{aj}<p_*H_u/2
                       \,\middle|\,M_{N,S\cup\{i\}}^\theta\right\}
 \le e^{-p_*(1-e^{-1}-1/2)H_u/W}
 \le e^{-c\eta N}.
 \tag{Weighted-cutoff-survival}
\]
This preserves projected energy rather than bounding the number of discarded coordinates by a window-dependent fraction.

Now reveal every indicator and every raw value at a discarded coordinate.
The remaining coordinates are independent conditional truncated laws, with support diameter at most \(2R\) and variance at least \(1/2\).
The discarded values and the conditional means produce an arbitrary fixed offset.
The function \(F:=\|P\widehat B_i u\|\) of the remaining real coordinates is convex and has Lipschitz constant at most \(\sqrt3\).
Indeed the raw map at each physical coordinate has squared Frobenius norm at most \(2|u_1|^2+3|u_2|^2\le3\), and projection is contractive.
On weighted survival,
\[
 \mathbb E[F^2\mid\text{revealed data}]
 \ge\tfrac12\sum_{a,j}I_{aj}w_{aj}\ge c\eta N.
\]
The accepted bounded-product concentration inequality gives
\(\mathbb E|F-\operatorname{med}F|^2\le C_R\).
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality then yields
\[
 \operatorname{med}F\ge
 (\mathbb EF^2)^{1/2}-\sqrt{C_R}\ge c'\sqrt{\eta N}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\), with threshold allowed to depend on fixed \(\eta\).
Applying the lower tail and integrating the revealed data proves
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|P\widehat B_i u\|<a\sqrt{\eta N}
                   \mid M_{N,S\cup\{i\}}^\theta\}
 \le C e^{-c\eta N}
 \tag{Finite-variance-fixed-direction}
\]
on its channel-mass event.
The constants \(a,c,C\) do not depend on the fixed small window.

<a id="finite-variance-physical-net"></a>
### A summable polynomial guard and the physical Schur bound

Let
\[
 \Omega_N:=
 \left\{\sum_{a=1}^2\sum_{r<s}|\xi_{a,rs}|^2\le N^4\right\}.
\]
Its complement has probability at most \(C/N^2\) by Markov, using only the off-diagonal second moments.
On \(\Omega_N\), every raw fresh border has norm at most \(C_\alpha N^2\), for all the deletions under consideration.
Choose a deterministic complex-unit-sphere net of mesh
\[
 h_N:=a\sqrt{\eta N}/(2C_\alpha N^2).
\]
Its cardinality is at most \(C\eta^{-2}N^6\).
On \(\Omega_N\), a lower bound \(a\sqrt{\eta N}\) at every net point gives half that lower bound at every unit \(u\).
Taking a union over the at most \(N^2\) pairs \((S,i)\) in (Finite-variance-fixed-direction), and adding the guard complement only once, gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{&\text{all required channel-mass inequalities hold, but}\\
            &\exists(S,i): B_i^TPB_i\not\succeq c_1\eta I_2\}\\
 &\le C N^{-2}+C\eta^{-2}N^8e^{-c\eta N}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Summable-physical-failure}
\]
The full raw-row guard is used only for deterministic net extension and is never inserted into the conditional distribution.
The right side is summable for every fixed \(\eta>0\).
The channel-mass event holds almost surely eventually by the weighted weak theorem.
First Borel--Cantelli therefore gives \(B_i^TPB_i\succeq c_1\eta I_2\) simultaneously for all these physical blocks, almost surely eventually.

The retained resolvent
\(R_i:=(M_{N,S\cup\{i\}}^\theta-i\eta)^{-1}\)
satisfies \(\operatorname{Im}R_i\succeq(2\eta)^{-1}P\).
Its physical Schur matrix is
\[
 S_i:=A_i^\theta-i\eta I_2-B_i^TR_iB_i.
\]
The real symmetric exposed block \(A_i^\theta\), including arbitrary realized diagonal values, has no imaginary part.
Hence \(-\operatorname{Im}S_i\succeq c_1 I_2/2\), which implies \(\|S_i^{-1}\|\le2/c_1\).
This is the desired physical block of \((M_{N,S}^\theta-i\eta)^{-1}\).
The scalar spectral inequality
\[
 \mathbf1_{[-\eta,\eta]}(M_{N,S}^\theta)
 \preceq2\eta\operatorname{Im}(M_{N,S}^\theta-i\eta)^{-1}
\]
then proves (Finite-variance-physical-AS).
Intersect the resulting events over one decreasing countable sequence of windows.
Their mass bounds put every closest eigenvalue in each fixed window eventually, and their physical projection bounds make the maximum mass of every corresponding unit vector tend to zero.
This proves the asserted almost-sure leverage statement, without eigenvalue simplicity.

<a id="finite-variance-residual-splitting"></a>
### Full residual estimates without a fixed norm guard

Work on the probability-one event just constructed.
At every fixed sufficiently small window \(h>0\), the channel mass gives at least \(chn\) eigenvalues in \([-h,h]\), uniformly over \(|S|\le1\).
For an invertible minor, deleting any one eigenmode loses at most one of those nonzero eigenvalues.
Thus
\[
 \|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2\ge(chn-1)/h^2.
 \tag{Residual-size-from-mass}
\]
Taking \(N\to\infty\) and then decreasing \(h\) through the countable windows gives uniform divergence relative to \(n\).
No bounded-norm basic size estimate is needed.

For a fixed such \(h\), split \(E_w=E_<+E_>\) at the actual low-energy projector.
The residual commutes with its actual minor, including removal of a vector in a repeated eigenspace.
The high part has norm at most \(h^{-1}\).
The physical projector bound and spectral orthogonality give
\[
 d_i(E_w)\le C h\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2+2h^{-2}.
\]
For the physical diagonal blocks,
\[
 \|P_iE_<P_i\|_{\rm F}^2
 \le\|P_iP_<\|^2\|E_<P_i\|_{\rm F}^2,
\]
whereas the high block has squared Frobenius norm at most \(2h^{-2}\).
Summing the two-term square bound gives
\[
 \mathcal D(E_w)\le C h\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2+4nh^{-2}.
\]
Divide by (Residual-size-from-mass)'s diverging normalized energy, first let \(N\to\infty\) at fixed \(h\), and then decrease \(h\).
Both ratios tend to zero uniformly over all invertible minors and removed modes, proving (Finite-variance-full-residual-AS).

For the full inverse, choose a closest mode.
Writing \(\eta_i(w):=\|P_iw\|^2\), spectral orthogonality gives
\[
 A_Z^2=q^2+\|E_w\|_{\rm F}^2,\qquad
 d_i(Z)=q^2\eta_i(w)+d_i(E_w),
\]
\[
 \mathcal D(Z)\le2\mathcal D(E_w)+2q^2\max_i\eta_i(w).
\]
The residual limits and closest-mode leverage imply all the normalized physical limits for \(Z\).
Also \(\|Z\|^2\ge A_Z^2/(2n)\), proving its divergence.
These statements use no visible-block comparison or auxiliary Gram estimate.

<a id="finite-variance-atom-corollary"></a>
### Consistent actual inverses by a deterministic shift

At each fixed \(N\), there are finitely many ordered eigenvalue laws of the unshifted minors with \(|S|\le2\).
Their negative atoms form a countable deterministic set.
Choose \(0<\theta_N<N^{-10}\) outside that set, exactly as in the fixed atom-avoidance construction.
Every required shifted minor is then invertible with probability one.
A countable union over dimensions makes this simultaneous for every prescribed cross-size coupling.
The chosen shift depends only on the marginal laws and tends to zero, so it is included in the theorem just proved.
This removes the invertibility exception from its soft spectral and inverse-energy conclusions, not from an unproved least-value estimate.

<a id="remaining-scope"></a>
## Remaining norm-sensitive geometry

The estimates concern full physical rows and diagonal blocks of the inverse residual.
They do not assert that its visible symmetric blocks carry a fixed fraction of that energy.
The old comparison uses the norms of the actual diagonal pencil blocks, which can be unbounded for finite-variance off-diagonal laws.
Likewise, no all-mode auxiliary Gram bound, joint approximate-input gap on growing center ranges, complete fourth-layer coefficient bound, intermediate logarithmic estimate or matrix least-value floor is proved here.
These are the remaining interfaces before the [finite-variance conditional bridge](Yin_C_F0194_v14.md#statement) could yield an original-model determinant conclusion.
The present proof retains the original laws and all dimensions rather than replacing them by bounded laws or a new Task contract.
No new external theorem or source-proof inspection is claimed.
The weighted-resolvent argument and the fresh-row weighted cutoff were developed by collaborating research agents.
The complete fixed statement and proof then received the [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v10.md#finite-variance-physical-regularity) selected below.
