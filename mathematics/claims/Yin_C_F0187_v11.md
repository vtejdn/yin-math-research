# Intermediate singular values for finite-variance two-channel pencils

This version extends the bounded-law middle estimate of [C187v9](Yin_C_F0187_v9.md#statement) to the original real finite-second-moment laws at a weaker, purely proportional singular-value scale.
A graph representation of part of the actual retained kernel replaces the bounded norm and thin-rectangle inputs.
The graph trace argument was developed through ordinary collaboration between the main development agent and its subagent.
The complete fixed statement and proof then received the [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v11.md#finite-variance-middle-reviewed) selected below.
No novelty claim is made.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

Let \(W_1^{(N)},W_2^{(N)}\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
For input \(a=1,2\), the upper off-diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\xi_a/\sqrt N\), where \(\xi_a\) is fixed, real, centered and has variance one.
The diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_a/\sqrt N\), where \(\zeta_a\) is fixed, real, centered and has finite second moment.
All upper-triangular coordinates and both inputs are independent within each size, and cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
Put
\[
 \alpha:=\sqrt2,\qquad X:=W_1^{(N)}+W_2^{(N)},\qquad
 Y:=W_1^{(N)}+\alpha W_2^{(N)},\qquad
 L_N:=\begin{pmatrix}X&I_N\\I_N&Y\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Singular values are listed in decreasing order.
Fix \(C_0>5\), and define the common event and its failure probability by
\[
 \Omega_N:=\{\|X\|_{\rm F}^2+\|Y\|_{\rm F}^2\le C_0N\},
 \qquad p_N:=\mathbb P(\Omega_N^c).
 \tag{Frobenius-guard}
\]
Then \(p_N\to0\).
There is a constant \(c>0\) such that, for every fixed \(D>0\), there are constants \(A_D,C_D>0\) for which, with
\[
 k_N:=\left\lceil A_D\sqrt{N\log N}\right\rceil,
\]
eventually
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\exists\,4k_N\le j\le2N-1:
 s_{2N-j}(L_N)<c(j/N)^{3/2}\right\}
 \le p_N+C_DN^{-D}.
 \tag{Finite-variance-middle}
\]
The common Frobenius exception is paid once.
No summable bound on \(p_N\), almost-sure middle estimate, or fixed operator-norm bound is asserted.

Write \(a_1\le\cdots\le a_{2N}\) for the singular values in increasing order and put \(r_N:=4k_N+2\).
There are constants \(C,t_0>0\) such that, outside the same exceptional event, simultaneously for \(0<t<t_0\),
\[
 \frac1{2N}\sum_{\substack{r\ge r_N\\a_r<t}}(-\log a_r)
 \le Ct^{2/3}(1+|\log t|).
 \tag{Finite-variance-middle-log}
\]
Let \(s_1,s_2\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\), and set
\[
 \ell:=\begin{pmatrix}s_1+s_2&1\\1&s_1+\sqrt2s_2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \tau_2:=\operatorname{tr}_2\otimes\tau.
\]
The singular law \(\nu_{|\ell|}\) satisfies
\(\nu_{|\ell|}([0,t])\le Ct^{2/3}\) for sufficiently small \(t>0\), and
\(\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell)\) is finite.
If, in addition, there are fixed constants \(c_*,A>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)<c_*N^{-A}\}\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{Additional-polynomial-floor}
\]
then
\[
 \frac1{2N}\log|\det L_N|
 \longrightarrow\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell)
 \quad\hbox{in probability}.
 \tag{Finite-variance-conditional-log}
\]
The additional polynomial floor is not proved here.
The conclusions concern this fixed two-channel pencil with coupling \(I_N\), not arbitrary matrix coefficients or the full [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) and [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) obligations.

## Fixed inputs and scope

The [physical block deletion](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#physical-block-distance) and [block negative-second-moment identity](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#block-negative-second-moment) are used only at their exact deterministic interfaces.
Their algebra is restated below at the present deletion counts; the positive coefficient-frame hypothesis and earlier mesoscopic cutoff are not used.
The probabilistic input is the accepted bounded-product convex concentration inequality (9) in [C187v2](Yin_C_F0187_v2.md#argument), for convex Lipschitz functions of independent bounded real coordinates.
Its recorded Götze--Naumov--Tikhomirov source-inspection boundary is retained; this argument does not audit the external theorem's proof.
The [weighted cutoff calculation in C187v10](Yin_C_F0187_v10.md#finite-variance-projected-row) supplies the same finite-variance mechanism.
Here its projected energy has size \(k^2/N\), and the projection is an actual kernel projection rather than a spectral-window projection.
The calculation below makes this changed interface explicit and assumes no channel-window mass theorem.
The limiting singular law follows from the accepted [finite-variance Hermitian matrix-polynomial weak convergence](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration).
No fixed norm or tall-rectangle estimate from the bounded-law middle proof is invoked.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Complete proof

<a id="finite-variance-frobenius"></a>
### One Frobenius event for all retained minors

For independent copies of the two off-diagonal laws, define
\[
 H:=(\xi_1+\xi_2)^2+(\xi_1+\alpha\xi_2)^2.
\]
This is integrable and \(\mathbb EH=5\).
The off-diagonal contribution to
\((\|X\|_{\rm F}^2+\|Y\|_{\rm F}^2)/N\) is
\[
 \frac2{N^2}\sum_{1\le r<s\le N}H_{rs},
\]
where the \(H_{rs}\) are independent copies of \(H\) within each size.
The finite-mean weak law gives convergence in probability to \(5\).
For completeness, this weak law follows by truncating \(H\) at a fixed level, applying the bounded-variable variance estimate, and bounding the average discarded part by Markov; the fixed truncation is then sent to infinity.
The normalized diagonal contribution has expectation
\[
 \frac1N\,\mathbb E\bigl[(\zeta_1+\zeta_2)^2+
                              (\zeta_1+\alpha\zeta_2)^2\bigr]\longrightarrow0.
\]
It converges to zero in probability by Markov.
Thus \(p_N\to0\), and every principal minor has squared Frobenius norm no larger than that of the parent input combination.
No assertion about independence across sizes is needed.

<a id="kernel-graph-trace"></a>
### A deterministic trace bound for the actual kernel

Fix \(1\le k\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), and define
\[
 T_k:=\{N-k+1,\ldots,N\},\qquad
 V_i:=[N]\setminus\{i\},\qquad U_{i,k}:=V_i\setminus T_k
\]
for a retained index \(i\notin T_k\).
For this subsection write \(V:=V_i\), \(U:=U_{i,k}\), \(T:=T_k\), and \(n:=|V|=N-1\).
Let \(H_i\) be the span of the rows with physical indices in \(U\) of
\[
 M_i:=\begin{pmatrix}X[V,V]&I_n\\I_n&Y[V,V]\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal K:=H_i^\perp\), and let \(P\) be its real orthogonal projection in \(\mathbb R^V\oplus\mathbb R^V\).
There are at most \(2(n-k)\) spanning rows, so \(\dim\mathcal K\ge2k\).
Every \(z=(u,v)\in\mathcal K\) satisfies
\[
 u_U+Y[U,V]v=0,\qquad v_U+X[U,V]u=0.
 \tag{Actual-kernel-equations}
\]
Let \(\Pi_1,\Pi_2\) denote the two channel projections on this space.

Imposing \(v_T=0\) removes at most \(k\) dimensions.
Choose a \(k\)-dimensional subspace
\(\mathcal K_1\subset\mathcal K\cap\{v_T=0\}\).
Define \(A:\mathbb R^V\to\mathbb R^V\) to be \(X[U,V]\) followed by zero extension from \(U\) to \(V\).
The second equation in (Actual-kernel-equations) gives \(v=-Au\) on \(\mathcal K_1\).
Consequently the projection of \(\mathcal K_1\) to its first channel is injective and has a \(k\)-dimensional image \(F\).
For an isometry \(C:\mathbb R^k\to F\), an orthonormal basis matrix of \(\mathcal K_1\) is
\[
 Q:=\binom{C}{-AC}(I_k+C^TA^TAC)^{-1/2}.
\]
The orthogonal projection \(P_1:=QQ^T\) satisfies \(P_1\preceq P\).
Taking the first-channel trace and using Cauchy--Schwarz on the positive eigenvalues of \(I_k+C^TA^TAC\) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Tr}(P\Pi_1)
 &\ge\operatorname{Tr}(P_1\Pi_1)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(I_k+C^TA^TAC)^{-1}\\
 &\ge\frac{k^2}{k+\operatorname{Tr}(C^TA^TAC)}
 \ge\frac{k^2}{k+\|X[V,V]\|_{\rm F}^2}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{First-channel-graph-trace}
\]
For the last inequality, \(CC^T\) is an orthogonal projection, so
\(\|AC\|_{\rm F}^2\le\|A\|_{\rm F}^2\le\|X[V,V]\|_{\rm F}^2\).
Interchanging the channels and instead imposing \(u_T=0\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(P\Pi_2)
 \ge\frac{k^2}{k+\|Y[V,V]\|_{\rm F}^2}.
 \tag{Second-channel-graph-trace}
\]
The two lower bounds may use different subspaces of \(\mathcal K\).
They are trace bounds, not a claim that every kernel vector has a fixed fraction of its norm in each channel.
On the minor-measurable condition
\[
 \|X[V,V]\|_{\rm F}^2+\|Y[V,V]\|_{\rm F}^2\le C_0N,
 \tag{Minor-Frobenius-guard}
\]
they imply
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(P\Pi_a)\ge c_1 k^2/N,\qquad a=1,2,
 \quad c_1:=(C_0+1)^{-1}.
 \tag{Kernel-trace-energy}
\]
No invertibility, full row rank, rectangle estimate or operator-norm bound has been assumed.

<a id="finite-variance-kernel-row"></a>
### Fixed truncation and fresh-row concentration at energy \(k^2/N\)

Retain the first \(N-k\) physical row blocks of \(\sqrt N L_N\), and call the resulting matrix \(\mathsf B\).
For a retained physical index \(i\), delete the two columns of index \(i\) from the current row block and every other row block.
The other projected rows have span \(H_i\) just defined; their common factor \(\sqrt N\) does not alter the span.
Condition on both input principal minors on \(V_i\).
This fixes \(P\) and (Minor-Frobenius-guard), and leaves the \(2(N-1)\) original fresh off-diagonal coordinates independent with their original fixed laws.
Every mirrored occurrence of a fresh entry has disappeared with the deleted column.
The constant auxiliary identity and the current diagonal entries also disappear from the projected current block.

Call the resulting raw current block \(F_i\).
For a fixed real unit vector \(a=(a_1,a_2)\), write
\[
 F_i^Ta=\sum_{j\in V_i}
      \bigl(g_{1j}(a)\xi_{1j}+g_{2j}(a)\xi_{2j}\bigr),
\]
\[
 g_{1j}(a):=a_1e_{1j}+a_2e_{2j},\qquad
 g_{2j}(a):=a_1e_{1j}+\alpha a_2e_{2j},\qquad
 w_{bj}:=\|Pg_{bj}(a)\|^2.
\]
Here \(e_{bj}\) are the channel-coordinate vectors.
One has \(0\le w_{bj}\le2\).
Define
\[
 \Sigma:=\begin{pmatrix}2&1+\alpha\\1+\alpha&3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad\gamma:=\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)>0,\qquad
 H_a:=\sum_{b,j}w_{bj}.
\]
The determinant of \(\Sigma\) is \((\alpha-1)^2>0\).
The per-coordinate covariance matrix
\(\operatorname{diag}(a_1,a_2)\Sigma\operatorname{diag}(a_1,a_2)\)
is at least \(\gamma\operatorname{diag}(a_1^2,a_2^2)\).
Thus, on (Minor-Frobenius-guard),
\[
 H_a\ge\gamma\sum_{b=1}^2 a_b^2\operatorname{Tr}(P\Pi_b)
       \ge\gamma c_1 k^2/N.
 \tag{Kernel-projected-variance}
\]

Choose one fixed cutoff \(R\) such that, for both laws,
\[
 p_b:=\mathbb P\{|\xi_b|\le R\}\ge\tfrac12,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi_b\mid|\xi_b|\le R)\ge\tfrac12.
\]
Such a choice follows from the fixed centered variance-one finite-second-moment hypotheses.
Put \(p_*:=\min_b p_b\) and \(I_{bj}:=\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_{bj}|\le R\}}\).
These indicators are independent, conditionally on the two minors.
For \(0\le w\le2\), the inequality
\(1-e^{-w/2}\ge(1-e^{-1})w/2\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\left(-\tfrac12\sum_{b,j}I_{bj}w_{bj}\right)
 \le \exp[-p_*(1-e^{-1})H_a/2].
\]
Exponential Markov implies
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\sum_{b,j}I_{bj}w_{bj}<p_*H_a/2
                      \,\middle|\,\text{two minors}\right\}
 \le \exp[-p_*(1-e^{-1}-1/2)H_a/2].
 \tag{Kernel-weighted-survival}
\]
The cutoff is independent of \(N,k,P,a\); no bound on the count of discarded coordinates relative to \(k^2/N\) is used.

Reveal the entire indicator pattern and every discarded raw coordinate.
The remaining coordinates are independent conditional truncated laws, with variance at least \(1/2\) and support diameter at most \(2R\).
The discarded values and conditional means contribute an arbitrary fixed offset.
As a function of these remaining real coordinates,
\[
 Z_a:=\|PF_i^Ta\|
\]
is convex and has Lipschitz constant at most \(\sqrt3\).
Indeed the squared Frobenius norm of the raw two-input map at one physical coordinate is \(2a_1^2+3a_2^2\le3\), and \(P\) is contractive.
On weighted survival,
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_a^2\mid\text{revealed data}]
 \ge\tfrac12\sum_{b,j}I_{bj}w_{bj}\ge p_*H_a/4.
\]
The accepted bounded-product convex concentration inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{|Z_a-\operatorname{med}Z_a|>t
                        \mid\text{revealed data}\}
 \le C\exp(-ct^2/R^2).
\]
Integration yields
\(\mathbb E|Z_a-\operatorname{med}Z_a|^2\le C_R\).
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{med}Z_a
 \ge(\mathbb E Z_a^2)^{1/2}-\sqrt{C_R}
 \ge c_2k/\sqrt N
\]
once \(k^2/N\) exceeds a fixed constant.
Applying the lower concentration tail and then integrating the revealed data, including (Kernel-weighted-survival), proves
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|PF_i^Ta\|<c_3k/\sqrt N
                      \mid\text{two minors}\}
 \le C\exp(-ck^2/N)
 \tag{Finite-variance-kernel-direction}
\]
on (Minor-Frobenius-guard).
All constants are uniform over \(i,k,a\) and the actual conditioned minors.

<a id="finite-variance-kernel-net"></a>
### Auxiliary directions and the common exception

Take a deterministic \(N^{-2}\)-net of the real unit circle, with at most \(CN^4\) points.
On \(\Omega_N\), every raw projected current block satisfies
\[
 \|F_i\|^2\le\|F_i\|_{\rm F}^2
 =N\sum_{j\ne i}(X_{ij}^2+Y_{ij}^2)
 \le C_0N^2,
 \qquad \|F_i\|\le\sqrt{C_0}\,N.
 \tag{Raw-border-Frobenius}
\]
The deterministic net-extension error is therefore at most \(\sqrt{C_0}/N\).
For \(k\ge k_N\), this is eventually at most \(c_3k/(2\sqrt N)\).
Every minor condition (Minor-Frobenius-guard) also holds on \(\Omega_N\).
The full event \(\Omega_N\) is never inserted into the conditional fresh-row distribution.
Rather, for each pair \((i,k)\), the conditional estimate is integrated only on its minor-measurable guard, and \(\Omega_N\) is used afterward for deterministic extension.

Let \(H_i^{\rm full}\) be the span of all other rows of \(\mathsf B\), identified with their transposes, and define the full row-distance Gram
\[
 G_i:=\mathsf B_iP_{(H_i^{\rm full})^\perp}\mathsf B_i^T.
\]
Coordinate contraction under deletion of the two columns gives, for every real \(a\),
\[
 a^TG_i a
 =\operatorname{dist}(\mathsf B_i^Ta,H_i^{\rm full})^2
 \ge\|PF_i^Ta\|^2.
\]
A union over the net and all at most \(N^2\) pairs \((i,k)\) yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{\Omega_N\ \hbox{and some }&
 k_N\le k\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\ i\notin T_k:\\
 &G_i\not\succeq c_4k^2N^{-1}I_2\}
 \le CN^6\exp(-ck_N^2/N).
 \end{split}
 \tag{Finite-variance-Grams}
\]
For sufficiently large \(A_D\), the right side is at most \(C_DN^{-D}\).
Adding \(p_N\) once gives the single event used in the middle estimate.

<a id="finite-variance-middle-indices"></a>
### Full row rank, the block identity and the index count

On the preceding good event, every retained matrix \(\mathsf B\) has full row rank.
Indeed a nonzero row relation would give a nonzero combination from one physical row block in the span of the others, contradicting \(G_i\succ0\).
For its positive definite row Gram, the block Schur formula gives
\[
 ((\mathsf B\mathsf B^T)^{-1})_{ii}=G_i^{-1}.
\]
Taking the trace gives
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^{2(N-k)}s_r(\mathsf B)^{-2}
 =\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\operatorname{Tr}G_i^{-1}
 \le C N^2/k^2.
 \tag{Finite-variance-negative-second-moment}
\]
Fix \(4k_N\le j\le2N-1\), and put \(k:=\lfloor j/4\rfloor\).
Eventually
\[
 k_N\le k\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\qquad
 k\ge j/8,\qquad j-2k+1\ge j/2.
\]
The index \(2N-j\) belongs to the singular list of \(\mathsf B\).
At least \(j-2k+1\) entries in that list are no larger than \(s_{2N-j}(\mathsf B)\), whence
\[
 (j-2k+1)s_{2N-j}(\mathsf B)^{-2}\le C N^2/k^2.
\]
Row-deletion interlacing and the raw factor \(\sqrt N\) imply
\[
 s_{2N-j}(L_N)
 \ge N^{-1/2}s_{2N-j}(\mathsf B)
 \ge c\frac{\sqrt j\,k}{N^{3/2}}
 \ge c'(j/N)^{3/2}.
\]
This proves (Finite-variance-middle).
In particular its profile has no additional \(N\)-dependent prefactor.

<a id="finite-variance-middle-log"></a>
### Logarithmic consequences under the separate floor

On the same good event, after decreasing a fixed constant if necessary,
\[
 a_r\ge c_5\left(\frac r{2N}\right)^{3/2},
 \qquad r\ge r_N.
\]
For small fixed \(t>0\), an index in this range with \(a_r<t\) must satisfy
\(r\le2N(t/c_5)^{2/3}\).
Summing
\(-\log c_5+\tfrac32\log(2N/r)\) up to that upper bound and using the elementary integral comparison for the decreasing logarithm gives (Finite-variance-middle-log).
If that upper bound is below \(r_N\), the relevant sum is empty.
The same count gives
\[
 \frac1{2N}\#\{r:a_r<t\}
 \le\frac{r_N}{2N}+C t^{2/3}.
 \tag{Finite-variance-small-mass}
\]

The accepted weak spectral convergence applied to \(L_N\), followed by the continuous absolute-value map, gives convergence of its singular laws to \(\nu_{|\ell|}\).
At a continuity point \(t>0\) of the limiting singular law, the left side of (Finite-variance-small-mass) converges in probability to \(\nu_{|\ell|}([0,t])\).
Since the good event has probability tending to one and \(r_N/N\to0\), the limit is at most \(Ct^{2/3}\).
Decreasing continuity points give the same bound for every sufficiently small \(t>0\).
Consequently the free singular law has no atom at zero and has integrable negative logarithm.
Its support is bounded because \(\ell\) is a fixed polynomial in bounded semicircular elements.
Thus \(\log\Delta_{\tau_2}(\ell)\) is finite.

Assume now (Additional-polynomial-floor).
On its good event, the omitted bottom indices have total negative-logarithmic contribution at most
\[
 \frac{r_N}{2N}\bigl(A\log N+|\log c_*|\bigr)
 =O\!\left(N^{-1/2}(\log N)^{3/2}\right)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Finite-variance-bottom-log}
\]
Together with (Finite-variance-middle-log), this proves negative-logarithmic uniform integrability in probability in the required iterated limit.

Positive logarithms are controlled on the same Frobenius event:
\[
 \frac1{2N}\sum_{r=1}^{2N}a_r^2
 =\frac{\|L_N\|_{\rm F}^2}{2N}
 =\frac{\|X\|_{\rm F}^2+\|Y\|_{\rm F}^2+2N}{2N}
 \le(C_0+2)/2.
\]
Hence, for \(T>1\),
\[
 \frac1{2N}\sum_{a_r>T}\log a_r
 \le\frac{C_0+2}{2}
       \sup_{x>T}\frac{\log x}{x^2}\longrightarrow0
 \quad(T\to\infty).
 \tag{Finite-variance-positive-log}
\]
For fixed \(0<t<1<T\), the function
\(x\mapsto\log(\min(T,\max(t,x)))\) is bounded and continuous on \([0,\infty)\).
Weak convergence gives convergence of its integrals.
The last three logarithmic estimates allow \(t\downarrow0\) and \(T\uparrow\infty\), outside events of probability tending to zero, and prove (Finite-variance-conditional-log).
This deduction uses only in-probability control of the common Frobenius event and does not upgrade the middle estimate to an almost-sure statement.
