# Auxiliary Gram separation for finite-variance low-energy two-channel modes

A bounded compression of both original inputs gives a joint residual gap sufficient for low-energy auxiliary Gram separation.
The proof also excludes collapse of either auxiliary component, using independent bounded coordinates after conditioning on a fixed-cutoff tail.
The complete fixed proof has [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v13.md#finite-variance-low-mode-gram-reviewed), selected by the proved assessment below.
It does not assert a least singular-value floor or novelty.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

Let \(C_N,D_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
For input \(a=1,2\), the upper off-diagonal coordinates are independent copies of \(\xi_a/\sqrt N\), where \(\xi_a\) is fixed, real, centered and has variance one.
The diagonal coordinates are independent copies of \(\zeta_a/\sqrt N\), where \(\zeta_a\) is fixed, real, centered and has finite second moment.
All upper-triangular coordinates and both inputs are independent within each size, and cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
For \(|S|\le1\), the matrices \(C_{N,S},D_{N,S}\) are their corresponding principal minors with the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
For the full or single-deletion input minors write \(C:=C_{N,S}\), \(D:=D_{N,S}\), \(n:=N-|S|\), and
\[
 X:=C+D,\qquad Y:=C+\alpha D,\qquad
 M:=\begin{pmatrix}X&I_n\\I_n&Y\end{pmatrix},\qquad \alpha:=\sqrt2.
\]
For every fixed \(0\le K_0<\infty\), there is a fixed \(g>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\exists\,|S|\le1,\ |\lambda|\le K_0,\ w=(u,v):
 \|w\|=1,\ Mw=\lambda w,\
 \lambda_{\min}\begin{pmatrix}\|u\|^2&\langle u,v\rangle\\
                         \langle u,v\rangle&\|v\|^2\end{pmatrix}<g
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Candidate-low-mode-Gram}
\]
Every vector and matrix here is real.
The claim includes all unit vectors in repeated low-energy eigenspaces, with no invertibility assumption.


For the specific compression \(Q\) constructed in [the proof](#bounded-compression), one also has the following intermediate conclusion.
For every fixed \(R_*>0\), there is a fixed \(\epsilon>0\) such that, with probability tending to one, simultaneously for all \(|S|\le1\),
\[
 \inf_{\substack{z\in\mathbb R^n,\ \|z\|=1\\a,b\in[-R_*,R_*]}}
 \max\{\|Q(C-aI_n)z\|,\|Q(D-bI_n)z\|\}\ge\epsilon.
\]
The compression depends on the first input and the exposed second-input tail, not on its remaining bounded coordinates.
Each stated conclusion has failure probability at most a common \(p_N=o(1)\) plus \(Ce^{-cN}\), with fixed constants allowed to depend on the chosen energy or center range.
The common tail and first-input spectral-count failures are included in \(p_N\) only once.
No almost-sure assertion, growing-energy-range estimate, full fourth-layer lower bound or determinant floor follows here.

## Complete proof

<a id="conditional-small-subspaces"></a>
### Conditional coordinates and the entropy budget

Use a fixed raw cutoff \(L\) for the second input as in C187v12, and write \(D_N=T_N+W_N\).
The tail data consist of every cutoff indicator, all bad raw values and all diagonal entries.
Conditional on these data, \(T_N\) is fixed, and the nonzero raw upper coordinates of \(W_N\) are independent, centered, supported in intervals of diameter at most \(2L\), and have conditional variance at least \(1/2\).
The bad coordinates and diagonal coordinates of \(W_N\) are zero.
Choose \(L\) so that the full bad-edge graph has maximum degree at most \(\delta N\), with \(\delta:=1/32\), outside \(Ce^{-cN}\).
The exponential Markov calculation in C187v12 applies unchanged.
A fixed \(C_T<\infty\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|T_N\|_{\rm F}^2>C_TN\}\longrightarrow0
 \tag{Gram-common-tail}
\]
by the finite-mean weak law for its squared raw entries and Markov for its finite-second-moment diagonals.

The conditional quadratic-form replacement sum is at most \(8L^2/N\).
The accepted bounded-differences inequality and a \(1/4\)-net of size at most \(9^N\) therefore give a fixed \(B_L\ge1\) with
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|W_N\|>B_L\mid\text{tail data}\}\le Ce^{-cN}.
\]
This holds for the actual missing-edge conditional matrix, without an identical-distribution assumption.

We will use the following consequence for projections chosen after conditioning on the full first input and the tail data.
For any unit \(z\in\mathbb R^n\), the raw off-diagonal vectors
\(g_{ij}:=z_je_i+z_ie_j\) satisfy
\[
 \sum_{i<j}g_{ij}g_{ij}^T
 =I_n+zz^T-2\operatorname{diag}(z_i^2).
\]
For every such fixed orthogonal projection \(P\),
\[
 \mathbb E[\|PWz\|^2\mid\text{conditioned data}]
 \ge\frac{\operatorname{rank}P-2-\delta N}{2N}.
 \tag{Gram-projected-variance}
\]
Indeed the full projected squared sum is at least \(\operatorname{rank}P-2\), and deleting bad edges costs at most
\(\sum_i\deg_{\rm bad}(i)z_i^2\le\delta N\).
The remaining conditional variances are at least \(1/2\).
If the codimension of \(P\) is a sufficiently small fixed fraction of \(n\), the right side is at least \(1/4\) eventually.
The function \(\|PWz\|\) is convex and \(\sqrt{2/N}\)-Lipschitz in the bounded raw coordinates.
The accepted bounded-product concentration and its integrated median error give fixed \(\gamma,c_L>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|PWz\|<\gamma\mid\text{conditioned data}\}
 \le Ce^{-c_LN}.
\]
One may take \(\gamma:=1/4\).
Choose \(\theta:=\min(1/2,\gamma/(8B_L))\), and then choose \(0<\kappa\le1/64\) with
\[
 \kappa\log(3/\theta)<c_L/4.
\]
For any conditioned subspace \(V\) of dimension at most \(\kappa n\), a fixed sphere net and the event \(\|W_N\|\le B_L\) imply
\[
 \inf_{\substack{z\in V\\\|z\|=1}}\|PWz\|\ge\gamma/2
 \tag{Gram-small-subspace}
\]
outside conditional probability \(Ce^{-cN}\), provided \(P\) has the stated small codimension.
The subspace and projection may both depend on the first input and tail data, but not on the remaining good coordinates.
Empty unit spheres cause no exception.

<a id="bounded-compression"></a>
### A bounded compression with arbitrarily small discarded dimension

Choose a fixed \(K>2\).
The first-input semicircle weak law and principal interlacing give, with probability tending to one, at most \(\kappa N/16\) eigenvalues of every \(C_{N,S}\) outside \([-K,K]\).
After fixing \(L,B_L,\kappa\), choose a further fixed \(H>0\) so large that
\[
 C_T/H^2\le\kappa/16.
\]
Let \(Q\) be the orthogonal projection onto
\[
 \operatorname{Ran}\mathbf1_{[-K,K]}(C)
 \cap\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf1_{[-H,H]}(T).
\]
On the common tail and first-input events, eventually
\[
 q:=\operatorname{codim}Q\le\kappa N/8\le\kappa n/4,
 \qquad \|QC\|\le K,\qquad \|QT\|\le H.
 \tag{Gram-compressed-inputs}
\]
For example \(Q=Q\mathbf1_{[-K,K]}(C)\), which proves the first operator bound even though the two spectral projections need not commute.
On \(\|W_N\|\le B_L\),
\[
 \|QD\|\le H+B_L,\qquad
 \|QX\|\le L_X:=K+H+B_L,\qquad
 \|QY\|\le L_Y:=K+\alpha(H+B_L).
\]
The additional threshold \(H\) is essential to this parameter order.
Fixing it equal to one would require the tail Frobenius density to be smaller than the cutoff-dependent entropy budget \(\kappa\), a comparison that finite variance alone does not supply.

<a id="component-lower-bound"></a>
### Exclusion of one-channel collapse

Put \(V_0:=\ker Q\).
Let \(P\) project onto
\[
 \operatorname{Ran}Q\cap(V_0+CV_0+TV_0)^\perp.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{codim}P\le4q\le\kappa n\), and
\[
 PV_0=PCV_0=PTV_0=0,\qquad
 P(X-\lambda I_n)|_{V_0}=PW|_{V_0},\qquad
 P(Y-\lambda I_n)|_{V_0}=\alpha PW|_{V_0}.
\]
The subspace \(V_0\), this projection and the coefficients are fixed under the permitted conditioning.
Thus (Gram-small-subspace) applies simultaneously to this one subspace for each minor.

Set \(A_0:=1+K_0+\max(L_X,L_Y)\).
Suppose \(Mw=\lambda w\), \(\|w\|=1\), \(|\lambda|\le K_0\), and \(\|u\|\le b\), where \(b>0\) is sufficiently small.
The first block equation gives
\[
 \|Qv\|\le A_0\|u\|.
\]
For \(b\le1/2\), one has \(\|v\|\ge1/2\).
Therefore \(z:=v/\|v\|\) is within \(2A_0b\) of \(V_0\).
If \(b<1/(8A_0)\), its normalized projection \(z'\) onto \(V_0\) exists and satisfies
\(\|z-z'\|\le4A_0b\).
If \(V_0=\{0\}\), the distance estimate is already impossible.
The second block equation and \(\|P(Y-\lambda I_n)\|\le A_0\) yield
\[
 \|P(Y-\lambda I_n)z'\|
 \le2b+4A_0^2b.
\]
On the other hand this norm is
\(\alpha\|PWz'\|\ge\alpha\gamma/2\).
A sufficiently small fixed \(b\) gives a contradiction.
Interchanging \(u,v\) uses the other block equation and the coefficient one of \(W\) in \(X\), giving the same conclusion for \(\|v\|\).
For example choose
\[
 b_*:=\min\left(\frac14,\frac1{16A_0},
                    \frac{\gamma}{8(2+4A_0^2)}\right).
\]
After a union over all \(N+1\) minors, one obtains
\[
 \|u\|,\|v\|\ge b_*
 \tag{Gram-component-lower-bound}
\]
for every stated low-energy unit mode, outside the common \(o(1)\) events and an exponentially small error.
No relation between the eigenmode and the remaining random coordinates was assumed: the subspace estimates hold for all vectors in the relevant subspaces before choosing a mode.

<a id="compressed-joint-gap"></a>
### A joint gap for the same compression

Fix a center range \(R_*>0\), to be specified below after \(b_*\) is fixed.
Choose a fixed \(h>0\) so small that every interval of length \(2h\) has semicircle mass at most \(\kappa/8\).
A finite grid \(\{a_j\}\) in \([-R_*,R_*]\) will have mesh at most the final residual threshold \(\epsilon\).
The first-input weak law, interlacing and a finite union ensure that each corresponding low-window subspace has dimension at most \(\kappa n/2\), with probability tending to one.
All these first-input requirements can be combined with the earlier high-energy count in one common event.

For each grid point define
\[
 E_j:=\mathbf1_{\{|C-a_jI_n|>h\}},\qquad
 A_j:=(C-a_jI_n)^{-1}E_j,\qquad
 V_j:=\operatorname{Ran}(I_n-E_j)+A_j(\ker Q).
\]
The notation for \(A_j\) means the bounded spectral function equal to
\((t-a_j)^{-1}\) when \(|t-a_j|>h\), and zero otherwise; it assumes no invertibility of \(C-a_jI_n\).
One has \(\|A_j\|\le1/h\) and
\(\dim V_j\le\kappa n/2+q\le3\kappa n/4\).

For any real unit \(z\), the exact identity
\[
 z=(I_n-E_j)z+
       A_j(I_n-Q)(C-a_jI_n)z+
       A_jQ(C-a_jI_n)z
 \tag{Compressed-window-decomposition}
\]
shows that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(z,V_j)
 \le h^{-1}\|Q(C-a_jI_n)z\|.
\]
No interchange of \(Q\) with \(C\) is made.
If \(\|Q(C-aI_n)z\|\le\epsilon\) and \(|a-a_j|\le\epsilon\), this distance is at most \(2\epsilon/h\).
For \(\epsilon<h/8\), the normalized projection \(z'\) onto \(V_j\) exists and obeys
\(\|z-z'\|\le4\epsilon/h\).

Let \(P_j\) project onto
\[
 \operatorname{Ran}Q\cap(V_j+TV_j)^\perp.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{codim}P_j\le q+2\dim V_j\le7\kappa n/4\), small enough for (Gram-projected-variance).
On \(V_j\),
\[
 P_j(D-bI_n)=P_jW,\qquad
 \|P_j(D-bI_n)\|\le H+B_L+|b|.
\]
Apply (Gram-small-subspace) to every \(V_j,P_j\).
There are a fixed number of grid points and \(N+1\) minors, so the total conditional failure is still exponentially small.
If, in addition to the first compressed residual, one had
\(\|Q(D-bI_n)z\|\le\epsilon\), then
\[
 \|P_j(D-bI_n)z'\|
 \le\epsilon+4(H+B_L+R_*)\epsilon/h.
\]
Choose a fixed \(\epsilon>0\) sufficiently small that this is below \(\gamma/2\).
Now fix the finite \(a\)-grid at mesh at most \(\epsilon\).
The resulting contradiction proves, uniformly over the minors,
\[
 \inf_{\substack{\|z\|=1\\a,b\in[-R_*,R_*]}}
 \max\{\|Q(C-aI_n)z\|,\|Q(D-bI_n)z\|\}\ge\epsilon
 \tag{Candidate-compressed-joint-gap}
\]
outside common \(o(1)\) events and an exponentially small error.
There is no grid for \(b\), since the projection annihilates \(V_j\).

<a id="low-mode-gram-conversion"></a>
### Conversion to the low-mode auxiliary Gram bound

For a stated unit eigenmode put
\[
 \Delta:=\|u\|^2\|v\|^2-\langle u,v\rangle^2,\qquad
 t:=\frac{\langle u,v\rangle}{\|v\|^2},\qquad
 e:=u-tv.
\]
Then \(e\perp v\) and \(\|e\|^2=\Delta/\|v\|^2\).
If \(\Delta\le b_*^4/4\), the component lower bound implies
\[
 b_*/2\le|t|\le1/b_*,\qquad \|e\|\le\sqrt\Delta/b_*.
\]
Set \(x_0:=\lambda-t^{-1}\) and \(y_0:=\lambda-t\).
The exact block equations give
\[
 (X-x_0I_n)v=t^{-1}(\lambda I_n-X)e,\qquad
 (Y-y_0I_n)v=-e.
\]
After left multiplication by \(Q\), these residuals are bounded by
\(2A_0\|e\|/b_*\) and \(\|e\|\), respectively.
Using
\[
 C=\frac{\alpha X-Y}{\alpha-1},\qquad
 D=\frac{Y-X}{\alpha-1},
\]
define the scalar centers
\[
 a_0:=\frac{\alpha x_0-y_0}{\alpha-1},\qquad
 b_0:=\frac{y_0-x_0}{\alpha-1}.
\]
They lie in some fixed interval \([-R_*,R_*]\) depending only on \(K_0,b_*\).
This choice of \(R_*\) is made before choosing \(h,\epsilon\) and the grid in the preceding subsection.
For a fixed \(C_*<\infty\), depending only on the already fixed constants,
\[
 \max\left\{
 \left\|Q(C-a_0I_n)\frac v{\|v\|}\right\|,
 \left\|Q(D-b_0I_n)\frac v{\|v\|}\right\|
 \right\}\le C_*\sqrt\Delta.
\]
This contradicts (Candidate-compressed-joint-gap) whenever \(\Delta\) is below a sufficiently small fixed positive threshold.
The auxiliary Gram matrix has trace one and nonnegative eigenvalues, so its least eigenvalue is at least its determinant \(\Delta\).
This proves the displayed low-mode Gram bound.

The common good events are the single tail Frobenius event and a single first-input spectral-count event, both of probability tending to one.
Their complements are added to the exponentially small mask, conditional norm and sphere-net errors.
They are counted once before the union over minor indices.
All later subspaces are measurable with respect to the first input and the tail data, which preserves independence of the remaining conditional good coordinates throughout.
The parameter order is cutoff \(L\), bounded-coordinate concentration and net budget \(\kappa\), large tail spectral threshold \(H\), fixed \(K>2\), component lower bound \(b_*\), center range \(R_*\), spectral width \(h\), residual tolerance \(\epsilon\) and the finite grid.
The conclusion is restricted to fixed \(|\lambda|\le K_0\).
It does not supply the fourth-layer coercivity or graph-area comparison required by [C194v14](Yin_C_F0194_v14.md#statement), and therefore does not establish a finite-variance least-value floor.
