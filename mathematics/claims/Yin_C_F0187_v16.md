# Finite-channel signed determinant noncancellation

This material isolates a deterministic coefficient mechanism for the continuing matrix-pencil and repeated-variable investigations.
It extends the whole-subspace comparable-mode question to every fixed number of real channels.
The hypotheses below are conditional geometric inputs, not assertions about every original-law random minor.
The complete argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v16.md#finite-channel-signed-determinant-reviewed), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-finite-channel-reviewed).

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact conditional conclusions

Let \(p\ge1\) be an integer, let \(\mathcal H,\mathcal E\) be real Hilbert spaces, and let \(U_1,\ldots,U_p:\mathcal H\to\mathcal E\) be bounded linear maps.
For \(a\in\mathbb R^p\), write \(U(a)=\sum_i a_iU_i\).
Let \(A:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H\) be bounded and selfadjoint.
Define
\[
 B_A(x)=\bigl[\langle U_i^*x,A U_j^*x\rangle\bigr]_{i,j=1}^p,
 \qquad h_A(x)=\det B_A(x),\qquad x\in\mathcal E.
\]
Thus \(B_A(x)\) is a real symmetric matrix and \(h_A\) is a continuous homogeneous polynomial of degree \(2p\).

First, suppose that \(\dim(\ker A)^\perp\ge p\) and every \(U(a)\), \(a\ne0\), is injective on \((\ker A)^\perp\).
Then
\[
 h_A\not\equiv0.
 \tag{Finite-channel-nonvanishing}
\]
Neither positivity, compactness nor a bounded inverse of \(A\) is required for this assertion.

Second, fix \(0<g\le1/p\), \(K\ge1\) and \(0<d\le1\).
There exists \(b=b(p,g,K,d)>0\) such that, in every finite dimension, the following conditions imply the displayed uniform conclusion:
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^pU_i^*U_i=I_{\mathcal H},\qquad
 \bigl[\langle U_i h,U_jh\rangle\bigr]_{i,j=1}^p
       \succeq g\|h\|^2 I_p\quad(h\in\mathcal H),
\]
\[
 A=A^*,\qquad \|A\|=1,\qquad
 \|A\|_F^2\le K,\qquad s_p(A)\ge d,
\]
\[
 \sup_{\|x\|=1}|h_A(x)|\ge b.
 \tag{Finite-channel-uniform-separation}
\]
Singular values are ordered decreasingly and padded with zeros.
The constant is independent of both Hilbert-space dimensions and of the signs of the eigenvalues of \(A\).
If the prescribed parameters allow no such tuple, this implication is vacuous.
By homogeneity, a nonzero weight with \(\|A\|_F^2\le K\|A\|^2\) and \(s_p(A)\ge d\|A\|\) has lower bound \(b\|A\|^p\).

This is a lower bound for the raw homogeneous polynomial.
It does not yet bound the coefficients with distinct physical coordinates, their loads, conditional small-ball probabilities or graph-normalized distances.
It neither supplies the stated whole-subspace Gram hypothesis for general Wigner pencils nor resolves [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract), [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract) or the full-sequence [F0012](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract).

## Complete proof

<a id="real-transitive-step"></a>
### A real transitive square matrix space contains an invertible matrix

For this paragraph, transitivity of a linear space \(\mathcal S\subset M_p(\mathbb R)\) means that \(\mathcal S v=\mathbb R^p\) for each nonzero \(v\in\mathbb R^p\).
Suppose its maximal rank is \(r<p\).
The case \(r=0\) contradicts transitivity immediately.
Otherwise invertible left and right changes of basis preserve transitivity and put a maximal-rank element in the form \(T_0=\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)\).
For any \(T=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}&T_{12}\\T_{21}&T_{22}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal S\), the upper-left block of \(T_0+tT\) is invertible for small real \(t\).
The bound \(\operatorname{rank}(T_0+tT)\le r\) makes its Schur complement zero:
\[
 tT_{22}-t^2T_{21}(I_r+tT_{11})^{-1}T_{12}=0.
\]
Divide by nonzero \(t\) and let \(t\to0\).
Thus \(T_{22}=0\) for every \(T\in\mathcal S\), so any nonzero \(v\) in the last \(p-r\) coordinates has \(\mathcal S v\) contained in the first \(r\) coordinates.
This contradicts transitivity and proves the real form of the cited result.

<a id="signed-exact-nonvanishing"></a>
### Maximum rank and the first derivative exclude signed cancellation

Restrict the domain to \(\mathcal H'=(\ker A)^\perp=\overline{\operatorname{ran}A}\).
Selfadjointness makes this a reducing subspace, and \(A'=A|_{\mathcal H'}\) is injective.
Replacing \(U_i\) by its restriction leaves \(B_A(x)\) unchanged.
The hypotheses on \(U(a)\) survive, so suppress the primes and assume henceforth that \(A\) is injective and \(\dim\mathcal H\ge p\).
No inverse of this operator is taken.

Define \(S(x):\mathcal H\to\mathbb R^p\) by
\[
 (S(x)h)_i=\langle U_i h,x\rangle.
\]
Its adjoint satisfies \(S(x)^*a=U(a)^*x\), and \(B_A(x)=S(x)A S(x)^*\).
For each nonzero \(h\), the vectors \(U_1h,\ldots,U_ph\) are linearly independent: a nontrivial relation would contradict the injectivity of \(U(a)\).
Choose any \(p\)-dimensional subspace \(\mathcal H_0\subset\mathcal H\) and an orthonormal basis there.
The square matrix space
\[
 \{\,S(x)|_{\mathcal H_0}:x\in\mathcal E\,\}
\]
is transitive, because for each nonzero \(h\in\mathcal H_0\) the inner products against these \(p\) independent vectors attain every value in \(\mathbb R^p\).
The preceding lemma therefore gives \(x\) for which \(S(x)|_{\mathcal H_0}\) is invertible, and \(S(x)\) has row rank \(p\).

Suppose, for a contradiction, that \(k=\max_x\operatorname{rank}B_A(x)<p\).
We may choose \(x_0\) simultaneously with \(\operatorname{rank}B_A(x_0)=k\) and \(\operatorname{rank}S(x_0)=p\).
For \(k>0\), choose a nonzero \(k\)-minor polynomial of \(B_A\) and the preceding nonzero determinant polynomial of \(S|_{\mathcal H_0}\).
On the affine line joining points witnessing their respective nonvanishing, neither polynomial is identically zero.
Avoid their finitely many roots to obtain \(x_0\).
For \(k=0\), use any point with row rank \(p\) for \(S\).
This argument needs no finite-dimensional assumption on \(\mathcal E\).

Fix \(0\ne a\in\ker B_A(x_0)\).
In the orthogonal decomposition \(\operatorname{ran}B_A(x_0)\oplus\ker B_A(x_0)\), the first block \(D_0\) of \(B_A(x_0)\) is invertible when \(k>0\).
For any \(y\in\mathcal E\), consider the symmetric matrix polynomial \(B_A(x_0+ty)\).
Its upper-left block remains invertible near \(t=0\).
Maximality of \(k\) forces the corresponding Schur complement to vanish.
At \(t=0\) both off-diagonal blocks are zero, so the derivative of the kernel-to-kernel block is zero.
For \(k=0\) the whole polynomial is zero and the same conclusion is immediate.
In particular
\[
 0=a^T B_A'(x_0)[y]a
   =2\langle U(a)A S(x_0)^*a,y\rangle
 \quad(y\in\mathcal E).
 \tag{Finite-channel-kernel-derivative}
\]
Thus \(U(a)A S(x_0)^*a=0\).
The injectivity of \(U(a)\) and then \(A\) gives \(S(x_0)^*a=0\).
This contradicts the row rank \(p\) of \(S(x_0)\).
The argument uses invertibility of \(D_0\), not positivity, so it proves (Finite-channel-nonvanishing) for arbitrary signs.

<a id="uniform-gram-compactness"></a>
### Uniformity from countable Gram compactness and an operator-norm tail

Suppose the uniform conclusion fails for fixed \(p,g,K,d\).
Choose a sequence of admissible finite-dimensional tuples, indexed by \(n\), whose unit-sphere suprema tend to zero.
Restrict each tuple to the nonzero spectral support of \(A_n\).
The isometric-stack and whole-subspace Gram hypotheses survive, and the polynomial is unchanged.
Choose an orthonormal eigenbasis \(e_{j,n}\), with eigenvalues \(\lambda_{j,n}\) in decreasing absolute value.
Pass to a subsequence on which the support dimensions are either a fixed finite integer \(r\ge p\), or tend to infinity.
In the latter case every fixed label is eventually present.

Put \(u_{i,j,n}=U_{i,n}e_{j,n}\).
All inner products among these countably many labels are bounded, as are their eigenvalues.
A diagonal subsequence makes every such scalar converge.
The limiting positive semidefinite Gram form defines a real Hilbert space \(\mathcal E_\infty\), obtained by quotienting its null vectors and completing the span of labels \(u_{i,j}\).
On \(\mathcal H_\infty=\mathbb R^r\) in the fixed-dimensional case, or \(\ell^2\) otherwise, set
\[
 U_i e_j=u_{i,j},\qquad A e_j=\lambda_j e_j,\qquad
 \lambda_j=\lim_n\lambda_{j,n}.
\]
The identities \(\sum_i\langle u_{i,j,n},u_{i,l,n}\rangle=\delta_{jl}\) pass to the limit.
Consequently the \(U_i\) extend to bounded operators with an isometric stack.
The whole-subspace Gram lower bound passes first for finitely supported vectors and then, by continuity, for every \(h\in\mathcal H_\infty\).
Likewise
\[
 |\lambda_1|=1,\qquad |\lambda_p|\ge d,\qquad
 \sum_j\lambda_j^2\le K.
\]
Thus the limiting weight is selfadjoint and compact in the infinite case, and has rank at least \(p\).

Let \(A^{(J)}\) keep the first \(J\) eigenvalue labels, and use the same convention for each \(A_n^{(J)}\).
Ordering by absolute value gives, uniformly before and after the limit,
\[
 \|A-A^{(J)}\|\le t_J,\qquad
 \|A_n-A_n^{(J)}\|\le t_J,\qquad
 t_J:=\sqrt{\frac K{J+1}}.
 \tag{Finite-channel-operator-tail}
\]
In the finite-support case the tail is zero once all labels are retained.
No uniform Frobenius-tail estimate is asserted.
Every entry of \(B_A(x)\) and \(B_{A^{(J)}}(x)\) has absolute value at most \(\|x\|^2\), and the entrywise difference is at most \(t_J\|x\|^2\).
Expanding determinants over permutations and telescoping the \(p\) factors in each product yields
\[
 |h_A(x)-h_{A^{(J)}}(x)|
 \le p\,p!\,t_J\|x\|^{2p}.
 \tag{Finite-channel-determinant-tail}
\]
The same inequality holds uniformly for every sequence member.

For any fixed finite linear combination \(x\) of limiting labels, form \(x_n\) using the identical coefficients and labels in the \(n\)-th space.
Their norms converge, and at each fixed \(J\) all entries of \(B_{A_n^{(J)}}(x_n)\) converge to those of \(B_{A^{(J)}}(x)\).
Homogeneity and the vanishing unit-sphere supremum imply \(h_{A_n}(x_n)\to0\).
Apply the determinant-tail estimate on both sides of this finite-label limit, then let \(J\to\infty\).
It follows that \(h_A(x)=0\) on the label span.
Continuity gives this identity on all of \(\mathcal E_\infty\).

Finally, the limiting Gram bound implies
\[
 \|U(a)h\|^2\ge g\|a\|^2\|h\|^2.
\]
Hence every \(U(a)\), \(a\ne0\), is injective.
The limiting weight has rank at least \(p\), so (Finite-channel-nonvanishing) contradicts the identity just obtained.
This proves the asserted dimension-independent constant and completes the argument.
