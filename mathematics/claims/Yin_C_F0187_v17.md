# Finite-variance exclusion of nearly rank-one three-channel modes

This theorem concerns the original finite-second-moment three-channel path pencil.
It excludes approximate modes whose three physical components are nearly collinear, without a bounded operator-norm assumption on either original input.
The argument conditions on one original input and the other input's tail, retaining the same bounded compression for both geometric branches.
The complete argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v17.md#finite-variance-three-channel-rank-one-reviewed), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-finite-variance-three-channel-rank-one-reviewed).

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact original model and conclusion

Let \(C_N,D_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
For input \(j=1,2\), its strict upper entries are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\xi_j\) is a fixed real centered law of variance one.
Its diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\zeta_j\) is fixed, real, centered and has finite second moment.
All upper-triangular coordinates and both inputs are independent within each size, and the coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
There is no density, boundedness or higher-moment hypothesis.

For \(S\subset[N]\), \(|S|\le1\), put \(n=N-|S|\) and let \(C=C_{N,S}\), \(D=D_{N,S}\) be the corresponding principal minors, retaining the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Set
\[
 T=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\sqrt2),\qquad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L_{N,S}=I_3\otimes C+T\otimes D+K\otimes I_n.
\]
For \(V=[u_1\ u_2\ u_3]\in M_{n,3}(\mathbb R)\), define
\[
 \mathcal F_\lambda(V)=CV+DVT+VK-\lambda V.
\]
Thus \(\|\mathcal F_\lambda(V)\|_F=\|(\mathcal L_{N,S}-\lambda I_{3n})(u_1,u_2,u_3)\|\).
Order the singular values of \(V\) decreasingly, padding with zeros when necessary.

For every fixed \(K_0\ge0\), there are fixed \(\rho_0,d_0,c,C_{\rm err}>0\) and \(p_N\to0\), depending only on the prescribed laws and \(K_0\), such that, for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
 \begin{array}{l}
 \exists\,S\subset[N],\ |S|\le1,\quad
 \exists\,\lambda\in[-K_0,K_0],\quad
 \exists\,V\in M_{n,3}(\mathbb R):\\
 \|V\|_F=1,\quad
 \|\mathcal F_\lambda(V)\|_F\le\rho_0,\quad
 s_2(V)<d_0
 \end{array}
 \right\}
 \le p_N+C_{\rm err}e^{-cN}.
 \tag{Three-channel-second-singular-bound}
\]
The conclusion is uniform over all these approximate modes, including arbitrary unit vectors in a repeated eigenspace, without any invertibility assumption.
In particular, at \(\lambda=0\), it applies to every real unit vector in the whole spectral subspace \(\operatorname{ran}\mathbf1_{[-\rho_0,\rho_0]}(\mathcal L_{N,S})\).
This is only a second-singular-value assertion for the matrix of physical components.
It does not assert a positive lower bound for \(V^TV\), for \(V^TV+TV^TVT\), or for each individual \(\|u_j\|\).
Rank-two modes with a small individual component, sixth-degree coefficient energy and loads, graph distances and a determinant floor remain outside this statement.
No almost-sure failure bound or conclusion for the unrestricted determinant or Brown-measure Tasks is asserted.

## Complete proof

<a id="original-tail-conditioning"></a>
### Tail conditioning and one common bounded-coordinate event

Fix \(\delta_{\rm mask}=1/32\).
Apply the fixed-cutoff construction to the first raw law \(\xi_1\).
For a sufficiently large fixed \(L\), write
\[
 p_L=\mathbb P\{|\xi_1|\le L\},\qquad
 m_L=\mathbb E[\xi_1\mid|\xi_1|\le L],\qquad
 \eta_L=\xi_1\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_1|>L\}}
              +m_L\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_1|\le L\}}.
\]
Choose \(L\) so that \(p_L>0\), the good conditional variance is at least \(1/2\), and
\(\mathbb P\{|\xi_1|>L\}<\delta_{\rm mask}/(2e)\).
The cited split permits these choices under exactly finite variance.
Decompose
\[
 C_N=\Theta_N+W_N
\]
by putting the raw \(\eta_L\) coordinates and every original first-input diagonal in \(\Theta_N\), and putting \(\xi_1-m_L\) on good off-diagonal edges and zero elsewhere in \(W_N\).
All entries retain the factor \(N^{-1/2}\).

Let \(\mathcal A_N\) be the sigma-field generated by every cutoff indicator, all bad raw values and all first-input diagonals, and condition on
\[
 \mathcal B_N=\sigma(D_N,\mathcal A_N).
\]
The independence of the original inputs leaves the remaining good raw upper coordinates of \(W_N\) conditionally independent and centered, with variance at least \(1/2\) and support diameter at most \(2L\).
Bad edges and the diagonal of \(W_N\) are zero.
The tail \(\Theta_N\) is fixed under this conditioning.
No mixed linear combination of the two inputs is conditioned upon.

The fixed tail weak-law and mask calculations give a constant \(C_\Theta<\infty\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\Theta_N\|_F^2>C_\Theta N\}=o(1),\qquad
 \mathbb P\{\max_i\deg_{\rm bad}(i)>\delta_{\rm mask}N\}
 \le C e^{-cN}.
\]
The conditional bounded norm calculation gives a fixed \(B_L\ge1\), uniformly in the tail data,
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|W_N\|>B_L\mid\mathcal B_N\}\le Ce^{-cN}.
\]
The same full-matrix tail, mask and bounded-coordinate events control every principal single deletion.

Here is the small-subspace input in its required form.
On the common good-mask event, for a fixed minor, any \(\mathcal B_N\)-measurable orthogonal projection \(P\), and a fixed real unit vector \(z\), the variance calculation gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\|PWz\|^2\mid\mathcal B_N]
 \ge\frac{\operatorname{rank}P-2-\delta_{\rm mask}N}{2N}.
 \tag{Three-channel-projected-variance}
\]
Indeed the vectors \(z_je_i+z_ie_j\) have outer-product sum
\(I_n+zz^T-2\operatorname{diag}(z_i^2)\), and removing bad edges costs at most \(\delta_{\rm mask}N\).
The retained conditional variances are at least \(1/2\).
For projections of sufficiently small fixed fractional codimension, this lower bound is at least \(1/4\) eventually.
The accepted bounded-product concentration calculation for the convex \(\sqrt{2/N}\)-Lipschitz function \(\|PWz\|\) consequently gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|PWz\|<\gamma\mid\mathcal B_N\}
 \le Ce^{-c_LN},\qquad \gamma=1/4.
\]
Choose \(\theta=\min(1/2,\gamma/(8B_L))\) and then \(0<\kappa\le1/64\) with
\[
 \kappa\log(3/\theta)<c_L/4.
\]
The sphere-net argument from the fixed input implies, for every specified \(\mathcal B_N\)-measurable subspace \(E\) with \(\dim E\le\kappa n\),
\[
 \inf_{\substack{z\in E\\\|z\|=1}}\|PWz\|\ge\gamma_0,
 \qquad \gamma_0:=\gamma/2,
 \tag{Three-channel-small-subspace}
\]
outside conditional probability \(Ce^{-cN}\), on the common norm event, whenever \(P\) has the stated small codimension.
An empty unit sphere imposes no condition.
Only the explicitly constructed spaces below are used; no assertion simultaneous over every conditioned subspace is needed.

<a id="one-common-compression"></a>
### One compression for the coordinate and joint-gap branches

Choose a fixed \(K_D>2\).
The accepted first-input spectral-count argument, here applied to the fully conditioned original input \(D_N\), gives at most \(\kappa N/16\) eigenvalues outside \([-K_D,K_D]\) in every required minor, outside one common \(o(1)\) event.
After \(L,B_L,\kappa,C_\Theta\) have been fixed, choose \(H\) large enough that
\[
 C_\Theta/H^2\le\kappa/16.
\]
For each minor let \(Q\) project onto
\[
 \operatorname{ran}\mathbf1_{[-K_D,K_D]}(D)
 \cap\operatorname{ran}\mathbf1_{[-H,H]}(\Theta),
 \qquad \Theta=\Theta_{N,S}.
\]
On the common tail and spectral-count events, eventually
\[
 q:=\operatorname{codim}Q\le\kappa N/8\le\kappa n/4,
 \qquad \|QD\|\le B_D:=K_D,\qquad
 \|QC\|\le B_C:=H+B_L.
 \tag{Three-channel-compressed-norms}
\]
For example \(Q=Q\mathbf1_{[-K_D,K_D]}(D)\), and similarly for \(\Theta\); no commutation between the two spectral projections is used.
The choice of \(H\) after the entropy budget avoids requiring a tail-decay rate not implied by finite variance.
The projection \(Q\) depends on \(D_N,\mathcal A_N\), not on the remaining bounded coordinates.

Put \(V_0=\ker Q\), and let \(P_0\) project onto
\[
 \operatorname{ran}Q\cap(V_0+DV_0+\Theta V_0)^\perp.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{codim}P_0\le4q\le\kappa n\), and on \(V_0\),
\[
 P_0(C+tD-\lambda I_n)=P_0W
 \quad(t,\lambda\in\mathbb R).
 \tag{Three-channel-coordinate-identity}
\]
Thus (Three-channel-small-subspace) for this one \(V_0,P_0\) gives
\[
 \inf_{\substack{z\in V_0\\\|z\|=1}}\|P_0Wz\|\ge\gamma_0.
 \tag{Three-channel-tail-space-gap}
\]

The same \(Q\) also admits the following joint gap for every fixed center range \(R_*>0\):
\[
 \inf_{\substack{\|z\|=1\\a,b\in[-R_*,R_*]}}
 \max\{\|Q(C-aI_n)z\|,\|Q(D-bI_n)z\|\}
 \ge\epsilon
 \tag{Three-channel-compressed-joint-gap}
\]
for a fixed \(\epsilon>0\), with the same type of common \(o(1)\) and exponential exceptions.
The construction may choose \(R_*\) after the constants in (Three-channel-compressed-norms); it does not rechoose \(Q\).

For clarity, apply the fixed proof with spectral windows of the conditioned \(D\), not the fresh \(C\).
Choose \(h>0\) so that every interval of length \(2h\) has semicircle mass at most \(\kappa/8\).
For a finite grid \(b_j\) in \([-R_*,R_*]\), put
\[
 E_j=\mathbf1_{\{|D-b_jI_n|>h\}},\qquad
 A_j=(D-b_jI_n)^{-1}E_j,\qquad
 V_j=\operatorname{ran}(I_n-E_j)+A_jV_0.
\]
The spectral function defining \(A_j\) is zero on the omitted window and has norm at most \(1/h\), requiring no invertibility of \(D-b_jI_n\).
The first-input weak law and principal interlacing, used on \(D_N\) and this fixed finite grid, give
\(\dim V_j\le3\kappa n/4\) simultaneously over all required minors, outside one common \(o(1)\) event.
The exact spectral decomposition gives
\[
 z=(I_n-E_j)z+A_j(I_n-Q)(D-b_jI_n)z
                    +A_jQ(D-b_jI_n)z.
\]
If \(\|Q(D-bI_n)z\|\le\epsilon\) and \(|b-b_j|\le\epsilon\), the distance to \(V_j\) is at most \(2\epsilon/h\).
For \(\epsilon<h/8\), its normalized projection \(z'\) satisfies \(\|z-z'\|\le4\epsilon/h\).

Let \(P_j\) project onto \(\operatorname{ran}Q\cap(V_j+\Theta V_j)^\perp\).
Its codimension is at most \(q+2\dim V_j\le7\kappa n/4\), within the projected-variance budget.
On \(V_j\), \(P_j(C-aI_n)=P_jW\), and globally \(\|P_j(C-aI_n)\|\le B_C+R_*\).
Apply (Three-channel-small-subspace) to these fixed \(V_j,P_j\).
If also \(\|Q(C-aI_n)z\|\le\epsilon\), then
\[
 \|P_j(C-aI_n)z'\|
 \le\epsilon+4(B_C+R_*)\epsilon/h.
\]
Choose \(\epsilon>0\) so small that this is less than \(\gamma_0\), and now fix the grid at mesh at most \(\epsilon\).
This contradicts (Three-channel-small-subspace) and proves (Three-channel-compressed-joint-gap).
There is no grid for the fresh-input center \(a\), since \(P_jV_j=0\).
All spaces and projections used by the conditional net are measurable under \(\mathcal B_N\).
There are finitely many grid points and \(N+1\) minors, so their conditional exponential errors remain exponential after a union bound.

<a id="compressed-rank-one-gap"></a>
### A uniform compressed residual gap for rank-one matrices

Set
\[
 A=1+B_C+\sqrt2B_D+\sqrt2+K_0.
\]
For every \(E\in M_{n,3}(\mathbb R)\) and \(|\lambda|\le K_0\),
\[
 \|Q\mathcal F_\lambda(E)\|_F\le A\|E\|_F.
 \tag{Three-channel-compressed-error}
\]
Define, in this order,
\[
 \bar r=\min\left(\frac14,\frac{\gamma_0}{4(1+2A)}\right),
 \qquad \bar h=\bar r/A,\qquad \delta_T=\sqrt2-1,
\]
\[
 J_0=\frac{2\sqrt3}{\delta_T\bar h},\qquad
 R_*=1+K_0+\sqrt2J_0.
\]
Use precisely this fixed center box in (Three-channel-compressed-joint-gap), obtaining \(\epsilon\), and then set
\[
 r_*=\min\left(\bar r,\frac{\epsilon}{2J_0}\right).
\]
On the common event just constructed,
\[
 \|Q\mathcal F_\lambda(ub^T)\|_F\ge r_*
 \quad
 (\|u\|=\|b\|=1,\ u\in\mathbb R^n,\ b\in\mathbb R^3,\
       |\lambda|\le K_0).
 \tag{Three-channel-rank-one-gap}
\]

To prove this, denote the left side by \(r\) and suppose \(r\le r_*\).
First suppose that \(\|b-\sigma e_k\|\le\bar h\) for some \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\).
By (Three-channel-compressed-error),
\[
 \|Q\mathcal F_\lambda(u\sigma e_k^T)\|_F
 \le r+A\bar h\le2\bar r\le1/2.
\]
The \(k\)-th column of this coordinate expression is
\(\sigma Q(C+t_kD-\lambda I_n)u\), and its other columns are
\(\sigma(Qu)(Ke_k)^T\).
Since \(K e_k\perp e_k\) and \(\|Ke_k\|\ge1\), each of
\[
 \|Qu\|,\qquad\|Q(C+t_kD-\lambda I_n)u\|
\]
is at most \(r+A\bar h\).
If \(V_0=\{0\}\), this contradicts \(\|Qu\|=1\).
Otherwise the normalized projection \(z\) of \(u\) onto \(V_0\) exists and satisfies
\[
 \|u-z\|\le2\|Qu\|\le2(r+A\bar h).
\]
Because \(P_0\le Q\), the global operator bound
\(\|P_0(C+t_kD-\lambda I_n)\|\le A\) holds.
The coordinate identity therefore gives
\[
 \|P_0Wz\|
 \le(1+2A)(r+A\bar h)
 \le2(1+2A)\bar r\le\gamma_0/2,
\]
contradicting (Three-channel-tail-space-gap).
The fact that the fresh coefficient of \(C\) is one in all three channels is used here.

It remains that \(b\) has distance greater than \(\bar h\) from every \(\pm e_k\).
Choose \(k\) maximizing \(b_k^2\), so \(b_k^2\ge1/3\).
The distance bound and \(\bar h\le1\) imply
\[
 1-b_k^2\ge\bar h^2-\bar h^4/4\ge3\bar h^2/4.
\]
For \(t_1=0,t_2=1,t_3=\sqrt2\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_b(T)
 =\sum_{i<j}(t_i-t_j)^2b_i^2b_j^2
 \ge\frac{\delta_T^2}{3}(1-b_k^2)
 \ge\frac{\delta_T^2\bar h^2}{4}.
\]
The Gram matrix of \(b,Tb\) has trace at most three and this variance as determinant.
Hence there are dual vectors \(\ell_1,\ell_2\in\mathbb R^3\) with
\[
 b^T\ell_1=1,\quad(Tb)^T\ell_1=0,\qquad
 b^T\ell_2=0,\quad(Tb)^T\ell_2=1,\qquad
 \|\ell_i\|\le J_0.
\]
Indeed the smaller eigenvalue of that Gram matrix is at least
\(\delta_T^2\bar h^2/12\), so its column matrix has a right-dual operator of norm at most \(J_0\).

The exact rank-one residual is
\[
 Q\mathcal F_\lambda(ub^T)
 =(QCu)b^T+(QDu)(Tb)^T+(Qu)(Kb-\lambda b)^T.
\]
Right multiplication by the two dual vectors gives
\[
 \|Q(C-cI_n)u\|,\ \|Q(D-dI_n)u\|\le J_0r,
\]
where
\[
 c=\lambda-(Kb)^T\ell_1,\qquad d=-(Kb)^T\ell_2.
\]
Both centers lie in \([-R_*,R_*]\), since \(\|K\|=\sqrt2\).
As \(J_0r\le\epsilon/2\), this contradicts the same-compression joint gap.
The two cases prove (Three-channel-rank-one-gap), uniformly over all vectors and centers on the one previously chosen event.

<a id="approximate-mode-second-singular-value"></a>
### Approximate modes and all physical single deletions

Let \(\|V\|_F=1\), \(|\lambda|\le K_0\), and
\(\|\mathcal F_\lambda(V)\|_F\le\rho\).
Its leading singular decomposition has the form
\[
 V=s\,ub^T+E,\qquad
 \|u\|=\|b\|=1,\qquad
 s=s_1(V)\ge1/\sqrt3,\qquad
 \|E\|_F\le\sqrt2s_2(V).
\]
Linearity, (Three-channel-rank-one-gap) and (Three-channel-compressed-error) give
\[
 \frac{r_*}{\sqrt3}
 \le s r_*
 \le\|Q\mathcal F_\lambda(V)\|_F+
       \|Q\mathcal F_\lambda(E)\|_F
 \le\rho+\sqrt2A\,s_2(V).
\]
Choose
\[
 \rho_0=\frac{r_*}{2\sqrt3},\qquad
 d_0=\frac{r_*}{2\sqrt6A}.
 \tag{Three-channel-final-constants}
\]
Then every such approximate mode with \(\rho\le\rho_0\) has \(s_2(V)\ge d_0\).
All error multiplication has occurred after left multiplication by \(Q\); neither \(\|C\|\) nor \(\|D\|\) is bounded or used.
No estimate of \(\|(I-Q)V\|_F\) is assumed.

The constant order is fixed cutoff, bounded-coordinate norm and net budget, then conditioned-input and tail spectral thresholds, then \(A,\bar r,\bar h,J_0,R_*\), then the joint-gap window and tolerance \(\epsilon\), then the finite center grid, and finally \(r_*,\rho_0,d_0\).
There is no choice depending on a random approximate mode or on a subsequently shrinking residual.
The common \(p_N=o(1)\) consists of the full tail Frobenius and conditioned-input spectral-count failures.
The mask, conditional norm and finitely many small-subspace failures over all \(N+1\) minors have total probability \(C_{\rm err}e^{-cN}\), after adjusting fixed constants.
This proves the stated probability bound.
It uses only independence within a size, so every allowed cross-size coupling has the same conclusion.
For a unit vector in \(\operatorname{ran}\mathbf1_{[-\rho_0,\rho_0]}(\mathcal L_{N,S})\), spectral calculus gives the approximate-mode premise at \(\lambda=0\), proving the stated whole-subspace consequence.

<a id="remaining-rank-two-interface"></a>
## Remaining rank-two interface

The argument excludes nearly rank-one component matrices, not a rank-two matrix with one small column.
In particular it does not prove a lower bound for the combined Gram \(V^TV+TV^TVT\).
If the first component is small, the first mode equation only makes \(Qu_2\) small; a projected second equation still contains the uncontrolled third component.
Further elimination can produce spaces depending on the fresh \(W\) and terms involving \(W^2\).
The linear small-subspace estimate above applies to spaces fixed under \(\mathcal B_N\), so it cannot simply be reused on those random spaces.
This is an unresolved extension problem, not a counterexample to the original model or a premise used in the present proof.
