# Finite-channel determinant tensors at high effective rank

This theorem addresses the high-effective-rank raw coefficient branch of the continuing three-channel investigation.
It extends the finite tensor-contraction mechanism of C187v6 to a fixed arbitrary channel number.
Its geometric hypothesis remains a condition to be supplied by the actual random model.
The complete deterministic argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v18.md#whole-proof-finding), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-high-rank-tensor-reviewed).
No novelty claim is made.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact deterministic assertions

Fix an integer \(p\ge1\) and \(g>0\).
Let \(\mathcal E\) be a finite-dimensional real Euclidean space and let \(K_{ij}=K_{ji}\), \(1\le i,j\le p\), be selfadjoint operators on \(\mathcal E\).
Suppose \(0<Q\le F\) and
\[
 \|K_{ij}\|_F\le F,\qquad \|K_{ij}\|\le Q,\qquad
 \left\|\sum_{i,j}a_i a_j K_{ij}\right\|_F
       \ge gF\|a\|^2\quad(a\in\mathbb R^p).
 \tag{Tensor-quadratic-separation}
\]
Identify \(K_{ij}\) with its order-two tensor, and use arithmetic-average symmetrization in every tensor space below.
Write
\[
 h(x)=\det[x^TK_{ij}x]_{i,j=1}^p
     =\langle\mathscr T,x^{\otimes2p}\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathscr T=\sum_{\sigma\in S_p}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
       \operatorname{Sym}_{2p}\bigotimes_{i=1}^p K_{i\sigma(i)}.
\]
There are \(R=R(p,g)\ge1\) and \(c=c(p,g)>0\), independent of \(\dim\mathcal E\), such that
\[
 F^2\ge RQ^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|\mathscr T\|_F^2\ge cF^{2p}.
 \tag{High-rank-raw-tensor}
\]
If the geometric hypotheses have no realization, the implication is vacuous.
The matrices and their spectra may have arbitrary signs.

In particular, let \(U_1,\ldots,U_p:\mathcal H\to\mathcal E\) be real linear maps with
\[
 \sum_i U_i^*U_i=I_{\mathcal H},\qquad
 [\langle U_i v,U_jv\rangle]_{i,j}
       \succeq g\|v\|^2I_p\quad(v\in\mathcal H).
\]
For a nonzero selfadjoint \(A\) on \(\mathcal H\), set
\[
 K_{ij}=\frac12(U_i A U_j^*+U_j A U_i^*).
\]
Then the raw determinant tensor above has squared norm at least \(c\|A\|_F^{2p}\) whenever
\(\|A\|_F^2\ge R\|A\|^2\).
This is a tensor coefficient lower bound, not a dimension-independent lower bound of order \(\|A\|_F^p\) on the unit sphere.

There is also the following physically local consequence.
Fix real \(t_1,\ldots,t_p\), and let \(Z=Z^T\ne0\) act on \(\mathbb R^p\otimes\mathbb R^n\), in auxiliary-first order.
Put
\[
 P_i=I_p\otimes e_ie_i^T,\quad
 F_Z=\|Z\|_F,\quad Q_Z=\|Z\|,\quad
 d_i=\|P_iZ\|_F^2,\quad d_*=\max_i d_i,\quad
 \mathcal D=\sum_i\|P_iZP_i\|_F^2.
\]
For \(z=(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\oplus\mathbb R^n\), write \(V_a z=x+t_a y\), and define
\[
 R(z)e_a=e_a\otimes V_a z,\qquad
 K_{ab}=\frac12(V_a^TZ_{ab}V_b+V_b^TZ_{ba}V_a).
\]
Thus \(h_Z(z)=\det(R(z)^TZR(z))=\det[z^TK_{ab}z]\).
For each fixed \(\eta>0\), there are \(R_*=R_*(p,t,\eta)\), \(\varepsilon_*=\varepsilon_*(p,t,\eta)>0\) and \(c_*=c_*(p,t,\eta)>0\) such that
\[
 \inf_{\|a\|=1}\left\|\sum_{b,c}a_ba_cK_{bc}\right\|_F
       \ge\eta F_Z,\qquad
 F_Z^2\ge R_*Q_Z^2,\qquad
 \mathcal D+d_*\le\varepsilon_*F_Z^2
 \tag{Physical-high-rank-inputs}
\]
imply the following coefficient bound.
Substitute independent formal signs \(x_i=\epsilon_{i,1}/\sqrt N\), \(y_i=\epsilon_{i,2}/\sqrt N\), retaining any specified original parent normalization \(N\).
If \(c_J\) are the complete degree-\(2p\) Walsh coefficients and \(\mathcal J_{\rm phys}\) consists of the \(2p\)-element sets with all physical positions different, then
\[
 \sigma_{2p,\rm phys}^2:=\sum_{J\in\mathcal J_{\rm phys}}c_J^2
       \ge c_*F_Z^{2p}/N^{2p}.
 \tag{Physical-high-rank-energy}
\]
Moreover, for every \(i\), the load of the complete degree-\(2p\) layer obeys
\[
 L_i^{(2p)}:=
 \sum_{\substack{|J|=2p\\J\cap(\{i\}\times\{1,2\})\ne\varnothing}}c_J^2
 \le C_{p,t}F_Z^{2p-2}d_i/N^{2p}.
 \tag{Physical-high-rank-load}
\]
In particular, at fixed \(p,t,\eta\), along a sequence with the first two inputs in (Physical-high-rank-inputs) and
\((\mathcal D+d_*)/F_Z^2\to0\), the stated lower bound holds eventually and the maximum load divided by complete degree-\(2p\) energy tends to zero.
Both original row coordinates at a physical position remain separate independent variables.
No assertion about actual random-minor geometry, an inverse-rank branch probability, conditional small-ball estimates, graph-normalized distances or a Task completion is included.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Complete proof

<a id="symmetric-space-separation"></a>
### A separated symmetric matrix space contains an invertible element

Let \(k_{ij}=k_{ji}\) belong to a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space \(\mathcal K\).
Suppose
\[
 \left\|\sum_{i,j}a_ia_jk_{ij}\right\|>0
 \quad\text{for every nonzero }a\in\mathbb R^p.
\]
For \(y\in\mathcal K\), put \(B(y)=[\langle k_{ij},y\rangle]_{i,j}\).
Assume for a contradiction that every \(B(y)\) is singular.
Choose an element \(B_0\) with maximal rank \(r<p\).
If \(r=0\), every \(k_{ij}=0\), already a contradiction.
Otherwise an orthogonal change of auxiliary basis writes \(B_0=\operatorname{diag}(D,0)\), where \(D\) is real symmetric and invertible.
For any \(B=B(y)\), the upper-left block of \(B_0+sB\) is invertible for small real \(s\).
Maximal rank makes its Schur complement vanish:
\[
 sB_{22}-s^2B_{21}(D+sB_{11})^{-1}B_{12}=0.
\]
Dividing by nonzero \(s\) and passing to zero gives \(B_{22}=0\) for every \(B\).
Choose a nonzero auxiliary vector \(a\) in the kernel block, and return it to the original basis.
Then \(a^TB(y)a=0\) for every \(y\), so \(\sum a_i a_jk_{ij}=0\), a contradiction.
Thus the homogeneous polynomial \(\det B(y)\) is not identically zero.
The argument uses invertibility of \(D\), not positivity.

<a id="reduced-determinant-tensor"></a>
### A dimension-independent reduced tensor bound

View the \(K_{ij}\) as vectors in the real Hilbert space of selfadjoint operators with Frobenius inner product.
Define its symmetric order-\(p\) tensor
\[
 \mathscr D=\sum_{\sigma\in S_p}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
            \operatorname{Sym}_p\bigotimes_{i=1}^p K_{i\sigma(i)}.
\]
There is \(m=m(p,g)>0\) such that (Tensor-quadratic-separation) and the Frobenius upper bounds imply
\[
 \|\mathscr D\|_F^2\ge mF^{2p}.
 \tag{Reduced-tensor-bound}
\]
To prove it, divide all \(K_{ij}\) by \(F\), and consider their Gram matrix, using one label for each unordered auxiliary pair.
There are \(q=p(p+1)/2\) labels.
Their Gram matrices are positive semidefinite, have diagonal at most one, and belong to a fixed compact subset of \(M_q(\mathbb R)\).
The quadratic separation inequality is closed in these entries for every \(a\), hence remains true in any limit.
The squared norm of \(\mathscr D\) is a polynomial in the finitely many Gram entries, and is therefore continuous.

If no positive \(m\) existed, take a convergent sequence of Gram matrices with reduced tensor norm tending to zero, and realize the limiting Gram matrix by vectors \(k_{ij}\) in \(\mathbb R^q\).
The limiting symmetric tensor is zero.
Pairing it with \(y^{\otimes p}\) shows \(\det[\langle k_{ij},y\rangle]=0\) for every \(y\).
But the limiting quadratic combination has norm at least \(g\|a\|^2\), contrary to the preceding symmetric-space argument.
This proves (Reduced-tensor-bound) uniformly in the original physical dimension.

<a id="bipartite-contraction"></a>
### Double-edge contractions and the longer-cycle error

The two tensor norms satisfy
\[
 \left|\|\mathscr T\|_F^2-
       \frac{2^p p!}{(2p)!}\|\mathscr D\|_F^2\right|
 \le (p!)^2 Q^2F^{2p-2}.
 \tag{Tensor-contraction-comparison}
\]
Here is a direct finite expansion with the normalization explicit.
For two ordered lists of \(p\) symmetric matrices, the inner product of their fully symmetrized order-\(2p\) product tensors is the average over all \((2p)!\) permutations of the slots on the second side.
Each permutation joins each slot of a matrix on the first side to one slot of a matrix on the second.
The resulting bipartite multigraph has \(p\) vertices on each side, all of degree two, and is a disjoint union of even cycles.

When every component is a double edge, each of the \(p\) matrices on the first side is matched to one matrix on the second, in \(p!\) possible vertex matchings.
For each vertex matching there are \(2^p\) slot matchings.
Its contraction equals the product of the \(p\) corresponding Frobenius inner products.
Summing these terms over both determinant expansions gives exactly
\[
 \frac{2^p}{(2p)!}
 \sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_p}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma\tau)
 \sum_{\pi\in S_p}
 \prod_{i=1}^p
 \langle K_{i\sigma(i)},K_{\pi(i)\tau(\pi(i))}\rangle_F
 =\frac{2^p p!}{(2p)!}\|\mathscr D\|_F^2.
\]
All remaining slot permutations contain a cycle of length \(r\ge4\).
The contraction on a cycle is a trace of a product of its \(r\) matrices; symmetry removes orientation-dependent transposes.
Its absolute value is at most \(Q^{r-2}F^2\), by placing two factors in Frobenius norm and all others in operator norm.
A double edge has absolute contraction at most \(F^2\).
Because \(Q\le F\), the product over all cycles of any remaining permutation is at most \(Q^2F^{2p-2}\).
The slot average has total weight at most one, and the two determinant expansions have \((p!)^2\) terms.
This proves (Tensor-contraction-comparison).
For \(p=1\) there are no longer cycles; the bound still holds with zero error.

Put \(b_p=2^p p!/(2p)!\).
Choose \(R\ge\max(1,2(p!)^2/(b_pm))\).
Then (Reduced-tensor-bound) and (Tensor-contraction-comparison) give
\[
 \|\mathscr T\|_F^2\ge (b_pm/2)F^{2p}.
\]
This is the first assertion.

<a id="whole-subspace-input"></a>
### Whole-subspace channel geometry supplies the quadratic separation

Under the channel-map assumptions in the statement, every \(U_i\) has norm at most one.
Consequently \(\|K_{ij}\|\le\|A\|\) and \(\|K_{ij}\|_F\le\|A\|_F\).
For \(U(a)=\sum_i a_iU_i\),
\[
 \sum_{i,j}a_i a_j K_{ij}=U(a)AU(a)^*,\qquad
 U(a)^*U(a)\succeq g\|a\|^2I_{\mathcal H}.
\]
For \(a\ne0\), write \(B=U(a)^*U(a)\).
Then
\[
 \|U(a)AU(a)^*\|_F
 =\|B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}\|_F
 \ge g\|a\|^2\|A\|_F.
\]
The equality follows by cyclicity of finite traces, and the inequality by multiplying on both sides by \(B^{-1/2}\).
No positivity of \(A\) is used.
Apply the first assertion with \(F=\|A\|_F\), \(Q=\|A\|\).

<a id="physical-high-rank-transfer"></a>
### Complete local collision and load conversion

Return to the physically local statement and put \(\Lambda=\max_a\sqrt{1+t_a^2}\).
On the row-variable space let \(\widehat P_i\) project onto the two coordinates \(x_i,y_i\).
Direct block extraction and symmetric averaging give
\[
 \|K_{ab}\|\le\Lambda^2Q_Z,\quad
 \|K_{ab}\|_F\le\Lambda^2F_Z,\quad
 \|\widehat P_iK_{ab}\|_F^2\le\Lambda^4d_i,\quad
 \sum_i\|\widehat P_iK_{ab}\widehat P_i\|_F^2
       \le\Lambda^4\mathcal D.
 \tag{Local-factor-bounds}
\]
For the row bound, the unsymmetrized term uses row \(i\) of \(Z_{ab}\), and its transpose uses row \(i\) of \(Z_{ba}\).
Both squared row sums are included in \(d_i\).
For the diagonal bound the two-by-two block is
\((Z_{ab})_{ii}(v_av_b^T+v_bv_a^T)/2\), where \(v_a=(1,t_a)^T\).
Its squared norm is at most \(\Lambda^4|(Z_{ab})_{ii}|^2\).
These observations prove the last two bounds without identifying the two input variables.

Use the first assertion with \(F=\Lambda^2F_Z\), \(Q=\Lambda^2Q_Z\), and \(g=\eta/\Lambda^2\).
For \(F_Z^2/Q_Z^2\) above a fixed threshold, it gives
\(\|\mathscr T\|_F^2\ge c_0F_Z^{2p}\), where \(c_0>0\) depends only on \(p,t,\eta\).
Let \(\mathscr T_{\rm rep}\) restrict the tensor to ordered \(2p\)-tuples with a repeated physical position, and let \(\mathscr T_{\rm dist}\) be the complementary restriction.
These projections are orthogonal and commute with slot symmetrization.

For one product of \(p\) quadratic-factor tensors, a collision between two slots in the same factor contributes at most
\(\Lambda^{4p}\mathcal D F_Z^{2p-2}\) to the squared tensor norm.
A collision between slots from two different factors contributes at most
\[
 \Lambda^{4p}F_Z^{2p-4}\sum_i d_i^2
 \le\Lambda^{4p}d_*F_Z^{2p-2}.
\]
The case of two different factors occurs only for \(p\ge2\).
There are \(\binom{2p}{2}\) possible pairs of slots and \(p!\) determinant terms.
Contraction of symmetrization, the union bound on the nonnegative coordinate sums, and the squared norm bound for a finite sum prove
\[
 \|\mathscr T_{\rm rep}\|_F^2
       \le C_{p,t}F_Z^{2p-2}(\mathcal D+d_*).
 \tag{Local-collision-bound}
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon_*>0\) so that \(C_{p,t}\varepsilon_*\le c_0/2\).
The orthogonal decomposition then gives
\(\|\mathscr T_{\rm dist}\|_F^2\ge(c_0/2)F_Z^{2p}\).

For \(2p\) distinct individual row variables, the coefficient after the sign substitution is
\[
 c_J=\frac{(2p)!}{N^p}\mathscr T_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_{2p}}.
\]
Any repetition of one individual variable reduces the Walsh degree below \(2p\), so it cannot contribute to this coefficient.
Every unordered set of \(2p\) different physical positions, with its specified input labels, has exactly \((2p)!\) ordered representations.
It follows that
\[
 \sigma_{2p,\rm phys}^2
   =\frac{(2p)!}{N^{2p}}\|\mathscr T_{\rm dist}\|_F^2,
\]
proving (Physical-high-rank-energy).

For one slot of an unsymmetrized product, (Local-factor-bounds) bounds its group-\(i\) restricted squared norm by
\(\Lambda^{4p}F_Z^{2p-2}d_i\).
Jensen's inequality for the arithmetic-average symmetrization and the finite determinant sum therefore give
\[
 \|\widehat P_i^{(1)}\mathscr T\|_F^2
       \le (p!)^2\Lambda^{4p}F_Z^{2p-2}d_i.
\]
The union bound over \(2p\) slots bounds the squared norm of the restriction to all tuples meeting group \(i\) by \(2p\) times this quantity.
Restricting further to distinct individual variables can only decrease it.
The same factorial counting now proves (Physical-high-rank-load) for the complete top layer, including terms using both \(x_i\) and \(y_i\).
Dividing by its energy, which is at least the physically distinct energy, proves the asserted vanishing relative loads.

The quadratic separation in (Physical-high-rank-inputs) has an explicit sufficient geometric source.
If \(Z=UAU^T\), where the stacked coordinate map \(U=(U_1,\ldots,U_p)^T\) is an isometry, and
\[
 [\langle V_a^TU_av,V_b^TU_bv\rangle]_{a,b}
       \succeq\eta\|v\|^2I_p\quad(v\in\mathcal H),
\]
then its scalar quadratic combination is
\((\sum a_bV_b^TU_b)A(\sum a_cV_c^TU_c)^T\).
The preceding sandwich argument bounds its Frobenius norm below by \(\eta\|A\|_F\), and \(\|A\|_F=F_Z\).
This verifies the implication from whole-subspace combined Gram geometry without asserting that geometry for the original finite-variance three-channel model.
