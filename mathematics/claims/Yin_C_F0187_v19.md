# Combined auxiliary Gram separation for finite-variance three-channel modes

This theorem gives a strict combined auxiliary Gram bound for every sufficiently accurate bounded-energy mode of the original real finite-second-moment three-channel path pencil.
It combines the adopted second-singular-value input with new exclusion of all three small components, including a rectangular four-copy estimate at the first-component endpoint.
The complete argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v19.md#finite-variance-three-channel-combined-reviewed), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-finite-variance-three-channel-combined-reviewed).
No bounded operator norm of either original input is assumed.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact original model and conclusions

Let \(C_N,D_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
For input \(j=1,2\), its strict upper entries are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\xi_j\) is a fixed real centered law of variance one.
Its diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\zeta_j\) is fixed, real, centered and has finite second moment.
All upper-triangular coordinates and the two inputs are independent within each size, and the coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
No density, bounded-support or higher-moment assumption is imposed.

For every \(S\subset[N]\) with \(|S|\le1\), put \(n=N-|S|\) and let \(C=C_{N,S}\), \(D=D_{N,S}\) denote the corresponding principal minors, with the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Define
\[
 \alpha=\sqrt2,\quad T=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\alpha),\quad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathcal L_{N,S}=I_3\otimes C+T\otimes D+K\otimes I_n.
\]
For a real component matrix \(V=[u_1\ u_2\ u_3]\in M_{n,3}(\mathbb R)\), write
\[
 \mathcal F_\lambda(V)=CV+DVT+VK-\lambda V,
 \qquad G(V)=V^TV+TV^TVT.
\]
The Frobenius residual equals the Euclidean residual of the corresponding block vector under \(\mathcal L_{N,S}-\lambda I_{3n}\).
Singular values of \(V\) are ordered decreasingly and padded with zeros when necessary.

For every fixed \(K_0\ge0\), there are fixed constants \(\rho,d,m,g,c,C_{\rm err}>0\) and \(p_N\to0\), depending only on the two prescribed laws and \(K_0\), such that, for all sufficiently large \(N\), outside an event of probability at most
\[
 p_N+C_{\rm err}e^{-cN},
\]
the following implication holds simultaneously for every permitted \(S\), every real \(\lambda\in[-K_0,K_0]\), and every real \(V\):
\[
 \|V\|_F=1,\qquad \|\mathcal F_\lambda(V)\|_F\le\rho
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 s_2(V)\ge d,\quad \min_{1\le j\le3}\|u_j\|\ge m,\quad
 G(V)\succeq gI_3.
 \tag{Combined-mode-input}
\]
In particular this covers every unit vector in a repeated eigenspace, without an invertibility assumption.

The same event gives a whole-subspace conclusion.
For every center \(\lambda_0\in[-K_0,K_0]\), every \(0\le r\le\rho\), and every real isometric embedding
\[
 U:\mathcal H\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{ran}\mathbf1_{[\lambda_0-r,\lambda_0+r]}(\mathcal L_{N,S}),
 \qquad Uh=(U_1h,U_2h,U_3h),
\]
define \(\widetilde U_jh=(U_jh,t_jU_jh)\in\mathbb R^n\oplus\mathbb R^n\), where \((t_1,t_2,t_3)=(0,1,\alpha)\).
Then
\[
 [\langle\widetilde U_i h,\widetilde U_jh\rangle]_{i,j=1}^3
 \succeq g\|h\|^2I_3\qquad(h\in\mathcal H).
 \tag{Combined-whole-low-subspace}
\]
This concerns all vectors in the subspace, not just a selected eigenbasis.
The zero subspace satisfies the statement vacuously.
There is no assertion that the single-input Gram \(V^TV\) is positive definite.
The failure bound is not claimed summable, and this theorem makes no sixth-degree coefficient-probability, inverse-rank, graph-distance, least-value-floor, determinant or Brown-measure conclusion.

## Fixed inputs and exact boundaries

The adopted [C187v17 second-singular-value conclusion](Yin_C_F0187_v17.md#statement) supplies fixed \(d_0,\rho_0>0\) uniformly over all bounded-energy approximate modes and all full or single-deletion minors in precisely this model.
Its proof exposes the complete \(D_N\) input and the \(C_N\) tail.
The new column argument below instead exposes the complete \(C_N\) input and the \(D_N\) tail.
These are two separately established events for the original joint law; they are intersected by an ordinary union bound at the end.
The new conditional coordinate laws are never conditioned on the C187v17 good event.

The [conditional tail split](Yin_C_F0187_v12.md#conditional-tail-split) supplies the exact centered bounded remainder, tail weak law and missing-edge degree bound with the original normalization.
The [conditional bounded norm calculation](Yin_C_F0187_v12.md#conditional-bounded-norm) supplies the uniform norm event for that actual nonidentically distributed missing-edge remainder.
The [projected variance and fixed-small-subspace calculation](Yin_C_F0187_v13.md#conditional-small-subspaces) and [bounded-compression construction](Yin_C_F0187_v13.md#bounded-compression) supply the covariance method, spectral counts and compressed norm interfaces used below.
The [spectral-window step](Yin_C_F0187_v13.md#compressed-joint-gap) supplies the scalar window-count mechanism and noncommuting preimage decomposition, which are restated for the new spaces below.
The present proof constructs its new projections, checks their dimensions and chooses the tail threshold and entropy parameters explicitly.
No two-channel mode conclusion is used for the three-channel pencil.

The direct concentration input is the accepted bounded-product convex concentration inequality (9) recorded in [C187v2](Yin_C_F0187_v2.md#argument), valid for independent real bounded coordinates and convex Lipschitz functions.
It is applied both to the original bounded remainder and to independent differences of its rectangular coordinate blocks.
Its recorded primary-source statement/applicability inspection and unreviewed original-source-proof boundary are unchanged.
The four-copy probability inequality is proved here by Cauchy--Schwarz, rather than invoked as an additional external theorem.
All new project reasoning is contained in this C; no working draft supplies a logical input.

## Complete proof

<a id="separate-original-law-events"></a>
### The second-singular-value event and the fresh conditional law

Fix \(K_0\), and take the deterministic constants \(\rho_0,d_0\) and event \(\Omega_{\rm rank,N}\) from C187v17.
Reduce \(d_0\) if necessary so that \(0<d_0\le1\), and write \(d=d_0\) in the column argument.
We will prove that, outside another event of probability \(p_N'+Ce^{-cN}\), all normalized modes satisfying \(s_2(V)\ge d\) and a sufficiently small residual have three uniformly nonzero columns.
This new assertion is proved directly for the original law, independently of whether \(\Omega_{\rm rank,N}\) holds on a particular sample.

Apply the fixed-cutoff split to the second input \(D_N\).
At a sufficiently large fixed cutoff \(L\), put
\[
 m_L=\mathbb E[\xi_2\mid |\xi_2|\le L],\qquad
 \eta_L=\xi_2\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_2|>L\}}
                  +m_L\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_2|\le L\}}.
\]
Place every raw \(\eta_L\) off-diagonal entry and every original second-input diagonal in \(\Theta_N\).
On a good edge place the raw coordinate \(\xi_2-m_L\) in \(W_N\), and put zero on its bad edges and diagonal.
Thus \(D_N=\Theta_N+W_N\), with every entry retaining \(N^{-1/2}\).
Choose \(L\) so that the conditional good variance is at least \(1/2\) and the bad-edge probability is smaller than \(1/(64e)\).

Let \(\mathcal A_N\) contain all second-input cutoff indicators, all bad raw values and all second-input diagonals, and condition on
\[
 \mathcal B_N:=\sigma(C_N,\mathcal A_N).
\]
The remaining good raw upper coordinates of \(W_N\) are conditionally independent and centered, with support diameter at most \(2L\) and variance at least \(1/2\).
The tail is fixed, and bad edges and diagonal coordinates of \(W_N\) are zero.
The fixed inputs give constants \(C_\Theta,B_L\) with
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\Theta_N\|_F^2>C_\Theta N\}=o(1),\qquad
 \mathbb P\{\max_i\deg_{\rm bad}(i)>N/32\}\le Ce^{-cN},
\]
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|W_N\|>B_L\mid\mathcal B_N\}\le Ce^{-cN}.
\]
These full-matrix events control every required minor.
All subsequent conditional variance and lower-tail assertions are restricted to tail data with maximum missing-edge degree at most \(N/32\); the probability of the excluded tail data is added separately.
The norm event is used only for approximation and net extension, never inserted into the conditional law.

For a minor, write \(\Theta,W\) for the restrictions and let \(Q\) be a \(\mathcal B_N\)-measurable orthogonal projection with
\[
 q:=\operatorname{codim}Q\le\kappa n/4,\qquad
 0<\kappa\le1/64,\qquad V_0=\ker Q.
\]
Its actual spectral construction and the final choice of \(\kappa\) are given below.
The three residual equations, used throughout, are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (C-\lambda I)u_1+u_2&=r_1,\\
 (C+D-\lambda I)u_2+u_1+u_3&=r_2,\\
 (C+\alpha D-\lambda I)u_3+u_2&=r_3,
 \end{aligned}
 \qquad \|r_j\|\le\varepsilon.
 \tag{Two-column-residual-equations}
\]
The symbol for this common residual bound will also be written \(\rho\) when selecting the first-column tolerance.

<a id="linear-fixed-space-input"></a>
### The linear fixed-space estimate used by the other two columns

For a \(\mathcal B_N\)-measurable orthogonal projection \(P\), the C187v13 covariance identity gives, for every fixed real unit vector \(z\),
\[
 \mathbb E[\|PWz\|^2\mid\mathcal B_N]
 \ge\frac{\operatorname{rank}P-2-N/32}{2N}.
\]
Indeed \(\sum_{i<j}(z_je_i+z_ie_j)(z_je_i+z_ie_j)^T=I_n+zz^T-2\operatorname{diag}(z_i^2)\), the retained variance is at least \(1/(2N)\), and deleting bad edges costs at most \(N/32\) before that factor.
If \(\operatorname{codim}P\le13\kappa n/4\), the lower bound is at least \(1/4\) eventually.
The norm is convex and \(\sqrt{2/N}\)-Lipschitz in the remaining raw coordinates.
The accepted concentration and integrated median-error bound give
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|PWz\|<1/4\mid\mathcal B_N\}\le Ce^{-c_{\rm lin}N}.
\]
Fix \(\theta=\min(1/2,1/(32B_L))\).
When \(\kappa\log(3/\theta)<c_{\rm lin}/4\), the same sphere-net extension on \(\|W_N\|\le B_L\) gives, for every specified conditioned subspace \(E\) with \(\dim E\le\kappa n\),
\[
 \inf_{\substack{z\in E\\\|z\|=1}}\|PWz\|\ge\Gamma,
 \qquad \Gamma=1/8,
 \tag{Two-column-fixed-subspace-noise}
\]
outside conditional probability \(Ce^{-cN}\).
Only the explicitly constructed finite collection of subspaces and projections below is used; this is not an assertion over every subspace chosen after the noise.
An empty unit sphere creates no condition.

<a id="quadratic-endpoint-reduction"></a>
### The exact compressed quadratic constraint

For \(|\lambda|\le K_0\), define
\[
 B_\lambda=C+\Theta+W-\lambda I,
 \qquad H_\lambda=C+\alpha\Theta+\alpha W-\lambda I,
 \qquad \alpha=\sqrt2,
\]
\[
 \Psi_\lambda(z,w):=
 QH_\lambda\bigl[w-QB_\lambda z\bigr],
 \qquad z,w\in V_0.
 \tag{Endpoint-quadratic-vector}
\]
On the event used for the approximation argument, suppose that
\[
 \|Q(C-\lambda I)\|,\ \|QB_\lambda\|,\ \|QH_\lambda\|\le B
 \tag{Endpoint-compressed-bounds}
\]
for a fixed \(B\ge1\), uniformly in the stated energy range.

Let \(V=[u_1\ u_2\ u_3]\) satisfy \(\|V\|_F=1\), \(s_2(V)\ge d\), and the three residual equations (Two-column-residual-equations), with \(\|r_j\|\le\rho\).
Assume \(\|u_1\|\le b\), and set
\[
 r:=Bb+\rho,\qquad z=(I-Q)u_2,\qquad w=(I-Q)u_3.
\]
The first residual equation gives \(\|Qu_2\|\le r\), hence \(\|u_2-z\|\le r\).
The second equation, after multiplication by \(Q\), gives the full-vector approximation
\[
 \left\|u_3-\bigl[w-QB_\lambda z\bigr]\right\|
 \le e:=\rho+b+Br.
 \tag{Endpoint-full-vector-approximation}
\]
This uses \(Q B_\lambda\) on the entire error vector, with operator norm bounded by \(B\); it never uses an uncompressed norm of \(B_\lambda\).
The third equation gives \(\|QH_\lambda u_3\|\le\rho+r\), and therefore
\[
 \|\Psi_\lambda(z,w)\|
 \le\rho+r+Be
 =(B^2+B+2)\rho+(B^3+2B)b.
 \tag{Endpoint-quadratic-residual}
\]
Both parameters lie in the unit ball of the fixed space \(V_0\).
The rank-one approximation \([0\ 0\ u_3]\) gives
\[
 \|z\|\ge\sqrt{d^2-b^2}-r.
 \tag{Endpoint-parameter-size}
\]
Thus a sufficiently small first column creates a small quadratic residual with two fixed-space parameters, the first of norm at least \(d/2\).
The remaining task is a uniform lower bound for this quadratic vector on that parameter set.

<a id="quadratic-fixed-parameter-gap"></a>
### A fixed-parameter quadratic lower tail independent of the background

The following calculation does not require (Endpoint-compressed-bounds).
Fix all the conditioned data, any symmetric background matrices \(C,\Theta\), any \(\lambda\), any \(w\), and any nonzero deterministic \(z\).
For this fixed-parameter calculation, \(z,w\) need not belong to \(V_0\).
Only the stated codimension bound and the conditional coordinate law of \(W\) are used.
We prove that fixed constants \(c_0,c_L,C_L>0\) give
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\Psi_\lambda(z,w)\|\le c_0\|z\|\mid
                     C_N,\text{tail data}\}
 \le C_Le^{-c_LN}.
 \tag{Endpoint-fixed-parameter-small-ball}
\]
Here the expression defining \(\Psi\) is extended to these arbitrary fixed vectors by the same displayed formula.
The constants are uniform over the fixed backgrounds, vectors, energy, projection and permitted minor; the exponential rate may depend on the cutoff \(L\).

Partition the physical coordinates into three blocks of sizes differing by at most one.
Choose as \(I\) one of those blocks with \(\|z_I\|\ge\|z\|/\sqrt3\), and call the other two blocks \(J,K\).
This choice depends only on the fixed vector \(z\).
Let \(A\) contain the remaining random coordinates on the unordered \(I\)-to-\(J\) edges and their symmetric partners, let \(B'\) contain those on the \(J\)-to-\(K\) edges, and put \(U=W-A-B'\).
The coordinate families \(A,B',U\) are independent under the tail conditioning; missing edges remain zero in each family.
The symbol \(B'\) denotes this edge matrix, not the compressed norm constant \(B\) or the matrix \(B_\lambda\).

Take independent copies \(A^{(0)},A^{(1)}\) and \(B^{(0)},B^{(1)}\) with their same conditional laws, retain one common \(U\), and write
\[
 \Delta A=A^{(0)}-A^{(1)},\qquad
 \Delta B=B^{(0)}-B^{(1)}.
\]
Every nonzero raw coordinate of either difference is centered, independent of the other coordinates in that difference, has variance at least one, and has support diameter at most \(4L\).
The two difference families are independent of each other and of \(U\).
Let \(\Pi_J,\Pi_K\) be the orthogonal projections onto
\[
 \mathbb R^J\cap\operatorname{Ran}Q,
 \qquad \mathbb R^K\cap\operatorname{Ran}Q,
\]
respectively, identifying the coordinate spaces with their embedded subspaces of \(\mathbb R^n\).
Their ranks are at least \(|J|-\operatorname{codim}Q\) and \(|K|-\operatorname{codim}Q\).

For fixed \(U\), expand the four-corner alternating difference of \(\Pi_K\Psi\) at the four choices \((A^{(i)},B^{(j)})\).
Its constant and linear terms cancel, leaving
\[
 -\alpha\Pi_K Q
       (\Delta A Q\Delta B+\Delta B Q\Delta A)z
 =-\alpha\Pi_K\Delta B Q\Delta A z.
 \tag{Endpoint-four-corner-identity}
\]
Indeed \(\Pi_KQ=\Pi_K\), and \(\Pi_K\Delta A=0\) because \(\Delta A\) has support only in the \(I,J\) blocks.
This exact identity removes all background matrices, \(w\), \(\lambda\), and the common remaining edge family \(U\).

Put \(v=(Q\Delta A z)_J\).
Because \(\Pi_JQ=\Pi_J\),
\[
 \Pi_Jv=\Pi_J\Delta A_{JI}z_I.
 \tag{Endpoint-first-rectangular-input}
\]
For any fixed vector \(a\in\mathbb R^I\), the exact independent-coordinate covariance sum and the missing-edge degree bound give
\[
 \mathbb E\|\Pi_J\Delta A_{JI}a\|^2
 \ge\frac{\operatorname{rank}\Pi_J-N/32}{N}\|a\|^2
 \ge\frac14\|a\|^2
 \tag{Endpoint-rectangular-variance}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).
For the first inequality, each input coordinate loses at most \(N/32\) edges, each squared projected coordinate norm is at most one, and the full rectangular sum is \(\operatorname{rank}\Pi_J\|a\|^2\).
The second inequality uses \(|J|\ge n/3-1\), \(n\ge N-1\), and \(\operatorname{codim}Q\le n/256\).
The same calculation holds for \(\Pi_K\Delta B_{KJ}a\) with \(a\in\mathbb R^J\).

Each of these vector norms is convex and \(\|a\|/\sqrt N\)-Lipschitz in its independent raw difference coordinates.
Apply the accepted bounded-product convex concentration at support diameter \(4L\), followed by the same integrated median-error argument used in C187v13.
More explicitly, if \(F\) is either displayed rectangular norm and \(m_F\) is its median, integration of that concentration bound gives
\[
 \|F-m_F\|_{L^2}\le C_L\|a\|/\sqrt N,
 \qquad m_F\ge\sqrt{\mathbb EF^2}-C_L\|a\|/\sqrt N
                 \ge\|a\|/4
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Applying the lower-tail concentration at distance \(\|a\|/8\) from that median gives the following estimate.
The second-moment lower bound in (Endpoint-rectangular-variance) implies that, for a fixed \(a_* >0\), for example \(a_*=1/8\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\Pi_J\Delta A_{JI}a\|<a_*\|a\|\}
 \le C_Le^{-c_L'N},
 \tag{Endpoint-rectangular-lower-tail}
\]
uniformly for every nonzero fixed \(a\), and similarly for the \(K,J\) rectangle.
The concentration scale is a fixed multiple of \(L\|a\|/\sqrt N\), so the positive lower threshold and exponential rate are independent of the magnitude of \(a\).
No norm event for \(W\), its copies or the background matrices is imposed in this calculation.

The first application, with \(a=z_I\), gives
\[
 \|v\|\ge\|\Pi_Jv\|\ge a_*\|z\|/\sqrt3
\]
outside exponentially small probability.
Conditional on \(\Delta A\), the vector \(v\) is fixed and \(\Delta B\) is still independent.
Since \(\Pi_K\Delta B Q\Delta A z=\Pi_K\Delta B_{KJ}v\), the second application gives
\[
 \alpha\|\Pi_K\Delta B Q\Delta A z\|
 \ge\frac{\alpha a_*^2}{\sqrt3}\|z\|
 \tag{Endpoint-decoupled-vector-lower}
\]
outside another exponentially small probability.
This proves an exponentially small lower tail for the four-corner difference itself.

To pass back to the original vector, for fixed \(U\) let \(f(A,B')\) be the indicator of \(\|\Psi_\lambda(z,w)\|\le t\), and put \(p(U)=\mathbb E_{A,B'}f\).
Two applications of Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 p(U)^4
 \le\mathbb E\!\left[
 f(A^{(0)},B^{(0)})f(A^{(1)},B^{(0)})
 f(A^{(0)},B^{(1)})f(A^{(1)},B^{(1)})
 \right].
 \tag{Endpoint-four-cycle-probability}
\]
For completeness, integrate first over the two independent \(B\)-copies to obtain the square of \(\mathbb E_B[f(A^{(0)},B)f(A^{(1)},B)]\), apply Cauchy--Schwarz in the two \(A\)-variables, and then apply it to \(\mathbb E_A f(A,B)\) as a function of \(B\).
If all four indicators equal one, the norm of the projected four-corner difference is at most \(4t\).
Taking \(t=c_0\|z\|\), with
\[
 c_0:=\frac{\alpha a_*^2}{8\sqrt3},
\]
puts this upper bound strictly below the threshold in (Endpoint-decoupled-vector-lower).
Integrating over the common family \(U\) and using \((\mathbb E_U p(U))^4\le\mathbb E_U p(U)^4\) proves (Endpoint-fixed-parameter-small-ball), after taking a fourth root and decreasing the exponential rate.
Thus fixing the other edges for the four-copy argument does not leave a hidden dependence on a favorable choice of those edges.

<a id="endpoint-entropy-and-compression"></a>
### Parameter order and the small-space entropy budget

Fix the desired \(d>0\) and \(K_0\) first.
Choose the raw cutoff \(L\), its conditional norm constant \(B_L\), the common tail Frobenius constant \(C_\Theta\), and the rectangular exponential rate \(c_L\) above.
These choices do not depend on a tail spectral threshold or on the small-space dimension budget.
Fix \(K_C>2\).
For a prospective small \(\kappa>0\), set
\[
 H(\kappa):=\max\{1,4\sqrt{C_\Theta/\kappa}\},\qquad
 B(\kappa):=\max\{1,K_C+\alpha(H(\kappa)+B_L)+K_0\}.
\]
On the common event \(\|\Theta_N\|_F^2\le C_\Theta N\), the number of tail eigenvalues outside \([-H,H]\) is at most \(\kappa N/16\).
The first-input weak law and principal interlacing give the same upper count outside \([-K_C,K_C]\), with probability tending to one for each fixed \(\kappa\).
Take
\[
 Q=\text{projection onto }
 \operatorname{Ran}\mathbf1_{[-K_C,K_C]}(C)
 \cap\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf1_{[-H,H]}(\Theta).
\]
Then \(q:=\operatorname{codim}Q\le\kappa n/4\) eventually, and on \(\|W_N\|\le B_L\) it satisfies (Endpoint-compressed-bounds) with \(B=B(\kappa)\).
In particular \(B(\kappa)=O(1+\kappa^{-1/2})\), with fixed law- and energy-dependent constants.

Use a \(\delta\)-net with centers in the annulus \(\{z\in V_0:d/2\le\|z\|\le1\}\), another \(\delta\)-net in the unit ball of \(V_0\), and a deterministic \(\delta_\lambda\)-grid of \([-K_0,K_0]\), where
\[
 \delta:=\frac{c_0d}{32(B^2+B+1)},\qquad
 \delta_\lambda:=\frac{c_0d}{32(B+1)}.
 \tag{Endpoint-net-meshes}
\]
A maximal separated set supplies each vector net with at most \((1+2/\delta)^q\) points and keeps its first-parameter centers of norm at least \(d/2\).
An empty annulus requires no net and makes the endpoint implication impossible already.
The energy grid has at most \(2+2K_0/\delta_\lambda\) points.
All these nets are chosen from the conditioned data before exposing \(W\).

On the actual norm event, the Lipschitz bound in \((z,w)\) is
\[
 \|\Psi_\lambda(z,w)-\Psi_\lambda(\widetilde z,\widetilde w)\|
 \le B^2\|z-\widetilde z\|+B\|w-\widetilde w\|.
\]
For \(z,w\in V_0\), the identities \(Qz=Qw=0\) additionally give
\[
 \Psi_\lambda(z,w)-\Psi_\mu(z,w)
 =(\lambda-\mu)QB_\mu z,
\]
so its norm is at most \(B|\lambda-\mu|\) when \(\|z\|\le1\).
The total net approximation error is therefore at most \(c_0d/16\).
Consequently a parameter triple with \(\|\Psi_\lambda(z,w)\|\le c_0d/4\) would give a net triple with norm at most \(5c_0d/16<c_0\|\widetilde z\|\), to which (Endpoint-fixed-parameter-small-ball) applies.

Choose \(\kappa\le1/64\) sufficiently small that
\[
 \frac\kappa2\log(1+2/\delta(\kappa))<c_L/2.
 \tag{Endpoint-entropy-choice}
\]
This is possible because \(d\) was fixed first, \(B(\kappa)=O(1+\kappa^{-1/2})\), and the left side tends to zero as \(\kappa\downarrow0\).
Choose \(\kappa\) simultaneously small enough that \(\kappa\log(3/\theta)<c_{\rm lin}/4\), so the two-column argument below uses this same compression.
Its own later spectral-window and residual constants are chosen only afterward.
This parameter choice does not require the tail Frobenius density to dominate a cutoff-dependent entropy constant.

The union bound over the two vector nets and the energy grid now gives conditional failure at most \(Ce^{-cN}\) for
\[
 \inf_{\substack{z,w\in V_0\\d/2\le\|z\|\le1,\ \|w\|\le1\\|\lambda|\le K_0}}
 \|\Psi_\lambda(z,w)\|>c_0d/4
 \tag{Endpoint-uniform-quadratic-gap}
\]
on \(\|W_N\|\le B_L\).
The norm event supplies only the Lipschitz extension from the nets.
It was not part of the conditioning or of any of the four copied coordinate laws, and its own exponentially small failure is added separately.

<a id="first-column-exclusion"></a>
### Exclusion of the first small column

Let
\[
 M_B:=B^3+B^2+3B+2,
\]
and choose a fixed \(\eta_1>0\) such that
\[
 \eta_1\le\min\left\{\frac d4,\frac{d}{4(B+1)},
                            \frac{c_0d}{8M_B}\right\}.
\]
If \(\|u_1\|\le\eta_1\) and \(\rho\le\eta_1\), (Endpoint-parameter-size) gives \(\|z\|>d/2\), while (Endpoint-quadratic-residual) gives
\[
 \|\Psi_\lambda(z,w)\|\le M_B\eta_1\le c_0d/8.
\]
This contradicts (Endpoint-uniform-quadratic-gap).
Hence \(\|u_1\|>\eta_1\) for every normalized approximate mode in the stated energy range satisfying \(s_2(V)\ge d\) and residual at most \(\eta_1\), on the event just constructed.

The full first-input high-energy count and full tail Frobenius bound contribute one common \(p_N=o(1)\), outside which every full or single-deletion minor has the required compression dimension.
The mask event, conditional norm event and net estimates have exponentially small errors, and a union over the \(N+1\) minors preserves that scale.
Thus the first-column conclusion holds with failure at most \(p_N+Ce^{-cN}\), still under the original input laws and conditioning.
The constants are fixed after \(d,K_0\) and the laws, independently of \(N\).


<a id="remaining-two-column-estimates"></a>
## The other two columns under the same permitted conditioning

Throughout these two tests, take \(\|V\|_F=1\), \(s_2(V)\ge d\), and the stated residual equations with \(\|r_j\|\le\varepsilon\).

<a id="two-column-fixed-spaces"></a>
### One fixed spectral grid and the two new projections

Choose \(h>0\) so that every interval of length \(2h\) has semicircle mass at most \(\kappa/8\).
A finite grid \(a_j\) covering \([-K_0,K_0]\) will have mesh at most \(\tau\), where the positive constant \(\tau\) is chosen below after \(h\).
The scalar weak law and principal interlacing used in C187v13 imply, on one common first-input event of probability tending to one, that
\[
 \dim\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf1_{\{|C-a_jI|\le h\}}
 \le\kappa n/2
\]
for every grid point and every permitted minor.
The grid is deterministic and finite, so its eventual choice introduces no circular dependence on this weak-law event.

Define the bounded spectral function and fixed subspace
\[
 A_j=(C-a_jI)^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{|C-a_jI|>h\}},\qquad
 E_j=\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf1_{\{|C-a_jI|\le h\}}+A_jV_0.
\]
Then \(\|A_j\|\le h^{-1}\), \(\dim E_j\le3\kappa n/4\), and the exact spectral decomposition gives, for every vector \(u\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(u,E_j)
 \le h^{-1}\|Q(C-a_jI)u\|.
 \tag{Two-column-spectral-preimage}
\]
Indeed decompose \(u\) into its low-window component, \(A_j(I-Q)(C-a_jI)u\), and \(A_jQ(C-a_jI)u\); the first two terms belong to \(E_j\).
This identity does not commute \(Q\) past \(C\).

For the third-column test, let \(P_j^{(3)}\) be the projection onto
\[
 \operatorname{Ran}Q\cap
 (E_j+V_0+CV_0+\Theta V_0)^\perp.
\]
Its codimension is at most \(\dim E_j+4q\le7\kappa n/4\).
It annihilates \(E_j,V_0,CV_0,\Theta V_0\), and hence
\[
 P_j^{(3)}(C+D-\lambda I)z=P_j^{(3)}Wz
 \quad(z\in V_0).
 \tag{Two-column-third-noise-identity}
\]
Apply (Two-column-fixed-subspace-noise) with \(E=V_0\) and \(P=P_j^{(3)}\).

For the second-column test, put \(F_j:=E_j+V_0\), and let \(P_j^{(2)}\) project onto
\[
 \operatorname{Ran}Q\cap(F_j+CF_j+\Theta F_j)^\perp.
\]
Now \(\dim F_j\le\kappa n\) and \(\operatorname{codim}P_j^{(2)}\le q+3\dim F_j\le13\kappa n/4\).
This projection satisfies
\[
 P_j^{(2)}(C+\alpha D-\lambda I)z
 =\alpha P_j^{(2)}Wz\quad(z\in F_j).
 \tag{Two-column-second-noise-identity}
\]
Apply (Two-column-fixed-subspace-noise) with \(E=F_j\) and \(P=P_j^{(2)}\).
Both projections, both tested spaces and the grid are fixed before the remaining coordinates of \(W\) are exposed.
All projected operator norms used to compare nearby vectors are bounded by \(B\), because these projections have range contained in \(\operatorname{Ran}Q\).

<a id="third-column-exclusion"></a>
### Excluding a small third column

Suppose \(\|u_3\|\le b\), and choose a grid point with \(|\lambda-a_j|\le\tau\).
Set
\[
 r:=Bb+\varepsilon,\qquad s:=r+\varepsilon+\tau.
\]
The third equation and (Endpoint-compressed-bounds) give \(\|Qu_2\|\le r\).
The first equation then gives \(\|Q(C-a_jI)u_1\|\le s\), because \(\|u_1\|\le1\).
Let \(z_2\) be the orthogonal projection of \(u_2\) onto \(V_0\), and let \(v_1\) be that of \(u_1\) onto \(E_j\).
Then
\[
 \|u_2-z_2\|\le r,\qquad \|u_1-v_1\|\le s/h.
\]
Apply \(P_j^{(3)}\) to the second residual equation, using its annihilation of \(v_1\) and (Two-column-third-noise-identity), to obtain
\[
 \|P_j^{(3)}Wz_2\|
 \le Br+s/h+b+\varepsilon.
 \tag{Two-column-third-upper}
\]
The rank-one approximation \([u_1\ 0\ 0]\) to \(V\) gives
\[
 d\le s_2(V)\le\sqrt{\|u_2\|^2+\|u_3\|^2},\qquad
 \|z_2\|\ge\sqrt{d^2-b^2}-r.
\]
Here the first displayed inequality records the input \(s_2(V)\ge d\), while the second follows from the operator-norm approximation bound and its Frobenius upper bound.
Consequently sufficiently small fixed \(b,\varepsilon,\tau\) make \(\|z_2\|\ge d/2\) and the right side of (Two-column-third-upper) less than \(\Gamma d/2\), contradicting (Two-column-fixed-subspace-noise).

<a id="second-column-exclusion"></a>
### Excluding a small second column

Suppose instead that \(\|u_2\|\le b\), and choose \(a_j\) as above.
The first residual equation gives
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(u_1,E_j)\le a:=(b+\varepsilon+\tau)/h.
\]
The second equation gives \(\|Q(u_1+u_3)\|\le r:=Bb+\varepsilon\).
Choose the orthogonal projection \(v_1\) of \(u_1\) onto \(E_j\).
The vector
\[
 -v_1+(I-Q)(u_1+u_3)\in F_j
\]
is within \(a+r\) of \(u_3\).
Thus the orthogonal projection \(z_3\) of \(u_3\) onto \(F_j\) satisfies
\[
 \|u_3-z_3\|\le e:=a+r.
\]
Applying \(P_j^{(2)}\) to the third residual equation and using (Two-column-second-noise-identity) yields
\[
 \alpha\|P_j^{(2)}Wz_3\|\le Be+b+\varepsilon.
 \tag{Two-column-second-upper}
\]
The same rank-one approximation \([u_1\ 0\ 0]\) now gives
\[
 \|u_3\|\ge\sqrt{d^2-b^2},\qquad
 \|z_3\|\ge\sqrt{d^2-b^2}-e.
\]
Again sufficiently small fixed \(b,\varepsilon,\tau\) contradict (Two-column-fixed-subspace-noise).

### Simultaneous constants and original-law probability

Here is one common choice that makes both contradictions quantitative.
After fixing \(L,B_L,\kappa,H,K_C,B,h\), define
\[
 E_*:=3/h+B+1,\qquad
 A_3:=B(B+1)+(B+3)/h+2,\qquad
 A_2:=BE_*+2.
\]
Choose a fixed positive \(\eta_2\) with
\[
 \eta_2\le\min\left\{
 \frac d4,\frac{d}{4(B+1)},\frac{d}{4E_*},
 \frac{\Gamma d}{4A_3},\frac{\alpha\Gamma d}{4A_2}
 \right\},
\]
and set \(b_2=\varepsilon_2=\tau=\eta_2\).
Use \(b=b_2\) in the preceding tests.
For \(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_2\), the third-column test has \(r\le(B+1)\eta_2\), \(s\le(B+3)\eta_2\) and upper bound at most \(A_3\eta_2\le\Gamma d/4\).
The second-column test has \(e\le E_*\eta_2\) and upper bound at most \(A_2\eta_2\le\alpha\Gamma d/4\).
In both tests the projected vector has norm at least \(\sqrt{15}d/4-d/4>d/2\), giving a contradiction with margin.
If a tested space is zero, this same positive norm bound is already impossible.

Now take the deterministic finite \(\tau\)-grid and the corresponding first-input spectral-window event.
The full first-input high-energy and grid-window counts and the full tail Frobenius bound contribute one common \(p_N=o(1)\), before the union over minors.
The missing-edge event and conditional bounded norm have exponentially small failure.
For each conditioned minor, the finite collection of fixed spaces and projections above has exponentially small failure by the same covariance, convex concentration and sphere-net calculation as C187v13.
A union over the fixed grid and \(N+1\) full or single-deletion minors preserves an exponentially small error after decreasing its rate.
This proves the stated conditional two-column estimate uniformly over all the vectors in (Combined-mode-input), even though a vector subsequently selected as an actual mode depends on \(W\).
The uniform fixed-space estimates were established before making that selection.
No full operator-norm bound on either original finite-variance input, independence of a mixed matrix, or conditioning on a good event for the fresh coordinates has been substituted into the model.


<a id="combined-Gram-geometry"></a>
## From three column bounds to a strict combined Gram bound

We first give the complete deterministic conversion.
Let \(\|V\|_F=1\), \(s_2(V)\ge d\), and \(\min_j\|u_j\|\ge m\), with \(0<d\le1\).
Put
\[
 q_G:=\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(V^TV+TV^TVT)},\qquad
 \delta_T:=\sqrt2-1.
\]
If \(q_G\ge d/2\), the desired lower bound below is immediate.
Otherwise choose a unit minimizing vector \(a\in\mathbb R^3\) and a unit smallest right singular vector \(b\) of \(V\).
Then
\[
 \|Va\|^2+\|VTa\|^2=q_G^2,\qquad s_3(V)\le q_G.
\]
On \(b^\perp\), the norm of \(V\) from below is at least \(s_2(V)\ge d\).
Consequently
\[
 \|P_{b^\perp}a\|\le q_G/d,\qquad
 \|P_{b^\perp}Ta\|\le q_G/d,\qquad
 |\langle a,b\rangle|\ge\sqrt3/2.
\]
Decompose \(a=\langle a,b\rangle b+a_\perp\) and use \(\|T\|=\sqrt2\) to obtain
\[
 \|P_{b^\perp}Tb\|
 \le\frac{2(1+\sqrt2)}{\sqrt3\,d}\,q_G.
\]
Choose \(k\) with \(b_k^2=\max_jb_j^2\ge1/3\).
For \((t_1,t_2,t_3)=(0,1,\sqrt2)\), the diagonal variance identity gives
\[
 \|P_{b^\perp}Tb\|^2
 =\sum_{i<j}(t_i-t_j)^2b_i^2b_j^2
 \ge\frac{\delta_T^2}{3}(1-b_k^2).
\]
Since \(\|V\|\le1\), decompose \(e_k=b_kb+P_{b^\perp}e_k\) to get
\[
 \|u_k\|=\|Ve_k\|
 \le s_3(V)+\sqrt{1-b_k^2}
 \le\left(1+\frac{2(1+\sqrt2)}{d\delta_T}\right)q_G
 \le\frac{13}{d}q_G.
\]
The last step uses \(d\le1\) and \(1+2(1+\sqrt2)/\delta_T=7+4\sqrt2<13\).
Thus \(q_G\ge dm/13\) in this second case, and in all cases
\[
 G(V)\succeq\min\{d^2/4,d^2m^2/169\}\,I_3.
 \tag{Combined-deterministic-Gram}
\]
This conversion is independent of the input matrices and uses no full operator norm or mode equation.

## Original-law event intersection and the whole low spectral subspace

Let \(\eta_1>0\) be the first-column tolerance and lower bound obtained above, and let \(b_2>0\) and \(\varepsilon_2>0\) be the corresponding common second- and third-column constants.
Take
\[
 \rho:=\min(\rho_0,\eta_1,\varepsilon_2),\qquad
 m:=\min(\eta_1,b_2),\qquad
 g:=\min\{d^2/4,d^2m^2/169\}.
\]
The C187v17 event \(\Omega_{\rm rank,N}\) gives \(s_2(V)\ge d\) to every normalized mode with residual at most \(\rho\).

This proves (Combined-mode-input), simultaneously for all required minors and all the stated centers and vectors.
No cross-size independence or summable probability estimate was used.

For the whole-subspace assertion, take a nonzero \(h\in\mathcal H\) and normalize it to unit norm.

 V_h=[U_1h\ U_2h\ U_3h]

 \|\mathcal F_{\lambda_0}(V_h)\|_F

The already simultaneous approximate-mode result therefore applies to every such \(h\), without selecting an eigenbasis or taking an additional union bound.

 \langle\widetilde U_i h,\widetilde U_jh\rangle

so the combined-map Gram is exactly \(G(V_h)\).
Homogeneity restores the factor \(\|h\|^2\), and \(h=0\) is immediate.
This proves (Combined-whole-low-subspace).
