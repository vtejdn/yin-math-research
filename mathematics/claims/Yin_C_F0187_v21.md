# Three-channel low-rank raw layers and a product-scale third-pole gap

This is a fixed conditional theorem for the real three-channel path Schur polynomial.
It supplies three conditional quantitative raw coefficient estimates and an explicit conditional transfer for the sixth layer.
The complete argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v21.md#whole-proof-finding), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-three-channel-low-rank-reviewed).
The hypotheses do not assert spectral-branch coverage or product-scale physical spread in the original random model.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact conditional conclusions

Keep the fixed slopes and path corner
\[
 \alpha=\sqrt2,\qquad (t_1,t_2,t_3)=(0,1,\alpha),\qquad
 T=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2,t_3),\qquad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
There is no arbitrary-coefficient or complex-input extension in this statement.
Fix \(n\ge1\) and a real symmetric \(3n\)-by-\(3n\) matrix \(Z\) with \(\|Z\|>0\).
In the original-model application \(Z=M^{-1}\) for an invertible retained minor \(M\), but the deterministic assertions only use the displayed physical coordinates and hypotheses.
Write its signed spectral decomposition as
\[
 Z=\sum_j\lambda_j w_jw_j^T,\qquad
 q_j=|\lambda_j|,\qquad q_1\ge q_2\ge\cdots,\qquad Q=q_1.
\]
Indices run through \(1,\ldots,3n\), and \(q_j=0\) is understood for larger indices.
The \(w_j\) are real orthonormal vectors in \(\mathbb R^3\otimes\mathbb R^n\).
In auxiliary-first order write \(w_j=(u_{1j},u_{2j},u_{3j})\).
Keep both original fresh normalized borders \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\), put \(z=(x,y)\), and define
\[
 R(z)e_a=e_a\otimes(x+t_a y),\qquad
 (t_1,t_2,t_3)=(0,1,\alpha),\qquad B_Z(z)=R(z)^TZR(z).
\]
For real \(a,b,\theta\), the exposed-corner form is
\[
 S_i=K+\operatorname{diag}(a+t_1b,\ a+t_2b,\ a+t_3b)+\theta I_3,
\]
where \(a,b\) are the two original normalized diagonal entries in the random application and \(\theta=0\) unless a common scalar translation is used.
A scalar translation of the whole parent adds the same \(\theta\) to this corner and to its retained minor; it is not a fresh random input.
The true Schur polynomial is
\[
 p_3(z)=\det(S_i-B_Z(z)).
\]
For symmetric three-by-three matrices \(S,B\), expansion over columns gives
\[
 \det(S-B)=\det S-\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}S\,B)
                     +\operatorname{tr}(S\operatorname{adj}B)-\det B.
\]
Consequently its homogeneous parts are exactly
\[
 H_0=\det S_i,\quad H_2=-\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}S_i\,B_Z),
 \quad H_4=\operatorname{tr}(S_i\operatorname{adj}B_Z),
 \quad H_6=-\det B_Z.
 \tag{Three-low-full-parts}
\]
These are parts of the genuine Schur polynomial, not polynomials formed by replacing the input laws.

Define the combined mode maps and evaluation vectors by
\[
 \widetilde U_a e_j=(u_{aj},t_a u_{aj}),\qquad
 r_j(z)=(\langle\widetilde U_1e_j,z\rangle,
         \langle\widetilde U_2e_j,z\rangle,
         \langle\widetilde U_3e_j,z\rangle)^T.
\]
Then
\[
 B_Z(z)=\sum_j\lambda_j r_j(z)r_j(z)^T,\qquad
 \|R(z)\|\le\sqrt3\,\|z\|.
 \tag{Three-low-mode-expansion}
\]
On every spectral span the original stacked component map is isometric, so
\[
 I\preceq S_{\rm cmb}:=\sum_a\widetilde U_a^*\widetilde U_a\preceq3I.
 \tag{Three-low-stack-bounds}
\]
In the conditional statements below, the required head-mode span additionally satisfies
\[
 [\langle\widetilde U_a h,\widetilde U_b h\rangle]_{a,b=1}^3
       \succeq g\|h\|^2I_3,\qquad 0<g\le1.
 \tag{Three-low-head-Gram}
\]
It is required for every vector in that span, not only for its eigenbasis.

Write \(\mathcal E_k=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_k\}\) for the first \(k\) mode coordinates.
For each fixed \(g\in(0,1]\), there are constants
\[
 \gamma_1(g),\varepsilon_1(g),\gamma_2(g),\varepsilon_2(g),
 b_4(g),b_3(g),\delta_3(g)>0
\]
independent of \(n,N,Z,a,b,\theta\) and all eigenvalue signs, with the following three conclusions.
The first uses (Three-low-head-Gram) on \(\mathcal E_1\), the second on \(\mathcal E_2\), and the third on \(\mathcal E_3\).

1. If \(q_2\le\gamma_1q_1\) and \(\|S_i-K\|\le\varepsilon_1\), then
   \[
    \sup_{\|z\|=1}|H_2(z)|\ge gq_1.
   \]
2. If \(q_2>0\), \(q_3\le\gamma_2q_2\), and \(\|S_i-K\|\le\varepsilon_2\), then
   \[
    \sup_{\|z\|=1}|H_4(z)|\ge \tfrac12 b_4(g)q_1q_2.
   \]
3. If \(q_3>0\) and \(q_4\le\delta_3(g)q_3\), then, without any condition on \(S_i\),
   \[
    \sup_{\|z\|=1}|H_6(z)|\ge \tfrac12 b_3(g)q_1q_2q_3.
   \]

The arguments below define positive \(b_4,b_3\) at fixed finite-dimensional parameters and permit the explicit threshold choices
\[
 \gamma_1=g/8,\quad \varepsilon_1=g/72,\quad
 \gamma_2=\min\{1,b_4/324\},\quad
 \varepsilon_2=\min\{1,b_4/108\},\quad
 \delta_3=\min\{1,b_3/378\}.
 \tag{Three-low-thresholds}
\]
The symmetric arithmetic-average order-\(d\) tensor \(\mathscr T_d\) representing \(H_d\) has Frobenius norm at least the corresponding displayed lower bound, since
\(H_d(z)=\langle\mathscr T_d,z^{\otimes d}\rangle\).
No lower bound on \(q_2/q_1\) is needed in the quartic conclusion, and neither \(q_2/q_1\) nor \(q_3/q_1\) is bounded below in the sixth conclusion.
No effective-rank upper bound or Frobenius tail bound is required for these three raw conclusions.

There is also the following conditional sixth-layer coefficient statement.
Let \(N\ge n\) be an integer.
Use original normalization \(z=\epsilon/\sqrt N\), where the \(2n\) coordinates of \(\epsilon\) are formal independent signs, with physical group \(i\) consisting of the two variables \(x_i,y_i\).
Put
\[
 F=\|Z\|_F,\quad P_i=I_3\otimes e_ie_i^T,\quad
 d_i=\|P_iZ\|_F^2,\quad d_*=\max_i d_i,\quad
 \mathcal D=\sum_i\|P_iZP_i\|_F^2,\quad A_*=q_1q_2q_3.
\]
Let \(\widehat p_3(J)\) denote the Walsh coefficient for a set of individual variables \(J\), and define the complete sixth-layer energy and physical-group loads by
\[
 \sigma_6^2=\sum_{|J|=6}|\widehat p_3(J)|^2,\qquad
 L_i^{(6)}=\sum_{\substack{|J|=6\\J\cap\{x_i,y_i\}\ne\varnothing}}
                    |\widehat p_3(J)|^2.
\]
Along any sequence satisfying the third conclusion at fixed \(g\) and
\[
 F^4(\mathcal D+d_*)=o(A_*^2),
 \tag{Three-low-transfer-condition}
\]
one has \(\sigma_6^2\ge c_g A_*^2/N^6\) eventually and
\(\max_i L_i^{(6)}/\sigma_6^2\to0\).
The physically distinct subenergy already has this lower bound.
For every real \(3n\)-by-three boundary matrix \(\mathcal B\) with \(\|\mathcal B\|\le B_0\),
\[
 \sqrt{\det(I_3+\mathcal B^TZ^2\mathcal B)}
 \le\prod_{j=1}^3\sqrt{1+B_0^2q_j^2}
 \le C_{B_0}\prod_{j=1}^3(1+q_j).
\]
Thus when \(q_3\ge1\), the conditional coefficient scale \(\sigma_6\) is at least a constant times this graph area divided by \(N^3\), uniformly on the stated boundary ball.
This is not a small-ball or graph-distance probability conclusion.
For non-Rademacher input laws the sign substitution is a formal coefficient calculation, not a representation of their conditional border law.
The complete lower Walsh layers can contain cancellations from higher homogeneous degrees, so the first two raw bounds do not assert corresponding second- or fourth-layer Walsh energy bounds.

## Complete argument

<a id="quadratic-head"></a>
### A quantitative quadratic homogeneous part near rank one

Let \(c=(1,0,-1)^T\).
Direct cofactors give
\[
 \operatorname{adj}K=-cc^T,\qquad \det K=0.
\]
For \(S_i=K\),
\[
 H_2(z)=c^TB_Z(z)c.
\]
Suppose the first mode belongs to a span satisfying (Three-low-head-Gram).
The vector
\(v_c=\widetilde U_1e_1-\widetilde U_3e_1\) has
\(\|v_c\|^2\ge2g\).
Writing \(E_1=Z-\lambda_1w_1w_1^T\), so \(\|E_1\|=q_2\), gives
\[
 H_2(z)=\lambda_1\langle v_c,z\rangle^2
                       +c^TR(z)^TE_1R(z)c.
\]
For \(\|z\|=1\), one has
\(\|R(z)c\|^2=\|x\|^2+\|x+\alpha y\|^2\le4\).
Hence
\[
 \sup_{\|z\|=1}|H_2(z)|\ge2gQ-4q_2
 \quad(S_i=K).
\]
For any two real three-by-three matrices, the exterior-square representation of the adjugate and the tensor product difference give
\[
 \|\operatorname{adj}A-\operatorname{adj}B\|
 \le(\|A\|+\|B\|)\|A-B\|.
 \tag{Three-low-adjugate-difference}
\]
If \(\varepsilon=\|S_i-K\|\le1\), then \(\|K\|=\sqrt2\) gives
\(\|\operatorname{adj}S_i-\operatorname{adj}K\|\le4\varepsilon\).
Since \(\|B_Z(z)\|\le3Q\) on the unit sphere, the trace error is at most \(36\varepsilon Q\).
The choices \(\gamma_1=g/8\) and \(\varepsilon_1=g/72\) therefore give
\[
 q_2\le\gamma_1 Q,\quad \|S_i-K\|\le\varepsilon_1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{\|z\|=1}|H_2(z)|\ge gQ.
 \tag{Three-low-quadratic-raw}
\]
The arithmetic-average symmetric order-two tensor representing \(H_2\) therefore has Frobenius norm at least \(gQ\).
This is a raw homogeneous coefficient bound; it is not yet the degree-two Walsh energy of \(p_3\).

<a id="quartic-head"></a>
### A quantitative quartic homogeneous part near rank two

Let \(Z^{[2]}=\lambda_1w_1w_1^T+\lambda_2w_2w_2^T\), with \(q_2>0\), and assume (Three-low-head-Gram) on its two-dimensional mode span.
Put
\[
 w(z)=r_1(z)\times r_2(z).
\]
Cofactor expansion of the sum of two rank-one matrices gives
\[
 \operatorname{adj}B_{Z^{[2]}}(z)
       =\lambda_1\lambda_2 w(z)w(z)^T.
\]
The path matrix consequently gives the exact main term
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}B_{Z^{[2]}}(z))
       =2\lambda_1\lambda_2 w_2(z)(w_1(z)+w_3(z)).
 \tag{Three-low-quartic-main}
\]

Both quadratic factors are nonzero polynomials under the whole-span Gram assumption.
For \(w_2\), use the pair of maps \((\widetilde U_3,\widetilde U_1)\) restricted to this two-dimensional mode span.
For every nonzero \(h\), their two images are linearly independent.
Thus the square evaluation-matrix space
\[
 \left\{\,[\langle L_a e_j,z\rangle]_{a,j=1}^2:z\in\mathbb R^{2n}\,\right\}
\]
is transitive: its action on any nonzero \(h\in\mathbb R^2\) ranges over \(\mathbb R^2\).
The accepted real transitivity step in C187v16 supplies an invertible member, whose determinant is \(w_2(z)\).
For \(w_1+w_3\), use the maps
\[
 L_1=\widetilde U_2,\qquad L_2=(\widetilde U_3-\widetilde U_1)/\sqrt2.
\]
Their two auxiliary coefficient vectors are orthonormal, so their Gram also has lower bound \(g\|h\|^2I_2\).
Their evaluation determinant is \((w_1+w_3)/\sqrt2\), and the same argument applies.
The product of two nonzero real polynomials is nonzero.

This nonvanishing has a dimension-independent constant in the present rank-two problem.
Apply the following compactness to the weight-free polynomial \(2w_2(w_1+w_3)\), having factored out \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\).
Only the six vectors \(\widetilde U_a e_j\), \(a\le3,j\le2\), occur.
Their polynomial depends only on their span, and its unit-sphere supremum is unchanged by an isometric realization of their Gram matrix.
Their six-by-six Gram matrix is positive semidefinite and bounded by the stack bound, and the whole-span lower Gram inequalities are closed conditions on it.
These matrices therefore form a compact set.
Realize each such Gram matrix by the columns of its positive square root in \(\mathbb R^6\).
This square root is continuous, and so is the unit-sphere supremum of the resulting quartic polynomial on this fixed six-dimensional space.
That supremum is positive at every admissible Gram matrix by the preceding transitivity argument.
If the compact admissible set is nonempty, its minimum is therefore a fixed \(b_4(g)>0\).
If it is empty, take \(b_4(g)=1\); the quartic assertion is then vacuous.
Consequently
\[
 \sup_{\|z\|=1}
 \left|\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}B_{Z^{[2]}}(z))\right|
       \ge b_4(g)Qq_2.
 \tag{Three-low-quartic-uniform}
\]
This compactness uses a fixed six-vector Gram matrix, rather than assuming that C187v16's third-singular-value parameter may tend to zero.

Now \(\|Z-Z^{[2]}\|=q_3\).
On the unit sphere the corresponding Schur-matrix difference has norm at most \(3q_3\), while each full or truncated Schur matrix has norm at most \(3Q\).
By (Three-low-adjugate-difference),
\[
 \|\operatorname{adj}B_Z-\operatorname{adj}B_{Z^{[2]}}\|
       \le18Qq_3.
\]
Tracing against \(K\) bounds the resulting error by \(54\sqrt2 Qq_3\le81Qq_3\).
The corner perturbation has a sharper bound than \(O(\varepsilon Q^2)\).
Indeed split \(B_Z=\lambda_1r_1r_1^T+B_{E_1}\).
The first term has zero adjugate, and the quadratic expansion gives
\[
 \|\operatorname{adj}B_Z\|
 \le C(Qq_2+q_2^2)\le C'Qq_2
 \quad(\|z\|=1).
\]
More precisely, \(s_1(B_Z)\le3Q\) and \(s_2(B_Z)\le3q_2\) imply
\(\|\operatorname{adj}B_Z\|\le9Qq_2\), so tracing against \(S_i-K\) gives at most \(27\varepsilon Qq_2\).
Therefore
\[
 \sup_{\|z\|=1}
 \left|H_4(z)-\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}B_{Z^{[2]}}(z))\right|
       \le81Qq_3+27\varepsilon Qq_2.
\]
Choosing \(\gamma_2=\min\{1,b_4/324\}\) and \(\varepsilon_2=\min\{1,b_4/108\}\) proves
\[
 q_3\le\gamma_2q_2,\quad \|S_i-K\|\le\varepsilon_2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{\|z\|=1}|H_4(z)|\ge\tfrac12 b_4(g)Qq_2.
 \tag{Three-low-quartic-raw}
\]
The symmetric order-four tensor has at least this Frobenius norm.
No lower bound on \(q_2/Q\) was used.

For the original finite-second-moment diagonal laws, the small-corner requirement can itself be supplied in probability simultaneously over the parent indices.
For any fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
 N\mathbb P\{|\zeta|>\delta\sqrt N\}
 \le\delta^{-2}\mathbb E[\zeta^2\mathbf1_{\{|\zeta|>\delta\sqrt N\}}]
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
An ordinary union bound for the two diagonal families, and \(\theta_N\to0\) if used, gives \(\max_i\|S_i-K\|\to0\) in probability.
No summable rate or almost-sure uniform diagonal bound is asserted.

<a id="third-pole-gap"></a>
### A third-pole spectral gap gives the full raw sixth layer at the product scale

This branch is independent of the exposed corner \(S_i\).
Assume (Three-low-head-Gram) on the span of the first three inverse modes and \(q_3>0\).
Let
\[
 Z^{[3]}=\sum_{j=1}^3\lambda_jw_jw_j^T,\qquad
 \mathsf R_3(z)=[r_1(z)\ r_2(z)\ r_3(z)].
\]
The exact rank-three truncation has
\[
 \det B_{Z^{[3]}}(z)
 =\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3\,\det\mathsf R_3(z)^2.
 \tag{Three-low-rank-three-factorization}
\]
This is a statement about the truncation, not a claim that an invertible large physical minor has rank-three inverse.

The quantitative constant in this identity does not depend on the ratios \(q_2/Q\) or \(q_3/Q\).
Restrict the combined maps to this three-dimensional mode span, and put
\(S_{\rm cmb}=\sum_a\widetilde U_a^*\widetilde U_a\).
By (Three-low-stack-bounds), \(I\preceq S_{\rm cmb}\preceq3I\).
The maps \(\widehat U_a=\widetilde U_aS_{\rm cmb}^{-1/2}\) have an isometric stack and whole-span Gram lower bound \(g/3\).
Apply C187v16 to the identity spectral weight before normalization, which becomes the positive weight \(S_{\rm cmb}\) after this change of maps.
After dividing that weight by its norm, its squared Frobenius norm is at most \(3\), and its third singular value is at least \(1/3\).
Thus the fixed parameters in that theorem are
\[
 (p,g',K',d')=(3,g/3,3,1/3).
\]
The quadratic evaluation matrix is unchanged by this simultaneous transformation.
Writing \(b_3(g)>0\) for the resulting constant gives
\[
 \sup_{\|z\|=1}\det\mathsf R_3(z)^2\ge b_3(g),
 \qquad
 \sup_{\|z\|=1}|\det B_{Z^{[3]}}(z)|
       \ge b_3(g)q_1q_2q_3.
 \tag{Three-low-rank-three-uniform}
\]
Here C187v16 is used at fixed nondegenerate parameters on the identity weight, rather than at the possibly degenerating ratios of the actual inverse eigenvalues.

The tail estimate also respects this product scale.
For \(\|z\|=1\), put
\[
 B_0=B_{Z^{[3]}}(z),\qquad E=B_{Z-Z^{[3]}}(z).
\]
The singular-value product inequality and \(\|R(z)\|^2\le3\) imply
\[
 s_1(B_0)\le3q_1,\quad s_2(B_0)\le3q_2,\quad \|E\|\le3q_4.
\]
Set \(q_4=0\) when the spectral truncation has no tail.
For arbitrary three-by-three \(B_0,E\),
\[
 \det(B_0+E)-\det B_0
 =\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}B_0\,E)
  +\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}E\,B_0)+\det E.
\]
Since \(\|\operatorname{adj}B_0\|=s_1(B_0)s_2(B_0)\), the absolute value is bounded by
\[
 3s_1(B_0)s_2(B_0)\|E\|
       +3s_1(B_0)\|E\|^2+\|E\|^3.
\]
It follows uniformly on the unit sphere that
\[
 |\det B_Z-\det B_{Z^{[3]}}|
 \le81q_1q_2q_4+81q_1q_4^2+27q_4^3
 \le189q_1q_2q_4.
 \tag{Three-low-product-tail}
\]
Thus, for
\[
 \delta_3(g)=\min\{1,b_3(g)/378\},
\]
one has the genuine full-inverse raw bound
\[
 q_4\le\delta_3(g)q_3
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{\|z\|=1}|H_6(z)|
       \ge\tfrac12 b_3(g)q_1q_2q_3.
 \tag{Three-low-third-gap-sixth}
\]
The order-six symmetric tensor has at least this Frobenius norm.
The estimate permits arbitrary eigenvalue signs and uses no effective-rank upper bound, no \(q_3/Q\) lower bound, and no Frobenius bound on the tail.
It is still conditional on the actual head-mode Gram hypothesis and the displayed third-to-fourth spectral gap.

<a id="graph-and-collision"></a>
### Graph normalization, exact sign reduction, and what can be transferred now

For any real \(3n\)-by-three boundary matrix \(\mathcal B\) with \(\|\mathcal B\|\le B_0\), singular-value inequalities give
\[
 \sqrt{\det(I_3+\mathcal B^TZ^2\mathcal B)}
 \le\prod_{j=1}^3\sqrt{1+B_0^2q_j^2}
 \le C_{B_0}\prod_{j=1}^3(1+q_j).
 \tag{Three-low-graph-upper}
\]
This applies to the actual combined border \(R(x,y)\) on any fixed double-ball support.
When \(q_3\ge1\), the right side is at most a fixed multiple of \(q_1q_2q_3\).
Thus the third-gap sixth-layer raw scale is the one needed for a top-degree coefficient scale of graph area divided by \(N^3\).

For that sixth layer, the existing fixed local collision estimates give an explicit conditional conversion.
Put
\[
 F=\|Z\|_F,\quad P_i=I_3\otimes e_ie_i^T,\quad
 d_i=\|P_iZ\|_F^2,\quad d_*=\max_i d_i,\quad
 \mathcal D=\sum_i\|P_iZP_i\|_F^2,\quad A_*=q_1q_2q_3.
\]
Let \(\mathscr T_6\) represent \(H_6\).
C187v18's pure physical calculation gives
\[
 \|\mathscr T_{6,\rm rep}\|_F^2\le C F^4(\mathcal D+d_*),\qquad
 \sigma_{6,\rm phys}^2=\frac{720}{N^6}
                             \|\mathscr T_{6,\rm dist}\|_F^2,\qquad
 L_i^{(6)}\le C F^4d_i/N^6.
 \tag{Three-low-product-collision-interface}
\]
Here \(\mathscr T_{6,\rm rep}\) is the restriction to ordered six-tuples with at least one repeated physical position, and \(\mathscr T_{6,\rm dist}\) is its orthogonal complement.
The quantity \(\sigma_{6,\rm phys}^2\) sums the coefficient squares with six distinct physical positions and hence is at most \(\sigma_6^2\).
The load bound is for the complete sixth layer, including terms using both variables from one physical position.
The local quadratic-factor tensor for indices \(a,b\) is the real symmetric coefficient matrix of the quadratic polynomial \((B_Z(z))_{ab}\).
To see the exact scope of the reused calculation, each local quadratic-factor tensor has Frobenius norm at most \(3F\), squared row-group norm at most \(9d_i\), and summed squared same-group block norm at most \(9\mathcal D\).
