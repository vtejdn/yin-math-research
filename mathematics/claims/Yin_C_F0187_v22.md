# Mixed head and residual determinant coefficients for separated inverse scales

This theorem treats a finite spectral head and a signed residual on their own scales.
It gives a raw determinant bound for residual clusters of bounded effective rank without requiring a gap after the third inverse singular value.
It does not supply physical-coordinate coefficient spread or an original-model probability estimate.
The main-agent argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v22.md#mixed-head-residual-reviewed), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-mixed-head-residual-reviewed).

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact conditional assertions

Fix integers \(p\ge2\) and \(1\le k<p\).
Let \(\mathcal H,\mathcal E\) be real Hilbert spaces and let \(U_1,\ldots,U_p:\mathcal H\to\mathcal E\) be bounded linear maps.
Write \(U(a)=\sum_i a_iU_i\), and for \(x\in\mathcal E\) define
\[
 S(x)h=(\langle U_ih,x\rangle)_{i=1}^p.
\]
Let \(J:\mathbb R^k\to\mathcal H\) be an isometry and let \(A=A^*\) be bounded with \(AJ=0\).
Put
\[
 F(x)=S(x)J,\qquad B_A(x)=S(x)AS(x)^T,
\]
\[
 M_{A,J}(x)=[t^k]\det(B_A(x)+tF(x)F(x)^T).
 \tag{Mixed-head-coefficient}
\]
Here \([t^k]\) denotes the coefficient of \(t^k\); the determinant has degree at most \(k\) in \(t\).
This coefficient is homogeneous of degree \(2p\) in \(x\) and of degree \(p-k\) in \(A\).

Suppose that \(\dim\overline{\operatorname{ran}A}\ge p-k\) and each \(U(a)\), \(a\ne0\), is injective on
\[
 \mathcal H'=\operatorname{ran}J\oplus\overline{\operatorname{ran}A}.
\]
Then
\[
 M_{A,J}\not\equiv0.
 \tag{Mixed-head-exact}
\]
No positivity, compactness or bounded inverse of the residual is assumed for this assertion.

For the quantitative assertion fix \(g,\Lambda>0\), \(K\ge1\) and \(0<d\le1\).
There is \(b=b(p,k,g,\Lambda,K,d)>0\) such that, in every finite dimension, the hypotheses
\[
 \sum_iU_i^*U_i\preceq\Lambda I,\qquad
 [\langle U_ih,U_jh\rangle]_{i,j=1}^p\succeq g\|h\|^2I_p,
 \quad h\in\mathcal H,
\]
\[
 J^*J=I_k,\quad AJ=0,\quad A=A^*,\quad
 \|A\|=1,\quad\|A\|_F^2\le K,\quad s_{p-k}(A)\ge d
 \tag{Mixed-head-uniform-inputs}
\]
imply
\[
 \sup_{\|x\|=1}|M_{A,J}(x)|\ge b.
 \tag{Mixed-head-uniform}
\]
The lower Gram hypothesis is on the whole space, not only on an eigenbasis.
The constant is independent of both dimensions and all signs of the nonzero residual eigenvalues.
If the fixed parameters admit no tuple the implication is vacuous.

Here is its separated-scale consequence.
Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_k\) be orthonormal head vectors and let
\[
 Z=\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_je_je_j^T+A,\qquad Ae_j=0,
 \quad q_j=|\lambda_j|>0,\qquad r=\|A\|>0.
\]
Assume the same map bounds, \(\|A\|_F^2\le Kr^2\), and \(s_{p-k}(A)\ge dr\).
There is \(\delta>0\), depending only on the displayed fixed parameters, such that
\[
 r\le\delta\min_{1\le j\le k}q_j
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{\|x\|=1}|\det(S(x)ZS(x)^T)|
 \ge\frac b2\left(\prod_{j=1}^kq_j\right)r^{p-k}.
 \tag{Mixed-head-separated}
\]
The head eigenvalues need not be mutually comparable and may have either sign.

For the physical three-channel combined maps, \(\Lambda=3\) is permitted.
Ordering the absolute inverse eigenvalues as \(q_1\ge q_2\ge\cdots\), the consequence covers each of the following conditional branches:

- A one-mode head, with \(q_2\le\delta q_1\), \(q_3\ge dq_2\), and \(\sum_{j\ge2}q_j^2\le Kq_2^2\), gives a raw sixth-degree lower bound of size \(q_1q_2^2\), and hence of size \(q_1q_2q_3\).
- A two-mode head, with \(q_3\le\delta q_2\) and \(\sum_{j\ge3}q_j^2\le Kq_3^2\), gives a raw sixth-degree lower bound of size \(q_1q_2q_3\); here \(d=1\).

Both assertions require the whole-space combined Gram hypothesis for the spectral support being used.
Neither requires \(q_4/q_3\) to be small.
They do not cover residual effective rank tending to infinity at its own scale.
They do not imply lower bounds for distinct-physical-coordinate coefficients, complete-layer loads, graph distances, least singular values or logarithmic determinants.

## Complete candidate argument

<a id="saddle-identity"></a>
### A bordered symmetric matrix identifies the mixed coefficient

For a symmetric \(p\)-by-\(p\) matrix \(B\) and a \(p\)-by-\(k\) matrix \(F\), put
\[
 \mathcal K(B,F)=\begin{pmatrix}B&F\\F^T&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
 [t^k]\det(B+tFF^T)=(-1)^k\det\mathcal K(B,F).
 \tag{Mixed-head-saddle-identity}
\]
For full-column-rank \(F\), choose an orthonormal basis in \(\mathbb R^p\) whose first \(k\) vectors span \(\operatorname{ran}F\).
In that basis \(F=(R,0)^T\) with invertible \(R\).
The coefficient on the left is \(\det(RR^T)\) times the determinant of the compression of \(B\) to \((\operatorname{ran}F)^\perp\).
Expanding the bordered determinant along its last \(k\) rows and the corresponding columns gives \((-1)^k\det(R)^2\) times the same compressed determinant.
This proves the identity on the full-column-rank set and then everywhere by polynomial continuity.
It also proves that replacing \(FF^T\) by \(FDF^T\), for any diagonal \(k\)-by-\(k\) matrix \(D\), multiplies this top coefficient by \(\det D\).

<a id="mixed-kernel-derivative"></a>
### Maximum rank excludes vanishing of the mixed coefficient

Restrict \(\mathcal H\) to \(\mathcal H'\) from the statement.
This preserves \(B_A,F\), because \(A\) and \(J\) take values in that space.
Its orthogonal decomposition is \(\operatorname{ran}J\oplus\overline{\operatorname{ran}A}\), and the restriction of \(A\) to the second summand is injective by selfadjointness.
The injectivity of every \(U(a)\) on this space is retained.

Choose a \(p\)-dimensional subspace \(\mathcal H_0\) containing \(\operatorname{ran}J\).
For each nonzero \(h\in\mathcal H_0\), the vectors \(U_1h,\ldots,U_ph\) are linearly independent.
Consequently the real square matrix space
\[
 \{S(x)|_{\mathcal H_0}:x\in\mathcal E\}
\]
is transitive in the exact sense of the accepted lemma.
That lemma supplies \(x\) for which this restriction is invertible.
At such a point \(S(x)\) has row rank \(p\) and \(F(x)\) has column rank \(k\).

Suppose now that \(M_{A,J}\) vanishes identically.
By the bordered identity the symmetric polynomial matrix
\[
 \mathcal K(x)=\begin{pmatrix}S(x)AS(x)^T&S(x)J\\J^TS(x)^T&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
has maximal rank \(v<p+k\).
There is a point \(x_0\) attaining this rank while the preceding restriction of \(S(x_0)\) is invertible.
Indeed choose a nonzero maximal minor polynomial and that nonzero restriction-determinant polynomial, restrict to the finite-dimensional span of witnesses, and avoid the zeros of their nonzero product.
This argument also applies when \(\mathcal E\) is infinite dimensional.

Let \((a,c)\ne0\) belong to \(\ker\mathcal K(x_0)\).
Because \(F(x_0)\) has full column rank, \(a=0\) would imply \(c=0\), so \(a\ne0\).
In the orthogonal decomposition into the range and kernel of \(\mathcal K(x_0)\), its range block is invertible.
For any \(y\in\mathcal E\), maximality of \(v\) forces the Schur complement of that block in \(\mathcal K(x_0+ty)\) to vanish for small real \(t\).
Its off-diagonal blocks vanish at zero, so differentiation at zero gives
\[
 0=(a,c)^T\mathcal K'(x_0)[y](a,c)
   =2\langle U(a)(AS(x_0)^Ta+Jc),y\rangle.
 \tag{Mixed-head-kernel-derivative}
\]
The rank-zero case has the same conclusion directly.
The injectivity of \(U(a)\) on \(\mathcal H'\) yields
\[
 AS(x_0)^Ta+Jc=0.
\]
The two terms lie in orthogonal summands, hence \(c=0\) and \(AS(x_0)^Ta=0\).
The lower kernel equation also gives \(J^TS(x_0)^Ta=0\).
Since \(A\) is injective on the residual summand, these facts imply \(S(x_0)^Ta=0\).
This contradicts the row rank \(p\) of \(S(x_0)\).
The exact nonvanishing statement follows, without taking an inverse of the residual or imposing a sign on it.

<a id="mixed-compactness"></a>
### Uniformity retains the head while compactifying the residual

Suppose the uniform conclusion fails at fixed parameters.
Restrict each tuple to its head plus nonzero residual spectral support.
Choose the first \(k\) labels as the columns of \(J\), and label the remaining orthonormal eigenvectors of \(A\) in decreasing absolute residual eigenvalue order.
Pass to a subsequence on which residual support dimensions are either a fixed integer at least \(p-k\) or tend to infinity.
All inner products of the countably many vectors \(U_ie_j\) are bounded by \(\Lambda\), and all residual eigenvalues lie in \([-1,1]\).
A diagonal subsequence makes these scalar quantities converge.

As in the accepted Gram construction, quotient the limiting positive semidefinite Gram form by its null space and complete to obtain \(\mathcal E_\infty\).
The limiting domain is the fixed head \(\mathbb R^k\) plus a finite residual space or \(\ell^2\).
The limiting maps satisfy both the stack upper bound and the whole-space Gram lower bound, first on finitely supported vectors and then by continuity.
Let \(J_\infty\) embed the head and let \(A_\infty\) be zero there and diagonal with the limiting residual eigenvalues on the remainder.
Then
\[
 A_\infty J_\infty=0,\quad\|A_\infty\|=1,\quad
 s_{p-k}(A_\infty)\ge d,\quad
 \sum_j\lambda_j(A_\infty)^2\le K.
\]
In particular the residual still has rank at least \(p-k\).

Keeping the first \(l\) residual labels gives \(A^{[l]}\), with uniform operator-norm error
\[
 \|A-A^{[l]}\|\le\sqrt{K/(l+1)}=:\varepsilon_l
 \tag{Mixed-head-tail}
\]
both before and after the limit.
No Frobenius-tail convergence is needed.
For \(\|x\|=1\), one has \(\|S(x)\|\le\sqrt{p\Lambda}\), so the norms of \(B_A\) and \(FF^T\) are at most \(p\Lambda\), and the change in \(B_A\) is at most \(p\Lambda\varepsilon_l\).
Evaluate \(\det(B_A+tFF^T)\) at the fixed \(k+1\) points \(t=0,1,\ldots,k\).
The determinant expansion and telescoping of its \(p\) factors bound every change by \(C(p,k,\Lambda)\varepsilon_l\).
The coefficient of \(t^k\) is a fixed linear combination of these values by polynomial interpolation, because the degree in \(t\) is at most \(k\).
Consequently
\[
 |M_{A,J}(x)-M_{A^{[l]},J}(x)|
 \le C(p,k,\Lambda)\varepsilon_l\|x\|^{2p}.
 \tag{Mixed-head-coefficient-tail}
\]

For a finite linear combination \(x\) of limiting Gram labels, take the same combination before the limit.
Its norm converges, and every fixed truncated coefficient converges.
The assumed vanishing unit-sphere suprema, homogeneity and the tail estimate imply \(M_{A_\infty,J_\infty}(x)=0\), first by passing the sequence limit and then letting \(l\to\infty\).
Density and continuity give this identity for all \(x\in\mathcal E_\infty\).
The limiting Gram inequality implies \(\|U(a)h\|^2\ge g\|a\|^2\|h\|^2\), so every nonzero channel combination remains injective.
The exact nonvanishing result, restricted to the head plus the limiting residual support if necessary, contradicts that identity.
This establishes the uniform constant \(b\).

<a id="mixed-separated-scales"></a>
### The lower mixed coefficient dominates at a head-to-residual gap

For a unit \(x\), write \(v_j=S(x)e_j\), so \(\|v_j\|\le\sqrt{p\Lambda}\), and put \(B=S(x)AS(x)^T\), with \(\|B\|\le p\Lambda r\).
Expand
\[
 \det\left(B+\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_jv_jv_j^T\right)
   =\sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}\left(\prod_{j\in I}\lambda_j\right)c_I(x).
 \tag{Mixed-head-multiaffine}
\]
Each variable appears at most once because its update has rank one.
The coefficient using every head direction is \(c_{\{1,\ldots,k\}}=M_{A,J}\), by the bordered identity and its diagonal-weight extension.
The uniform theorem applied to \(A/r\) gives
\[
 \sup_{\|x\|=1}|c_{\{1,\ldots,k\}}(x)|\ge br^{p-k}.
\]
The column expansion also gives for every subset \(I\)
\[
 |c_I(x)|\le (p!)^2(p\Lambda)^p r^{p-|I|}.
 \tag{Mixed-head-subset-bound}
\]
Indeed choose the \(|I|\) distinct columns receiving those rank-one updates and assign the distinct head labels to them.
There are at most \(p!\) assignments; every update column has norm at most \(p\Lambda\), and every remaining column has norm at most \(p\Lambda r\).
The permutation formula bounds each determinant by \(p!\) times the product of the column norms, giving the displayed estimate directly.

Set \(q_*:=\min_{j\le k}q_j\) and suppose \(r/q_*\le1\).
Summing all proper subsets in (Mixed-head-multiaffine) yields
\[
 \left|\det(SZS^T)-\left(\prod_{j=1}^k\lambda_j\right)M_{A,J}\right|
 \le C_*\left(\prod_{j=1}^kq_j\right)r^{p-k}\frac r{q_*},
\]
\[
 C_*:=(p!)^2(p\Lambda)^p(2^k-1).
 \tag{Mixed-head-relative-remainder}
\]
For each missing set of head labels the relative factor is a product of \(r/q_j\); every nonempty such product is at most \(r/q_*\).
Taking \(\delta=\min\{1,b/(2C_*)\}\) proves (Mixed-head-separated).
This comparison retains each head eigenvalue separately and has no loss from their mutual ratios or signs.

<a id="three-channel-use-and-limits"></a>
### Three-channel application and the unresolved probability interfaces

For a real physical inverse with orthonormal modes \(w_j=(u_{1j},u_{2j},u_{3j})\), take
\[
 U_ae_j=(u_{aj},t_au_{aj}),\qquad(t_1,t_2,t_3)=(0,1,\sqrt2).
\]
Orthogonality of the modes gives \(\sum_aU_a^*U_a\preceq3I\).
The quadratic evaluation matrix is exactly the three-channel border compression, so its determinant is the raw sixth homogeneous term, up to its harmless overall sign in the Schur polynomial.
Apply the preceding theorem with \(p=3\), \(k=1\) or \(k=2\), on a spectral support satisfying the whole-space combined Gram bound.
This gives the two branches stated above, including residual clusters with \(q_4\) comparable to \(q_3\).

In the original model the established combined Gram bound is only on a sufficiently small low spectral subspace.
If the theorem is applied there, its passage to the complete inverse requires a separate tail comparison at the product scale.
For example, when the discarded operator norm is at most \(\rho^{-1}\), the first two retained inverse singular values are \(q_1,q_2\), and \(q_3\to\infty\), the elementary three-by-three determinant expansion bounds the raw polynomial error by \(C_\rho q_1q_2\).
This is negligible relative to \(q_1q_2q_3\); the divergence and the low-subspace hypotheses must still be supplied for the actual matrices being used.
This conditional comparison is not an adoption of an unreviewed probability theorem.

The remaining residual branch can have \(\sum_{j>k}q_j^2/q_{k+1}^2\to\infty\) while the full effective rank \(\sum_jq_j^2/q_1^2\) stays bounded.
Neither the present compactness theorem nor a full-weight high-rank theorem covers it automatically.
A mixed high-rank tensor estimate or another mechanism is still needed for that regime.
Even the raw branches proved conditionally here require an additional physical collision/load argument at the product scale before a complete sixth-layer coefficient estimate follows.
No least-value floor, middle-logarithm control, determinant limit, general complex-coefficient theorem or Brown convergence is asserted.
