# Mixed high-residual-rank tensors and all-scale three-channel determinant coefficients

This theorem gives deterministic raw sixth-order tensor bounds on a finite-dimensional real spectral support.
It gives two mixed high-residual-rank estimates and combines them with the adopted bounded-residual and comparable-mode inputs to cover every singular-value ratio under a whole-space Gram hypothesis.
The two mixed estimates also have a weaker sufficient interface using head-space Gram geometry and full-residual quadratic Frobenius separation.
The complete argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v23.md#mixed-high-residual-reviewed), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-mixed-high-residual-reviewed).
No statement below supplies the whole-space geometry on a complete physical inverse or the unresolved transfer to complete physical coefficients.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact conditional assertions

Let \(\mathcal H,\mathcal E\) be finite-dimensional real Hilbert spaces and let \(U_1,U_2,U_3:\mathcal H\to\mathcal E\) satisfy
\[
 \sum_{a=1}^3U_a^*U_a\preceq\Lambda I,\qquad
 [\langle U_ah,U_bh\rangle]_{a,b=1}^3
       \succeq g\|h\|^2I_3
 \quad(h\in\mathcal H),
 \tag{Mixed-high-map-inputs}
\]
where \(g,\Lambda>0\) are fixed.
For a nonzero domain these conditions imply \(3g\le\Lambda\).
Write
\[
 U(a):=\sum_{i=1}^3a_iU_i,\qquad
 S(x)h:=(\langle U_ih,x\rangle)_{i=1}^3,\qquad
 B_Z(x):=S(x)ZS(x)^*.
\]
For \(Z=Z^*\), let \(\mathscr T_6(Z)\) be the arithmetic-average symmetric tensor representing \(\det B_Z(x)\).
All tensor norms below are Frobenius norms, and all polynomial coefficient tensors use this same symmetric convention.
For a homogeneous polynomial \(f\), write \(\mathscr T(f)\) for its symmetric coefficient tensor.

For \(k=1\) or \(k=2\), consider
\[
 Z=\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_je_je_j^*+A,\qquad
 Ae_j=0,\qquad A=A^*,\qquad
 q_j:=|\lambda_j|>0,\qquad
 r:=\|A\|>0,\qquad F:=\|A\|_F,
 \tag{Mixed-high-decomposition}
\]
where the \(e_j\) are orthonormal and the head magnitudes are ordered decreasingly.
There exist constants \(R_1,R_2\ge1\), \(0<\eta_1,\eta_2\le1\) and \(c_1,c_2>0\), depending only on \(g,\Lambda\), such that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 k=1:\quad &F^2\ge R_1r^2,\quad r\le\eta_1q_1
   &&\Longrightarrow&
   \|\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F&\ge c_1q_1F^2,\\
 k=2:\quad &F^2\ge R_2r^2,\quad r\le\eta_2q_2
   &&\Longrightarrow&
   \|\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F&\ge c_2q_1q_2F.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Mixed-high-branches}
\]
There is no requirement that \(F\) be small relative to a head eigenvalue, and eigenvalues may have either sign.

For the two mixed estimates only, the whole-space Gram hypothesis may be replaced by the following weaker alternative.
Keep the stack upper bound on \(\mathcal H\), put \(\mathcal H_{\rm head}:=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_k\}\), and assume, for a fixed \(g_0>0\),
\[
 [\langle U_ah,U_bh\rangle]_{a,b=1}^3
       \succeq g\|h\|^2I_3
       \quad(h\in\mathcal H_{\rm head}),
 \qquad
 \|U(a)AU(a)^*\|_F\ge g_0|a|^2F
       \quad(a\in\mathbb R^3).
 \tag{Mixed-high-weaker-inputs}
\]
Then both conclusions in (Mixed-high-branches) hold with possibly different positive constants depending only on \(g,g_0,\Lambda\).
This alternative does not impose a vector Gram lower bound on the residual spectral support.

Together with the fixed inputs specified below, these estimates give the all-scale conclusion
\[
 \|\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F\ge c(g,\Lambda)\,q_1q_2q_3,
 \tag{Mixed-high-all-scales}
\]
for every finite-dimensional selfadjoint \(Z\) under (Mixed-high-map-inputs), where \(q_1\ge q_2\ge q_3>0\) are its first three singular values.
This all-scale assertion retains the whole-space Gram hypothesis and is not asserted merely from the weaker two-branch interface.
The zero-product case has only the trivial nonnegative lower bound.
The constants are independent of both dimensions and all eigenvalue signs.
If the fixed map parameters admit no such tuple, the implications are vacuous.

## Complete candidate argument

<a id="finite-codimension"></a>
### Finite-codimension loss at the residual operator scale

Put
\[
 E_0:=\operatorname{span}\{U_ae_j:1\le a\le3,\ 1\le j\le k\},
 \qquad Y:=E_0^\perp,\qquad P:=P_Y,\qquad Q:=I-P,
 \qquad m:=\dim E_0\le3k.
\]
For every selfadjoint operator \(B\) on \(\mathcal E\), orthogonality of its blocks gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|B-PBP\|_F^2
 &=\|QBQ\|_F^2+2\|QBP\|_F^2\\
 &\le2\|QB\|_F^2
 \le2m\|B\|^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Mixed-high-finite-codimension}
\]
Apply this to \(B=LAL^*\), where \(L:=U(a)\) restricted to the residual spectral support.
The whole-space Gram inequality and stack upper bound imply
\[
 L^*L\succeq g|a|^2I,\qquad \|L\|^2\le\Lambda|a|^2.
\]
The signed sandwich estimate used in C187v18 then yields
\[
 \|LAL^*\|_F\ge g|a|^2F,\qquad
 \|LAL^*\|\le\Lambda|a|^2r.
\]
For \(a\ne0\), the polar factor of \(L\) is isometric, and multiplying \((L^*L)^{1/2}A(L^*L)^{1/2}\) on both sides by \((L^*L)^{-1/2}\) proves the Frobenius lower bound without positivity of \(A\).
The case \(a=0\) is immediate.
Consequently
\[
 \|PU(a)AU(a)^*P\|_F
 \ge |a|^2\bigl(gF-\sqrt{2m}\Lambda r\bigr).
 \tag{Mixed-high-projected-separation}
\]
This is a Frobenius separation statement for quadratic coefficient matrices.
It does not assert that the projected maps \(PU_a\) retain a whole-space vector Gram lower bound.

<a id="head-witnesses"></a>
### Uniform head witnesses

When \(k=1\), choose
\[
 x_0:=U_1e_1/\|U_1e_1\|\in E_0,\qquad
 v:=S(x_0)e_1.
\]
The Gram inequality gives \(\|U_1e_1\|^2\ge g\), so \(x_0\) is well defined, has norm one and satisfies \(|v|^2\ge g\).

When \(k=2\), let \(\mathcal H_{\rm head}:=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\}\), set \(U_i^{\rm h}:=U_i|_{\mathcal H_{\rm head}}\), \(i=1,2\), and put
\[
 S_2:=(U_1^{\rm h})^*U_1^{\rm h}+
       (U_2^{\rm h})^*U_2^{\rm h}.
\]
Then \(2gI\preceq S_2\preceq\Lambda I\).
The maps \(U_i^{\rm h}S_2^{-1/2}\), \(i=1,2\), have an isometric stack and whole-span Gram lower bound \(g/\Lambda\).
The transformed identity weight is \(S_2\); after division by its norm it has squared Frobenius norm at most \(2\) and second singular value at least \(2g/\Lambda\).
Thus C187v16 applies at the fixed parameters
\[
 (p,g',K',d')=(2,g/\Lambda,2,2g/\Lambda).
\]
Denote its positive constant by \(b_{\rm pair}\) and put \(\kappa_2:=4g^2b_{\rm pair}>0\).
The original identity-weight determinant for this pair of maps is
\[
 \det\bigl[\langle (U_a^{\rm h})^*x,(U_b^{\rm h})^*x\rangle\bigr]_{a,b=1}^2
 =
 \left(\det\bigl[\langle U_ae_j,x\rangle\bigr]_{a,j=1}^2\right)^2.
\]
Restoring the weight contributes \(\|S_2\|^2\ge4g^2\).
Therefore a unit vector \(x_0\in E_0\) can be chosen so that, for
\[
 v_j:=S(x_0)e_j,\qquad w:=v_1\times v_2,
\]
one has \(|w|^2\ge\kappa_2\).
The determinant depends only on the span of the four vectors \(U_ae_j\), \(a,j\le2\), so its unit-sphere maximum is attained within that span and hence within \(E_0\).
This construction depends on the head geometry, not on its eigenvalue ratios or on the residual.

<a id="orthogonal-bidegrees"></a>
### Exact bidegrees and their tensor norms

Fix the appropriate unit \(x_0\) above.
For \(y\in Y\), every head evaluation \(S(y)e_j\) vanishes, and hence
\[
 B_Z(tx_0+y)=t^2\mathsf W+t\mathsf D(y)+\mathsf E(y),
 \qquad
 \mathsf W:=\mathsf H+\mathsf C,
\]
\[
 \mathsf H:=\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_jv_jv_j^T,\qquad
 \mathsf C:=S(x_0)AS(x_0)^*,\qquad
 \mathsf E(y):=S(y)AS(y)^*,
\]
\[
 \mathsf D_{ab}(y):=
 \langle U_a^*x_0,AU_b^*y\rangle+
 \langle U_a^*y,AU_b^*x_0\rangle.
\]
The cubic determinant expansion gives exactly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_2(y)&:=[t^4]\det B_Z(tx_0+y)
   =\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}\mathsf W\,\mathsf E(y))
       +\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf W\operatorname{adj}\mathsf D(y)),\\
 Q_4(y)&:=[t^2]\det B_Z(tx_0+y)
   =\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf W\operatorname{adj}\mathsf E(y))
       +\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf E(y)\operatorname{adj}\mathsf D(y)).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Mixed-high-exact-bidegrees}
\]
For example the first formula contains the terms of types \((t^2,t^2,1)\) and \((t^2,t,t)\), while the second contains types \((t^2,1,1)\) and \((t,t,1)\).
The trace-adjugate identities already include their multiplicities, so there is no additional factor \(2\), \(3\) or factorial.

Let \(V_s\) be the symmetric tensor of \(Q_s\), for \(s=2,4\).
Restriction of \(\mathscr T_6(Z)\) to \(\mathbb Rx_0\oplus Y\) is an orthogonal tensor projection.
Within that space, components having different numbers of \(x_0\) slots are orthogonal.
The component of bidegree \(t^{6-s}y^s\) is
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}_6(x_0^{\otimes(6-s)}\otimes V_s),
\]
whose squared norm is \(\|V_s\|_F^2/\binom6s\): the \(\binom6s\) distinct placements of the \(Y\)-slots are orthogonal, and arithmetic symmetrization gives equal weights.
It follows that
\[
 \|\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F\ge\|V_s\|_F/\sqrt{15},
 \qquad s=2,4.
 \tag{Mixed-high-tensor-extraction}
\]
This extracts a coefficient of the complete determinant, so cancellation with a different bidegree cannot erase the resulting tensor lower bound.

<a id="local-coefficient-bounds"></a>
### Coefficient bounds used in both branches

The stack bound implies \(\|S(x)\|\le\sqrt\Lambda\|x\|\) and \(\|U_a\|\le\sqrt\Lambda\).
In particular
\[
 \|\mathsf H\|\le\Lambda q_1,\qquad
 \|\mathsf C\|\le\Lambda r.
\]
Each linear entry of \(\mathsf D\) has its coefficient vector in \(Y\) of norm at most \(2\Lambda r\).
Each quadratic entry of \(\mathsf E\) has its symmetric coefficient matrix
\[
 K_{ab}:=\tfrac12P(U_aAU_b^*+U_bAU_a^*)P
 \quad\text{on }Y,
\]
with \(\|K_{ab}\|_F\le\Lambda F\) and \(\|K_{ab}\|\le\Lambda r\).
The symmetric tensor of a product has norm at most the product of the factor tensor norms, because arithmetic symmetrization is a contraction.
All determinant and adjugate sums have fixed size three in the following estimates.

<a id="two-head-high-residual"></a>
### Two head modes and a high-rank residual

For \(k=2\), the exact rank-two identity is
\[
 \operatorname{adj}\mathsf H
       =\lambda_1\lambda_2ww^T.
\]
Thus the main term in \(Q_2\) is \(\lambda_1\lambda_2w^T\mathsf E(y)w\), whose quadratic tensor is
\[
 \lambda_1\lambda_2PU(w)AU(w)^*P.
\]
Since \(m\le6\), (Mixed-high-projected-separation) gives its norm at least
\[
 q_1q_2|w|^2(gF-\sqrt{12}\Lambda r).
\]
Set
\[
 R_2:=\max\{1,48\Lambda^2/g^2\}.
\]
For \(F^2\ge R_2r^2\), the main term is therefore at least
\[
 \tfrac12g\kappa_2q_1q_2F.
 \tag{Mixed-high-two-head-main}
\]

Here is a dimension-independent bound for the entire remainder.
The exterior-square representation of the adjugate and the tensor-product difference give
\[
 \|\operatorname{adj}X-\operatorname{adj}Y\|
 \le(\|X\|+\|Y\|)\|X-Y\|
\]
for three-by-three matrices.
Applying this inequality gives
\[
 \|\operatorname{adj}(\mathsf H+\mathsf C)
                      -\operatorname{adj}\mathsf H\|
 \le\Lambda^2(2q_1r+r^2).
\]
For a three-by-three constant matrix \(M\), \(\sum_{a,b}|M_{ab}|\le3\sqrt3\|M\|\).
When \(r\le q_2\le q_1\), the tensor of the resulting trace against \(\mathsf E\) has norm at most \(9\sqrt3\Lambda^3q_1rF\).
Each entry of \(\operatorname{adj}\mathsf D\) is the difference of two products of linear forms, and its quadratic tensor norm is at most \(8\Lambda^2r^2\).
Tracing against \(\mathsf H+\mathsf C\) gives an additional bound \(48\sqrt3\Lambda^3q_1r^2\).
Since \(F\ge r\), together these give
\[
 \|V_2-\mathscr T(\lambda_1\lambda_2w^T\mathsf E w)\|_F
 \le100\Lambda^3q_1rF.
 \tag{Mixed-high-two-head-error}
\]
Choose
\[
 \eta_2:=\min\{1,g\kappa_2/(400\Lambda^3)\},\qquad
 c_2:=g\kappa_2/(4\sqrt{15}).
\]
If \(r\le\eta_2q_2\), the error is at most half of (Mixed-high-two-head-main).
Equation (Mixed-high-tensor-extraction) proves the proposed two-head branch with these constants.
The factor \(F\) occurs in both the main term and its relevant error, so this argument imposes no upper bound on \(F/q_2\).

<a id="one-head-high-residual"></a>
### One head mode and a high-rank residual

For \(k=1\), write \(\mathsf H=\lambda_1vv^T\), where \(|v|^2\ge g\).
The main term in \(Q_4\) is
\[
 \lambda_1v^T\operatorname{adj}\mathsf E(y)v.
\]
Choose a three-by-two matrix \(V\) with orthonormal columns spanning \(v^\perp\).
The cofactor identity gives
\[
 v^T\operatorname{adj}\mathsf E(y)v
       =|v|^2\det(V^T\mathsf E(y)V).
\]
Put \(\overline U_i:=\sum_aV_{ai}U_a\), \(i=1,2\), and consider the symmetric quadratic coefficient matrices of this compressed two-by-two polynomial matrix on \(Y\).
Each has Frobenius norm at most \(\Lambda F\) and operator norm at most \(\Lambda r\).
For \(a\in\mathbb R^2\), its scalar quadratic combination has matrix
\[
 P U(Va)A U(Va)^*P.
\]
Because \(|Va|=|a|\) and \(m\le3\), its norm is at least
\[
 |a|^2(gF-\sqrt6\Lambda r).
\]
If \(F^2/r^2\ge24\Lambda^2/g^2\), this is at least \(gF|a|^2/2\).

Let \(R_{\rm hr}(p,\gamma)\) and \(c_{\rm hr}(p,\gamma)\) denote respectively the threshold and positive squared-norm constant of the first abstract statement of C187v18.
Apply it with \(p=2\), scale parameters \(\Lambda F,\Lambda r\), and separation parameter \(g/(2\Lambda)\).
Define
\[
 R_1:=\max\{1,24\Lambda^2/g^2,R_{\rm hr}(2,g/(2\Lambda))\},
 \qquad
 c_4:=\Lambda^2\sqrt{c_{\rm hr}(2,g/(2\Lambda))}.
\]
For \(F^2\ge R_1r^2\), the quartic tensor of \(\det(V^T\mathsf E V)\) has norm at least \(c_4F^2\).
The main term of \(Q_4\) consequently has norm at least \(gc_4q_1F^2\).

The remaining terms of \(Q_4\) are exactly
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(\mathsf C\operatorname{adj}\mathsf E)
       +\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf E\operatorname{adj}\mathsf D).
\]
Each entry of \(\operatorname{adj}\mathsf E\) has quartic tensor norm at most \(2\Lambda^2F^2\), so the first term is bounded by \(6\sqrt3\Lambda^3rF^2\).
The nine entrywise products in the second trace, with the preceding \(8\Lambda^2r^2\) adjugate bound, have total tensor norm at most \(72\Lambda^3r^2F\).
Therefore
\[
 \|V_4-\mathscr T(\lambda_1v^T\operatorname{adj}\mathsf E\,v)\|_F
 \le100\Lambda^3rF^2.
 \tag{Mixed-high-one-head-error}
\]
Choose
\[
 \eta_1:=\min\{1,gc_4/(200\Lambda^3)\},\qquad
 c_1:=gc_4/(2\sqrt{15}).
\]
If \(r\le\eta_1q_1\), the error removes at most half the main norm.
Equation (Mixed-high-tensor-extraction) gives the proposed one-head branch.
Again the argument imposes no upper bound on \(F/q_1\).

<a id="weaker-residual-interface"></a>
### The two branches need only head Gram and residual quadratic separation

Assume (Mixed-high-weaker-inputs) and the global stack upper bound, rather than the whole-space Gram hypothesis.
The head-witness argument is unchanged, because it uses the Gram lower bound only on the one- or two-dimensional head.
In particular \(|v|^2\ge g\) and \(|w|^2\ge\kappa_2(g,\Lambda)\) still hold.
The coefficient identities, tensor extraction and all upper error bounds use the stack bound and remain unchanged.

For the sole residual lower-bound step, apply (Mixed-high-finite-codimension) directly to \(U(a)AU(a)^*\).
The assumed Frobenius separation and the stack upper bound now give
\[
 \|PU(a)AU(a)^*P\|_F
 \ge |a|^2\bigl(g_0F-\sqrt{2m}\Lambda r\bigr).
 \tag{Mixed-high-weaker-projected}
\]
This argument does not use a polar inverse or injectivity of \(U(a)\) on the residual support.

For two heads, set
\[
 \widetilde R_2:=\max\{1,48\Lambda^2/g_0^2\},\qquad
 \widetilde\eta_2:=\min\{1,g_0\kappa_2/(400\Lambda^3)\},\qquad
 \widetilde c_2:=g_0\kappa_2/(4\sqrt{15}).
\]
At \(F^2\ge\widetilde R_2r^2\), (Mixed-high-weaker-projected) makes the main quadratic tensor at least \(g_0\kappa_2q_1q_2F/2\).
The unchanged error (Mixed-high-two-head-error) removes at most half of it when \(r\le\widetilde\eta_2q_2\), proving the second mixed conclusion with \(\widetilde c_2\).

For one head, the auxiliary plane \(v^\perp\) still yields the scalar combination \(PU(Va)AU(Va)^*P\).
Its Frobenius separation is at least \(g_0F|a|^2/2\) once \(F^2/r^2\ge24\Lambda^2/g_0^2\).
C187v18 therefore applies with scales \(\Lambda F,\Lambda r\) and separation parameter \(g_0/(2\Lambda)\).
Define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widetilde R_1&:=\max\{1,24\Lambda^2/g_0^2,
                         R_{\rm hr}(2,g_0/(2\Lambda))\},\\
 \widetilde c_4&:=\Lambda^2\sqrt{c_{\rm hr}(2,g_0/(2\Lambda))},\\
 \widetilde\eta_1&:=\min\{1,g\widetilde c_4/(200\Lambda^3)\},
 \qquad
 \widetilde c_1:=g\widetilde c_4/(2\sqrt{15}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The main quartic tensor then has norm at least \(g\widetilde c_4q_1F^2\).
Combining the unchanged error (Mixed-high-one-head-error) with \(r\le\widetilde\eta_1q_1\) and the tensor extraction proves the first mixed conclusion with \(\widetilde c_1\).
Thus the weaker alternative proves exactly the two asserted branches, without extending the hypotheses of the following all-scale assembly.

<a id="all-scale-assembly"></a>
### Proposed all-scale assembly with ordered fixed thresholds

Now let the \(q_j\) be all singular values of \(Z\), in decreasing order, with eigenvalue signs retained separately.
Write
\[
 F_k^2:=\sum_{j>k}q_j^2,\qquad r_k:=q_{k+1},
 \quad k=1,2,\qquad F_0:=\|Z\|_F.
\]
All spectral restrictions retain (Mixed-high-map-inputs).
The following order fixes constants without a circular dependence.

First fix \(K_2:=R_2\).
Let \(\delta_{\rm b,2}>0\) be the C187v22 separated-scale threshold at parameters \((p,k,g,\Lambda,K,d)=(3,2,g,\Lambda,K_2,1)\), and set
\[
 \delta_2:=\min\{1,\eta_2,\delta_{\rm b,2}\}.
\]
When \(q_3\le\delta_2q_2\), use the two-head high-rank branch if \(F_2^2\ge K_2q_3^2\), obtaining a bound at least \(c_2q_1q_2q_3\).
If \(F_2^2<K_2q_3^2\), C187v22 applies with residual norm \(q_3\), first residual singular value \(q_3\) and \(d=1\), giving a unit-sphere lower bound at the same product scale and hence the required tensor bound.
This covers the entire second-gap region, regardless of the ratio \(q_2/q_1\).

Next fix \(K_1:=R_1\).
Let \(\delta_{\rm b,1}>0\) be the C187v22 threshold at parameters \((3,1,g,\Lambda,K_1,\delta_2)\), and set
\[
 \delta_1:=\min\{1,\eta_1,\delta_{\rm b,1}\}.
\]
In the region \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\) and \(q_2\le\delta_1q_1\), use the one-head high-rank branch when \(F_1^2\ge K_1q_2^2\).
Otherwise C187v22 applies with residual norm \(q_2\) and second residual singular value \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\).
Both cases give a positive fixed multiple of \(q_1q_2^2\), which dominates \(q_1q_2q_3\).

The remaining region has \(q_2>\delta_1q_1\) and \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\), hence \(q_3>\delta_1\delta_2q_1\).
Put \(R_3:=R_{\rm hr}(3,g/\Lambda)\).
If \(F_0^2\ge R_3q_1^2\), apply C187v18 directly to the three-by-three coefficient family with scales \(\Lambda F_0,\Lambda q_1\) and separation parameter \(g/\Lambda\).
The signed sandwich bound supplies its scalar quadratic separation.
Its tensor lower bound is a positive fixed multiple of \(F_0^3\), and hence of \(q_1q_2q_3\).

If instead \(F_0^2<R_3q_1^2\), set
\[
 S_{\rm cmb}:=\sum_aU_a^*U_a,\qquad
 \widehat U_a:=U_aS_{\rm cmb}^{-1/2},\qquad
 A':=S_{\rm cmb}^{1/2}ZS_{\rm cmb}^{1/2}.
\]
The inequalities \(3gI\preceq S_{\rm cmb}\preceq\Lambda I\) show that the transformed maps have an isometric stack and Gram lower bound \(g/\Lambda\).
This simultaneous transformation preserves the quadratic evaluation matrix.
Moreover
\[
 3gq_1\le\|A'\|\le\Lambda q_1,\qquad
 \|A'\|_F\le\Lambda F_0,\qquad
 s_3(A')\ge3gq_3.
\]
After normalizing \(A'\) by its norm, C187v16 therefore applies at the fixed parameters
\[
 \left(
 3,\frac g\Lambda,\frac{\Lambda^2R_3}{9g^2},
 \frac{3g\delta_1\delta_2}{\Lambda}
 \right).
\]
These parameters are in the theorem's permitted ranges because \(3g\le\Lambda\), \(R_3\ge1\) and \(0<\delta_1,\delta_2\le1\).
Its unit-sphere bound is at least a positive fixed multiple of \(q_1^3\), and thus gives the desired tensor bound at the product scale.
Taking the minimum of the finitely many positive branch constants gives the candidate constant in (Mixed-high-all-scales).
Every threshold has been selected from earlier fixed constants, and no cutoff depends on the actual dimension or spectrum.

<a id="physical-transfer-limit"></a>
### Physical support, tensor-versus-supremum and unresolved transfer

For the three-channel physical combined maps with slopes \(0,1,\sqrt2\), the stack upper bound allows \(\Lambda=3\).
The all-scale assertion requires the whole-space Gram hypothesis on the actual spectral support to which it is applied.
On the complete \(3n\)-dimensional mode space of an invertible physical matrix, \(U_1\) has image in \(\mathbb R^n\oplus\{0\}\) and rank at most \(n\), so it cannot satisfy that hypothesis.
