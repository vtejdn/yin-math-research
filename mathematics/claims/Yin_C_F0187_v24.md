# All-scale physical sixth-layer coefficients for the finite-variance three-channel path

This theorem supplies the actual inverse-coefficient interface of the three-channel path.
It combines fixed-deletion physical estimates with head-residual tensor bounds and a mixed-factor collision estimate.
Its complete argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v24.md#all-scale-physical-whole-proof), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-all-scale-physical-reviewed).
The argument keeps the actual real finite-second-moment model and does not assume that the complete inverse support has a whole-space Gram lower bound.
No least-singular-value floor or determinant Task conclusion is asserted.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Original model and candidate conclusions

Use exactly the original real finite-second-moment model, fixed deletion bound \(r\), deterministic shift \(\theta_N\to0\), minors \(M_{N,S}\), and physical projections \(P_i\) of [C187v20](Yin_C_F0187_v20.md#statement).
For each actual invertible minor, write
\[
 Z=M_{N,S}^{-1},\qquad
 Z=\sum_j\lambda_j e_je_j^T,\qquad
 q_j=|\lambda_j|,\quad q_1\ge q_2\ge\cdots.
\]
All assertions below are uniform over \(|S|\le r\), on these actual invertible branches, without conditioning on their probability.
The finite number of smallest dimensions has no bearing on the limits.
The choices of an eigenbasis within repeated eigenspaces do not affect the conclusion.

Let \(n=N-|S|\), \(t=(0,1,\sqrt2)\), \(V_a(x,y)=x+t_ay\), and
\[
 K_{ab}(Z)=\tfrac12(V_a^TZ_{ab}V_b+V_b^TZ_{ba}V_a),\qquad
 h_Z(x,y)=\det[(x,y)^TK_{ab}(Z)(x,y)]_{a,b=1}^3.
\]
Write \(\mathscr T_6(Z)\) for its arithmetic-average symmetric tensor.
After the formal substitution \(x_i=\epsilon_{i,1}/\sqrt N\), \(y_i=\epsilon_{i,2}/\sqrt N\), let \(c_J\) be the complete degree-six Walsh coefficients.
Put
\[
 \sigma_{6,\mathrm{phys}}^2
   =\sum_{\substack{|J|=6\\\text{six different physical positions}}}|c_J|^2,
 \qquad
 \sigma_{6,\mathrm{complete}}^2=\sum_{|J|=6}|c_J|^2,
 \qquad
 L_i^{(6)}=\sum_{\substack{|J|=6\\J\cap\{(i,1),(i,2)\}\ne\varnothing}}|c_J|^2.
\]
There is a fixed \(c>0\), depending only on the laws and the fixed deletion bound, such that, with probability tending to one, every permitted invertible minor satisfies
\[
 \|\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F\ge c q_1q_2q_3,\qquad
 \sigma_{6,\mathrm{phys}}^2\ge c(q_1q_2q_3)^2/N^6.
 \tag{All-scale-physical-energy}
\]
Moreover, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\!\left\{\exists S:\ |S|\le r,\ M_{N,S}\text{ invertible},\
       \max_iL_i^{(6)}>\varepsilon\sigma_{6,\mathrm{complete}}^2\right\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{All-scale-complete-load}
\]
Write \(E_6=(\sigma_{6,\mathrm{complete}}^2)^{1/2}\).
For every fixed \(B_0<\infty\), on the same asymptotically full-probability scope,
\[
 E_6
 \ge c_{B_0}N^{-3}
       \sqrt{\det(I_3+\mathcal B^TZ^2\mathcal B)}
 \qquad(\|\mathcal B\|\le B_0),
 \tag{All-scale-graph-area}
\]
where \(E_6\) is the Euclidean norm of the complete degree-six coefficient vector.
The boundary matrix \(\mathcal B\) is any real \(3n\)-by-three matrix in the stated ball.

These are formal coefficient assertions.
They do not identify the original finite-variance row law with signs, prove its conditional anti-concentration, or handle graph distances or the middle logarithmic range.

## Complete candidate argument

<a id="residual-quadratic-separation"></a>
### Full residuals inherit quadratic separation without full-support vector geometry

For \(k=0,1,2\), put
\[
 A_k=Z-\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_je_je_j^T,\qquad F_k=\|A_k\|_F.
\]
C187v20 gives, uniformly over the actual invertible minors and all the choices above,
\[
 F_k^2/n\to\infty\quad\text{almost surely},\qquad
 \varepsilon_k:=
 \frac{\mathcal D(A_k)+d_*(A_k)}{F_k^2}\to0
 \quad\text{in probability},
 \tag{Residual-own-scale-spread}
\]
where \(d_i(A)=\|P_iA\|_F^2\), \(d_*(A)=\max_i d_i(A)\), and
\(\mathcal D(A)=\sum_i\|P_iAP_i\|_F^2\).
In particular \(q_{k+1}^2\ge F_k^2/(3n)\), so \(q_1,q_2,q_3\to\infty\) uniformly almost surely on these branches.

Write \(e_j=(u_{1j},u_{2j},u_{3j})\), and define the combined maps on the mode space by
\[
 U_ae_j=(u_{aj},t_au_{aj}).
\]
Here the \(e_j\) on the left denotes the corresponding coordinate basis of that mode space.
The stack bound is \(\sum_aU_a^*U_a\preceq3I\).
Fix a sufficiently small spectral cutoff \(\rho>0\) for the C187v20 Gram event, with constant \(g>0\).
The complete head of size at most two lies in this low spectral support eventually, since \(q_2\to\infty\).
On that event the Gram lower bound holds for its whole head space, including all linear combinations.

For the low and high spectral parts of any \(A_k\), write \(A_k=A_{k,<}+R_k\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\) the removed modes are low, and
\[
 \|R_k\|_F\le \sqrt{3n}/\rho=o(F_k),\qquad
 \|A_{k,<}\|_F/F_k\to1.
\]
For \(U(a)=\sum_a a_aU_a\), the signed sandwich inequality on the low support and the stack bound give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|U(a)A_kU(a)^*\|_F
 &\ge g|a|^2\|A_{k,<}\|_F
       -3|a|^2\|R_k\|_F\\
 &\ge (g-o(1))|a|^2 F_k.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Full-residual-quadratic-separation}
\]
The error is uniform over \(a\), minors and the stated mode choices.
Thus the weaker C187v23 interface holds with \(g_0=g/2\) on an event of probability tending to one.
This comparison is in Frobenius norm at the residual's own scale.
It does not turn a unit-sphere polynomial bound into a tensor bound.

For the heads also put \(\eta_{ji}=\|P_ie_j\|^2\), \(\chi_j=\max_i\eta_{ji}\).
For each fixed small \(h>0\), \(e_1,e_2\) eventually belong to the \([-h,h]\) spectral subspace of \(M_{N,S}\).
C187v20 bounds the physical compression of its projector by \(2C_0h\), with probability tending to one.
Hence \(\chi_1,\chi_2\to0\) uniformly in probability, by choosing \(h\) after a prescribed tolerance.
This uses the projector assertion directly, rather than dividing a complete row-energy bound by a small head eigenvalue.

<a id="mixed-physical-collisions"></a>
### Mixed factors retain physical collision and load bounds on their own scales

For any real symmetric physical matrix \(D\), its quadratic factors satisfy the local bounds of C187v18:
\[
 \|K_{ab}(D)\|_F\le C_t\|D\|_F,\quad
 \|\widehat P_iK_{ab}(D)\|_F^2\le C_t d_i(D),\quad
 \sum_i\|\widehat P_iK_{ab}(D)\widehat P_i\|_F^2
       \le C_t\mathcal D(D),
 \tag{Mixed-local-factors}
\]
where \(\widehat P_i\) is the two-coordinate physical group in the row-variable space.
For a head \(H_j=\lambda_je_je_j^T\),
\[
 \|H_j\|_F=q_j,\qquad d_i(H_j)=q_j^2\eta_{ji},\qquad
 \mathcal D(H_j)=q_j^2\sum_i\eta_{ji}^2\le q_j^2\chi_j.
\]
These bounds apply to the symmetric \(K_{ab}(H_j)\), even though that quadratic matrix need not have rank one.

Consider a tensor product of three quadratic factors with scales \(f_\ell>0\).
Suppose factor \(\ell\) has squared norm at most \(Cf_\ell^2\), group row mass at most \(Cf_\ell^2\rho_{\ell i}\), and summed physical diagonal mass at most \(Cf_\ell^2\epsilon_\ell\).
If two colliding slots lie in the same factor, their restricted squared norm is at most
\(C(\prod_\ell f_\ell^2)\epsilon_\ell\).
If they lie in different factors \(\ell,m\), it is at most
\[
 C\Bigl(\prod_\ell f_\ell^2\Bigr)\sum_i\rho_{\ell i}\rho_{mi}
 \le C\Bigl(\prod_\ell f_\ell^2\Bigr)\max_i\rho_{\ell i},
\]
provided \(\sum_i\rho_{mi}\le C\).
All our factors have this property: take \(\rho_{ji}=\eta_{ji}\) for a head and
\(\rho_{Ai}=d_i(A)/\|A\|_F^2\) for a residual.
The restriction to all tensors meeting a fixed group \(i\) is at most
\[
 C\Bigl(\prod_\ell f_\ell^2\Bigr)\sum_\ell\rho_{\ell i}.
\]
There are only fifteen slot pairs and six slots.
Union bounds, arithmetic-average symmetrization and the squared-norm bound for a fixed finite sum preserve these estimates with a fixed constant.
The repeated-physical-coordinate projection commutes with symmetrization.

The determinant is affine separately in each spectral head coefficient, since
\(B_{H_j}(x)=\lambda_j(S(x)e_j)(S(x)e_j)^T\) has rank at most one.
In its ordinary determinant expansion, all terms using the same head twice or three times therefore sum to zero as a polynomial.
They also sum to zero as the unique symmetric order-six tensor.
This cancellation is made after symmetric summation; no individual unsymmetrized term is discarded as zero.
For one head there are six retained products of scale \(F^3\) and eighteen of scale \(q_1F^2\), where \(F=\|A_1\|_F\).
For two heads there are six of scale \(F^3\), eighteen of each scale \(q_jF^2\), and thirty-six of scale \(q_1q_2F\), where \(F=\|A_2\|_F\).
Thus their summed scales are at most \((18+6C_*)q_1F_1^2\) when \(F_1\le C_*q_1\), and at most \((36+36C_*+6C_*^2)q_1q_2F_2\) when \(F_2\le C_*q_2\).

Consequently, if \(F_1\le C_*q_1\) and \(W=q_1F_1^2\), then
\[
 \|\mathscr T_{\rm rep}(Z)\|_F^2
       \le C_{C_*}(\chi_1+\varepsilon_1)W^2,\qquad
 L_i^{(6)}
       \le C_{C_*}N^{-6}W^2
           \left(\eta_{1i}+\frac{d_i(A_1)}{F_1^2}\right).
 \tag{One-head-mixed-collisions}
\]
If \(F_2\le C_*q_2\) and \(W=q_1q_2F_2\), then
\[
 \|\mathscr T_{\rm rep}(Z)\|_F^2
       \le C_{C_*}(\chi_1+\chi_2+\varepsilon_2)W^2,
\]
\[
 L_i^{(6)}
       \le C_{C_*}N^{-6}W^2
           \left(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i}+\frac{d_i(A_2)}{F_2^2}\right).
 \tag{Two-head-mixed-collisions}
\]
The coefficient bound uses the same finite factorial counting as C187v18 and covers the complete top layer, including terms that use both individual variables from one physical group.
For no heads, its pure collision and load bounds hold on scale \(W=F_0^3\), with error \(\varepsilon_0\), whenever a raw lower bound on that scale is separately available.

<a id="complete-inverse-spectral-cover"></a>
### A fixed spectral allocation gives a compatible lower bound and collision scale

Use the weaker C187v23 constants \(R_1,R_2,\eta_1,\eta_2\) for \(g,g/2,3\).
Choose \(\delta_2\le\eta_2\) small enough for the two-head C187v22 assertion at residual-rank bound \(R_2\) and \(d=1\).
Then choose \(\delta_1\le\eta_1\) small enough for the one-head C187v22 assertion at residual-rank bound \(R_1\) and \(d=\delta_2\).
Both numbers are fixed and may also be required to be at most one.
Choose a fixed \(R_0\ge1\) sufficient for the C187v18 three-channel high-rank assertion using (Full-residual-quadratic-separation) at \(k=0\).
All constants are chosen before the dimension or realized spectrum.

If \(F_0^2\ge R_0q_1^2\), C187v18 gives
\(\|\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F\ge cF_0^3\).
Use \(W=F_0^3\) and the pure physical bounds.
For every remaining case \(F_0\le\sqrt{R_0}q_1\).

If \(q_2\le\delta_1q_1\) and \(F_1^2\ge R_1q_2^2\), the weaker one-head C187v23 estimate gives the lower bound \(cW\) with \(W=q_1F_1^2\).
The one-head physical estimate applies because \(F_1\le\sqrt{R_0}q_1\).

If \(q_2\le\delta_1q_1\), \(F_1^2<R_1q_2^2\), and \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\), use C187v22 on the fixed low spectral support.
The head and the first two residual modes lie there eventually, and the low residual still has norm \(q_2\), second singular value at least \(\delta_2q_2\), and squared Frobenius norm at most \(R_1q_2^2\).
It supplies a unit-sphere raw lower bound \(c q_1q_2^2\).
The discarded high spectral weight has bounded operator norm.
The three-by-three determinant difference is \(O_\rho(q_1q_2+q_1+1)\), uniformly on the unit sphere, because the first two singular values of the low compression are at most \(3q_1,3q_2\).
Since \(q_2\to\infty\), this error is \(o(q_1q_2^2)\).
Thus the complete tensor has norm at least \(c q_1q_2^2\ge(c/R_1)W\), with \(W=q_1F_1^2\), and the one-head physical estimate again applies.

If \(q_2>\delta_1q_1\) and \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\), the first three singular values are comparable with fixed ratio \(\delta_1\delta_2\).
Apply C187v16 to the low spectral support after its standard stack normalization.
Indeed its stack \(G=\sum U_a^*U_a\) lies between \(3gI\) and \(3I\); use maps \(U_aG^{-1/2}\) and weight \(G^{1/2}Z_<G^{1/2}\).
The new Gram constant, effective-rank bound and third-singular-value ratio are bounded below or above by fixed constants depending only on \(g,R_0,\delta_1,\delta_2\).
For example \(\|G^{1/2}Z_<G^{1/2}\|\ge3gq_1\), its Frobenius norm is at most \(3F_0\), and its third singular value is at least \(3gq_3\).
C187v16 therefore gives a unit-sphere bound \(c q_1^3\).
The same bounded-tail determinant comparison is \(o(q_1^3)\), since \(q_1\to\infty\).
Use \(W=F_0^3\le R_0^{3/2}q_1^3\) and the pure physical bounds.

All cases left have \(q_3\le\delta_2q_2\).
Moreover,
\[
 F_2/q_2\le C_*:=\max\{\sqrt{R_1},\sqrt{R_0}/\delta_1\}.
 \tag{Two-head-residual-size}
\]
For \(q_2\le\delta_1q_1\), the one-head high-residual case was already removed, so \(F_2\le F_1<\sqrt{R_1}q_2\).
For \(q_2>\delta_1q_1\), the remaining full-rank bound gives the other displayed estimate.
If \(F_2^2\ge R_2q_3^2\), use the weaker two-head C187v23 estimate to get \(cW\), with \(W=q_1q_2F_2\).
Otherwise C187v22 on the low support gives a unit-sphere lower bound \(c q_1q_2q_3\).
The bounded high-tail error \(O_\rho(q_1q_2+q_1+1)\) is negligible because \(q_3\to\infty\).
Since \(F_2\le\sqrt{R_2}q_3\), the resulting full-tensor bound is again at least \(c'W\).
In both subcases (Two-head-residual-size) permits the two-head physical estimate.

The cases are exhaustive and use a finite number of fixed constants.
Each chosen \(W\) is at least \(q_1q_2q_3\).
Every use of a unit-sphere lower bound above belongs to a bounded-effective-rank branch in which that lower bound is actually supplied; the high-residual branches are instead applied directly to the complete inverse through residual quadratic separation.

<a id="sixth-layer-and-graph"></a>
### Distinct physical energy, complete loads and graph normalization

In every branch the preceding argument gives \(\|\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F\ge cW\).
Its repeated-physical tensor has squared norm \(o(W^2)\) uniformly in probability, by (Residual-own-scale-spread) and the head delocalization.
Since the repeated and distinct restrictions are orthogonal,
\[
 \|\mathscr T_{\rm dist}(Z)\|_F^2\ge c'W^2
\]
on an event of probability tending to one.
Exact symmetrization counting gives
\[
 \sigma_{6,\mathrm{phys}}^2
       =6!N^{-6}\|\mathscr T_{\rm dist}(Z)\|_F^2.
\]
The mixed or pure load bounds have maximum \(o(W^2/N^6)\), uniformly in probability.
Dividing by \(\sigma_{6,\mathrm{complete}}^2\ge\sigma_{6,\mathrm{phys}}^2\) proves the complete-load assertion.
The proof retains the same minor and spectral decomposition throughout; none of the estimates conditions on the probability of a branch.

Finally, put \(E_6=(\sigma_{6,\mathrm{complete}}^2)^{1/2}\).
For every \(\|\mathcal B\|\le B_0\), singular-value comparison yields
\[
 \sqrt{\det(I_3+\mathcal B^TZ^2\mathcal B)}
 \le\prod_{j=1}^3\sqrt{1+B_0^2q_j^2}
 \le(1+B_0^2)^{3/2}q_1q_2q_3
\]
eventually, because \(q_3\ge1\).
The energy lower bound gives \(E_6\ge cN^{-3}q_1q_2q_3\), proving the asserted graph-area comparison.
The sign of the highest homogeneous term in the full Schur determinant is immaterial, and lower homogeneous degrees cannot contribute to its degree-six Walsh coefficients.
This supplies no lower-Walsh-layer conclusion or actual-row-law probability estimate.
