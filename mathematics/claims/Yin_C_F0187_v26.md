# Physical inverse spread for positive-frame pencils on the free spectrum

This theorem gives physical projector and complete-inverse spread for original real finite-second-moment pencils with a positive coefficient frame, at zero in the free spectrum.
The proof was developed for Yin through ordinary reasoning and authoring self-checks and is adopted with [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v26.md#positive-frame-spectral-spread-whole-proof).
Its complete geometric argument was developed in [D33](../drafts/Yin_D_F0033.md#positive-frame-divergent-free-inverse-energy), which supplies extraction provenance rather than a logical dependency.
This supplies a geometric branch of the [positive-frame comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_F0063_v3.md#positive-frame-physical-boundary), including its intended real symmetric three-channel scope.
The proof in fact applies to every fixed auxiliary dimension.
It replaces the path-specific lower bound \(\mu([-h,h])\ge ch\) by the full imaginary-resolvent energy.
The geometric branch assumption is that zero belongs to the free spectral support, equivalently that the free inverse-square integral diverges by the fixed rational inverse criterion applied below.
No such free spectral-support condition is claimed for every positive-frame tuple.

Its [statement and proof](Yin_C_F0095_v1.md#yin_c_f0095-proof) are used at this exact pointwise scope; the same reuse boundary is explicit in [C127](Yin_C_F0127_v1.md#reuse-check-and-exact-new-interface).

## Exact hypotheses and quantifiers

Fix integers \(m,d\ge1\), fixed real symmetric \(A_0,\ldots,A_d\in M_m(\mathbb R)\), and the original independent real finite-second-moment Wigner inputs.
More precisely, strict upper entries of input \(j\) are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), with fixed real centered variance-one \(\xi_j\), and diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), with fixed real centered finite-second-moment \(\zeta_j\).
All coordinates and inputs are independent within each size, and coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
Assume the original complex-direction coefficient frame
\[
 \chi:=\inf_{\substack{a,b\in\mathbb C^m\\\|a\|=\|b\|=1}}
                \sum_{j=1}^d|a^*A_jb|^2>0,\qquad
 \Lambda:=\sum_{j=1}^d\|A_j\|^2.
 \tag{Divergent-energy-frame}
\]
Let \(\mu\) be the scalar spectral probability law of
\(L=A_0\otimes1+\sum_jA_j\otimes s_j\) under \(\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau\).
Here \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\) are free centered variance-one semicircular elements.
Assume
\[
 0\in\operatorname{supp}\mu=\sigma(L).
 \tag{Free-spectral-point}
\]
The [fixed-criterion application](#free-gap-criterion) below proves, for every such selfadjoint matrix semicircular pencil even without a positive frame,
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}x^{-2}\,d\mu(x)<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 0\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu.
 \tag{Free-gap-inverse-square-equivalence}
\]
Consequently the geometric branch assumption is equivalent to
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}x^{-2}\,d\mu(x)=\infty,
 \quad\text{with }x^{-2}=+\infty\text{ at zero}.
 \tag{Divergent-free-inverse-square}
\]
This includes gapless spectral boundaries without assuming a density shape or a positive density at zero.
For \(h>0\), set
\[
 J(h):=\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{d\mu(x)}{x^2+h^2},
 \qquad q(h):=h^2J(h)\in(0,1].
 \tag{Full-imaginary-energy}
\]
Monotone convergence gives \(J(h)\to\infty\) as \(h\downarrow0\).
The quantity \(q(h)\) need not be bounded below as \(h\downarrow0\).

Fix integer bounds \(r,k_0\ge0\) for physical deletions and mode removals, and one deterministic real sequence \(\theta_N\to0\).
For \(|S|\le r+1\), retain all auxiliary coordinates at physical positions outside \(S\), put \(n=N-|S|\), and write
\[
 M_{N,S}=A_0\otimes I_n+\sum_j A_j\otimes W_{j,N,S}
                       +\theta_NI_{mn}.
\]
All minors retain the original normalization \(N^{-1/2}\).
Let \(P_i=I_m\otimes e_ie_i^T\) denote a retained physical block projection.

There is a fixed \(c>0\), independent of \(h\), such that for each fixed \(h>0\), almost surely eventually,
\[
 \max_{\substack{|S|\le r\\i\notin S}}
 \|P_i(M_{N,S}-ih)^{-1}P_i\|
 \le\frac1{h(1+cJ(h))},
\]
\[
 \max_{\substack{|S|\le r\\i\notin S}}
 \|P_i\mathbf1_{[-h,h]}(M_{N,S})P_i\|
 \le \rho(h):=\frac2{1+cJ(h)}.
 \tag{Divergent-energy-projector-bound}
\]
A single probability-one event can be chosen for all \(h\) in any prescribed countable set, in particular \(h=2^{-\ell}\), \(\ell\ge1\).
The eventual dimension threshold may depend on fixed \(h\).
No estimate with a size-dependent \(h_N\) or a uniform-in-\(h\) convergence rate is asserted.

On the invertible branch of any \(|S|\le r\), let \(Z=M_{N,S}^{-1}\).
Remove any \(k\le k_0\) orthonormal eigenmodes:
\[
 E=Z-\sum_{\ell=1}^k\lambda_\ell e_\ell e_\ell^T,\qquad
 Ze_\ell=\lambda_\ell e_\ell.
\]
The modes need not be the largest ones, and arbitrary choices within repeated eigenspaces are allowed.
Put
\[
 F_E=\|E\|_F,\quad Q_E=\|E\|,\qquad
 d_*(E)=\max_i\|P_iE\|_F^2,\qquad
 \mathcal D(E)=\sum_i\|P_iEP_i\|_F^2.
\]
Uniformly over all these actual invertible minors and mode choices, almost surely,
\[
 F_E^2/N\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad Q_E\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \frac{d_*(E)}{F_E^2}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \frac{\mathcal D(E)}{F_E^2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Divergent-energy-residual-spread}
\]
Uniformity means that eventually no permitted invertible minor and mode choice violates a specified finite threshold or tolerance.
An empty collection of invertible minors imposes no condition.
These are unconditional assertions intersected with invertibility, not assertions under conditioning on the probability of that branch.

## Complete candidate proof

<a id="free-gap-criterion"></a>
### The existing rational inverse criterion identifies the free branch

The element \(L\) is a bounded selfadjoint matrix polynomial in a finite semicircular family.
It is therefore a bounded matrix rational model covered by [C95's statement](Yin_C_F0095_v1.md#statement), with full corner \(e=I_m\).
Let \(\mathcal M=W^*(I_m,L)\) in \(M_m(W^*(s_1,\ldots,s_d))\), with the restricted faithful normalized trace.
If \(\int x^{-2}\,d\mu(x)<\infty\), then \(\mu(\{0\})=0\).
Faithfulness implies that the zero spectral projection of \(L\) vanishes.
Spectral calculus consequently defines an existing densely defined closed affiliated inverse \(L^{-1}\) in \(\mathcal M\), and
\[
 \|L^{-1}\|_{2,\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau}^2
 =\int x^{-2}\,d\mu(x)<\infty.
\]
Apply C95, equation (3), to \(f=L\) and \(\lambda=0\).
This existing square-integrable affiliated inverse is bounded and everywhere defined, hence \(0\notin\sigma(L)\).
This step uses precisely the existing-inverse premise in C95; neither nonzero \(L\) nor a formal rational expression alone would supply it.

Conversely, if \(0\notin\sigma(L)\), the bounded inverse has finite squared trace, so the integral is finite.
For a bounded selfadjoint element in a faithful tracial algebra, its scalar spectral support equals its spectrum: every nonzero spectral projection has strictly positive trace.
Thus absence of zero from that support is a genuine open free spectral gap, proving (Free-gap-inverse-square-equivalence).
This is a direct application of the adopted matrix rational criterion, not a new proof of that criterion.
It uses no \(\chi>0\) hypothesis and no limit comparison for finite random-matrix inverses.

<a id="scalar-weak-limits"></a>
### Scalar weak limits and the full resolvent weight

Apply C186v4 to the unshifted real selfadjoint matrix-valued affine polynomial.
Adding the deterministic real shift \(\theta_N\to0\) preserves its almost-sure weak limit.
The scalar principal-submatrix interlacing bound gives, uniformly over fixed deletion sets,
\[
 \sup_x|F_{M_{N,S}}(x)-F_{M_{N,\varnothing}}(x)|
 \le \frac{2|S|}{N-|S|}.
\]
Indeed the unnormalized eigenvalue counts differ by at most \(m|S|\), and changing the normalizing dimension from \(mN\) to \(m(N-|S|)\) contributes another such fraction.
Consequently the empirical scalar laws of all \(|S|\le r+1\) converge weakly to \(\mu\) on one probability-one event.
For the two families of bounded continuous tests used below, this also follows directly from the same counting bound and their finite total variations.
No weighted auxiliary weak theorem is needed.

Fix a pair \((S,i)\) with \(|S|\le r\), \(i\notin S\), and condition only on the original input minors at the further-deleted physical set.
Write
\[
 \widetilde M=M_{N,S\cup\{i\}},\qquad
 Q_h:=h\,\operatorname{Im}(\widetilde M-ih)^{-1}
     =h^2(\widetilde M^2+h^2I)^{-1}.
\]
This is a minor-measurable positive contraction, although it is generally not a projection.
For each fixed \(h>0\), the scalar weak limit gives, simultaneously for every such local minor, almost surely eventually,
\[
 \frac1N\operatorname{Tr}Q_h\longrightarrow m q(h),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}Q_h\ge\frac m2q(h)N.
 \tag{Full-imaginary-local-trace}
\]
The factor \(m\) and normalization \(1/N\) are retained here.
There is no assertion that a low spectral window contains order \(hN\) eigenvalues.

<a id="full-imaginary-covariance"></a>
### The original-row covariance works for any positive contraction

Let \(B_i\) be the fresh \(mn'\)-by-\(m\) border, \(n'=N-|S|-1\), and put \(\widehat B_i=\sqrt N B_i\).
The retained space is identified with \(\mathbb C^m\otimes\mathbb C^{n'}\).
For a complex unit direction \(a\),
\[
 \widehat B_i a=\sum_{j=1}^d\sum_{\ell=1}^{n'}
                  g_{j\ell}(a)\xi_{j\ell},\qquad
 g_{j\ell}(a)=(A_ja)\otimes e_\ell.
\]
All displayed original fresh variables remain independent under this local-minor conditioning.
Set
\[
 w_{j\ell}=\|Q_h^{1/2}g_{j\ell}(a)\|^2,\qquad
 H_a=\sum_{j,\ell}w_{j\ell},\qquad
 W=\max_j\|A_j\|^2>0.
\]
Then \(0\le w_{j\ell}\le W\).
For every unit \(a\), the exact frame definition gives
\[
 \sum_j(A_ja)(A_ja)^*\succeq\chi I_m.
\]
Indeed its quadratic form in a unit \(b\) is \(\sum_j|b^*A_ja|^2\).
Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_a
 &=\operatorname{Tr}
   \left[Q_h\left(\sum_j(A_ja)(A_ja)^*\otimes I_{n'}\right)\right]\\
 &\ge\chi\operatorname{Tr}Q_h
 \ge\frac{m\chi}{2}q(h)N
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Full-imaginary-fresh-covariance}
\]
on (Full-imaginary-local-trace).
This is a full-space original-noise covariance bound.
It uses no identity noise coefficient, separated slopes, low-mode auxiliary geometry, or deterministic relation from a path matrix.

Choose one fixed raw cutoff \(L_0\), independent of \(h,N,a\), such that for every prescribed law
\[
 \mathbb P\{|\xi_j|\le L_0\}\ge\tfrac12,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi_j\mid|\xi_j|\le L_0)\ge\tfrac12.
\]
Such a choice follows from the fixed finite-second-moment laws.
The weighted-indicator calculation in C187v10, applied now to the weights \(w_{j\ell}\in[0,W]\), shows that the retained weight is at least \(H_a/4\), except with probability at most \(e^{-cH_a}\).
For completeness, independence and
\(1-e^{-w/W}\ge(1-e^{-1})w/W\) give
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\!\left[-W^{-1}\sum_{j,\ell}
     \mathbf1_{\{|\xi_{j\ell}|\le L_0\}}w_{j\ell}\right]
 \le\exp[-(1-e^{-1})H_a/(2W)].
\]
Exponential Markov at retained weight \(H_a/4\) gives the stated bound with a positive constant depending on \(W\).

Reveal those indicators and all discarded raw values.
The retained variables are still independent conditional truncated laws with variance at least \(1/2\) and support diameter at most \(2L_0\).
The discarded values and conditional means give an arbitrary fixed offset.
The function
\[
 f_a=\|Q_h^{1/2}\widehat B_i a\|
\]
is convex and at most \(\sqrt\Lambda\)-Lipschitz in the retained real coordinates.
To see the latter bound, the raw map at each physical position has squared Frobenius norm \(\sum_j\|A_ja\|^2\le\Lambda\), the positions form orthogonal blocks before applying \(Q_h^{1/2}\), and this last factor is contractive.
On weighted survival its conditional second moment is at least \(H_a/8\).
The accepted bounded-product inequality and integration give
\(\mathbb E|f_a-\operatorname{med}f_a|^2\le C\), with \(C\) independent of \(h,N\) and the revealed offset.
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality consequently gives
\[
 \operatorname{med}f_a
 \ge(\mathbb Ef_a^2)^{1/2}-\sqrt C
 \ge c\sqrt{H_a}
\]
once \(H_a\) exceeds a fixed threshold.
One more application of its lower tail, followed by integration of the revealed data, proves
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\|Q_h^{1/2}\widehat B_i a\|
                  <a_0\sqrt{q(h)N}\,\middle|\,
                       \text{original local minor}\right\}
 \le C e^{-c q(h)N}
 \tag{Full-imaginary-fixed-direction}
\]
whenever that conditioned minor satisfies (Full-imaginary-local-trace).
The constants \(a_0,c,C>0\) depend on the fixed coefficients and laws, but not on fixed \(h\).
The eventual \(N\) threshold may depend on \(q(h)>0\).
Neither a full-parent event nor any fresh-row norm bound has entered this conditional law.

<a id="physical-projectors"></a>
### Simultaneous fixed-deletion projectors

Use the original off-diagonal raw-coordinate guard
\[
 \Omega_N=
 \left\{\sum_{j=1}^d\sum_{u<v}|\xi_{j,uv}|^2\le N^4\right\},
 \qquad \mathbb P(\Omega_N^c)\le C N^{-2}.
\]
On \(\Omega_N\), every raw border at every permitted deletion has norm at most \(C_A N^2\).
Since \(Q_h^{1/2}\) is contractive, the same bound controls the direction Lipschitz constant for the deterministic net extension.
A complex-unit-sphere net of mesh
\(a_0\sqrt{q(h)N}/(2C_A N^2)\) has at most \(C q(h)^{-m}N^{3m}\) points.
A lower bound \(a_0\sqrt{q(h)N}\) on that net gives half that lower bound for every unit direction.
Integrate (Full-imaginary-fixed-direction) only over its local trace condition and union over the \(O_r(N^{r+1})\) pairs.
There is a fixed \(c_0>0\), independent of \(h\), such that
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{&
 \text{all local trace conditions hold, but some pair has}\\
 &B_i^TQ_hB_i\not\succeq c_0q(h)I_m\}\\
 &\le C N^{-2}
       +C_r q(h)^{-m}N^{r+1+3m}e^{-c q(h)N}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Full-imaginary-summable-row-failure}
\]
The whole-parent guard is added only once for the net argument, not inserted into any conditional distribution.
For each fixed \(h>0\) the right side is summable.
The local trace conditions hold almost surely eventually by (Full-imaginary-local-trace).
First Borel--Cantelli therefore gives \(B_i^TQ_hB_i\succeq c_0q(h)I_m\) simultaneously for all pairs, almost surely eventually.
Unlike the path application in C187v20, this deduction has no separate nonsummable random combined-Gram event.

The physical Schur block of \(M_{N,S}-ih\) is
\[
 S_i(h)=A_i-ihI_m-B_i^T(\widetilde M-ih)^{-1}B_i,
\]
where \(A_i\) is real symmetric, including its realized original diagonal entries and the common real shift.
Thus
\[
 -\operatorname{Im}S_i(h)
 \succeq hI_m+\frac{c_0q(h)}hI_m
 =h(1+c_0J(h))I_m.
\]
For any vector \(v\), the imaginary part of \(v^*S_i(h)v\) then bounds \(\|S_i(h)v\|\) below by \(h(1+c_0J(h))\|v\|\).
The inverse norm is at most the reciprocal of this quantity, and \(S_i(h)^{-1}\) is the physical resolvent block.
The deterministic spectral inequality
\[
 \mathbf1_{[-h,h]}(M_{N,S})
 \preceq2h\,\operatorname{Im}(M_{N,S}-ih)^{-1}
\]
proves (Divergent-energy-projector-bound), with \(c=c_0\).
Intersect these probability-one events over \(h=2^{-\ell}\).
The free divergence hypothesis makes \(\rho(h)\to0\) on this sequence, regardless of the rate of \(q(h)\).

<a id="residual-energy"></a>
### Energy after removing any fixed number of modes

For \(B>0\), define the bounded continuous cap
\[
 \phi_B(x)=\min(x^{-2},B),\qquad \phi_B(0)=B.
\]
The scalar weak limit and fixed-deletion bound imply, almost surely,
\[
 \frac1N\operatorname{Tr}\phi_B(M_{N,S})
 \longrightarrow m\int\phi_B\,d\mu
\]
uniformly over \(|S|\le r\), simultaneously for positive integer \(B\).
On an invertible minor, deleting \(k\le k_0\) inverse modes loses at most \(k_0B\) from this capped sum.
Hence every residual above satisfies
\[
 \frac{F_E^2}{N}
 \ge\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}\phi_B(M_{N,S})-\frac{k_0B}{N}.
 \tag{Capped-residual-energy}
\]
First take \(N\to\infty\) at fixed integer \(B\), then let \(B\to\infty\).
By (Divergent-free-inverse-square), this proves uniform almost-sure divergence \(F_E^2/N\to\infty\).
Also \(Q_E^2\ge F_E^2/(mn)\to\infty\).
No quantitative eigenvalue count near zero, simplicity assumption, or actual inverse-moment convergence has been used.

<a id="residual-spread"></a>
### Separate row and diagonal spread at each residual's own scale

Fix a dyadic \(h>0\) and put
\[
 P_<:=\mathbf1_{[-h,h]}(M_{N,S}),\qquad
 E_<=EP_<,\qquad E_>=E(I-P_<).
\]
These selfadjoint pieces are spectrally orthogonal even when modes have been removed inside a repeated eigenspace.
They satisfy \(\|E_>\|\le h^{-1}\).
The already proved physical projector bound gives
\[
 \|P_iE_<\|_F^2\le\rho(h)F_E^2,\qquad
 \|P_iE_>\|_F^2\le m h^{-2}.
\]
The row energies add because \(E_<E_>=0\), so
\[
 d_*(E)\le\rho(h)F_E^2+m h^{-2}.
 \tag{Divergent-energy-row-split}
\]
For the physical diagonal blocks, use the different estimate
\[
 \|P_iE_<P_i\|_F^2
 \le\|P_iP_<\|^2\|E_<P_i\|_F^2
 \le\rho(h)\|E_<P_i\|_F^2.
\]
The squared Frobenius norm of each high diagonal block is at most \(mh^{-2}\).
The two-term square inequality and summation yield
\[
 \mathcal D(E)\le2\rho(h)F_E^2+2mn h^{-2}.
 \tag{Divergent-energy-diagonal-split}
\]
Divide by \(F_E^2\), use its uniform divergence relative to \(N\), and first send \(N\to\infty\) at fixed \(h\).
Then let the dyadic \(h\downarrow0\), so that \(\rho(h)\to0\).
This proves both almost-sure spread assertions in (Divergent-energy-residual-spread) on the common event.
The diagonal ratio was bounded separately and was not inferred from the maximum row ratio.

<a id="cutoff-and-heads"></a>
### Fixed spectral cutoffs and physical head mass

For any fixed \(\rho_0>0\), the same residual obeys
\[
 E_{\rho_0}:=E\mathbf1_{[-\rho_0,\rho_0]}(M_{N,S}),\quad
 \|E-E_{\rho_0}\|\le\rho_0^{-1},\quad
 \|E-E_{\rho_0}\|_F^2\le mn/\rho_0^2.
\]
Consequently, uniformly over the same choices, almost surely,
\[
 \frac{\|E_{\rho_0}\|_F}{F_E}\to1,\quad
 \frac{\|E-E_{\rho_0}\|_F}{F_E}\to0,\quad
 \frac{\|E_{\rho_0}\|_F^2}{N}\to\infty,\quad
 \frac{\|E-E_{\rho_0}\|}{Q_E}\to0.
\]
Eventually \(\|E_{\rho_0}\|=Q_E\).
Repeating the two separate splitting bounds with dyadic \(h<\rho_0\) gives both spread ratios for \(E_{\rho_0}\) relative to its own energy.

In particular every fixed number of largest inverse modes has vanishing maximal physical mass, almost surely uniformly over the invertible minors.
To see this, remove the preceding largest modes in (Capped-residual-energy); its divergence implies that the next largest inverse singular value also diverges.
Its underlying eigenvalue eventually belongs to each fixed \([-h,h]\), and every unit vector in that eigenspace has physical mass at most \(\rho(h)\).
This also covers repeated eigenvalues and any orthonormal choice of head vectors.

<a id="remaining-scope"></a>
## Applicability and the free-gap branch

For the real symmetric \(m=3,\chi_A>0\) model with (Divergent-free-inverse-square), the argument supplies complete-inverse and fixed-mode-residual energy divergence, physical head delocalization, and both own-scale row and diagonal spread.
The whole-space combined Gram and raw tensor inputs remain those already matched in L63v3.
Thus the geometric premises of a C187v24-type head/residual physical transfer now have an explicit route in this free-energy branch.
One must still write the actual fixed-\(d\) local-factor and complete-coefficient application, and the corresponding original-law probability conversion; the special path theorem is not an automatic general-coefficient conclusion.

The complementary case is now identified exactly: \(\int x^{-2}\,d\mu(x)<\infty\) means that zero lies in a free spectral gap.
In that case the displayed projector estimate does not tend to zero, and the capped-energy calculation does not force residual divergence.
The free gap and almost-sure weak convergence do not by themselves bound the actual inverse energy \(\|M_{N,S}^{-1}\|_F^2/N\) or exclude isolated very small finite-dimensional eigenvalues.
The [adopted low inverse-energy theorem](Yin_C_F0194_v18.md#decision-low-inverse-energy-reviewed) controls actual minors below its slow deterministic energy threshold.
Actual high inverse energy in the free-gap case remains a separate unresolved branch.

No general-coefficient physical coefficient theorem, original-law floor, determinant conclusion or general Task completion is asserted here.
The adopted scope is precisely this geometry and its stated direct source applications.
