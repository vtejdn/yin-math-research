# Complete physical sixth coefficients for positive-frame spectral points

This theorem supplies the fixed-input-count physical coefficient step for real symmetric three-channel pencils with a positive coefficient frame and zero in the free spectral support.
It converts complete-inverse geometry into sixth-degree energy, complete physical-group loads and the graph-area scale, while retaining every inverse spectral ratio regime.
The proof was developed for Yin through ordinary reasoning and authoring self-checks and is adopted with [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v27.md#positive-frame-complete-sixth-whole-proof).
The development in [D33](../drafts/Yin_D_F0033.md#positive-frame-fixed-d-sixth-coefficients) is extraction provenance, not a logical dependency.
No original-law least-value floor, logarithmic determinant conclusion or general Task completion is asserted.

## Exact model, coefficients and conclusion

Fix \(d\ge1\), real symmetric matrices \(A_0,\ldots,A_d\in M_3(\mathbb R)\), and the original real finite-second-moment Wigner inputs \(W_j^{(N)}\).
For each \(j\), strict upper entries are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), with fixed real centered variance-one law \(\xi_j\); diagonal entries are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), with fixed real centered finite-second-moment law \(\zeta_j\).
Lower entries are the symmetric copies.
All coordinates and inputs are independent within each size, while cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
Assume the original complex-direction coefficient frame
\[
 \chi_A:=\inf_{\substack{a,b\in\mathbb C^3\\\|a\|=\|b\|=1}}
              \sum_{j=1}^d|a^*A_jb|^2>0.
 \tag{Fixed-d-positive-frame}
\]
Let \(\mu\) be the scalar spectral probability law of
\(L=A_0\otimes1+\sum_jA_j\otimes s_j\) under \(\operatorname{tr}_3\otimes\tau\), where the \(s_j\) are free centered variance-one semicircular elements.
Assume \(0\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\).
By the [adopted criterion application in C187v26](Yin_C_F0187_v26.md#free-gap-criterion), this is equivalent to divergent free inverse-square integral, including gapless spectral boundaries.

Fix a deletion bound \(r\ge0\) and one deterministic real sequence \(\theta_N\to0\).
For a set \(S\) of at most \(r\) physical positions, put \(n=N-|S|\) and
\[
 M_{N,S}=(A_0+\theta_NI_3)\otimes I_n+
                    \sum_{j=1}^d A_j\otimes W_{j,N,S},
\]
where each input principal minor retains its original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
On its actual invertible branch put \(Z=M_{N,S}^{-1}\).
Write
\[
 Z=\sum_{\ell=1}^{3n}\lambda_\ell e_\ell e_\ell^T,\qquad
 q_\ell=|\lambda_\ell|,\quad q_1\ge q_2\ge\cdots,
\]
\[
 E_k=Z-\sum_{\ell=1}^k\lambda_\ell e_\ell e_\ell^T,\qquad
 F_k=\|E_k\|_F,\quad k=0,1,2.
\]
The whole calculation remains on this same actual inverse, retaining all eigenvalue signs.
For \(D=D^T\) on \(\mathcal H=\mathbb R^3\otimes\mathbb R^n\), use
\[
 d_i(D)=\|P_iD\|_F^2,\quad
 d_*(D)=\max_i d_i(D),\quad
 \mathcal D(D)=\sum_i\|P_iDP_i\|_F^2,\qquad
 P_i=I_3\otimes e_ie_i^T.
\]
The row-variable space is
\(\mathcal E=\bigoplus_{j=1}^d\mathbb R^n\).
Its physical group \(G_i=\{(i,j):1\le j\le d\}\) contains \(d\) distinct variables, and \(\widehat P_i\) is the corresponding rank-\(d\) projection.
Thus the variable labels agree with \([n]\times[d]\) in C194v19, even when coordinates are stored input-first.

For \(a=1,2,3\), define
\[
 (U_ah)_{j,i}=e_a^TA_jh_i,\qquad
 R(x)e_a=U_a^Tx=\sum_{j=1}^d(A_je_a)\otimes x_j,
\]
\[
 K_{ab}(D)=\tfrac12(U_aDU_b^T+U_bDU_a^T).
 \tag{Fixed-d-local-maps}
\]
The equality for \(R\) uses the real symmetry of \(A_j\).
For \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\), put
\[
 h_Z(x)=\det(R(x)^TZR(x))
       =\langle\mathscr T_6(Z),x^{\otimes6}\rangle,
\]
where every symmetric tensor uses arithmetic-average symmetrization.
If \(H=H^T\in M_3(\mathbb R)\) is any exposed corner, put \(p(x)=\det(H-R(x)^TZR(x))\).
Its homogeneous degree-six part is \(-h_Z\); all other homogeneous degrees are at most four.
Under formal independent signs \(x_{j,i}=\epsilon_{i,j}/\sqrt N\), let \(c_J\) be the complete degree-six Walsh coefficients of \(p\).
Define
\[
 \sigma_{6,\mathrm{phys}}^2
 =\sum_{\substack{|J|=6\\\text{six distinct physical positions}}}|c_J|^2,
 \qquad
 \sigma_6^2=\sum_{|J|=6}|c_J|^2,\qquad
 L_i^{(6)}=\sum_{\substack{|J|=6\\J\cap G_i\ne\varnothing}}|c_J|^2.
 \tag{Fixed-d-complete-coefficients}
\]
A set contributing to \(\sigma_6^2\) may contain several different input labels at the same physical position.
No two individual variables in such a set are identified.

The conclusion obtained from the stated geometric input is a fixed \(c>0\) such that, almost surely eventually, uniformly over \(|S|\le r\) on their invertible branches,
\[
 \sigma_{6,\mathrm{phys}}^2
 \ge cN^{-6}(q_1q_2q_3)^2,\qquad
 \max_i L_i^{(6)}/\sigma_6^2\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Fixed-d-physical-sixth-target}
\]
For every fixed \(B_0<\infty\), on the same scope,
\[
 \sigma_6\ge c_{B_0}N^{-3}
       \sqrt{\det(I_3+\mathcal B^TZ^2\mathcal B)}
 \qquad(\|\mathcal B\|\le B_0).
 \tag{Fixed-d-coefficient-area-target}
\]
The statements are formal coefficient conclusions, not original-law anti-concentration.
Their almost-sure strength follows from the exact strength of C187v26's geometric input; no new summable least-value estimate is asserted.

## Complete candidate proof

<a id="whole-space-local-maps"></a>
### Whole-space Gram and physically local factor bounds

Put \(g=\chi_A\) and \(\Lambda=\sum_j\|A_j\|^2\).
The [whole-space calculation in L63v3](../ledger/Yin_L_F0063_v3.md#positive-frame-candidate) gives
\[
 [\langle U_ah,U_bh\rangle]_{a,b=1}^3\succeq g\|h\|^2I_3,\qquad
 \sum_{a=1}^3U_a^TU_a\preceq\Lambda I.
 \tag{Fixed-d-whole-Gram}
\]
Directly, for real \(z\in\mathbb R^3\), its first quadratic form is
\(\sum_{j,i}|z^TA_jh_i|^2\ge g|z|^2\sum_i\|h_i\|^2\), since the original complex-direction frame includes these real directions.
The stack is exactly \((\sum_jA_j^2)\otimes I_n\), and its trace over the three Gram directions also gives \(3gI\preceq\sum_aU_a^TU_a\).
In particular \(3g\le\Lambda\).
These statements hold on the complete inverse space and every spectral restriction, without a low-energy cutoff.

The decisive local identities are
\[
 \widehat P_iU_a=U_aP_i,\qquad U_a^T\widehat P_i=P_iU_a^T,
 \qquad \|U_a\|\le\sqrt\Lambda.
\]
They give, for every real symmetric \(D\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|K_{ab}(D)\|_F&\le\Lambda\|D\|_F,&
 \|K_{ab}(D)\|&\le\Lambda\|D\|,\\
 \|\widehat P_iK_{ab}(D)\|_F^2&\le\Lambda^2d_i(D),&
 \sum_i\|\widehat P_iK_{ab}(D)\widehat P_i\|_F^2
     &\le\Lambda^2\mathcal D(D).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Fixed-d-local-factor-bounds}
\]
For example, the unsymmetrized row term is \(U_aP_iDU_b^T\), and the diagonal term is \(U_aP_iDP_iU_b^T\).
The other symmetrized term has the same bound; the triangle inequality proves the displayed estimates.
There is no factor growing with \(n\) or \(N\).
Changing the physical variable group from two to fixed \(d\) changes the fixed maps and constants, not this identity.

For every residual \(E_k\), the scalar quadratic combination is
\[
 \sum_{a,b}z_az_bK_{ab}(E_k)=U(z)E_kU(z)^T,\qquad
 U(z)=\sum_a z_aU_a.
\]
The whole-space Gram implies \(U(z)^TU(z)\succeq g|z|^2I\).
For \(z\ne0\), its left inverse has norm at most \((\sqrt g|z|)^{-1}\).
Applying that inverse on both sides shows
\[
 \|U(z)E_kU(z)^T\|_F\ge g|z|^2F_k,
 \tag{Fixed-d-signed-quadratic-separation}
\]
including all signs in \(E_k\).
This verifies the high-rank coefficient interface directly on the complete residual.

### Mixed physical collision and group-load estimates

Write \(\mathsf H_\ell=\lambda_\ell e_\ell e_\ell^T\),
\(\eta_{\ell i}=\|P_ie_\ell\|^2\), and
\(\vartheta_\ell=\max_i\eta_{\ell i}\).
Then
\[
 \|\mathsf H_\ell\|_F=q_\ell,\qquad
 d_i(\mathsf H_\ell)=q_\ell^2\eta_{\ell i},\qquad
 \mathcal D(\mathsf H_\ell)
   =q_\ell^2\sum_i\eta_{\ell i}^2\le q_\ell^2\vartheta_\ell.
\]
Put
\[
 \varepsilon_k=\frac{\mathcal D(E_k)+d_*(E_k)}{F_k^2}.
\]
C187v26 with mode-removal bound at least two supplies
\(\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2\to0\) almost surely uniformly over the actual invertible minors and head choices.

Here is the explicit fixed-\(d\) transfer of [C187v24's mixed collision mechanism](Yin_C_F0187_v24.md#mixed-physical-collisions).
For a product of three quadratic-factor tensors with scales \(f_\ell>0\), suppose factor \(\ell\) has squared norm at most \(Cf_\ell^2\), group-\(i\) row mass at most \(Cf_\ell^2\rho_{\ell i}\), and total within-group diagonal mass at most \(Cf_\ell^2\epsilon_\ell\), with \(\sum_i\rho_{\ell i}\le1\).
A collision of two slots in that same factor has squared mass at most
\(C(\prod_\ell f_\ell^2)\epsilon_\ell\).
A collision between slots of distinct factors \(\ell,t\) has squared mass at most
\[
 C\Bigl(\prod_\ell f_\ell^2\Bigr)
       \sum_i\rho_{\ell i}\rho_{ti}
 \le C\Bigl(\prod_\ell f_\ell^2\Bigr)\max_i\rho_{\ell i}.
\]
These are sums over all \(d\) individual coordinates in the relevant group.
The within-factor sum is precisely controlled by the rank-\(d\) diagonal block in (Fixed-d-local-factor-bounds), and the cross-factor sum by its group row masses.
There are fifteen possible pairs of six slots.
The squared mass of all tuples meeting one fixed group \(i\) is at most
\[
 C\Bigl(\prod_\ell f_\ell^2\Bigr)\sum_\ell\rho_{\ell i},
\]
by the union over the six slots.
The collision and group-incidence coordinate projections commute with slot symmetrization.
Arithmetic-average symmetrization is a contraction, so these estimates survive symmetrization and any fixed finite sum of determinant terms.
No argument here requires \(d=2\).

For a head use \(f_\ell=q_\ell\), \(\rho_{\ell i}=\eta_{\ell i}\), and \(\epsilon_\ell=\vartheta_\ell\).
For a residual use \(f=F_k\), \(\rho_i=d_i(E_k)/F_k^2\), and \(\epsilon=\varepsilon_k\).
The required row masses sum to one in both cases.
The matrix \(R(x)^T\mathsf H_\ell R(x)\) has rank at most one, so the determinant polynomial is separately affine in each head coefficient.
Accordingly all terms using one head twice or three times cancel after their symmetric tensor sum.
Individual unsymmetrized terms are not discarded as zero.

With one retained head, the surviving expansion has six products of scale \(F_1^3\) and eighteen of scale \(q_1F_1^2\).
If \(F_1\le C_*q_1\), their scale sum is at most \((6C_*+18)q_1F_1^2\).
With two heads it has six products of scale \(F_2^3\), eighteen of each scale \(q_\ell F_2^2\), and thirty-six of scale \(q_1q_2F_2\).
If \(F_2\le C_*q_2\), their scale sum is at most
\((6C_*^2+36C_*+36)q_1q_2F_2\).
These counts depend on the three-by-three determinant, not the number of original inputs.

Let \(\mathscr T_{\mathrm{rep}}\) restrict \(\mathscr T_6(Z)\) to tuples having a repeated physical position.
Let \(\mathscr M_i\mathscr T_6(Z)\) restrict it to all tuples meeting physical group \(G_i\).
The preceding estimates give the following alternatives, with constants depending only on fixed \(C_*,d,A_1,\ldots,A_d\):
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{scale }W&
 \|\mathscr T_{\mathrm{rep}}\|_F^2/W^2&
 \|\mathscr M_i\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F^2/W^2\\ \hline
 F_0^3&
 \le C\varepsilon_0&
 \le C\,d_i(E_0)/F_0^2\\
 q_1F_1^2,\ F_1\le C_*q_1&
 \le C(\vartheta_1+\varepsilon_1)&
 \le C(\eta_{1i}+d_i(E_1)/F_1^2)\\
 q_1q_2F_2,\ F_2\le C_*q_2&
 \le C(\vartheta_1+\vartheta_2+\varepsilon_2)&
 \le C(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i}+d_i(E_2)/F_2^2).
 \end{array}
 \tag{Fixed-d-mixed-collision-loads}
\]
Each row is an upper estimate; a compatible lower tensor scale is supplied next.
In particular the coarse scale \(F_0^3\) is not imposed in a separated-pole branch.

### Individual-variable Walsh reduction and physical group loads

Let \(\mathscr T_{\mathrm{dist}}\) restrict the symmetric tensor to six distinct physical positions.
It is orthogonal to \(\mathscr T_{\mathrm{rep}}\).
The geometric quantities in (Fixed-d-mixed-collision-loads) tend to zero uniformly on the stated probability-one event.
Together with (Fixed-d-compatible-tensor-scale), this gives
\[
 \|\mathscr T_{\mathrm{dist}}\|_F^2\ge c'W^2
\]
eventually for every permitted invertible minor.
The conclusion uses a finite number of fixed branch constants, so changing regimes along the sequence causes no extra exception.

For six distinct individual variable indices \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_6\), the complete coefficient of their set \(J\) is exactly
\[
 c_J=-6!N^{-3}
      (\mathscr T_6(Z))_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_6}.
 \tag{Fixed-d-top-Walsh-coefficient}
\]
The minus sign is from the degree-six part of \(p\).
A repeated individual variable in a raw degree-six monomial lowers its Walsh degree by at least two; a lower raw degree cannot produce degree six.
Thus repeated-variable reduction adds no other contribution to (Fixed-d-top-Walsh-coefficient).
Six different physical positions imply six different individual variables, while the converse need not hold.
Every unordered individually distinct six-set has exactly \(6!\) ordered representations, independently of \(d\).
It follows that
\[
 \sigma_{6,\mathrm{phys}}^2
 =6!N^{-6}\|\mathscr T_{\mathrm{dist}}\|_F^2
 \ge cN^{-6}W^2.
 \tag{Fixed-d-physical-energy}
\]

Let \(\mathscr T_{\mathrm{ind}}\) be the restriction to six distinct individual variables, with no restriction on their physical positions.
The same exact counting gives
\[
 \sigma_6^2=6!N^{-6}\|\mathscr T_{\mathrm{ind}}\|_F^2,\qquad
 L_i^{(6)}
 =6!N^{-6}\|\mathscr M_i\mathscr T_{\mathrm{ind}}\|_F^2
 \le6!N^{-6}\|\mathscr M_i\mathscr T_6(Z)\|_F^2.
\]
The last column of (Fixed-d-mixed-collision-loads) therefore has maximum \(o(W^2/N^6)\), while \(\sigma_6^2\ge\sigma_{6,\mathrm{phys}}^2\ge cW^2/N^6\).
This proves the grouped-load conclusion for the complete sixth layer, including coefficients using several input variables from a single physical position.
No estimate here replaces a physical group by a single sign.

### Graph area and the exact fixed-input probability interface

\(q_3^2\ge F_2^2/(3n)\); hence \(q_3\ge1\) eventually uniformly on the actual invertible branches.

 \le(1+B_0^2)^{3/2}q_1q_2q_3.

Together with (Fixed-d-physical-energy), this proves (Fixed-d-coefficient-area-target).

 \tag{Fixed-d-measurable-area-scale}

 \sigma_i\ge\kappa a_iN^{-3}

with fixed \(\kappa>0\).
