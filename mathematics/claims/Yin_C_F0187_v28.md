# An all-center joint gap and the two-sided companion covariance

This project deduction extends the accepted compact-center real Wigner joint gap to all scalar centers and matches its covariance consequence for the actual companion of \(AB+2BA-zI\).
The complete statement is adopted after [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v28.md#all-center-companion-reviewed), at its exact real-input and covariance scope.
Its [D11 development](../drafts/Yin_D_F0011.md#all-center-joint-gap-and-companion-covariance) is extraction provenance, not a proof dependency.
The full physical Schur calculation remains at the [continuing draft](../drafts/Yin_D_F0011.md#two-sided-physical-schur-continuation), outside this Claim's statement.
No complete small-ball, least-value or logarithmic conclusion is claimed.

<a id="all-center-gap-model"></a>
## Exact model and candidate conclusions

Let \(A_N,B_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner inputs.
For input \(j=1,2\), the strict upper entries are independent copies of \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\xi_j\) is a fixed real centered variance-one law; the diagonals are independent copies of \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\zeta_j\) is fixed, real, centered and has finite second moment.
All upper-triangular coordinates and the two inputs are independent within each size, with arbitrary dependence across sizes.
For \(S\subset[N]\), \(|S|\le1\), put \(n=N-|S|\), and retain the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization in the principal minors \(A_{N,S},B_{N,S}\).
The two input laws may differ, and no moment above two is assumed.

There exist constants \(\rho,c,C>0\) and a deterministic sequence \(\eta_N\to0\), depending only on the fixed laws, such that with probability at least \(1-\eta_N-Ce^{-cN}\), simultaneously for all these \(S\),
\[
 \inf_{\substack{u\in\mathbb R^n,\ \|u\|=1\\a,b\in\mathbb R}}
 \max\{\|(A_{N,S}-aI)u\|,\|(B_{N,S}-bI)u\|\}\ge\rho.
 \tag{All-center-real-gap}
\]
On this same event, for every \(u\in\mathbb C^n\) and every \(a,b\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 \|(A_{N,S}-aI)u\|^2+\|(B_{N,S}-bI)u\|^2
 \ge\rho^2\|u\|^2.
 \tag{All-center-complex-sum-gap}
\]
Consequently the corresponding maximum is at least \(\rho\|u\|/\sqrt2\).
No summability or rate for \(\eta_N\) is claimed.

Here is the precise covariance consumer.
For \(u,v,t\in\mathbb C^n\), let \(\alpha,\beta\) be independent real vectors with independent coordinates of laws \(\xi_1,\xi_2\), respectively, independent of these coefficients.
Put
\[
 X=(\alpha^Tv+2\beta^Tt,\ \beta^Tu,\ \alpha^Tu)^T,
 \qquad \Gamma(u,v,t):=\mathbb E[XX^*].
\]
For every fixed \(K\ge1\), define
\[
 a_\rho:=\min\{1/2,\rho/(8\sqrt3)\},\qquad
 c_\rho:=\rho^2/110592.
\]
Outside probability at most \(\eta_N+Ce^{-cN}+C N^{1-2K}\), the following holds simultaneously for all \(S\) and all complex triples with
\[
 \|u\|^2+\|v\|^2+\|t\|^2=1,\qquad
 \delta:=\bigl(\|v-B_{N,S}u\|^2+\|t-A_{N,S}u\|^2\bigr)^{1/2}
 \le a_\rho N^{-K}:
\]
\[
 \|u\|\ge\frac{N^{-K}}{2\sqrt3},\qquad
 \det\Gamma\ge\frac{\rho^2}{6912}N^{-6K},\qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma/N)\ge c_\rho N^{-6K-1}.
 \tag{Companion-polynomial-covariance}
\]
These are uniform deterministic Gram bounds on that event.
They are actual conditional Hermitian covariance bounds for a candidate chosen measurably from a physical principal minor before exposing its two original off-diagonal rows.
The conditional interpretation is not asserted for a candidate depending on those rows.
The first equation of the pencil below is not used, so the conclusion applies to all parameters \(z\in\mathbb C\) when its graph error satisfies the displayed hypothesis.
It does not give positive six-dimensional real covariance, arithmetic anticoncentration, or a bound on the total small-ball probability.

## Complete candidate argument

<a id="all-center-gap-proof"></a>
### Accepted conditional subspace interface with both input orders

Apply C187v12 once with \(A\) as first input and \(B\) as second, and once with the order reversed.
For either order write the second input as \(D_N=T_N+W_N\), using that proof's fixed cutoff.
Its conditioning sigma-field consists of the full first input and the second input's good/bad mask, bad raw values and all diagonals.
The centered good strict-upper coordinates of \(W_N\) remain conditionally independent with their actual truncated laws; missing bad edges are zero, and no replacement Gaussian or identically distributed matrix is used.
The two orientations may use different fixed cutoffs and constants.

For each orientation, C187v12 supplies \(\kappa>0\), \(g=1/8\), a fixed \(B_L\ge1\), and common tail and mask events whose failures are respectively \(p_{T,N}\to0\) and \(Ce^{-cN}\).
For any first-input-measurable real subspace \(V\subset\mathbb R^n\) with \(\dim V\le\kappa n\), its actual projection is
\[
 P_0=\mathbf1_{[-1,1]}(T_{N,S}),\qquad
 P=\operatorname{proj}\bigl(\operatorname{Ran}P_0\cap(T_{N,S}V+V)^\perp\bigr).
\]
On the tail event, it satisfies \(PV=PT_{N,S}V=0\), \(\|PT_{N,S}\|\le1\), and the codimension bound from that proof.
Outside a conditional \(Ce^{-cN}\) event, on the two common guards, simultaneously for every \(w\in V\) and every real \(b\),
\[
 \|P(D_{N,S}-bI)w\|=\|PW_{N,S}w\|\ge g\|w\|,
 \qquad
 \|P(D_{N,S}-bI)\|\le C_0+|b|,
 \quad C_0=1+B_L.
 \tag{All-center-compressed-interface}
\]
The second bound is an operator bound on the entire ambient space, not only on \(V\).
The same conditional bounded-norm event controls all principal minors of \(W_N\).
If \(V=\{0\}\), its lower-bound assertion is vacuous and the proof below treats it without selecting a nonexistent unit vector.
The projection may depend on the realized second-input tail; this is exactly the dependence allowed by the adopted proof.

<a id="all-center-gap-high-centers"></a>
### A large center selects a small high-spectral subspace

Fix \(R=3>2\).
For the orientation with first input \(A\), use
\[
 V_{A,S}=\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf1_{\mathbb R\setminus[-R,R]}(A_{N,S}).
\]
The scalar semicircle law gives a full-parent event, failing with probability \(p_{H,A,N}\to0\), on which the full \(A_N\) has at most \(\kappa N/2\) eigenvalues outside \([-R,R]\).
Principal interlacing changes this count by at most two for one deletion, so eventually \(\dim V_{A,S}\le\kappa(N-|S|)\) for every \(S\).
This uses no operator-norm bound for \(A_N\).
The reverse orientation uses the same construction for \(B\) and its own \(\kappa\).
Apply (All-center-compressed-interface) to these \(N+1\) subspaces in each orientation.
The conditional union costs at most \(Ce^{-cN}\) after changing constants.
Integrating the conditional estimates pays each orientation's common tail, high-spectrum and mask exception once.
Include also the accepted C187v12 compact-center gap on \([-2R,2R]^2\), with constant \(\epsilon_{2R}>0\).
The aggregate common exceptional bound is \(\eta_N+Ce^{-cN}\) for some \(\eta_N\to0\); no common \(o(1)\) term is multiplied by \(N\).

On this event suppose a real unit \(u\) has both residuals at most \(\rho\).
Choose \(\rho>0\) smaller than \(\epsilon_{2R}/2\), \(R/2\), and
\[
 \frac{g}{4(3+C_0/R)}
 \tag{All-center-constant-choice}
\]
for both orientations.
If both centers lie in \([-2R,2R]\), the compact-center gap is a contradiction.
Otherwise orient the inputs so that \(|a|\ge|b|\) and \(|a|>2R\), where \(a\) is the first input's center.
Let \(w\) be the orthogonal projection of \(u\) onto its \(V_{A,S}\).
Spectral calculus on the complementary interval gives
\[
 d:=\|u-w\|\le\frac{\rho}{|a|-R}\le\frac{\rho}{R}\le\frac12,
 \qquad \|w\|\ge\frac12.
\]
In particular this case cannot occur with \(V_{A,S}=\{0\}\).
Using the lower bound on \(w\) and the ambient compressed-transfer bound on \(u-w\),
\[
 \frac g2\le g\|w\|
 \le\|P(B_{N,S}-bI)w\|
 \le\rho+(C_0+|b|)\frac{\rho}{|a|-R}
 \le\rho(3+C_0/R)\le\frac g4,
\]
since \(|b|\le|a|\) and \(|a|/(|a|-R)\le2\).
This contradiction proves (All-center-real-gap).
The parameter order is the two fixed cutoffs and subspace constants, then the fixed compact interval and its gap, and finally \(\rho\), all before \(N\to\infty\).
The all-center gap is a measurable property: for fixed real unit \(u\), minimizing either squared residual over its real center gives its Rayleigh quotient, leaving a continuous function on a compact unit sphere.
This also defines a minor-measurable event for each individual \(S\).

<a id="all-center-complex-extension"></a>
### Complex vectors and centers on the same real-input event

For real \(a,b\) and \(u=p+iq\), the sum of the two squared residuals splits into the sums for real vectors \(p,q\).
The real maximum bound implies each such sum is at least \(\rho^2\) times the squared vector norm, including zero vectors by homogeneity.
Adding proves (All-center-complex-sum-gap) for real centers.
For a Hermitian \(H\), complex \(u\), and \(a=\alpha+i\beta\),
\[
 \|(H-aI)u\|^2=\|(H-\alpha I)u\|^2+\beta^2\|u\|^2,
\]
because \(u^*(H-\alpha I)u\) is real.
Applying this identity to both inputs proves the full complex-center assertion without changing \(\rho\).
The input matrices remain real symmetric throughout; this is not an extension to complex-entry Wigner laws.

<a id="companion-hermitian-covariance"></a>
### Exact fresh-row covariance and approximate graph stability

Put \(s=\|u\|^2\).
Independence, centering and variance one of the raw coordinates give exactly
\[
 \Gamma=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \|v\|^2+4\|t\|^2&2u^*t&u^*v\\
 2t^*u&s&0\\
 v^*u&0&s
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Two-sided-row-Hermitian-covariance}
\]
For \(u\ne0\), write \(v_\perp=v-u(u^*v)/s\) and \(t_\perp=t-u(u^*t)/s\).
The Schur complement of its last two entries yields
\[
 \det\Gamma=s^2\Delta,\qquad
 \Delta=\|v_\perp\|^2+4\|t_\perp\|^2.
 \tag{Two-sided-covariance-determinant}
\]
For exact companions \(v=B_{N,S}u\), \(t=A_{N,S}u\), their minimizing centers are their real Rayleigh quotients.
Thus (All-center-complex-sum-gap) implies \(\Delta\ge\rho^2s\).
For approximate companions, write \(e_v=v-B_{N,S}u\), \(e_t=t-A_{N,S}u\).
Contraction of the projection onto \(u^\perp\) and the triangle inequality on the weighted direct sum give
\[
 \sqrt\Delta\ge\rho\|u\|-\sqrt{\|e_v\|^2+4\|e_t\|^2}
 \ge\rho\|u\|-2\delta.
 \tag{Approximate-companion-gap}
\]
Only the positive lower-bound regime of this inequality will be squared.

<a id="companion-normalization-and-average"></a>
### Polynomial mass, original-row interpretation and the average

The full-parent event \(H_N(K)=\{\max(\|A_N\|,\|B_N\|)\le N^K\}\) has
\[
 \mathbb P(H_N(K)^c)
 \le N^{-2K}\mathbb E(\|A_N\|_F^2+\|B_N\|_F^2)
 \le C N^{1-2K}.
\]
Indeed each input has \(\mathbb E\|A_N\|_F^2=N-1+\mathbb E\zeta_1^2\), and similarly for \(B_N\).
Every principal minor inherits this norm bound.
For a unit triple with \(\delta\le a_\rho N^{-K}\le1/2\),
\[
 1\le\sqrt{1+2N^{2K}}\,\|u\|+\delta,
 \qquad \|u\|\ge\frac{N^{-K}}{2\sqrt3}.
\]
The choice of \(a_\rho\) ensures \(2\delta\le(\rho/2)\|u\|\).
Hence (Approximate-companion-gap) gives \(\Delta\ge\rho^2s/4\), and \(s\ge N^{-2K}/12\).
Substitution into (Two-sided-covariance-determinant) proves the displayed determinant bound.
Moreover \(\operatorname{tr}\Gamma=2s+\|v\|^2+4\|t\|^2\le4\), so the product of the two largest eigenvalues is at most \(16\).
This proves \(\lambda_{\min}\Gamma\ge\rho^2N^{-6K}/110592\), and division by \(N\) gives (Companion-polynomial-covariance).
For any fixed \(q>0\), taking \(K\ge\max\{1,(q+1)/2\}\) makes only this norm-guard failure \(O(N^{-q})\).
The common joint-gap error \(\eta_N\) remains \(o(1)\), not an arbitrary-power or summable error.

For \(S=\{i\}\), the two original off-diagonal rows are independent of \((A_{N,S},B_{N,S})\), have laws \(\alpha/\sqrt N,\beta/\sqrt N\), and may also be conditioned independently of the two diagonal entries at \(i\).
For a triple measurable with respect to that minor before the row exposure, (Two-sided-row-Hermitian-covariance) divided by \(N\) is precisely its conditional Hermitian covariance.
Let \(G_{N,S}\) be the minor-measurable event consisting of its all-center real gap and its two operator-norm bounds \(N^K\).
The common events used above imply all \(G_{N,S}\) simultaneously, so
\[
 \mathbb P\Bigl(\bigcup_{|S|\le1}G_{N,S}^c\Bigr)
 \le\eta_N+Ce^{-cN}+CN^{1-2K},\qquad
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb P(G_{N,\{i\}}^c)
 \le\eta_N+Ce^{-cN}+CN^{1-2K}.
 \tag{Companion-common-and-average-error}
\]
There is no extra \(N\) cost in the average or in the common \(o(1)\) guards.
The full-parent event is used to prove these unconditional bounds; conditioning on that event is not used to claim the original fresh-row law survives.
On each minor-measurable \(G_{N,\{i\}}\), the conditional conclusion applies whenever the chosen triple satisfies the graph-error hypothesis.

<a id="companion-use-boundary"></a>
### Exact companion use and the missing probabilistic interface

The actual pencil is
\[
 \mathcal L_{N,S}(z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -zI&A_{N,S}&2B_{N,S}\\
 B_{N,S}&-I&0\\
 A_{N,S}&0&-I
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Actual-two-sided-companion}
\]
For a unit \((u,v,t)\) with \(\|\mathcal L_{N,S}(z)(u,v,t)^T\|\le a_\rho N^{-K}\), its last two block residuals have combined norm \(\delta\le a_\rho N^{-K}\).
Thus the deterministic covariance conclusion derived above applies uniformly in \(z\), including nonreal parameters, without a parameter net.
This does not produce an admissible minor-measurable candidate for an adaptive near-kernel of a larger matrix.
If a triple depends on the fresh rows, the formula is still its deterministic coefficient Gram, but freezing it does not supply its asserted conditional random law.

A positive \(3\times3\) Hermitian covariance does not imply a positive \(6\times6\) real covariance of \((\Re X,\Im X)\); already real coefficients yield \(X\in\mathbb R^3\).
Even positive real covariance would not exclude original-law atoms or give the required high-concentration profile bound.
The statement provides neither the source-to-structured reduction nor a complete Schur small-ball estimate, least-value floor, middle negative-logarithm profile, logarithmic determinant convergence or Brown convergence for \(AB+2BA\).
The first useful next test is whether a legally minor-measurable object can carry this gap through the [full physical Schur expression retained in D11](../drafts/Yin_D_F0011.md#two-sided-physical-schur-continuation) while retaining the same fresh rows in its normalization.
