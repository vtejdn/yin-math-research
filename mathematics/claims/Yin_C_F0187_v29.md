# Uniform joint Wigner gap after erasing a small set of coordinates

This fourth-moment variant strengthens the [retained real finite-variance all-center gap](Yin_C_F0187_v28.md#statement) by allowing complex input laws and deletion of a small positive fraction of output coordinates.
The v28 result and its original weaker moment assumptions remain at their exact scope.
The complete statement and its auxiliary sparse lemma are adopted after [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v29.md#erased-joint-gap-whole-proof).
No column-distance small-ball or least-singular-value conclusion is claimed.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact statement

Let \(A_N,B_N\) be two independent complex Hermitian Wigner matrices with fixed centered variance-one off-diagonal laws of finite fourth absolute moment.
Their real diagonal laws are fixed, centered, and have finite second moment.
All upper coordinates and both inputs are independent within each size, with original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization and arbitrary cross-size coupling.
Real-imaginary dependence and phase-line laws are allowed.
Fix \(K\ge1\) and an integer \(r\ge0\).
For \(D\subset[N]\), let \(A_D,B_D\) be the principal minors outside \(D\), keeping the original normalization.
There exist \(\delta,c_*,c,C>0\) and \(N_0\), depending on these fixed inputs, such that for all \(N\ge N_0\), outside an event of probability at most \(Ce^{-cN}\), the following holds simultaneously for every \(|D|\le r\) on its minor norm guard \(\max(\|A_D\|,\|B_D\|)\le K\).
For every \(S\subset[N]\setminus D\) with \(|S|\le\delta N\), put \(T=[N]\setminus(D\cup S)\).
For every \(u\in\mathbb C^{[N]\setminus D}\) and every \(a,b\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 \|P_T(A_D-aI)u\|^2+\|P_T(B_D-bI)u\|^2
 \ge c_*^2\|u\|^2.
 \tag{Erased-all-center-joint-gap}
\]
The probability assertion estimates guarded failures and does not assert that the norm guard itself has a summable complement.
The finite-second-moment diagonal assumption enters the spectral-window input; it is not replaced by the more general diagonal scope of the auxiliary sparse lemma below.

<a id="sparse-two-image"></a>
## Auxiliary sparse two-image separation

Use two independent complex Hermitian Wigner matrices \(A_N,B_N\) with the fixed centered variance-one off-diagonal laws and finite fourth absolute moments of [C196](../claims/Yin_C_F0196_v1.md#statement).
The diagonal laws are arbitrary for the guarded assertion.
Fix \(K\ge1\) and a nonnegative integer \(r\).
For \(D\subset[N]\), write \(A_D,B_D\) for the principal minors outside \(D\), retaining the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
There exist \(\delta,g,c,C>0\), depending only on \(K,r\) and the two fourth-moment bounds, such that outside an event of probability at most \(Ce^{-cN}\), the following holds simultaneously for every \(|D|\le r\) on the event \(\max(\|A_D\|,\|B_D\|)\le K\).
For every \(S\subset[N]\setminus D\) with \(|S|\le\delta N\), every \(h\in\mathbb C^S\), and all \(a,b\in\mathbb C\), putting \(T=[N]\setminus(D\cup S)\),
\[
 \|aA_{T,S}h+bB_{T,S}h\|
 \ge g\sqrt{|a|^2+|b|^2}\,\|h\|.
 \tag{Sparse-two-image-gap}
\]
Equivalently, the Gram matrix of the two columns \(A_{T,S}h,B_{T,S}h\) is bounded below by \(g^2\|h\|^2I_2\).
In particular the two images cannot be proportional when \(h\ne0\).
Neither independence of real and imaginary parts nor a probability estimate for the norm-guard complement is assumed.

## Proof of the sparse two-image estimate

Reuse the [complex affine fourth-moment and rectangular estimates in C196](../claims/Yin_C_F0196_v1.md#affine-small-ball), which explicitly permit different coordinate laws and their conjugates.
They are a proved input, with their actual Rudelson--Vershynin method attribution retained there.
For fixed disjoint \(D,S\), fixed unit \(h\in\mathbb C^S\), and fixed \((a,b)\in\mathbb C^2\) of Euclidean norm one, each coordinate of \(aA_{T,S}h+bB_{T,S}h\) is a sum over \(2|S|\) independent centered raw entries divided by \(\sqrt N\).
The coefficient vector has squared norm \((|a|^2+|b|^2)\|h\|^2=1\).
Every raw entry has variance one and a common fourth-moment bound, even when its position changes its law to the complex conjugate.
Distinct output rows use disjoint unordered pairs, and the two input matrices are independent.
Thus the output rows are independent.
For \(N\) sufficiently large and \(|S|\le N/8\), their number \(|T|\) is at least \(N/5\).
The cited rectangular bound supplies constants \(g_0,c_0>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|aA_{T,S}h+bB_{T,S}h\|<g_0\}
 \le e^{-c_0N}.
 \tag{Fixed-rectangular-test}
\]
No norm event is conditioned into this estimate.

Choose \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\) so that \(2\sqrt2K\eta\le g_0/2\).
Fix an \(\eta\)-net of the complex unit sphere in \(\mathbb C^S\), of size at most \((1+2/\eta)^{2|S|}\), and one of the unit sphere in \(\mathbb C^2\), of size at most \((1+2/\eta)^4\).
On the minor norm guard, replacing \(h\) and \((a,b)\) by their net approximants changes the output norm by at most \(2\sqrt2K\eta\).
Indeed the first change costs at most \((|a|+|b|)K\eta\le\sqrt2K\eta\), and the second costs at most \(K(|a-a_0|+|b-b_0|)\le\sqrt2K\eta\).
Thus a violation of (Sparse-two-image-gap) with \(g=g_0/2\) forces one of the net tests in (Fixed-rectangular-test) to fail.

Take \(s=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\), initially with \(\delta<1/8\), and first take the union only over \(|S|=s\).
For \(|D|\le r\), the number of pairs \((D,S)\) is at most \((r+1)N^r{N\choose s}\).
Consequently the probability of any guarded failure is at most
\[
 (r+1)N^r{N\choose s}
 (1+2/\eta)^{2s+4}e^{-c_0N}.
\]
Choose \(\delta>0\) sufficiently small that
\(\delta\log(e/\delta)+2\delta\log(1+2/\eta)<c_0/2\).
The displayed probability is then at most \(Ce^{-cN}\), after absorbing the fixed-degree polynomial factor.
Every smaller support can be enlarged to size \(s\) while remaining disjoint from \(D\), and restricting to the smaller output set of that enlargement can only decrease the output norm.
The estimate for size \(s\) therefore implies the statement for all smaller supports.
Homogeneity restores arbitrary \(h,a,b\), including zero vectors and coefficients.

## Proof of the erased all-center joint gap

### Center cancellation forces mass outside the erased set

Use the [sparse two-image lemma](#sparse-two-image) with constants \(\delta_0,g,c_0,C_0\), and restrict throughout to its good event and the stated minor norm guard.
Its estimate holds for every complex coefficient pair on the same event.
Suppose \(\|u\|=1\) and the square root of the left side of (Erased-all-center-joint-gap) is at most \(\epsilon\).
Write \(u=h+t\), where \(h=P_Su\) and \(t=P_Tu\), and put
\[
 e_A=P_T(A_D-aI)u,\qquad e_B=P_T(B_D-bI)u.
\]
If \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), the identity
\[
 (bA_{T,S}-aB_{T,S})h
 =be_A-ae_B-P_T(bA_D-aB_D)t
\]
and the sparse lemma give, after dividing by \(\sqrt{|a|^2+|b|^2}\),
\[
 g\|h\|\le\epsilon+\sqrt2K\|t\|.
 \tag{Center-cancelled-root-mass}
\]
For \(a=b=0\), using the coefficient pair \((1,0)\) in the sparse lemma gives the same weaker inequality directly from the first residual.
Take
\[
 \tau=\min\{1/2,g/(4\sqrt2K)\}.
\]
If \(\epsilon\le g/4\) and \(\|t\|<\tau\), then \(\|h\|\ge\sqrt3/2\), while the right side of (Center-cancelled-root-mass) is at most \(g/2\), a contradiction.
Thus \(\|t\|\ge\tau\).
Each residual now gives
\[
 |a|,|b|\le R:=(K+g/4)/\tau.
 \tag{Erased-center-bound}
\]
It is therefore enough to exclude small residuals for centers in this fixed complex disk.
Shrinking the eventual \(\delta\) below \(\delta_0\) preserves all these conclusions.

### An independent second input separates every small fixed subspace

Let \(V\subset\mathbb C^{[N]\setminus D}\) be any subspace measurable with respect to the first input, of dimension at most \(\kappa' N\).
Fix a unit \(v\in V\), a deterministic erased set \(S\), and a fixed center \(b_0\) with \(|b_0|\le R+1\).
Partition \([N]\setminus D\) deterministically into two nearly equal sets \(I,J\).
Use the orientation for which \(\|v_J\|\ge1/\sqrt2\); this choice depends only on the already fixed vector.
Condition on the first input and the original second-input coordinates within \(I\).
On the output set \(I\setminus S\),
\[
 ((B_D-b_0I)v)_{I\setminus S}
 =B_{I\setminus S,J}v_J+w,
\]
where the complex center vector \(w\) is fixed by this conditioning.
The rectangle consists of independent original off-diagonal entries, possibly conjugated according to their positions, and its distinct output rows use disjoint unordered pairs.
The mirrored rectangle is not an additional independent family.
For \(\delta\le1/16\) and large \(N\), the number of output rows is at least \(N/5\).
C196's rectangular affine bound therefore gives constants \(b_*,c_B>0\), depending only on the fourth moments, such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|P_T(B_D-b_0I)v\|<b_*\mid A_N\}
 \le e^{-c_BN}.
 \tag{Fixed-second-input-test}
\]
One may take \(b_*\) to be the C196 rectangular threshold divided by \(\sqrt2\).
No norm event is conditioned into the fresh rectangle law.

Choose a fixed sphere-net radius \(\eta_V\in(0,1/2)\) with
\((K+R+1)\eta_V\le b_*/4\), and let \(\beta=b_*/2\).
A complex unit-sphere net of \(V\) has size at most \((1+2/\eta_V)^{2\dim V}\).
On \(\|B_D\|\le K\), if the test norm is at least \(b_*\) at every net point, it is greater than \(\beta\) at every unit vector of \(V\).
Conditional on the first input, the probability of failure of this guarded subspace assertion is consequently at most
\[
 (1+2/\eta_V)^{2\kappa'N}e^{-c_BN}.
 \tag{Small-subspace-conditional-bound}
\]
The net may be chosen measurably from the subspace by a deterministic orthonormal-basis rule and a fixed Euclidean sphere net.
This estimate is uniform in that first-input realization, the subspace, erased set and fixed center, provided the displayed dimension bound holds.

### Spectral windows keep the first-input approximation small

Choose \(\kappa>0\) so small that
\[
 4\kappa\log(1+2/\eta_V)<c_B/4,
\]
and later impose \(\delta\le\kappa\).
Choose a fixed \(h\in(0,1)\) sufficiently small that every complex disk of radius \(h\) meets the semicircle law in mass less than \(\kappa/8\).
This follows directly from its bounded density on the real line.
Choose fixed sufficiently fine nets \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\) of the center disk \(\{|z|\le R\}\); their precision will be fixed in the last step and their points have modulus at most \(R+1\).

For each \(a_0\in\mathcal A\), the expected normalized eigenvalue count of the full \(A_N\) in \(\{|\lambda-a_0|\le h\}\) tends to its semicircle mass.
The boundary has zero semicircle mass, including the tangent or empty cases.
The expected count is therefore at most \(\kappa/4\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), simultaneously for the finite center net.
The C186v4 row-block concentration bound for an interval indicator implies that the full-parent count exceeds \(\kappa N/2\) with probability at most \(Ce^{-c_A N}\).
For a complex center, its intersection with the real line is an interval, a point, or the empty set; the same bounded-variation count argument applies.
Principal interlacing changes an interval count by at most \(2r\) after at most \(r\) physical deletions.
Thus, on one event with exponentially small failure, all these minor windows have dimension at most \(\kappa N\).
Only expected weak convergence and bounded-variation concentration are used here; no rate for convergence of the expectations is asserted or needed.

For each \(D,S,a_0\), let \(F\) be the spectral projection of the Hermitian \(A_D\) onto \(\{|\lambda-a_0|\le h\}\), and define
\[
 R_{a_0}=(A_D-a_0I)^{-1}(I-F),
 \qquad
 V_{D,S,a_0}=\operatorname{Ran}F+\operatorname{Ran}(R_{a_0}P_S).
 \tag{Erased-spectral-subspace}
\]
The inverse notation means the bounded spectral multiplier outside the disk, so it is defined even if \(A_D-a_0I\) is singular.
Its norm is at most \(h^{-1}\).
The subspace is measurable from the first input and has dimension at most \(\kappa N+|S|\le2\kappa N\).
If \(\|P_T(A_D-a_0I)u\|\le e\), the exact identity
\[
 u=Fu+R_{a_0}P_S(A_D-a_0I)u
       +R_{a_0}P_T(A_D-a_0I)u
\]
gives \(\operatorname{dist}(u,V_{D,S,a_0})\le e/h\).

Apply (Small-subspace-conditional-bound) to all these subspaces and all \(b_0\in\mathcal B\), on the first-input spectral-count event.
There are at most \((r+1)N^r\binom Ns\) pairs \((D,S)\) with \(|S|=s=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\).
Choose \(\delta>0\) below \(\delta_0,\kappa,1/16\) so that \(\delta\log(e/\delta)<c_B/4\).
The support count, the subspace-net entropy and the fixed center-net cardinalities then leave a bound \(Ce^{-cN}\) after the conditional union and integration.
Together with the spectral-count and sparse-lemma failures, the same bound controls all required exceptions.
All smaller erased sets follow by enlarging them to size \(s\), because retaining additional output coordinates can only increase the joint residual norm.

### Final approximation and parameter order

Choose a fixed \(\gamma>0\) such that
\[
 \gamma\le\min\left\{g/4,h/8,
       \frac{\beta h}{16(K+R+2)}\right\},
\]
and take both center nets to have mesh at most \(\gamma\).
The constants \(g,R,\eta_V,\beta,\kappa,h\) were chosen before this precision, so the order has no circular dependence.
The finite center nets do not change the leading entropy exponents above.
Suppose now that a unit \(u\) has joint residual at most \(\gamma\).
The first step bounds both centers by \(R\), so choose \(a_0,b_0\) from the respective nets within \(\gamma\).
Then
\[
 \|P_T(A_D-a_0I)u\|\le2\gamma,
 \qquad\operatorname{dist}(u,V_{D,S,a_0})\le2\gamma/h\le1/4.
\]
Normalize the orthogonal projection of \(u\) onto this subspace to get a unit \(v\) with \(\|u-v\|\le4\gamma/h\).
On the second-input norm guard,
\[
 \|P_T(B_D-b_0I)v\|
 \le2\gamma+(K+R+1)4\gamma/h<\beta.
\]
This contradicts the simultaneous subspace lower bound.
Taking \(c_*\) smaller than \(\gamma\), if needed to make all endpoints immaterial, proves (Erased-all-center-joint-gap).


## Distance-problem boundary

A companion application must also retain root mass and establish the complete determinant coefficient and same-row denominator bounds.
No original-law determinant small-ball conclusion follows from this gap alone.
