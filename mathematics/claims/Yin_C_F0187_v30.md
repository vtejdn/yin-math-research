# Actual high-rank outer sixth tensors for AB+2BA at nonreal parameters

This is a deterministic actual-inverse coefficient statement for real symmetric inputs.
It retains the two outer bidegrees of the complete physical Schur determinant, avoiding cancellation with the constant auxiliary inverse contribution.
Its proof extends the finite contraction mechanism of [C187v18](Yin_C_F0187_v18.md#bipartite-contraction) to complex physical tensors with a nonsymmetric auxiliary matrix; that extension is proved explicitly below.
The new noncancellation argument uses the actual inverse equations and a two-by-two reduced matrix space.
It does not assume a positive frame, an erased-coordinate gap, or a probability law for the inputs.
The complete theorem is adopted after [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0191_v30.md#actual-outer-high-rank-reviewed).
The retained erased-gap statement in v29 remains separate and is not a hypothesis of this theorem.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact deterministic statement

Fix \(K\ge1\) and \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R\).
Let real symmetric \(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb R)\) satisfy \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K\), and suppose
\[
 P=AB+2BA,\qquad M=P-zI_n,\qquad G=M^{-1}
\]
are defined with \(M\) invertible.
Write \(F=\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) and \(q=\|G\|\).
For real variables \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\), put
\[
 h_B(x,y)=\det\!\left([Bx,x,y]^T G[Bx,x,y]\right),
 \qquad
 h_A(x,y)=\det\!\left([Ay,x,y]^T G[Ay,x,y]\right).
 \tag{Outer-Gram-polynomials}
\]
All coefficient tensors below are the unique arithmetic-average symmetric complex tensors on the real variable space \(\mathbb R^n\oplus\mathbb R^n\); their norms use the Hermitian Euclidean inner product on the complexified tensor space.
There are constants \(C_0,R,c>0\), depending only on \(K,z\), such that
\[
 F\ge C_0\sqrt n,\qquad F^2\ge Rq^2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|\mathscr T(h_B)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cF^3,
 \qquad \|\mathscr T(h_A)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cF^3.
 \tag{Actual-outer-high-rank-tensor}
\]
There is also an approximate-inverse version.
For any fixed \(L>0\), replace \(G\) in (Outer-Gram-polynomials) by any nonzero complex matrix \(E\) satisfying
\[
 \|ME\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\|EM\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le L\sqrt n.
 \tag{Two-sided-small-residual}
\]
Writing \(F_E=\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\), \(q_E=\|E\|\), the same two raw tensor conclusions hold when \(F_E\ge C_0\sqrt n\) and \(F_E^2\ge Rq_E^2\), with constants allowed to depend also on \(L\).
In particular this version applies, with \(L=2\), to every matrix obtained from the singular-value expansion of the actual \(G\) by removing any selected modes, provided its remaining energy and effective rank meet those bounds.
The complete Schur identities below concern the actual inverse \(G\), not an arbitrary \(E\).

For the actual companion
\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\B&-I&0\\A&0&-I\end{pmatrix},
\]
and actual physical borders
\[
 R(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&x^T&2y^T\\y^T&0&0\\x^T&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 C(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&x&2y\\y&0&0\\x&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
let \(D\in M_3(\mathbb C)\) be any fixed exposed corner and let \(\Phi_6\) be the complete homogeneous degree-six part of
\(\det(D-R(x,y)Q^{-1}C(x,y))\).
If \(\Phi_{a,b}\) denotes its component of degree \(a\) in \(x\) and degree \(b\) in \(y\), then the exact identities are
\[
 \Phi_{4,2}=4h_B,\qquad \Phi_{2,4}=4h_A.
 \tag{Actual-outer-bidegrees}
\]
In particular, under the displayed high-rank conditions,
\[
 \|\mathscr T(\Phi_6)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cF^3.
 \tag{Actual-complete-high-rank-tensor}
\]
These are raw coefficient-tensor assertions.
They do not alone supply six-distinct-physical-coordinate energy, an original-law small-ball estimate or a least-singular-value floor.

## Complete proof

<a id="individual-input-gap"></a>
### A nonreal approximate eigenvector is separated from each input spectrum

Set \(\eta=|\operatorname{Im}z|>0\).
For every complex vector \(u\) and every real \(b\), the symmetry of \(A,B\) gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Im}\langle u,Pu\rangle|
   =|\operatorname{Im}\langle Au,Bu\rangle|
   \le K\|u\|\,\|(B-bI)u\|.
\]
Here the contribution of \(b\langle Au,u\rangle\) is real.
Consequently
\[
 \eta\|u\|\le\|Mu\|+K\|(B-bI)u\|.
 \tag{Individual-B-gap}
\]
For complex \(b\), selfadjointness gives
\(\|(B-\operatorname{Re}b)u\|\le\|(B-b)u\|\), so the same inequality holds.
The analogous estimate holds for \(A-aI\), and for \(P^T-\overline z I\), with the same constants.

There are \(c_1,C_1>0\), depending only on \(K,z\), such that for every \((a,b)\in\mathbb C^2\) with \(|a|^2+|b|^2=1\), putting \(D_B=aB+bI\),
\[
 c_1\|u\|\le\|D_Bu\|+C_1\|Mu\|.
 \tag{Uniform-affine-B-gap}
\]
Indeed, if \(|a|\ge[4(K+1)]^{-1}\), multiply (Individual-B-gap) at center \(-b/a\) by \(|a|\) and absorb its fixed bounds.
Otherwise \(\|D_Bu\|\ge(|b|-K|a|)\|u\|\ge\|u\|/2\).
Applying this columnwise to \(G\) and using the Euclidean triangle inequality across columns gives
\[
 \|D_BG\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c_1F-C_1\sqrt n.
\]
Applying the transpose-left counterpart to \(G^*\) and \(D_B^*\) similarly gives
\[
 \|GD_B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c_1F-C_1\sqrt n.
 \tag{Two-sided-affine-inverse-gap}
\]
Both exact inverse identities \(MG=GM=I\) are used here.

<a id="actual-commutator-bound"></a>
### An almost commuting matrix cannot be an almost nonreal inverse mode

For every complex \(Y\in M_n(\mathbb C)\),
\[
 \eta\|Y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le\|Y(P-zI)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
                +K\|[B,Y]\|_{\mathrm{HS}},
 \tag{Right-commutator-bound}
\]
and the same bound holds with \((P-zI)Y\) in the first term.
To verify the first assertion, put \(H=Y^*Y\).
The trace \(\operatorname{Tr}(YPY^*)=\operatorname{Tr}(PH)\) has imaginary part equal up to sign to \(\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Tr}(ABH)\).
Since
\[
 2i\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Tr}(ABH)
       =\operatorname{Tr}(A[B,H]),
\]
and
\[
 \|[B,H]\|_1
 \le2\|Y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\|[B,Y]\|_{\mathrm{HS}},
\]
its absolute imaginary part is at most \(K\|Y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\|[B,Y]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\).
Taking the imaginary part of \(\operatorname{Tr}(Y(P-zI)Y^*)\), then applying the Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, proves (Right-commutator-bound), with the zero matrix handled directly.
For the left assertion use \(H=YY^*\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}(Y^*(P-zI)Y)\).
The analogous commutator estimate with \(A\) in place of \(B\) holds by the same calculation.

<a id="reduced-two-channel-separation"></a>
### The actual two-channel coefficient space has no common kernel or cokernel

Write \(\operatorname{Sym}T=(T+T^T)/2\), and form four complex symmetric physical tensors
\[
 K_{11}=\operatorname{Sym}(BGB),\quad
 K_{12}=\operatorname{Sym}(BG),\quad
 K_{21}=\operatorname{Sym}(GB),\quad
 K_{22}=G_s:=\operatorname{Sym}G.
 \tag{B-reduced-coefficients}
\]
Their Hilbert--Schmidt norms are at most \(C_KF\), and their operator norms are at most \(C_Kq\).
There are fixed \(g>0\) and a sufficiently large \(C_0\) such that, if \(F\ge C_0\sqrt n\), every unit \((a,b)\in\mathbb C^2\) satisfies both
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|aK_{11}+bK_{21}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
   +\|aK_{12}+bK_{22}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}&\ge gF,\\
 \|aK_{11}+bK_{12}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
   +\|aK_{21}+bK_{22}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}&\ge gF.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Reduced-two-sided-separation}
\]

For the first inequality put \(D_B=aB+bI\), \(Y=D_BG\), \(C_0'=\operatorname{Sym}Y\) and \(C_1'=\operatorname{Sym}(YB)\).
These are exactly its second and first coefficient combinations, respectively.
The two exact symmetry identities imply
\[
 [Y,B]=2(C_1'-BC_0').
\]
Also \(Y(P-zI)=D_B\), whose Hilbert--Schmidt norm is at most \((K+1)\sqrt n\).
The commutator bound and (Two-sided-affine-inverse-gap) therefore give
\[
 \|C_0'\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\|C_1'\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
                  \ge cF-C\sqrt n.
\]
Taking \(C_0\) sufficiently large proves the first inequality in (Reduced-two-sided-separation).
For the second, take \(Y=GD_B\), \(C_0'=\operatorname{Sym}Y\), \(C_1'=\operatorname{Sym}(BY)\).
Then \([B,Y]=2(C_1'-C_0'B)\), and \((P-zI)Y=D_B\).
The left commutator bound and the other affine inverse gap prove the assertion.

<a id="two-by-two-singular-spaces"></a>
### An elementary complex two-by-two singular-space argument

Every complex linear subspace \(\mathcal V\subset M_2(\mathbb C)\) consisting entirely of singular matrices has a common nonzero right kernel vector or a common nonzero left annihilator.
If \(\mathcal V=\{0\}\), this is immediate.
Otherwise choose a rank-one member and use invertible left and right changes of basis to make it \(E_{11}\).
For every \(T\in\mathcal V\), the polynomial identity \(\det(E_{11}+tT)=0\) gives \(T_{22}=0\), while \(\det T=0\) then gives \(T_{12}T_{21}=0\).
If the linear functional \(T\mapsto T_{12}\) is not identically zero, choose \(T_0\) where it is nonzero.
For any \(T\), all but at most one scalar \(t\) have \((T_0+tT)_{12}\ne0\), and hence \((T_0+tT)_{21}=0\).
This polynomial identity in \(t\) forces \(T_{21}=0\) for every \(T\).
Thus either every \(T_{12}=0\) or every \(T_{21}=0\); together with \(T_{22}=0\), this supplies a common kernel or cokernel.
Undoing the invertible changes proves the assertion.

View the four tensors in (B-reduced-coefficients) as vectors in a complex Hilbert space and define
\[
 \mathscr D_2
  =\operatorname{Sym}_2(K_{11}\otimes K_{22}
                         -K_{12}\otimes K_{21}).
\]
The upper bounds and (Reduced-two-sided-separation) imply
\[
 \|\mathscr D_2\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c_2F^2
 \tag{B-reduced-determinant-bound}
\]
for a fixed \(c_2>0\).
For completeness, divide by \(F\) and consider the Hermitian Gram matrix of the four coefficient vectors.
Its entries range over a fixed compact set of positive semidefinite four-by-four matrices, and both families of inequalities in (Reduced-two-sided-separation) remain true in a limit.
The squared norm of \(\mathscr D_2\) is a polynomial in these Gram entries and their conjugates.
If its infimum were zero, realize a limiting Gram matrix by vectors \(k_{ij}\in\mathbb C^4\).
Their reduced determinant tensor would vanish, so for every complex linear functional \(\ell\) on their span,
\[
 \det[\ell(k_{ij})]_{i,j=1}^2=0.
\]
The elementary singular-space assertion gives a common nonzero left or right annihilator of this matrix space.
Every such vector yields one of the two vanishing pairs of coefficient combinations forbidden by the limiting separation inequalities.
This contradiction proves (B-reduced-determinant-bound), uniformly in \(n\).
No real-only classification is being used for these complex coefficient vectors.

<a id="symmetric-inverse-scale"></a>
### The transpose-symmetric inverse retains its full scale

Put
\[
 H_z=\frac{P+P^T}{2}-zI,\qquad
 J=\frac{P-P^T}{2},\qquad
 G_a=\frac{G-G^T}{2}.
\]
The real symmetric matrix \((P+P^T)/2\) gives \(\|H_z^{-1}\|\le\eta^{-1}\), and \(\|J\|\le K^2\).
Subtracting \((H_z+J)G=I\) and \((H_z-J)G^T=I\) yields
\[
 H_zG_a=-JG_s,\qquad
 G=(I-H_z^{-1}J)G_s.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \|G_s\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge(1+K^2/\eta)^{-1}F.
 \tag{Symmetric-inverse-scale}
\]
This actual-inverse identity is essential; the result does not claim an analogous lower bound for arbitrary nonsymmetric matrices.

<a id="outer-reduced-cubic"></a>
### Orthogonal quadratic sectors isolate a nonzero reduced cubic tensor

On the row-variable space \(\mathcal E=\mathbb R^n\oplus\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
 V_1(x,y)=Bx,\qquad V_2(x,y)=x,\qquad V_3(x,y)=y,
 \qquad
 T_{ij}=\operatorname{Sym}(V_i^TGV_j).
\]
Then \(h_B(x,y)=\det[(x,y)^TT_{ij}(x,y)]_{i,j=1}^3\).
The physical tensors \(T_{ij}\) are complex symmetric, but \(T_{ij}\) and \(T_{ji}\) need not agree.
Their Hilbert--Schmidt and operator norms are at most \(\Lambda F\) and \(\Lambda q\), with fixed \(\Lambda=\Lambda(K)\ge1\).
Let
\[
 \mathscr D_3
 =\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
             \operatorname{Sym}_3\bigotimes_{i=1}^3T_{i\sigma(i)}
\]
be the reduced cubic tensor in the Hilbert space of complex symmetric physical quadratic tensors.

That Hilbert space is the orthogonal sum of its \(xx\), \(xy\), and \(yy\) blocks.
The projection of \(\mathscr D_3\) to the sector with two \(xx\) factors and one \(yy\) factor is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}_3(\mathscr D_2\otimes G_s^{(yy)}).
\]
Indeed these are precisely the determinant permutations fixing the third auxiliary index; every other permutation has one \(xx\) and two \(xy\) factors.
The three placements of the \(yy\) factor are orthogonal, so the squared norm of this projection is
\[
 \frac13\|\mathscr D_2\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
                    \|G_s\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.
\]
The preceding two bounds consequently give
\[
 \|\mathscr D_3\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c_3F^3.
 \tag{Outer-reduced-cubic-bound}
\]
No cancellation with the \(xx,xy,xy\) sector is possible in this reduced tensor norm.

<a id="complex-contraction"></a>
### Complex double-edge contractions and the high-rank error

Let \(\mathscr T_B=\mathscr T(h_B)\).
The exact double-edge count and longer-cycle bound give
\[
 \left|\|\mathscr T_B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
           -\frac{2^3 3!}{6!}\|\mathscr D_3\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\right|
       \le36\Lambda^6q^2F^4.
 \tag{Complex-outer-contraction}
\]
Here is the full extension of the accepted real contraction argument to the present tensors.
For two ordered triples of symmetric physical tensors, expand the inner product of their arithmetic-average symmetric order-six products as the average over all \(6!\) matchings of the six slots on one side with the six slots on the other.
The first-side coefficients are conjugated in this Hermitian inner product.
Each slot matching produces a bipartite multigraph with three vertices on each side, each vertex of degree two.
It is a union of double edges and even cycles.

If every component is a double edge, there are \(3!\) vertex matchings and \(2^3\) slot matchings over each one.
Each contraction is the product of the three corresponding Hermitian Hilbert--Schmidt inner products.
After summing both determinant expansions, these terms are exactly \((2^3 3!/6!)\|\mathscr D_3\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\).
This count does not use auxiliary symmetry \(T_{ij}=T_{ji}\).

Every other slot matching contains a cycle of length \(r\ge4\).
Physical transpose symmetry permits its contraction to be written as a trace of an alternating product of conjugated first-side matrices and second-side matrices, with harmless transposes if an orientation is reversed.
Conjugation and transpose preserve both operator and Hilbert--Schmidt norms.
Putting two factors in Hilbert--Schmidt norm and the other \(r-2\) in operator norm bounds that cycle by \((\Lambda q)^{r-2}(\Lambda F)^2\).
A double edge is bounded by \((\Lambda F)^2\).
Since \(q\le F\), the product over all components of any remaining matching is at most \(\Lambda^6q^2F^4\).
The slot average has total weight at most one and the two determinant expansions have \((3!)^2=36\) terms.
This proves (Complex-outer-contraction) without a Gaussian limit theorem or a real-coefficient assumption.

Choose \(R\) sufficiently large relative to \(\Lambda,c_3\).
Then (Outer-reduced-cubic-bound), (Complex-outer-contraction) and \(F^2\ge Rq^2\) imply \(\|\mathscr T_B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cF^3\).
The same proof with \(A\) in place of \(B\) gives the second bound in (Actual-outer-high-rank-tensor); the individual gap and commutator steps were established for both inputs.
Interchanging the roles of the row variables changes the auxiliary ordering from \([Ay,y,x]\) to \([Ay,x,y]\); swapping the last two rows and the last two columns preserves the determinant and its tensor norm.

<a id="small-residual-extension"></a>
### Extension to two-sided small-residual matrices

Let \(E\) satisfy (Two-sided-small-residual), and put \(F_E=\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\), \(q_E=\|E\|\).
The affine vector inequalities give, by the same columnwise argument and its adjoint counterpart,
\[
 \|D_BE\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c_1F_E-C_1\|ME\|_{\mathrm{HS}},\qquad
 \|ED_B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c_1F_E-C_1\|EM\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.
\]
For \(Y=D_BE\), one has \(YM=D_B(EM)\); for \(Y=ED_B\), one has \(MY=(ME)D_B\).
Both residual norms are at most \((K+1)L\sqrt n\).
The exact symmetry and commutator identities in the two-channel argument do not require invertibility.
Consequently (Reduced-two-sided-separation), with \(G\) replaced by \(E\), holds once \(F_E\ge C(K,z,L)\sqrt n\).

The transpose-symmetric scale estimate has a controlled error.
Write \(E_s=(E+E^T)/2\), \(E_a=(E-E^T)/2\), keeping the same \(H_z,J\) as above.
Direct subtraction gives
\[
 H_zE_a+JE_s=\frac12\{ME-(EM)^T\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 F_E\le(1+K^2/\eta)\|E_s\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
                  +\frac{L\sqrt n}{2\eta}.
\]
After increasing the fixed lower threshold for \(F_E/\sqrt n\), this bounds \(\|E_s\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) below by a positive fixed multiple of \(F_E\).
The complex Gram compactness, orthogonal-sector extraction and full contraction comparison now apply without any further change, with \(F_E,q_E\) in place of \(F,q\).
This proves the stated approximate-inverse version.

Finally, write the singular expansion \(G=\sum_j q_j u_jv_j^*\), and let \(E=\sum_{j\in I}q_j u_jv_j^*\) for any retained subset \(I\).
Then
\[
 ME=\sum_{j\in I}v_jv_j^*,\qquad
 EM=\sum_{j\in I}u_ju_j^*,
\]
so both matrices are orthogonal projections with Hilbert--Schmidt norms at most \(\sqrt n\).
Thus every such nonzero inverse residual satisfies (Two-sided-small-residual) with \(L=2\), as asserted.

<a id="complete-outer-identity"></a>
### Exact outer bidegrees of the complete original Schur determinant

Set
\[
 U_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&2\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \widetilde Q=(U_0^T\otimes I)Q
      =\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\A&0&-I\\2B&-2I&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The transformed row and column borders are \(R'\) and \(R'^T\), where
\[
 R'=\begin{pmatrix}0&x^T&2y^T\\x^T&0&0\\2y^T&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct block inversion gives
\[
 \widetilde Q^{-1}=WGV+Z_0,\qquad
 W=\begin{bmatrix}I\\B\\A\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 V=\begin{bmatrix}I&2B&A/2\end{bmatrix},
\]
where \((Z_0)_{23}=-I/2\), \((Z_0)_{32}=-I\), and all other blocks vanish.
Thus
\[
 R'Z_0R'^T=-3(x^Ty)E_{11},\qquad
 R'W=\begin{bmatrix}(Bx+2Ay)^T\\x^T\\2y^T\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 VR'^T=\begin{bmatrix}2Bx+Ay&x&2y\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Write \(M_G=(R'W)G(VR'^T)\).
The complete degree-six part of the transformed determinant is
\[
 -\det M_G+3(x^Ty)\operatorname{cof}_{11}(M_G).
\]
Its corner-independent cofactor has bidegree \((2,2)\), so the second term has bidegree \((3,3)\) only.
In the first determinant the \((4,2)\) term chooses \(Bx\) in its first row and \(2Bx\) in its first column; the third row and column each contribute their factor two.
Consequently that term is \(-8h_B\).
The \((2,4)\) term similarly chooses \(2Ay\) and \(Ay\), giving \(-8h_A\).
Finally the transformed Schur determinant is \(\det U_0^T=-2\) times the original one.
Dividing by this factor proves (Actual-outer-bidegrees).
Different \((x,y)\) bidegrees occupy orthogonal tensor coordinate sectors, so the complete sixth tensor has norm at least the norm of either outer component.
This proves (Actual-complete-high-rank-tensor), including the complete auxiliary correction.

<a id="physical-transfer-boundary"></a>
## Physical transfer boundary

The theorem controls the raw arithmetic-symmetric tensor of the actual complete polynomial on its original row variables.
To apply the original-law matching bridge, one must still keep a comparable amount of energy on six distinct physical positions and make the normalized physical-group loads vanish.
The quadratic tensors used here are formed from the actual matrices \(G,BG,GB,BGB\), so separate estimates for their physical row/column loads and within-position diagonal blocks are an appropriate interface.
These matrices occur as bounded fixed multiples of blocks of the original companion inverse, with \(BGB\) in its \((2,3)\) block.
Small maximum row load alone does not imply small total within-position diagonal mass.
Compressing the variables to a low-load reservoir also requires a new argument: such compression need not preserve the exact commutator identities used above.
No physical-coefficient or probability conclusion is asserted merely from the raw tensor lower bound.
