# Complete raw outer coefficients across inverse singular-value profiles

This theorem assembles a deterministic raw sixth-tensor bound for the actual inverse of \(AB+2BA-zI\) at a nonreal parameter.
It treats all relative singular-value profiles once the inverse energy after two modes exceeds the stated scale.
Its six-case allocation retains a compatible coefficient scale for a later physical-coordinate argument.
The argument below contains the mixed high-rank extraction, exact Hilbert--Schmidt kernel noncancellation, the countable-word compactness construction and the finite allocation.
No necessary proof is left in a working draft.
The theorem does not itself assert a physical-coefficient, original-law probability or Brown-measure conclusion.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact all-profile raw theorem

Fix \(K\ge1\) and \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R\).
Let \(n\ge3\), let real symmetric \(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb R)\) satisfy \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K\), and suppose
\[
 M=AB+2BA-zI_n,\qquad G=M^{-1}
\]
is defined.
Write the singular expansion and its first residuals as
\[
 G=\sum_{j=1}^nq_ju_jv_j^*,\qquad q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_n>0,
 \qquad E_k=\sum_{j>k}q_ju_jv_j^*,\quad F_k=\|E_k\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \quad(k=0,1,2).
\]
There are constants \(K_0,K_1,K_2,L\ge1\), \(\delta_1,\delta_2\in(0,1)\), and \(c,C>0\), depending only on \(K,z\), with the following property.
Assume
\[
 F_2\ge L\sqrt n.
 \tag{All-profile-energy}
\]
Apply the following six rows in order and assign the indicated scale \(W\).
For the last three rows, \(\mathcal U\) abbreviates the three inequalities
\(F_0<K_0q_1\), \(F_1<K_1q_2\), and \(F_2<K_2q_3\).

| Row | Exact conditions | Assigned \(W\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | \(F_0\ge K_0q_1\) | \(F_0^3\) |
| 2 | \(F_0<K_0q_1\), \(F_1\ge K_1q_2\) | \(q_1F_1^2\) |
| 3 | \(F_0<K_0q_1\), \(F_1<K_1q_2\), \(F_2\ge K_2q_3\) | \(q_1q_2F_2\) |
| 4 | \(\mathcal U\), \(q_3\le\delta_2q_2\) | \(q_1q_2q_3\) |
| 5 | \(\mathcal U\), \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\), \(q_2\le\delta_1q_1\) | \(q_1q_2^2\) |
| 6 | \(\mathcal U\), \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\), \(q_2>\delta_1q_1\) | \(q_1^3\) |

These rows partition all admitted inverse singular-value profiles.
For real variables \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\), put
\[
 h_B(G)(x,y)=\det([Bx,x,y]^TG[Bx,x,y]).
\]
Every coefficient tensor below uses arithmetic-average symmetrization and the Hermitian Euclidean norm on the complexified real tensor space.
Then, in its assigned row,
\[
 \|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cW,
 \qquad W\ge q_1q_2q_3.
 \tag{All-profile-raw-lower}
\]
The bounds are uniform in all singular-value ratios and all choices of singular vectors.

The assigned scale also controls every surviving term of an unprojected expansion of this polynomial.
In rows 1 and 6, the full expansion has product scale \(F_0^3\le CW\).
In rows 2 and 5, its one-head expansion has scales \(q_1F_1^2,F_1^3\le CW\).
In rows 3 and 4, its two-head expansion has scales \(q_1q_2F_2,q_1F_2^2,q_2F_2^2,F_2^3\le CW\).
Repeated occurrences of the same rank-one head are identically zero and are cancelled before these upper estimates.

For the actual companion, original physical borders and arbitrary fixed exposed corner in [C187v30](Yin_C_F0187_v30.md#statement), the complete original Schur numerator has exact outer component
\(\Phi_{4,2}=4h_B(G)\).
Its complete sixth tensor therefore has norm at least a fixed multiple of \(W\), including every auxiliary constant contribution.
On the full original row support \(\|x\|,\|y\|\le2\), the same actual graph area satisfies
\[
 \sqrt{\det(I_3+R(x,y)Q^{-1}Q^{-*}R(x,y)^*)}\le CW.
 \tag{All-profile-graph-upper}
\]
This is a deterministic full-denominator comparison, not a physical-coordinate or probability assertion.
For an ambient dimension \(N\ge n\), the stronger condition \(F_2^2/N\ge L^2\) suffices for every conclusion with the same constants.

## Actual inverse, outer polynomial and elementary tensor bounds

Fix \(K\ge1\), \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R\), and real symmetric \(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb R)\) with \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K\).
Assume \(M=AB+2BA-zI\) is invertible, and write
\[
 G=M^{-1}=\sum_{j=1}^n q_j u_jv_j^*,\qquad
 q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_n>0.
\]
Both singular-vector lists are orthonormal.
For \(k=0,1,2\), put
\[
 E_k=\sum_{j>k}q_ju_jv_j^*,\qquad
 F_k=\|E_k\|_{\mathrm{HS}},\qquad H_j=u_jv_j^*.
\]
Thus \(E_0=G\), \(\|E_k\|=q_{k+1}\), and \(F_k^2=\sum_{j>k}q_j^2\).
Only dimensions \(n\ge3\) are relevant when \(F_2\) is bounded below by a positive multiple of \(\sqrt n\).
For a complex matrix \(T\), define
\[
 h_B(T)(x,y)=\det([Bx,x,y]^TT[Bx,x,y]),\qquad x,y\in\mathbb R^n.
\]
Let \(\mathscr T(p)\) denote the arithmetic-average symmetric complex coefficient tensor of a homogeneous degree-six polynomial on \(\mathbb R^{2n}\), with its Hermitian Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
Define the mixed polynomials
\[
 \ell_1(E,H)=[t]h_B(E+tH),\qquad
 \ell_2(E,H_1,H_2)=[t_1t_2]h_B(E+t_1H_1+t_2H_2).
 \tag{Mixed-leading-polynomials}
\]
They have degree two and degree one in \(E\), respectively.

Each entry of \([Bx,x,y]^TT[Bx,x,y]\) has quadratic tensor norm at most \(C_K\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\).
Indeed its coefficient matrix is obtained by multiplying \(T\) on the left and right by bounded copies of \(I,B\), embedding into the two variable spaces and symmetrizing; each operation has the stated norm bound.
Tensor products multiply Hilbert--Schmidt norms, and arithmetic symmetrization is an orthogonal projection and therefore contractive.
Expanding the determinant and its mixed coefficients gives, for unit Hilbert--Schmidt rank-one heads,
\[
 \|\mathscr T(h_B(E))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C_K\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^3,
\]
\[
 \|\mathscr T(\ell_1(E,H))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C_K\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,
 \qquad
 \|\mathscr T(\ell_2(E,H_1,H_2))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C_K\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.
 \tag{Elementary-mixed-upper-bounds}
\]
If the same rank-one head occurs twice among the three determinant factors, its mixed determinant vanishes pointwise: its matrix-valued quadratic form has rank at most one.
Consequently the following expansions are exact:
\[
 h_B(q_1H_1+E_1)=q_1\ell_1(E_1,H_1)+h_B(E_1),
 \tag{One-head-exact-expansion}
\]
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_B(q_1H_1+q_2H_2+E_2)
 &=q_1q_2\ell_2(E_2,H_1,H_2)
   +q_1\ell_1(E_2,H_1)\\
 &\quad+q_2\ell_1(E_2,H_2)+h_B(E_2).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Two-head-exact-expansion}
\]
No restriction or replacement of the original variable law is involved in these deterministic identities.

## Mixed high-rank residuals

### Two raw statements at their compatible scales

Fix real symmetric \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K\), a nonreal \(z\), and the actual inverse \(G=(AB+2BA-zI)^{-1}\).
For \(k=1,2\), write its first \(k\) singular modes as
\[
 G=\sum_{j=1}^k q_j u_jv_j^*+E,\qquad
 q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_k>0,\qquad
 F=\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}},\quad r=\|E\|.
\]
The retained \(u_j\) and \(v_j\) are orthonormal, and \(E\) is the remaining singular expansion.
There are fixed constants \(C_0,R,c>0\) and \(\eta_0>0\), depending only on \(K,z\), such that
\[
 F\ge C_0\sqrt n,\qquad F^2\ge Rr^2,\qquad r\le\eta_0q_k
 \tag{Mixed-outer-scale}
\]
imply
\[
 k=1:\quad\|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c q_1F^2,
 \qquad
 k=2:\quad\|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c q_1q_2F,
 \tag{Mixed-outer-raw-bounds}
\]
where \(h_B(G)=\det([Bx,x,y]^TG[Bx,x,y])\).
These bounds therefore hold, with changed constants, for the outer \((4,2)\) layer of the complete original Schur determinant.
No bound on \(F/q_k\) is required for these raw conclusions.

<a id="one-head-outer-proof"></a>
### One head: keep it only in the yy entry

Let \(k=1\), and abbreviate its vectors by \(u,v\).
Choose the real orthogonal projection \(P_X\) onto the orthogonal complement of the real and imaginary parts of
\[
 u,\quad Bu,\quad\overline v,\quad B\overline v.
\]
Its codimension is at most eight.
Let \(P_Y\) project onto the real span of the real and imaginary parts of \(u,\overline v\), of dimension at most four.
Use these projections independently on the two original variable spaces, restricting \(x\) to \(X\) and \(y\) to \(Y\).
This is an orthogonal projection in coefficient-tensor space, not a change to any original random input law.

Every head entry involving \(x\) vanishes exactly.
The only surviving head entry in the three-by-three matrix is
\[
 H_{33}(y)=q_1(y^Tu)(v^*y).
\]
Its quadratic symmetric tensor has norm at least \(q_1/\sqrt2\), since
\[
 \|\operatorname{Sym}_2(u\otimes\overline v)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
       =\tfrac12(1+|\langle u,\overline v\rangle|^2)\ge\tfrac12,
\]
and the projection onto \(Y\) retains both vectors exactly.

The residual \(E\) satisfies \(\|ME\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\|EM\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le2\sqrt n\), as proved for singular-mode residuals in C187v30.
Its two-channel quadratic coefficient tensors therefore have the uniform reduced left/right separation from that proof when \(F\ge C_0\sqrt n\).
Each has operator norm at most \(C_Kr\).
For a complex symmetric tensor \(T\), removing a real subspace of codimension \(d\) changes its Hilbert--Schmidt norm by at most \(\sqrt{2d}\|T\|\): decompose \(T-P_XTP_X\) into its orthogonal row/column blocks and use the rank bound on each removed side.
Thus all four residual two-channel tensors change by at most \(C_Kr\).
Increasing \(R\) in (Mixed-outer-scale) preserves their reduced left/right separation and the compact two-by-two determinant lower bound.

Let \(d_E(x)=\det([Bx,x]^TE[Bx,x])\), with \(x\in X\).
The degree-four version of the complex contraction count has double-edge factor \(2^2 2!/4!=1/3\) and error at most \(C r^2F^2\).
Indeed its slot graph has two vertices on each side; the only non-double-edge possibility is a four-cycle, bounded by two operator norms and two Hilbert--Schmidt norms.
Hence, after increasing \(R\) again,
\[
 \|\mathscr T(d_E|_X)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cF^2.
\]
The restriction of \(h_B(G)\) is exactly
\[
 H_{33}(y)d_E(x)+h_B(E)|_{X\oplus Y}.
\]
The leading product uses four \(x\) slots and two \(y\) slots.
Arithmetic symmetrization of tensors on these orthogonal variable spaces has norm \(\sqrt{4!2!/6!}=1/\sqrt{15}\) times the product of their norms.
Thus its tensor norm is at least \(c q_1F^2\).

Every residual \(xx\) quadratic tensor has norm at most \(C_KF\).
Every residual \(xy\) or \(yy\) tensor on this restriction has norm at most \(C_Kr\), because at least one side is compressed to the fixed-dimensional space \(Y\).
The all-residual determinant consists of products of type \(xx,xx,yy\) or \(xx,xy,xy\), so its tensor norm is at most
\[
 C_K(rF^2+r^2F)\le C_K' rF^2.
\]
A sufficiently small fixed \(\eta_0\) in \(r\le\eta_0q_1\) absorbs this error.
The unprojected tensor norm is no smaller than the restricted one, proving the first assertion of (Mixed-outer-raw-bounds).

<a id="two-head-outer-proof"></a>
### Two heads: keep them only in the xx block

Let \(k=2\).
Take \(X\) to be the real span of all real and imaginary parts of
\[
 u_j,\quad Bu_j,\quad\overline v_j,\quad B\overline v_j,
 \qquad j=1,2,
\]
so \(\dim X\le16\).
Take \(Y\) orthogonal to the real and imaginary parts of all \(u_j,\overline v_j\), so its codimension is at most eight.
The head matrix is now supported entirely in the two-by-two \(xx\) corner.
Writing \(U_2=[u_1,u_2]\), \(V_2=[v_1,v_2]\), its determinant is
\[
 d_H(x)=q_1q_2
     \det([Bx,x]^TU_2)\det(V_2^*[Bx,x]).
 \tag{Two-head-quartic-factorization}
\]
The restriction to \(X\) retains every linear form in this identity.

Both determinant polynomials have uniform noncancellation.
For \(w\in\operatorname{Ran}U_2\), the singular expansion gives
\(\|Mw\|\le q_2^{-1}\|w\|\).
For \(w\in\operatorname{Ran}\overline V_2\), the analogous estimate holds for \(P^T-zI\).
The individual-input inequality in C187v30 therefore bounds
\(\inf_b\|(B-bI)w\|\) below by a fixed multiple of \(\|w\|\) when \(q_2^{-1}\le|\operatorname{Im}z|/2\).
This condition follows from a sufficiently large \(C_0\), since \(F\le\sqrt n\,r\) and \(q_2\ge r\) imply \(q_2\ge C_0\).
Consequently the maps \(x\mapsto(x^TBw,x^Tw)\) are uniformly onto \(\mathbb C^2\) after complexification, on both head spaces.
Equivalently, for standard real Gaussian \(x\) and either \(w=U_2h\) or \(w=\overline V_2h\),
\(\mathbb E|\alpha_1x^TBw+\alpha_2x^Tw|^2\ge c\|\alpha\|^2\|h\|^2\).
This follows from the scalar-center gap when \(|\alpha_1|\) is bounded below relative to \(\|\alpha\|\), and from domination by \(\alpha_2w\) otherwise.

The accepted DMR08 Proposition 5.1 shows that each corresponding transitive complex matrix space contains an invertible matrix.
Thus both determinant polynomials in (Two-head-quartic-factorization) are nonzero, and their product is nonzero even though they share the same variables.
For uniformity, collect the eight complex linear forms in the two two-by-two matrices into their sixteen real components and use a standard real Gaussian variable on \(X\).
Their covariance matrices have uniformly bounded entries, and the quantitative onto inequalities survive every convergent covariance subsequence.
The limiting determinant product is still nonzero on the real Gaussian support, by complexification and the same elementary singular-space argument.
A nonzero polynomial on a real vector space has strictly positive squared Gaussian integral, and that integral is continuous in the finite covariance matrix by the finite Gaussian moment formula.
Compactness gives a uniform positive lower bound.
The polynomial depends on at most sixteen real dimensions, so the Gaussian norm is at most a fixed constant times its coefficient-tensor norm.
Therefore
\[
 \|\mathscr T(d_H|_X)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c q_1q_2.
\]

The residual symmetric tensor \(E_s=(E+E^T)/2\) has norm at least \(cF\) by the small-residual symmetric-inverse identity in C187v30 and \(F\ge C_0\sqrt n\).
The restriction \(P_YE_sP_Y\) loses at most \(C r\) in Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
A sufficiently large \(R\) thus gives
\[
 \|P_YE_sP_Y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c'F.
\]
The leading term of the restricted determinant is
\(d_H(x)y^TEy\), whose coefficient-tensor norm is at least \(c q_1q_2F\) by the same orthogonal \((4,2)\) tensor product calculation.

All residual \(xx\) and \(xy\) tensors have norm at most \(C_Kr\), since \(X\) has bounded dimension; the residual \(yy\) tensor has norm at most \(F\).
The entire head \(xx\) matrix has entry-tensor norms at most \(C_Kq_1\).
Expanding the two-by-two corner and the remaining determinant permutations, every other term has total norm bounded by
\[
 C_K(q_1rF+q_1r^2+r^2F+r^3)
       \le C_K'(q_1rF+r^2F).
\]
Relative to \(q_1q_2F\), this is at most \(C_K''r/q_2\), since \(r\le q_2\le q_1\).
Taking \(\eta_0\) sufficiently small proves the second assertion of (Mixed-outer-raw-bounds).

## Exact kernel operators and mixed outer noncancellation

This exact algebraic lemma supplies the noncancellation input for the bounded-effective-rank limits below.
Let \(\mathcal H_{\mathbb R}\) be a separable real Hilbert space, let \(\mathcal H\) be its complexification, and let \(A,B\) be bounded real selfadjoint operators with norms at most \(K\).
Transpose means \(T^T=\mathcal C T^*\mathcal C\), where \(\mathcal C\) is the canonical conjugation.
Fix \(z\notin\mathbb R\), and write \(P=AB+2BA\), \(M=P-zI\).
Suppose \(E\ne0\) is Hilbert--Schmidt and
\[
 ME=EM=0.
 \tag{Exact-two-sided-kernel}
\]
The Hilbert--Schmidt assumption is retained: the trace-class commutator argument below is not asserted for an arbitrary compact operator.
For real \(x,y\), define
\[
 d_E(x)=\det([Bx,x]^TE[Bx,x]),\qquad
 h_E(x,y)=\det([Bx,x,y]^TE[Bx,x,y]).
\]
If \(\operatorname{rank}E\ge2\), then \(d_E\) is not identically zero.
If \(\operatorname{rank}E\ge3\), then \(h_E\) is not identically zero.

There are also two mixed statements.
Let \(u_j,v_j\) be orthonormal lists of length \(k=1\) or \(k=2\), satisfying
\[
 Mu_j=0,\qquad v_j^*M=0,\qquad
 Ev_j=0,\qquad E^*u_j=0,
\]
and put \(H_j=u_jv_j^*\).
For \(k=1\) and \(\operatorname{rank}E\ge2\), the coefficient of \(t\) in \(h_{E+tH_1}\) is a nonzero polynomial in \(x,y\).
For \(k=2\) and \(\operatorname{rank}E\ge1\), the coefficient of \(t_1t_2\) in \(h_{E+t_1H_1+t_2H_2}\) is a nonzero polynomial.
These are exact nonvanishing conclusions, with no uniform numerical lower bound yet claimed.

### Exact commutator exclusion and symmetric rank

The individual-input and commutator calculations of C187v30 extend to bounded operators and Hilbert--Schmidt \(Y\): every trace used there is trace class, and the same norm inequalities and cyclicity with bounded factors hold.
In particular
\[
 |\operatorname{Im}z|\|Y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le\|YM\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+K\|[B,Y]\|_{\mathrm{HS}},
\]
and the left-sided version also holds.
For every unit complex pair \((a,b)\), the exact kernel condition gives
\[
 \|(aB+bI)E\|_{\mathrm{HS}},\quad
 \|E(aB+bI)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c_{K,z}\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.
\]
Thus the reduced two-by-two coefficient space of \(E\) has no common nonzero left or right annihilator, by the exact symmetry/commutator identities in C187v30 with zero residual error.

Let \(E_s=(E+E^T)/2\), \(E_a=(E-E^T)/2\), \(H_z=(P+P^T)/2-zI\), and \(J=(P-P^T)/2\).
Subtracting \(ME=0\) and \((EM)^T=0\) gives
\[
 H_zE_a+JE_s=0,\qquad E=(I-H_z^{-1}J)E_s.
 \tag{Exact-kernel-symmetric-factor}
\]
Here \(H_z^{-1}\) is bounded by \(|\operatorname{Im}z|^{-1}\), because \((P+P^T)/2\) is real selfadjoint.
Consequently \(E_s\ne0\), and \(\operatorname{rank}E_s\ge\operatorname{rank}E\), including the finite-rank comparisons used below.

### The two-by-two polynomial cannot vanish

If \(\operatorname{rank}E=2\), use its singular factorization \(E=U\operatorname{diag}(s_1,s_2)V^*\).
The ranges of \(U\) and \(\overline V\) belong to the kernels of \(M\) and \(M^T\), respectively.
The exact individual \(B\) gap therefore makes each linear matrix space \([Bx,x]^TU\) and its transpose-left counterpart complex-transitive.
The accepted DMR08 Proposition 5.1 makes both determinant polynomials nonzero at these transitive two-channel spaces.
Their product
\[
 d_E(x)=s_1s_2\det([Bx,x]^TU)\det(V^*[Bx,x])
\]
is nonzero, even though the factors use the same variable.

Now suppose \(\operatorname{rank}E\ge3\), and assume for contradiction that \(d_E\) vanishes identically.
Write the four continuous quadratic polynomials
\[
 f(x)=x^TE_sx,\qquad g(x)=x^TBEx,\qquad
 h(x)=x^TEBx,\qquad k(x)=x^TBEBx.
\]
Then \(fk=gh\).
An identity on real vectors is also an identity of complex polynomials on every finite complexified subspace, since a polynomial vanishing on all of \(\mathbb R^d\) has every coefficient zero.
There is a finite-dimensional real subspace whose complexified restriction of \(f\) has rank at least three.
Indeed the symmetric bilinear form of rank at least three contains a nondegenerate three-dimensional complex restriction, by successive orthogonal splitting of a vector with nonzero square; the real and imaginary parts of its vectors lie in a finite-dimensional real subspace.
Choose increasing finite-dimensional real subspaces \(V_m\) containing such a restriction and with dense union.

On every complexified \(V_m\), the quadratic polynomial \(f\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb C\).
A reducible homogeneous quadratic is a product of two linear forms and has symmetric rank at most two, whereas these restrictions have rank at least three.
Unique factorization in the finite polynomial ring therefore implies \(g=\lambda_m f\) or \(h=\lambda_m f\) on \(V_m\).
In the first case cancellation in \(fk=gh\) gives \(k=\lambda_m h\), and in the second it gives \(k=\lambda_m g\).
Pass to an unbounded subsequence with the same one of these two alternatives.
A fixed nonzero coefficient of \(f\) on the initial subspace determines \(\lambda_m\) from \(g\) or \(h\), so this scalar is constant along that subsequence.
The resulting identities hold on the dense union and then everywhere by continuity.
They give a common left or right annihilator of the global reduced coefficient space, contradicting the exact commutator exclusion.
This proves the two-by-two assertion also for infinite-rank Hilbert--Schmidt \(E\).

### A scalar Schur step proves the three-by-three assertion

Choose real \(y\) with \(f(y)=y^TEy\ne0\), which exists since \(E_s\ne0\).
Set
\[
 E_y=E-Ey\,f(y)^{-1}y^TE.
\]
This is Hilbert--Schmidt, satisfies \(ME_y=E_yM=0\), and has rank at least \(\operatorname{rank}E-1\).
For finite rank its rank is exactly one less: on \(\operatorname{Ran}E\), the left factor \(I-Ey\,f(y)^{-1}y^T\) has kernel exactly the span of \(Ey\).
Thus if \(\operatorname{rank}E\ge3\), the preceding two-by-two conclusion applies to \(E_y\).
For every \(x\), scalar Schur complementation gives
\[
 h_E(x,y)=f(y)\det([Bx,x]^TE_y[Bx,x]).
\]
The right side is not identically zero in \(x\), proving the three-by-three assertion.

### One head: oblique removal preserves the residual rank

For \(k=1\), abbreviate \(u=u_1\), \(v=v_1\), and choose real \(y\) with
\(a=y^Tu\ne0\) and \(b=v^*y\ne0\).
Such a choice exists because two proper real linear zero sets cannot cover the real Hilbert space.
Define
\[
 E^{,y}=(I-u y^T/a)E(I-yv^*/b).
\]
It is Hilbert--Schmidt and remains a two-sided kernel operator: the left correction has range in \(\mathbb Cu\subset\ker M\), and the right correction is annihilated by \(v^*M=0\).
Its rank equals that of \(E\).
To see the right-side range preservation, put \(Q_y=I-yv^*/b\).
Since \(Ev=0\) and \(v^*v=1\), \(EQ_yv=-Ey/b\), so \(Ey\in\operatorname{Ran}(EQ_y)\).
The identity \(E=EQ_y+Ey\,v^*/b\) then proves \(\operatorname{Ran}(EQ_y)=\operatorname{Ran}E\).
The left factor has kernel \(\mathbb Cu\), disjoint from \(\operatorname{Ran}E\) because \(E^*u=0\), so it preserves the rank of this range.

Put \(X=[Bx,x]\).
Schur complementation in the bottom-right scalar of \([X,y]^T(E+tuv^*)[X,y]\), followed by expansion as \(t\to\infty\), gives
\[
 [t]h_{E+tH_1}(x,y)=ab\det(X^TE^{,y}X).
 \tag{Exact-one-head-coefficient}
\]
Explicitly the two-by-two Schur complement tends to
\(X^TEX-X^TEy\,v^*X/b-X^Tu\,y^TEX/a+X^Tu\,(y^TEy)v^*X/(ab)\), which is \(X^TE^{,y}X\), while the scalar factor has leading term \(tab\).
The determinant is affine in \(t\) because the head has rank one.
If \(\operatorname{rank}E\ge2\), the two-by-two assertion for \(E^{,y}\) makes the right side nonzero for some \(x\).
This proves exact one-head mixed noncancellation.

### Two heads: a two-by-two Schur step preserves the residual rank

For \(k=2\), put \(U=[u_1,u_2]\), \(V=[v_1,v_2]\), and \(H=UV^*\).
The exact individual \(B\) gap on both head spaces and the two-by-two transitivity argument give a real \(x\) for which
\[
 X=[Bx,x],\qquad A_X=X^THX=(X^TU)(V^*X)
\]
is invertible.
Define
\[
 E^{,X}=(I-HX A_X^{-1}X^T)E
                 (I-X A_X^{-1}X^TH).
\]
It is Hilbert--Schmidt and satisfies both exact kernel equations, because \(MH=HM=0\).
It has the same rank as \(E\).
For the right factor \(Q_X\), use \(EV=0\), \(HV=U\), and the invertibility of \(X^TU\) to get
\[
 EQ_XV=-EX A_X^{-1}X^TU.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{Ran}(EX)\subset\operatorname{Ran}(EQ_X)\), and \(E=EQ_X+EX A_X^{-1}X^TH\) gives equality of the ranges of \(E\) and \(EQ_X\).
The left factor is a projection with kernel \(\operatorname{Ran}U\): the identity \(HX A_X^{-1}X^TH=H\) follows from the invertible two-by-two factors of \(A_X\).
This kernel is disjoint from \(\operatorname{Ran}E\), since \(E^*U=0\), proving rank preservation.

Schur complementation in the upper two-by-two block of \([X,y]^T(E+tH)[X,y]\) gives
\[
 [t^2]h_{E+tH}(x,y)=\det(A_X)\,y^TE^{,X}y.
 \tag{Exact-two-head-coefficient}
\]
The order-\(t\) scalar Schur term vanishes because \(HX A_X^{-1}X^TH=H\); its constant term is exactly \(y^TE^{,X}y\), while the upper determinant has leading term \(t^2\det A_X\).
If \(E\ne0\), rank preservation and (Exact-kernel-symmetric-factor) imply \((E^{,X})_s\ne0\), so this coefficient is nonzero for some real \(y\).
Finally each individual head can occur at most once in the determinant.
Thus the coefficient of \(t^2\) after setting \(t_1=t_2=t\) is exactly the coefficient of \(t_1t_2\), proving the stated two-head mixed assertion.

The next compactness construction preserves both operator equations, the real structure, rank lower bounds and head/residual orthogonality to obtain uniform finite-dimensional consequences of this exact lemma.

## Three bounded-profile lower bounds

For every fixed \(C\ge1\) and \(d\in(0,1]\), there exist \(c>0\) and \(C_0<\infty\), depending only on \(K,z,C,d\), such that the following implications hold uniformly in \(n,A,B\) and all singular-vector choices:

| Profile | Hypotheses | Raw lower bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B0 | \(F_0\le Cq_1\), \(q_3\ge dq_1\), \(q_1\ge C_0\sqrt n\) | \(\|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cq_1^3\) |
| B1 | \(F_1\le Cq_2\), \(q_3\ge dq_2\), \(q_2\ge C_0\sqrt n\) | \(\|\mathscr T(\ell_1(E_1,H_1))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cq_2^2\) |
| B2 | \(F_2\le Cq_3\), \(q_3\ge C_0\sqrt n\) | \(\|\mathscr T(\ell_2(E_2,H_1,H_2))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cq_3\) |

The constants for B2 do not depend on \(d\).
The assertions control the leading mixed coefficient before any absorption of the other terms.
Their proof below does not assume that a normalized Hilbert--Schmidt tail retains all its norm in a limit.
It retains its needed rank and its full two-sided kernel equations instead.

<a id="word-gram-compactness"></a>
## Countable real word-Gram compactness

A common contradiction proof treats all three rows of the table.
For B0 take \(k=0\), \(s_m=q_{1,m}\), and normalized tail \(T_m=G_m/s_m\).
For B1 take \(k=1\), \(s_m=q_{2,m}\), and \(T_m=E_{1,m}/s_m\), with the one unit head retained separately.
For B2 take \(k=2\), \(s_m=q_{3,m}\), and \(T_m=E_{2,m}/s_m\), with the two unit heads retained separately.
If the relevant uniform constants did not exist, choose successive examples with \(s_m\ge m\sqrt{n_m}\) and the displayed raw norm divided by its stated scale less than \(1/m\).
Thus \(s_m\to\infty\), \(\|T_m\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C\), and the normalized raw tensor norm tends to zero.
Write
\[
 T_m=\sum_{j=1}^{n_m-k}\lambda_{j,m}a_{j,m}b_{j,m}^*,\qquad
 \lambda_{j,m}=q_{k+j,m}/s_m.
\]
The coefficients are nonnegative and decreasing, \(\lambda_{1,m}=1\), and
\[
 \sum_j\lambda_{j,m}^2\le C^2,\qquad
 \lambda_{j,m}\le C/\sqrt j.
 \tag{Uniform-operator-tail}
\]
In B0 one also has \(\lambda_{3,m}\ge d\), and in B1 one has \(\lambda_{2,m}\ge d\).

### Bounded dimension subsequences

If a subsequence of \(n_m\) is bounded, pass further to a fixed dimension \(n\).
Compactness of bounded real symmetric matrices, unitary matrices and bounded singular-value lists gives entrywise limits \(A_\infty,B_\infty,T_\infty\) and of every needed head vector, keeping the original real structure.
Since
\[
 M_mT_m=s_m^{-1}\sum_{j>k}v_{j,m}v_{j,m}^*,\qquad
 T_mM_m=s_m^{-1}\sum_{j>k}u_{j,m}u_{j,m}^*,
\]
the limit satisfies \(M_\infty T_\infty=T_\infty M_\infty=0\).
The normalized tail has rank at least three, two or one in B0, B1 or B2, by the retained singular-value lower bounds.
For each head, \(q_{i,m}\ge s_m\), so its exact singular equations imply \(M_\infty u_i=0\) and \(v_i^*M_\infty=0\).
Head/tail orthogonality and both orthonormal head lists pass to the limit.
Polynomial entries and their mixed coefficients converge in this fixed dimension.
The exact kernel noncancellation lemma above therefore contradicts the normalized raw tensor norm tending to zero.

### Unbounded dimensions and the real Gram quotient

It remains to consider a subsequence with \(n_m\to\infty\).
At every size include the following countable formal labels: the real and imaginary parts of every tail vector \(a_{j,m},b_{j,m}\), every retained head vector \(u_{i,m},v_{i,m}\), and every finite word in \(A_m,B_m\) applied to any of those real vectors.
Include the empty word.
For a label beyond the available tail length at a given size, assign the zero vector; every fixed label has its actual singular vector for all sufficiently large \(m\).
The norm of a word label of length \(l\) is at most \(K^l\).
All real pairwise Gram entries therefore lie in bounded intervals depending only on the two labels.
A diagonal subsequence makes every such real Gram entry and every \(\lambda_{j,m}\) converge.
There are countably many quantities, so the usual successive subsequence construction applies.

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the real vector space of finite formal linear combinations of labels, with bilinear form given by their limiting Gram entries.
This form is positive semidefinite, since its value on any finite combination is the limit of its squared Euclidean norm.
Quotient by its zero-norm subspace and complete to obtain a separable real Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_{\mathbb R}\).
For each finite combination \(x\), define the formal prefix actions \(A_\infty x\), \(B_\infty x\) by prefixing the corresponding letter to every word label.
The finite-dimensional inequalities imply after passage to the limit
\[
 \|A_\infty x\|\le K\|x\|,\qquad \|B_\infty x\|\le K\|x\|.
\]
These maps preserve the zero-norm subspace, are well defined on the quotient, and extend continuously to the completion.
The original real symmetries give
\[
 \langle A_\infty x,y\rangle=\langle x,A_\infty y\rangle,
 \qquad
 \langle B_\infty x,y\rangle=\langle x,B_\infty y\rangle
\]
on the dense finite-label space, hence everywhere.
Thus they are bounded real selfadjoint operators of norms at most \(K\).
They act by the prescribed words on all the labels, so every finite polynomial identity tested on those vectors is preserved by Gram convergence.

Complexify this real Hilbert space and let \(\mathcal C\) be its canonical conjugation.
For every complex vector label, reconstruct its limit as its real-part label plus \(i\) times its imaginary-part label.
All complex inner products are recovered from the four corresponding real Gram entries.
Consequently the tail vectors \(a_j,b_j\) are two orthonormal lists, the head vectors are orthonormal on each side, and all head/tail orthogonality relations survive.
The use of a real quotient also preserves the transpose convention \(T^T=\mathcal C T^*\mathcal C\) required by the exact kernel lemma; this would not follow merely by keeping arbitrary complex covariance entries without their real structure.

Let \(\lambda_j=\lim_m\lambda_{j,m}\).
They are decreasing and nonnegative, and for every finite \(J\),
\(\sum_{j\le J}\lambda_j^2\le C^2\).
Hence
\[
 T_\infty=\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_j a_jb_j^*
 \tag{Limit-Hilbert-Schmidt-tail}
\]
converges in Hilbert--Schmidt norm and has norm at most \(C\).
Its singular values are the nonzero \(\lambda_j\), with multiplicity, because its two lists are orthonormal.
In particular its rank is at least three, two or one in the three respective cases.
No equality of its Hilbert--Schmidt norm to the limiting norms of \(T_m\) is claimed or needed.
The decreasing order gives the uniform operator-tail bounds
\[
 \left\|T_m-\sum_{j\le J}\lambda_{j,m}a_{j,m}b_{j,m}^*\right\|
 \le\frac{C}{\sqrt{J+1}},\qquad
 \left\|T_\infty-\sum_{j\le J}\lambda_j a_jb_j^*\right\|
 \le\frac{C}{\sqrt{J+1}}.
 \tag{Uniform-tail-operator-approximation}
\]
The finite-size sum is taken only over available indices, so the bound also covers small sizes.

### The exact kernel equations and head orthogonality survive

Put \(M_\infty=A_\infty B_\infty+2B_\infty A_\infty-zI\).
For every \(j\) with \(\lambda_j>0\), one has \(q_{k+j,m}=s_m\lambda_{j,m}\to\infty\).
The original singular equations are
\[
 M_ma_{j,m}=q_{k+j,m}^{-1}b_{j,m},\qquad
 M_m^*b_{j,m}=q_{k+j,m}^{-1}a_{j,m}.
\]
The squared norms of their left sides are finite linear combinations of the convergent real Gram entries of words of length at most two.
Thus
\[
 M_\infty a_j=0,\qquad M_\infty^*b_j=0
 \quad\text{whenever }\lambda_j>0.
\]
Boundedness of \(M_\infty\) and convergence of the series give
\[
 M_\infty T_\infty=T_\infty M_\infty=0.
 \tag{Limit-two-sided-kernel}
\]
For every retained head, \(q_{i,m}\ge s_m\to\infty\), so the same argument gives
\[
 M_\infty u_i=0,\qquad v_i^*M_\infty=0.
\]
The preserved singular-vector orthogonality gives
\[
 T_\infty v_i=0,\qquad T_\infty^*u_i=0.
\]
These are exactly the head/tail interfaces of the one- and two-head noncancellation statements above.

### Finite-word tests detect the limiting polynomial

The exact kernel lemma applies to \(A_\infty,B_\infty,T_\infty\), with the retained heads when present.
It gives a nonzero real-variable polynomial among
\[
 h_{B_\infty}(T_\infty),\qquad
 \ell_1(T_\infty,u_1v_1^*),\qquad
 \ell_2(T_\infty,u_1v_1^*,u_2v_2^*)
\]
in B0, B1 and B2, respectively.
The lemma is used at its Hilbert--Schmidt scope, not merely for an arbitrary compact limit.
Each polynomial is continuous in its two real Hilbert-space variables: its entries use bounded operators and continuous bilinear forms, followed by a finite determinant or mixed-coefficient operation.
Its nonzero value can therefore be approximated by a nonzero value at a pair \(x,y\) in the dense finite real-label span.
Choose such a pair once and for all.
Let \(x_m,y_m\) be the same finite real linear combinations of the corresponding original word vectors.
Their norms, and the norms of \(B_mx_m\), stay bounded and converge to the corresponding limiting norms.

For a fixed \(J\), every matrix entry formed with the first \(J\) terms of \(T_m\) converges to its limiting entry, since it is a finite sum of products of Gram entries and \(\lambda_{j,m}\).
The error from the tail in each entry is bounded by
\(C(J+1)^{-1/2}\) times the two test-vector norms, uniformly in \(m\), by (Uniform-tail-operator-approximation).
First send \(m\to\infty\) at fixed \(J\), and then \(J\to\infty\).
This proves entrywise convergence of the full three-by-three test matrices.
The head test entries converge directly from their finite Gram entries.
Finite determinants and the coefficients of their finite polynomials in \(t\), or \(t_1,t_2\), consequently converge to the nonzero limiting test value.

On the other hand, any finite-dimensional degree-six tensor satisfies
\[
 |p(x_m,y_m)|\le\|\mathscr T(p)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
                   (\|x_m\|^2+\|y_m\|^2)^3.
\]
Apply this to the normalized polynomial in the relevant contradiction sequence.
Its tensor norm tends to zero and its test-vector norms stay bounded, so its values tend to zero.
This contradicts the nonzero limit just obtained.
Both the bounded- and unbounded-dimension alternatives are therefore impossible, proving all three bounded-profile lower bounds with fixed constants.
The proof gives no effective numerical rate for those compactness constants, which is not required by the uniform statement.

<a id="bounded-profile-absorption"></a>
## Absorbing the other terms in the bounded head cases

For a fixed tail bound \(C\), B2 gives constants \(c_2,C_{02}\) such that
\[
 \|\mathscr T(\ell_2(E_2,H_1,H_2))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c_2q_3
\]
when \(F_2\le Cq_3\) and \(q_3\ge C_{02}\sqrt n\).
The error in (Two-head-exact-expansion) is at most
\[
 C_K\{(q_1+q_2)F_2^2+F_2^3\}
 \le C_K(2C^2+C^3)\frac{q_3}{q_2}\,q_1q_2q_3,
\]
using \(q_3\le q_2\le q_1\).
Choose a fixed \(\delta_2\in(0,1)\) so that
\(C_K(2C^2+C^3)\delta_2\le c_2/2\).
Then
\[
 F_2\le Cq_3,\quad q_3\ge C_{02}\sqrt n,\quad q_3\le\delta_2q_2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge(c_2/2)q_1q_2q_3.
 \tag{Bounded-two-head-complete}
\]
This threshold is chosen without any later one-head constant.

For fixed \(C\) and fixed \(d>0\), B1 gives constants \(c_1,C_{01}\).
The all-residual term in (One-head-exact-expansion) is at most \(C_KC^3q_2^3\).
Choose \(\delta_1\in(0,1)\) so that \(C_KC^3\delta_1\le c_1/2\).
Then
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 F_1\le Cq_2,\quad q_3\ge dq_2,\quad q_2\ge C_{01}\sqrt n,
 \quad q_2\le\delta_1q_1\\
 \Longrightarrow\quad
 \|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge(c_1/2)q_1q_2^2.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{Bounded-one-head-complete}
\]
In the allocation below \(d=\delta_2\) is fixed first, so there is no circular choice between the two separation thresholds.

## A noncircular six-case allocation covering every sufficiently energetic inverse

Use the full high-rank constants \(C_H,R_H,c_H\) from C187v30.
Use the one-head and two-head high-rank constants proved above, denoted by \(C_{H1},R_{H1},c_{H1},\eta_1\) and \(C_{H2},R_{H2},c_{H2},\eta_2\).
These are already fixed from their separate proofs and depend only on \(K,z\).
Reduce \(\eta_1,\eta_2\) to at most one if needed.
Choose constants in this order:

1. Choose \(K_0\ge\max(1,\sqrt{R_H})\).
2. Choose \(K_1\ge\max(1,\sqrt{R_{H1}},K_0/\eta_1)\).
3. Choose \(K_2\ge\max(1,\sqrt{R_{H2}},K_1/\eta_2)\).
4. Apply B2 with \(C=K_2\), obtaining \(c_2,C_{02}\), and choose \(\delta_2\) by (Bounded-two-head-complete).
5. Apply B1 with \(C=K_1,d=\delta_2\), obtaining \(c_1,C_{01}\), and choose \(\delta_1\) by (Bounded-one-head-complete).
6. Apply B0 with \(C=K_0,d=\delta_1\delta_2\), obtaining \(c_0,C_{00}\).
7. Choose \(L\ge\max(1,C_H,C_{H1},C_{H2},K_2C_{02},K_2C_{01},K_2C_{00})\).

All choices now depend only on \(K,z\).
The same constants apply when these are minors of an ambient dimension \(N\ge n\); the stronger condition \(F_2\ge L\sqrt N\) implies the displayed \(n\)-dimensional threshold.
Assume
\[
 F_2\ge L\sqrt n.
 \tag{All-scale-energy-threshold}
\]
Apply the following rows in order, stopping at the first applicable row.
The conditions marked as prior failures are retained as part of their row.

| Row | Exact branch conditions, after preceding rows fail | Assigned scale \(W\) | Input |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | \(F_0\ge K_0q_1\) | \(F_0^3\) | Full high-rank theorem |
| 2 | \(F_0<K_0q_1\), \(F_1\ge K_1q_2\) | \(q_1F_1^2\) | One-head high-rank theorem |
| 3 | \(F_0<K_0q_1\), \(F_1<K_1q_2\), \(F_2\ge K_2q_3\) | \(q_1q_2F_2\) | Two-head high-rank theorem |
| 4 | \(F_0<K_0q_1\), \(F_1<K_1q_2\), \(F_2<K_2q_3\), \(q_3\le\delta_2q_2\) | \(q_1q_2q_3\) | Bounded two-head absorption |
| 5 | Same three tail upper bounds, \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\), \(q_2\le\delta_1q_1\) | \(q_1q_2^2\) | Bounded one-head absorption |
| 6 | Same three tail upper bounds, \(q_3>\delta_2q_2\), \(q_2>\delta_1q_1\) | \(q_1^3\) | Bounded comparable profile B0 |

These six cases partition all inverse singular profiles satisfying (All-scale-energy-threshold).
Every threshold and scale comparison needed by its input follows as follows.

Row 1 has \(F_0\ge F_2\ge L\sqrt n\) and \(F_0^2\ge R_Hq_1^2\).
In row 2,
\[
 q_2\le F_1/K_1\le F_0/K_1<(K_0/K_1)q_1\le\eta_1q_1,
 \qquad F_1\le K_0q_1.
\]
Together with \(F_1\ge F_2\ge L\sqrt n\) and \(F_1^2\ge R_{H1}q_2^2\), these are the one-head high-rank hypotheses and its compatible upper allocation.
In row 3,
\[
 q_3\le F_2/K_2\le F_1/K_2<(K_1/K_2)q_2\le\eta_2q_2,
 \qquad F_2\le K_1q_2.
\]
The energy and effective-rank conditions follow from \(F_2\ge L\sqrt n\) and \(F_2^2\ge R_{H2}q_3^2\), giving the two-head theorem and its compatible upper allocation.

For rows 4--6, the third tail upper bound and the energy threshold give
\[
 q_3> (L/K_2)\sqrt n.
\]
Thus \(q_3,q_2,q_1\) exceed every bounded-profile absolute-scale threshold fixed in step 7.
Row 4 now satisfies (Bounded-two-head-complete) with \(C=K_2\).
Row 5 satisfies (Bounded-one-head-complete) with \(C=K_1,d=\delta_2\).
In row 6, \(q_3>\delta_1\delta_2q_1\), so B0 applies with \(C=K_0,d=\delta_1\delta_2\).
This proves, with one positive constant \(c\) equal to the minimum of the six fixed lower constants,
\[
 \|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cW
 \quad\text{on its assigned row.}
 \tag{Six-profile-raw-lower-bound}
\]
The exact complete outer Schur identity of C187v30 gives \(\Phi_{4,2}=4h_B(G)\), so the same branchwise lower scale applies to that component of the actual complete numerator.
No middle bidegree or auxiliary constant correction can change these outer coefficients.

## Complete polynomial scale and full same-row graph area

The branch scales also bound all unprojected head/residual tensor terms above by fixed multiples of \(W\).
For row 1 this follows from \(\|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|\le C_KF_0^3\).
For row 2, the one-head expansion has scales \(q_1F_1^2\) and \(F_1^3\), and \(F_1\le K_0q_1\).
For row 3, the two-head expansion has scales \(q_1q_2F_2\), \((q_1+q_2)F_2^2\) and \(F_2^3\), and \(F_2\le K_1q_2\).
For row 4 the same expansion uses \(F_2\le K_2q_3\), with \(q_3\le q_2\le q_1\).
For row 5 use \(F_1\le K_1q_2\) and \(q_2\le q_1\) in the one-head expansion.
For row 6 use \(F_0\le K_0q_1\).
Thus each row is compatible with estimates of every quadratic factor on its own head or residual scale; no physical-error estimate is required to be small relative to a lower scale than that assigned to its complete expansion.

In every row,
\[
 W\ge q_1q_2q_3.
 \tag{Assigned-scale-dominates-three-poles}
\]
For rows 1, 2 and 3 use \(F_0\ge q_1\), \(F_1\ge q_2\) and \(F_2\ge q_3\), respectively; the remaining rows follow directly from the ordered \(q_j\).
Also \(F_2^2\le(n-2)q_3^2\), so the energy threshold and \(L\ge1\) give \(q_3\ge1\).
For the original companion inverse,
\[
 Q^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}I\\B\\A\end{bmatrix}
 G\begin{bmatrix}I&A&2B\end{bmatrix}+\operatorname{diag}(0,-I,-I).
\]
Its first three singular values are at most \(C_Kq_j+1\), \(j=1,2,3\), by the bounded-factor singular-value inequalities and a bounded additive perturbation.
For the complete original fresh border \(R(x,y)\), its norm is bounded by a fixed constant whenever \(\|x\|,\|y\|\le2\).
The same-row graph area therefore satisfies
\[
 \sqrt{\det(I_3+RQ^{-1}Q^{-*}R^*)}
 \le C_K\prod_{j=1}^3(1+q_j)
 \le C_K' q_1q_2q_3\le C_K'W.
 \tag{All-scale-full-graph-area}
\]
This is the full actual inverse and all original border coordinates.
It is not a compressed or independently resampled denominator.
The six-profile theorem supplies the raw lower numerator scale and this compatible upper denominator scale for every sufficiently energetic actual inverse.
A separate physical-coordinate transfer and exact original-law matching argument are still needed for the probability floor.

<a id="remaining-scope"></a>
## Boundary of the raw theorem

The result controls the arithmetic-symmetric tensor of the complete actual outer polynomial at a scale compatible with its singular-value profile.
Passing to six distinct physical positions still requires physical row/column and within-position diagonal spread for the actual full inverse, heads and singular residuals.
The original-law matching step must compare those complete coefficients with the full same-row graph denominator.
Neither conclusion is inferred from the deterministic raw theorem alone.
