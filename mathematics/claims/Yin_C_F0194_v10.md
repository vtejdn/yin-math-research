# Elliptic mirror-pair least-value floors with a corrected mixed and profile argument

This is a same-family least-singular-value proof variant for the exact elliptic input needed by the [additive Brown candidate](Yin_C_F0186_v13.md#inputs).
The [NO15 Theorem 1.9 statement](../audits/literature/NO15/Yin_U002.md#least-value-theorem) identifies the established result and its original scope.
The proof below replaces the arithmetic-dependent mixed geometry and profile interfaces locally; it does not assert that the printed source proof or its literal claims have been corrected in a published edition.
Earlier physical versions retain their own statements, assessments and evidence.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact raw-matrix theorem

Fix centered complex variables \((U,V)\) with \(E|U|^2=E|V|^2=1\), and fixed parameters \(0\le\mu\le1\), \(-1<\rho<1\), satisfying the [elliptic covariance convention](../audits/literature/NO15/Yin_U002.md#elliptic-pair):
\[
 E(\Re U)^2=E(\Re V)^2=\mu,\qquad
 E(\Im U)^2=E(\Im V)^2=1-\mu,
\]
\[
 E\Re U\Re V=\mu\rho,\quad
 E\Im U\Im V=-(1-\mu)\rho,\quad
 E\Re U_i\Im U_j=0\quad(i,j\in\{1,2\}),
 \qquad U_1=U,\ U_2=V.
\]
The components of a pair need not be independent or identically distributed.
Let \(X_N\) have independent mirror pairs \(((X_N)_{ij},(X_N)_{ji})\), \(i<j\), with this fixed law, and independent identically distributed complex diagonals with one fixed centered finite-second-moment law.
These unordered-pair variables and diagonals are mutually independent.
All laws are fixed independently of \(N\).

For every \(b,\alpha>0\) there are fixed \(A,N_0>0\), depending only on these laws, \(\mu,\rho,b,\alpha\), such that
\[
 \sup_{\max_{i,j}|F_{ij}|\le N^\alpha}
 P\{s_{\min}(X_N+F)\le N^{-A}\}\le N^{-b}
 \qquad(N>N_0).
 \tag{NO-floor}
\]
The supremum is over deterministic complex shifts with no symmetry, rank or normalized norm restriction.
The bound includes singular realizations.
For a shift independent of \(X_N\), conditioning gives the same bound on its polynomial entry guard and adds only that guard's failure probability otherwise.
This is a one-dimension marginal estimate, so summable choices permit first Borel--Cantelli under every cross-dimensional coupling preserving the stated marginals.
No density, moment above two, or independence between real and imaginary parts is assumed.

## Complete local argument

### Pair nondegeneracy and uniform cutoff laws

Write \((U,V)\) for the raw NO15 pair and \(Z=(U-U',V-V')\) for the difference of two independent copies.
The covariance definition gives
\[
 E U\overline V=(2\mu-1)\rho,\qquad
 E|U|^2=E|V|^2=1.
\]
Thus the Hermitian covariance of \((U,V)\), and consequently that of \(Z\), is positive definite because \(|(2\mu-1)\rho|<1\).
In particular the support of \(Z\) spans \(\mathbb C^2\) over \(\mathbb C\).
Choose two support points which are complex linearly independent, then small bounded neighborhoods \(H_1,H_2\) of those points.
There are fixed \(C,d,\delta>0\) such that
\[
 P\{Z\in H_j\}\ge\delta,\quad
 \|z\|\le C\ (z\in H_1\cup H_2),\quad
 |\det(z,z')|\ge d\ (z\in H_1,z'\in H_2).
 \tag{Pair-separation}
\]
Individual components of these vectors may be zero; no ratio or lower bound on each component is needed.

Condition each independent raw pair on \(|U|,|V|\le n^\kappa\), and each independent diagonal on its analogous cutoff.
The discarded probability is \(O(n^{2-2\kappa})\), by the second moments and a union bound.
Pair independence survives, although the conditioned covariance need not retain the exact elliptic equations.
The difference-pair laws converge in total variation to the unconditioned difference law, so (Pair-separation), with smaller fixed probabilities, holds for all sufficiently large \(n\).
The proof below uses that separation and two nondegenerate marginal laws, rather than asserting unchanged elliptic moments after conditioning.

For each marginal and for each of the scalar laws \(\eta(U-U')\) and \(\eta(V-V')\), where \(P\{\eta=0\}=1/2\) and \(P\{\eta=\pm1\}=1/4\), the cutoff family has a common TV08 controlled-second-moment constant after a fixed phase for that law.
Here is a direct justification of uniformity for these families.
Choose a fixed bounded subevent of the original pair law on which the relevant scalar has positive variance; for a difference use two such independent bounded events and retain the nonzero sign outcomes.
Its positive subprobability measure is contained in every sufficiently large cutoff.
Rotate a principal real covariance direction of that fixed submeasure to the real axis.
For every complex \(z,w\), integrating \((\Re(zx-w))^2\) over that submeasure is bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \((\Re z)^2\), by its conditional variance and the minimizing choice of \(w\).
Choose the scalar cutoff in TV08's definition to contain this fixed bounded event.
The full nonnegative integral is larger, and the conditional second moments have a common finite upper bound.
Enlarging one fixed control constant proves the assertion for the whole family.
The same bounded subevents give fixed one-variable concentration strictly below one at a fixed positive radius.

### A mixed geometric inverse statement from a real scalar theorem

Let independent pairs have a law whose difference satisfies (Pair-separation), uniformly if the law varies with the cutoff.
Suppose
\[
 \sum_i(|a_i|^2+|b_i|^2)=1,\qquad
 Q\left(\sum_i(a_iU_i+b_iV_i),\beta\right)\ge n^{-B},
 \tag{Mixed-concentration}
\]
where \(Q(Y,t)=\sup_cP\{|Y-c|\le t\}\).
For each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) there is a symmetric proper progression \(P\subset\mathbb C\), of rank and cardinality bounded by \(O_{B,\varepsilon}(1)\) and \(n^{O_{B,\varepsilon}(1)}\), such that all but \(O(n^\varepsilon)\) pairs \((a_i,b_i)\) have both coordinates \(O(\beta\sqrt{\log n})\)-close to \(P\).
The constants can depend on the fixed separation data, but not on the precision exponent in a polynomially small \(\beta\).
Only a polynomial integer-box bound and geometry are asserted, not NO15's disputed arithmetic generators or its optimal progression size.

To prove this, put \(a_i'=a_i/\beta\), \(b_i'=b_i/\beta\), and
\[
 f(t)=\sum_i E\|\Re((a_i'Z_1+b_i'Z_2)t)\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}^2.
\]
Gaussian Fourier inversion, followed by symmetrization of each characteristic function, gives
\[
 n^{-B}\le e^\pi\int_{\mathbb C}e^{-4f(t)}e^{-\pi|t|^2}\,dt.
 \tag{Mixed-Fourier}
\]
Indeed the squared characteristic-function modulus is the expectation of the cosine of the independent difference, and \(1-\cos(2\pi x)\ge8\|x\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}^2\).
The square-root inequality \(\sqrt{1-x}\le e^{-x/2}\) gives the displayed exponent.
The first smoothing inequality follows from \(\mathbf1_{|w-c|\le1}\le e^\pi e^{-\pi|w-c|^2}\).

Choose fixed sufficiently large constants in \(R^2=C_B\log n\) and \(L=C_B\log n\).
The Gaussian tail outside \(|t|\le R\), and the integral over \(f(t)>L\), are each smaller than one quarter of the lower bound in (Mixed-Fourier).
Thus
\[
 T=\{t:|t|\le R,\ f(t)\le L\}
 \quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad |T|\ge c n^{-B}.
\]
Tonelli and (Pair-separation) allow a choice \(c=(c_1,c_2)\in H_1\) and \(c'=(c_1',c_2')\in H_2\) for which the integrals over \(T\) of the two corresponding sums of squared distances are each at most \(L|T|/\delta\).
By Markov's inequality there is a subset of \(T\) of measure at least \(|T|/2\) on which the sum of those two costs is at most \(8L/\delta\).

Let \(\zeta\) be a real compound-Poisson scalar: take a Poisson variable of mean one and sum that many independent fair signs.
Its characteristic function is \(E e^{2\pi i s\zeta}=\exp(\cos(2\pi s)-1)\), which is positive.
Use independent copies for the \(2n\) coefficients
\[
 w_i=c_1a_i+c_2b_i,\qquad
 w_{n+i}=c_1'a_i+c_2'b_i.
\]
Since \(1-\cos(2\pi x)\le2\pi^2\|x\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}^2\), Fourier inversion on the preceding subset yields
\[
 E\exp\left(-\pi\left|\beta^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}w_j\zeta_j\right|^2\right)
 \ge |T|\exp(-C L-\pi R^2)/2\ge n^{-D}
\]
for fixed \(D\).
Choosing \(S^2=C_D\log n\) and subtracting the contribution where the modulus exceeds \(S\) gives
\[
 P\left\{\left|\sum_jw_j\zeta_j\right|\le\beta S\right\}\ge\tfrac12n^{-D}.
\]
The two-by-two matrix with rows \(c,c'\) has bounded norm and bounded inverse, so \(\sigma^2=\sum_j|w_j|^2\) is bounded above and below by positive constants.
Normalize by \(\sigma\), identify complex coefficients with \(\mathbb R^2\), and apply the accepted Ngu12a Theorem 1.3 to the real scalar \(\zeta\).
This law has two distinct atoms of positive probability and therefore satisfies its fixed anti-concentration condition.
The theorem supplies a proper symmetric GAP \(Q\), of bounded rank and polynomial cardinality, approximating all but \(n^\varepsilon\) of the \(w_j\)'s within \(O(\beta S)\).
Invert the bounded two-by-two matrix for each pair outside that exceptional set.
Both \(a_i,b_i\) are then close to the symmetric progression obtained by adding the four appropriate scalar multiples of \(Q\).
Its displayed box has bounded rank and polynomial volume.
Apply the symmetric properization interface in Ngu12a Lemma 2.2, and retain the containing symmetric proper progression.
This proves the mixed geometric assertion, including the coordinate-zero cases excluded by the literal ratio formulation of NO15 Claim A.2.

For coefficient norm \(\sigma_0\ne1\), apply the argument after normalization at radius \(\beta/\sigma_0\) and multiply the progression back by \(\sigma_0\).
The approximation remains \(O(\beta\sqrt{\log n})\).
If \(\sigma_0\le\beta\), the zero progression already gives a sufficient approximation.

The same conclusion holds when any prescribed subset of the independent pairs has the reversed law \((V,U)\).
At such indices interchange \(a_i,b_i\) and write the sum using copies of \((U,V)\) instead.
The total coefficient norm is unchanged, and the conclusion places both coefficient lists in the same proper progression.
Interchanging them back therefore preserves that conclusion, without assigning different progressions to the two orientation groups.
This is the pair-orientation version used for every exposed principal border below.

### Recovering the bounded integer row relations

Use NO15 Lemma 4.3, PDF p. 13, only as its arithmetic-free decoupling statement, proved by the Cauchy--Schwarz argument in Appendix B, pp. 53--55.
Those Cauchy--Schwarz steps require independence of the pairs at distinct indices, not identical orientation of their two components, so they apply to a prescribed mix of the original and reversed pair laws.
Its loss is a fixed power of the small-ball probability and a factor \(O(\sqrt{\log n})\) in the radius.
Condition on one side of a balanced cut \(J\sqcup J^c\).
For a set of conditional configurations of polynomial probability, the remaining mixed linear form has polynomial concentration.
Apply the preceding mixed geometric assertion to its two coefficient lists, then properize.
This supplies the conclusion needed in Lemma 4.5: all but \(O(n^\varepsilon)\) row contractions lie within \(\beta n^{O(1)}\) of a proper symmetric progression of bounded rank and polynomial size.
No rational-coordinate assertion about its generators is used.
Apply the full-span reduction of Ngu12a Theorem 2.1 to those progression points.

The choices in this probabilistic argument can be made measurably without a quantitative arithmetic description.
Increase the approximation tolerance by a fixed factor and enumerate rational complex generators, together with the finitely bounded integer boxes and coordinate patterns.
For a fixed finite proper box, injectivity is an open condition on its generators; density therefore supplies such a rational approximation preserving properness and the enlarged strict tolerance for all the selected coefficients.
Choosing the first admissible element of this countable enumeration is Borel, since its tests are finitely many continuous inequalities.
The later pigeonholes classify only the bounded index and integer-coordinate patterns, not the countably many generator values.
Thus this measurable choice supplies no unproved finite-generator count and changes no entropy estimate.

Choose at most a fixed number of spanning indices and their integer coordinate vectors.
All coordinates are bounded by the polynomial progression size.
There are polynomially many possible index tuples and coordinate matrices, so pigeonhole them on a conditional set of still polynomial probability.
The nonzero determinant \(k\) of the fixed full-rank integer coordinate matrix is common to all remaining row indices and is polynomially bounded.
Cramer's rule gives, for all but \(O(n^\varepsilon)\) rows in \(J\), integers \(k_{ij}\) with polynomial bounds such that
\[
 P_y\left\{\left|\sum_{h\in J^c}
       \left(k A_{ih}+\sum_{j\in I_0}k_{ij}A_{jh}\right)y_h\right|
       \le\beta n^{C}\right\}\ge n^{-C}.
 \tag{Cut-row-relations}
\]
Here \(I_0\subset J\) has bounded size and \(y_h\) is the difference of the two second components at index \(h\); its law is \(V-V'\) or \(U-U'\), according to the prescribed pair orientation.
Dropping the unused conditioned first-component variables preserves this lower bound.
The rank-zero case has \(k=1\), empty \(I_0\), and the same conclusion.
This is precisely the determinant argument of NO15 pp. 14--16 with its proper-coordinate hypothesis now supplied.

For completeness the aggregation over cuts also preserves polynomial constants.
Choose a uniformly random cut and discard the exponentially rare cuts whose sides are not both of order \(n\).
Pigeonhole \(I_0,k\) and its bounded size over all remaining cuts, obtaining a class \(G\) of probability at least \(n^{-C}\).
Except for \(O(\log n)\) indices, \(P\{i\in J\mid G\}\ge1/4\).
To verify this, if a set \(H\) of \(h\) indices all had smaller conditional marginals, Markov gives conditional probability at least \(1/4\) that \(|H\cap J|\le h/3\).
Under the unconditioned uniform cut this event has probability at most
\[
 2^{h/3}(3/4)^h=e^{-c h},
\]
by the exponential Markov bound for its independent bits.
Thus \(n^{-C}/4\le e^{-ch}\), forcing \(h=O(\log n)\).
The average number of indices of \(J\) omitted from (Cut-row-relations), conditional on \(G\), is \(O(n^\varepsilon)\).
Another Markov bound therefore leaves all but \(O(n^\varepsilon)\) indices with a fixed positive conditional probability of belonging to its good row set.
For each such index pigeonhole its bounded integer coefficient tuple, losing another polynomial factor.
On these cuts the row and every basis index lie in \(J\), so (Cut-row-relations) is exactly the original row combination tested against \(\mathbf1_{h\in J^c}y_h\).
Drop the cut-class restriction and integrate over independent uniform cut bits and the \(y_h\)'s.
The resulting independent scalar laws are \(z_h=\eta_h y_h\), since both possible difference laws are symmetric.
Thus each index has one of the two fixed controlled scalar families \(\eta(V-V')\) and \(\eta(U-U')\), in its prescribed orientation.
We obtain the source's required form
\[
 P_z\{|\langle k r_i(A)+\sum_{j\in I_0}k_{ij}r_j(A),z\rangle_{\rm bil}|\le\beta n^C\}
 \ge n^{-C}
 \tag{Bilinear-inverse}
\]
for all but \(O(n^\varepsilon)\) rows, with one fixed basis and nonzero \(k\).
The pairing here is the algebraic sum of coordinate products, without conjugation.
The structural exponent \(C\) is independent of the fine radius exponent.

### The two-marginal joint profile count

The complete [C_F0194_v7 finite-family argument](Yin_C_F0194_v7.md#generic-finite-family-count) extends from the two conjugate orientations to any two fixed nondegenerate marginal laws with the uniform cutoff controls above.
Here the full matrix still has independent unordered pairs; its two entries within a pair may be arbitrarily dependent.
For a subset of coefficients define the concentration envelope by maximizing over all assignments of either marginal law to each coordinate.
Under a simultaneous permutation or transposition the unexposed entries in successive rows have such assignments.
At each step reveal the whole unordered pairs incident to that row and its later columns, including both components.
After conditioning on these earlier revelations the remaining pairs are independent, while earlier pair partners and the deterministic shift change only the center.
This gives the same row-product inequality as in that fixed proof, with only a polynomial loss for omitted rows and the first tail factor.

The nondegenerate single-variable concentration constants are the smaller of the two marginal constants.
The linear oscillation bound used in the degenerate branch also survives: among any \(k\) large coefficients at least \(k/2\) have the same marginal law, so convolution reduces to the already proved one-law oscillation estimate.
Its constants are uniform for the two cutoff families by the same bounded-support difference events used above.
Consequently the degenerate sparse approximants have subexponential cardinality and an exponential row-product bound, just as in the fixed proof.

For nondegenerate candidates retain its numerical choices \(\varepsilon_*=1/100\), \(\delta_* =\varepsilon_*\), \(\theta_* =\varepsilon_*/10\), and reservoir size \(q=\lceil n^{1-\varepsilon_*}\rceil\).
The reservoir gives every retained tail concentration at most \(n^{-b_0}\), where \(b_0=1/2-3\varepsilon_*/4\).
Take a stable radius \(t_\ell\), with enlargement \(t_{\ell+1}=n^{K_0+3}t_\ell\), and split the monotone tail concentrations into bins \([r_h,n^{\varepsilon_*}r_h)\) with block lengths \(m_h\).
The stable product increases by at most \(n^{\varepsilon_* n}\), and there are only a fixed number of blocks after the precision is fixed.

In each block choose a maximizing marginal assignment, then split it into its two law groups.
Convolution gives concentration at least \(r_h\) for each group.
For groups of length \(m\ge n^{1-\delta_*}\), bin their coefficient norm \(\sigma\le2\) on the \(t_\ell\)-grid; the case \(\sigma<t_\ell\) uses zero.
For the other groups TV08 applies to unit coefficients at radius \(t_\ell/s_\sigma\), where \(s_\sigma\le\sigma<s_\sigma+t_\ell\).
Multiplying the net by \(s_\sigma\) gives coordinate error at most \(2t_\ell\).
The common controlled-moment constants, polynomial concentration range and lower radius condition are exactly those checked in the fixed proof.
Its cardinality is
\[
 1+O(t_\ell^{-1})
 \bigl[m^{(-1/2+\varepsilon_*)m}r_h^{-m}+e^{o(m)}\bigr].
\]
The first term in brackets is at least \(n^{\varepsilon_*m/4}\), so the additive term and zero option are absorbed legitimately.
It is at most \(n^{-g m}r_h^{-m}\), with \(g=(1/2-\varepsilon_*)(1-\delta_*)>0.48\).
Short law groups and reservoir coordinates use direct grids on a total of \(o(n)\) coordinates; the law labels cost only \(2^n\), and norm-bin choices have polynomial cost.
The zero approximants for small-norm large groups were already included and absorbed in their displayed net cardinality.
Thus the resulting joint profile net satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal N_{\ell,\mathbf m}|
 \le e^{O(n)+o(n)}n^{-g p}\prod_h r_h^{-m_h},
 \qquad p=n-q+O(1).
 \tag{Corrected-profile-count}
\]

For each used net point choose an original candidate in the same profile and stable scale which it approximates.
Those are deterministic choices before exposing the matrix.
The row error and the error in every marginal assignment of each tail sum are at most \(C n^{K_0+1}t_\ell\).
The enlarged radius lies below \(t_{\ell+1}\), so the chosen witness's stable product bounds the actual row probability of the net point by
\[
 n^{O(1)}n^{2\varepsilon_* n}\prod_h r_h^{m_h}.
 \tag{Corrected-profile-probability}
\]
Both displayed estimates use the same net, witness class, bins and block lengths.
Their product is at most \(e^{O(n)+o(n)}n^{-g p+2\varepsilon_*n}\le n^{-n/3}\) eventually.
The polynomial profile choices, subexponential reservoir choices and omitted-row choices preserve this bound.
This proves exponential exclusion of every deterministic family of size \(n^{C_*n+o_s(n)}\), with its leading \(C_*\) independent of the fine precision exponent \(s\).
It is a joint-profile replacement for the source's (31)--(32), rather than an assertion that a single TV08 net supplies both bounds.

### Economical cofactor witnesses

Normalize the adjugate of a fixed principal minor \(D\) to a coefficient matrix \(A\) with Frobenius norm one.
For a border at original index \(i\), the ordered entries \((M_{ij},M_{ji})\) have noise law \((V,U)\) for \(j<i\) and \((U,V)\) for \(j>i\).
Use precisely these deterministic orientations in the preceding decoupling, mixed geometry and integer-row relation.
If the conditional border determinant has concentration at least \(n^{-B}\) at radius \(n^{-a}\), (Bilinear-inverse) gives polynomially bounded integer row combinations \(r_i'\), for all but \(O(n^\varepsilon)\) indices.
They have polynomial concentration at radius \(n^{-a+C}\) for the independent two-law scalar assignment \(z_h\) obtained above.
The adjugate identities hold for singular minors as well.

If some \(\|r_i'\|_2\ge n^{-a/2}\), its unit normalization \(v\) has scalar concentration at least \(n^{-C}\) at radius \(n^{-a/2+C}\).
Also \(vD\) is zero outside the bounded set consisting of its row index and the basis indices, because \(\operatorname{adj}(D)D=\det(D)I\).
TV08 and norm binning provide an initial coordinate cover for these unit vectors, with cardinality \(n^{C_*n+o_a(n)}\) and coordinate radius \(n^{-a/2+C'}\), where \(C_*\) is independent of \(a\).
For this initial cover split the coordinates into their two \(z_h\)-law groups.
Convolution bounds the concentration of each group from below by the concentration of the full sum.
Apply the common controlled-moment net to each large group's normalized coefficients and norm bins; grid the short groups, exactly as in the initial-cover construction of C_F0194_v7.
Their law labels cost at most \(2^n\), their short-group costs are \(n^{o_a(n)}\), and their product has the stated leading entropy independent of \(a\).
This uses finite nets and never asserts a rational-generator description.
For sufficiently large \(a\) the rounded vectors have norm between one half and two and are near kernels of \(D^T\) at the fine tolerance required by the profile count.

Otherwise all \(\|r_i'\|_2<n^{-a/2}\).
Choose a column \(w\) of \(A\) with norm at least \(n^{-1/2}\).
For the good indices it obeys
\[
 |k w_i+\sum_{j\in I_0}k_{ij}w_j|\le n^{-a/2}.
\]
Round its bounded number of basis entries and its \(O(n^\varepsilon)\) exceptional entries on a sufficiently fine polynomial grid, and define all other entries by the displayed bounded integer relations.
There are \(n^{O(1)n}\) choices for the integers, while all chosen scalar values cost \(n^{O_a(n^\varepsilon)}\).
The resulting family is economical with leading entropy independent of \(a\).
Its error from \(w\) is \(n^{-a/2+C}\) per coordinate.
Multiplication by one of polynomially many positive integers of size \(O(\sqrt n)\) puts its norm between one half and two.
Since \(Dw\) is supported at its column index, these vectors are near kernels of \(D\) outside one row, at tolerance \(n^{-a/2+C'}\).
Apply the joint profile exclusion to both cases after choosing \(a\) sufficiently large.
The probability of a minor admitting either witness is \(O(e^{-cn})\).

### Full-rank reduction and the singular case

On the polynomial entry cutoff, if a full-rank \(M\) has \(s_{\min}(M)\le n^{-L}\), then
\[
 \|\operatorname{adj}M\|_{\mathrm F}
 =|\det M|\|M^{-1}\|_{\mathrm F}\ge n^L|\det M|.
\]
Expanding cofactors along one border shows that for at least one principal \((n-1)\)-minor \(D_i\),
\[
 \|\operatorname{adj}D_i\|_{\mathrm F}\ge n^{L-C_K}|\det M|.
\]
The expansion uses only polynomially bounded entries and a polynomial number of terms; the diagonal cofactor is expanded along a row of \(D_i\).
Conditional on \(D_i\) and the diagonal border entry, the normalized determinant is a bilinear form of independent mirror pairs with deterministic shifts and an arbitrary center.
If its concentration at radius \(n^{-a}\) is below \(n^{-B}\), that border contributes at most \(n^{-B}\).
Otherwise the economical cofactor argument gives a near-kernel witness of the minor, whose probability is exponentially small.
Take a union over the \(n\) borders and choose \(B\) above the desired polynomial failure exponent.
Then choose \(L\) large enough for the fixed structural constants and all fine tolerances.
This proves the requested polynomial bound on the full-rank event, uniformly over the polynomial shift class in the cutoff model.
Choose the cutoff exponent first so its discarded probability is smaller than the desired bound, and use two extra powers of \(n\) to absorb constants.

There is no need to change the entry law to settle exact singularity.
For each fixed size and shift, \(\det(M+tI)\) is a monic polynomial in the real variable \(t\) for every realization.
Fubini therefore gives a deterministic sequence of real \(t_j\to0\) such that \(M+t_jI\) has full rank almost surely for every \(j\).
The already proved full-rank bound has the same constants for those shifts, since \(F+t_jI\) remains inside a polynomial entry bound with one larger fixed exponent.
Continuity of \(s_{\min}\) and Fatou yield
\[
 P\{s_{\min}(M)<n^{-L}\}
 \le\liminf_j P\{s_{\min}(M+t_jI)\le n^{-L}\}\le n^{-B}.
\]
Increasing \(L\) by one supplies the closed threshold requested in (NO-floor), including singular realizations.
All constants depend only on the fixed laws, pair separation, shift exponent and requested failure exponent, and are independent of the particular shift.
This removes the dimension-dependent smoothing-law issue in the source's Section 2.5 without weakening the conclusion.
