# Least-value floors for complex mirror pairs with positive Hermitian covariance

This version extends the fixed [elliptic argument in C_F0194_v10](Yin_C_F0194_v10.md#argument) from its EC covariance pattern to positive Hermitian covariance of the centered mirror pair.
It retains that argument's geometric, integer-coordinate and profile modules, and supplies the changed applicability proof below.
The exact generalization and its Hermitian specialization have a [fresh independent whole-proof finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v11.md#positive-pair-whole-proof), selected in the assessment below.
The earlier versions and their adopted scopes remain at their fixed addresses.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Fixed mirror-pair theorem

Let \((U,V)\) be one fixed pair of centered complex random variables satisfying
\[
 E|U|^2=E|V|^2=1,\qquad
 \gamma:=E(U\overline V),\qquad |\gamma|<1.
 \tag{Positive-pair}
\]
Equivalently its complex Hermitian covariance is
\[
 G=E\!\left[
   \begin{pmatrix}U\\V\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}\overline U&\overline V\end{pmatrix}
       \right]
   =\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma\\\overline\gamma&1\end{pmatrix}
   \succ0.
\]
For each \(N\), let \(X_N\) have independent unordered mirror pairs
\(((X_N)_{ij},(X_N)_{ji})\), \(i<j\), each with this law.
Let its diagonal entries be independent copies of one fixed complex law \(\zeta\) with \(E|\zeta|^2<\infty\), independent of every off-diagonal pair.
The diagonal law may be noncentered or deterministic.
All these laws are independent of \(N\), and the components within a mirror pair may be arbitrarily dependent.

For every \(b,\alpha>0\) there exist \(A,N_0>0\), depending only on the fixed laws, \(b\) and \(\alpha\), such that
\[
 \sup_{\max_{i,j}|F_{ij}|\le N^\alpha}
 P\{s_{\min}(X_N+F)\le N^{-A}\}\le N^{-b},
 \qquad N>N_0,
 \tag{Pair-floor}
\]
where the supremum ranges over deterministic complex matrices \(F\).
There is no symmetry, rank or normalized norm restriction on \(F\).
The estimate includes exact singularity and uses no moment above two, bounded density, or independence between the real and imaginary parts.

For a random \(F_N\) independent of \(X_N\), the same constants give
\[
 P\{s_{\min}(X_N+F_N)\le N^{-A}\}
 \le N^{-b}
       +P\{\max_{i,j}|(F_N)_{ij}|>N^\alpha\}.
 \tag{Independent-shift}
\]
All assertions are one-dimension marginal estimates.
Consequently, if \(b>1\) and the shift-guard failures are summable, the displayed floor holds eventually almost surely under every coupling between dimensions preserving the stated within-dimension laws and independence.
The [normalized Hermitian consequence below](#hermitian-specialization) is included in this statement.

<a id="hermitian-specialization"></a>
## Non-line complex Hermitian specialization

Let \(\xi\) be one fixed complex law with
\[
 E\xi=0,\qquad E|\xi|^2=1,\qquad |E\xi^2|<1.
 \tag{Non-line-law}
\]
Let \(W_N\) be a Hermitian Wigner matrix with independent upper entries \(\xi/\sqrt N\), conjugate lower entries, and independent identically distributed real diagonal entries \(\zeta/\sqrt N\) of one fixed finite-second-moment law.
The diagonal and upper-entry families are independent.
Then for every \(b,\alpha>0\) there are \(A,N_0>0\) such that
\[
 \sup_{\max_{i,j}|F_{ij}|\le N^\alpha}
 P\{s_{\min}(W_N+F)\le N^{-A}\}\le N^{-b},
 \qquad N>N_0,
 \tag{Hermitian-floor}
\]
for arbitrary deterministic complex shifts \(F\).
The independent-random-shift and cross-dimensional conclusions above also apply.

To match the pair theorem, take \((U,V)=(\xi,\overline\xi)\).
Writing \(m=E\xi^2\), its Hermitian covariance is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&m\\\overline m&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}=1-|m|>0.
\]
Apply (Pair-floor) to \(X_N=\sqrt N W_N\) and the shift \(\sqrt N F\), whose entries are bounded by \(N^{\alpha+1/2}\), and divide singular values by \(\sqrt N\).
The strict inequality in (Non-line-law) is equivalent to the centered law not being supported on a real line through the origin after a fixed phase rotation: equality in \(|E\xi^2|\le E|\xi|^2\) holds precisely when \(\xi^2\) has one fixed phase almost surely.

This specialization lies outside the EC statement of [C_F0194_v10](Yin_C_F0194_v10.md#statement).
Indeed that covariance pattern forces \(EUV=\rho\), whereas here \(EUV=E|\xi|^2=1\).
The present proof therefore uses the retained argument modules with a new applicability argument, rather than invoking the old elliptic theorem for this pair.

## Applicability proof for the larger pair class

<a id="pair-separation-and-cutoffs"></a>
### Positive covariance, repeated differences and common cutoffs

Let \(P=(U,V)^T\), and let \(P',P'',P'''\) be independent copies.
For
\[
 Z=P-P',\qquad
 \widehat Z=(P-P')-(P''-P''')
\]
centering and independence give
\[
 EZZ^*=2G,\qquad
 E\widehat Z\widehat Z^*=4G.
 \tag{Repeated-difference-Gram}
\]
Thus neither support is contained in a proper complex linear subspace of \(\mathbb C^2\).
Otherwise a nonzero complex linear functional vanishing on that support would have zero second moment, contradicting the corresponding positive-definite matrix.

For each of \(Z,\widehat Z\), choose two complex linearly independent support points and sufficiently small bounded neighborhoods \(H_1,H_2\).
Continuity of the determinant gives fixed constants \(C,d,\delta>0\), allowed to differ between the two difference laws, such that
\[
 P\{Z\in H_j\}\ge\delta,\qquad
 \|z\|\le C,\qquad
 |\det(z,z')|\ge d
 \quad(z\in H_1,\ z'\in H_2),
 \tag{Separated-directions}
\]
and the corresponding assertion holds for \(\widehat Z\) with its chosen neighborhoods.
No lower bound on either individual coordinate is required.

Condition each independent raw pair on \(\max(|U|,|V|)\le N^\kappa\), and each diagonal on \(|\zeta|\le N^\kappa\).
The union bound and second moments discard probability \(O(N^{2-2\kappa})\).
The remaining unordered pairs and diagonals are independent.
The conditional pair law converges in total variation to the original pair law.
The same is true for its two-copy and four-copy product laws and their difference images, so both sets of fixed separated neighborhoods retain uniform positive probabilities for all large \(N\).
No exact covariance equation is asserted for a conditionally truncated law.

This repeated-difference point is needed explicitly in the decoupling step below: there the independent pairs entering the mixed inverse theorem are copies of \(Z\), and that theorem tests the difference \(\widehat Z\) of two such copies.
Both applications therefore have fixed separation constants independent of the fine radius exponent.
All principal minors keep the original ambient cutoff \(N^\kappa\); for their comparable dimensions \(n\), its polynomial amplitude bound is absorbed by one fixed larger exponent, as in v7.

Positive definiteness of \(G\) implies that each marginal has positive variance.
For each of \(U,V\) and of the scalar laws \(\eta(U-U')\), \(\eta(V-V')\), where \(P(\eta=0)=1/2\) and \(P(\eta=\pm1)=1/4\), select a fixed bounded subevent of the requisite raw pair copies on which the scalar has positive conditional variance.
For the difference laws include a nonzero sign outcome.
This gives a fixed positive subprobability measure contained in every sufficiently large cutoff law, with uniformly positive weight after conditioning.
Rotate a principal eigenvector of its real covariance matrix to the real axis.
For every \(z,w\in\mathbb C\), its integral of \((\Re(zx-w))^2\) is then bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \((\Re z)^2\), since minimizing over \(w\) leaves that conditional variance.
Choose one fixed TV08 scalar cutoff containing this bounded submeasure.
The remaining integrand is nonnegative, while conditional second moments are uniformly bounded.
Thus each of the four families has one fixed phase and one common controlled-second-moment constant.
The same bounded difference events give one-variable concentration strictly below one at a fixed positive radius.
These scalar families may use different fixed phases and constants; taking the largest control constant and smallest concentration margin suffices.

If \(E\zeta\ne0\), replace \(\zeta\) by \(\zeta-E\zeta\) and add \(E\zeta I\) to the deterministic shift before making these cutoffs.
Finite second moment is preserved, and a fixed increase of the shift exponent absorbs this constant diagonal shift.
Thus the centered-diagonal setup of v10 covers the stated arbitrary fixed diagonal law.

<a id="mixed-geometry"></a>
### The mixed geometric inverse module

The mixed inverse module in v10 begins with independent pairs whose difference law has (Separated-directions).
For coefficients with \(\sum_i(|a_i|^2+|b_i|^2)=1\), polynomial concentration at radius \(\beta\) of \(\sum_i(a_iU_i+b_iV_i)\) yields, by Gaussian smoothing and characteristic-function symmetrization, a Fourier set of polynomial measure and cost \(O(\log n)\).
These two operations use independence across indices and the difference law; they use no covariance identity.

Averaging over the two separated neighborhoods chooses bounded directions \(c,c'\) with bounded complex inverse matrix.
For
\[
 w_i=c_1a_i+c_2b_i,\qquad
 w_{n+i}=c'_1a_i+c'_2b_i,
\]
that inverse bounds \(\sum_j|w_j|^2\) both above and below.
The fixed real compound-Poisson scalar with characteristic function
\(\exp(\cos(2\pi t)-1)\) then transfers the Fourier cost to polynomial concentration of an independent real-scalar sum at radius \(O(\beta\sqrt{\log n})\).
Its positive atoms at zero and one verify Ngu12a's anti-concentration condition.
After norm normalization, the complex coefficients are vectors in \(\mathbb R^2\), exactly the ambient domain of the accepted real-scalar theorem.

Its bounded-rank proper symmetric progression approximates all but \(O(n^\varepsilon)\) of the \(w_j\).
Inverting the same bounded complex matrix puts both original coefficient lists near the sum of four scalar multiples of that progression.
Its bounded-rank polynomial integer box has a proper symmetric containing progression by the accepted properization interface.
All approximation and structural exponents are independent of the precision exponent in a polynomially small \(\beta\).
For nonunit coefficient norm, v10's normalization and zero-progression case apply unchanged.

At indices with reversed pair law \((V,U)\), interchange \(a_i,b_i\) before applying this single construction, and interchange them back afterward.
Both lists remain near the same progression.
No identical marginal laws, conjugation symmetry, separated coordinate ratios or EC moment equations enter this argument.
The preceding cutoff and repeated-difference checks establish its hypotheses for both raw pairs and the difference pairs required next.

<a id="decoupling-and-integer-relations"></a>
### Decoupling, orientations and integer row relations

For a bilinear expression \(\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}U_iV_j\) with arbitrary deterministic linear terms and center, fix a cut \(J\sqcup J^c\).
The accepted arithmetic-free decoupling argument uses successive Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities across these two independent index blocks, subtracting independent copies of each entire block.
For \(Z_i=(Z_{1i},Z_{2i})\), the remaining expression is
\[
 \sum_{i\in J,\,j\in J^c}
   \bigl(A_{ij}Z_{1i}Z_{2j}
           +A_{ji}Z_{1j}Z_{2i}\bigr).
 \tag{Decoupled-cut}
\]
The same-side and deterministic linear terms disappear.
Gaussian smoothing and its tail estimate give the fixed fourth-power loss in concentration and the \(O(\sqrt{\log n})\) radius enlargement recorded in v10.
Those inequalities require no independence between \(Z_{1i}\) and \(Z_{2i}\).
They hold for any fixed mixture of original and reversed orientations, since each whole indexed pair remains independent of the other pairs.

Conditioning on the complete pairs in \(J^c\) leaves independent difference pairs in \(J\).
Their two coefficients are \(\sum_{j\in J^c}A_{ij}Z_{2j}\) and \(\sum_{j\in J^c}A_{ji}Z_{1j}\).
Apply the mixed geometric module to these two lists using the separation of \(\widehat Z\) proved above.
Polynomial conditional-probability selection, properization and full-span reduction now give exactly the proper integer-coordinate model used in v10's bounded row-relation construction.

That construction chooses boundedly many spanning indices, pigeonholes their polynomially bounded integer-coordinate matrix and applies Cramer's rule.
The common nonzero integer determinant and all row coefficients have polynomial bounds independent of the fine radius exponent.
Neither step uses information about the scalar law beyond the geometric model just obtained.
The measurable rational approximation of generators preserves the fixed finite-box properness and enlarged tolerance, while the later counts classify only the finite index and integer patterns.

The conditional row relation depends on the second components in the opposite block after those patterns have been fixed.
Discarding unused first-component conditions is marginalization of an event and preserves its probability lower bound; it does not require random second components after conditioning on their partners.
The cut aggregation, exceptional-index estimate and rowwise pigeonholes in v10 therefore apply unchanged.
After dropping the cut-class restriction and integrating independent cut bits, the scalar variables have laws
\(\eta(U-U')\) or \(\eta(V-V')\), according to the fixed orientations.
These laws have the common controlled-moment and nondegeneracy properties established above.
This supplies v10's (Bilinear-inverse), including its ordinary algebraic coordinate pairing without conjugation.

### Joint profiles and economical cofactor witnesses

The two-marginal joint profile proof of v10 takes the concentration envelope over all assignments of the two marginal laws.
For a fixed vector and row order, reveal the entire unordered pairs incident to the current row and its later columns.
After earlier revelations the remaining pairs are still independent, and each fresh coordinate has the appropriate fixed marginal law.
The already revealed mirror partners, diagonal and deterministic shift affect only the center.
Thus the row-product inequality holds for arbitrary within-pair dependence and every deterministic orientation pattern.

The degenerate branch uses bounded difference events and the degree-one oscillation bound.
Among large coefficients one can retain the larger same-law group and use convolution, so the two marginal constants suffice.
For the nondegenerate branch, each block's maximizing assignment is split into its two law groups.
Convolution, coefficient-norm binning and the common controlled-moment TV08 theorem give the same large-group nets, while short groups and the reservoir use direct grids.
The law labels cost at most \(2^n\).
The additive \(\exp(o(m))\) in TV08's cardinality is absorbed by the same reservoir concentration bound as in v10.

The deterministic original witness chosen for each used net point belongs to the same profile and stable scale.
The common polynomial amplitude bound controls both its row error and every assignment of each tail sum.
Hence v10's corrected profile count and matching row probability retain their common concentration factors, and their product is at most \(n^{-n/3}\) eventually.
The low-product and degenerate branches retain their stated exponential estimates.
This proves the economical deterministic-family exclusion with leading entropy independent of fine precision.
The same reasoning applies to the transpose: reverse the prescribed pair orientations and use the same two marginal laws.

For a principal border at index \(i\), the incident pairs have law \((V,U)\) for \(j<i\) and \((U,V)\) for \(j>i\).
They are independent of the principal minor.
Normalize its nonzero adjugate in Frobenius norm and apply the established bilinear inverse with exactly those orientations.
If a good integer row combination has sufficiently large norm, its unit normalization has polynomial concentration for the appropriate two difference-marginal laws.
Splitting those coordinates into their two law groups, using the common TV08 constants and norm bins, gives v10's economical initial cover.
The adjugate identity makes these rounded vectors near kernels of the transpose minor outside boundedly many rows.

In the other branch, a column of the normalized adjugate has norm at least \(n^{-1/2}\).
The fixed integer relations determine all its good coordinates from boundedly many basis entries, up to the stated small error.
Grid only the basis and exceptional coordinates, retain the polynomial integer choices, and use the adjugate identity to obtain near-kernel vectors of the minor outside one row.
Both resulting families have the same leading entropy independent of precision as in v10, so the joint profile exclusion bounds their probability by \(O(e^{-cn})\).
The adjugate identities also hold for singular minors.
All uses of a pair law in these two witness branches have now been matched; neither requires EC.

### Full-rank assembly, singularity and parameter order

Reuse v10's deterministic cofactor expansion on the polynomial entry cutoff.
For full-rank \(M=X_N+F\) with \(s_{\min}(M)\le N^{-L}\), it produces a principal minor whose adjugate norm is at least \(N^{L-C_K}|\det M|\).
Conditional on this minor and the diagonal border entry, the normalized bordered determinant is the preceding bilinear expression with deterministic linear terms and arbitrary center.
Low conditional concentration contributes at most \(N^{-B}\) for that border.
High concentration gives one of the economical minor witnesses, already excluded with exponentially small probability.
The union over all principal borders and the choice of sufficiently large \(L,B\) are unchanged.

Choose the cutoff exponent first so that \(O(N^{2-2\kappa})\) is smaller than the desired failure bound with a fixed power margin.
The pair-separation, marginal-control, geometric and row-count constants are then fixed.
Choose the conditional probability exponents, the structural constants and finally the fine radius exponent in the order of v10 and v7.
The normalized structural bounds are independent of that last exponent, and all sufficiently-large thresholds may depend on the fixed laws and chosen \(b,\alpha\).
Two extra powers of \(N\) absorb constants and the border union.
This gives (Pair-floor) on full-rank realizations uniformly over the polynomial shift class.

For exact singularity, \(\det(M+tI)\) is a monic polynomial in real \(t\) for each realization.
Fubini supplies a deterministic sequence \(t_j\to0\) for which each \(M+t_jI\) is full rank almost surely.
The uniform full-rank estimate applies to \(F+t_jI\) inside a polynomial guard with one larger fixed exponent.
Continuity of \(s_{\min}\), Fatou and then a one-unit increase in the threshold exponent give the closed floor event, including singular matrices, exactly as in v10.
Conditioning on an independent random shift proves (Independent-shift).
First Borel--Cantelli gives the asserted summable conclusion without independence between dimensions.
This completes the applicability proof.

## Scope change and remaining assessment

On 2026-11-05, an agent developing the argument for Yin replaced the EC pattern of v10 by (Positive-pair), preserving unit marginal variances and fixed-law finite-second-moment input.
The new mathematical point is that the retained proof uses the positive Hermitian Gram only through separated difference directions and fixed nondegenerate marginals, with the repeated-difference interface supplied explicitly here.
This adds the non-line Hermitian case, while v10's earlier elliptic statement and evidence retain their original scope.
The arbitrary fixed complex diagonal law is covered by the explicit centering conversion.

The strict positive-definiteness hypothesis cannot simply be dropped for all mirror-pair laws.
For example \(\xi=i\eta\), with centered real variance-one \(\eta\) and zero diagonal, gives odd-dimensional Hermitian matrices that are also transpose-skew and hence singular identically at zero shift.
Here \(|E\xi^2|=1\), so this obstruction is outside the claimed specialization.
No assertion is made that every phase-line law obstructs a floor.

The authoring agent checked the changed covariance, cutoff, repeated-difference, orientation and row-count interfaces as ordinary proof development.
This is not a formal independent review of the enlarged statement.
No independent finding covering the old EC scope is selected as evidence for the new scope.
The new independent finding checks this fixed argument and its exact reuse of v10 at the enlarged scope; the selected decision below owns its adoption.
The broader determinant Tasks and any consuming polynomial linearization retain their separate hypothesis-matching and logarithmic obligations.
