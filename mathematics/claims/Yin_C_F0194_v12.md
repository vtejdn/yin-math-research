# A conditional least-value floor from two original sign rows

This version develops a different conditional floor within the C_F0194 family: two original independent sign rows are retained at an actual quadratic-pencil minor.
The earlier [mirror-pair theorem](Yin_C_F0194_v11.md#statement) keeps its separate fixed statement and assessment; its conclusion is not an input here.
The cancelling-minor calculation in [D_F0032](../drafts/Yin_D_F0032.md#two-row-model) motivated this extension, but all necessary algebra and estimates are fixed below.
The only non-elementary probabilistic input is the accepted [MNV16 matching-rank theorem](../audits/literature/MNV16/Yin_U001.md#matching-rank).
The conditional theorem has [independent whole-proof coverage](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v12.md#two-original-row-whole-proof), selected below; it does not solve [F0013](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract) or [F0014](../tasks/Yin_T_F0014_v1.md#contract).

<a id="statement"></a>
## Conditional two-row theorem

Fix \(\alpha=\sqrt2\), \(K\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\), and \(M\ge1\).
There are \(\delta,c,C>0\) and \(N_0\), depending only on \(K,M\), with the following property.
Let \(N\ge N_0\), \(n=N-1\), and let \(C_1,C_2\in M_n(\mathbb R)\) be deterministic symmetric matrices such that
\[
 X'=C_1+C_2,\qquad Y'=C_1+\alpha C_2,\qquad
 \mathcal M=
 \begin{pmatrix}X'&K I_n\\K I_n&Y'\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\hbox{is invertible},\qquad
 \|\mathcal M^{-1}\|\le M.
 \tag{Minor-guard}
\]
No bound on \(\|\mathcal M\|\), \(C_1\), or \(C_2\) is assumed.
Let \(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n,\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_n\) be independent fair signs, and put
\[
 x=N^{-1/2}\varepsilon,\qquad y=N^{-1/2}\eta,\qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}a&x^T\\x&C_1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}b&y^T\\y&C_2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad |a|+|b|\le\delta .
 \tag{Original-rows}
\]
The exposed diagonals \(a,b\in\mathbb R\) are deterministic in this conditional statement.
Set
\[
 L_N=
 \begin{pmatrix}A+B&K I_N\\K I_N&A+\alpha B\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Q_N=(A+\alpha B)(A+B)-K^2 I_N .
\]
Then, uniformly over every displayed minor and exposed diagonal,
\[
 \mathbb P_{\varepsilon,\eta}
 \left\{s_{\min}(L_N)\le cN^{-4}
       \ \hbox{or}\ s_{\min}(Q_N)\le cN^{-4}\right\}
 \le C\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/34}.
 \tag{Two-row-floor}
\]
The bound includes exact singularity.
In particular it holds with \(a=b=0\).

The proof also gives a uniform small-ball bound for the exact two-by-two Schur determinant \(p\) defined below:
\[
 \mathbb P_{\varepsilon,\eta}\{|p|\le N^{-4}\}
 \le C\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/34}.
 \tag{Schur-small-ball}
\]
This is a bound for that Schur determinant, rather than an unnormalized claim about \(\det L_N\).

<a id="diagonal-corollary"></a>
### Fixed-position finite-second-moment diagonals

Let \(A,B\) instead be independent normalized real Wigner matrices whose off-diagonal laws are fair signs and whose diagonal laws are fixed real \(\zeta_1,\zeta_2\) with finite second moments.
The diagonal laws may be centered, as in the original Tasks, but centering is not needed for this corollary.
At one fixed exposed physical index let \(\mathcal G_N\) denote (Minor-guard) for the two corresponding original principal minors.
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\mathbb P\left(\mathcal G_N\cap\left\{
       s_{\min}(L_N)\le cN^{-4}
       \ \hbox{or}\ 
       s_{\min}(Q_N)\le cN^{-4}\right\}\right)\\
 &\hspace{20mm}\le
 C\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/34}
 +\frac{2(\mathbb E|\zeta_1|^2+\mathbb E|\zeta_2|^2)}
        {\delta^2N}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Guarded-Wigner-floor}
\]
Consequently the unrestricted failure probability is bounded by the displayed right side plus \(\mathbb P(\mathcal G_N^c)\).
No estimate for \(\mathbb P(\mathcal G_N^c)\) is asserted.
In particular, conditioning on a rare prescribed minor atom is not being converted into a typical-minor statement.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Proof

All constants in the proof may depend on \(K,M\), with \(\alpha=\sqrt2\) fixed.
An admissible diagonal guard is
\[
 \delta=\min\left\{
 1,\frac{K^2}{4\bigl(1+\alpha+(\alpha-1)^2M\bigr)}
 \right\}.
 \tag{Diagonal-guard}
\]
The estimates are eventually uniform in \(N\); increasing \(N_0\) absorbs fixed constants and integer rounding.

<a id="exact-schur"></a>
### Actual minor and original sign polynomial

Write the actual symmetric inverse as
\[
 \mathcal M^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}G&H\\H^T&J\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 u=x+y,\quad v=x+\alpha y,\quad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}u^T&0\\0&v^T\end{pmatrix}.
\]
After simultaneously placing the two exposed physical coordinates first,
\[
 L_N\simeq
 \begin{pmatrix}D_0&R\\R^T&\mathcal M\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 D_0=\begin{pmatrix}a+b&K\\K&a+\alpha b\end{pmatrix},
\]
and its Schur complement is
\[
 S=D_0-R\mathcal M^{-1}R^T
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
  a+b-u^TGu&K-u^THv\\
  K-u^THv&a+\alpha b-v^TJv
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Exact-Schur}
\]
Thus
\[
 p:=\det S
 =(a+b-u^TGu)(a+\alpha b-v^TJv)-(K-u^THv)^2 .
 \tag{Original-polynomial}
\]
The blocks \(G,H,J\) are always those of this actual inverse.
No independence or freely chosen inverse-block model is used.

Put \(m=2n\), \(w=(\varepsilon,\eta)\), and define
\[
 P=(I_n\ \ I_n),\qquad V=(I_n\ \ \alpha I_n),
\]
\[
 \mathsf A=P^TGP,\qquad
 \mathsf B=V^TJV,\qquad
 \mathsf T=\tfrac12(P^THV+V^TH^TP).
\]
These real symmetric \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrices satisfy
\[
 \|\mathsf A\|+\|\mathsf B\|+\|\mathsf T\|\le C,
 \qquad
 u^TGu=N^{-1}w^T\mathsf A w,\quad
 v^TJv=N^{-1}w^T\mathsf B w,\quad
 u^THv=N^{-1}w^T\mathsf T w .
 \tag{Quadratic-forms}
\]
Replace \(w_i^2\) by one, as required by the actual sign law.
The unique multilinear expansion of \(p\) is
\[
 p(w)=p_0+\sum_{|E|=2}p_Ew_E+\sum_{|E|=4}p_Ew_E
      =p_0+p_2(w)+p_4(w),
 \qquad w_E=\prod_{i\in E}w_i.
 \tag{Cube-expansion}
\]
There are no odd layers.
Write
\[
 \sigma_d^2=\sum_{|E|=d}p_E^2,\qquad
 \Lambda_{i,d}=\sum_{\substack{|E|=d\\i\in E}}p_E^2
 \quad(d=2,4).
\]
Orthogonality of the sign characters gives \(\mathbb E p=p_0\), \(\mathbb E p_4^2=\sigma_4^2\), and \(\operatorname{Var}p=\sigma_2^2+\sigma_4^2\).

<a id="uniform-coefficients"></a>
### Uniform coefficient and load bounds

For a matrix \(U\), write \(\operatorname{off}(U)\) for its zero-diagonal part.
For the three Schur entries write
\[
 f=f_0-\frac2N\sum_{i<j}\mathsf A_{ij}w_iw_j,\qquad
 h=h_0-\frac2N\sum_{i<j}\mathsf B_{ij}w_iw_j,\qquad
 r=r_0-\frac2N\sum_{i<j}\mathsf T_{ij}w_iw_j,
\]
where
\[
 f_0=a+b-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf A}{N},\qquad
 h_0=a+\alpha b-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf B}{N},\qquad
 r_0=K-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf T}{N}.
\]
All three constants are bounded uniformly.
Since \(p=fh-r^2\), its quadratic coefficient matrix
\((\mathcal Q_2)_{ij}=p_{\{i,j\}}\) for \(i\ne j\), with zero diagonal, is exactly
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal Q_2=\operatorname{off}\bigg[
 &-\frac2N(f_0\mathsf B+h_0\mathsf A-2r_0\mathsf T)\\
 &+\frac4{N^2}\bigl(
    \mathsf A^\circ\mathsf B^\circ+
    \mathsf B^\circ\mathsf A^\circ-
    2(\mathsf T^\circ)^2\bigr)\bigg],
 \qquad U^\circ=\operatorname{off}(U).
 \end{split}
 \tag{Quadratic-coefficients}
\]
The second line includes exactly the products of two edges sharing one sign coordinate.
For real symmetric \(U\), \(\|\operatorname{off}(U)\|\le2\|U\|\), since every diagonal entry is at most \(\|U\|\) in modulus.
It follows that
\[
 \|\mathcal Q_2\|\le \frac C N,\qquad
 |p_{\{i,j\}}|\le\frac C N,\qquad
 \Lambda_{i,2}\le\frac C{N^2},\qquad
 \sigma_2^2\le\frac C N.
 \tag{Quadratic-bounds}
\]
Here the row-square sum of the symmetric matrix \(\mathcal Q_2\) is at most \(\|\mathcal Q_2\|^2\), and \(2\sigma_2^2=\sum_i\Lambda_{i,2}\).

For a four-element set \(E\), let the following sum run over its three unordered partitions into two pairs:
\[
 p_E=\frac4{N^2}
 \sum_{\{e,f\}:\ e\sqcup f=E}
   \left(\mathsf A_e\mathsf B_f+
         \mathsf A_f\mathsf B_e-
         2\mathsf T_e\mathsf T_f\right).
 \tag{Quartic-coefficients}
\]
For symmetric \(U\), put
\[
 \ell_i(U)=\sum_{j\ne i}U_{ij}^2,\qquad
 \mathcal E(U)=\sum_{j<k}U_{jk}^2.
\]
Applying the finite-sum Cauchy--Schwarz inequality to (Quartic-coefficients) and summing all four-sets containing \(i\) gives
\[
 \Lambda_{i,4}\le\frac C{N^4}
 \left[
  \ell_i(\mathsf A)\mathcal E(\mathsf B)
 +\ell_i(\mathsf B)\mathcal E(\mathsf A)
 +\ell_i(\mathsf T)\mathcal E(\mathsf T)
 \right].
 \tag{Quartic-load}
\]
Indeed every squared product pairs an edge incident to \(i\) with an edge on the other two coordinates; dropping the distinctness restrictions only enlarges these nonnegative sums.
The operator bounds in (Quadratic-forms) give \(\ell_i(U)\le C\) and \(\mathcal E(U)\le Cm\) for these three matrices.
Consequently
\[
 \Lambda_{i,4}\le\frac C{N^3},\qquad
 \sigma_4^2=\tfrac14\sum_i\Lambda_{i,4}\le\frac C{N^2},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}p\le\frac C N.
 \tag{Uniform-loads}
\]
These bounds do not assert any positive fourth-layer energy.

<a id="anti-diagonal-witness"></a>
### A witness that forces a useful constant, quadratic, or quartic layer

On the sign subcube \(\eta=-\varepsilon\), one has \(u=0\) and \(v=(1-\alpha)\varepsilon/\sqrt N\).
Therefore
\[
 p(\varepsilon,-\varepsilon)
 =(a+b)\left(a+\alpha b-\frac{(\alpha-1)^2}{N}
                         \varepsilon^TJ\varepsilon\right)-K^2 .
 \tag{Restriction}
\]
In particular \(p=-K^2\) on this subcube when \(a=b=0\), for every actual minor.
Thus at zero exposed diagonals the whole sign support cannot collapse identically to a singular Schur complement.

Label the sign coordinates by \(\epsilon_i,\eta_i\), and set
\[
 d_i=p_{\{\epsilon_i,\eta_i\}},\qquad
 e_{ij}=p_{\{\epsilon_i,\eta_i,\epsilon_j,\eta_j\}}\quad(i<j).
\]
Averaging (Restriction) over its \(n\) independent signs kills every monomial except the constant and these paired monomials, so
\[
 p_0-\sum_i d_i+\sum_{i<j}e_{ij}
 =\mathcal W,\qquad
 \mathcal W=(a+b)\left(a+\alpha b-
            \frac{(\alpha-1)^2}{N}\operatorname{Tr}J\right)-K^2 .
 \tag{Paired-identity}
\]
Since \(\|J\|\le M\), (Diagonal-guard) implies
\[
 |\mathcal W+K^2|
 \le \delta\bigl((1+\alpha)\delta+(\alpha-1)^2M\bigr)
 \le K^2/4,
 \qquad |\mathcal W|\ge 3K^2/4.
 \tag{Witness-gap}
\]
This retains a quantitative witness without assuming a uniform nonzero highest sign degree.

<a id="three-branches"></a>
### Three branches using only MNV

First suppose \(|p_0|\ge K^2/4\).
For large \(N\), \(|p|\le N^{-4}\) implies \(|p-p_0|\ge K^2/8\).
Chebyshev's inequality and (Uniform-loads) give
\[
 \mathbb P\{|p|\le N^{-4}\}\le C/N.
 \tag{Constant-branch}
\]
This also covers a degree-zero polynomial, for which the probability is eventually zero.

Now suppose \(|p_0|<K^2/4\) and
\[
 \sigma_4^2\ge N^{-5/2}.
 \tag{Large-quartic}
\]
Set
\[
 \lambda_4=\frac{\sigma_4}{\sqrt2\,m^2}
       \ge cN^{-13/4}.
\]
There are at most \(m^4\) fourth-degree monomials, so deleting coefficients with \(|p_E|<\lambda_4\) removes at most \(\sigma_4^2/2\) of their squared energy.
Greedily select a remaining four-set and delete every remaining set meeting it.
By (Uniform-loads), each selection deletes at most \(4CN^{-3}\) of energy.
The selected disjoint four-sets therefore number at least
\[
 r_4\ge c\,\sigma_4^2N^3\ge cN^{1/2},
 \tag{Quartic-matching}
\]
with the lower bound weakened by a fixed factor if needed for integer rounding.
For large \(N\), \(r_4\ge2\) and \(2N^{-4}<\lambda_4\).
Apply [MNV16 Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/MNV16/Yin_U001.md#matching-rank) at degree four to the full real multilinear polynomial \(p/\lambda_4\).
Its lower-degree coefficients and constant term are unrestricted by that theorem.
The event under consideration is contained in an interval of length one after this rescaling, and hence
\[
 \mathbb P\{|p|\le N^{-4}\}
 \le C\sqrt{\log r_4}\,r_4^{-1/17}
 \le C\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/34}.
 \tag{Quartic-branch}
\]

It remains to consider \(|p_0|<K^2/4\) and
\[
 \sigma_4^2<N^{-5/2}.
 \tag{Small-quartic}
\]
The paired fourth-degree coefficients satisfy
\[
 \left|\sum_{i<j}e_{ij}\right|
 \le \sqrt{\binom n2}\,\sigma_4
 \le C N^{-1/4}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(N\), this is at most \(K^2/4\).
Together with (Paired-identity) and (Witness-gap), this yields
\[
 \left|\sum_i d_i\right|\ge K^2/4=:a_*.
 \tag{Paired-quadratic-mass}
\]
By (Quadratic-bounds), \(|d_i|\le C_0/N\) for a fixed \(C_0\).
Set \(\lambda_2=a_*/(2N)\).
The indices with \(|d_i|<\lambda_2\) contribute at most \(a_*/2\) to \(\sum_i|d_i|\), whereas that full sum is at least \(a_*\).
There are therefore at least
\[
 r_2\ge \frac{a_*N}{2C_0}
 \tag{Quadratic-matching}
\]
indices with \(|d_i|\ge\lambda_2\).
Their monomials \(\varepsilon_i\eta_i\) are pairwise disjoint on the original \(2n\)-sign cube.

Let \(q=p_0+p_2\), and keep \(p_4\) as the exact multilinear remainder.
For large \(N\), \(N^{-4}\le\lambda_2/8\), so
\[
 \{|p|\le N^{-4}\}
 \subseteq
 \{|q|\le3\lambda_2/8\}
 \ \cup\ \{|p_4|>\lambda_2/4\}.
\]
The first event concerns an interval of length \(3\lambda_2/4<\lambda_2\).
Apply MNV at degree two to \(q/\lambda_2\), using (Quadratic-matching), and apply Chebyshev to the second event:
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{|p|\le N^{-4}\}
 &\le C\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/9}
     +\frac{16\sigma_4^2}{\lambda_2^2}\\
 &\le C\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/9}+CN^{-1/2}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Quadratic-branch}
\]
This argument uses no independence between \(p_2\) and \(p_4\).
In particular it does not apply a degree-two theorem directly to a polynomial with an undiscarded nonzero fourth layer.
The remainder is controlled at scale \(N^{-1}\), which is the paired-matching scale, rather than at the final scale \(N^{-4}\).

The three exhaustive branches prove (Schur-small-ball).
They include a vanishing fourth layer and do not require a universal fourth-degree energy lower bound.

<a id="singular-value-conversion"></a>
### Conversion to the full pencil and original quadratic polynomial

Since the original signs have fixed modulus,
\[
 \|R\|\le 1+\alpha,\qquad
 \|S\|\le\|D_0\|+M\|R\|^2\le C.
\]
The exact block factorization is
\[
 L_N\simeq
 \begin{pmatrix}I_2&R\mathcal M^{-1}\\0&I_{2n}\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}S&0\\0&\mathcal M\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}I_2&0\\\mathcal M^{-1}R^T&I_{2n}\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Block-factorization}
\]
Both triangular inverse norms are at most \(1+M\|R\|\le C\).
For a two-by-two matrix, the product of its singular values is its determinant modulus, so \(s_{\min}(S)\ge |p|/C\).
Together with \(s_{\min}(\mathcal M)\ge M^{-1}\), this gives
\[
 s_{\min}(L_N)\ge c_0\min\{1,|p|\}.
 \tag{Normalized-pencil-floor}
\]
Thus a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(N^{-4}\) for \(s_{\min}(L_N)\) forces \(|p|\le N^{-4}\).
This step retains the minor inverse and both triangular factors.

After swapping the two auxiliary column blocks, \(L_N\) becomes
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}K I_N&A+B\\A+\alpha B&K I_N\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its Schur complement over the first block is \(-Q_N/K\).
Hence \(L_N\) and \(Q_N\) are singular simultaneously; if invertible, the lower-right block of the displayed inverse is \(-KQ_N^{-1}\).
Therefore
\[
 \|Q_N^{-1}\|\le |K|^{-1}\|L_N^{-1}\|,
 \qquad s_{\min}(Q_N)\ge |K|\,s_{\min}(L_N).
\]
Taking \(c>0\) sufficiently small relative to \(c_0\) and \(|K|\), the union of the two floor events in (Two-row-floor) is contained in \(\{|p|\le N^{-4}\}\).
This proves the conditional theorem.

For the Wigner corollary, condition on both original principal minors and the two exposed diagonal values.
The off-diagonal boundary signs remain independent and fair.
The deterministic theorem applies on \(\mathcal G_N\) when \((|\zeta_1|+|\zeta_2|)/\sqrt N\le\delta\).
The complementary diagonal event has probability at most
\[
 \mathbb P\{|\zeta_1|+|\zeta_2|>\delta\sqrt N\}
 \le\frac{2(\mathbb E|\zeta_1|^2+\mathbb E|\zeta_2|^2)}
           {\delta^2N}.
\]
Integrating the conditional bound and adding this event proves (Guarded-Wigner-floor).
No union over exposed physical indices is taken.

<a id="scope-and-boundary"></a>
## Mathematical advance and unresolved use

The new conclusion is uniform over every actual minor satisfying (Minor-guard), not merely the cancelling minor.
The anti-diagonal witness forbids whole-support collapse at zero exposed diagonals, and the coefficient estimates turn that witness into either a constant gap, a fourth-degree matching, or a large paired quadratic matching.
Thus low sign degree is covered by an explicit alternative rather than assumed away.

The probability bound tends to zero but is not summable over all dimensions.
It gives neither an arbitrary polynomial failure exponent nor an automatic almost-sure full-sequence floor.
The entry-law conclusion here is for two original real Rademacher off-diagonal rows; other fixed finite-second-moment off-diagonal laws are not obtained by this argument.
The small deterministic diagonal guard and fixed-position finite-second-moment corollary do not permit arbitrary large exposed shifts.

For a typical-minor use, one must still control failure of (Minor-guard), including singular minors and inverse norms growing with \(N\).
The constants here depend on the fixed \(M\); substituting \(M=M_N\to\infty\) is not justified by the statement.
Even an applicable least-value floor would still need its actual combination with the intermediate and bottom logarithmic estimates required for the original determinant contracts.
No finite-target determinant equality or arbitrary matrix-coefficient theorem is claimed.

The source input is only the already accepted real multilinear Rademacher theorem at degrees four and two, with its coefficient threshold and interval scaling explicitly checked above.
Its original proof and external dependencies are not re-audited here.
No Gaussian invariance theorem, mixed-factor conditioning, or unproved typical-minor assertion is used.
