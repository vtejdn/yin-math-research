# Graph-normalized row energy and an averaged two-channel least-value reduction

This version fixes a graph normalization that matches a conditional fourth-layer coefficient scale to physical column distances.
The full-matrix step uses the already reviewed [rectangular input](Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#rectangles), while the dominant-mode application uses the reviewed [physical and residual controls](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#statement) and [cross-energy theorem](Yin_C_F0187_v5.md#actual-cross-energy-statement).
The earlier [fixed-inverse theorem](Yin_C_F0194_v12.md#statement) retains its own scope; no fixed or polynomial upper bound on the minor inverse is assumed below.
The reviewed whole-layer estimate in [C_F0187_v6](Yin_C_F0187_v6.md#statement) additionally covers minors above a fixed effective inverse rank.
The remaining average of singular minors or minors in neither coefficient branch is explicit and is not asserted to vanish.
This project deduction has [independent whole-proof coverage](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v13.md#graph-energy-and-averaged-reduction) at its exact stated probability-reduction scope.

<a id="statement"></a>
## General conditional energy-to-matrix reduction

Fix real \(\alpha,K\).
Let \(W_N\) be real symmetric, with independent off-diagonal fair signs divided by \(\sqrt N\), and independent real diagonal entries independent of that off-diagonal family.
Let \(B_N\) be any independent real symmetric random matrix, and set
\[
 L_N:=\begin{pmatrix}W_N+B_N&K I_N\\K I_N&W_N+\alpha B_N\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Two-channel-model}
\]
No moment bound on \(B_N\) or the diagonal entries is imposed in this norm-guarded reduction.

For physical index \(i\), condition on the full \(B_N\), the principal minor \(W_N^{(i)}\), and \((W_N)_{ii}\), and denote this sigma-field by \(\mathcal F_i\).
The remaining original row has \(n:=N-1\) independent fair signs.
After placing the two exposed coordinates first, write
\[
 L_N\simeq
 \begin{pmatrix}A_i&\mathcal B_i(\varepsilon)^T\\
                 \mathcal B_i(\varepsilon)&M_i\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here \(A_i\) and the actual physical minor \(M_i\) are \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable, and the \(2n\)-by-\(2\) matrix \(\mathcal B_i\) is affine in the row signs.
On the invertible-minor branch put
\[
 Z_i:=M_i^{-1},\qquad
 S_i:=A_i-\mathcal B_i^TZ_i\mathcal B_i,\qquad
 p_i:=\det S_i,\qquad
 \Gamma_i:=I_2+\mathcal B_i^TZ_i^2\mathcal B_i.
 \tag{Conditional-Schur-data}
\]
Reduce all repeated signs in \(p_i\) using \(\varepsilon_j^2=1\).
The resulting real multilinear polynomial has degree at most four.
Define
\[
 \sigma_{4,i}^2:=\sum_{|E|=4}|(p_i)_E|^2,\qquad
 \tau_{4,i}:=
 \frac{\displaystyle\max_j\sum_{\substack{|E|=4\\j\in E}}|(p_i)_E|^2}
      {\sigma_{4,i}^2}
\]
where the denominator is positive.

Fix \(\kappa_0>0\), let \(N^{-1/2}\le t_N\to0\) be deterministic, and suppose there are \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable events \(\mathcal G_i\) and positive scales \(a_i\) such that on \(\mathcal G_i\)
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 M_i\text{ is invertible},\qquad
 \sup_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^{n}}\sqrt{\det\Gamma_i(\varepsilon)}
       \le a_i,\\
 \sigma_{4,i}\ge\kappa_0 a_i/N^2,\qquad
 \tau_{4,i}\le t_N .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{Graph-energy-conditions}
\]
The scales may depend arbitrarily on the conditioned data and on \(N\).
No inverse-norm bound is part of these hypotheses.

Set \(r_N:=\lfloor(16t_N)^{-1}\rfloor\), which is at least two eventually.
There are \(c,C>0\), depending only on the fixed parameters and \(\kappa_0\), such that for every deterministic \(b_N\ge1\) and all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{\|L_N\|\le b_N,\
             s_{\min}(L_N)\le c b_N^{-2}N^{-5}\}
 \le{}& Ce^{-cN}
       +\frac C N\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)\\
      &+C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/17}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Graph-energy-matrix-reduction}
\]
The conclusion includes full singular matrices of every corank.
The three terms tend to zero if the average good-conditioning failure tends to zero, but the theorem does not prove that last hypothesis.

<a id="dominant-branch-statement"></a>
### Application retaining only the singular and nondominant minor exception

Now fix \(\alpha=\sqrt2\), \(K=1\), and let \(W_1:=W_N,W_2:=B_N\) follow the bounded real model of [C_F0187_v5](Yin_C_F0187_v5.md#statement), with the first off-diagonal law Rademacher.
Thus the second off-diagonal law is fixed, real, centered, variance one and bounded; both diagonal laws are fixed, real, centered and bounded; and all upper coordinates and the two inputs are independent within each size.
Zero diagonal laws are allowed, and cross-size coupling is unrestricted.

For each invertible \(M_i\), choose a measurable closest-to-zero real unit eigenmode \(w_i\), and put
\[
 M_iw_i=\lambda_iw_i,\qquad q_i:=\lambda_i^{-1},\qquad
 E_i:=M_i^{-1}-q_iw_iw_i^T
      =\begin{pmatrix}F_i&H_i\\H_i^T&J_i\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad T_i:=(H_i+H_i^T)/2,
\]
\[
 \mathcal R_i^2
 :=\|\operatorname{off}F_i\|_{\rm F}^2+
   \|\operatorname{off}J_i\|_{\rm F}^2+
   \|\operatorname{off}T_i\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
The choice is made from the minor only and is fixed before exposing the row.
All input estimates used below are uniform in this choice, including choices within repeated eigenspaces.
For example, ordered eigenvalues, a fixed tie rule, and the first nonzero coordinate projection onto the chosen eigenspace give such a Borel choice.

There exist \(K_*,c,C>0\), depending only on the fixed laws, such that, with
\[
 \mathcal H_i:=
 \{M_i\text{ is singular}\}
 \ \cup\
 \{M_i\text{ is invertible and }|q_i|<K_*\mathcal R_i\},
 \tag{Unresolved-minor-exception}
\]
one has
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)\le cN^{-5}\}
 \le o(1)+\frac C N\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb P(\mathcal H_i).
 \tag{Dominant-branch-reduction}
\]
The same bound holds for the event
\[
 s_{\min}(L_N)\le cN^{-5}
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 s_{\min}(Q_N)\le cN^{-5},\qquad
 Q_N:=(W_1+\sqrt2W_2)(W_1+W_2)-I_N .
 \tag{Quadratic-reduction}
\]
The \(o(1)\) is an unconditional dimension-wise error at the fixed-law scope; no summability is asserted.
Neither the singular-minor term nor the nondominant-minor term has been shown to vanish.
These statements are probability reductions, not unconditional least-value floors or solutions of the original determinant Tasks.

<a id="combined-branch-statement"></a>
### Combining the high-effective-rank and dominant branches

In the same bounded model, use the fixed threshold \(r_*\) of [C_F0187_v6](Yin_C_F0187_v6.md#statement), and on each invertible minor define
\[
 r_{Z_i}:=\frac{\|M_i^{-1}\|_{\rm F}^2}{\|M_i^{-1}\|^2},\qquad
 \mathcal J_i:=\{M_i\text{ is singular}\}
 \ \cup\
 \{M_i\text{ is invertible},\ r_{Z_i}<r_*,\
                              |q_i|<K_*\mathcal R_i\}.
 \tag{Combined-minor-exception}
\]
Then there are fixed \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)\le cN^{-5}
                 \text{ or }s_{\min}(Q_N)\le cN^{-5}\}
 \le o(1)+\frac C N\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb P(\mathcal J_i).
 \tag{Combined-branch-reduction}
\]
The remaining invertible regime has bounded effective inverse rank and no sufficiently dominant single mode.
Neither this regime nor the singular-minor term is shown to have vanishing average probability.
The high-effective-rank input has its own [reviewed decision](Yin_C_F0187_v6.md#decision-whole-layer-rank-reviewed); the general bridge and dominant-branch consequence do not require it.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Complete proof

<a id="graph-distance"></a>
### Exact graph normalization

For a real symmetric matrix
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}A&B^T\\B&M\end{pmatrix},\qquad A\in M_2(\mathbb R),
 \quad M\text{ invertible},
\]
put \(Z:=M^{-1}\), \(S:=A-B^TZB\), and
\[
 \Gamma:=I_2+B^TZ^2B,\qquad
 U:=\begin{pmatrix}I_2\\-ZB\end{pmatrix}\Gamma^{-1/2}.
\]
Then \(U^TU=I_2\) and \(U^T\binom{B^T}{M}=0\).
The remaining columns have full column rank because their lower block is \(M\).
Thus \(U\) is an orthonormal basis of their orthogonal complement.
If \(C_{\rm exp}:=\binom{A}{B}\) is the two-column exposed block, its distance matrix is
\[
 D:=U^TC_{\rm exp}=\Gamma^{-1/2}S.
 \tag{Exact-graph-distance}
\]
The two singular values of \(U D\) equal those of \(D\), so this also computes the distance after orthogonal projection in the original ambient space.
Consequently
\[
 \|D\|\le\|L\|,\qquad
 \det D=\det S/\sqrt{\det\Gamma},
\]
and
\[
 \{s_{\min}(D)\le r,\ \|L\|\le b\}
 \subseteq
 \{|\det S|\le br\sqrt{\det\Gamma}\}.
 \tag{Distance-event}
\]
This retains only the graph area \(\sqrt{\det\Gamma}\).
A bound on two triangular elimination inverses, or on a two-sided Schur compression, is not substituted for this distance identity.

<a id="conditional-matching"></a>
### Coefficient energy, matching and a finite interval

Fix good conditioning at index \(i\).
There are at most \(n^4\) fourth-degree monomials.
Delete those with coefficient modulus below
\[
 \ell_i:=\frac{\sigma_{4,i}}{\sqrt2\,n^2}
       \ge\frac{\kappa_0 a_i}{\sqrt2\,N^4}.
 \tag{Matching-threshold}
\]
At most half of their squared energy is deleted.
A greedy selection of a retained four-set removes all intersecting sets, whose total squared energy is at most
\(4\tau_{4,i}\sigma_{4,i}^2\).
Therefore the number of selected disjoint monomials is at least
\((8\tau_{4,i})^{-1}\), and in particular at least \(r_N\) eventually.
Integer rounding is absorbed by the factor sixteen in the definition of \(r_N\).

The accepted [MNV16 Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/MNV16/Yin_U001.md#matching-rank), applied to \(p_i/\ell_i\) at degree four, gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{|p_i|\le\rho\mid\mathcal F_i\}
 \le C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/17}
 \quad\hbox{if }2\rho<\ell_i .
 \tag{Conditional-small-ball}
\]
One may use the actual matching rank, which is at least \(r_N\); the displayed bound follows for large \(N\) from the eventual monotonicity of \(\sqrt{\log r}\,r^{-1/17}\).
All lower-degree and constant coefficients are unrestricted.
The polynomial has real coefficients and is multilinear on the actual remaining independent fair-sign row, so no Gaussian or two-copy substitution is being assumed.

Choose a sufficiently small fixed \(c_1>0\), depending on \(\kappa_0\), and set
\[
 r:=c_1b_N^{-1}N^{-4}.
\]
On \(\mathcal G_i\), (Distance-event) and the uniform graph-area hypothesis imply
\[
 \{d_i\le r,\ \|L_N\|\le b_N\}
 \subseteq\{|p_i|\le c_1a_iN^{-4}\},
\]
where \(d_i\) denotes the two-column distance.
Take \(2c_1<\kappa_0/\sqrt2\).
The interval requirement in (Conditional-small-ball) then holds uniformly, regardless of the magnitude of the conditioned \(a_i\).
Integrating gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{d_i\le c_1b_N^{-1}N^{-4},\ \|L_N\|\le b_N\}
 \le\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
       +C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/17}.
 \tag{Integrated-distance}
\]

<a id="block-averaging"></a>
### Many physical indices without a union loss

The fixed [all-small-support rectangular result](Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#rectangles) applies to a normalized real finite-second-moment Wigner off-diagonal law with arbitrary independent diagonal entries and every deterministic shift, uniformly in that shift.
It supplies fixed \(\delta,a,c,C>0\) such that, except with probability \(Ce^{-cN}\), every rectangle \((W+F)[I,J]\), with
\[
 |J|=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor,\qquad I:=[N]\setminus J,
\]
has least singular value at least \(a/\sqrt N\).
Only this precise result is invoked from that fixed Claim.

Condition on the whole independent \(B_N\), and apply it twice, with shifts \(B_N\) and \(\alpha B_N\).
A union over these two events costs only a fixed factor, and they need not be independent.
For the two-channel matrix,
\[
 L_N[I,J]=\operatorname{diag}
       ((W_N+B_N)[I,J],(W_N+\alpha B_N)[I,J]),
\]
because the identity coupling has no entries across disjoint physical sets.
Its least singular value is therefore at least \(a/\sqrt N\) on an event \(\mathcal A_N\) with
\(\mathbb P(\mathcal A_N^c)\le Ce^{-cN}\).
The constants and event probability are uniform over the conditioned second input.

Suppose \(\|L_N\|\le b_N\), \(\|w\|=1\), and
\(\|L_Nw\|\le a/(4\sqrt N)\).
Put \(k_N:=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\) and
\[
 \theta_N:=\frac a{8b_NN}.
\]
If at most \(k_N\) physical blocks \(w_i\in\mathbb R^2\) had norm larger than \(\theta_N\), choose \(J\) containing them.
Then
\[
 \|w_I\|\le\sqrt N\theta_N=\frac a{8b_N\sqrt N},
 \qquad \|w_J\|\ge\tfrac12
\]
eventually, and on \(\mathcal A_N\)
\[
 \|(L_Nw)_I\|
 \ge\frac a{\sqrt N}\|w_J\|-b_N\|w_I\|
 \ge\frac{3a}{8\sqrt N},
\]
a contradiction.
Thus more than \(k_N\) physical blocks satisfy \(\|w_i\|>\theta_N\).

For every index, including when its retained principal minor is singular, let \(Q_i\) be the orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement of the span of all columns outside that physical block.
Let \(C_i\) be its two columns, and define \(d_i:=s_{\min}(Q_iC_i)\).
No rank assumption or two-dimensionality of the complement is needed for this definition.
Since
\[
 Q_iL_Nw=Q_iC_iw_i,
\]
a block with \(\|w_i\|>\theta_N\) in a vector of residual at most \(\epsilon\) satisfies \(d_i\le\epsilon/\theta_N\).
Use a real least singular vector of \(L_N\).
For \(0\le\epsilon\le a/(4\sqrt N)\), the preceding count and Markov's inequality give
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\mathbb P\{\|L_N\|\le b_N,\ s_{\min}(L_N)\le\epsilon\}\\
 &\qquad\le Ce^{-cN}
 +\frac2{\delta N}\sum_{i=1}^N
 \mathbb P\{d_i\le8b_NN\epsilon/a,\ \|L_N\|\le b_N\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Distance-average}
\]
The argument includes full singular matrices of every corank and uses no independence between these \(N\) distance events.
Take
\(\epsilon:=c b_N^{-2}N^{-5}\), with \(c>0\) small enough that
\(8b_NN\epsilon/a\le c_1b_N^{-1}N^{-4}\).
This residual is also below \(a/(4\sqrt N)\) eventually since \(b_N\ge1\).
Substitution of (Integrated-distance) proves (Graph-energy-matrix-reduction).

<a id="pole-area"></a>
### The graph area on a largest-mode decomposition

Let \(Z:=M^{-1}\) and choose a closest-to-zero real unit eigenmode of \(M\).
Write
\[
 Z=qww^T+E,\qquad Ew=0,\qquad |q|=\|Z\|,\qquad \|E\|\le|q|.
\]
For a boundary matrix with \(\|B\|\le B_0\), put
\[
 a:=B^Tw,\qquad \Gamma_0:=I_2+B^TE^2B .
\]
The identity \(Ew=0\) gives
\[
 \Gamma=\Gamma_0+q^2aa^T,\qquad
 \det\Gamma=\det\Gamma_0+q^2a^T\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma_0)a.
\]
Let \(T:=(1+B_0^2\|E\|^2)^{1/2}\).
Then
\[
 \sqrt{\det\Gamma}\le T^2+|q|B_0T
 \le C_{B_0}(1+|q|)(1+\|E\|).
 \tag{Pole-area-bound}
\]
The first inequality uses \(\|\Gamma_0\|\le T^2\), the two-dimensional equality
\(\|\operatorname{adj}\Gamma_0\|=\|\Gamma_0\|\), and \(\|a\|\le B_0\).
The second uses \(\|E\|\le|q|\).
Thus, if
\[
 |q|\ge1,\quad \mathcal R\ge1,\quad
 \|E\|_{\rm F}\le C_0\mathcal R,
\]
then
\[
 \sup_{\|B\|\le B_0}\sqrt{\det\Gamma}
 \le C_{B_0,C_0}|q|\mathcal R.
 \tag{Pole-graph-scale}
\]
There is no upper bound on \(|q|\) here and no pole-dominance assumption in this graph calculation.

For comparison, without any mode decomposition one always has
\[
 \sqrt{\det\Gamma}\le1+B_0^2\|Z\|^2
                         \le1+B_0^2\|Z\|_{\rm F}^2 .
\]
Hence a fourth-layer energy estimate at scale \(\|Z\|_{\rm F}^2/N^2\) can also use the general theorem when \(\|Z\|_{\rm F}^2\ge1\).
This is the graph scale used by the separate high-effective-rank application below.

<a id="dominant-coefficients"></a>
### Full fourth-layer energy and loads on the dominant branch

Write \(w=(u,v)\),
\[
 E=\begin{pmatrix}F&H_0\\H_0^T&J\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T_0:=(H_0+H_0^T)/2,
\]
and use \(\mathcal R,\rho_j,\eta_j\) as defined in
[C_F0187_v5](Yin_C_F0187_v5.md#actual-cross-energy-statement) and its preceding residual definitions.
Explicitly,
\[
 \rho_j:=\sum_{k\ne j}(F_{jk}^2+J_{jk}^2+(T_0)_{jk}^2),
 \qquad \eta_j:=u_j^2+v_j^2,\qquad \sum_j\rho_j=\mathcal R^2.
\]
The fourth Walsh layer of the Schur determinant has the exact decomposition
\[
 p_4=q s_4+e_4,
\]
\[
 s(x):=(u^Tx)^2(x^TJx)+(v^Tx)^2(x^TFx)
          -2(u^Tx)(v^Tx)(x^TT_0x),
 \qquad
 e(x):=(x^TFx)(x^TJx)-(x^TT_0x)^2,
 \tag{Pole-polynomials}
\]
where the subscript four means actual fourth Walsh component after \(x_j=\varepsilon_j/\sqrt N\).
The rank-one square cancels identically.
The conditioned second-input border and the exposed diagonals affect only raw degrees at most three, so they do not change this fourth component.

Here are the elementary coefficient bounds needed in addition to the reviewed cross-energy input:
\[
 \begin{split}
 L_j(s_4)&\le\frac C{N^4}(\eta_j\mathcal R^2+\rho_j),\\
 L_j(e_4)&\le\frac C{N^4}\rho_j\mathcal R^2,\qquad
 \sigma(e_4)\le C_e\mathcal R^2/N^2,\\
 L_j(p_4)&\le\frac C{N^4}
       \bigl(q^2\eta_j\mathcal R^2+(q^2+\mathcal R^2)\rho_j\bigr).
 \end{split}
 \tag{Pole-coefficient-bounds}
\]
To verify them, a fourth coefficient of a product of two quadratic forms is \(4/N^2\) times the sum over the three partitions of four distinct indices into two pairs, with the two assignments of the matrices to the pairs.
For a fixed coordinate \(j\), the finite-sum square inequality bounds its load by
\[
 \frac C{N^4}\bigl(\ell_j(A)\|\operatorname{off}B\|_{\rm F}^2+
                  \ell_j(B)\|\operatorname{off}A\|_{\rm F}^2\bigr),
 \qquad \ell_j(A):=\sum_{k\ne j}|A_{jk}|^2 .
\]
Each rank-one or symmetrized rank-one factor from \(u,v\) has total squared norm at most a fixed constant and row energy at most \(C\eta_j\).
Each residual factor has total off-diagonal squared norm at most \(\mathcal R^2\) and row energy at most \(\rho_j\).
This proves the first two load bounds.
Summing the second over \(j\), with every four-set counted four times, gives the bound on \(\sigma(e_4)\).
The last line follows from \(|q s_E+e_E|^2\le2q^2|s_E|^2+2|e_E|^2\).
This proof uses the actual independent-sign basis and does not replace a Walsh norm by an unrestricted Gaussian polynomial norm.

Suppose now that
\[
 \sigma(s_4)^2\ge c_*\mathcal R^2/N^4,\qquad
 |q|\ge K_*\mathcal R,\qquad
 K_*:=\max\{1,2C_e/\sqrt{c_*}\}.
\]
The coefficient-space triangle inequality gives
\[
 \sigma(p_4)\ge\frac{\sqrt{c_*}}2\,|q|\mathcal R/N^2,\qquad
 \tau_4(p)\le C_{c_*}
       \left(\max_j\eta_j+\max_j\rho_j/\mathcal R^2\right).
 \tag{Dominant-energy-load}
\]
This is the sole use of pole dominance.
It prevents cancellation between \(q s_4\) and \(e_4\); the cross-energy result alone does not do that.

<a id="dominant-probability-assembly"></a>
### Matching the fixed probability inputs

The [bounded norm event](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#spectral-residual-spread) gives a fixed \(b_0\ge1\) such that \(\mathbb P\{\|L_N\|>b_0\}\le Ce^{-cN}\).
The raw bounded laws also give a uniform bound on every boundary coupling \(\mathcal B_i(\varepsilon)\) over all allowed first-row signs, after the other input is fixed.
The bound depends only on the laws and fixed coefficients.

Use the following exact reviewed inputs, uniformly over all single physical minors and all closest-mode choices on their invertible branches: the [closest-mode physical leverage and residual row spread](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#statement), the [residual divergence](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#spectral-residual-spread), the [comparison \(\mathcal R^2\ge c\|E\|_{\rm F}^2\)](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#actual-residual-blocks), and the [cross-energy lower bound](Yin_C_F0187_v5.md#actual-cross-energy-statement).
The [fixed minor spectral mass](Yin_C_F0187_v4.md#weighted-minor-mass) makes the closest eigenvalue tend to zero in the same uniform probability sense.
Thus, outside a bad event of probability \(o(1)\) intersected with invertibility, one has simultaneously
\[
 |q_i|\ge1,\qquad \mathcal R_i\ge1,\qquad
 \|E_i\|_{\rm F}\le C_0\mathcal R_i,\qquad
 \sigma(s_{4,i})^2\ge c_*\mathcal R_i^2/N^4,
\]
and the maxima of \(\eta_j\) and \(\rho_j/\mathcal R_i^2\) tend to zero.
These are unconditional bad-event statements; no probability has been divided by a minor's invertibility probability.

Choose a deterministic \(t_N\to0\), enlarged to be at least \(N^{-1/2}\), slowly enough that the load bound in (Dominant-energy-load) exceeds \(t_N\) only on another uniform \(o(1)\) bad event.
For each \(i\), let \(\mathcal G_i\) impose the preceding good conditions and \(|q_i|\ge K_*\mathcal R_i\).
Every such condition depends only on its minor, and hence is \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable.
Choose
\[
 a_i:=C_1|q_i|\mathcal R_i
\]
on this event, with \(C_1\) large enough for (Pole-graph-scale), and assign any positive value outside it.
Then (Graph-energy-conditions) hold with one fixed \(\kappa_0>0\).
Moreover
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
 \le\frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal H_i)+o(1).
\]
Applying (Graph-energy-matrix-reduction) with \(b_N=b_0\), and adding its exponentially small norm exception, proves (Dominant-branch-reduction).

For the quadratic consequence, swap the two auxiliary column blocks of \(L_N\).
The result is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}I_N&W_1+W_2\\W_1+\sqrt2W_2&I_N\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose lower Schur complement is \(-Q_N\).
Thus \(L_N\) and \(Q_N\) are singular simultaneously.
When invertible, the lower-right inverse block is \(-Q_N^{-1}\), so
\(\|Q_N^{-1}\|\le\|L_N^{-1}\|\) and \(s_{\min}(Q_N)\ge s_{\min}(L_N)\).
The same inequality is trivial when both are singular.
This proves (Quadratic-reduction).

<a id="combined-probability-assembly"></a>
### The full-energy branch and the remaining minor regime

The [whole-layer energy and load conclusion](Yin_C_F0187_v6.md#statement) applies to the same original normalization and bounded laws.
Its [fresh-row interpretation](Yin_C_F0187_v6.md#fresh-row-scope) identifies that fourth layer with the actual conditional \(p_{4,i}\), because here there is exactly one physical deletion and the first off-diagonal law is Rademacher.
In particular, after excluding an unconditional uniform \(o(1)\) bad event, every invertible minor with \(r_{Z_i}\ge r_*\) satisfies
\[
 \sigma_{4,i}\ge c_2\|Z_i\|_{\rm F}^2/N^2,
 \qquad \tau_{4,i}\le t_N,
 \tag{High-rank-input}
\]
where a deterministic \(t_N\to0\), at least \(N^{-1/2}\), may be chosen slowly enough for both coefficient branches.
This statement uses the complete fourth layer, including its possible cross-versus-residual cancellation.
The bounded norm event and invertibility give
\(\|Z_i\|_{\rm F}^2\ge2(N-1)/b_0^2\ge1\) eventually, since \(\|M_i\|\le b_0\).
Thus the unconditional graph bound gives
\[
 \sup_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\det\Gamma_i(\varepsilon)}
 \le C_2\|Z_i\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
This bound is uniform over the entire remaining sign support, and uses the same deterministic boundary norm bound as before.

Let \(\mathcal G_i^{\rm high}\) impose invertibility, \(r_{Z_i}\ge r_*\), \(\|M_i\|\le b_0\), and (High-rank-input).
Let \(\mathcal G_i^{\rm dom}\) be the good dominant event constructed in the preceding subsection, with the same \(t_N\), additionally intersected with \(\|M_i\|\le b_0\).
Set \(\mathcal G_i:=\mathcal G_i^{\rm high}\cup\mathcal G_i^{\rm dom}\).
All these conditions are \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable: the norm condition is on the retained minor, not on the fresh-row-dependent full matrix.
The event that some minor violates this norm condition is contained in the fixed full-matrix norm exception, since every \(M_i\) is a principal submatrix of \(L_N\).
Give the high-effective-rank good event priority on the intersection, and define the positive scale on \(\mathcal G_i\) by
\[
 a_i:=
 \begin{cases}
 C_2\|Z_i\|_{\rm F}^2,&\text{on }\mathcal G_i^{\rm high},\\
 C_1|q_i|\mathcal R_i,&\text{on }\mathcal G_i^{\rm dom}\setminus\mathcal G_i^{\rm high}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{Piecewise-graph-scale}
\]
Assign any positive value outside \(\mathcal G_i\).
The energy, load and area conditions in (Graph-energy-conditions) hold with the smaller of two fixed positive energy constants.

 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i^c)
 \le\frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal J_i)+o(1).
\]
No conditioning on invertibility and no division by the probability of either coefficient branch occur here.
