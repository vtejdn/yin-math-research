# Normalized symmetrized odd-odd cofactor energy for three real Wigner factors

This project deduction fixes a coefficient estimate for the actual two-border cofactor graph of a three-real-factor product.
It gives an aggregate energy lower bound relative to the realized augmented-adjugate denominator on guards with arbitrary polynomial failure rates.
The denominator estimate depends on the second-smallest singular value of the shifted background, and extends to a background of corank one.
The complete argument has received [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v15.md#finding), adopted in the [proved decision](#decision-normalized-odd-odd-energy-reviewed).
No novelty or priority claim is made.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact deterministic and probabilistic statements

Let \(m\ge2\), let \(A_0,B_0,C_0\) be real symmetric \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrices with \(B_0,C_0\) invertible, and fix \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\).
Put
\[
 E=B_0^{-1},\qquad G=C_0^{-1},\qquad D=A_0-zGE.
\]
For arbitrary real \(b,c\) and \(x,y\in\mathbb R^m\), define
\[
 u=Ex,\quad v=Gy,\quad s_B=b-x^TEx,\quad s_C=c-y^TGy,
 \quad \ell=1+v^Tu,
\]
\[
 U=(Gu,v),\qquad V=(u,Ev),\qquad
 \mathsf S=\begin{pmatrix}s_B&0\\-\ell&s_C\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \mathcal K(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}D&zU\\V^T&\mathsf S\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Odd-energy-objects}
\]
For \(1\le j,k\le m\), let \(\mathcal K^{(k\mid j)}\) mean deletion of top-block row \(k\) and top-block column \(j\), retaining both augmented rows and columns, and define
\[
 \mathcal A_{jk}=(-1)^{j+k}\det\mathcal K^{(k\mid j)},\qquad
 \mathcal Q=(\mathcal A+\mathcal A^T)/2.
\]
Every entry is a polynomial of total border degree at most four.
All transposes in these polynomial definitions are ordinary transposes; all norms below are complex Hilbert-space operator or Frobenius norms.

For \(j<k\), define the matrix \(\mathscr B_{jk}\) to be the negative of the coefficient matrix of the monomials \(x_a y_c\) in \(\mathcal Q_{jk}\), and set
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm oo}^2=\sum_{j<k}\|\mathscr B_{jk}\|_F^2.
\]
The coefficient identity asserted here is
\[
 \Pi_{\rm oo}\mathcal Q_{jk}(x,y)=-x^T\mathscr B_{jk}y,\qquad
 \Pi_{\rm oo}f=\frac{f(x,y)-f(-x,y)-f(x,-y)+f(-x,-y)}4.
 \tag{Odd-energy-projection}
\]
In particular this entire odd-in-each-border part is bilinear, and \(\mathscr B_{jk}\) is independent of \(b,c\).
The definition and identity allow singular \(D\).

For the following deterministic estimates, assume only \(s_{m-1}(D)>0\), with singular values ordered decreasingly, so \(D\) can be invertible or have corank one.
Write
\[
 \lambda=s_{m-1}(D)^{-1},\qquad
 h=\|\mathsf S\|,\qquad p=\|U\|\,\|V\|,\qquad
 \mathcal K_\lambda=h^2+8|z|hp\lambda+7|z|^2p^2\lambda^2.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm oo}
 \ge
 \frac{|z|\sqrt{m-1}\,\|\operatorname{adj}D\|_F}
      {2\|B_0\|\,\|C_0\|\,\|D\|},
 \qquad
 \|\mathcal A(x,y)\|_F
 \le\sqrt m\,\|\operatorname{adj}D\|\,\mathcal K_\lambda.
 \tag{Odd-energy-deterministic-bounds}
\]
Consequently, when \(\mathcal A(x,y)\ne0\),
\[
 \frac{\mathcal E_{\rm oo}}{\|\mathcal A(x,y)\|_F}
 \ge
 \frac{|z|\sqrt{(m-1)/m}}
      {2\|B_0\|\,\|C_0\|\,\|D\|\,\mathcal K_\lambda}.
 \tag{Odd-energy-realized-normalization}
\]
There is no least-singular-value lower assumption on \(D\), no fixed bound on an original input norm, and no assumed lower bound for a background symmetric cofactor edge.
The quantities \(h,p,\mathcal A\) retain their actual border dependence.
The estimates also make sense without \(s_Bs_C\ne0\); the following graph identification uses that extra event.

Let
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}b&x^T\\x&B_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C=\begin{pmatrix}c&y^T\\y&C_0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
When \(B,C\) are invertible, let \(D_i\) be the principal block \(A_0-z(C^{-1}B^{-1})_{\widehat i,\widehat i}\), where the omitted coordinate is the first displayed one.
Then
\[
 \mathcal A=s_Bs_C\operatorname{adj}D_i,\qquad
 \frac{|\mathcal Q_{jk}(x,y)|}{\|\mathcal A(x,y)\|_F}
 =
 \frac{|(\operatorname{sym}\operatorname{adj}D_i)_{jk}|}
      {\|\operatorname{adj}D_i\|_F}
 \quad\text{if }\operatorname{adj}D_i\ne0.
 \tag{Odd-energy-actual-graph}
\]

For the probability assertion, for each \(N\), let \(W_1,W_2,W_3\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
Input \(r\) has independent strict upper entries with fixed real law \(\xi_r/\sqrt N\), where
\(\mathbb E\xi_r=0\) and \(\mathbb E\xi_r^2=1\), and independent real diagonal entries with fixed law \(\zeta_r/\sqrt N\), where \(\mathbb E\zeta_r^2<\infty\).
The diagonal family is independent of the off-diagonal family.
No moment above two, boundedness, density, or non-atomicity is required.
Cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
For every omitted index \(i\), use the actual principal blocks
\(A_0=(W_1)_{\widehat i,\widehat i}\), \(B_0=(W_2)_{\widehat i,\widehat i}\), \(C_0=(W_3)_{\widehat i,\widehat i}\), and the actual second and third borders \(x_i,y_i,b_i,c_i\), with \(m=N-1\) and the original \(\sqrt N\) normalization.

For every requested \(B>0\), there are fixed \(L,A_2\ge1\), \(C,N_0\), depending only on the three input laws, \(z,B\), and events \(\Omega_N\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P(\Omega_N^c)\le C N^{-B},\qquad N\ge N_0.
\]
On \(\Omega_N\), both full factors \(W_2,W_3\) and all their one-index principal minors are invertible.
For every omitted index the basic bounds
\[
 \max\{\|A_0\|,\|B_0\|,\|C_0\|,\|E\|,\|G\|,
            |b_i|,|c_i|,\|x_i\|,\|y_i\|\}\le N^L,\qquad
 s_{m-1}(D)\ge N^{-A_2}
 \tag{Odd-energy-probability-guards}
\]
hold simultaneously.
For a constant \(c_z>0\) and every such index, these imply
\[
 \mathcal E_{{\rm oo},i}
 \ge c_zN^{-16L-2A_2}\|\mathcal A_i(x_i,y_i)\|_F.
 \tag{Odd-energy-formal-probability-conclusion}
\]
The coefficient energy is a function of the actual principal backgrounds, whereas the denominator is evaluated at the actual fresh borders.
For general real laws this is a formal monomial-coefficient estimate, not a claim about an orthogonal chaos or its variance.

If the second and third off-diagonal laws are Rademacher, keep the first-input law and all diagonal laws at their preceding scope.
Conditioning on the three principal backgrounds and the two exposed diagonal entries makes \(x_i=\varepsilon/\sqrt N\), \(y_i=\eta/\sqrt N\) independent normalized fair signs.
For their mixed-bilinear Walsh coefficients,
\[
 \sigma_{{\rm oo},i}^2
 :=\sum_{j<k}\sum_{a,c=1}^m
       |\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{i,jk}(\varepsilon_a\eta_c)|^2
 =\mathcal E_{{\rm oo},i}^2/N^2,
\]
and on the same events
\[
 \sigma_{{\rm oo},i}
 \ge c_zN^{-16L-2A_2-1}\|\mathcal A_i(x_i,y_i)\|_F.
 \tag{Odd-energy-Walsh-probability-conclusion}
\]
The event \(\Omega_N\) is retained as an event, not additional conditioning that would preserve the fresh-border law.
Choosing \(B>1\) makes these simultaneous guards and coefficient comparisons eventually valid almost surely under every cross-size coupling.
This conclusion concerns coefficient comparisons only.

No small-ball bound, matching or load bound, least-singular-value floor for the full product, planar-defect estimate, or Brown convergence assertion is included.
The probability statement covers real inputs at each fixed nonzero \(z\); it is not a theorem for general complex Hermitian laws or a uniform assertion over the spectral plane.

## Complete argument

<a id="actual-border-algebra"></a>
### The actual inverse-product minor and its augmented adjugate

When \(s_Bs_C\ne0\), put \(\beta=s_B^{-1}\) and \(\alpha=s_C^{-1}\).
Direct block multiplication verifies
\[
 B^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\beta&-\beta u^T\\-\beta u&E+\beta uu^T\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 C^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-\alpha v^T\\-\alpha v&G+\alpha vv^T\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The actual product order is therefore
\[
 (C^{-1}B^{-1})_{\widehat i,\widehat i}
 =GE+\beta Guu^T+\alpha vv^TE+\alpha\beta\ell\,vu^T.
 \tag{Fixed-fresh-product-block}
\]
Set
\[
 \mathsf R=\begin{pmatrix}\beta&0\\\alpha\beta\ell&\alpha\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\mathsf R^{-1}=\mathsf S\) and
\[
 D_i=D-zU\mathsf RV^T.
\]
Taking the lower-corner Schur complement of \(\mathcal K\) gives
\(\det\mathcal K=s_Bs_C\det D_i\).
The same calculation after deletion of top-block row \(k\) and column \(j\) gives
\[
 \mathcal A_{jk}=s_Bs_C(\operatorname{adj}D_i)_{jk}.
 \tag{Fixed-fresh-adjugate}
\]
This deleted-determinant identity does not require \(D_i\) to be invertible.
Symmetrization and cancellation of the same nonzero scalar \(s_Bs_C\) prove the actual normalized graph identity
\[
 \frac{|\mathcal Q_{jk}(x,y)|}{\|\mathcal A(x,y)\|_F}
 =
 \frac{|(\operatorname{sym}\operatorname{adj}D_i)_{jk}|}
      {\|\operatorname{adj}D_i\|_F}.
 \tag{Fresh-graph}
\]

The upper-right and lower-left entries of \(\mathcal K\) have border degree at most one, its lower corner has degree at most two, and its upper-left block has degree zero.
In a determinant term selecting \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\) augmented columns from top rows, exactly \(k\) augmented rows select top columns.
Its total border degree is at most \(k+k+2(2-k)=4\).
The same count applies to every deleted cofactor.
This proves the degree assertion directly from the original borders.

<a id="odd-odd-coefficient"></a>
### The exact odd-odd layer and its adjugate derivative

For an index \(a\), let \(J_a:\mathbb C^m\to\mathbb C^{m-1}\) delete coordinate \(a\), retaining the other coordinates in increasing order.
Fix \(j,k\), initially on the branch where
\[
 T=J_k D J_j^T
\]
is invertible, and define
\[
 Z=J_j^T T^{-1}J_k,\quad
 \delta=(-1)^{j+k}\det T,\quad R_{\rm loc}=zEZG,
\]
\[
 P=(I+R_{\rm loc})E,\qquad
 Q=G(I+R_{\rm loc}),\qquad L=EG+ER_{\rm loc}^TG.
\]
Taking the top-left Schur complement in the deleted augmented matrix gives the two-by-two matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 b-x^TPx&-x^TR_{\rm loc}y\\
 -1-x^TLy&c-y^TQy
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For example, its lower-left correction is
\[
 -1-v^Tu-zv^TEZG u
 =-1-x^T(EG+zEGZ^TEG)y,
\]
and \(ER_{\rm loc}^TG=zEGZ^TEG\).
The other entries follow from the same multiplication, using \(E^T=E\) and \(G^T=G\).
Consequently
\[
 \mathcal A_{jk}
 =\delta\left[(b-x^TPx)(c-y^TQy)
          -(x^TR_{\rm loc}y)(1+x^TLy)\right].
 \tag{Fixed-cofactor-Schur}
\]
All terms except \(-\delta x^TR_{\rm loc}y\) are even in each border separately.
Thus
\[
 \Pi_{\rm oo}\mathcal A_{jk}
 =-z x^TE\Omega_{jk}G y,\qquad
 \Omega_{jk}=(-1)^{j+k}J_j^T\operatorname{adj}(T)J_k.
 \tag{Fixed-cofactor-odd-odd}
\]
This last identity is polynomial in the entries of \(D\), and hence extends from the dense branch of invertible \(T\) to every \(D\).
Only this adjugate layer identity is extended; no uncancelled product of the local inverse formulas is being used at singular \(T\).
It proves the stated bilinear odd-odd projection and its independence of \(b,c\).

Now assume \(D\) is invertible and write \(R=D^{-1}\), \(d=\det D\).
For any matrix perturbation \(\Delta\), differentiating the deleted determinant gives
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}(\operatorname{adj}(D+t\Delta))_{jk}\right|_{t=0}
 =\operatorname{tr}(\Omega_{jk}\Delta).
\]
On the other hand, differentiating \(\operatorname{adj}D=(\det D)D^{-1}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}(\operatorname{adj}(D+t\Delta))_{jk}\right|_{t=0}
 &=d\left[R_{jk}\operatorname{tr}(R\Delta)-(R\Delta R)_{jk}\right]\\
 &=\operatorname{tr}\left(d[R_{jk}R-Re_ke_j^TR]\Delta\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Equality for all \(\Delta\) yields
\[
 \Omega_{jk}=d[R_{jk}R-Re_ke_j^TR].
\]
For \(j<k\), set
\[
 a_{jk}=(R_{jk}+R_{kj})/2,\qquad
 H_{jk}=e_je_k^T+e_ke_j^T,\qquad
 T_{jk}=a_{jk}R-\tfrac12RH_{jk}R.
\]
Taking the actual half-sum of the two cofactors now gives
\[
 \mathscr B_{jk}=\frac z2 E(\Omega_{jk}+\Omega_{kj})G
               =zd\,ET_{jk}G.
 \tag{Fixed-sym-odd-coefficient}
\]
It is the coefficient of the actual symmetrized graph, rather than a single unsymmetrized cofactor.
The remaining deterministic inequalities are first proved on this invertible branch and subsequently extended in their cancelled form.

<a id="aggregate-energy"></a>
### An aggregate identity removes the need to select a large background edge

Multiplication on the right by \(D\) gives
\[
 T_{jk}D=a_{jk}I-\frac12RH_{jk}.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{tr}(RH_{jk})=2a_{jk}\), the Frobenius cross term is
\(-\operatorname{Re}(\overline{a_{jk}}\operatorname{tr}(RH_{jk}))=-2|a_{jk}|^2\).
The two nonzero columns of \(RH_{jk}\) are \(Re_k\) and \(Re_j\).
Consequently
\[
 \|T_{jk}D\|_F^2
 =(m-2)|a_{jk}|^2
   +\frac14\bigl(\|Re_j\|^2+\|Re_k\|^2\bigr),
 \tag{Normalized-odd-aggregate-identity}
\]
including for complex \(R\).
Summing over all unordered pairs and using \(m\ge2\) proves
\[
 \sum_{j<k}\|T_{jk}\|_F^2
 \ge\frac{1}{\|D\|^2}
       \sum_{j<k}\|T_{jk}D\|_F^2
 \ge\frac{(m-1)\|R\|_F^2}{4\|D\|^2}.
 \tag{Normalized-odd-aggregate-lower}
\]
In particular the rank-two remainder cannot cancel the entire aggregate energy, even if every background symmetric cofactor edge \(d a_{jk}\) vanishes.

For any complex matrix \(T\),
\[
 \|ETG\|_F
 \ge \frac{\|T\|_F}{\|B_0\|\,\|C_0\|},
\]
because \(T=B_0(ETG)C_0\).
Since \(|d|\|R\|_F=\|\operatorname{adj}D\|_F\), this yields
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm oo}
 \ge
 \frac{|z|\sqrt{m-1}\,\|\operatorname{adj}D\|_F}
      {2\|B_0\|\,\|C_0\|\,\|D\|}.
 \tag{Normalized-odd-adjugate-lower}
\]
This is an aggregate coefficient estimate, not a lower bound for every edge or a statement about the value of \(\mathcal Q(x,y)\) at a random border.

<a id="rank-two-upper"></a>
### The exact rank-two adjugate expansion loses only the second inverse singular value

Use the original border quantities
\[
 u=Ex,\quad v=Gy,\quad
 s_B=b-x^TEx,\quad s_C=c-y^TGy,\quad \ell=1+v^Tu,
\]
\[
 U=(Gu,v),\quad V=(u,Ev),\quad
 \mathsf S=\begin{pmatrix}s_B&0\\-\ell&s_C\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix \(\mathsf S\) is the lower corner in (Odd-energy-objects); on the full-factor invertible branch it equals \(\mathsf R^{-1}\) from the actual border calculation.
On the current \(D\)-invertible branch put
\[
 q=s_1(R)=\|R\|,\quad \lambda=s_2(R),\quad
 p=\|U\|\,\|V\|,\quad h=\|\mathsf S\|.
\]
Here singular values of \(R\) are ordered decreasingly.
Write
\[
 \mathsf C=V^TRU,\qquad \mathsf H=\mathsf S-z\mathsf C.
\]
Taking the top-left block of the adjugate of
\(\begin{pmatrix}D&zU\\ V^T&\mathsf S\end{pmatrix}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal A
 =d\left[\det(\mathsf H)R+
               zRU\operatorname{adj}(\mathsf H)V^TR\right].
 \tag{Normalized-odd-full-adjugate}
\]
One can first compute with \(\mathsf H\) invertible.
Both sides are polynomial in its two-by-two entries, so the formula then holds for every \(\mathsf H\), including when the actual minor is singular.
It does not use an inverse bound for \(s_B\), \(s_C\), or the actual minor.

For a two-by-two matrix, the adjugate is linear and
\[
 \det(\mathsf S-z\mathsf C)
 =\det\mathsf S-z\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(\mathsf S)\mathsf C)
                   +z^2\det\mathsf C.
\]
Hence
\[
 \mathcal A/d=\det(\mathsf S)R+z\Phi_2(R)+z^2\Phi_3(R),
\]
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_2(R)
 &=RU\operatorname{adj}(\mathsf S)V^TR
    -\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(\mathsf S)V^TRU)R,\\
 \Phi_3(R)
 &=\det(V^TRU)R-RU\operatorname{adj}(V^TRU)V^TR.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Normalized-odd-update-layers}
\]
The apparent powers \(q^2\) and \(q^3\) in these expressions cancel.

Here is a complete operator-norm calculation of that cancellation.
Choose a singular-value decomposition
\[
 R=K+F,\qquad K=q\,\alpha\beta^*,\qquad
 \|\alpha\|=\|\beta\|=1,\quad \|F\|=\lambda\le q.
\]
The star occurs only in this Hilbert-space decomposition; no polynomial transpose has been replaced.
Set \(M=U\operatorname{adj}(\mathsf S)V^T\), so \(\operatorname{rank}M\le2\) and \(\|M\|\le hp\).
The identities \(\|\operatorname{adj}(\mathsf S)\|=\|\mathsf S\|\) for two-by-two matrices and \(\|V^T\|=\|V\|\) also hold over \(\mathbb C\).
Because \(KMK=\operatorname{tr}(MK)K\), the quadratic part of \(\Phi_2(K+F)\) in \(K\) vanishes.
Its remaining terms are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_2(K+F)={}&KMF+FMK-\operatorname{tr}(MK)F
                         -\operatorname{tr}(MF)K\\
              &+FMF-\operatorname{tr}(MF)F.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Use \(|\operatorname{tr}(MK)|\le q\|M\|\) and
\(|\operatorname{tr}(MF)|\le2\lambda\|M\|\), the latter following from the rank-two nuclear-norm bound.
The first four terms have total norm at most \(5q\lambda hp\), and the last two at most \(3\lambda^2hp\).
Thus
\[
 \|\Phi_2(R)\|\le8q\lambda hp.
 \tag{Normalized-odd-quadratic-bound}
\]

For the cubic part put
\[
 A=V^TKU,\quad C=V^TFU,\quad
 t=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(C)A).
\]
The matrix \(A\) has rank at most one, \(\|A\|\le qp\), and \(\|C\|\le\lambda p\).
The rank-one determinant identity and the linearity of the two-by-two adjugate give
\[
 \det(A+C)=\det C+t,\qquad
 \operatorname{adj}(A+C)=\operatorname{adj}A+\operatorname{adj}C.
\]
Writing \(A=q(V^T\alpha)(\beta^*U)\) shows that
\[
 \operatorname{adj}(A)V^T\alpha=0,\qquad
 (\beta^*U)\operatorname{adj}(A)=0,
 \qquad KU\operatorname{adj}(C)V^TK=tK.
\]
The last equality cancels the remaining term quadratic in \(K\).
The exact remainder is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_3(K+F)={}&\det(C)(K+F)+tF\\
 &-KU\operatorname{adj}(C)V^TF
  -FU\operatorname{adj}(C)V^TK\\
 &-FU\operatorname{adj}(C)V^TF
  -FU\operatorname{adj}(A)V^TF.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Normalized-odd-cubic-remainder}
\]
In addition to \(|\det C|\le\lambda^2p^2\), the rank-one representation of \(A\) gives
\(|t|\le q\lambda p^2\).
Five displayed contributions are bounded by \(q\lambda^2p^2\) each, and two by \(\lambda^3p^2\) each.
Consequently
\[
 \|\Phi_3(R)\|\le7q\lambda^2p^2.
 \tag{Normalized-odd-cubic-bound}
\]
This calculation also covers zero or rank-deficient \(U,V,A,C\).

Combining the three layers and using \(|d|q=\|\operatorname{adj}D\|\) proves
\[
 \|\mathcal A(x,y)\|_F
 \le\sqrt m\,\|\operatorname{adj}D\|\,\mathcal K_\lambda(x,y),
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_\lambda=h^2+8|z|hp\lambda+7|z|^2p^2\lambda^2.
 \tag{Normalized-odd-full-upper}
\]
Only \(\lambda=1/s_{m-1}(D)\), the reciprocal of the second-smallest singular value of \(D\), occurs in \(\mathcal K_\lambda\).
No factor \(1/s_m(D)\) remains.
The border dependence of \(h,p\), and therefore of this bound, has been retained.

<a id="corank-one-normalization"></a>
### Corank-one extension and the actual normalized coefficient bound

Assume now only \(s_{m-1}(D)>0\), and set \(\lambda=1/s_{m-1}(D)\).
The matrix \(D\) may have corank one.
The coefficients \(\mathscr B_{jk}\), the augmented cofactor \(\mathcal A\), and \(\operatorname{adj}D\) are continuous polynomial functions of \(D\) with \(E,G,U,V,\mathsf S\) fixed.
Approximate \(D\) by invertible matrices.
On such an approximating sequence, \(s_{m-1}(D)\) stays positive, \(\|D\|>0\) stays bounded away from zero, and \(\lambda\) converges.
Therefore (Normalized-odd-adjugate-lower) and (Normalized-odd-full-upper), in the forms already free of \(dR\) or \(dR^2\), extend to this corank-one branch.
This is a continuity argument for explicit finite-dimensional polynomial inequalities, not a claim that a singular event has small probability.
It does not extend uncancelled inverse expressions across a singular matrix.

On the branch \(\mathcal A(x,y)\ne0\), division gives the deterministic estimate
\[
 \frac{\mathcal E_{\rm oo}}{\|\mathcal A(x,y)\|_F}
 \ge
 \frac{|z|\sqrt{(m-1)/m}}
 {2\|B_0\|\,\|C_0\|\,\|D\|\,\mathcal K_\lambda(x,y)}.
 \tag{Normalized-odd-ratio}
\]
Indeed the stronger intermediate right-hand side has the additional factor
\(\|\operatorname{adj}D\|_F/\|\operatorname{adj}D\|\ge1\).
If \(\mathcal K_\lambda=0\), the upper bound forces \(\mathcal A=0\), so no division by zero occurs on the stated branch.
Equivalently, the bound can always be stated as a coefficient-energy lower comparison with \(\|\mathcal A(x,y)\|_F\), treating the zero-adjugate case without a ratio.
The two-factor full invertibility guards are needed only when identifying this denominator with the original minor's normalized graph through (Fresh-graph).

For normalized Rademacher borders \(x=\varepsilon/\sqrt N\), \(y=\eta/\sqrt N\), define
\[
 \sigma_{\rm oo}^2
 =\sum_{j<k}\sum_{a,c=1}^m
       |\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{jk}(\varepsilon_a\eta_c)|^2
 =\frac{\mathcal E_{\rm oo}^2}{N^2}.
 \tag{Normalized-odd-Walsh-energy}
\]
The exact coefficient equality follows from the preceding odd-odd identity; the other Walsh layers cannot cancel these mixed-bilinear coefficients.
Thus (Normalized-odd-ratio) divided by \(N\) compares this deterministic conditional Walsh energy with the actual random denominator at every border realization in the specified guard.
For a general real law the same deterministic monomial-coefficient estimate holds, but this calculation does not identify it with an orthogonal Walsh energy or a variance.
No symmetry, third moment, or fourth moment of a general law is silently imposed.

<a id="probability-guards"></a>
### All required polynomial guards have summable exceptional probabilities

The probability specialization uses three independent real Wigner inputs with fixed centered variance-one off-diagonal laws and fixed finite-second-moment real diagonal laws, normalized by the original \(\sqrt N\).
It applies to each fixed \(z\ne0\).
It does not cover arbitrary complex Hermitian input laws or establish a uniform theorem over the spectral plane.
