# Positive-frame physical rectangles and block-distance averaging

This theorem extends the scalar [all-small-support rectangle mechanism](Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#rectangles) to fixed matrix coefficients with a positive frame.
It addresses a separate probability interface for the [matrix-pencil Task](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract), without assuming an identity noise coefficient or a path representation.
The proof was developed for Yin through ordinary reasoning and authoring self-checks.
The exact statement and proof are adopted by the [proved rectangle decision at the family entrance](Yin_C_F0194_v18.md#decision-positive-frame-rectangles-reviewed), with [independent whole-proof inspection](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v18.md#positive-frame-rectangles-whole-proof).
This physical version retains its initial candidate decision without rewriting that earlier assessment.
The earlier fixed three-channel floor remains at [C194v16](Yin_C_F0194_v16.md#decision-three-channel-sixth-bridge-reviewed); the present theorem neither extends its Schur-polynomial anti-concentration nor supplies a new least-value floor.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

Fix integers \(m,d\ge1\) and matrices \(A_1,\ldots,A_d\in M_m(\mathbb C)\), independently of \(N\), with
\[
 \chi_A:=\inf_{\|u\|=\|v\|=1}\sum_{j=1}^d|u^*A_jv|^2>0.
 \tag{Rectangle-frame}
\]
The unit vectors in this definition are complex.
For every \(j\), let \(W_j^{(N)}\) be a Hermitian Wigner matrix with strict upper entries independently distributed as \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\xi_j\) is fixed, complex, centered and has \(\mathbb E|\xi_j|^2=1\).
Its independent diagonal entries have a fixed centered real law of finite second moment and the same normalization.
All upper-triangular coordinates and inputs are independent within each size; cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
No density, higher moment or independence of the real and imaginary parts is assumed.
The \(A_j\) need not be selfadjoint.
Let \(F_N\in M_{mN}(\mathbb C)\) be deterministic, or independent of all the Wigner inputs, with no norm bound, and put
\[
 M_N=F_N+\sum_{j=1}^d A_j\otimes W_j^{(N)}.
\]
Coordinates are henceforth ordered in \(N\) physical blocks of size \(m\).
For disjoint physical sets \(I,J\), the notation \(M_N[I,J]\) retains all auxiliary coordinates of each selected physical index.

There are fixed \(\delta\in(0,1/4]\), \(a,c,C>0\) and \(N_0\), depending only on \(m,d\), the noise coefficients and their off-diagonal laws, such that
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\exists J\subset[N],\ |J|=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor:
 s_{\min}\bigl(M_N[[N]\setminus J,J]\bigr)<\frac a{\sqrt N}\right\}
 \le Ce^{-cN},\qquad N\ge N_0.
 \tag{Positive-frame-rectangles}
\]
The constants are uniform over \(F_N\) and the diagonal laws.
For an independent random \(F_N\), the same bound holds conditionally on its entire value and hence unconditionally.
The assertion is simultaneous over the displayed supports for each specified shift; it does not assert one event that works for every shift chosen after observing the Wigner inputs.

Let \(\mathcal R_N\) be the complementary good event in (Positive-frame-rectangles).
For every deterministic \(b_N\ge1\), on \(\mathcal R_N\cap\{\|M_N\|\le b_N\}\), every unit vector \(w=(w_i)_{i=1}^N\in\bigoplus_i\mathbb C^m\) with
\(\|M_Nw\|\le a/(4\sqrt N)\) has more than \(\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\) indices satisfying
\[
 \|w_i\|>\frac a{8b_NN}.
 \tag{Positive-frame-block-spread}
\]
Let \(C_i\) be the \(m\) columns of \(M_N\) at physical index \(i\), let \(Q_i\) project orthogonally to the span of all its other columns, and define
\[
 d_i:=s_{\min}(Q_iC_i).
\]
These objects are defined even for singular matrices.
For every deterministic \(0\le u_N\le a/(4\sqrt N)\), one has
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\mathbb P\{\|M_N\|\le b_N,\ s_{\min}(M_N)\le u_N\}\\
 &\quad\le Ce^{-cN}
 +\frac2{\delta N}\sum_{i=1}^N
   \mathbb P\left\{d_i\le\frac{8b_NNu_N}{a},\ \|M_N\|\le b_N\right\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Positive-frame-distance-average}
\]
No inverse, corank restriction, selfadjointness of \(M_N\), or independence between the distance events is required.
For the intended selfadjoint positive-frame pencils, take \(F_N=A_0\otimes I_N+\theta_NI_{mN}\) with fixed \(A_0=A_0^*\) and any deterministic real scalar \(\theta_N\).
The rectangle itself does not depend on either term of this special shift.
The real symmetric size-three positive-frame subclass is included, but the rectangle and averaging statements have the full complex scope above.
The earlier diagonal noise tuple \(I_3,T\) has zero frame and retains its separate path-specific rectangle argument.
They do not control any \(d_i\) probability by themselves and do not prove a determinant limit or complete F0013.

<a id="inputs"></a>
## Inputs and reuse boundary

The only external probability input is the accepted bounded-product convex concentration inequality (9) in [C187v2](Yin_C_F0187_v2.md#argument), valid for independent bounded real Euclidean coordinate blocks.
Complex scalar coordinates are regarded as real two-dimensional blocks; their internal real and imaginary parts are never separated.
The inspected original-source scope and its unreviewed source-proof boundary remain at that fixed input.
The fixed-cutoff shift-absorption argument of [C194v3](Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#linear-codimension), the auxiliary covariance and fixed-dimensional net of [C187v3](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#uniform-block-direction), and its [block Gram identity](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#block-negative-second-moment) supply the existing mechanisms.
The new argument below combines them for whole physical column blocks, absorbs an entire deterministic block-map range, and proves the uniform support union and averaging explicitly.
It does not invoke C187v3's middle singular-value conclusion or C194v16's special identity-noise rectangles as if they already covered this statement.
The constant-frame lower bound is used in the column orientation \(\sum_jA_jvv^*A_j^*\), whose applicability is checked below.

## Proof

<a id="block-column-distance"></a>
### Fixed-cutoff distance of every direction in a physical column

Consider \(p\) independent physical blocks of a random \(mp\)-by-\(m\) matrix \(X\), whose \(i\)-th block is
\[
 X_i=\sum_{j=1}^d A_j\omega_{j,i}.
\]
All \(\omega_{j,i}\) are independent, and each has the law of \(\xi_j\) or \(\overline{\xi_j}\), with arbitrary deterministic orientations.
Let \(H\subset\mathbb C^{mp}\) be a deterministic complex subspace of codimension at least \(mp/2\), and let \(B\) be any deterministic \(mp\)-by-\(m\) matrix.
We prove, uniformly in \(H,B\) and all orientations, that
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\inf_{\|v\|=1}\operatorname{dist}((X+B)v,H)<c_0\sqrt p\right\}
 \le C_0e^{-c_0'p}
 \tag{Block-column-distance}
\]
for sufficiently large \(p\).
The result also applies conditionally when \(H,B\) are independent of this fresh family.

Choose a fixed \(R\ge1\) so large that
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^d\mathbb P\{|\xi_j|>R\}\le1/64,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi_j\mid |\xi_j|\le R)\ge1/2
 \quad(1\le j\le d),
\]
where the variance of a complex scalar is its centered squared modulus.
Finite second moments ensure both conditions; conjugation preserves them.
Let \(G\) consist of the physical indices at which all \(d\) amplitudes are at most \(R\).
Independence across physical indices and exponential Markov at parameter one give
\[
 \mathbb P\{|G^c|>p/8\}
 \le e^{-p/8}\bigl(1+(e-1)/64\bigr)^p
 \le e^{-c_1p}.
 \tag{Block-cutoff-count}
\]
Condition on a particular \(G\) satisfying \(|G^c|\le p/8\).
All retained scalar coordinates remain independent, with the corresponding original law conditioned on its own modulus cutoff.
Write \(X_G=\overline X_G+X_G^0\), where \(\overline X_G\) is its conditional mean and \(X_G^0\) is centered.
Let \(H_G\) be the coordinate projection of \(H\) onto the \(m|G|\) retained coordinates and set
\[
 H_G':=H_G+\operatorname{ran}(B_G+\overline X_G).
\]
The added range has complex dimension at most \(m\), regardless of the size of the shift.
Therefore, for \(p\ge8\),
\[
 \operatorname{codim}H_G'
 \ge mp/2-m|G^c|-m\ge mp/4.
 \tag{Block-range-codimension}
\]
Let \(P_G\) be the orthogonal projection onto \((H_G')^\perp\).
Coordinate contraction and the added range imply simultaneously for every \(v\) that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}((X+B)v,H)
 \ge\operatorname{dist}((X_G+B_G)v,H_G')
 =\|P_GX_G^0v\|.
 \tag{Block-range-absorption}
\]
In particular the large deterministic shift has disappeared before the auxiliary net is formed.
The projection depends only on the conditioned cutoff set and deterministic data, not on the values of the retained centered variables.

For any fixed unit \(v\in\mathbb C^m\), the centered vector \(X_G^0v\) has block-diagonal covariance, with the block at physical index \(i\) equal to
\[
 T_{v,i}=\sum_j\operatorname{Var}(\omega_{j,i}\mid |\omega_{j,i}|\le R)
                   A_jvv^*A_j^*\succeq(\chi_A/2)I_m.
\]
Indeed testing this inequality against \(u\) gives the sum in (Rectangle-frame).
Independence and centering eliminate the cross terms; no pseudo-variance hypothesis is used.
With \(Y(v)=\|P_GX_G^0v\|\), this gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Y(v)^2\mid G]
 \ge(\chi_A/2)\operatorname{Tr}P_G\ge\chi_Amp/8.
 \tag{Column-frame-covariance}
\]
As a function of the independent bounded complex scalar blocks, \(Y(v)\) is convex and \(M_A\)-Lipschitz, where
\(M_A=(\sum_j\|A_j\|^2)^{1/2}\).
The support diameters are at most \(2R\).
The accepted concentration inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{|Y(v)-\operatorname{med}Y(v)|\ge t\mid G\}
 \le C\exp[-ct^2/(M_A^2R^2)].
\]
Integration bounds \(\mathbb E[(Y(v)-\operatorname{med}Y(v))^2\mid G]\) by \(CM_A^2R^2\).
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality and (Column-frame-covariance) then give constants \(c_2,c_3>0\) with
\[
 \operatorname{med}Y(v)\ge2c_2\sqrt p,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P\{Y(v)<c_2\sqrt p\mid G\}\le Ce^{-c_3p}
\]
for sufficiently large \(p\), uniformly in all conditioned data and \(v\).

On the entire retained-coordinate support, the deterministic bound
\[
 \|X_G^0\|\le K_R\sqrt p,
 \qquad K_R:=2R\sum_j\|A_j\|
\]
holds, because each centered scalar has modulus at most \(2R\).
Choose a fixed \(\varepsilon\)-net of the unit sphere in \(\mathbb C^m\), where
\(\varepsilon=\min(1/2,c_2/(2K_R))\).
It has at most \((1+2/\varepsilon)^{2m}\) points.
A union over this fixed number of points, followed by the deterministic extension bound \(K_R\sqrt p\,\varepsilon\), proves
\[
 \inf_{\|v\|=1}Y(v)\ge(c_2/2)\sqrt p
\]
outside \(Ce^{-c_3p}\), conditionally on every allowed \(G\).
Integrating over \(G\), adding (Block-cutoff-count), and applying (Block-range-absorption) prove (Block-column-distance).
No full-column norm event or inverse-dependent condition was inserted into the fresh law.

<a id="all-support-rectangles"></a>
### From independent column blocks to every small physical support

First condition on \(F_N\) if it is random.
Fix any \(\delta\le1/4\), let \(k=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\ge1\), choose a physical set \(J\) of size \(k\), and put \(I=[N]\setminus J\), \(p=N-k\).
Consider the raw rectangle
\[
 \mathsf B:=\sqrt N\,M_N[I,J]\in M_{mp,mk}(\mathbb C).
\]
Its \(k\) physical column blocks are independent.
Every random coordinate is an off-diagonal entry crossing disjoint sets, so no mirrored occurrence appears elsewhere in this rectangle, and all input families are independent.
Each column block has exactly the form \(X+B\) above, with the inherited conjugation orientations and its deterministic shift.

Conditional on the other \(k-1\) column blocks, their span \(H\) has codimension at least
\[
 mp-m(k-1)=m(p-k+1)\ge mp/2.
\]
Apply (Block-column-distance) to that fresh block.
Except with probability \(CN e^{-c_4N}\), all block distance Grams satisfy
\[
 G_j:=\mathsf B_j^*P_{H_j^\perp}\mathsf B_j\succeq c_5N I_m.
 \tag{Rectangle-block-Grams}
\]
Here the constants can be chosen uniformly over \(\delta\le1/4\), all supports and all shifts.
On this event \(\mathsf B\) has full column rank: a nonzero column relation would place a nonzero combination from one block in the span of the other blocks, contradicting its Gram lower bound.
The block Schur-complement identity for the positive definite Gram matrix gives
\[
 ((\mathsf B^*\mathsf B)^{-1})_{jj}=G_j^{-1},
 \qquad
 \sum_{r=1}^{mk}s_r(\mathsf B)^{-2}
 =\sum_{j=1}^k\operatorname{Tr}G_j^{-1}
 \le\frac{mk}{c_5N}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 s_{\min}(M_N[I,J])
 =N^{-1/2}s_{\min}(\mathsf B)
 \ge\sqrt{\frac{c_5}{mk}}\ge\frac{a_0}{\sqrt N}
\]
for a fixed \(a_0>0\).
This reproduces the block Gram identity directly; it does not treat the correlated auxiliary columns as independent scalars.

Choose \(\delta>0\) small enough that \(2\delta\log(e/\delta)<c_4/4\).
For large \(N\), the number of supports is at most \(\exp(2\delta N\log(e/\delta))\).
The union over supports absorbs both this factor and the preceding polynomial factor into \(Ce^{-cN}\).
Decreasing \(a_0\) to \(a\) if necessary proves (Positive-frame-rectangles).
All bounds were uniform in the conditioned shift, so integration also proves the independent-random-shift statement.
The finitely many rectangle singular-value conditions define the measurable good event \(\mathcal R_N\).

<a id="physical-spread-and-average"></a>
### Coarse block spread and the exact distance average

Work on \(\mathcal R_N\cap\{\|M_N\|\le b_N\}\), and suppose a unit vector \(w\) with the stated small residual has at most \(k\) physical blocks exceeding \(h_N=a/(8b_NN)\).
Choose \(J\) of size \(k\) containing them and put \(I=[N]\setminus J\).
Then
\[
 \|w_I\|\le\sqrt N h_N=\frac a{8b_N\sqrt N},
 \qquad \|w_J\|\ge\tfrac12
\]
for large \(N\).
The rectangle bound and coordinate contraction give
\[
 \|(M_Nw)_I\|
 \ge\frac a{\sqrt N}\|w_J\|-\|M_N[I,I]\|\|w_I\|
 \ge\frac{3a}{8\sqrt N},
\]
contradicting \(\|M_Nw\|\le a/(4\sqrt N)\).
This proves (Positive-frame-block-spread) for every such vector on the same event.

For any physical index with \(w_i\ne0\), the exact identity
\[
 Q_iM_Nw=Q_iC_iw_i
\]
implies \(d_i\le\|M_Nw\|/\|w_i\|\).
On the parent least-value event, choose a complex unit least singular vector; its existence is a finite-dimensional fact and no measurable choice is needed for the resulting pointwise indicator inequality.
The preceding spread gives at least \(\delta N/2\) indices with
\(d_i\le8b_NNu_N/a\), for sufficiently large \(N\).
Thus
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbf1_{\{\|M_N\|\le b_N,\ s_{\min}(M_N)\le u_N\}}
 &\le\mathbf1_{\mathcal R_N^c}\\
 &\quad+\frac2{\delta N}\sum_i
 \mathbf1_{\{d_i\le8b_NNu_N/a,\ \|M_N\|\le b_N\}}.
 \end{split}
\]
Taking expectations proves (Positive-frame-distance-average), including \(u_N=0\) and every possible corank.
The norm guard remains inside the events and never changes the fresh-coordinate distribution used for the rectangles.

<a id="remaining-interface"></a>
## What this supplies and what remains

For positive-frame coefficients this argument supplies the physical small-support and many-index probability interface left open in the [coefficient-class comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_F0063_v3.md#positive-frame-physical-boundary).
In particular it does not require conditioning away all but an identity noise direction.
The auxiliary-column covariance uses exactly the original independent noises, and permits all fixed complex coefficients satisfying the stated frame condition.
For the real selfadjoint size-three candidate, it can be combined with any separately proved three-column graph-distance estimate at the normalization displayed in (Positive-frame-distance-average).
The complete Schur coefficients or full-numerator alternative, their fixed-original-law anti-concentration, graph normalization and actual-minor probabilities remain separate obligations.
No such input is inferred from the rectangle estimate or from the already available intermediate singular-value theorem.
The general zero-frame coefficients, scalar polynomial linearizations that fail the frame condition, and the full-sequence planar defect remain unresolved.
