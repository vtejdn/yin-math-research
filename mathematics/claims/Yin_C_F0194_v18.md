# Low inverse-energy Schur control for real finite-variance pencils

This theorem supplies complete Schur and graph-distance control on a bounded normalized inverse-energy branch for arbitrary fixed real symmetric coefficients and original real finite-second-moment Wigner inputs.
Its proof was developed for Yin through ordinary reasoning and authoring self-checks.
The [current assessment](#decision-low-inverse-energy-reviewed) adopts the low inverse-energy statement after independent whole-proof inspection.
The complete argument was developed in [D33](../drafts/Yin_D_F0033.md#positive-frame-bounded-inverse-energy); that is extraction provenance, not a logical dependency.
This answers the first low-energy test in [L63v3](../ledger/Yin_L_F0063_v3.md#positive-frame-physical-boundary).
The conclusion uses the complete Schur block and is stronger than a bounded inverse-operator branch.
It complements the separate [positive-frame rectangle argument](Yin_C_F0194_v17.md#statement), which is not used in this proof and does not supply the remaining high-energy distance probabilities.
It does not require a positive coefficient frame, although the intended next application is the real symmetric \(m=3,\chi_A>0\) class.
All necessary probability estimates below follow from the original entry independence, finite second moments, elementary quadratic-form truncation, and the displayed resolvent identities.
No operator-valued Dyson equation theorem, external local law, Gaussian replacement or further original-source result is invoked.

## Original model, three deletion levels and the conditional conclusion

Fix integers \(m,d\ge1\) and real symmetric \(A_0,\ldots,A_d\in M_m(\mathbb R)\), and put
\[
 \Lambda:=\sum_{j=1}^d\|A_j\|^2,\qquad
 \mathcal S(H):=\sum_{j=1}^d A_j H A_j.
\]
Each \(W_j^{(N)}\) is real symmetric.
Use the original real finite-second-moment model: independent upper off-diagonal entries of input \(j\) have law \(\xi_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\xi_j\) is fixed, centered and has variance one.
Its diagonal entries have law \(\zeta_j/\sqrt N\), where \(\zeta_j\) is fixed, real, centered and has finite second moment.
All these coordinates and inputs are independent within each size, while cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
Fix an integer deletion bound \(r\ge0\) and one deterministic real sequence \(\theta_N\to0\), common to every matrix considered.
Write
\[
 A_N:=A_0+\theta_N I_m,\qquad
 L_N^\theta=A_N\otimes I_N+\sum_j A_j\otimes W_j^{(N)}.
\]
For a deterministic \(T\subset[N]\), \(|T|\le r\), first retain the parent on \([N]\setminus T\), then expose a physical index \(i\notin T\).
Its minor has physical set \(J=[N]\setminus(T\cup\{i\})\), size \(n=N-|T|-1\), and is denoted by \(M=M_{T,i}\).
Every entry retains the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization, including in further single deletions inside \(M\).
The latter internal deletions are used only at the nonreal parameter in the proof.
They are not identified with the exposed parent or assumed invertible at zero.

Put the exposed physical block first and identify the retained space with \(\mathbb R^m\otimes\mathbb R^n\), so that
\[
 L_{N,[N]\setminus T}^\theta
 \simeq
 \begin{pmatrix}A_N+D_i&R_i(x)^T\\R_i(x)&M\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 D_i:=\sum_j A_j W_{j,ii},\qquad
 R_i(x)e_a=\sum_j(A_je_a)\otimes x_j.
\]
Let \(\mathcal F_{T,i}\) be the sigma-field generated by the original retained input minors on \(J\) and the exposed original diagonal entries.
The \(d\) original border vectors \(x_j\), with coordinates \(\xi_{j,k}/\sqrt N\), are independent of this sigma-field and of one another.
On the invertible branch define
\[
 Z=M^{-1},\quad
 \mathcal E=\frac{\|Z\|_F^2}{N},\quad
 g_0=\frac1N\sum_{k\in J}Z_{kk},\quad
 B_0=A_N-\mathcal S(g_0),
\]
\[
 S(x)=A_N+D_i-R_i(x)^TZR_i(x),\qquad
 \Gamma(x)=I_m+R_i(x)^TZ^2R_i(x),\qquad
 F(x)=\Gamma(x)^{-1/2}S(x).
 \tag{Energy-branch-objects}
\]
The \(Z_{kk}\) are the actual \(m\)-by-\(m\) physical diagonal blocks.
The symbol \(\mathcal E\) denotes normalized inverse energy, not a bound on \(\|Z\|\).

For each fixed \(K\ge1\), put
\[
 h_K:=\sqrt{mK},\qquad c_K:=\frac1{4h_K},
\]
and define the \(\mathcal F_{T,i}\)-measurable event
\[
 \mathcal G_{T,i}(K):=
 \left\{M\text{ invertible},\ \mathcal E\le K,\
       \|B_0g_0-I_m\|\le\frac12,\
       \|D_i\|\le\frac1{8h_K}\right\}.
 \tag{Energy-branch-good-conditioning}
\]
The conclusions proved below are
\[
 \sup_{\substack{|T|\le r\\i\notin T}}
 \mathbb P\{M\text{ invertible},\ \mathcal E\le K,\
                         \mathcal G_{T,i}(K)^c\}\longrightarrow0
 \tag{Energy-branch-relative-bad-minors}
\]
and a deterministic \(\epsilon_N(K)\to0\), uniform over these conditionings, such that on \(\mathcal G_{T,i}(K)\)
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(S)<c_K\mid\mathcal F_{T,i}\}
 \le\epsilon_N(K),\qquad
 \mathbb P\{|\det S|<c_K^m\mid\mathcal F_{T,i}\}
 \le\epsilon_N(K).
 \tag{Energy-branch-full-numerator}
\]
For every \(t,u>0\), on the same conditioning,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(F)<t\mid\mathcal F_{T,i}\}
 &\le\epsilon_N(K)+(m+\Lambda K)c_K^{-2}t^2,\\
 \mathbb P\!\left\{\frac{|\det S|}{\sqrt{\det\Gamma}}<u
                      \,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{T,i}\right\}
 &\le\epsilon_N(K)+\frac{m+\Lambda K}{mc_K^2}u^{2/m}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Energy-branch-normalized-tails}
\]
The supremum in (Energy-branch-relative-bad-minors) is over marginal probabilities.
It is not the probability of a union over the deletion sets.
No condition depending on the fresh border, including \(S\) being close to \(B_0\), is inserted into \(\mathcal F_{T,i}\) or the good event.

A single deterministic sequence \(K_N\to\infty\), with \(K_N\le\log(N+1)\), also supplies the [slow-threshold distance and normalized-numerator conclusions](#slow-thresholds) for every fixed \(\beta>0\), uniformly over the same marginal fixed-deletion probabilities.
No simultaneous-minor conclusion or parent least-value floor is asserted.

## Complete proof

<a id="quadratic-concentration"></a>
### A finite-second-moment quadratic estimate at Frobenius scale

Let \(v\in\mathbb R^{dn}\) have independent coordinates of the \(d\) prescribed centered variance-one laws, and let \(H\) be a deterministic complex matrix of this size.
For each fixed \(C<\infty\),
\[
 \sup_{\|H\|_F^2\le CN}
 \mathbb E\left|\frac1N(v^THv-\operatorname{Tr}H)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Energy-branch-quadratic-concentration}
\]
This also holds conditionally when \(H\) is measurable under a sigma-field independent of \(v\) and satisfies the displayed bound there.

To prove it, split the expression into its off-diagonal part and
\(N^{-1}\sum_\alpha H_{\alpha\alpha}(v_\alpha^2-1)\).
Independence and centering give the off-diagonal second-moment bound
\[
 \mathbb E\left|
 \frac1N\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}H_{\alpha\beta}v_\alpha v_\beta
 \right|^2
 \le \frac{2\|H\|_F^2}{N^2}.
\]
The only nonzero pairings are the two ordered versions of the same distinct pair, so no fourth moment of a single coordinate is used.
For the diagonal part, fix a truncation level \(R\), center \(v_\alpha^2\mathbf1_{\{v_\alpha^2\le R\}}\), and use independence to bound its \(L^2\) norm by \(C_R\|H\|_F/N\).
For the centered remainder, put
\[
 \omega(R):=\max_{1\le j\le d}
             \mathbb E[\xi_j^2\mathbf1_{\{\xi_j^2>R\}}]\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its \(L^1\) norm is at most
\[
 \frac{2\omega(R)}{N}\sum_\alpha|H_{\alpha\alpha}|
 \le 2\sqrt{dC}\,\omega(R).
\]
First send \(N\to\infty\) at fixed \(R\), and then send \(R\to\infty\).
These estimates prove the assertion for complex \(H\) as well, using absolute squares and the complex Frobenius norm.
For the actual border, define \(U_a:\mathbb R^m\otimes\mathbb R^n\to\bigoplus_{j=1}^d\mathbb R^n\) by \((U_ah)_{j,k}=e_a^TA_jh_k\), where \(h_k\) is the \(k\)-th physical component.
Symmetry of the coefficients gives \(R_i(x)e_a=U_a^Tx\), and \(\|U_a\|\le\sqrt\Lambda\).
The coefficient matrix of entry \((a,b)\) of \(R_i^TZR_i\) is \(U_aZU_b^T\), whose Frobenius norm is at most \(\Lambda\|Z\|_F\).
Consequently, uniformly on \(\mathcal E\le K\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \|R_i^TZR_i-\mathcal S(g_0)\|
 \,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{T,i}\right]\le\delta_N(K),
 \qquad \delta_N(K)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Energy-branch-border-concentration}
\]
The exact conditional mean is \(\mathcal S(g_0)\): mixed input indices and distinct physical positions have zero covariance, and each remaining original coordinate has variance \(1/N\).

<a id="fixed-eta-identity"></a>
### The actual pencil supplies its zero-parameter mean inverse relation

For a fixed \(\eta>0\), let
\[
 G_\eta=(M-i\eta I)^{-1},\qquad
 g_\eta=\frac1N\sum_{k\in J}(G_\eta)_{kk},\qquad
 B_\eta=A_N-i\eta I_m-\mathcal S(g_\eta).
\]
Every actual internal single-deletion resolvent has norm at most \(\eta^{-1}\).
For \(k\in J\), write \(G_\eta^{(k)}\) for this resolvent and use the original internal row \(R_k\).
The exact Schur identity is
\[
 (G_\eta)_{kk}^{-1}
   =A_N+D_k-i\eta I_m-R_k^TG_\eta^{(k)}R_k.
 \tag{Energy-branch-fixed-eta-Schur}
\]
The inverse of the \(m\)-by-\(m\) diagonal block exists because the imaginary part of the resolvent is strictly positive.
All variables in \(R_k\) remain independent of the original internal minor.

The normalized partial trace of \(G_\eta^{(k)}\), using \(1/N\), differs from \(g_\eta\) by at most \(5m/(N\eta)\) in operator norm.
Indeed, decouple the \(k\)-th physical block while retaining its real symmetric diagonal block.
The difference between the coupled and decoupled matrices has rank at most \(2m\).
Their resolvent difference has rank at most \(2m\), norm at most \(2/\eta\), and therefore nuclear norm at most \(4m/\eta\).
For any block matrix \(X\), trace duality gives
\(\|\sum_kX_{kk}\|\le\|X\|_1\), where \(\|\cdot\|_1\) is nuclear norm.
Removing the decoupled \(m\)-by-\(m\) resolvent adds at most \(m/\eta\) to this bound, which proves the stated normalized estimate.

Apply (Energy-branch-quadratic-concentration) to \(G_\eta^{(k)}\), whose squared Frobenius norm is at most \(mN/\eta^2\).
Since \(\mathbb E\|D_k\|=O(N^{-1/2})\), the errors
\[
 E_k:=D_k-
       \bigl(R_k^TG_\eta^{(k)}R_k-\mathcal S(g_\eta)\bigr)
\]
satisfy \(\sup_k\mathbb E\|E_k\|\to0\) at fixed \(\eta\).
This statement uses the unconditional original minor law; it is not obtained by conditioning on a zero-parameter inverse-energy event.
Multiplying (Energy-branch-fixed-eta-Schur) on the right by \((G_\eta)_{kk}\) and summing gives
\[
 B_\eta g_\eta=\frac nN I_m-
                    \frac1N\sum_{k\in J}E_k(G_\eta)_{kk},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\left\|B_\eta g_\eta-\frac nN I_m\right\|
 \le\frac1{N\eta}\sum_{k\in J}\mathbb E\|E_k\|
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Energy-branch-fixed-eta-identity}
\]
No stability or uniqueness assertion for a limiting matrix equation has entered.
Since \(n=N-|T|-1\), replacing \(n/N\) by one costs at most \((r+1)/N\).

Now work only on the random event that \(M\) is invertible and \(\mathcal E\le K\).
Spectral calculus and the same partial-trace nuclear-norm bound give the deterministic inequalities
\[
 \|g_0\|,\ \|g_\eta\|\le\frac{\sqrt{mn}\|Z\|_F}{N}
                      \le h_K,\qquad
 \|g_\eta-g_0\|\le\frac{\eta\|Z\|_F^2}{N}\le\eta K.
 \tag{Energy-branch-return-to-zero}
\]
For the second inequality, each real nonzero eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(M\) contributes
\[
 \left|\frac1{\lambda-i\eta}-\frac1\lambda\right|
 \le\frac{\eta}{\lambda^2}
\]
to the nuclear norm before division by \(N\).
In particular this step allows individual inverse singular values as large as \(\sqrt{KN}\).

Choose \(a\) with \(\|A_N\|\le a\) eventually.
Because \(\|\mathcal S\|\le\Lambda\), the preceding bounds imply
\[
 \|B_0g_0-B_\eta g_\eta\|
 \le\eta\bigl(aK+h_K+2\Lambda K h_K\bigr)
 \tag{Energy-branch-zero-defect-comparison}
\]
on that energy event.
For any fixed tolerance, choose \(\eta>0\) sufficiently small at fixed \(K\), apply (Energy-branch-fixed-eta-identity) as \(N\to\infty\), and then let \(\eta\downarrow0\).
Thus
\[
 \sup_{\substack{|T|\le r\\i\notin T}}
 \mathbb P\{M\text{ invertible},\ \mathcal E\le K,\
                    \|B_0g_0-I_m\|>\varepsilon\}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\varepsilon>0).
 \tag{Energy-branch-zero-identity}
\]
The marginal uniformity follows either from the displayed estimates or from physical permutation invariance and the finite set of deletion sizes.
It does not require a local law uniform over a random family of conditioned minors.

<a id="complete-numerator"></a>
### The complete numerator and the graph normalization

On \(\|B_0g_0-I_m\|\le1/2\), the product \(B_0g_0\) is invertible, and
\[
 B_0^{-1}=g_0(B_0g_0)^{-1},\qquad
 \|B_0^{-1}\|\le2h_K,\qquad
 s_{\min}(B_0)\ge(2h_K)^{-1}.
 \tag{Energy-branch-mean-Schur-gap}
\]
The original exposed diagonal satisfies \(\mathbb E\|D_i\|=O(N^{-1/2})\).
Together with (Energy-branch-zero-identity), this proves (Energy-branch-relative-bad-minors).
On the good conditioning, (Energy-branch-border-concentration) and Markov's inequality show that
\[
 \|R_i^TZR_i-\mathcal S(g_0)\|\le(8h_K)^{-1}
\]
except with conditional probability at most \(8h_K\delta_N(K)\).
Combining this fresh-row conclusion with the already conditioned diagonal bound gives
\[
 s_{\min}(S)\ge(2h_K)^{-1}-2(8h_K)^{-1}=c_K.
\]
This proves (Energy-branch-full-numerator), with
\(\epsilon_N(K):=\min(1,8h_K\delta_N(K))\).
It controls the full polynomial \(\det(A_N+D_i-R_i^TZR_i)\), including all repeated-coordinate and lower-degree contributions.
It does not infer a lower complete Walsh layer from a raw homogeneous coefficient.
The nonzero constant comparison is \(\det B_0\), justified by the actual minor equation, rather than an arbitrary deterministic corner.

Indeed, the \(m\) columns of \(U=\binom{I_m}{-ZR_i}\Gamma^{-1/2}\) are orthonormal, are orthogonal to all retained columns of the parent, and span their orthogonal complement because \(M\) is invertible.
Thus the actual distance matrix is \(F=U^T\binom{A_N+D_i}{R_i}=\Gamma^{-1/2}S\), and
\[
 |\det F|=\frac{|\det S|}{\sqrt{\det\Gamma}}.
\]
The unnormalized numerator lower bound is \(c_K^m\).
There is no assertion that the graph area stays bounded on every fresh-row realization.
Instead its conditional trace obeys the exact estimate
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma\mid\mathcal F_{T,i}]
 &=m+\frac1N
     \operatorname{Tr}\left[
       \left(\sum_j A_j^2\otimes I_n\right)Z^2\right]\\
 &\le m+\Lambda\mathcal E\le m+\Lambda K.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Energy-branch-graph-trace}
\]
The first trace is \(m\)-dimensional and the trace on the right is \(mn\)-dimensional.
On \(s_{\min}(S)\ge c_K\), one has
\[
 s_{\min}(F)\ge\frac{c_K}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma}},\qquad
 |\det F|\ge c_K^m
                      \left(\frac{m}{\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma}\right)^{m/2}.
\]
The determinant inequality uses
\(\det\Gamma\le(\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma/m)^m\).
Markov's inequality applied to (Energy-branch-graph-trace) now proves both bounds in (Energy-branch-normalized-tails).
Neither a parent norm guard, a fresh-row ball nor a copied sign support is needed for this low-energy branch.

<a id="slow-thresholds"></a>
### Slow thresholds and averaged exceptions

Fix the original coefficient tuple, laws, deletion bound \(r\), and the single deterministic sequence \(\theta_N\to0\).
For each integer \(K\ge1\), the relative bad-minor supremum in (Energy-branch-relative-bad-minors), plus \(\epsilon_N(K)\), tends to zero.
Choose increasing deterministic cutoffs \(N_K\) so that this sum is at most \(1/K\) for all \(N\ge N_K\).
Enlarge the cutoffs if necessary and choose an integer sequence \(K_N\to\infty\) with
\[
 1\le K_N\le\log(N+1),\qquad N\ge N_{K_N}
\]
eventually.
Then the same relative bad-minor supremum at \(K_N\), plus the conditional row error \(\epsilon_N(K_N)\), tends to zero.
This is a diagonal choice from fixed-\(K\) assertions; it is not a claimed rate for finite-second-moment concentration.

For every fixed \(\beta>0\), (Energy-branch-normalized-tails) yields
\[
 \sup_{\substack{|T|\le r\\i\notin T}}
 \mathbb P\{M_{T,i}\text{ invertible},\
          \mathcal E_{T,i}\le K_N,\
          s_{\min}(F_{T,i})<N^{-\beta}\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Energy-branch-slow-threshold-distance}
\]
Indeed the remaining conditional trace term is at most
\[
 16mK_N(m+\Lambda K_N)N^{-2\beta}=o(1).
\]
The identical argument with the second bound in (Energy-branch-normalized-tails) gives
\[
 \sup_{\substack{|T|\le r\\i\notin T}}
 \mathbb P\{M_{T,i}\text{ invertible},\
          \mathcal E_{T,i}\le K_N,\
          |\det F_{T,i}|<N^{-\beta}\}\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{Energy-branch-slow-threshold-normalized-numerator}
\]
because its trace term is \(O((K_N+K_N^2)N^{-2\beta/m})\).
The sequence \(K_N\) can be chosen once for these conclusions for all fixed \(\beta>0\); the diagonal choice does not depend on \(\beta\).
A bounded inverse operator automatically lies in this branch for a suitable fixed \(K\), but the branch also permits unbounded inverse operators.

For \(T=\varnothing\), the average of any of these bad probabilities over \(1\le i\le N\) tends to zero.
More generally the same holds for any deterministic probability-weighted average over the displayed fixed-deletion pairs.
This supplies the mean exception interface needed for a later many-index argument; it does not prove a simultaneous good event for all pairs.
If a separately chosen common vanishing shift makes all required minors invertible almost surely, the invertibility qualifiers can be removed for that shifted family.
The proof applies to that one deterministic shift without making a row-dependent or minor-dependent shift.
It neither supplies return to the unshifted parent nor claims a least-value floor for the whole parent.

<a id="remaining-scope"></a>
## Remaining high-energy scope

The remaining partition is now concrete: on the low branch \(\mathcal E\le K_N\), the complete numerator and its graph normalization are controlled directly; only \(\mathcal E>K_N\) remains for the coefficient mechanism.
This high-energy inequality alone does not give the physical hypotheses of C187v24.
For example, it does not imply that
\[
 \frac1N\left\|Z-\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_je_je_j^T\right\|_F^2
 \longrightarrow\infty\qquad(k=1,2),
\]
since one or two large inverse modes can carry all diverging energy.
Nor does a single Frobenius lower bound imply small physical collision mass or delocalized head modes.
The positive frame supplies the abstract maps, but the head and residual physical transfer, and its original-law probability conversion, still need exact arguments on this remaining branch.
The low-energy branch is adopted with [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v20.md#low-inverse-energy-whole-proof).
It is not a full positive-frame least-value theorem or either general Task's completion.
