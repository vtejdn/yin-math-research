# Structured companion pairs for the actual skew inverse-product shift

This project deduction gives an arbitrary-power rarity estimate for structured pairs in the real three-factor model.
The statement keeps the actual inverse-product shift and the exact companion relation.
Its new step recovers the companion from equations outside a fixed-fraction block, before an independent original rectangle inside that block is exposed.
The [selected assessment](#decision-structured-companion-reviewed) adopts the exact guarded statement after independent whole-proof review.
No conditional-numerator, least-singular-value, Brown-measure or full F0012 conclusion is part of the statement.

The extraction inputs are the [two-column exploration L66v16](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v16.md#two-column-long-profile-precision-test) and the [D11 exact-companion investigation](../drafts/Yin_D_F0011.md#outside-equations-exact-companion-recovery).
The necessary two-column arguments are supplied in full below, rather than invoked through L.
The [independent R200v23 finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v23.md#joint-two-column-profile-whole-scope) checked only the earlier two-column subclasses and their limitations; it is not a review of this new complete count.

## Exact statement

For every N, let \(A_N,B_N,C_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
For each input, its strict upper entries have one fixed real law \(\xi/\sqrt N\), with mean zero and variance one, and its diagonal entries have one fixed real law \(\zeta/\sqrt N\), with mean zero and finite second moment.
The three inputs may have different laws.
All upper coordinates and inputs are independent within a dimension; their coupling across dimensions is arbitrary.
Fix \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R\).

For each outer index \(i\in[N]\), let \(n=N-1\) and, on invertibility of the actual full B and C, set
\[
 X_i=(A_N)_{\widehat i,\widehat i},\qquad
 F_i=-z(C_N^{-1}B_N^{-1})_{\widehat i,\widehat i},
\]
\[
 S_i=X_i+\tfrac12(F_i+F_i^T),\qquad
 K_i=\tfrac12(F_i-F_i^T),\qquad D_i=S_i+K_i.
 \tag{SP-actual-model}
\]
Transposition here is algebraic transposition, without complex conjugation.
All inverse-product blocks are principal blocks of the full inverse product, not products of inverses of minors.

Fix \(q>0\), real exponents \(\gamma_F,k_0,L\ge1\), an integer \(C_0\ge0\), and a constant \(C_*\ge0\).
For every positive integer a and outer i, let \(\mathcal U_{i,N}(a;B_N,C_N)\) be a finite measurable family of vectors in \(\mathbb C^n\), determined by B,C alone, such that
\[
 \tfrac12\le\|u\|_2\le2,\qquad
 |\mathcal U_{i,N}(a;B_N,C_N)|\le n^{C_*n+r_a(n)}.
 \tag{SP-family}
\]
For each fixed a the remainder \(r_a(n)=o(n)\) is deterministic and uniform in i and in the conditioning values of B,C.
The leading coefficient \(C_*\) is independent of a.
No further GAP property is required by this statement; the rational GAP candidates motivating it satisfy precisely this entropy condition.
The family must not depend on the first-input entries X.

Let \(\mathcal G_{i,N}\) be the event
\[
 B_N,C_N,S_i\ \hbox{invertible},\qquad
 \|F_i\|\le N^{\gamma_F},\qquad
 \max(\|S_i\|,\|K_i\|)\le N^{k_0},\qquad
 \|S_i^{-1}\|\le N^L.
 \tag{SP-guards}
\]
All norms are Euclidean operator norms.
Set \(Q_0=2k_0+L\).
There is an integer \(a_*\), depending only on the fixed laws, z, q and the displayed fixed exponents and constants, such that for every fixed integer \(a\ge a_*\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sup_{i\in[N]}\mathbb P\bigl\{\mathcal G_{i,N},\
 &\exists u\in\mathcal U_{i,N}(a;B_N,C_N),\
 \exists E\subset[n],\ |E|\le C_0,\\
 &v=S_i^{-1}K_i u,\quad
 |(S_i u-K_i v)_j|\le N^{-a+Q_0}\quad(j\notin E)
 \bigr\}
 \le C_qN^{-q}+Ce^{-cN}
 \end{split}
 \tag{SP-pair-rarity}
\]
for all sufficiently large N.
The necessary lower bound on N may depend on the chosen fixed a and its specified remainder \(r_a\).
The other constants are uniform in i and in the admissible families.
The exact relation \(S_iv-K_iu=0\) holds on every row, including E; only the other equation is permitted to omit E.

This is a marginal probability assertion intersected with the stated guards.
It asserts no probability bound for the complement of (SP-guards), no D floor, and no high-matching or conditional-concentration conclusion.
In the argument those full-matrix guards remain event restrictions and are never used to condition the law of an unexposed block.

## Complete argument

Fix an outer i and suppress its subscript.
Write \(\delta_N=N^{-a+Q_0}\).
On (SP-guards), every companion has
\[
 \|v\|\le \|S^{-1}\|\,\|K\|\,\|u\|
 \le2N^{L+k_0}.
 \tag{SP-companion-norm}

<a id="ec-actual-skew-bulk"></a>
### The actual inverse-product skew part has a polynomial bulk

Let \(b,c\) be free centered variance-one semicircular elements.
The bounded self-adjoint polynomial \(Q=i(bc-cb)\) has
\[
 \tau(Q^2)=
 -\tau(bcbc)+\tau(bc^2b)+\tau(cb^2c)-\tau(cbcb)=2,
 \qquad \|Q\|\le8.
 \tag{EC-free-commutator-mass}
\]
The two alternating fourth moments vanish by freeness and centering; the other two traces equal one.
This is a free bounded calculation, not a claim that the untruncated finite-N fourth moments exist.

Hence there are a fixed \(a_{\rm com}>0\), a bounded continuous real function f of finite total variation, and \(d>0\), such that
\[
 0\le f\le1,\qquad f(x)=0\ (|x|\le a_{\rm com}),\qquad \tau f(Q)=d.
\]
For \(Q_N=i(B_NC_N-C_NB_N)\), the accepted [C186v4 expected weak limit](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#statement) gives
\(\mathbb E\,N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}f(Q_N)\to d\).
Its [row-block concentration bound](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#row-concentration) therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}f(Q_N)<d/2\right\}
 \le2e^{-cN}
 \tag{EC-commutator-bulk}
\]
for all sufficiently large N.
Indeed the expectation is at least \(3d/4\) eventually, and the deviation \(d/4\) is fixed.
Thus at least \(\gamma_{\rm bulk}N\), \(\gamma_{\rm bulk}=d/2>0\), singular values of \(B_NC_N-C_NB_N\) exceed \(a_{\rm com}\) except on the displayed exponential event.
Only positive free spectral mass away from zero is used; no absence of a free atom at zero is needed.

For an arbitrary prescribed q, choose a fixed integer \(b_0>(q+1)/2\).
The original second moment gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\max(\|B_N\|,\|C_N\|)>N^{b_0}\}
 \le C N^{1-2b_0}=O(N^{-q}).
\]
On factor invertibility, the exact identity is
\[
 C_N^{-1}B_N^{-1}-B_N^{-1}C_N^{-1}
 =(B_NC_N)^{-1}(C_NB_N-B_NC_N)(C_NB_N)^{-1}.
 \tag{EC-inverse-commutator}
\]
Multiplication verifies both terms, so no product of principal inverses occurs.
Write
\[
 \mathfrak K_N:=-\frac z2
 (C_N^{-1}B_N^{-1}-B_N^{-1}C_N^{-1}).
\]
The least singular values of the two inverse factors in (EC-inverse-commutator) are the reciprocals of \(\|B_NC_N\|\) and \(\|C_NB_N\|\).
Thus, on the displayed norm guard and the commutator bulk event, at least \(\gamma_{\rm bulk}N\) singular values of \(\mathfrak K_N\) are at least
\[
 (|z|a_{\rm com}/2)N^{-4b_0}\ge N^{-L_K},
 \qquad L_K:=4b_0+1
\]
eventually.

A principal deletion of h rows and columns loses at most \(2h\) singular values above the same threshold.
To see this, pad the retained block by zero on the deleted coordinates; its difference from the full matrix is supported on those rows or columns and has rank at most \(2h\).
The singular-value rank inequality proves the assertion without any subset union.
It follows that, for some fixed \(\eta>0\), every outer principal block \(K=(\mathfrak K_N)_{\widehat i,\widehat i}\) has at least \(\eta n\) singular values at least \(N^{-L_K}\), outside
\[
 O(N^{-q})+2e^{-cN}
 \tag{EC-actual-K-failure}
\]
on the factor-invertibility guard.
One may take \(\eta=\min(\gamma_{\rm bulk}/2,1/4)\).
The event (SP-guards) supplies factor invertibility and the bound \(\|F\|\le N^{\gamma_F}\).
No estimate for the failure of that event is needed for this statement.
This new bulk is obtained for the actual B,C distribution; it is not inferred from high matching or the D floor.
The [L66v11 rare actual background](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v11.md#actual-invertible-background) has rank-two K and illustrates that those other deterministic guards alone would not imply this bulk.

<a id="ec-outside-identification"></a>
### A common block-outside identification matrix

Fix the integer
\[
 M:=\lceil32/\eta\rceil,
\]
and partition \([n]\) deterministically into M balanced nonempty blocks \(P_1,\ldots,P_M\).
For every ordered pair of distinct blocks \((P_\ell,P_h)\), put
\[
 I=P_\ell\cup P_h,\quad r=|I|,\quad J=I^c,\quad p=|J|.
\]
There are only \(M(M-1)\) choices.
For large N, \(r\le\eta n/8\), while each single block has size at least \(n/(2M)\).

Condition first on actual B,C and the original entries \(X_{JJ}\).
Do not expose \(X_{II}\).
Write
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}H&Y^T\\Y&S_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}K_I&k^T\\-k&K_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 u=\binom\alpha w,\quad v=\binom t{v_J},\quad G=S_0^{-1}.
\]
The unexposed cross block has the exact form \(Y=N^{-1/2}V+Y_0\), with pr independent original real first-input coordinates in V.
The opposite cross block is its transpose, not an independent copy.
The matrix H, and specifically the original \(X_{II}\), is still unexposed.

The [C188v2 symmetric-shift theorem](Yin_C_F0188_v2.md#statement) supplies
\(\|G\|\le N^{L_0}\) at arbitrary-power failure for these finitely many J.
Its normalization is \(X_{JJ}=\sqrt{p/N}\,W_p\), with \(p/N\) bounded above and below by fixed positive constants.
On the counted event, \(\|S_0\|\le\|S\|\le N^{k_0}\) and \(\|K\|\le N^{k_0}\).
After conditioning on B,C and \(X_{JJ}\), these particular internal-block bounds are measurable; if either fails, the counted guarded event is empty.
Only the symmetric-minor inverse exception is charged here, once before candidate counting.
The full S norm remains an event restriction and is not imposed on the unexposed entries as a conditional law.

On the K bulk, \(K_0\) has at least \(\eta n-2r\) singular values at least \(N^{-L_K}\).
Set \(A_0:=K_0G\).
It has the same number of singular values at least
\(\lambda_N:=N^{-L_K-k_0}\), since \(s_{\min}(G)=\|S_0\|^{-1}\).
Fix \(E_J\subset J\), \(e:=|E_J|\le C_0\), and let \(\Pi\) restrict to \(J\setminus E_J\).
Define the \(p-e\) by r identification matrix
\[
 C_E:=\Pi(A_0Y+k).
 \tag{EC-identification-matrix}
\]
Its randomness comes only from the original cross block Y.

Here is the needed constant-codimension version of the [accepted C194v3 distance proof](Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#linear-codimension).
For every fixed \(\theta>0\), a real iid vector \(\xi\in\mathbb R^p\) with the fixed original finite-second-moment law satisfies
\[
 \mathbb P\{\operatorname{dist}(\xi+f,V)\le c_\theta\sqrt p\}
 \le C_\theta e^{-c_\theta p}
 \tag{EC-fixed-fraction-distance}
\]
uniformly over complex subspaces of codimension at least \(\theta p\) and deterministic shifts f.
In that proof choose a fixed cutoff with tail probability small enough that more than \(\theta p/4\) discarded coordinates has exponentially small probability, and with retained conditional variance at least \(1/2\).
Projection and adjoining the mean and shift then leave codimension at least \(\theta p/2\) for large p.
The squared projected norm has expectation at least \(\theta p/4\).
The same accepted bounded-product convex concentration and median argument gives (EC-fixed-fraction-distance).
This is only a constant adjustment of the inspected proof, with the cutoff integrated back to the original law.

The matrix \(\Pi A_0\) has at least
\[
 d_E:=\lfloor\eta n\rfloor-2r-e
\]
singular values at least \(\lambda_N\).
Condition on all columns of \(C_E\) except column j; their span has dimension at most \(r-1\).
Project onto a d_E-dimensional good left singular subspace of \(\Pi A_0\), invert its retained singular values, and pull back to the corresponding right singular subspace.
The preimage of that projected span has complex codimension at least
\[
 d_E-(r-1)=\lfloor\eta n\rfloor-3r-e+1\ge\eta n/2.
 \tag{EC-column-codimension}
\]
The shift caused by \(Y_0\), k and the other columns is deterministic under this conditioning.
More explicitly, this singular-subspace transformation lower bounds the column distance by
\(\lambda_N N^{-1/2}\operatorname{dist}(\xi+f,V)\).
Applying (EC-fixed-fraction-distance) gives distance at least \(c\lambda_N\) except with probability \(Ce^{-cN}\).

After a union over r columns, every column distance has that lower bound.
For a unit vector t choose a coordinate with modulus at least \(r^{-1/2}\) and project \(C_Et\) away from the other columns.
This gives
\[
 s_{\min}(C_E)\ge c\lambda_N/\sqrt r\ge N^{-L_C},
 \qquad L_C:=L_K+k_0+2
 \tag{EC-identification-floor}
\]
for large N.
The bound is common to all u, since \(C_E\) does not involve u.
A union over the \(O(N^{C_0})\) sets \(E_J\) and the fixed block-pair family still has failure \(Ce^{-cN}\).
This probability statement is first proved for the original cross-block law.
It is not obtained by conditioning on a full S norm or on the event (EC-identification-floor) before exposing Y.

After Y has actually been exposed, \(C_E\), its full-column-rank Moore--Penrose inverse \(C_E^\dagger\), and all outside data are fixed.
The entire original block \(X_{II}\) remains independent and unexposed.
This is the exposure boundary used in the next step.

<a id="ec-companion-recovery"></a>
### The actual equations pin the companion using only the exposed outside

For each deterministic candidate u and omission set E, put
\[
 b:=G(-k\alpha+K_0w),\qquad c:=S_0w-K_0b.
\]
The exact outside companion equation gives
\[
 v_J=b-GYt.
 \tag{EC-outside-exact}
\]
The other outside equation is
\[
 (Su-Kv)_J=Y\alpha+c+(A_0Y+k)t.
 \tag{EC-outside-first}
\]
On its retained rows it has residual vector \(\zeta\) with \(\|\zeta\|\le\sqrt N\,\delta_N\).
Define, using only the exposed outside and u,
\[
 t_*:=-C_E^\dagger\Pi(Y\alpha+c),\qquad
 v_*:=\binom{t_*}{\,b-GYt_*\,}.
 \tag{EC-recovered-companion}
\]
Because \(C_E^\dagger C_E=I_r\), every actual feasible pair satisfies
\[
 t-t_*=C_E^\dagger\zeta,\qquad
 \|t-t_*\|\le N^{L_C+1/2}\delta_N.
\]
On the full norm guard, \(\|Y\|\le N^{k_0}\), so
\[
 \|v-v_*\|
 \le(1+\|GY\|)\|t-t_*\|
 \le N^{-a+E_v},\qquad
 E_v:=Q_0+L_C+L_0+k_0+2.
 \tag{EC-recovery-error}
\]
This norm guard is used as an intersection in the estimated event, not to change the remaining block's law.

There is one such \(v_*\) for each u,E and ordered block pair.
No grid for t or v is used.
The exact equation \(Sv-Ku=0\) and the actual center \(Y\alpha+c\) were essential to its definition.
The compatibility constraints left in the outside first equation and both inside equations may still fail; no assertion is made that an arbitrary reconstructed pair is feasible.
Every original feasible pair is close to its reconstruction, which is the only direction used for the probability upper bound.
All coordinates of \(v_*\) are fixed before \(X_{II}\) is exposed.

For the small-ball comparison below, impose the usual polynomial cutoff only on original first-input entries in the current principal minor.
With sufficiently large fixed \(\kappa\), its complement has probability \(O(N^{-q})\).
Conditional on these independent cutoffs the unexposed raw entries are iid with the law \(\xi_n=(\xi\mid|\xi|\le N^\kappa)\), retaining the uniform nondegeneracy established in the joint-profile setup below.
The identification failure has already been bounded under the original law and is paid separately; no exponential assertion for that failure is inferred by reconditioning on the cutoffs.
The auxiliary symmetric-minor failure is likewise bounded under the original law.
The full S norm is already a restriction in (SP-guards); its failure need not be charged or assigned a conditional probability.
For the remaining success event, all actual outside values are simply exposed while the unexposed coordinates retain their independent conditional law.
Only the n-by-n principal first-input block X is involved; the omitted original diagonal \(A_{ii}\) is not truncated or conditioned on.

<a id="ec-low-profile-rectangle"></a>
### Low global joint concentration transfers to a predetermined original rectangle

Let \(a_j=(u_j,v_j)\in\mathbb C^2\), and write \(\rho_{\rm all}(\mathbf a,\beta)\) for the joint concentration of
\(\sum_{j=1}^n a_j\xi_{n,j}\).
Use the [joint-profile split](#high-joint-product-count) proved below, with a fixed threshold \(C_1\) and \(\beta=n^{-s}\), chosen in the final parameter order.
For any selected ordering, every tail concentration is at least \(\rho_{\rm all}(\mathbf a,\beta)\).
Consequently, a low prefix product
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^{p'}\rho_j(\mathbf a,\beta)<n^{-C_1n},
 \qquad p'\le n,
\]
implies
\[
 \rho_{\rm all}(\mathbf a,\beta)<n^{-C_1n/p'}\le n^{-C_1}.
 \tag{EC-low-global-concentration}
\]
Thus it suffices to count the larger feasible-pair event satisfying (EC-low-global-concentration), without retaining its earlier reservoir or permutation.

For the predetermined partition into M blocks, let \(\rho_\ell\) denote the concentration of the sum over \(P_\ell\).
Independence of disjoint original coordinates and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \rho_{\rm all}(\mathbf a,\beta)
 \ge\prod_{\ell=1}^M\rho_\ell(\mathbf a,\beta/M).
 \tag{EC-partition-convolution}
\]
One may take approximating maximizing centers and then pass to the supremum.
Therefore some block \(R=P_\ell\) satisfies
\[
 \rho_R(\mathbf a,\beta/M)<n^{-C_1/M}.
 \tag{EC-reservoir-small-ball}
\]
Choose any distinct block \(T=P_h\), and use \(I=R\cup T\) in the preceding recovery.
The proof sums over all finitely many ordered block pairs; it never chooses a center after the entries of \(X_{II}\) have been observed.

The concentration of the original pair must now be transferred, not silently assigned to \(v_*\).
For every realization of the cutoff scalar variables and every subset R, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \left\|\sum_{j\in R}(0,v_j-v_{*,j})\xi_{n,j}\right\|
 \le N^{\kappa+1/2}\|v-v_*\|
 \le N^{-a+E_v+\kappa+1/2}.
 \tag{EC-profile-transfer-error}
\]
Also the raw paired residual at \(v_*\), on retained rows of the actual event, has norm at most
\[
 N^{1/2}\delta_N+
 2N^{k_0+1/2}\|v-v_*\|
 \le N^{-a+E_v+k_0+2}.
 \tag{EC-row-transfer-error}
\]
The first term is the original first-equation residual, and the second bounds both the K and S actions on the companion difference.
The full S norm is again only an event restriction.

Choose \(a\) after s and the recovery exponents so that, for all large N, both (EC-profile-transfer-error) and (EC-row-transfer-error) are at most
\[
 \gamma_N:=\beta/(4M).
\]
Then (EC-reservoir-small-ball) implies the outside-measurable condition
\[
 \rho_R((u,v_*),\gamma_N)
 \le\rho_R((u,v),2\gamma_N)
 \le\rho_R((u,v),\beta/M)
 <n^{-C_1/M}.
 \tag{EC-transferred-small-ball}
\]
If the left side fails this bound for the reconstructed pair determined by an exposed outside, the original low event for that u,E and reservoir is empty.
This statement avoids conditioning on an event defined using the original v, which may depend on the still-unexposed block.
For fixed u,E,R,T, let \(\mathcal B_{\rm out}\) denote the bound on the left side of (EC-transferred-small-ball).
It is determined by the exposed outside.
With
\[
 \mathscr R_j(u,v'):=\sqrt N\bigl((Su-Kv')_j,(Sv'-Ku)_j\bigr),
\]
the original feasible event with reservoir R, low concentration and the retained guards is contained in
\[
 \mathcal B_{\rm out}\ \cap\
 \bigcap_{j\in T\setminus E}\{\|\mathscr R_j(u,v_*)\|\le\gamma_N\}.
 \tag{EC-measurable-event-inclusion}
\]
This inclusion holds for every original companion v witnessing that event, even if it depends on the fresh entries.

Now expose all entries internal to T and all entries internal to R, including their diagonals, leaving only the original T-by-R rectangle unexposed.
For each \(j\in T\setminus E\), the fresh part of the raw paired residual at \((u,v_*)\) is exactly
\[
 \sum_{k\in R}(u_k,v_{*,k})\xi_{n,jk}.
\]
Every other term is fixed by the allowed conditioning.
These sums are independent across j because T and R are disjoint and no mirrored entry occurs twice in this rectangle.
The shift terms are the actual paired residual centers.
By (EC-row-transfer-error) and (EC-transferred-small-ball), the conditional probability of the necessary retained row events is at most
\[
 n^{-(C_1/M)|T\setminus E|}
 \le n^{-C_1n/(3M^2)}
 \tag{EC-original-rectangle-product}
\]
eventually, since \(|T\setminus E|\ge n/(3M)\).

For every exposed outside on the identification guard, there is only one reconstructed vector for each u,E and ordered pair.
Union over (SP-family), the \(O(N^{C_0})\) omission choices and the fixed block-pair family gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\text{feasible structured pair with (EC-low-global-concentration), all good events}\}
 \le n^{(C_*-C_1/(3M^2))n+o_{a}(n)}.
 \tag{EC-low-class-count}
\]
The identification and original-law guard exceptions were paid once, not once per candidate.
Taking
\[
 C_1>6M^2(C_*+1)
 \tag{EC-coarse-threshold}
\]
makes the right side at most \(n^{-cn}\).
The fine-precision factor of an unrestricted companion grid has not appeared: v was recovered from actual outside equations.
There is no product of mutually conditioned row probabilities.

<a id="original-law-joint-exposure"></a>
## The original-law joint profile and its two compressed subclasses

This subsection supplies the complete count for the degenerate and high-product classes.
It applies uniformly to fixed actual F,K and to the bound (SP-companion-norm).
For each deterministic pair \(u,v\), keep all actual shift terms; the original companion itself need not be deterministic before the covering argument.

Pass to raw scaling
\[
 R:=\sqrt N X,\qquad
 \mathsf F:=\sqrt N\,\operatorname{sym}F,\qquad
 \mathsf K:=\sqrt N K.
\]
Then the actual paired row residual is
\[
 \mathcal L_i(u,v)
 :=\left(
 (Ru+\mathsf Fu-\mathsf Kv)_i,\,
 (Rv+\mathsf Fv-\mathsf Ku)_i
 \right)\in\mathbb C^2.
 \tag{TC-raw-residual}
\]
Its second component is zero on all rows for the original pair.
Dropping the paired constraint on E enlarges the counted event by omitting only \(C_0\) rows; the exact original companion relation is not changed.
For fixed u,v, the two deterministic shift terms in (TC-raw-residual) remain their actual values.
They enter row small-ball bounds as fixed centers, not newly chosen centers defining a different feasible event.

Choose an integer \(\kappa>(q+2)/2\).
The second-moment union bound gives probability \(O(N^{2-2\kappa})=O(N^{-q})\) that any raw original first-input entry in X exceeds \(N^\kappa\); the diagonal contribution is no larger.
This is the same original-law truncation used in [C194v1](Yin_C_F0194_v1.md#truncation).
Conditional on the independent truncations, the strict upper entries have law \(\xi_n=(\xi\mid |\xi|\le N^\kappa)\) and remain iid.
Since this conditional law tends to the fixed nondegenerate real law in total variation, Condition 1 holds with constants \(c_1,c_2,c_3\) independent of N.
The source constants therefore remain uniform.
No centering or higher moment is added.
Condition on the independent diagonal values.
Choose a fixed integer exponent \(k\ge1\) so that entries of R and of both deterministic raw shifts have modulus at most \(n^k\) on these events.
Choose a fixed integer \(m\) with \(\|v\|\le n^m\).
These choices precede every fine precision below.
All bounds below are uniform in the conditioned diagonal and actual deterministic shifts in this range.

For a deterministic candidate pair write
\[
 a_j:=(u_j,v_j)\in\mathbb C^2,\qquad
 \rho_i(\mathbf a,t)
 :=\sup_{z\in\mathbb C^2}
    \mathbb P\left\{\left\|\sum_{j=i}^n a_j\xi_{n,j}-z\right\|\le t\right\}.
 \tag{TC-joint-tail-profile}
\]
There is one original real scalar \(\xi_{n,j}\) multiplying each coefficient vector.
This is a real-vector linear form in ambient dimension four, not two independently sampled scalar rows and not a theorem for correlated complex scalar inputs.
The tail concentrations are nondecreasing in i.

Whenever a tail has norm
\(\sigma=(\sum_{j=i}^n(|u_j|^2+|v_j|^2))^{1/2}>0\), apply the source to \(a_j/\sigma\) at radius \(t/\sigma\).
Its concentration is exactly \(\rho_i(\mathbf a,t)\).
The norm bound is \(\sigma\le2+n^m\); the zero tail has concentration one.
The source normalization therefore costs only fixed polynomial exponents and does not replace v by a bounded unit vector while leaving u's scale unchanged.
In the rational-generator counting clause used in C194v1, rescaling a generator of form \((t/\sigma)p_{ij}/p\) returns \(tp_{ij}/p\).
The variable norm cancels.
At polynomial t, the four real coordinates of the rescaled generators have polynomial height, with constants allowed to depend on the fixed precision and on m.
Thus the same finite descriptor argument applies in dimension four.

Expose the symmetric original upper entries in increasing row order after a deterministic simultaneous permutation.
At row i the remaining random sum is
\(\sum_{j=i+1}^n a_j\xi_{n,ij}\).
Every other contribution, including both actual shifts, is fixed by that exposure.
Covering the joint support ball of polynomial radius in \(\mathbb R^4\) gives, for \(t\ge n^{-s}\),
\[
 \rho_i(\mathbf a,t)\ge n^{-D_s},
 \qquad D_s:=4(s+k+m+3)+1.
\]
The same holds for the bounded rounded pairs below.
Consequently the legal exposure bound is
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\mathcal L_i(u,v)\|\le t\text{ on prescribed }n-C_0
                    \text{ rows}\}
 \le n^{D_s(C_0+1)}\prod_{i=1}^n\rho_i(\mathbf a,t).
 \tag{TC-triangular-product}
\]
The missing diagonal term shifts the product from \(\rho_1\) to \(\rho_2\), and the displayed polynomial factor pays for that one factor and the bounded omitted rows.
This is one ordered exposure of the original symmetric matrix.
It never multiplies mutually conditioned whole-row events.

Place a largest u-coordinate last, with
\[
 |u_n|\ge(2\sqrt n)^{-1}.
\]
Every tail then contains it.
Projection onto that complex coordinate direction and the source anti-concentration condition give a fixed \(p_0<1\) for every tail at \(t\le c/\sqrt n\).
In particular the elementary fixed-pair bound is \(n^{O_s(1)}p_0^{n-C_0-1}\).
It remains essential to count an actual deterministic family; the existence of the random companion is not covered by this bound alone.

<a id="joint-degenerate-count"></a>
## The joint degenerate class is compressible without a fine net for v

Fix \(\varepsilon=1/100\), let
\[
 q_n=\lceil n^{1-\varepsilon}\rceil,\qquad
 \theta=\varepsilon/10,\qquad t_*=n^{-k-5}.
\]
Call a pair degenerate if every \(q_n\)-coordinate subset has joint concentration at least
\(q_n^{-1/2+\theta}\) at radius \(t_*\).
For each such subset, normalize its coefficient vectors as above and use the real-four-dimensional inverse theorem with exception count \(\lfloor q_n/2\rfloor\).
Its exponent B can be fixed, for example B=1, independently of the eventual fine precision.
The allowed source exception exponent can also be fixed, since \(\lfloor q_n/2\rfloor\) lies between \(q_n^{\epsilon_{\rm src}}\) and \(q_n\) for any fixed \(0<\epsilon_{\rm src}<1\) and large n.
The progression-size bound becomes
\[
 \max\{Cq_n^{-\theta},1\}=1
\]
eventually.
A proper symmetric progression of size one is \(\{0\}\).
Thus at least half of every such subset has \(\|a_j\|\le t_*\).
It follows that at most \(\lfloor q_n/2\rfloor\) coordinates of the full pair have norm greater than \(t_*\).

Round those coordinates to an \(\mathbb R^4\) lattice of mesh \(t_*\), and set every other coordinate to zero.
The possible rounded pairs number at most
\[
 \exp(O_{k,m}(q_n\log n))=\exp(o(n)).
 \tag{TC-degenerate-count}
\]
This covers continuous v directly; no fine grid with \(n^{O(an)}\) cost is used.
The coordinatewise rounding error is \(O(t_*)\) in both columns.
Because (TC-raw-residual) is linear in both columns with entry bounds \(n^k\), the row tolerance increases by at most \(O(n^{k+1}t_*)=O(n^{-4})\).
The largest u-coordinate survives.
The fixed-pair triangular bound and (TC-degenerate-count) therefore give \(e^{-cn}\) for existence of such a degenerate feasible pair, whenever the original raw row tolerance is smaller than \(n^{-4}\).
The omission choices are only polynomially many.
This is a uniform upper bound for a genuine subclass of the actual event, with the original companion equation still supplying each feasible pair.

<a id="high-joint-product-count"></a>
## A fixed high joint-product class also compresses both columns

For a nondegenerate pair select a \(q_n\)-subset with joint concentration below \(q_n^{-1/2+\theta}\), add a largest u-coordinate if needed, and put this reservoir last.
Its size is \(q_0\in\{q_n,q_n+1\}\).
The reservoir and prescribed permutation choices cost \(\exp(O(q_n\log n))=\exp(o(n))\).
Let \(p=n-q_0\).
For each fixed such ordering, every tail starting at \(i\le p\) contains the reservoir, so
\[
 \rho_i(\mathbf a,t)\le n^{-b_{\rm tail}},\qquad
 b_{\rm tail}=1/2-3\varepsilon/4,
 \qquad t\le t_*.
 \tag{TC-long-tail-upper}
\]
The ordering can change the product; no earlier product classification is transported across a permutation.

Fix a constant \(C_1>0\) before choosing the integer fine profile precision s.
The subclass considered here satisfies, in the ordering actually used,
\[
 \pi_{\beta}(\mathbf a):=\prod_{i=1}^p\rho_i(\mathbf a,\beta)
       \ge n^{-C_1n},\qquad \beta=n^{-s}.
 \tag{TC-high-product-class}
\]
Take
\[
 R_n=n^{k+3},\qquad t_\ell=R_n^\ell\beta,\qquad
 L_1=\lceil C_1/\varepsilon\rceil+2.
\]
Choose s after \(C_1,k,m,\varepsilon\), large enough that
\[
 s\ge(k+3)(L_1+1)+k+5,
 \quad\hbox{so}\quad t_{L_1+1}\le t_*.
 \tag{TC-available-scale-range}
\]
At some \(0\le\ell\le L_1\),
\[
 \pi_{t_{\ell+1}}(\mathbf a)
       \le n^{\varepsilon n}\pi_{t_\ell}(\mathbf a),
 \tag{TC-stable-scale}
\]
since otherwise iteration from (TC-high-product-class) would exceed one.

Partition the possible \(\rho_i(\mathbf a,t_\ell)\) into bins
\([r_h,n^\varepsilon r_h)\) between \(n^{-D_s}\) and \(n^{-b_{\rm tail}}\).
There are \(H=O_{s,k,m}(1)\) bins and at most \((n+1)^H\) monotone profiles.
At the first index of each block apply the same real-four-dimensional inverse theorem to the whole corresponding tail with exception count
\[
 n'=\lfloor n^{1-2\varepsilon}\rfloor.
\]
Each tail has length at least \(q_n\), so this is within the source's permitted range.
Its polynomial concentration exponent is fixed after s is fixed.
The normalized-tail and rational-generator calculation above applies, including when v has polynomial norm.
Both coordinates of all but \(n'\) pairs in the block are approximated together within \(t_\ell\) by a single proper symmetric progression \(Q_h\subset\mathbb R^4\), with
\[
 |Q_h|\le C r_h^{-1}n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}.
 \tag{TC-joint-progression-size}
\]
The maximum with one is harmless by (TC-long-tail-upper).
The inverse theorem therefore does compress v as well as u; it is not merely a theorem for the first column.

The rank and generator choices contribute only a polynomial number of descriptors for fixed s.
Round the remaining \(Hn'+q_0=o(n)\) coordinate pairs on a four-real-coordinate \(t_\ell\) lattice.
Their positions and values cost \(\exp(o(n))\), since their norm is polynomially bounded.
The number of rounded pairs in a fixed profile is at most
\[
 \exp(O_s(n)+o(n))\,
 n^{-(1/2-\varepsilon)p}\prod_h r_h^{-m_h}.
 \tag{TC-joint-profile-count}
\]
This construction is finite even if the initial pair class is continuous.
For each rounded image choose one original witness from the deterministic profile class, before imposing any random row event.
Neither that witness nor the descriptor choice is selected from an observed R.

A feasible original pair gives row tolerance at most
\(t'=\beta+Cn^{k+1}t_\ell\) for its rounded image.
Its actual deterministic shift terms change by the same polynomially controlled error.
For every tail the rounded coefficient sum differs from its witness by at most \(Cn^{k+1}t_\ell\), hence
\[
 \rho_i(\mathbf a',t')\le\rho_i(\mathbf a,t_{\ell+1}).
\]
Use (TC-triangular-product), discard the last \(q_0\) factors, then use (TC-stable-scale) and the bin upper endpoints.
The probability of a rounded feasible pair is at most
\[
 n^{O_s(1)+2\varepsilon n}\prod_h r_h^{m_h}.
 \tag{TC-joint-profile-probability}
\]
Multiplying (TC-joint-profile-count) and (TC-joint-profile-probability) cancels the concentration factors and gives
\[
 \exp(O_s(n)+o(n))\,
 n^{-(1/2-\varepsilon)p+2\varepsilon n}\le n^{-n/3}
\]
for large n.
The bounded scale choices, polynomial profiles and \(\exp(o(n))\) reservoir choices preserve a bound \(n^{-cn}\).
The original raw residual must be at most \(\beta\), which is achieved by choosing \(a\) sufficiently large after s.
No initial unrestricted fine net for v is used in this high-product subclass.

These two subclass estimates use only u's norm and its resulting large coordinate, without a finite-family entropy bound.
The low-product class instead uses exactly the finite-family condition (SP-family).
The complementary nondegenerate low-product class is treated by the outside recovery and original-rectangle argument above.

## Parameter order and exhaustive probability assembly

Fix the original laws, the nonreal z, q, the guard exponents \(\gamma_F,k_0,L\), the omission bound \(C_0\) and the leading entropy coefficient \(C_*\).
First choose the original B,C norm cutoff \(b_0>(q+1)/2\), the skew-bulk constants \(\eta,L_K\), and the raw first-input cutoff \(\kappa>(q+2)/2\).

Choose
\[
 L_C=L_K+k_0+2,\qquad E_v=Q_0+L_C+L_0+k_0+2.
\]

 C_1>6M^2(C_*+1),\qquad \varepsilon=1/100,\qquad
 s\ge(k+3)(\lceil C_1/\varepsilon\rceil+3)+k+5.
\]

\[
 a_*>s+E_v+\kappa+k_0+10.
 \tag{SP-fine-precision}
\]
For every fixed \(a\ge a_*\), the raw original residual is below \(n^{-s}\), the degenerate rounding tolerance is valid, and both profile-transfer and row-transfer errors are below \(\gamma_N=n^{-s}/(4M)\).
