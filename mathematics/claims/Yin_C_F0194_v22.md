# Structured companion counts under a polynomial skew bulk

This project deduction for Yin extracts a deterministic-shift count from the mechanism of [C194v21](Yin_C_F0194_v21.md#argument).
The earlier [reviewed decision](Yin_C_F0194_v21.md#decision-structured-companion-reviewed) concerns its actual three-factor inverse-product shift only.
It is not invoked as an already proved theorem for arbitrary deterministic shifts.
Here the required skew bulk is an explicit hypothesis, and all uniformity and original-law exposure steps are supplied at that larger scope.
A separate free-polynomial calculation verifies this hypothesis for an independent polynomial background with nonzero reversal-skew part.

The [selected assessment](#decision-skew-bulk-count-qualified-reviewed) adopts the complete statement after independent re-review of the corrected additive-floor assembly.
The generic count, independent-background integration and polynomial skew-bulk argument retain their unaffected [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v25.md#generic-skew-bulk-and-additive-whole-proof).

Logarithmic, Brown and outer-polynomial conclusions are not part of this statement.

## Exact deterministic-shift statement

Fix a real off-diagonal law \(\xi\) with mean zero and variance one and a real diagonal law \(\zeta\) with mean zero and finite second moment.
For \(N\ge3\), put \(n=N-1\), and let \(X_N\in M_n(\mathbb R)\) be symmetric with independent upper coordinates, having laws \(\xi/\sqrt N\) off the diagonal and \(\zeta/\sqrt N\) on the diagonal.
This is the exact normalization of a principal minor of the original size-\(N\) input.

Fix \(q>0\), \(\gamma,k_0,L,L_K\ge1\), \(0<\eta\le1/4\), an integer \(C_0\ge0\), and \(C_*\ge0\).
Let \(F_N\in M_n(\mathbb C)\) be any deterministic matrix satisfying
\[
 \|F_N\|\le N^\gamma,\qquad
 K_N=\tfrac12(F_N-F_N^T),\qquad
 \#\{j:s_j(K_N)\ge N^{-L_K}\}\ge\eta n.
 \tag{GB-shift-hypotheses}
\]
Transposition is algebraic transposition, with no coefficient conjugation.
Put
\[
 S_N=X_N+\tfrac12(F_N+F_N^T).
\]
For each positive integer \(a\), let \(\mathcal U_N(a;F_N)\subset\mathbb C^n\) be a deterministic finite family with
\[
 \tfrac12\le\|u\|\le2,\qquad
 |\mathcal U_N(a;F_N)|\le n^{C_*n+r_a(n)},
 \qquad r_a(n)=o(n).
 \tag{GB-family}
\]
For each fixed \(a\), the remainder is deterministic and uniform over the admitted shifts and families.
The leading coefficient \(C_*\) is independent of \(a\).
The families cannot depend on the original first-input coordinates in \(X_N\).

Let
\[
 \mathcal G_N=
 \{S_N\text{ invertible},\
   \max(\|S_N\|,\|K_N\|)\le N^{k_0},\
   \|S_N^{-1}\|\le N^L\},\qquad Q_0=2k_0+L.
\]
There is an integer \(a_*\), depending only on the fixed laws and displayed fixed parameters, such that for every fixed integer \(a\ge a_*\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{\mathcal G_N,\ &
  \exists u\in\mathcal U_N(a;F_N),\
  \exists E\subset[n],\ |E|\le C_0,\\
 &v=S_N^{-1}K_Nu,\quad
 |(S_Nu-K_Nv)_j|\le N^{-a+Q_0}\quad(j\notin E)\}\\
 &\le C_qN^{-q}+Ce^{-cN}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{GB-pair-count}
\]
The constants are uniform in the admitted deterministic shifts and families.
The sufficiently large lower bound on \(N\) may depend on the fixed chosen \(a\) and its prescribed remainder \(r_a\).
The companion equation \(S_Nv-K_Nu=0\) holds on every row, including \(E\).
Only the other equation may omit a bounded number of rows.
No estimate on \(\mathcal G_N^c\) or on the least singular value of \(X_N+F_N\) is part of this statement.

## Independent random backgrounds

The same estimate applies conditionally on a background sigma-field \(\mathscr Y_N\) independent of the complete \(X_N\), when \(F_N\) and the finite candidate families are \(\mathscr Y_N\)-measurable.
Intersect the event with the \(\mathscr Y_N\)-measurable shift-and-bulk conditions (GB-shift-hypotheses).
Uniformity in the deterministic data permits integration over this background.
If the shift-and-bulk conditions are removed from the event, their complementary probability must be added.
Independence only of \(F_N\) is insufficient if the family is allowed to depend on additional background data correlated with \(X_N\).

## Specialization to independent polynomial shifts

Let \(W_1^{(N)},\ldots,W_d^{(N)}\) be independent original real Wigner inputs within each dimension, with fixed centered variance-one off-diagonal laws and centered finite-second-moment diagonal laws.
Their cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
Fix \(Q\in\mathbb C\langle x_2,\ldots,x_d\rangle\).
Reverse each word without conjugating its coefficient to define \(Q^{\mathrm{rev}}\), and set
\[
 T=\tfrac12(Q-Q^{\mathrm{rev}}).
\]
For this specialization assume \(T\ne0\) as a formal polynomial.
For every fixed \(z\in\mathbb C\) and outer index \(i\), set
\[
 X_i=(W_1^{(N)})_{\widehat i,\widehat i},\qquad
 F_i=(Q(W_2^{(N)},\ldots,W_d^{(N)})-zI_N)_{\widehat i,\widehat i},
 \qquad
 S_i=X_i+\operatorname{sym}F_i,\quad K_i=\operatorname{skew}F_i.
 \tag{GB-polynomial-model}
\]
Then (GB-pair-count) holds uniformly in \(i\) on the corresponding \(S_i,K_i,S_i^{-1}\) guards, for any background-measurable finite families with (GB-family).
For each prescribed \(q\), the polynomial norm exponent \(\gamma\) can be chosen before \(a\), and the removed shift-and-bulk failures cost \(O(N^{-q})+Ce^{-cN}\).
The norm guard, the exact companion and the finite-family conditions are retained.
Repeated variables inside \(Q\) are allowed.

This assertion includes every fixed real or nonreal \(z\), including zero.
The reason is the exact identity \(K_i=T(W_2,\ldots,W_d)_{\widehat i,\widehat i}\), independent of \(z\), together with the complete deterministic-shift proof below.
It is not an extension inferred solely from the corresponding identity in C194v21.

If \(T=0\), the full shift \(Q(W_2,\ldots,W_d)-zI\) is complex symmetric.
C188v2 then directly supplies the previously accepted arbitrary-power floor for \(W_1+Q-zI\) on its polynomial shift guard.
That branch does not satisfy the present positive-bulk hypothesis and is not passed through (GB-pair-count).

<a id="additive-floor-statement"></a>
## Least-value floor for one exposed additive input

In the same original real fixed-law model, let \(Q\in\mathbb C\langle x_2,\ldots,x_d\rangle\) be arbitrary, including repeated variables and either reversal branch.
For every fixed \(z\in\mathbb C\) and \(b>0\), there exist \(A,C,N_0>0\), depending only on the fixed laws, \(Q,z,b\), such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(W_1^{(N)}+Q(W_2^{(N)},\ldots,W_d^{(N)})-zI_N)
                    \le N^{-A}\}
       \le C N^{-b}\qquad(N\ge N_0).
 \tag{GB-additive-floor}
\]
This is a consequence of the new count, with its complete original-law assembly below.
It includes all fixed real and nonreal parameters, with constants permitted to depend on the parameter.
It requires that the entire background omit the distinguished first input.
It does not assert an arbitrary scalar-polynomial floor or a theorem for a general matrix-coefficient pencil.

## Complete generic count argument

Suppress \(N\) in \(F,S,K\), put \(\delta_N=N^{-a+Q_0}\), and restrict the event to \(\mathcal G_N\).
Every actual companion then satisfies
\[
 \|v\|\le2N^{L+k_0}.
 \tag{GB-companion-bound}
\]
The fixed shift is independent of every original first-input coordinate.
Neither the full \(S\) norm guard nor the existence of a feasible pair is imposed as a conditional law on unexposed entries.

<a id="generic-identification"></a>
### A common outside identification event

Choose \(M=\lceil32/\eta\rceil\) and partition \([n]\) into \(M\) deterministic balanced blocks.
For each ordered pair of distinct blocks, put \(I=P_\ell\cup P_h\), \(r=|I|\), \(J=I^c\), \(p=|J|\).
For large \(N\), \(r\le\eta n/8\), and each single block has size at least \(n/(2M)\).
Only a fixed number of block pairs is used.

Condition on \(X_{JJ}\), leaving \(X_{II}\) and the cross block unexposed, and write
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}H&Y^T\\Y&S_0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}K_I&k^T\\-k&K_0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 u=\binom{\alpha}{w},\quad v=\binom{t}{v_J}.
\]
Here \(Y=N^{-1/2}V+Y_0\), with \(pr\) independent original real coordinates in \(V\).
The mirrored block is \(Y^T\), not an independent copy.
The entire block \(X_{II}\) remains independent and unexposed.

C188v2 gives \(\|G\|\le N^{L_{\rm int}}\), \(G=S_0^{-1}\), outside probability \(O(N^{-q})\), uniformly for these finitely many \(J\).
Indeed \(X_{JJ}=\sqrt{p/N}\,W_p\), with \(p/N\) bounded away from zero, and the independent complex-symmetric shift has a fixed polynomial entry bound.
Choose the inverse exponent before the final precision.
On the counted full guard, \(\|S_0\|\le N^{k_0}\) and \(\|K\|\le N^{k_0}\).
Those particular internal-block restrictions are measurable after the current conditioning.
If they fail, the original counted event is empty.

Deleting \(r\) rows and columns from \(K\) loses at most \(2r\) singular values above a fixed threshold.
This follows by padding the retained block with zeros: the difference has rank at most \(2r\).
Thus \(K_0\) has at least \(\eta n-2r\) singular values at least \(N^{-L_K}\).
The matrix \(A_0=K_0G\) has that many singular values at least \(\lambda_N=N^{-L_K-k_0}\), because \(s_{\min}(G)=\|S_0\|^{-1}\).

For \(E_J\subset J\), \(|E_J|=e\le C_0\), let \(\Pi\) restrict to \(J\setminus E_J\), and define
\[
 C_E=\Pi(A_0Y+k).
 \tag{GB-identification-matrix}
\]
Its random columns depend on disjoint original cross-block coordinates.
The matrix \(\Pi A_0\) has at least \(d_E=\lfloor\eta n\rfloor-2r-e\) singular values at least \(\lambda_N\).
Condition on its other random columns.
The span of those columns has dimension at most \(r-1\).
Projection to a good left singular subspace, inversion of its retained singular values and pullback to the right singular subspace leave complex codimension at least
\[
 d_E-(r-1)\ge\eta n/2
\]
for large \(N\).

The fixed-cutoff argument of C194v3 gives, for every fixed \(\theta>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\operatorname{dist}(\xi+f,V)\le c_\theta\sqrt p\}
       \le C_\theta e^{-c_\theta p}
 \tag{GB-fractional-distance}
\]
uniformly in complex subspaces \(V\) of codimension at least \(\theta p\) and deterministic complex shifts \(f\), for an original real iid vector \(\xi\).
For this constant adjustment, choose a fixed cutoff with conditional variance at least \(1/2\) and with tail probability small enough that more than \(\theta p/4\) discarded coordinates has exponentially small probability.
Project away the discarded coordinates and adjoin the deterministic mean and shift.
The remaining codimension is at least \(\theta p/2\), and the squared projected norm has expectation at least \(\theta p/4\).
The accepted bounded-product convex concentration and median argument then give the displayed bound, integrating the cutoff coordinates back into the original law.
No real-subspace or isotropic complex-scalar hypothesis is inserted.

The singular-subspace transformation lower bounds each column distance in (GB-identification-matrix) by \(\lambda_NN^{-1/2}\operatorname{dist}(\xi+f,V)\).
Consequently every column has distance at least \(c\lambda_N\) from the span of the others except on an exponential event.
For a unit vector \(t\), choose \(|t_j|\ge r^{-1/2}\) and project \(C_Et\) away from the other columns.
A union over columns, the \(O(N^{C_0})\) omission sets and the fixed block family gives the common event
\[
 s_{\min}(C_E)\ge N^{-L_C},\qquad L_C=L_K+k_0+2,
 \tag{GB-identification-floor}
\]
outside probability \(Ce^{-cN}\).
It is common to all \(u\), since \(C_E\) does not depend on \(u\).
This is proved for the original cross-block law before any first-input polynomial cutoff.

Expose \(Y\) after obtaining this probability estimate.
All outside data, \(C_E\), and its full-column-rank Moore--Penrose inverse are then fixed, while \(X_{II}\) is still independent and unexposed.

### Exact companion recovery without a companion net

For fixed \(u,E\) and a block pair define, from the exposed outside,
\[
 b=G(-k\alpha+K_0w),\qquad c=S_0w-K_0b.
\]
The exact outside companion equation and the other outside equation are
\[
 v_J=b-GYt,\qquad
 (Su-Kv)_J=Y\alpha+c+(A_0Y+k)t.
\]
The latter has residual \(\zeta\) of norm at most \(\sqrt N\delta_N\) on the retained outside rows.
Set
\[
 t_*=-C_E^\dagger\Pi(Y\alpha+c),\qquad
 v_*=\binom{t_*}{b-GYt_*}.
 \tag{GB-outside-reconstruction}
\]
Every actual feasible pair satisfies
\[
 t-t_*=C_E^\dagger\zeta,\qquad
 \|v-v_*\|\le N^{-a+E_v},\qquad
 E_v=Q_0+L_C+L_{\rm int}+k_0+2.
 \tag{GB-recovery-error}
\]
Here \(\|Y\|\le N^{k_0}\) follows on the original full norm guard.
It bounds the error on that event and does not condition the law of the remaining block.

There is exactly one reconstructed vector for each \(u,E\) and ordered block pair after the outside is exposed.
No grid for \(v\) or its \(r\) interior coordinates is used.
The actual outside center is retained in (GB-outside-reconstruction).
An arbitrary reconstructed pair need not be feasible; the necessary-event implication from an actual pair to its close reconstruction is the direction used.

Choose a fixed integer \(\kappa>(q+2)/2\).
The probability that any raw coordinate of \(X_N\) exceeds \(N^\kappa\) is \(O(N^{2-2\kappa})=O(N^{-q})\), by the original second moments.
Conditional on these coordinatewise independent cutoffs, the upper off-diagonal law is \(\xi_n=(\xi\mid|\xi|\le N^\kappa)\).
It remains iid and satisfies the source nondegeneracy condition uniformly in \(N\), by total variation convergence to the fixed nondegenerate law.
The diagonal cutoffs likewise preserve independence.
No centering or higher moment is introduced.
The identification and internal-inverse failures were already estimated under the original law and are paid separately; they are not re-estimated by conditioning on this cutoff event.

<a id="generic-joint-profiles"></a>
### Original-law joint profiles and the degenerate class

The following complete profile estimates use only fixed complex shift entries, the original real scalar law and the polynomial companion norm.
They make explicit the uniform content of the corresponding mechanism in C194v21.

Pass to raw variables
\[
 R=\sqrt N X_N,\qquad \mathsf F=\sqrt N\operatorname{sym}F_N,
 \qquad \mathsf K=\sqrt N K_N,
\]
and paired row residuals
\[
 \mathcal L_j(u,v)=
 ((Ru+\mathsf Fu-\mathsf Kv)_j,\
  (Rv+\mathsf Fv-\mathsf Ku)_j)\in\mathbb C^2.
 \tag{GB-raw-pair}
\]
On the original event the second component is zero on every row.
For an upper bound one may omit both constraints on the bounded set \(E\).
Condition on the independent diagonal values after the cutoff.
Choose fixed integers \(k,m\ge1\), before all fine precisions, such that the raw entries and both deterministic shifts have modulus at most \(n^k\), and \(\|v\|\le n^m\).
All subsequent bounds are uniform in those fixed diagonal and shift values.

For a deterministic pair put \(a_j=(u_j,v_j)\in\mathbb C^2=\mathbb R^4\) and
\[
 \rho_i(\mathbf a,t)=
 \sup_{y\in\mathbb C^2}
 \mathbb P\left\{\left\|\sum_{j=i}^n a_j\xi_{n,j}-y\right\|\le t\right\}.
\]
One original real scalar multiplies each vector coefficient.
No pair of independently sampled scalar rows replaces this form.
The tail concentrations are nondecreasing in \(i\).

For a nonzero tail of norm \(\sigma\), apply the real-four-dimensional linear inverse theorem to \(a_j/\sigma\) at radius \(t/\sigma\).
Its concentration is unchanged.
The coefficient norm is at most \(2+n^m\); zero tails have concentration one.
In the rational-generator clause, rescaling a generator \((t/\sigma)p_{ij}/p\) returns \(tp_{ij}/p\).
Thus the variable normalization cancels, leaving polynomially many rational descriptors at a fixed polynomial precision.
This is the same rational-coordinate input retained in C194v1, now with its ambient dimension explicitly four.

For \(t\ge n^{-s}\), covering the support ball of polynomial radius gives \(\rho_i(\mathbf a,t)\ge n^{-D_s}\), with \(D_s=4(s+k+m+3)+1\).
After a deterministic simultaneous row-column permutation, expose the original upper coordinates in increasing row order.
The fresh part at row \(i\) is \(\sum_{j>i}a_j\xi_{n,ij}\); all other terms, including the actual two centers, are fixed.
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\mathcal L_i(u,v)\|\le t
                   \text{ on prescribed }n-C_0\text{ rows}\}
 \le n^{D_s(C_0+1)}\prod_{i=1}^n\rho_i(\mathbf a,t).
 \tag{GB-triangular-product}
\]
The prefactor pays for the one missing diagonal factor and the bounded omitted rows.
This is one lawful triangular exposure, not a product of overlapping whole-row conditionings.

Place a largest \(u\)-coordinate last, with \(|u_n|\ge(2\sqrt n)^{-1}\).
Each tail then has concentration at most a fixed \(p_0<1\) for \(t\le c/\sqrt n\), by the fixed real-law anti-concentration condition and projection onto this coefficient direction.
This supplies an exponential probability for a fixed pair, but does not alone count a continuous companion family.

Fix \(\epsilon=1/100\), \(q_n=\lceil n^{1-\epsilon}\rceil\), \(\theta=\epsilon/10\), and \(t_*=n^{-k-5}\).
Call a pair degenerate if every \(q_n\)-coordinate subset has joint concentration at least \(q_n^{-1/2+\theta}\) at radius \(t_*\).
Apply the inverse theorem on each subset with \(\lfloor q_n/2\rfloor\) exceptions.
After the normalization above, its proper symmetric progression has size \(\max\{Cq_n^{-\theta},1\}=1\), hence is \(\{0\}\), for large \(n\).
At least half the coefficients of every such subset have norm at most \(t_*\).
Therefore at most \(\lfloor q_n/2\rfloor\) coefficients of the full pair have norm greater than \(t_*\).

Round those exceptional pairs on an \(\mathbb R^4\) lattice of mesh \(t_*\), and set the others to zero.
Their positions and values have at most \(\exp(O_{k,m}(q_n\log n))=\exp(o(n))\) possibilities.
The coordinatewise error is \(O(t_*)\); (GB-raw-pair) changes by at most \(O(n^{k+1}t_*)=O(n^{-4})\) per row, and the large \(u\)-coordinate survives.
The fixed-pair exponential bound in (GB-triangular-product) then gives \(e^{-cn}\) for the degenerate feasible class, once the original raw residual is smaller than \(n^{-4}\).
This counts both columns directly and costs no precision-\(a\) net for the companion.

<a id="generic-high-product"></a>
### The nondegenerate high joint-product class

For a nondegenerate pair choose a \(q_n\)-subset of concentration below \(q_n^{-1/2+\theta}\), add a largest \(u\)-coordinate if necessary, and place this reservoir last.
The reservoir has \(q_0\in\{q_n,q_n+1\}\) coordinates.
Its choices and a prescribed permutation cost \(\exp(O(q_n\log n))=\exp(o(n))\).
Put \(p'=n-q_0\).
For all tails starting at \(i\le p'\),
\[
 \rho_i(\mathbf a,t)\le n^{-b_{\rm tail}},
 \qquad b_{\rm tail}=1/2-3\epsilon/4,\qquad t\le t_*.
\]
The strict exponent slack follows from \((1-\epsilon)(1/2-\theta)>1/2-3\epsilon/4\).

Fix \(C_1>0\) before choosing the integer profile precision \(s\).
For each actual selected ordering, first consider
\[
 \pi_\beta(\mathbf a)=\prod_{i=1}^{p'}\rho_i(\mathbf a,\beta)
       \ge n^{-C_1n},\qquad\beta=n^{-s}.
 \tag{GB-high-product}
\]
Use \(R_n=n^{k+3}\), \(t_j=R_n^j\beta\) and \(L_1=\lceil C_1/\epsilon\rceil+2\).
Choose \(s\ge(k+3)(L_1+1)+k+5\), so \(t_{L_1+1}\le t_*\).
For some \(0\le j\le L_1\),
\[
 \pi_{t_{j+1}}(\mathbf a)\le n^{\epsilon n}\pi_{t_j}(\mathbf a);
\]
otherwise iteration from (GB-high-product) exceeds one.

Bin the possible concentrations at \(t_j\) into \([r_h,n^\epsilon r_h)\), between \(n^{-D_s}\) and \(n^{-b_{\rm tail}}\).
There are \(O_{s,k,m}(1)\) bins and polynomially many monotone profiles.
At the first index of each bin block, apply the four-dimensional inverse theorem to the corresponding entire tail with \(n'=\lfloor n^{1-2\epsilon}\rfloor\) exceptions.
Each tail has at least \(q_n\) entries, so the source exception range is satisfied.
The proper symmetric progression has
\[
 |Q_h|\le C r_h^{-1}n^{-1/2+\epsilon}.
\]
The maximum with one is harmless by the strict tail exponent margin.

Rational generator descriptors cost polynomially many choices for fixed \(s\).
The \(O_s(n')+q_0=o(n)\) exceptional pairs, their locations and their coarse values cost \(\exp(o(n))\).
For a fixed profile, the rounded-pair count is at most
\[
 \exp(O_s(n)+o(n))\
 n^{-(1/2-\epsilon)p'}\prod_h r_h^{-m_h},
 \tag{GB-profile-entropy}
\]
where \(m_h\) is the number of coordinates in that bin block.
A deterministic original profile witness may be chosen for each rounded image before any random row feasibility event is tested.
This is a finite cover even when \(v\) ranges continuously.

A feasible original pair has rounded row tolerance \(t'=\beta+Cn^{k+1}t_j\).
The raw upper coordinates and deterministic shift terms obey the same entry bound, so both the coefficient-sum error and center error are absorbed by \(t_{j+1}\).
Hence the rounded tail concentration at \(t'\) is at most the original witness's concentration at \(t_{j+1}\).
Using (GB-triangular-product), discarding the reservoir's last factors, the stable scale and the bin endpoints gives probability at most
\[
 n^{O_s(1)+2\epsilon n}\prod_h r_h^{m_h}
\]
for each such rounded pair.
Multiplication by (GB-profile-entropy) cancels the concentration factors and leaves
\[
 \exp(O_s(n)+o(n))\
 n^{-(1/2-\epsilon)p'+2\epsilon n}\le n^{-n/3}
\]
for large \(n\).
The scale, profile and reservoir unions preserve \(n^{-cn}\).
The original raw residual is at most \(\beta\) when \(a\) is chosen sufficiently large after \(s\).
The high-product test is made in the ordering actually used; it is never transported across a permutation.

### Low global concentration and one fresh original rectangle

For any selected ordering, each tail concentration is at least the full concentration \(\rho_{\rm all}(\mathbf a,\beta)\).
Thus the complementary low prefix product implies
\[
 \rho_{\rm all}(\mathbf a,\beta)<n^{-C_1n/p'}\le n^{-C_1}.
 \tag{GB-low-global}
\]
It suffices to count this larger low-global-concentration event, without retaining its earlier reservoir ordering.

For the fixed partition into \(M\) blocks, independence of disjoint scalar coordinates gives
\[
 \rho_{\rm all}(\mathbf a,\beta)
 \ge\prod_{\ell=1}^{M}\rho_{P_\ell}(\mathbf a,\beta/M).
\]
Take approximating maximizing centers and apply the triangle inequality, then take suprema, to justify the display without requiring an attained maximizing center.
Some block \(R=P_\ell\) therefore satisfies
\[
 \rho_R(\mathbf a,\beta/M)<n^{-C_1/M}.
\]
Choose a distinct block \(T=P_h\) and use \(I=R\cup T\) in the outside reconstruction.
There are only finitely many ordered choices, all from the predetermined partition.

The concentration of the actual pair must be transferred to its outside-measurable reconstruction.
For every realization of the cutoff scalars,
\[
 \left\|\sum_{j\in R}(0,v_j-v_{*,j})\xi_{n,j}\right\|
 \le N^{\kappa+1/2}\|v-v_*\|
 \le N^{-a+E_v+\kappa+1/2}.
 \tag{GB-profile-transfer}
\]

## Polynomial background: a symmetric branch or a genuine skew bulk

For real symmetric substitution, the exact identity is
\[
 Q(W_2,\ldots,W_d)^T=Q^{\mathrm{rev}}(W_2,\ldots,W_d).
\]
Complex coefficients are unchanged under this transposition.
Therefore the skew part of \(Q(W_2,\ldots,W_d)-zI\) is \(T(W_2,\ldots,W_d)\), for every complex \(z\).

If \(T=0\), the shift is complex symmetric and independent of \(W_1\).
For every requested failure power, \(\mathbb E\|W_j^{(N)}\|_F^2=O(N)\) gives a polynomial norm guard for the finitely many background inputs with that failure power.
The fixed finite word expansion of \(Q\) then gives a polynomial upper norm bound for \(Q-zI\).
C188v2 applies on this guard, including the original diagonal law, and the guard failure is added after integrating.
No restriction on repeated background variables, complex coefficients or \(z\) is used in this symmetric branch.

Suppose \(T\ne0\), and let \(h\) be its highest nonzero formal degree.
In the accepted full Fock model \(s_j=\ell_j+\ell_j^*\), the length-\(h\) component of \(T(s)\Omega\) is exactly the nonzero combination of word vectors with its degree-\(h\) coefficients.
Any annihilation lowers the resulting length, and shorter monomials cannot contribute to that component.
Thus for \(t=T(s_2,\ldots,s_d)\),
\[
 \tau(t^*t)=\|t\Omega\|^2>0.
 \tag{GB-free-nonzero}
\]
This is a bounded free calculation, not an assertion about finite-\(N\) high trace moments.

Choose \(a_{\rm bulk}>0\) and a continuous bounded-variation function \(0\le f\le1\), vanishing on \([0,a_{\rm bulk}^2]\), with \(d_0=\tau(f(t^*t))>0\).
Such a function exists by (GB-free-nonzero) and positivity of \(t^*t\).
Apply the C186v4 expected weak theorem to the fixed self-adjoint polynomial \(T^*T\).
It gives
\[
 \mathbb E\,N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr} f(T(W)^*T(W))\longrightarrow d_0.
\]
The same theorem's bounded-variation row concentration gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}f(T(W)^*T(W))<d_0/2\}
       \le Ce^{-cN}
\]
eventually, since the expectation is at least \(3d_0/4\).
The degree and the number of occurrences of any background variable affect only fixed rank-change constants.
No untruncated moment of \(T(W)^*T(W)\) is used.

Consequently at least \(d_0N/2\) singular values of the full \(T(W)\) exceed \(a_{\rm bulk}\), outside this exponential event.
Every outer principal deletion loses at most two such values, by the rank argument above.
Choose a fixed \(0<\eta\le\min(d_0/4,1/4)\).
Uniformly for all outer indices, eventually \(K_i\) has at least \(\eta n\) singular values at least \(N^{-1}\).
Thus \(L_K=1\) is sufficient for the deterministic count.
The event is common to all outer indices and incurs no index-union loss.

For any prescribed \(q\), choose a background norm exponent \(b_0>(q+1)/2\).
A finite union and \(\mathbb E\|W_j\|_F^2=O(N)\) give background norm failure \(O(N^{-q})\).
The fixed word expansion of \(Q\), including its complex coefficients and the fixed scalar \(z\), then gives \(\|Q(W)-zI\|\le N^\gamma\) for a fixed \(\gamma\) chosen before any fine precision.
Its principal blocks obey the same bound.
The full background is independent of every \(X_i\), so the random-background version applies.
Removing these common shift-and-bulk guards proves the polynomial guarded-pair specialization, uniformly in \(i\), with failure \(O(N^{-q})+Ce^{-cN}\).
This proves the all-fixed-\(z\) scope of that specialization using the whole generic count, not only the fact that the skew polynomial is independent of \(z\).

 \tag{GB-complete-numerator}

## Adopted scope and remaining boundaries

The generic statement is a count under a polynomial singular-value bulk of a deterministic complex skew part.
It does not cover every deterministic nonsymmetric shift: shifts without such a bulk remain outside it.
For an independent polynomial background, formal reversal gives an exhaustive dichotomy between a complex-symmetric shift and a nonzero skew polynomial with an actual high-probability bulk.
That supplies the all-fixed-parameter floor for \(x_1+Q(x_2,\ldots,x_d)\), including arbitrary repetition of background variables.

The earlier [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v25.md#generic-skew-bulk-and-additive-whole-proof) covered the uniform-shift extraction, polynomial bulk and complete additive-floor assembly.

The argument now retains that event and its probability cost, and the [independent recheck](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0190_v23.md#two-real-classes-repaired-whole-proof) found no remaining gap in the correction or affected additive-floor chain.
The generic count and skew-bulk calculation are unaffected by this finding, and the earlier C194v21 and C186v23 reviews retain their original scopes.

The floor supplies the additive input to the separately adopted [C186v24 logarithmic and planar assembly](Yin_C_F0186_v24.md#decision-two-real-classes-reviewed) with C187v2 and C186v4.
Those logarithmic and Brown conclusions and an outer-polynomial closure are not part of the present statement.
A polynomial with repeated occurrences of every prospective exposed input, or a general matrix-coefficient linearization, is not addressed by the required fresh additive exposure.
