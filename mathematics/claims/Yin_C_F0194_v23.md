# Qualitative smoothing of a diffuse single inverse pole

This project deduction for Yin tests a sufficient probability mechanism in the real positive-frame free-gap branch of [F13](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract).
It replaces a polynomial-rate comparison requirement by qualitative Gaussian smoothing under an explicit positive-mass diffusion hypothesis.
A separate original-law rectangle argument verifies that hypothesis on a fixed operator-norm minor branch.
The actual average coverage of those branches is not asserted.
The complete statement is adopted after [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v26.md#diffuse-single-pole-whole-proof), with its actual-model coverage assumptions retained.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact statements

Use independent real symmetric Wigner inputs with fixed centered variance-one off-diagonal laws and fixed centered finite-second-moment diagonal laws, all normalized by the original size factor \(N^{-1/2}\).
Coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
Let \(A_0,\ldots,A_d\) be fixed real symmetric auxiliary matrices with
\[
 \chi=\inf_{\|a\|=\|b\|=1,\ a,b\in\mathbb C^m}
       \sum_j|a^*A_jb|^2>0,
 \qquad \Lambda=\sum_j\|A_j\|^2.
\]
Set \(L_N=A_0\otimes I_N+\sum_jA_j\otimes W_j^{(N)}\).

<a id="constant-cut-statement"></a>
### Constant-scale cuts in the original real pencil

For every fixed deletion bound \(r\), there are fixed \(a,\delta,c,C>0\) such that, except with probability \(Ce^{-cN}\), every physical deletion \(D\) with \(|D|\le r\) and every set \(J\subset[N]\setminus D\) with \(|J|=\lfloor\delta(N-|D|)\rfloor\) satisfy
\[
 s_{\min}\bigl(L_N[[N]\setminus(D\cup J),J]\bigr)\ge a.
 \tag{Constant-cut}
\]
The same event works after every scalar diagonal shift \(\theta I\), since these cuts do not contain that term.
This statement concerns the original real pencil's zero off-cut deterministic term; no arbitrary off-cut shift is included.
It is a stronger scale in this narrower scope than [C194v17](Yin_C_F0194_v17.md#statement).
It applies in every fixed auxiliary dimension.

<a id="diffuse-single-pole-statement"></a>
### Complete single-pole graph estimate with diffuse mass

For the remaining statements take \(m=3\).
For one actual physical deletion let \(M\) be the retained invertible minor of \(L_N+\theta_NI\), with its original normalization and deterministic \(\theta_N\to0\).
The sigma-field \(\mathcal F\) contains the entire retained original minors and the exposed original diagonal coordinates.
The remaining off-diagonal border is independent of \(\mathcal F\), with its original fixed laws.
Write its physical blocks as
\[
 R_k=N^{-1/2}\sum_j\xi_{j,k}A_j,
 \qquad R=(R_k)_k.
\]
On an \(\mathcal F\)-measurable event \(\mathcal B_N\), assume a measurable spectral decomposition
\[
 Z=M^{-1}=E+\lambda ee^T,\qquad Ee=0,\quad\|e\|=1,
 \quad |\lambda|\ge c_0N,\quad \|E\|_F^2/N\le K,
 \tag{Diffuse-pole-data}
\]
where \(c_0,K>0\) are fixed, and all objects are real.
Put
\[
 S_E=A_0+\theta_NI_3+D_i-R^TER,\quad
 z=\sqrt N R^Te,\quad
 S=S_E-(\lambda/N)zz^T,
\]
\[
 \Gamma_E=I_3+R^TE^2R,\quad
 \Gamma=\Gamma_E+(\lambda^2/N)zz^T,\quad
 F=\Gamma^{-1/2}S.
 \tag{Diffuse-complete-objects}
\]
The formula for \(\Gamma\) uses \(Ee=0\).
Assume an \(\mathcal F\)-measurable real symmetric \(B\) with
\[
 s_{\min}(B)\ge b,\quad \|B\|\le C_B,\quad
 \mathbb E[\|S_E-B\|\mid\mathcal F]\le q_N\longrightarrow0
 \tag{Diffuse-residual-comparison}
\]
on this event, uniformly in the admitted conditionings.
Here \(b,C_B>0\) are fixed and \(q_N\) is deterministic.
No definiteness of \(B\), sign of \(\lambda\), or upper bound for \(|\lambda|\) is required.

Finally assume an \(\mathcal F\)-measurable physical set \(T_N\) and a deterministic \(\nu_N\to0\) with
\[
 \sum_{k\in T_N}\|e_k\|^2\ge\kappa>0,
 \qquad\max_{k\in T_N}\|e_k\|\le\nu_N,
 \tag{Diffuse-positive-mass}
\]
where \(\kappa\) is fixed.
Then there is a deterministic \(\beta_N\to0\), depending only on the fixed parameters, laws and displayed sequences, such that on \(\mathcal B_N\)
\[
 \mathbb P\{|\det F|\le N^{-3}\mid\mathcal F\}\le\beta_N.
 \tag{Diffuse-graph-determinant}
\]
For each fixed parent norm constant \(B_0\), the actual physical column distance \(d_i=s_{\min}(Q_iC_i)\) consequently obeys
\[
 \mathbb P\{\mathcal B_N,\ d_i\le B_0^{-2}N^{-4},
                \ \|L_N+\theta_NI\|\le B_0\sqrt N\}\le\beta_N.
 \tag{Diffuse-distance}
\]
The parent norm event is an intersection, not part of a conditioning that would change the border law.

<a id="bounded-minor-and-parent-statements"></a>
### Bounded minors and the conditional parent conclusion

On (Constant-cut), the diffusion premise follows whenever \(\|M\|\le B_*\) for a fixed constant \(B_*\), with a fixed \(\kappa>0\) and \(\nu_N=N^{-1/4}\).
Thus the preceding estimate also holds on this bounded-minor branch with its other premises unchanged, after charging the exponentially small rectangle exception.
The original finite-second-moment model does not supply this fixed operator-norm bound with probability tending to one in general.

Suppose, on an infinite deterministic set of dimensions \(\mathcal N\), that every physical index has the above branch with common constants and deterministic error sequences, and
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb P(\mathcal B_{i,N}^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P\{\|L_N+\theta_NI\|>B_0\sqrt N\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Diffuse-mean-coverage}
\]
Then for a fixed \(c_*>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N+\theta_NI)\le c_*N^{-11/2}\}
     \longrightarrow0\qquad(N\in\mathcal N).
 \tag{Diffuse-parent-floor}
\]
If also \(\theta_N=o(N^{-11/2})\), the original unshifted matrix has the same floor with \(c_*/2\).
If zero is outside the spectrum of the free pencil \(L\), these conditions imply
\[
 \frac1{3N}\log|\det L_N|\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}
       \log\Delta_{\operatorname{tr}_3\otimes\tau}(L)
       \quad(N\in\mathcal N),
\]
and the original all-dimension almost-sure limsup equality under every allowed coupling.
No mean-coverage assertion, favorable deterministic set, full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic limit or general F13 completion is supplied by this conditional statement.

The exact statement and applicability are used; its original proof has not received an independent project audit.

The vector adaptation and all uniformity required here are proved below.

The [C194v17 distance average](Yin_C_F0194_v17.md#statement) and [C187v3 middle values](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#statement) are used at their existing adopted scopes.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Complete argument

<a id="constant-cut-argument"></a>
### Original-row L1 control and constant cuts

First consider a real linear combination \(X=\sum_\ell t_\ell\xi_\ell\), where each independent \(\xi_\ell\) has one of the finitely many original laws and \(\sum_\ell t_\ell^2=1\).
Use independent copies \(\xi'_\ell\).
There are fixed \(a_0,p_0>0\) such that \(\mathbb P\{|\xi_j-\xi'_j|\ge a_0\}\ge p_0\) for every input law, since each has variance one.
The symmetric differences have the same joint law as \(\varepsilon_\ell|\xi_\ell-\xi'_\ell|\) with independent fair signs, including at zero.
For deterministic \(b_\ell\), the sign sum \(V=\sum\varepsilon_\ell b_\ell\) has \(\mathbb EV^2=\sum b_\ell^2\) and \(\mathbb EV^4\le3(\sum b_\ell^2)^2\).
Hölder gives \(\mathbb E|V|\ge(\sum b_\ell^2)^{1/2}/\sqrt3\).
With \(U=\sum t_\ell^2\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_\ell-\xi'_\ell|\ge a_0\}}\), one has \(0\le U\le1\) and \(\mathbb EU\ge p_0\), hence \(\mathbb E\sqrt U\ge p_0\).
The triangle inequality and this sign calculation give
\[
 \mathbb E|X|\ge\tfrac12\mathbb E|X-X'|
       \ge a_0p_0/(2\sqrt3).
 \tag{Uniform-original-linear-L1}
\]
No fourth moment of an original entry was used.

Fix a real auxiliary unit vector \(a\) and a cut with \(k\) physical columns and \(l\) physical rows.
Project each output block onto \(a\).
The resulting raw scalar-output rows are independent copies of a vector in \(\mathbb R^{mk}\), with covariance \(I_k\otimes G_a\), where
\[
 G_a=\sum_j A_jaa^TA_j,\qquad \chi I_m\preceq G_a\preceq\Lambda I_m.
\]
Whitening by \(I_k\otimes G_a^{-1/2}\) makes the rows isotropic.
Every unit linear form of a whitened row has original-coordinate coefficient squares summing to one, so (Uniform-original-linear-L1) verifies KM15 uniformly in \(k\).
If \(l\ge c_1mk\), KM15 and the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization imply a lower bound \(c_3\sqrt{\chi l/N}\) for the projected cut and hence for the full cut, except with probability \(2e^{-c_2l}\).
The matrices are real, so their least singular values over real and complex vectors agree.

Choose \(\delta>0\) small enough that \((1-\delta)/(m\delta)>2c_1\) and the support entropy \(H(\delta)\) is less than \(c_2(1-\delta)/2\).
For \(n=N-|D|\), put \(k=\lfloor\delta n\rfloor\), \(l=n-k\).
The \(\binom nk\) supports and the \(O_r(N^r)\) deletion sets then have exponentially small total failure.
Since \(l/N\) is bounded below, this proves (Constant-cut) with fixed \(a>0\).
Each scalar affine diagonal term vanishes on the cut, so no choice of it changes the event.

<a id="gaussian-smoothing"></a>
### Uniform quadratic smoothing from a positive diffuse mass

Split \(z=z_T+h\) by physical indices in \(T_N\) and its complement.
Conditional on \(\mathcal F\), these two vectors are independent and each has second moment at most \(\Lambda\).
The diffuse covariance is
\[
 \Sigma_T=\sum_{k\in T_N,j}A_je_ke_k^TA_j,
 \qquad \chi\kappa I_3\preceq\Sigma_T\preceq\Lambda I_3.
\]
Every individual vector coefficient has norm at most \(C_A\nu_N\).
For each fixed \(t>0\), its Lindeberg tail sum is bounded by
\[
 \Lambda\max_j\mathbb E\bigl[\xi_j^2
       \mathbf1_{\{|\xi_j|>t/(C_A\nu_N)\}}\bigr]\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is uniform in the admitted data.

For completeness, apply the Chi21 swapping argument to a smooth compactly supported function on \(\mathbb R^3\).
Replace the independent vector summands by centered Gaussian vectors with the same covariance, one at a time.
The first and second Taylor terms cancel in expectation; the sum of remainders is bounded by the Hessian modulus at radius \(t\) times the bounded total variance, plus a fixed Hessian bound times the displayed Lindeberg tail.
The Gaussian tail sum also tends to zero, since its summed fourth moments are bounded by a constant times the largest individual coefficient square.
First let \(N\to\infty\), then \(t\downarrow0\).
Second-moment tightness and smooth approximation give weak convergence to the corresponding Gaussian along every subsequence on which \(\Sigma_T\) converges.
Thus this conclusion is uniform in the sequential-compactness sense needed below, without a quantitative convergence rate.

Let \(r_N=N/\lambda\in[-c_0^{-1},c_0^{-1}]\).
For every deterministic \(t_N\to0\),
\[
 \sup\mathbb P\left\{
  \left|r_N\det B-(z_T+h)^T\operatorname{adj}(B)(z_T+h)\right|
       \le t_N\ \middle|\ \mathcal F\right\}\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{Diffuse-quadratic-window}
\]
where the supremum is over all admitted data and the independent remainder's actual law.
To prove this, first discard \(\|h\|>R\) at cost at most \(\Lambda/R^2\), and condition on the remaining value of \(h\).
A contrary sequence has a subsequence on which \(B,r_N,h,\Sigma_T\) all converge, with \(B\) and \(\Sigma_T\) still invertible.
The limiting vector is a nondegenerate Gaussian.
Its displayed quadratic polynomial has no atom at zero: after whitening and orthogonal diagonalization, at least one squared Gaussian coordinate has a nonzero coefficient, so conditioning on the other coordinates leaves at most two roots.
Weak convergence, first to a fixed closed window and then to zero, contradicts the alleged positive small-ball mass.
Finally let \(R\to\infty\).
This proof allows both indefinite \(B\) and both pole signs.

<a id="complete-graph-comparison"></a>
### The same-row comparison and actual graph denominator

The rank-one determinant identity is exact even when \(S_E\) is singular:
\[
 H_N:=(N/\lambda)\det S
       =r_N\det S_E-z^T\operatorname{adj}(S_E)z.
\]
For every fixed \(R\), on \(\|z\|\le R\) and \(\|S_E-B\|\le1\), the difference between \(H_N\) and the polynomial in (Diffuse-quadratic-window) is at most \(C_R\|S_E-B\|\).
Markov's inequality and the uniform residual hypothesis, followed by the second-moment bound on \(z\), show that this difference tends to zero in probability uniformly over admitted conditionings.
No independence between \(z\) and \(S_E\) is used.
Combining with (Diffuse-quadratic-window) gives \(\sup\mathbb P\{|H_N|\le C/N\mid\mathcal F\}\to0\) for each fixed \(C\).

Original second moments give
\[
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_E\mid\mathcal F]\le3+\Lambda K,
 \qquad \mathbb E[\|z\|^2\mid\mathcal F]\le\Lambda.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_E\le N\) and \(\|z\|^2\le N\) fail with probability \(O(N^{-1})\).
On their intersection,
\[
 (N/|\lambda|)^2\det\Gamma
   =r_N^2\det\Gamma_E+N z^T\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma_E)z
   \le c_0^{-2}N^3+N^4\le C N^4.
\]
Therefore \(|\det F|\le N^{-3}\) implies \(|H_N|\le C/N\), proving (Diffuse-graph-determinant).
The graph construction identifies the singular values of \(F\) with those of \(Q_iC_i\), and \(\|F\|\le\|C_i\|\le\|L_N+\theta_NI\|\).
For a three-by-three matrix, \(|\det F|\le s_{\min}(F)\|F\|^2\).
This proves (Diffuse-distance) by integrating the conditional bound and retaining the parent norm as an intersection.

<a id="bounded-minor-diffusion"></a>
### Fixed-norm minors supply the missing diffuse mass

Let \(\|M\|\le B_*\) and suppose (Constant-cut) holds for this minor.
For a unit eigenvector \(e\) with \(\|Me\|=|\lambda|^{-1}\le a/4\), every support \(J\) of the indicated size obeys
\[
 a\|e_J\|\le\|(Me)_{J^c}\|+B_*\|e_{J^c}\|
                \le a/4+B_*\|e_{J^c}\|.
\]
Consequently \(\|e_{J^c}\|\) is at least \(\rho=\min\{1/2,a/(4B_*)\}>0\); a smaller value would make the left side at least \(\sqrt3a/2\) and the right side at most \(a/2\).
The set of blocks with \(\|e_k\|>N^{-1/4}\) has at most \(\sqrt N\) elements and can be extended to such a \(J\).
Its complement \(T_N\) therefore has squared mass at least \(\rho^2\) and maximum block at most \(N^{-1/4}\).
It is minor-measurable for any measurable spectral choice of \(e\).
This verifies (Diffuse-positive-mass); no assertion that all actual minors have bounded norm is made.

<a id="conditional-parent-and-log"></a>
### Averaging and the original determinant objective

Apply the adopted C194v17 distance average to the parent with \(b_N=B_0\sqrt N\).
Choose \(c_*>0\) so that its distance threshold \(8B_0N^{3/2}c_*N^{-11/2}/a_{\rm old}\) is at most \(B_0^{-2}N^{-4}\), where \(a_{\rm old}\) is that theorem's constant.
The probability bound is then the parent norm exception, its exponential rectangle exception, and a fixed multiple of
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P(\mathcal B_{i,N}^c)+\beta_N.
\]
This tends to zero by (Diffuse-mean-coverage), with no extra index-union factor and no conditioning on full-parent events.
This proves (Diffuse-parent-floor).
Weyl's inequality transfers it to the original matrix when \(\theta_N=o(N^{-11/2})\).

For the original unshifted matrix, C187v3 bounds all increasing singular values after \(O(N^{3/4})\) bottom indices below by a fixed multiple of their normalized index, with summable failure.
On the proved probability floor, the bottom negative-log sum divided by \(3N\) is \(O(N^{-1/4}\log N)\).
The middle negative tail is at most \(Ct(1+|\log t|)\), and C186v4 supplies weak convergence and positive-tail control.
Clipping the logarithm and then removing both cutoffs proves probability convergence along \(\mathcal N\).
In a free gap the free target is finite, and the accepted all-dimension upper bound is unchanged.
Choose deterministic \(N_k\in\mathcal N\) with lower-deviation probabilities at tolerance \(1/k\) at most \(2^{-k}\).
First Borel--Cantelli, equivalently the adopted C190v1 criterion, yields the reverse limsup for every cross-size coupling.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Scope and unresolved original-model input

The fixed-mass condition is stronger than having linearly many blocks above a polynomial threshold, as the [original-row example L63v8](../ledger/Yin_L_F0063_v8.md#polynomial-spread-is-not-diffuse-mass) demonstrates.
The present argument controls the complete same-row polynomial without a rate for \(q_N\), for either pole sign and without definite \(B\).
It does not establish that single-pole decompositions with bounded residual energy or diffuse mass cover the free-gap high-energy minors with vanishing mean failure.
Multiple poles and the original arbitrary auxiliary coefficient model remain outside the statement.
The constant-cut theorem does not turn a growing norm guard into fixed positive diffuse mass.
No conclusion about an arbitrary adaptive auxiliary null direction is inferred from a fixed-direction quadratic estimate.
These are explicit conditional probability and narrow actual-model statements, not a completion of F13 or F14.
