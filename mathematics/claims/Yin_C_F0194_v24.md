# Original-model diffuse mass from regularized physical cores

This project deduction for Yin extends the fixed-norm-minor diffusion mechanism of [C194v23](Yin_C_F0194_v23.md#bounded-minor-diffusion) to the original finite-second-moment real positive-frame model.
It uses a bounded principal core after a fixed small fraction of physical deletions, while retaining the cuts and conditioning of the original matrices.
The result removes a separate diffusion hypothesis in the single-pole application; it does not establish the coverage of single-pole or stable-residual branches.
The complete statement is adopted after [independent whole-proof inspection](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v27.md#original-diffuse-mass-whole-proof) and adoption of its exact constant-cut input.

## Simultaneous original-model diffusion

Fix \(m,d\ge1\), real symmetric \(A_0,\ldots,A_d\in M_m(\mathbb R)\), and the positive complex-direction frame
\[
 \chi_A=\inf_{\substack{a,b\in\mathbb C^m\\\|a\|=\|b\|=1}}
              \sum_{j=1}^d|a^*A_jb|^2>0.
\]
Use the original independent real Wigner inputs with fixed centered variance-one off-diagonal laws \(\xi_j\), fixed centered finite-second-moment diagonal laws \(\zeta_j\), and normalization \(N^{-1/2}\).
All upper coordinates and inputs are independent within each size, and cross-size coupling is arbitrary.
Put \(L_N=A_0\otimes I_N+\sum_jA_j\otimes W_j^{(N)}\), in physical-first ordering.

For every fixed deletion bound \(r\), there are fixed \(a,\kappa>0\) and a deterministic \(\gamma_N\to0\) such that, outside an event of probability at most \(\gamma_N\), the following holds simultaneously.
For every physical deletion \(\mathcal D\subset[N]\) with \(|\mathcal D|\le r\), every real \(|\theta|\le1\), and every real unit block vector \(e=(e_k)_{k\in\mathcal D^c}\) satisfying
\[
 M=(L_N+\theta I)[\mathcal D^c,\mathcal D^c],
 \qquad \|Me\|\le a/4,
\]
one has
\[
 \sum_{\substack{k\in\mathcal D^c\\\|e_k\|\le N^{-1/4}}}\|e_k\|^2
       \ge\kappa.
 \tag{Original-near-kernel-diffuse-mass}
\]
The statement requires neither a free spectral gap nor an operator-norm bound on the full parent or retained minor.
It is a marginal high-probability assertion uniform over the displayed physical objects, not a summable or almost-sure probability estimate under arbitrary coupling.
It asserts no single-pole decomposition or residual inverse-energy coverage.

## Complete argument

<a id="regularized-core-fixed-fraction"></a>
### A fixed small fraction of physical coordinates contains the norm obstruction

The exact regularization input says that for a real symmetric \(N\)-by-\(N\) matrix with iid centered variance-one entries on and above the diagonal, and any fixed \(0<\varepsilon\le1/2\), zeroing an at most \(\lceil\varepsilon N\rceil\)-by-\(\lceil\varepsilon N\rceil\) submatrix gives operator norm at most \(C\sqrt{N/\varepsilon}\), outside probability \(2e^{-c\varepsilon N}\).
The constants are absolute, and no symmetry of the scalar entry law, fourth moment or density is required.
The modified matrix need not be a principal compression; the next step accounts for that distinction.
The source's algorithmic theorem and its remark about more general diagonal entries are not needed.

Fix any \(0<\eta<1/4\), and take \(\varepsilon=\eta/(4d)\).
For each input \(j\), enlarge the probability space by independent auxiliary diagonal variables \(\omega_{j,k}\) with the original off-diagonal law \(\xi_j\).
Let \(H_j^{(N)}\) keep the actual original off-diagonal entries of \(W_j^{(N)}\) and replace its diagonal by \(\omega_{j,k}/\sqrt N\).
Thus \(\sqrt N H_j^{(N)}\) has exactly the source's iid on-and-above-diagonal law.
With total exception at most \(2d e^{-c\varepsilon N}\), for every \(j\) there are row and column sets \(I_j,J_j\), each of size at most \(\lceil\varepsilon N\rceil\), such that the zeroed matrix \(\widetilde H_j^{(N)}\) has
\[
 \|\widetilde H_j^{(N)}\|\le C\varepsilon^{-1/2}.
\]
Let
\[
 K=[N]\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^d(I_j\cup J_j).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(N\), its complement has size at most \(2d\lceil\varepsilon N\rceil\le\eta N\).
None of the modified entries occurs in the \(K,K\) block, and coordinate compression does not increase operator norm, so
\[
 H_j^{(N)}[K,K]=\widetilde H_j^{(N)}[K,K],
 \qquad \|H_j^{(N)}[K,K]\|\le C\varepsilon^{-1/2}.
 \tag{Common-physical-regularized-core}
\]
This obtains one common principal core of all the original input directions from possibly nonprincipal and different modifying rectangles.

For any fixed finite-second-moment real \(X\), one has
\[
 N\mathbb P\{|X|>\sqrt N/2\}
 \le4\mathbb E\!\left[X^2\mathbf1_{\{|X|>\sqrt N/2\}}\right]\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently the event
\[
 \max_{\substack{1\le j\le d\\1\le k\le N}}
       \bigl(|\zeta_{j,k}|+|\omega_{j,k}|\bigr)\le\sqrt N
\]
has probability tending to one.
On this event the diagonal replacement changes each normalized input norm by at most one, including after compression.
Define
\[
 d_N:=N\sum_{j=1}^d
 \left(\mathbb P\{|\zeta_j|>\sqrt N/2\}
       +\mathbb P\{|\xi_j|>\sqrt N/2\}\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The coupled construction therefore implies, with exception at most \(2d e^{-c\varepsilon N}+d_N\), existence of a set \(K\subset[N]\) with \(|K^c|\le\eta N\) such that
\[
 \|W_j^{(N)}[K,K]\|\le C\varepsilon^{-1/2}+1
       \quad(1\le j\le d).
 \tag{Original-law-bounded-principal-core}
\]
The last existence event depends only on the actual original matrices.
Since a successful coupled construction implies that original event, integrating out the auxiliary diagonals proves the stated probability bound in the original model itself.
Choose the first qualifying \(K\) in a fixed finite enumeration when a measurable selection is wanted; all its tests are Borel functions of the original matrices.
No auxiliary diagonal, changed original law or conditioning on a selected core is retained.

For every \(|\theta|\le1\), the same core satisfies
\[
 \|(L_N+\theta I)[K,K]\|
 \le \|A_0\|+1+
       (C\varepsilon^{-1/2}+1)\sum_j\|A_j\|
 =:B_\eta,
 \qquad B_\eta\ge1.
 \tag{Bounded-pencil-core}
\]
Every further principal compression of this core has the same bound.
The deletion proportion \(\eta\) can be any prescribed fixed positive constant, with the resulting bound depending on it.
No assertion about a uniformly bounded core after \(o(N)\) deletions, a growing truncation level or a triangular-array norm theorem is needed or proved here.

<a id="regularized-core-cut-diffusion"></a>
### Absorb the bad coordinates into the original cut support

Take \(a,\delta,c',C'>0\) from C194v23's constant-cut conclusion for the chosen fixed \(r\).
Its original-law proof uses the dimension-uniform linear-form \(L^1\) bound and KM15 Theorem 1.4, and then a union over all small supports and \(O_r(N^r)\) fixed physical deletions.
Thus, outside probability \(C'e^{-c'N}\), for every permitted \(\mathcal D\), with \(n=N-|\mathcal D|\), all supports \(J\subset\mathcal D^c\) of size \(k=\lfloor\delta n\rfloor\) satisfy
\[
 s_{\min}\bigl(L_N[\mathcal D^c\setminus J,J]\bigr)\ge a.
 \tag{Original-simultaneous-constant-cuts}
\]
These are cuts of the original matrices, and scalar diagonal shifts vanish on them.
This step uses exactly the zero-off-cut-shift scope of that input, not the arbitrary-shift conclusion of C194v17 at a stronger scale.

Choose \(\eta=\delta/8\) in the preceding core argument and intersect its original existence event with (Original-simultaneous-constant-cuts).
The total exception is at most
\[
 \gamma_N=2d e^{-c\varepsilon N}+d_N+C'e^{-c'N}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Original-diffusion-error}
\]
Fix any of the permitted \(\mathcal D,\theta,e\), and let \(K\) be a qualifying original core.
Inside this retained minor the bad coordinates \(B=\mathcal D^c\setminus K\) satisfy \(|B|\le\eta N\).
The set \(H=\{k\in\mathcal D^c:\|e_k\|>N^{-1/4}\}\) has fewer than \(\sqrt N\) elements, because \(\sum_k\|e_k\|^2=1\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\), uniformly in \(|\mathcal D|\le r\), one has \(|B\cup H|\le\lfloor\delta n\rfloor\).
Extend \(B\cup H\) to a set \(J\) of exactly that size, and put \(I=\mathcal D^c\setminus J\).
Then \(I\subset K\), every block of \(e_I\) has norm at most \(N^{-1/4}\), and \(\|M[I,I]\|\le B_\eta\).

The actual near-kernel equation on the rows \(I\) gives
\[
 M[I,J]e_J=(Me)_I-M[I,I]e_I.
\]
Using the original cut lower bound, not a law of the adaptively selected core, yields
\[
 a\|e_J\|
 \le \|Me\|+B_\eta\|e_I\|
 \le a/4+B_\eta\|e_I\|.
 \tag{Original-cut-core-inequality}
\]
Set \(\rho=\min\{1/2,a/(4B_\eta)\}>0\).
If \(\|e_I\|<\rho\), the left side is at least \(\sqrt3a/2\), whereas the right side is at most \(a/2\), a contradiction.
Thus \(\|e_I\|^2\ge\rho^2\), proving (Original-near-kernel-diffuse-mass) with \(\kappa=\rho^2\).

The choices of \(K,J,e\) may all be adaptive because the norm and cut conclusions were simultaneous deterministic facts on the same event.
In particular no theorem for fixed \(r\) deleted rows has been applied after deleting \(\eta N\) adaptive rows.
The argument deliberately leaves those bad coordinates in the column side \(J\) of an original full-minor cut.
It never treats the selected principal core as a fresh Wigner matrix and uses no independence of its remaining entries.
The proof is pointwise for every admitted vector and shift on this event, so no sphere net or union over eigenvectors is required.

<a id="regularized-core-minor-conditioning"></a>
### Minor measurability and the exact single-pole interface

For each specified retained physical minor, let \(\mathcal G_{\mathcal D,N}\) be the event that its own original cuts satisfy (Original-simultaneous-constant-cuts) and that its unshifted pencil contains a principal core of norm at most \(B_\eta-1\), after excluding at most \(\eta N\) of its physical coordinates.
The extra \(1\) in \(B_\eta\) then supplies the shifted core bound uniformly for all \(|\theta|\le1\).
This event is measurable from the actual original input minor and is independent of any subsequently exposed original border.
The parent construction proves that all these intrinsic minor events hold simultaneously outside (Original-diffusion-error), because its core compresses to each retained minor.
A first qualifying core in a fixed enumeration makes the same construction minor-measurable without using any parent border.
The parent event used to prove this marginal bound is not itself inserted into a fresh-row conditioning.

For the single-pole spectral choice in C194v23, \(|\lambda|\ge c_0N\) implies \(\|Me\|=|\lambda|^{-1}\le a/4\) eventually.
On the intrinsic minor good event the set
\[
 T_N=\{k:\|e_k\|\le N^{-1/4}\}
\]
is minor-measurable, has total squared mass at least the fixed \(\kappa\), and has maximum block norm at most \(N^{-1/4}\).
It is therefore the precise diffusion input of C194v23, with a deterministic rate and without a full-minor norm bound.

If \(\mathcal B_{i,N}\) is measurable in C194v23's conditioning sigma-field and carries its single-pole, bounded residual-energy and stable-background hypotheses, replace it by \(\mathcal B_{i,N}\cap\mathcal G_{i,N}\).
The additional original-model loss in the physical-index average obeys
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
       \mathbb P(\mathcal B_{i,N}\setminus\mathcal G_{i,N})
       \le\gamma_N\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Diffuse-interface-average}
\]
The original border laws in C194v23 are unchanged, and the new intersection is measurable in its conditioning sigma-field.
Together with the complete single-pole theorem in C194v23, this removes its separate positive-mass hypothesis at the additional vanishing average cost displayed above.
It does not prove that a single inverse pole with fixed normalized residual energy and stable invertible background covers the free-gap high-energy branch.
Multiple-pole coverage, a total negative-logarithmic-loss probability estimate and the general F13 conclusion remain unresolved.
