# Low-rank inverse residuals, physical incompressibility and one-mode Schur control

This project deduction strengthens the actual-minor interfaces for the real positive-frame branch of [F13](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract).
Its stable-background argument works for arbitrary fixed real symmetric auxiliary coefficients and a rank-o(N) inverse perturbation.
Its positive-frame argument gives linearly many near-kernel blocks at the natural N^(-1/2) scale.
Together with the adopted single-pole theorem, these results remove separate stability, diffusion and pole-size hypotheses from the bounded-residual one-mode branch.
The actual mean residual-tightness hypothesis remains unproved, and no general F13 or F14 completion is asserted.
This complete argument combines ordinary joint development in [D10](../drafts/Yin_D_F0010.md#low-rank-residual-stable-background) with the strengthened physical-spread deduction below.
That Draft is extraction provenance, not a proof input.
The complete statement is adopted after [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v28.md#low-rank-residual-whole-proof), retaining its explicit original-model coverage premise.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact scope of the conclusions

Use fixed centered real variance-one off-diagonal laws and fixed centered real finite-second-moment diagonal laws in independent real symmetric Wigner inputs, normalized by the original N^(-1/2), with arbitrary coupling across sizes.
All auxiliary coefficients are fixed and real symmetric.
The detailed statements below assert the following, with their exact conditioning and quantifiers retained.

1. [Stable residual backgrounds](#stable-background-statement): for any fixed auxiliary dimension, a real symmetric approximation E with bounded normalized squared Frobenius norm and rank(M^(-1)-E)=o(N) supplies a stable invertible residual Schur background outside a marginal exception tending to zero.
   No positive frame, free gap, commutation or magnitude bound on the removed part is required.
2. [Natural-scale physical spread](#natural-scale-spread): under the positive complex-direction coefficient frame, every sufficiently accurate unit near-kernel vector has a fixed positive fraction of its physical blocks between two fixed multiples of N^(-1/2), on one original-model event of probability tending to one.
   This holds simultaneously for every fixed physical deletion bound and every real scalar shift of magnitude at most one.
3. [Free-gap clipping](#free-gap-clipping): the bounded spectral truncation of an actual inverse supplies the first conclusion on its invertible branch, with only rank o(N) for the removed part, without controlling its size or its full Schur contribution.
4. [One exceptional mode at all scales](#single-exceptional-mode-all-scales): in auxiliary dimension three with positive frame, bounded residual inverse energy after removing one eigenmode gives a joint marginal bound tending to zero at the graph-determinant threshold N^(-3), with no pole-size or sign restriction.
5. [The parent and logarithmic consequence](#single-mode-average-tightness-parent): average tightness of the residual energy after removing a largest inverse eigenmode supplies a c_* N^(-9/2) parent floor in probability along the same deterministic dimensions.
   The constant c_* is independent of the energy cutoff K.
   A shift o(N^(-9/2)) transfers the floor to the original matrix; in a free gap it yields the probability logarithmic limit on those dimensions and the all-dimension almost-sure limsup under every allowed coupling.

The last assertion is conditional on the displayed average tightness, not an assertion that a free gap supplies it.
All marginal limits and their index averages are distinguished from simultaneous high-probability assertions and summable exceptions.
The following fixed inputs are used at their exact adopted scope: [C194v18](Yin_C_F0194_v18.md#decision-low-inverse-energy-reviewed), [C194v20](Yin_C_F0194_v20.md#decision-inverse-trace-norm-approximation-reviewed), [C194v23](Yin_C_F0194_v23.md#decision-diffuse-single-pole-reviewed), [C194v24](Yin_C_F0194_v24.md#decision-original-diffuse-mass-reviewed), [C186v4](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#decision-finite-variance-reviewed), [C187v3](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#decision-block-intermediate-reviewed), and [C190v1](Yin_C_F0190_v1.md#decision-coupling-criteria-reviewed).
The elementary parent Frobenius guard is reused only from its [exact argument in C186v22](Yin_C_F0186_v22.md#frobenius-norm-guard), independently of that theorem's spectral-point assumption.
No new external theorem or source edition is invoked.

<a id="stable-background-statement"></a>
### Exact original-model statement

Use the original real finite-second-moment model, fixed real symmetric auxiliary coefficients, and notation of [C194v18](Yin_C_F0194_v18.md#statement), in any fixed auxiliary dimension m.
Fix a physical deletion bound r, one deterministic real sequence theta_N tending to zero, and a constant K at least one.
For every deterministic deletion set T with |T| at most r and exposed physical index i outside T, let J=[N] minus (T union {i}), n=|J|, and let M be the retained minor of the shifted parent.
All entries retain their original normalization N^(-1/2), including internal single deletions in the fixed-eta identity.
Let F be the sigma-field generated by the original retained input minors and the exposed original diagonal coordinates.
The original off-diagonal border R is independent of F.
Put
\[
 A_N=A_0+\theta_N I_m,\qquad
 \mathcal S(H)=\sum_j A_jHA_j,\qquad
 \Lambda=\sum_j\|A_j\|^2,\qquad h=\sqrt{mK}.
\]
Choose a fixed a with ||A_N|| at most a eventually.
Let ell_N be any deterministic nonnegative integer sequence with ell_N/N tending to zero.
On an F-measurable event A_{T,i,N}, suppose that M is invertible and that an F-measurable real symmetric matrix E satisfies
\[
 Z=M^{-1},\qquad
 \frac{\|E\|_F^2}{N}\le K,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}(Z-E)\le\ell_N.
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-data}
\]
No commutation, sign, norm, or nuclear-norm hypothesis is imposed on Z-E.
The choices of E and the event may vary with T, i, and N.
Define on this branch
\[
 g_E=\frac1N\sum_{k\in J}E_{kk},\qquad
 B_E=A_N-\mathcal S(g_E),\qquad
 D_i=\sum_j A_jW_{j,ii},\qquad
 B=B_E+D_i.
\]
For every fixed epsilon greater than zero,
\[
 \sup_{\substack{|T|\le r\\i\notin T}}
 \mathbb P\{\mathcal A_{T,i,N},\
                  \|B_Eg_E-I_m\|>\varepsilon\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-equation}
\]
In particular, the F-measurable good event
\[
 \mathcal G_{T,i,N}
 =\mathcal A_{T,i,N}\cap
   \{\|B_Eg_E-I_m\|\le\tfrac12\}\cap
   \{\|D_i\|\le(4h)^{-1}\}
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-good-event}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \sup_{T,i}\mathbb P(\mathcal A_{T,i,N}\setminus\mathcal G_{T,i,N})
       \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-relative-cost}
\]
On this event B is real symmetric and
\[
 s_{\min}(B)\ge b:=(4h)^{-1},\qquad
 \|B\|\le C_B:=a+\Lambda h+(4h)^{-1}.
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-stability}
\]
There is a deterministic q_N(K) tending to zero, uniform in all the stated conditionings and choices, such that
\[
 S_E=A_N+D_i-R^TER,\qquad
 \mathbb E[\|S_E-B\|\mid\mathcal F]\le q_N(K)
       \quad\hbox{on }\mathcal G_{T,i,N}.
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-comparison}
\]
Every displayed supremum concerns marginal probabilities, not a union over the possible deletion sets or exposed indices.
The same error bounds therefore survive an average over the exposed indices without an extra factor of N.

## Complete arguments and application interfaces

### Rank comparison at a fixed nonreal parameter

For real symmetric X and eta greater than zero, write
\[
 f_\eta(X)=X(I-i\eta X)^{-1}.
\]
Spectral calculus gives ||f_eta(X)|| at most eta^(-1), while
\[
 f_\eta(Z)-f_\eta(E)
 =(I-i\eta Z)^{-1}(Z-E)(I-i\eta E)^{-1}
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-resolvent-identity}
\]
is the exact noncommuting identity already proved in C194v20.
Its rank is at most rank(Z-E), and its operator norm is at most 2/eta by the separate spectral bounds on its two terms.
Consequently,
\[
 \|f_\eta(Z)-f_\eta(E)\|_1\le\frac{2\ell_N}{\eta},\qquad
 \|f_\eta(E)-E\|_1\le\eta\|E\|_F^2.
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-resolvent-nuclear-bound}
\]
The second inequality follows from |t/(1-i eta t)-t| at most eta t^2 for real t.
For an actual spectral decomposition with E(Z-E)=0, the first bound improves to ell_N/eta, but the factor two is sufficient and avoids a commutation assumption.

Use the actual resolvent and its normalized partial trace
\[
 G_\eta=(M-i\eta I)^{-1}=f_\eta(Z),\qquad
 g_\eta=\frac1N\sum_{k\in J}(G_\eta)_{kk},\qquad
 B_\eta=A_N-i\eta I_m-\mathcal S(g_\eta).
\]
The partial-trace inequality ||sum_k X_kk|| at most ||X||_1, from trace duality, and the preceding bounds yield
\[
 \|g_\eta-g_E\|\le u_N(\eta):=\eta K+\frac{2\ell_N}{N\eta},\qquad
 \|g_E\|\le\frac{\sqrt{mn}\|E\|_F}{N}\le h,\qquad
 \|g_\eta\|\le h+u_N(\eta).
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-partial-trace-comparison}
\]
These are deterministic inequalities on the selected branch, valid for every allowed E at once.
They do not condition any internal original row on the selected approximation event.

The fixed-eta input of C194v18 gives, for every fixed eta greater than zero,
\[
 \sup_{T,i}\mathbb E\left\|B_\eta g_\eta-\frac nN I_m\right\|
       \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-actual-equation}
\]
This is an unconditional expectation for the actual original minor, whose internal fresh rows keep their prescribed laws.
Neither E nor a matrix with its inverse modes removed replaces that actual minor in this input.
The normalization difference satisfies |n/N-1| at most (r+1)/N.

Putting u=u_N(eta), the deterministic comparison is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|B_Eg_E-B_\eta g_\eta\|
 &\le\|B_E\|\|g_E-g_\eta\|
          +\|B_E-B_\eta\|\|g_\eta\|\\
 &\le [a+\Lambda(2h+u)]u+\eta(h+u).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Low-rank-residual-defect-comparison}
\]
For each fixed epsilon, first choose eta sufficiently small at fixed K so that the right side with u=eta K is less than epsilon/3.
Then take N sufficiently large that 2 ell_N/(N eta) and (r+1)/N give a combined additional error less than epsilon/3.
Markov's inequality applied to the unconditional fixed-eta input proves (Low-rank-residual-equation).
The bounds do not depend on the selected E, and the input is uniform over the stated marginal fixed-deletion laws.
No quantitative eta_N local law or interchange of unproved limits is used.

### The exposed diagonal and original border

On the product-defect event in (Low-rank-residual-good-event), the square matrix B_Eg_E is invertible and
\[
 B_E^{-1}=g_E(B_Eg_E)^{-1},\qquad
 \|B_E^{-1}\|\le2h.
\]
Hence s_min(B_E) is at least (2h)^(-1), and adding D_i with norm at most (4h)^(-1) proves both bounds in (Low-rank-residual-stability).
The original diagonal laws have finite second moment, so E||D_i||^2 is O(N^(-1)), uniformly in i.
This makes the diagonal failure probability tend to zero and completes (Low-rank-residual-relative-cost).
Including the exposed diagonal in B is permitted by the exact conditioning in C194v23; it does not expose any off-diagonal border coordinate.

For auxiliary coordinate a, use the coefficient map U_a from C194v18, with ||U_a|| at most sqrt(Lambda).
The coefficient matrix for the (a,b) entry of R^TER is U_a E U_b^T and has squared Frobenius norm at most Lambda^2 K N.
Its exact conditional mean is the corresponding entry of S(g_E), because the different original input colors and different physical coordinates have zero covariance and variance 1/N.
The accepted finite-second-moment quadratic estimate, summed over the fixed number of auxiliary entries, therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\|R^TER-\mathcal S(g_E)\|\mid\mathcal F]\le q_N(K)\to0
\]
uniformly on the branch.
Since S_E-B equals S(g_E)-R^TER, this is precisely (Low-rank-residual-comparison).
No independence between this residual error and a pole contribution from the same border is claimed or needed.


<a id="natural-scale-spread"></a>
## Natural-scale physical incompressibility

For this part let m,d be arbitrary fixed positive integers and assume
\[
 \chi_A=\inf_{\substack{u,v\in\mathbb C^m\\\|u\|=\|v\|=1}}
             \sum_j|u^*A_jv|^2>0,
 \qquad L_N=A_0\otimes I_N+\sum_j A_j\otimes W_j^{(N)}.
\]
For every fixed physical deletion bound r there are constants a_0,p,c_-,c_+>0 and an original-model event \(\mathcal I_N\), with \(\mathbb P(\mathcal I_N^c)\to0\), such that on that event the following assertion holds simultaneously.
For every \(\mathcal D\subset[N]\) with \(|\mathcal D|\le r\), every real \(|\theta|\le1\), and every real unit vector \(w=(w_i)_{i\in\mathcal D^c}\) satisfying
\[
 \|(L_N+\theta I)[\mathcal D^c,\mathcal D^c]w\|\le a_0,
\]
one has
\[
 \#\{i\in\mathcal D^c:
             c_-N^{-1/2}\le\|w_i\|\le c_+N^{-1/2}\}
       \ge pN.
 \tag{Natural-scale-physical-spread}
\]
The constants do not depend on an inverse-energy cutoff or on a parent norm guard.
This is not asserted to have summable failure.

Here is the complete strengthening of the [regularized-core cut argument in C194v24](Yin_C_F0194_v24.md#regularized-core-cut-diffusion).
Use its original simultaneous constant cuts with constants a,delta>0 and its original principal core K satisfying \(|K^c|\le\eta N\) and
\(\|(L_N+\theta I)[K,K]\|\le B_\eta\) for every \(|\theta|\le1\), where \(\eta=\delta/8\).
The existence of this core, including the original diagonal-law comparison, has already been proved in [C194v24's complete argument](Yin_C_F0194_v24.md#argument); the original source theorem is not applied again here.
Intersect the core event with the original cut event; their combined failure tends to zero and neither is conditioned into a fresh row law.

Choose a fixed \(c_+>0\) so large that \(c_+^{-2}\le\delta/8\).
For any of the stated \(\mathcal D,\theta,w\), put \(n=N-|\mathcal D|\) and
\[
 H=\{i\in\mathcal D^c:\|w_i\|>c_+/\sqrt N\}.
\]
Normalization gives \(|H|\le N/c_+^2\le\delta N/8\).
For all sufficiently large N, the union \(H\cup(\mathcal D^c\setminus K)\) has size at most \(\delta N/4<\lfloor\delta n\rfloor\).
Extend that union to J of size \(\lfloor\delta n\rfloor\), and put \(I=\mathcal D^c\setminus J\).
Then I is contained in K and every block on I is at most \(c_+/\sqrt N\).
For \(M=(L_N+\theta I)[\mathcal D^c,\mathcal D^c]\), the actual equation on I gives
\[
 a\|w_J\|\le\|Mw\|+B_\eta\|w_I\|.
\]
Take \(a_0=a/4\) and \(\rho=\min\{1/2,a/(4B_\eta)\}\).
If \(\|w_I\|<\rho\), the left side is at least \(\sqrt3a/2\), while the right side is at most a/2.
Consequently
\[
 \sum_{i\in I}\|w_i\|^2\ge\kappa:=\rho^2.
\]
Set \(c_-=\sqrt{\kappa/2}\).
The total squared mass on indices in I with \(\|w_i\|<c_-/\sqrt N\) is at most \(c_-^2=\kappa/2\).
The other indices in I therefore carry mass at least \(\kappa/2\), and each carries at most \(c_+^2/N\).
Their number is at least \(pN\), where \(p=\kappa/(2c_+^2)>0\).
This proves (Natural-scale-physical-spread).
All choices of w, J and K are allowed after the matrices are known, since the core and original cut bounds are simultaneous pointwise facts.
No adaptive core is treated as an independent Wigner matrix.

<a id="natural-scale-distance-average"></a>
### The corresponding distance average

For the whole parent \(L_N^\theta=L_N+\theta_NI\), let C_i be its m physical columns, Q_i the orthogonal projection onto the complement of all other columns, and \(d_i=s_{\min}(Q_iC_i)\).
These objects exist even when the parent is singular.
Fix any deterministic \(0\le u_N\le a_0\) and any deterministic \(b_N>0\).
Because the parent is real, a real unit right singular vector w may be selected for its least singular value.
On \(\mathcal I_N\cap\{s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le u_N\}\), at least pN indices have \(\|w_i\|\ge c_-/\sqrt N\).
For every such index,
\[
 d_i\|w_i\|\le\|Q_iC_iw_i\|
       =\|Q_iL_N^\theta w\|\le u_N.
\]
Pointwise counting and then expectation yield
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le u_N,\ \|L_N^\theta\|\le b_N\}\\
 &\quad\le\mathbb P(\mathcal I_N^c)
       +\frac1{pN}\sum_{i=1}^N
          \mathbb P\{d_i\le \sqrt N\,u_N/c_-,\
                         \|L_N^\theta\|\le b_N\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Natural-scale-distance-average}
\]
This is a new deduction from the stronger original cut/core event, rather than an assertion that C194v17 originally had this threshold.
No independence among the distance events is used.


<a id="free-gap-clipping"></a>
### Original free-gap clipping supplies the low-rank approximation

Now assume additionally that zero lies outside the spectrum of the free selfadjoint pencil L, and choose delta greater than zero with [-2 delta,2 delta] disjoint from that spectrum.
For an invertible actual minor, define its canonical spectral truncation
\[
 E=M^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{|M|\ge\delta\}},\qquad
 H=Z-E=M^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{0<|M|<\delta\}}.
 \tag{Free-gap-canonical-residual}
\]
Then ||E|| is at most delta^(-1), ||E||_F^2/N is at most m delta^(-2), and rank H is the number of minor eigenvalues in (-delta,delta).
These statements do not bound the size of the eigenvalues of H.

The [accepted almost-sure weak law](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#statement) for the fixed selfadjoint matrix polynomial L_N implies that the parent eigenvalue count in [-2 delta,2 delta], divided by N, tends to zero in probability, and in fact almost surely under every allowed coupling.
This uses a closed interval carrying no limiting mass, not a claim of spectral exclusion in finite dimensions.
For large N the shift has magnitude less than delta/2, and the count in a shifted minor's (-delta,delta) is bounded by the unshifted parent count in [-2 delta,2 delta] plus 2m(r+1), by eigenvalue interlacing at the two interval endpoints.
Thus the same normalized count bound applies simultaneously to the deterministic choices of T and i.
One may choose a deterministic ell_N=o(N), depending only on the fixed laws and coefficients, for which the probability that this parent count plus 2m(r+1) exceeds ell_N tends to zero.
For example, choose increasing N_k so that the normalized parent count exceeds 1/(2k) with probability at most 1/k for every N at least N_k, require also N_k at least 4m(r+1)k, and use ell_N=ceil(N/k) on the resulting intervals.
The fixed deletion term is then at most N/(2k), so the resulting threshold has the asserted vanishing failure probability.
The selected approximation consequently satisfies (Low-rank-residual-data), with K=max(1,m delta^(-2)), outside a vanishing exceptional event on the invertible branch.
Its intrinsic event {rank H at most ell_N} is measurable from the actual minor; the parent event is used only to bound its probability and is not put into the border conditioning.
This supplies a stable residual background for the canonical free-gap decomposition without any separate weighted weak-limit or operator-norm premise.
It gives no estimate for the complete contribution R^THR, no inverse-first-moment bound, and no multiple-pole anti-concentration theorem.

### Consequence for the single-pole interface and the remaining coverage problem

Take m=3 and the positive complex-direction frame of C194v23.
Suppose that, on an actual minor-measurable event A_{i,N},
\[
 Z=E+\lambda ee^T,\qquad Ee=0,\qquad \|e\|=1,\qquad
 |\lambda|\ge c_0N,\qquad \|E\|_F^2/N\le K
\]
with fixed c_0>0 and K.
The rank bound above holds with ell_N=1.
Intersect A_{i,N} with the stable-background good event and the intrinsic diffusion event supplied by [C194v24](Yin_C_F0194_v24.md#statement).
The excluded probability tends to zero uniformly in the marginal physical index, and hence in its average.
The exact single-pole theorem C194v23 then applies with the explicit F-measurable B above, fixed b and C_B, deterministic q_N(K), and diffusion rate N^(-1/4).
Its local argument also applies after the additional fixed deletion T: the diffuse covariance and Lindeberg bounds use only the stated block mass and maximum block norm, not an exact count of N-1 retained blocks.
The original covariance remains 1/N, the residual comparison and graph trace retain exactly that normalization, and the auxiliary graph and determinant still have size three.
Thus the local proof supplies the asserted uniform marginal fixed-deletion extension, while the whole-parent application below uses T empty.
No stable-background or diffuse-mass hypothesis remains to be proved on this bounded-residual single-pole branch.
Its graph-determinant and norm-guarded distance conclusions therefore hold on A_{i,N} with an unconditional error tending to zero, with no pole upper bound and no sign or definiteness restriction.

<a id="single-exceptional-mode-all-scales"></a>
### One exceptional inverse eigenmode at every magnitude

The order-N lower bound on the exceptional pole can also be removed from the resulting original-model small-ball assertion.
Keep m=3, the real positive-frame coefficients, and the original laws and fixed deletion convention above.
For each actual invertible minor choose a minor-measurable real eigenvalue lambda of Z and a corresponding real unit eigenvector e, and set E=Z-lambda ee^T.
The selection may be made by a fixed measurable eigenvalue and eigenspace convention; for example, select an eigenvalue of maximal absolute value, break the eigenvalue tie by its sign, and choose the first nonzero projected coordinate and normalize it in the chosen eigenspace.
The residual then satisfies Ee=0.
For each fixed K, define the bare spectral branch
\[
 \mathcal A_{T,i,N}(K)
 =\{M\text{ invertible},\ \|E\|_F^2/N\le K\}.
 \tag{Single-mode-bare-branch}
\]
There is no magnitude or sign restriction on lambda.
On this branch put
\[
 S=A_N+D_i-R^TZR,\qquad
 \Gamma=I_3+R^TZ^2R,\qquad F=\Gamma^{-1/2}S.
\]
Then a deterministic error epsilon_N(K) tends to zero and satisfies
\[
 \sup_{T,i}
 \mathbb P\{\mathcal A_{T,i,N}(K),\ |\det F|\le N^{-3}\}
       \le\epsilon_N(K).
 \tag{Single-mode-all-scale-graph}
\]
This is a joint, marginal bound under the actual original law, rather than a uniform conditional assertion on every bare spectral branch.
In particular it retains the cost of the stable-background and intrinsic diffusion exceptions.

To prove the assertion, first remove the stable-background bad event at its uniform vanishing cost for the fixed K, and write b=(4 sqrt(3K))^(-1).
For a fixed c>0, split the remaining conditionings into |lambda| at least cN and |lambda| less than cN.
On the first branch, the preceding C194v23 application, with c_0=c and C194v24 diffusion, gives an error tending to zero.
The diffusion event is used only through its intrinsic minor-measurable form.
When |lambda| is at least cN, its eigenvector is an admissible near-kernel vector since ||Me||=1/|lambda| tends to zero.
In particular this use does not require a diffuse eigenvector on the small-pole branch.

For the second branch the original conditional covariance gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\|R^T(\lambda ee^T)R\|\mid\mathcal F]
 =|\lambda|\,\mathbb E[\|R^Te\|^2\mid\mathcal F]
 \le\frac{\Lambda|\lambda|}{N}\le\Lambda c.
 \tag{Single-mode-small-pole-mean}
\]
The exact equality uses that the rank-one output has norm |lambda| times ||R^Te||^2, including a negative lambda.
Using the stable B and the residual comparison from (Low-rank-residual-comparison), Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(S)<b/2\mid\mathcal F\}
       \le\frac{2}{b}\bigl(q_N(K)+\Lambda c\bigr).
 \tag{Single-mode-small-pole-numerator}
\]
This compares the complete S with B and uses no independence between the residual and pole contributions.
Since Ee=0,
\[
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{tr}\Gamma\mid\mathcal F]
 \le3+\Lambda K+\frac{\Lambda\lambda^2}{N}
 \le3+\Lambda K+\Lambda c^2N,
 \tag{Single-mode-small-pole-graph-trace}
\]
where tr here is the ordinary trace of the three-by-three matrix.
On the event s_min(S) at least b/2 and tr Gamma at most b^2 N^2/16, one has s_min(F) at least 2/N and hence |det F| at least 8N^(-3).
Therefore on the small-pole branch
\[
 \mathbb P\{|\det F|\le N^{-3}\mid\mathcal F\}
 \le\frac{2}{b}\bigl(q_N(K)+\Lambda c\bigr)
       +\frac{16(3+\Lambda K+\Lambda c^2N)}{b^2N^2}.
 \tag{Single-mode-small-pole-complete-bound}
\]
After adding the vanishing stable-background and large-pole errors, take the upper limit in N at fixed c and K.
The result is at most 2 Lambda c/b.
Letting c decrease to zero proves (Single-mode-all-scale-graph), uniformly in the stated marginal indices.
The argument does not choose a random split or assume a uniform C194v23 estimate for a parameter c_N tending to zero.

For a fixed parent norm constant B_0, the same graph-column identity used in C194v23 gives
\[
 \sup_{T,i}\mathbb P\{\mathcal A_{T,i,N}(K),\
 d_i\le B_0^{-2}N^{-4},\
 \|L_{N,[N]\setminus T}^{\theta}\|\le B_0\sqrt N\}
       \le\epsilon_N(K).
 \tag{Single-mode-all-scale-distance}
\]
Indeed the actual orthonormal graph identifies d_i with s_min(F), while ||F|| is at most the parent norm, so this distance event implies |det F| at most N^(-3).
The parent norm event is used as an intersection, never as a condition on the fresh row law.

<a id="single-mode-average-tightness-parent"></a>
### Conditional parent floor from average tightness after removing one mode

For each single physical deletion i of the whole parent, choose the inverse eigenmode of maximal absolute value by the preceding measurable rule and denote its residual energy by
\[
 \mathcal E_{i,N}^{(1)}
 =\frac{\|M_{i,N}^{-1}\|_F^2
                  -\|M_{i,N}^{-1}\|^2}{N}
\]
on the invertible branch.
Here the second norm is the operator norm, so the expression is exactly ||E||_F^2/N even when the maximal absolute eigenvalue is repeated.
Set the residual energy to infinity when that actual minor is singular.
Suppose that along an infinite deterministic set of dimensions Ncal one has the explicit average-tightness condition
\[
 \lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\in\mathcal N}}
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
      \mathbb P\{\mathcal E_{i,N}^{(1)}>K\}=0.
 \tag{Single-mode-average-residual-tightness}
\]
This is an unproved hypothesis on the actual model, not a consequence asserted from the free gap or the rank-o(N) clipping above.
A vanishing average complement for one fixed K is a sufficient stronger condition.

The [original Frobenius guard](Yin_C_F0186_v22.md#frobenius-norm-guard) supplies a fixed B_0 for which the parent norm exceeds \(B_0\sqrt N\) with probability tending to zero.
Use the natural-scale spread and distance average just proved, with fixed constants p,c_-.
Choose once and for all
\[
 0<c_*\le c_-B_0^{-2},\qquad u_N=c_*N^{-9/2}.
\]
This choice does not depend on K.
For large N, \(u_N\le a_0\), and
\[
 \sqrt N\,u_N/c_-\le B_0^{-2}N^{-4}.
\]
At each fixed K apply (Single-mode-all-scale-distance) to the whole parent's single-deletion minors, together with (Natural-scale-distance-average).
It gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N^\theta)\le c_*N^{-9/2}\}
 &\le\mathbb P\{\|L_N^\theta\|>B_0\sqrt N\}
          +\mathbb P(\mathcal I_N^c)\\
 &\quad+\frac1p\left(
       \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P\{\mathcal E_{i,N}^{(1)}>K\}
                      +\epsilon_N(K)\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{Single-mode-tightness-parent-bound}
\]
First take the upper limit along \(\mathcal N\) at fixed K, and then let K tend to infinity using the displayed average-tightness hypothesis.
This proves the \(c_*N^{-9/2}\) floor in probability.
There is no choice of an uncontrolled growing energy cutoff and no union over physical indices.

If the deterministic shift also satisfies \(\theta_N=o(N^{-9/2})\), singular-value perturbation transfers the floor to the original parent with constant c_*/2.
If zero lies outside the free spectrum, combine that original floor with the [adopted positive-frame middle estimate](Yin_C_F0187_v3.md#statement) and [finite-variance weak and positive-logarithm controls](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#statement).
The first O(N^(3/4)) singular values contribute at most O(N^(-1/4) log N) to the normalized bottom logarithmic loss on the floor event.
The middle negative tail is at most C t(1+|log t|), and the free gap makes the limiting free logarithm finite.
Clipping the logarithm and then removing the two tails gives
\[
 \frac1{3N}\log|\det L_N|
       \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\log\Delta_{\operatorname{tr}_3\otimes\tau}(L)
       \quad(N\in\mathcal N).
\]
The [adopted arbitrary-coupling criterion](Yin_C_F0190_v1.md#statement), together with the already established all-dimension upper bound, then gives the required all-dimension almost-sure limsup equality for every prescribed coupling.
It does not give a full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic limit.
The unresolved original-model input has been reduced to (Single-mode-average-residual-tightness), or an alternative estimate that also handles the multiple-mode configurations excluded by that condition.
In particular, spectral clipping in a free gap only gives rank o(N); it does not imply tight inverse energy after removing just one eigenmode.
