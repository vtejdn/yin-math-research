# Two exceptional inverse modes and complete real positive-frame determinant control

This project deduction establishes a complete graph small-ball bound when the residual inverse energy after two eigenmodes is bounded.
Its new local estimate uses a common two-dimensional reservoir, possibly singular Gaussian smoothing and the adopted signed determinant nonvanishing theorem.
Combining it with the adopted high-residual theorem closes the real size-three positive-frame free-gap branch.
Together with the adopted spectral-point theorem this covers that entire real coefficient class, with the scope stated below.
The complete statement is adopted after [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v30.md#two-mode-free-gap-whole-proof), with the general Task boundaries below unchanged.
The [D10 two-mode development](../drafts/Yin_D_F0010.md#bounded-two-mode-schur-probe) is extraction provenance, not a proof input.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact model and conclusions

Fix \(d\ge1\) and real symmetric coefficients \(A_0,\ldots,A_d\in M_3(\mathbb R)\), with
\[
 \chi_A=\inf_{\substack{a,b\in\mathbb C^3\\\|a\|=\|b\|=1}}
          \sum_{j=1}^d|a^*A_jb|^2>0.
\]
For each input, the off-diagonal law is fixed, real, centered and variance one, and the diagonal law is fixed, real, centered and has finite second moment.
Upper-triangular coordinates and input colors are independent within each size, with symmetric lower copies and original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Coupling across sizes is arbitrary; no higher moment, density, bounded support or input-norm assumption is imposed.
Put
\[
 L_N=A_0\otimes I_N+\sum_jA_j\otimes W_j^{(N)},\qquad
 L=A_0\otimes1+\sum_jA_j\otimes s_j,\qquad
 \tau_3=\operatorname{tr}_3\otimes\tau,
\]
where the \(s_j\) are free centered variance-one semicircular elements in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state \(\tau\).

<a id="bounded-two-mode-statement"></a>
### Uniform joint marginal bound after removing two modes

Fix a physical deletion bound \(r\), any one deterministic real sequence \(\theta_N\to0\), and a finite \(K\ge1\).
For \(|D|\le r\) and \(i\notin D\), let \(M\) be the actual principal minor of \(L_N+\theta_N I\) outside \(D\cup\{i\}\).
Condition on its retained original input minors and the exposed original diagonal coordinates at \(i\), with sigma-field \(\mathcal F\); the remaining border \(R\) is independent with its original laws.
On the invertible branch set
\[
 Z=M^{-1},\quad q_j=s_j(Z),\quad q_1\ge q_2\ge\cdots,\qquad
 \mathcal E^{(2)}=\frac1N\sum_{j\ge3}q_j^2,
\]
\[
 S=A_0+\theta_N I_3+D_i-R^TZR,\quad
 \Gamma=I_3+R^TZ^2R,\qquad F=\Gamma^{-1/2}S,
 \quad D_i=\sum_j A_jW_{j,ii}^{(N)}.
\]
Choose the first two real orthonormal inverse eigenmodes in this absolute order by a fixed measurable spectral rule, and let \(E\) be the remaining spectral sum, so \(\|E\|_F^2/N=\mathcal E^{(2)}\).
For example, order signed eigenvalues measurably, break absolute-value ties by their signs, and use Gram--Schmidt on projected standard coordinate vectors inside repeated eigenspaces.
Then
\[
 \sup_{\substack{|D|\le r\\i\notin D}}
 \mathbb P\{M\text{ invertible},\ \mathcal E^{(2)}\le K,\
                         |\det F|\le N^{-6}\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Bounded-two-mode-graph}
\]
There is no spectral-point or free-gap assumption in this local assertion.
It is joint and marginal: exceptions in the stable-background and minor geometry are paid before the conditional smoothing argument.
It is not a uniform conditional claim on every bare bounded-energy minor.
The proof also covers any measurable choice of two orthogonal inverse eigenmodes with the same residual-energy bound.

For every fixed \(B_0>0\), the projected physical column distance \(d_i\) of the corresponding deleted parent consequently satisfies
\[
 \sup_{D,i}\mathbb P\{M\text{ invertible},\ \mathcal E^{(2)}\le K,\
 d_i\le B_0^{-2}N^{-7},\
 \|L_N+\theta_NI\|\le B_0\sqrt N\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Bounded-two-mode-distance}
\]
The full-parent norm event is an intersection, never an extra condition on the fresh border.

<a id="free-gap-conclusion"></a>
### The free-gap parent and logarithmic conclusions

If \(0\notin\sigma(L)\), there is a fixed \(a>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)\le aN^{-15/2}\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Free-gap-unshifted-floor}
\]
With ordinary Fuglede--Kadison determinants and \(\log0=-\infty\),
\[
 v=\log\Delta_{\tau_3}(L)\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 X_N=\frac1{3N}\log|\det L_N|\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}v,
 \qquad
 \limsup_N X_N=v\quad\text{almost surely}
 \tag{Free-gap-probability-log-and-limsup}
\]
under every prescribed cross-size coupling.
The probability-one event can depend on the fixed coefficients, laws and coupling.
There is no average inverse-residual-tightness premise.

<a id="entire-real-positive-frame-class"></a>
### Union with the adopted spectral-point branch

For all fixed real symmetric size-three coefficients with the displayed positive complex-direction frame, without a spectral-position assumption, the same finite target, convergence in probability and every-coupling almost-sure limsup hold.
A common weaker exponent for this entire class is \(17/2\): for some \(a>0\) depending on the fixed coefficients and laws,
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(L_N)\le aN^{-17/2}\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Real-positive-frame-all-position-floor}
\]
This does not assert constants uniform over varying coefficient tuples.
It does not complete unrestricted F13, allow general complex or arbitrary-size coefficients, prove the unrestricted scalar-polynomial F14 statement, or give a full-sequence almost-sure logarithmic or Brown-measure conclusion.

## Complete argument

The bounded-two-mode argument first uses only the original model above, without a free spectral gap.
Fix a physical deletion bound, a deterministic real shift tending to zero and a finite \(K\ge1\).
For each actual invertible retained minor, condition on its original input minors and the exposed original diagonal coordinates, denoting this sigma-field by \(\mathcal F\).
The off-diagonal border retains its original independent laws and original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
On a measurable branch assume the real spectral decomposition
\[
 Z=M^{-1}=E+\lambda_1e_1e_1^T+\lambda_2e_2e_2^T,
 \quad e_j^Te_k=\delta_{jk},\quad Ee_j=0,
 \quad \|E\|_F^2/N\le K.
 \tag{Two-mode-data}
\]
Relabel measurably so that \(|\lambda_1|\ge|\lambda_2|\).
In particular the branch obtained by deleting the two largest absolute inverse eigenmodes satisfies these hypotheses, with arbitrary measurable choices inside repeated eigenspaces.
Write \(A_N=A_0+\theta_NI_3\), \(\Lambda=\sum_j\|A_j\|^2\), and
\[
 S_E=A_N+D_i-R^TER,\quad z_j=\sqrt N R^Te_j,
 \quad S=S_E-\sum_{j=1}^2(\lambda_j/N)z_jz_j^T,
\]
\[
 \Gamma_E=I_3+R^TE^2R,\quad
 \Gamma=\Gamma_E+\sum_{j=1}^2(\lambda_j^2/N)z_jz_j^T,
 \qquad F=\Gamma^{-1/2}S.
 \tag{Two-mode-complete-objects}
\]
The formula for \(\Gamma\) uses the spectral orthogonality in (Two-mode-data).
The claim is that the joint marginal probability of this branch and \(|\det F|\le N^{-6}\) tends to zero, uniformly in the displayed fixed-deletion indices.
All suprema below concern marginal bounds and preserve physical-index averages without a union factor.

<a id="two-mode-background"></a>
### Stable background and original-law moment bounds

Apply [C194v25's rank-o(N) stable-background theorem](Yin_C_F0194_v25.md#stable-background-statement) with rank bound two.
Outside an \(\mathcal F\)-measurable exception of joint marginal probability tending to zero, it supplies a real symmetric \(B\) and deterministic \(q_N=q_N(K)\to0\) such that
\[
 s_{\min}(B)\ge b>0,\quad \|B\|\le C_B,
 \quad \mathbb E[\|S_E-B\|\mid\mathcal F]\le q_N.
 \tag{Two-mode-background}
\]
Here \(b,C_B\) depend on fixed \(K\) and the original coefficients and laws, not on the poles or exposed index.
Concretely \(g_E=N^{-1}\sum_kE_{kk}\) and \(B=A_N-\sum_jA_jg_EA_j+D_i\), on the exact good event specified there.
The diagonal guard in that event is paid marginally and is not imposed on the fresh off-diagonal row.
Original second moments give, uniformly on this good event,
\[
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_E\mid\mathcal F]\le3+\Lambda K,
 \qquad \mathbb E[\|z_j\|^2\mid\mathcal F]\le\Lambda.
 \tag{Two-mode-second-moments}
\]
No independence between \(S_E,z_1,z_2\) is asserted or required.

<a id="two-mode-smoothing"></a>
### A common reservoir and possibly singular six-dimensional smoothing

Fix constants \(0<c_2\le c_1\le1\), and first restrict to \(|\lambda_1|>c_1N\), \(|\lambda_2|>c_2N\).
For \(V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{e_1,e_2\}\), one has \(\|M|_V\|\le(c_2N)^{-1}\).
Use the [intrinsic minor core/cut event of C194v24](Yin_C_F0194_v24.md#argument), on which the proof of [C194v25's natural-scale spread](Yin_C_F0194_v25.md#natural-scale-spread) applies pointwise.
The common original-model event implies these intrinsic events simultaneously and has probability tending to one.
Its constants \(p,c_-,a_0>0\) are independent of \(c_1,c_2,N\).
For sufficiently large \(N\), every unit vector of \(V\) has at least \(pN\) physical blocks of norm at least \(c_-/\sqrt N\).
Let \(P_V\) be its projection, \(\Pi_k\) the physical block projection, and set
\[
 \nu_N=\sqrt{2/N},\qquad
 T=\{k:\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_kP_V\Pi_k)\le\nu_N\},
 \qquad \kappa=pc_-^2/2.
\]
At most \(2/\nu_N=o(N)\) positions are discarded, so the same minor-measurable \(T\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{k\in T}\|h_1e_{1,k}+h_2e_{2,k}\|^2\ge\kappa\|h\|^2
       \quad(h\in\mathbb R^2),\qquad
 \max_{k\in T}\sum_{j=1}^2\|e_{j,k}\|^2\le\nu_N.
 \tag{Two-mode-reservoir}
\]
Indeed, at least \(pN/2\) of the natural-scale lower blocks remain for every unit vector of \(V\), giving the common mass \(pc_-^2/2\).
This uses no free gap or inverse-energy lower bound.
Only the intrinsic minor event is used in conditioning; a full-parent event is used solely to bound its marginal failure.

Split the pair \(z=(z_1,z_2)=z_T+h_0\) into its retained and complementary physical positions.
Conditional on \(\mathcal F\), these parts are independent and both have total second moment at most \(2\Lambda\).
The centered retained vector in \(\mathbb R^6\cong M_{3,2}(\mathbb R)\) has covariance \(\Sigma\) with \(\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma\le2\Lambda\).
For every \(h\in\mathbb R^2\) and \(a\in\mathbb R^3\), the positive frame and (Two-mode-reservoir) give
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl[|a^T(z_Th)|^2\mid\mathcal F\bigr]
 =\sum_{k\in T,j}|a^TA_j(h_1e_{1,k}+h_2e_{2,k})|^2
 \ge\chi_A\kappa\|a\|^2\|h\|^2.
 \tag{Two-mode-transitive-covariance}
\]
The covariance \(\Sigma\) itself need not be invertible.
Its Gaussian support \(W=\operatorname{ran}\Sigma^{1/2}\subset M_{3,2}(\mathbb R)\) nevertheless satisfies
\[
 Wh=\mathbb R^3\qquad(h\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}),
 \tag{Two-mode-transitivity}
\]
because the covariance of that three-dimensional image is positive definite.
The admissible covariance matrices form a compact closed class, including their possible singular limits, and the same lower bound survives every limit.

Every individual vector coefficient of \(z_T\) has norm at most \(C_A\sqrt{\nu_N}\to0\), and the sum of coefficient squares is at most \(2\Lambda\).
Consequently its Lindeberg tail sum at any fixed radius \(t>0\) is bounded by
\[
 2\Lambda\max_j\mathbb E\left[\xi_j^2
       \mathbf1_{\{|\xi_j|>t/(C_A\sqrt{\nu_N})\}}\right]\longrightarrow0.
\]
The vector swapping proof in [C194v23](Yin_C_F0194_v23.md#gaussian-smoothing) applies in dimension six without a nonsingularity hypothesis.
Indeed, for a smooth compactly supported test function, replacing independent summands by Gaussian vectors with matching covariances cancels the first two Taylor terms.
The summed remainders are bounded by the Hessian modulus at radius \(t\) times the bounded total variance, plus a Hessian bound times this Lindeberg tail and the Gaussian tail sum.
The latter tends to zero because the summed Gaussian fourth moments are bounded by a constant times the largest coefficient square times the total coefficient energy.
First let \(N\to\infty\), then \(t\downarrow0\), and use second-moment tightness and smooth approximation.
Along every subsequence on which \(\Sigma\) converges, \(z_T\) therefore converges weakly to the centered Gaussian with that covariance, uniformly in the sequential compactness sense.
This directly adapts the already recorded swapping argument; it invokes no additional external theorem or source edition.

<a id="two-mode-signed-nonvanishing"></a>
### The quartic polynomial cannot vanish on the Gaussian support

For real symmetric invertible \(B\in M_3(\mathbb R)\), define
\[
 Q_B(u,v)=(u\times v)^TB(u\times v)
 =\det B\left[(u^TB^{-1}u)(v^TB^{-1}v)-(u^TB^{-1}v)^2\right].
 \tag{Two-mode-quartic}
\]
The cross-product formula is the cofactor identity for a three-dimensional two-column Gram determinant.
The [adopted exact signed nonvanishing theorem C187v16](Yin_C_F0187_v16.md#signed-exact-nonvanishing) applies directly and proves that \(Q_B\) is not identically zero on any \(W\) satisfying (Two-mode-transitivity).
Here is the complete matching of its hypotheses, including the potentially singular Gaussian support.
Take its channel count \(p=2\), Hilbert spaces \(\mathcal H=\mathbb R^3\), \(\mathcal E=W\) with the Frobenius inner product, and selfadjoint weight \(A=B^{-1}\).
For \(X=(x_1,x_2)\in W\), define \(U_j:\mathbb R^3\to W\) by \(\langle U_ja,X\rangle=a^Tx_j\).
If \(h\ne0\) and \(U(h)a=(h_1U_1+h_2U_2)a=0\), then \(a^TXh=0\) for every \(X\in W\).
Transitivity forces \(a=0\), so each \(U(h)\) is injective.
Moreover \(A\) is invertible and \(\dim(\ker A)^\perp=3\ge2\).
Its polynomial is precisely \(\det[X^TB^{-1}X]\), so the cited theorem and \(\det B\ne0\) give \(Q_B\not\equiv0\) on \(W\), for every signature of \(B\).
Only that exact nonvanishing conclusion is used; its separate quantitative whole-space Gram theorem and any original-model geometric hypotheses are not imported.
The cited material already retains the real transitivity argument and its DMR08 source scope.

Put \(\alpha_j=N/\lambda_j\), and define
\[
 P_{B,\alpha}(u,v)=\alpha_1\alpha_2\det B
 -\alpha_2u^T\operatorname{adj}(B)u
 -\alpha_1v^T\operatorname{adj}(B)v+Q_B(u,v).
 \tag{Two-mode-proxy}
\]
Its highest homogeneous part is \(Q_B\), independent of \(\alpha\) and unchanged by any affine shift of \((u,v)\).
A Gaussian on \(W\), translated by any \(h_0\in\mathbb R^6\), therefore assigns zero probability to its zero set.
To justify this also for singular covariances, use coordinates on \(W\): the Gaussian has a positive density there, while a nonzero real polynomial has Lebesgue-null zero set.
The latter follows by induction on dimension, treating the polynomial as a polynomial in the last coordinate and applying Fubini outside the zero set of one nonzero coefficient polynomial.

It follows that for every deterministic \(t_N\to0\), uniformly in the admitted strong-pole data,
\[
 \mathbb P\{|P_{B,\alpha}(z_1,z_2)|\le t_N\mid\mathcal F\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Two-mode-quartic-window}
\]
Indeed, discard \(\|h_0\|>R\) at cost at most \(2\Lambda/R^2\), then condition on \(h_0\).
Any contrary sequence has a subsequence on which \(B,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,h_0,\Sigma\) converge, since \(s_{\min}(B)\ge b\), \(\|B\|\le C_B\), and \(|\alpha_j|\le c_j^{-1}\).
The limit \(B\) is invertible, and the limiting covariance retains (Two-mode-transitive-covariance).
The swapping conclusion, continuity of the polynomial coefficients and tightness imply convergence in distribution of the polynomial values.
Their limiting law has no atom at zero by the preceding proof.
Passing first to each fixed closed window and then to zero contradicts the contrary sequence; finally let \(R\to\infty\).
Limits with either \(\alpha_j=0\) cause no difficulty because the same quartic part remains.

<a id="two-strong-poles"></a>
### Two strong poles and the complete graph denominator

For arbitrary symmetric \(X\), the rank-two determinant identity is polynomial and gives
\[
 \alpha_1\alpha_2\det S
 =\alpha_1\alpha_2\det S_E
 -\alpha_2z_1^T\operatorname{adj}(S_E)z_1
 -\alpha_1z_2^T\operatorname{adj}(S_E)z_2+Q_{S_E}(z_1,z_2).
 \tag{Two-mode-exact-determinant}
\]
Here \(Q_X(u,v)=(u\times v)^TX(u\times v)\) defines the expression even when \(X\) is singular.
On \(\|z_1\|^2+\|z_2\|^2\le R^2\) and \(\|S_E-B\|\le1\), the difference from (Two-mode-proxy) is at most \(C_{R,c_1,c_2}\|S_E-B\|\).
The comparison bound, Markov's inequality and the second moments make this difference tend to zero in conditional probability, uniformly, first on bounded \(z\) and then as \(R\to\infty\).
There is no conditional independence assumption in this comparison.

The guards \(\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_E\le N\) and \(\|z_1\|^2+\|z_2\|^2\le N\) fail with probability \(O_K(N^{-1})\).
On their intersection the positive rank-two determinant identity gives
\[
 \alpha_1^2\alpha_2^2\det\Gamma
 =\alpha_1^2\alpha_2^2\det\Gamma_E
 +N\alpha_2^2z_1^T\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma_E)z_1
 +N\alpha_1^2z_2^T\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma_E)z_2
 +N^2 Q_{\Gamma_E}(z_1,z_2)
 \le C_{c_1,c_2}N^5.
 \tag{Two-mode-normalized-graph}
\]
For the last estimate use \(\|\Gamma_E\|\le N\), \(\|\operatorname{adj}\Gamma_E\|\le N^2\), and \(\|z_1\times z_2\|^2\le N^2/4\).
Thus \(|\det F|\le N^{-6}\) implies
\[
 |\alpha_1\alpha_2\det S|\le C_{c_1,c_2}N^{-7/2}.
\]
The quartic window and same-row comparison prove vanishing conditional bad probability on the intrinsic strong-pole good event for each fixed \(c_1,c_2\).

<a id="mixed-two-mode-scales"></a>
### One strong pole and one weak pole

Now suppose \(|\lambda_1|>c_1N\), \(|\lambda_2|\le c_2N\), with \(0<c_2\le c_1\le1\).
Only \(e_1\) needs a reservoir; the preceding construction with \(V=\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}\) gives the same fixed positive mass and vanishing block norm on the intrinsic minor event.
The [single-pole Gaussian argument](Yin_C_F0194_v23.md#gaussian-smoothing) therefore gives, for \(|\alpha_1|\le c_1^{-1}\), the uniform qualitative window property for
\[
 P_1(z_1)=\alpha_1\det B-z_1^T\operatorname{adj}(B)z_1.
\]
Equivalently, if \(\omega_{c_1}(t)\) denotes the upper limit in \(N\) of the supremum of the conditional probability of \(|P_1|\le t\) over this good data, then \(\omega_{c_1}(t)\to0\) as \(t\downarrow0\).
This follows from exactly the compactness proof already given for that nondegenerate three-dimensional covariance.

Set \(S_0=S_E-(\lambda_2/N)z_2z_2^T\).
Then
\[
 \mathbb E[\|S_0-B\|\mid\mathcal F]\le q_N+\Lambda c_2,
 \qquad \alpha_1\det S=\alpha_1\det S_0-z_1^T\operatorname{adj}(S_0)z_1.
\]
On \(\|z_1\|\le R\) and \(\|S_0-B\|\le\varepsilon\le1\), the latter differs from \(P_1(z_1)\) by at most \(C_{R,c_1}\varepsilon\).
The exceptional cost is at most \(\Lambda/R^2+(q_N+\Lambda c_2)/\varepsilon\).

Let \(\Gamma_0=\Gamma_E+(\lambda_2^2/N)z_2z_2^T\).
On the same growing guards as above, \(\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_0\le N+c_2^2N^2\le2N^2\), and
\[
 \alpha_1^2\det\Gamma
 =\alpha_1^2\det\Gamma_0+Nz_1^T\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma_0)z_1
 \le C_{c_1}N^6.
\]
Thus the complete graph bad event implies \(|\alpha_1\det S|\le C_{c_1}N^{-3}\).
For fixed \(R,\varepsilon,c_1,c_2\), take the upper limit in \(N\) to bound its conditional probability by
\[
 \Lambda/R^2+\Lambda c_2/\varepsilon
       +\omega_{c_1}(2C_{R,c_1}\varepsilon).
 \tag{Two-mode-mixed-bound}
\]
First let \(c_2\downarrow0\) at fixed \(c_1,R,\varepsilon\), then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), then \(R\to\infty\).
The bound tends to zero; there is no demand for a uniform strong-pole theorem at a threshold depending on \(N\).
The sign of the weak pole is irrelevant, and no independence from \(z_1\) was used.

<a id="two-mode-all-scales"></a>
### Both poles weak and closure at every scale

If \(|\lambda_1|\le c_1N\), then both poles satisfy this bound and
\[
 \mathbb E[\|S-B\|\mid\mathcal F]\le q_N+2\Lambda c_1,
 \quad
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma\mid\mathcal F]
       \le3+\Lambda K+2\Lambda c_1^2N.
\]
Consequently \(s_{\min}(S)\ge b/2\) fails with conditional probability at most \(2(q_N+2\Lambda c_1)/b\).
When it holds and \(\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma\le b^2N^4/16\), one has \(s_{\min}(F)\ge2N^{-2}\), hence \(|\det F|\ge8N^{-6}\).
Markov's inequality therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{|\det F|\le N^{-6}\mid\mathcal F\}
 \le\frac{2(q_N+2\Lambda c_1)}b
 +\frac{16(3+\Lambda K+2\Lambda c_1^2N)}{b^2N^4}.
 \tag{Two-mode-weak-bound}
\]
Partition the branch into these three measurable pole ranges.
Pay the vanishing stable-background and intrinsic-spread exceptions marginally.
For fixed \(c_1,c_2\), the two-strong contribution tends to zero; the mixed contribution tends to zero when its upper limit is followed by \(c_2\downarrow0\), at fixed \(c_1\).
The remaining upper bound is at most \(4\Lambda c_1/b\), which tends to zero as \(c_1\downarrow0\).
This proves the asserted uniform joint marginal two-mode bound for each fixed \(K\), without a free gap, a pole-size premise or an average residual-tightness hypothesis.
It is not a conditional statement on every bare bounded-energy minor: the stable-background and geometric exceptions are explicitly charged first.


<a id="two-mode-distance"></a>
### The exact distance implication

The actual orthonormal graph for an invertible minor identifies \(d_i=s_{\min}(F)\).
On the stated parent norm event, \(\|F\|\le B_0\sqrt N\), so
\[
 |\det F|\le \|F\|^2d_i\le B_0^2N d_i.
\]
The distance threshold in (Bounded-two-mode-distance) therefore implies (Bounded-two-mode-graph)'s determinant threshold.
This is the same full graph identity used in C194v23 and C194v26; neither a numerator-only bound nor a change in the conditional row law is involved.

<a id="free-gap-assembly"></a>
### Joining the high and bounded residual branches

Assume \(0\notin\sigma(L)\).
Choose once a deterministic common \(0<\theta_N<N^{-9}\) making all required shifted minors invertible almost surely.
For completeness, at each size take the union of the negatives of all atoms of the ordered eigenvalue laws of the finitely many physical principal minors with at most \(r+2\) deletions, including the parent.
Each law has at most countably many atoms, so this union is countable and leaves points in the displayed interval.
A choice outside it has zero probability of coinciding with any such negative eigenvalue.
The choice depends only on the marginal laws and fixed coefficients, not on a realized row or cross-size coupling, and the original fresh border laws are unchanged.
This reproduces the exact original-minor atom-avoidance mechanism in C186v22 at the present shift scale.

Let \(c>0\) be the smaller of one and the constant in C194v26's high-residual graph theorem.
For each fixed \(K\), (Bounded-two-mode-graph) bounds the low-residual contribution at threshold \(cN^{-6}\) by a quantity tending to zero.
For the high-residual contribution define
\[
 H(K)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{D,i}
 \mathbb P\{\mathcal E^{(2)}>K,\ |\det F|\le cN^{-6}\}.
\]
