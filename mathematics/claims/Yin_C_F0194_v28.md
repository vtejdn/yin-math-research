# Three dominant inverse modes control a two-sided Wigner column distance

This conditional theorem advances the original-law least-value route for the real-input polynomial \(AB+2BA\).
It keeps the complete sixth-degree numerator, the same-row graph denominator and the actual physical-column distance.
The three-pole regime remains an explicit minor hypothesis, not a claim that these minors exhaust the ensemble.
The [v27 positive-frame result](Yin_C_F0194_v27.md#statement) stays fixed at its different scope.
The complete conditional theorem is adopted after [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v31.md#three-pole-original-distance-whole-proof).

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact conditional distance statement

Fix real symmetric Wigner inputs with fixed centered variance-one off-diagonal laws of finite fourth moment and fixed centered diagonal laws of finite second moment.
All upper off-diagonal coordinates, diagonal coordinates and the two input colors are independent within each size; lower coordinates are their symmetric copies, and coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
Keep their original normalization \(N^{-1/2}\).
Fix a nonreal \(z\), a norm bound \(K\), and positive constants \(\delta,\rho,c_0\).
At an actual physical minor of size \(n=N-1\), write \(A,B\) for the retained input matrices and suppose \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K\).
The proof also applies to a parent with any fixed number of physical deletions, with \(n=N-O(1)\), its retained companion in place of \(\mathcal L_N\), and the original normalization unchanged.
On the invertible branch put
\[
 G=(AB+2BA-zI)^{-1}
   =U\operatorname{diag}(q_1,q_2,q_3)V^*+E,
 \qquad q_1\ge q_2\ge q_3>0,
 \tag{Three-pole-minor-data}
\]
where \(q_j=s_j(G)\) are the three largest singular values, \(U,V\) have orthonormal columns, and \(E\) is the remaining singular expansion.
Let deterministic \(\epsilon_N,\beta_N>0\) tend to zero and impose
\[
 \|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\epsilon_Nq_3,
 \qquad q_3\ge\sqrt N/\beta_N.
 \tag{Three-pole-scale}
\]
The geometry hypothesis is that for every \(S\subset[n]\) with \(|S|\le\delta N\), writing \(T=S^c\), and every \(w\) in either \(\operatorname{Ran}U\) or \(\operatorname{Ran}\overline V\),
\[
 \|P_Tw\|\ge c_0\|w\|,
 \qquad
 \|P_T(A-aI)w\|^2+\|P_T(B-bI)w\|^2
       \ge\rho^2\|w\|^2\quad(a,b\in\mathbb C).
 \tag{Three-pole-erased-geometry}
\]
Both clauses are needed: the joint gap alone does not bound the row-map directions whose first auxiliary component is zero.
These are all actual-minor-measurable conditions.
Denote their intersection by \(\mathcal G_i\), with exposed diagonals included in the conditioning sigma-field \(\mathcal F_i\) but unrestricted.

Let \(d_i\) be the actual physical three-column distance for the original companion \(\mathcal L_N(z)\), as defined by projection in C196.
There exist fixed \(c,C>0\), depending only on the displayed constants and fixed laws, such that, on every conditioning in \(\mathcal G_i\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\mathcal L_N(z)\|\le B_*,\quad
                  d_i\le cB_*^{-2}N^{-6}\mid\mathcal F_i\}
 \le e_N+Ce^{-cr_N}
           +C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/25},
 \qquad B_*\ge1,
 \tag{Three-pole-original-distance}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\), where
\[
 t_N=C\{N^{-1/2}+(\epsilon_N+\beta_N)^2\},
 \qquad r_N=\lfloor(24t_N)^{-1}\rfloor\longrightarrow\infty,
 \tag{Three-pole-matching-size}
\]
and \(e_N\to0\) is the fixed-law two-copy row-norm exception defined in C194v19, summed over the two inputs.
The constant in \(t_N\) may be increased to absorb the real projection used below.
The parent norm event appears only inside the probability being estimated; it is not conditioned into the original border law.
In particular, the right side is uniform over the good minor and all its inverse magnitudes.


The pair-norm exception may be taken as
\[
 e_N=\sum_{j=1}^2\mathbb P\left\{
 N^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}[(\xi'_{j,k})^2+(\xi''_{j,k})^2]>4\right\},
\]
where the copies are independent with the original raw laws.
It tends to zero by the fixed-law weak law, also when \(n=N-O(1)\).
Write \(b_N\) for the right side of (Three-pole-original-distance), so \(b_N\to0\).
In particular,
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P\{\mathcal G_i,
 \|\mathcal L_N(z)\|\le B_*,\ d_i\le cB_*^{-2}N^{-6}\}
 \le b_N\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Three-pole-distance-average}
\]

<a id="actual-minor-corollary"></a>
### Actual Wigner geometry and parent reduction

There are common constants \(\delta,\rho,c_0>0\) and a common event \(\mathcal E_N\), with \(\mathbb P(\mathcal E_N^c)\le Ce^{-cN}\), such that every actual minor obeying its norm guard and (Three-pole-scale) also satisfies (Three-pole-erased-geometry) on \(\mathcal E_N\), for all sufficiently large \(N\).
This is uniform over parents with any fixed number of prior physical deletions.
Let \(\mathcal H_i\) require invertibility of the root minor and (Three-pole-scale), without separately imposing its geometry.
Put \(\Omega_{N,K}=\{\|A_N\|,\|B_N\|\le K\}\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(A_NB_N+2B_NA_N-zI)\le N^{-7}\}
 \le\mathbb P(\Omega_{N,K}^c)+Ce^{-cN}+Cb_N
       +\frac C N\sum_i\mathbb P(\Omega_{N,K}\cap\mathcal H_i^c).
 \tag{Three-pole-parent-reduction}
\]
Vanishing of the last averaged failure and the norm-guard failure would therefore give this polynomial probability floor.
No coverage of the three-pole regime is asserted.
The statement is for real inputs; complex Hermitian original-law matching and inverse profiles with a residual comparable to or larger than the third pole are outside its conclusion.

### Original and transformed physical blocks

At the selected position, write
\[
 A_N=\begin{pmatrix}a&x^T\\x&A\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B_N=\begin{pmatrix}b&y^T\\y&B\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The diagonal coordinates \(a,b\) are conditioned but unrestricted.
The original companion has physical blocks
\[
 \mathcal L_N=\begin{pmatrix}D_i&R_i\\C_i&Q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D_i=\begin{pmatrix}-z&a&2b\\b&-1&0\\a&0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
 R_i=\begin{pmatrix}0&x^T&2y^T\\y^T&0&0\\x^T&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C_i=\begin{pmatrix}0&x&2y\\y&0&0\\x&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(Q=\mathcal L(A,B;z)\).
Its lower two block equations show that \(Q\) is invertible exactly when \(AB+2BA-zI\) is invertible.
For \(U_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&2\\0&1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), transform every physical row block by \(U_0^T\).
The minor becomes \(\widetilde Q=(U_0^T\otimes I)Q\), the upper border becomes \(R'=U_0^TR_i\), and the lower border is exactly \(R'^T=(U_0^T\otimes I)C_i\).
Thus the transformed Schur matrix is \(U_0^T(D_i-R_iQ^{-1}C_i)\), with determinant \(-2\) times the original numerator.
Both borders still use the same original rows.

## Real-input two-sided pencil and deterministic inverse identities

For real symmetric \(A,B\), put
\[
 U=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&2\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \widetilde L=(U^T\otimes I)\mathcal L
 =\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\A&0&-I\\2B&-2I&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The random coefficient matrices are \(E_{12}+E_{21}\) and \(2(E_{13}+E_{31})\), both real symmetric.
The only transpose-skew part is the deterministic \((E_{23}-E_{32})\otimes I/2\).
The transpose-symmetric part has auxiliary lower block with both off-diagonal entries \(-3I/2\), and its root Schur complement is
\[
 \frac43(AB+BA)-zI.
\]
For fixed nonreal \(z\), this is the resolvent of a real symmetric matrix, so the complete symmetric part has a bounded inverse on each fixed input norm guard.
This direct block calculation is an auxiliary formulation, not an application of an existing arbitrary-shift Wigner floor to a matrix-coefficient pencil.

<a id="rank-three-leading-energy"></a>
## A rank-three dominant inverse has nonzero sixth energy

The following deterministic lemma supplies the leading sixth coefficient estimate.
It first controls raw symmetric tensors; the subsequent proof derives physical matching and original-law distances.
The algebraic ingredient is the accepted complex transitivity statement [DMR08, Proposition 5.1](../audits/literature/DMR08/Yin_U001.md#finite-dimensional-transitive-invertible).

Let real symmetric \(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb R)\) satisfy \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K_0\) and the uniform joint gap
\[
 \|(A-aI)w\|^2+\|(B-bI)w\|^2\ge\rho^2\|w\|^2
 \quad(w\in\mathbb C^n,\ a,b\in\mathbb C).
 \tag{Joint-graph-gap}
\]
For real variable vectors \(u,v\in\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
 L(u,v)=\begin{bmatrix}(Bu+Av)^T\\u^T\\v^T\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 C(u,v)=\begin{bmatrix}2Bu+\tfrac12Av&u&v\end{bmatrix}.
\]
For a complex \(G\in M_n(\mathbb C)\), let \(\mathcal T_6(G)\) be the unique arithmetic-average symmetric complex tensor representing
\[
 p_G(u,v)=\det\bigl(L(u,v)G C(u,v)\bigr)
\]
as a homogeneous degree-six polynomial on \(\mathbb R^{2n}\).
The tensor norm below is its Euclidean Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
There are constants \(c,C,\epsilon_0>0\), depending only on \(K_0,\rho\), such that if
\[
 G=\sum_{j=1}^3 q_j a_jb_j^*+E,\qquad
 q_1\ge q_2\ge q_3>0,
\]
with orthonormal complex triples \((a_j)\), \((b_j)\), then
\[
 \|\mathcal T_6(G)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \ge c q_1q_2q_3
   -C\bigl(q_1q_2\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
            +q_1\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^3\bigr).
 \tag{Rank-three-leading-energy}
\]
In particular, if \(\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\epsilon_0 q_3\), then
\[
 \|\mathcal T_6(G)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge(c/2)q_1q_2q_3.
\]
This assertion allows arbitrary ratios between the three retained poles.

### Exact rank-three factorization and transitivity

Write \(U=[a_1,a_2,a_3]\), \(V=[b_1,b_2,b_3]\), and \(G_3=U\operatorname{diag}(q_1,q_2,q_3)V^*\).
Then
\[
 p_{G_3}(u,v)=q_1q_2q_3
       \det(L(u,v)U)\det(V^*C(u,v)).
 \tag{Rank-three-factorization}
\]
For any nonzero \(h\in\mathbb C^3\), the first matrix space acting on \(h\) is the image of \(w=Uh\) under
\[
 (u,v)\longmapsto\bigl(u^TBw+v^TAw,\ u^Tw,\ v^Tw\bigr).
\]
Its complexification is onto \(\mathbb C^3\).
Indeed an annihilating triple \(\alpha\) would satisfy
\[
 \alpha_1Bw+\alpha_2w=0,\qquad
 \alpha_1Aw+\alpha_3w=0.
\]
If \(\alpha_1=0\), then \(\alpha_2=\alpha_3=0\), while if \(\alpha_1\ne0\), (Joint-graph-gap) gives a contradiction.
The same proof applies to the transpose of the second matrix space, acting on \(w=\overline Vh\), with the nonzero weights \(2\) and \(1/2\).
Each complexified three-by-three matrix space is thus transitive.
The accepted DMR08 proposition proves that each determinant in (Rank-three-factorization) is a nonzero polynomial.
Their product is a nonzero complex polynomial, including when the two matrix-valued maps depend on the same real variables.
There is no independence assumption between these determinants.

### Uniform tensor lower bound by finite-dimensional Gaussian compactness

Let \(X=(u,v)\) have standard real Gaussian coordinates.
The two displayed three-by-three matrices form one vector of eighteen complex linear forms, or thirty-six real linear forms.
Their real covariance has uniformly bounded norm and trace, depending only on \(K_0\).
The preceding transitivity has a quantitative form:
\[
 \mathbb E|\alpha^T L(X)U h|^2
 \ge c_0\|\alpha\|^2\|h\|^2,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E|\alpha^T C(X)^T\overline V h|^2
 \ge c_0\|\alpha\|^2\|h\|^2,
 \tag{Rank-three-quantitative-transitivity}
\]
for a constant \(c_0=c_0(K_0,\rho)>0\).
For the first inequality, the variance is
\(\|\alpha_1Bw+\alpha_2w\|^2+\|\alpha_1Aw+\alpha_3w\|^2\).
If \(|\alpha_1|\) is bounded below relative to \(\|\alpha\|\), the joint gap bounds this variance below.
Otherwise the terms \(\alpha_2w,\alpha_3w\) dominate their bounded perturbations \(\alpha_1Bw,\alpha_1Aw\).
The second inequality is identical with the two fixed nonzero weights absorbed into the constants.

Any sequence of these real covariance matrices has a convergent subsequence.
The quantitative transitivity inequalities survive in the limiting Gaussian support, after complexification.
The two determinant polynomials remain nonzero there by the same transitivity proposition, and their product remains nonzero on the real support.
A nonzero complex polynomial on a real vector space has Gaussian zero-set measure zero: at least one of its real or imaginary parts is nonzero, and induction on the number of coordinates with Fubini proves the assertion.
Its squared absolute value consequently has strictly positive expectation.
Gaussian moment formulas show that this expectation is a continuous function of the real covariance matrix.
Compactness therefore gives a positive uniform lower bound for
\(\mathbb E|\det(L(X)U)\det(V^*C(X))|^2\).

The rank-three polynomial depends on a real linear subspace of dimension at most thirty-six.
On this bounded-dimensional space, the Gaussian \(L^2\) norm of a homogeneous degree-six polynomial is at most a dimension-dependent constant times the Hilbert--Schmidt norm of its symmetric tensor.
This follows directly by expanding the Gaussian moments of degree twelve, or by equivalence of two norms in the fixed finite-dimensional homogeneous-polynomial space.
Real orthogonal changes of variables preserve the tensor norm and the standard Gaussian law.
Thus the preceding positive lower bound gives
\[
 \|\mathcal T_6(G_3)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge c q_1q_2q_3.
\]
The finite-dimensional support restriction is essential to this inference; no dimension-independent Gaussian-to-tensor upper estimate is claimed for an arbitrary high-rank \(G\).

### Perturbing the three-pole part

Each entry of \(L(u,v)T C(u,v)\), regarded as a quadratic tensor, has norm at most \(C_{K_0}\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\).
The mixed determinant is a symmetric trilinear map of these quadratic matrices, and arithmetic symmetrization contracts tensor Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
It therefore has tensor norm at most \(C_{K_0}\prod_{j=1}^3\|T_j\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\).
When the same rank-one matrix \(a_jb_j^*\) occurs in two slots, its mixed determinant vanishes, because \(L a_jb_j^* C\) has rank at most one for every variable value.
Expand \(p_{G_3+E}\) trilinearly and discard those repeated-head terms.
The terms with one residual factor have total norm at most \(Cq_1q_2\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\), the terms with two have norm at most \(Cq_1\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\), and the all-residual term has norm at most \(C\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^3\).
The triangle inequality proves (Rank-three-leading-energy), and a sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon_0\) gives its consequence.

### Connection with the actual sixth row layer and remaining scope

For the symmetric-noise companion above, its inverse is exactly
\[
 \widetilde Q^{-1}=WGV+Z_0,
 \qquad W=\begin{bmatrix}I\\B\\A\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad V=\begin{bmatrix}I&2B&A/2\end{bmatrix},
\]
where \(G=(AB+2BA-zI)^{-1}\) and \(Z_0\) has only auxiliary blocks \((2,3)=-I/2\), \((3,2)=-I\).
For the actual border
\[
 R'(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&x^T&2y^T\\x^T&0&0\\2y^T&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
one has \(R'W=L(x,2y)\) and \(VR'^T=C(x,2y)\).
The constant inverse part has the exact contraction
\[
 R'Z_0R'^T=-3(x^Ty)E_{11}.
\]
Thus, writing \(M_G=L(x,2y)G C(x,2y)\), the complete degree-six part of the actual Schur determinant is
\[
 -\det M_G+3(x^Ty)\operatorname{cof}_{11}(M_G).
 \tag{Complete-sixth-correction}
\]
The constant exposed corner contributes no degree-six term.
The cofactor is quadratic in \(G\), and its mixed expression vanishes when the same rank-one head occurs twice.
The same tensor-product bounds as above therefore bound its fourth tensor by
\[
 C\bigl(q_1q_2+q_1\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\bigr).
\]
The quadratic tensor of \(x^Ty\) has norm at most \(\sqrt n\), so the correction in (Complete-sixth-correction) has sixth tensor norm at most
\[
 C\sqrt n\bigl(q_1q_2+q_1\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\bigr).
\]
Consequently, along any sequence with \(\|E\|_{\mathrm{HS}}/q_3\to0\) and \(q_3/\sqrt N\to\infty\), the complete sixth tensor of the actual Schur numerator has norm at least \(c' q_1q_2q_3\) eventually.
The fixed substitution \((u,v)=(x,2y)\) changes constants only: its smallest singular value is one and its largest is two.
This closes raw noncancellation in the stated three-dominant-pole regime, including the actual constant auxiliary inverse contribution.
The next subsection supplies distinct-physical coefficient energy, low physical influence and the full graph comparison under an explicit erased-coordinate geometry hypothesis.
The regimes of fewer than three dominant poles with a large diffuse residual, and a general high-effective-rank inverse, remain separate.

<a id="erased-graph-transitivity"></a>
### Exact erased Gram formulas and approximate graph stability

The quantitative row-map step also has an exact Gram formulation that is useful for approximate companion graphs.
For a nonzero root vector \(w\), write \(w_T=P_Tw\), \(a_T=P_TAw\), \(b_T=P_TBw\), and assume the two clauses of (Three-pole-erased-geometry) for this vector.
The actual symmetric-border map of the right graph \((w,Bw,Aw)\), restricted to real variables \(x,y\) supported on \(T\), is
\[
 (x,y)\longmapsto
 \bigl(x^Tb_T+2y^Ta_T,\ x^Tw_T,\ 2y^Tw_T\bigr)^T.
\]
Let \(H_R\) be the Hermitian Gram matrix of its three complex coefficient rows in \(\mathbb C^{2|T|}\).
The last two coefficient rows are orthogonal, with squared norms \(\|w_T\|^2\) and \(4\|w_T\|^2\).
Orthogonally removing their span from the first row therefore gives the exact formula
\[
 \det H_R=4\|w_T\|^4
 \left\{\operatorname{dist}(b_T,\mathbb Cw_T)^2
       +4\operatorname{dist}(a_T,\mathbb Cw_T)^2\right\}.
 \tag{Erased-right-Gram}
\]
For the transpose-left graph \((w,2Bw,Aw/2)\), the corresponding map is
\[
 (x,y)\longmapsto
 \bigl(2x^Tb_T+y^Ta_T,\ x^Tw_T,\ 2y^Tw_T\bigr)^T,
\]
and its coefficient Gram matrix satisfies
\[
 \det H_L=4\|w_T\|^4
 \left\{4\operatorname{dist}(b_T,\mathbb Cw_T)^2
       +\operatorname{dist}(a_T,\mathbb Cw_T)^2\right\}.
 \tag{Erased-transpose-left-Gram}
\]
The second graph is the transpose of the exact row factor \([I,2B,A/2]\) in the transformed inverse, and applies to left mode vectors \(w\in\operatorname{Ran}\overline V\) in the rank-three factorization.
Minimizing the two independent complex centers in the erased joint gap shows that the unweighted sum of the two displayed squared distances is at least \(\rho^2\|w\|^2\).
Both determinants are consequently at least \(4c_0^4\rho^2\|w\|^6\).
The norm guard bounds either Gram trace by \(C_K\|w\|^2\).
The product of the two largest Gram eigenvalues is at most the square of that trace, so
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(H_R),\ \lambda_{\min}(H_L)
       \ge c(K,c_0,\rho)\|w\|^2.
 \tag{Erased-graph-coefficient-gap}
\]
This proves quantitative complex surjectivity, including directions in which the first auxiliary functional coefficient is small or zero.
The fixed substitution \((x,y)\leftrightarrow(u,v)=(x,2y)\) preserves this conclusion with fixed changed constants.

The same conclusion is stable for an approximate graph.
If a vector differs from either displayed exact graph by \(e\in\mathbb C^{3n}\), its symmetric-border coefficient matrix differs in operator norm by at most \(2\|e\|\), also after restriction to \(T\).
The singular-value perturbation inequality therefore preserves a lower bound \(c'\|w\|\) whenever \(\|e\|\le c''\|w\|\), for a sufficiently small fixed \(c''\).
If the approximate companion vector is normalized and \(\|e\|\le1/2\), the bounded exact graph norm also gives \(\|w\|\ge c_K>0\).
Thus sufficiently accurate normalized approximate graphs have a dimension-independent coefficient singular-value lower bound whenever their root vectors satisfy the two erased geometry clauses.
The actual-minor proof below supplies those clauses to the two singular mode spaces by C187v29 and C196.
This stability assertion is geometric; no Gaussian replacement of the original border law is inferred from it.

### Flattening the six graph modes while preserving transitivity

Write the columns of \(U,V\) as \(a_j,b_j\), and define the physical load
\[
 h_k=\sum_{j=1}^3\bigl(
 |(a_j)_k|^2+|(Aa_j)_k|^2+|(Ba_j)_k|^2

### Distinct physical coefficients and their loads

The squared mass where a specified physical position occurs in at least one slot has the same upper bound, by summing the six slot incidences.
