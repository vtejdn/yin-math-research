# Averaged physical distances and a probability floor for real fourth-moment Wigner products

This theorem closes the averaged physical-column distance estimate for the real-input polynomial \(AB+2BA\), using all inverse singular-value profiles.
It combines the fixed all-profile raw theorem with spread of the actual inverse and its first two root singular residuals.
It also includes the exterior-of-spectrum argument under finite second moments on the diagonal.
The conclusion is an in-probability polynomial least-singular-value floor for almost every fixed spectral parameter.
The complete theorem is adopted after [independent whole-proof review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v33.md#all-profile-distance-whole-proof); the preceding high-rank and three-pole results remain fixed at their own scopes.
No general complex-input matching theorem or almost-sure Brown-measure conclusion is asserted.

## Exact model and conclusions

Let \(A_N,B_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
Their strict upper entries are independent copies of fixed real centered variance-one laws \(\xi_A/\sqrt N,\xi_B/\sqrt N\), each with finite fourth moment.
Their real diagonal coordinates are independent copies of fixed centered finite-second-moment laws, also divided by \(\sqrt N\).
All upper pairs, diagonals and input colors are independent within each size; coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
Put
\[
 P_N=A_NB_N+2B_NA_N,\qquad P=s_1s_2+2s_2s_1,
\]
where \(s_1,s_2\) are standard free semicircular operators in a faithful tracial model.
Let \(Z_0\) be the deterministic full-area finite-invertibility set of C197v1, and put \(Z_*=Z_0\setminus\mathbb R\), which also has full planar Lebesgue measure.
Let
\[
 \mathcal L_N(z)=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A_N&2B_N\\B_N&-I&0\\A_N&0&-I\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Omega_{N,K}=\{\|A_N\|,\|B_N\|\le K\}.
\]
For each physical position \(i\), let \(d_i(z)\) be the least singular value of the three columns at \(i\) after orthogonal projection onto the complement of all other columns.
This projection-defined quantity is well defined even when those other columns are dependent.

For every fixed \(z\in Z_*\) and \(K\ge1\), there exists \(c_{K,z}>0\) such that
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb P\{\Omega_{N,K},\ d_i(z)\le c_{K,z}N^{-6}\}
       \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{All-profile-averaged-distance}
\]
For every fixed \(z\in Z_*\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(P_N-zI)\le N^{-7}\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Real-fourth-moment-probability-floor}
\]
There is no remaining exceptional inverse-profile term.
The assertions have no claimed rate or summability and do not strengthen the original Task's full finite-variance hypothesis to a proved conclusion.

A stronger exterior statement holds for every fixed \(z\in\rho(P)\), whether or not \(z\in Z_*\): with
\(\delta_z=\|(P-z)^{-1}\|^{-1}>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{s_{\min}(P_N-zI)\ge\delta_z/2\}\longrightarrow1.
 \tag{Exterior-constant-floor}
\]
On this exterior branch, the averaged distance assertion holds for every deterministic threshold tending to zero, on a fixed norm guard.
The spectrum here is the ordinary operator spectrum, not Brown-measure support.

### Uniform conditional theorem used on the spectral branch

Fix \(z\notin\mathbb R\), \(K\ge1\), and any fixed bound on prior physical deletions.
At an actual retained local minor of dimension \(n=N-O(1)\ge3\), keep the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization and write
\[
 G=(AB+2BA-zI)^{-1}=\sum_{j=1}^nq_ju_jv_j^*,\quad q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_n>0,
 \qquad E_k=\sum_{j>k}q_ju_jv_j^*,\quad F_k=\|E_k\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \quad(k=0,1,2).
\]
Choose any measurable singular-vector convention; all estimates are uniform in choices within ties.
For \(k=0,1,2\), define
\[
 \mathcal T_k=\{E_k,BE_k,E_kB,BE_kB\},\qquad
 D_k=\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_k}\sum_{\ell=1}^n|T_{\ell\ell}|^2,
\]
\[
 m_k=\max_\ell\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_k}
       (\|e_\ell^*T\|^2+\|Te_\ell\|^2),
\]
\[
 h_* =\max_\ell\sum_{j=1}^2
  (|(u_j)_\ell|^2+|(Bu_j)_\ell|^2+|(v_j)_\ell|^2+|(Bv_j)_\ell|^2),
 \qquad \Delta=h_*+\sum_{k=0}^2\frac{D_k+m_k}{F_k^2}.
 \tag{All-profile-physical-data}
\]
Let \(L\) be the all-profile energy constant of C187v31 at \(K,z\).
For any deterministic \(\alpha_N\downarrow0\) with \(\alpha_N\ge N^{-1}\), let \(\mathcal G_i\) be the event consisting of actual root-minor invertibility and
\[
 \|A\|,\|B\|\le K,\qquad F_2\ge L\sqrt N,\qquad \Delta\le\alpha_N.
 \tag{All-profile-good-minor}
\]
Condition on the original minor and the two exposed original diagonal entries, with sigma-field \(\mathcal F_i\).
There are fixed positive constants \(c,C,c_1\), independent of the inverse profile and of those diagonal values, such that for all sufficiently large \(N\), on \(\mathcal G_i\) and for every \(B_*\ge1\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\mathcal L_N(z)\|\le B_*,\quad
 d_i(z)\le cB_*^{-2}N^{-6}\mid\mathcal F_i\}
 \le e_N+Ce^{-cr_N}+C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/25}
 =:b_N\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{All-profile-conditional-distance}
\]
where \(r_N=\lfloor c_1/\alpha_N\rfloor\to\infty\) and
\[
 e_N=\sum_{a\in\{A,B\}}
 \mathbb P\left\{\frac1N\sum_{\ell=1}^N
       [ (\xi'_{a,\ell})^2+(\xi''_{a,\ell})^2]>4\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The raw copies in this expression are independent and have the original laws.
Using \(N\) copies bounds the corresponding exception for every retained row of length \(n\le N\).
For a parent with prior deletions, replace \(\mathcal L_N,d_i\) by its retained companion and its actual physical distance, leaving the ambient normalization and displayed powers of \(N\) unchanged.
The parent norm event is an intersection inside the probability, never a conditioning imposed on the fresh law.

## Complete argument

<a id="mixed-physical-transfer"></a>
### Six-profile energy and the correct factor expansion

On a good minor, assign \(W\) by the exhaustive ordered six-case rule in C187v31.
The theorem applies because \(F_2\ge L\sqrt N\ge L\sqrt n\).
For the complete original Schur numerator \(\Phi\), it gives
\[
 \Phi_{4,2}=4h_B(G),\qquad
 h_B(G)(x,y)=\det([Bx,x,y]^TG[Bx,x,y]),\qquad
 \|\mathscr T(h_B(G))\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge cW.
 \tag{All-profile-outer-energy}
\]
Here \(\Phi_{4,2}\) is the component of degree four in \(x\) and degree two in \(y\).
The constant auxiliary inverse contribution has another bidegree and cannot change these coefficients.

Use the full expansion for rows 1 and 6 of that rule, the one-head expansion for rows 2 and 5, and the two-head expansion for rows 3 and 4.
In the last two expansions cancel every group containing a repeated identical rank-one head as an entire polynomial before estimating its factors.
The cancellation is exact because the matrix-valued quadratic form of one head has rank at most one, so its determinant is affine in that head's scalar parameter.
For the actual heads \(H_j=u_jv_j^*\), the surviving product scales are respectively
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{full}&F_0^3\\
 \text{one head}&q_1F_1^2,\ F_1^3\\
 \text{two heads}&q_1q_2F_2,\ q_1F_2^2,\ q_2F_2^2,\ F_2^3.
 \end{array}
\]
There are only finitely many determinant terms, with a bound independent of the dimension and the profile.
C187v31 proves that the sum of their product scales is at most \(CW\) in the assigned row.
This compatibility is needed for physical errors as well as for the raw lower bound.

Let \(\pi_\ell\) retain the two fresh variables \((x_\ell,y_\ell)\).
For a residual \(E_k\), every quadratic entry of its three-by-three matrix is represented by a symmetric tensor obtained from a block embedding of \(E_k,BE_k,E_kB,BE_kB\) and their transposes.
Indeed the linear maps are \(V_1(x,y)=Bx,V_2(x,y)=x,V_3(x,y)=y\), so the representing matrix of \(V_a^TE_kV_b\) is its arithmetic transpose-symmetrization.
The explicit embeddings give, for every such quadratic tensor \(K\),
\[
 \|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C_KF_k,\qquad
 \sum_\ell\|\pi_\ell K\pi_\ell\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le CD_k,
\]
\[
 \|\pi_\ell K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\|K\pi_\ell\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le C\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_k}
       (\|e_\ell^*T\|^2+\|Te_\ell\|^2)\le Cm_k.
 \tag{Residual-quadratic-controls}
\]
The use of transpose is necessary because neither the root inverse nor its residuals need be symmetric.

A head entry factors exactly as
\[
 V_a^T(q_ju_jv_j^*)V_b=q_j(V_a^Tu_j)(v_j^*V_b).
\]
Keep it as two linear factors.
Their coefficient vectors are block embeddings of \(u_j,Bu_j,\overline v_j,B\overline v_j\); because \(B\) is real, the last two have exactly the coordinate magnitudes controlled by \(v_j,Bv_j\).
Each linear vector has norm at most \(C_K\) and squared physical-coordinate mass at most \(h_*\).
This is the point where the present physical matching proof uses real symmetric inputs.

Consequently every surviving determinant product is a tensor product of linear head factors and quadratic residual factors, followed by arithmetic symmetrization.
Its total tensor order is six.
Factor out its head singular-value weights and one \(F_k\) for each residual quadratic factor.
Call their product \(S\), so the sum of these \(S\)'s over all surviving terms is at most \(CW\).
The remaining factors have bounded Hilbert--Schmidt norms.
Every linear factor has maximal squared physical mass at most \(\Delta\), and every normalized quadratic factor has maximal row/column mass and total within-physical diagonal squared mass at most \(C\Delta\).

<a id="mixed-collision-bounds"></a>
### Repeated physical positions and individual incidences

Consider one unsymmetrized product with scale \(S\).
If two designated slots belong to the same residual quadratic factor and share a physical position, restricting that factor to its diagonal physical blocks gives squared mass at most \(C\Delta S^2\) by (Residual-quadratic-controls).
A linear factor contains only one slot, so it has no internal collision.
If the two designated slots belong to different factors, sum over their common physical position.
For either a linear or a quadratic factor, let \(a_\ell\) be the squared norm of its slice at that slot and position, after normalization.
Then \(\sum_\ell a_\ell\le C\) and \(\max_\ell a_\ell\le C\Delta\).
For the other factor use analogous \(b_\ell\), obtaining
\[
 \sum_\ell a_\ell b_\ell\le(\max_\ell a_\ell)\sum_\ell b_\ell\le C\Delta.
\]
The untouched slots contribute only bounded squared factor norms, proving the same \(C\Delta S^2\) collision estimate.
This includes collisions between the two linear factors originating from one head entry.

There are fifteen pairs of slots, a fixed number of permutations in the symmetrization, and a fixed number of surviving determinant products.
The triangle inequality therefore bounds the norm of the full outer tensor supported on any repeated physical position by
\[
 C\sqrt\Delta\sum S\le C\sqrt\Delta W.
 \tag{All-profile-collision-loss}
\]
For the incidence of one specified physical position in one specified slot, its sliced factor has squared norm at most \(C\Delta\), and all other factors contribute their bounded full squared norms.
The same finite-sum argument gives the separate maximal squared incidence bound
\[
 \max_\ell\{\text{squared outer-tensor mass incident to }\ell\}
       \le C\Delta W^2.
 \tag{All-profile-tensor-load}
\]
No coordinate was erased from the inverse or from any of its products, and no commutator identity was altered.

For \(\Delta\le\alpha_N\to0\), subtracting (All-profile-collision-loss) from (All-profile-outer-energy) leaves tensor norm at least \(cW\) on six distinct physical positions, for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Let \(\sigma^2\) be the coefficient energy of that selected \((4,2)\) family after substituting formal variables \((x,y)=\varepsilon/\sqrt N\).
For six distinct individual variables, the polynomial coefficient is \(6!\) times a corresponding arithmetic-symmetric tensor entry, and the sum of ordered entries gives the same fixed-factor energy conversion.
Thus
\[
 \sigma\ge cWN^{-3},\qquad
 \max_\ell\sum_{J\ni\ell}|c_J|^2\le C\alpha_NW^2N^{-6}
                                      \le C'\alpha_N\sigma^2.
 \tag{All-profile-physical-sixth}
\]
Here incidence means either original input at that physical position, and the sum is over the selected family.
Every selected coefficient belongs to the actual complete degree-six numerator.
Choose, measurably from the minor, its real or imaginary part retaining at least half this selected energy.
Its loads obey the same normalized bound after changing a fixed constant.
Other bidegrees, repeated-position terms and lower-degree coefficients remain in the actual polynomial and are unrestricted.

<a id="full-graph-identification"></a>
### The same complete graph realizes the physical distance

In physical block order write the original companion as
\[
 \mathcal L_N=\begin{pmatrix}D_i&R\\C&Q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}0&x^T&2y^T\\y^T&0&0\\x^T&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 C=\begin{pmatrix}0&x&2y\\y&0&0\\x&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The exposed corner \(D_i\) is fixed under the conditioning and includes the original diagonal values.
The exact companion identity is
\[
 Q^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}I\\B\\A\end{bmatrix}
 G\begin{bmatrix}I&A&2B\end{bmatrix}+\operatorname{diag}(0,-I,-I).
\]
Set
\[
 \Gamma=I_3+RQ^{-1}Q^{-*}R^*,\qquad
 F_i=\Gamma^{-1/2}(D_i-RQ^{-1}C).
\]
The rows of \(\Gamma^{-1/2}[I_3,-RQ^{-1}]\) are orthonormal and annihilate all outside columns.
Those columns have full rank because their retained square block \(Q\) is invertible, so these rows span their orthogonal complement.
Hence
\[
 d_i=s_{\min}(F_i),\qquad \|F_i\|\le\|\mathcal L_N(z)\|.
\]
The C187v31 full-graph comparison gives
\[
 \sup_{\|x\|,\|y\|\le2}\sqrt{\det\Gamma}
 \le C_K\prod_{j=1}^3(1+q_j)\le C_K'W=:a_i.
 \tag{All-profile-full-area}
\]
For completeness, the first three singular values of \(Q^{-1}\) are at most \(C_Kq_j+1\), the complete border \(R\) has bounded norm on this row support, and the graph area is the product of the three factors \(\sqrt{1+s_j(RQ^{-1})^2}\).
Also \(F_2^2\le(n-2)q_3^2\) and \(F_2\ge L\sqrt N\), with \(L\ge1,N\ge n\), give \(q_3\ge1\).
The assigned scale satisfies \(W\ge q_1q_2q_3\), proving the last inequality.
This denominator uses the complete actual inverse and every original row coordinate.
Equation (All-profile-physical-sixth) therefore yields \(\sigma\ge c a_iN^{-3}\).

<a id="exact-original-law-matching"></a>
### Exact two-copy matching for the complete numerator

The selected real coefficient family has at most \((2N)^6\) members.
Discard coefficients of magnitude below \(\sigma/[C(2N)^3]\), losing at most half the energy for a fixed sufficiently large \(C\).
At each greedy step, a chosen monomial touches six physical positions and removes at most six times the maximum physical load.
The bound in (All-profile-physical-sixth) therefore supplies at least \(r_N=\lfloor c_1/\alpha_N\rfloor\) pairwise disjoint physical six-sets, with every retained coefficient at least \(c a_iN^{-6}\) in modulus.
Take \(c_1\) sufficiently small, also below \(1/12\); \(\alpha_N\ge N^{-1}\) then keeps this chosen count below the physical packing limit.
The selected matching uses only the conditioned minor.

For each original raw coordinate, independently across colors and positions, use
\[
 \xi=m+b\epsilon,\qquad
 m=(\xi'+\xi'')/2,\quad b=(\xi'-\xi'')/2,
\]
where the two copies have the original law and \(\epsilon\) is an independent fair sign.
Averaging over both copies and the independent sign reproduces the original law, conditional on the old minor and exposed diagonals; conditioning further on fixed pairs leaves the fair-sign law used below.
Each fixed variance-one real law has constants \(b_0,p_0>0\) with \(\mathbb P\{|b|\ge b_0\}\ge p_0\); choose common constants for both laws.
Disjoint matching edges survive independently with probability at least \(p_0^6\), leaving a fixed positive fraction except with probability \(Ce^{-cr_N}\).
Conditioning on all pairs leaves all signs independent and fair.
Because the total original degree is at most six, translating the variables and reducing sign powers cannot change the coefficient of six distinct retained variables except for its exact multiplier \(\prod b\).
No excluded sixth or lower-degree term can contribute to that same six-variable coefficient.
The fair-sign polynomial thus has the required matching rank with coefficient threshold at least \(c a_iN^{-6}\).

For real pairs, \((|m|+|b|)^2\le(\xi')^2+(\xi'')^2\).
Outside the pair-measurable exception of probability at most \(e_N\), every sign choice of both complete normalized rows therefore satisfies \(\|x\|,\|y\|\le2\).
The fixed-law weak law gives \(e_N\to0\).
Throughout that paired support and the parent norm intersection,
\[
 |\det(D_i-RQ^{-1}C)|
 =|\det F_i|\sqrt{\det\Gamma}\le B_*^2d_i a_i.
\]
A sufficiently small fixed \(c\) in (All-profile-conditional-distance) makes the implied interval for the selected real component shorter than its surviving coefficient threshold.
Apply the exact MNV16 degree-six fair-sign estimate to this complete polynomial.
It gives \(C\sqrt{\log r_N}\,r_N^{-1/25}\), uniformly in every lower coefficient, exposed diagonal and interval center.
Adding the matching-survival and pair-norm exceptions proves (All-profile-conditional-distance).
The norm restriction entered only as an event implying this interval containment, not as a conditioning that changes the signs.

<a id="spectral-average"></a>
### Actual spectral minors and the averaged distance estimate

Now fix \(z\in Z_0\cap\sigma(P)\setminus\mathbb R\).
C197v2 gives, uniformly over all bounded-deletion minors on their own norm guards,
\[
 F_2^2/N\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 (D_k+m_k)/F_k^2\longrightarrow0\ (k=0,1,2),\qquad h_*\longrightarrow0
\]
in its guarded almost-sure sense.
Its real-input head statement controls \(B\overline v_j=\overline{Bv_j}\), exactly as required above.
All the finite minors are invertible almost surely at this fixed \(z\) by the definition of \(Z_0\).
The uniformity includes every choice within tied singular spaces.
Consequently the guarded maximum of \(\Delta\), set to zero if no guarded minor exists, tends to zero in probability.
Choose a deterministic nonincreasing sequence \(\alpha_N\to0\), enlarged to at least \(N^{-1}\), so that the probability that this maximum exceeds \(\alpha_N\) tends to zero.
One explicit selection chooses successive deterministic size cutoffs for tolerances \(1/j\) and failure probabilities \(1/j\), and keeps that tolerance until the next cutoff.
The probability of any guarded minor failing \(F_2\ge L\sqrt N\) also tends to zero.
These statements concern guarded failures; no eventual occurrence or summable complement of the norm guard is assumed.

On \(\Omega_{N,K}\), each local minor inherits its norm bound and
\(\|\mathcal L_N(z)\|\le B_{K,z}:=|z|+2+3K\).
Integrate the conditional estimate, intersect with the parent norm guard and add the preceding common good-minor failure.
It follows that
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P\{\Omega_{N,K},\ d_i\le cB_{K,z}^{-2}N^{-6}\}
 \le b_N+o(1)\longrightarrow0.
\]
There is no inverse-profile failure to add, because C187v31 partitions all profiles satisfying the energy threshold.
This proves (All-profile-averaged-distance) on the entire free spectral branch.

<a id="exterior-proof"></a>
### Exterior spectral inclusion under the actual moment and coupling assumptions

Fix \(z\in\rho(P)\), and put \(\delta_z=\|(P-z)^{-1}\|^{-1}>0\).
Write \(A_N=A_N^0+D_{A,N}\), \(B_N=B_N^0+D_{B,N}\), where the superscript zero means that all diagonal entries have been set to zero.
These zero-diagonal inputs meet And13-U001's full assumptions: the real off-diagonal laws satisfy its real/imaginary independence condition, and deterministic zero diagonals have finite fourth moments.
For the fixed selfadjoint polynomial
\[
 f_z(X,Y)=(XY+2YX-z)^*(XY+2YX-z),
\]
the free evaluation is at least \(\delta_z^2I\).
Its scalar spectral law is therefore supported in \([\delta_z^2,\infty)\).
Anderson's Theorem 2, with spectral inclusion tolerance \(7\delta_z^2/16\), gives
\[
 s_{\min}(A_N^0B_N^0+2B_N^0A_N^0-zI)\ge3\delta_z/4
 \tag{Exterior-zero-diagonal-floor}
\]
eventually almost surely under the source's infinite-array coupling.
Such a coupling realizes the present fixed zero-diagonal laws.
For each individual size its joint two-input law is exactly the one here, so the probability of this event tends to one under every prescribed cross-size coupling.
Only this marginal probability consequence is used.
The same coupling argument with Anderson's Corollary 1 gives
\[
 \|A_N^0\|\to2,\qquad \|B_N^0\|\to2\quad\text{in probability}.
 \tag{Zero-diagonal-norm-limit}
\]

For either fixed diagonal law \(d\), its finite second moment implies, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P\{N^{-1/2}\max_{i\le N}|d_i|>\varepsilon\}
 &\le N\mathbb P\{|d|>\varepsilon\sqrt N\}\\
 &\le\varepsilon^{-2}\mathbb E[d^2\mathbf1_{\{|d|>\varepsilon\sqrt N\}}]
 \longrightarrow0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\|D_{A,N}\|+\|D_{B,N}\|=o_{\mathbb P}(1)\).
Expanding the two products gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|P_N-(A_N^0B_N^0+2B_N^0A_N^0)\|
 \le3\bigl(&\|D_{A,N}\|\|B_N^0\|
 +\|D_{B,N}\|\|A_N^0\|\\
 &+\|D_{A,N}\|\|D_{B,N}\|\bigr)
 =o_{\mathbb P}(1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The least singular value changes by at most the operator norm of a perturbation, by its variational characterization.
Intersect (Exterior-zero-diagonal-floor) with the event that this perturbation is at most \(\delta_z/4\).
Both events have probability tending to one, proving (Exterior-constant-floor).
This argument also proves \(\|A_N\|,\|B_N\|\to2\) in probability.
It supplies no almost-sure conclusion under arbitrary cross-size coupling.

On the exterior constant-floor event the parent root inverse has norm at most \(2/\delta_z\).
The exact parent companion inverse gives, on \(\Omega_{N,K}\),
\[
 \|\mathcal L_N(z)^{-1}\|
 \le1+2(1+2K)(1+3K)/\delta_z=:c_{z,K}^{-1}.
\]
If \(C_i\) denotes its three columns and \(H_i\) the span of all the other columns, then for every unit \(v\in\mathbb C^3\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(C_iv,H_i)
 =\inf_w\|\mathcal L_N(E_iv+E_{i^c}w)\|
 \ge s_{\min}(\mathcal L_N)\inf_w\sqrt{1+\|w\|^2}
 \ge c_{z,K}.
\]
Here \(E_i,E_{i^c}\) are the canonical isometric coordinate embeddings.
Thus every physical distance is bounded below on this same parent event.
For any deterministic \(t_N\to0\), eventually
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\mathbb P\{\Omega_{N,K},\ d_i\le t_N\}
 \le\mathbb P\{s_{\min}(P_N-zI)<\delta_z/2\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
There is no union over minors in this deduction.
For completeness, a fixed deterministic bounded deletion changes the input law to \(\sqrt{n/N}\) times the standard \(n\)-dimensional pair.
Its quadratic polynomial scales by \(n/N\), and the additional error is \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) because the zero-diagonal norm bound and diagonal perturbation give \(\|P_n\|=O_{\mathbb P}(1)\).
The same constant-floor proof applies to each such deterministic minor after combining that scaling error with the diagonal error before using the fixed \(\delta_z/4\) perturbation allowance.
No simultaneous statement over adaptive exterior deletions is asserted.

### The parent probability floor and norm-guard removal

Every \(z\in Z_*\) lies either in \(\sigma(P)\) or in \(\rho(P)\).
The spectral average and exterior argument therefore prove (All-profile-averaged-distance) for every such fixed \(z\).
Apply C196's physical distance average with \(\varepsilon=N^{-7}\).
Its required distance threshold is \(C\sqrt N\varepsilon=CN^{-13/2}\), which is at most \(c_{K,z}N^{-6}\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
It follows that
\[
 \mathbb P\{\Omega_{N,K},\ s_{\min}(\mathcal L_N(z))\le N^{-7}\}
 \le Ce^{-cN}+o(1)\longrightarrow0.
\]
If a unit root vector has residual \(\|(P_N-zI)u\|\le N^{-7}\), its lift \((u,B_Nu,A_Nu)\) has norm at least one and companion residual \(((P_N-zI)u,0,0)\).
After normalizing the lift, the companion least singular value is at most \(N^{-7}\).
Therefore the same guarded probability bound holds for the root least-singular-value event.
Finally choose any fixed \(K>2\).
The preceding zero-diagonal norm limit and finite-second-moment diagonal argument give \(\mathbb P(\Omega_{N,K}^c)\to0\), so removing this guard proves (Real-fourth-moment-probability-floor).

All uses of almost-sure input geometry were reduced to uniform guarded failure probabilities before this final assembly.
The result is an in-probability floor for the real fourth-moment subcase, with arbitrary cross-size coupling and finite second diagonal moments.
No summable failure bound, complex original-law coefficient transfer or almost-sure Brown-measure theorem has been derived.
