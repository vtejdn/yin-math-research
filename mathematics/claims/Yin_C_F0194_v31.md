# Original common-complement reconstruction and finite-family exclusions for a two-sided Wigner product

This theorem retains the completed original-law reconstruction estimates for \(AB+2BA\), together with the exact finite-family low-concentration exclusions they support.
The [v30 averaged-distance and probability-floor theorem](Yin_C_F0194_v30.md#statement) remains separately valid at its adopted scope.
The present estimates have exponentially small guarded identification failures, but they do not establish a summable least-value floor, a summable planar defect or full-sequence almost-sure Brown convergence.
The complete argument and the precise remaining structural and entropy questions are retained below, without a rolling proof premise.
The complete statement and argument are adopted with the [independent whole-proof finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v34.md#original-reconstruction-whole-proof).

## Exact model and completed conclusions

For each \(N\), let \(A_N,B_N\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices.
Their strict upper entries have the fixed respective laws \(\xi_A/\sqrt N,\xi_B/\sqrt N\), with
\[
 \mathbb E\xi_A=\mathbb E\xi_B=0,\qquad
 \mathbb E\xi_A^2=\mathbb E\xi_B^2=1.
\]
Their real diagonal entries have fixed centered finite-second-moment laws divided by \(\sqrt N\).
All upper coordinates, diagonal coordinates and input colors are independent within each size; the coupling across sizes is arbitrary.
The two off-diagonal laws may differ.
The conclusions below require only their displayed second moments, so in particular apply to the real fourth-moment model.
All minors and rectangles retain the original ambient \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Write
\[
 P_N=A_NB_N+2B_NA_N,
 \qquad \Omega_{N,K_0}=\{\|A_N\|,\|B_N\|\le K_0\},
\]
where \(K_0\ge1\) is fixed, and fix \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R\).
Every norm guard in a probability below is an event intersection, not a conditioning that changes the fresh original law.
Constants may depend on the fixed laws, \(z\), the displayed norm bounds and positive dimension or variance constants, but not on \(N\), the deterministic shifts or the prescribed candidate vectors.
Probabilistic conclusions are for all sufficiently large \(N\).

1. **Spread linear images.**
   Let \(X_e\) be independent entries from a fixed finite list of centered variance-one real finite-second-moment laws.
   Let a real-linear map \(L:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb C^d\), with columns \(\ell_e\), satisfy
   \[
    \|L\|\le C_0N^{-1/2},\qquad
    c_0\le\sum_e\|\ell_e\|^2\le C_0.
   \]
   Then uniformly in every deterministic \(f\in\mathbb C^d\),
   \[
    \mathbb P\{\|LX-f\|\le c\}\le Ce^{-cN}.
   \]
   The dimensions \(m,d\) may vary.

2. **Stable symmetric part and covariance.**
   For deterministic real symmetric \(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb R)\), define
   \[
    Q=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\B&-I&0\\A&0&-I\end{pmatrix},
    \quad U=\begin{pmatrix}I&0&0\\0&0&2I\\0&I&0\end{pmatrix},
   \]
   \[
    S=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\A&0&-3I/2\\2B&-3I/2&0\end{pmatrix},
    \quad K_n=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&I/2\\0&-I/2&0\end{pmatrix}.
   \]
   Then \(U^TQ=S+K_n\), \(S^T=S\), \(K_n^T=-K_n\), and
   \(\|S\|+\|S^{-1}\|\le C_{K_0,z}\) whenever \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K_0\).
   If
   \[
    n^{-1}\|A-n^{-1}(\operatorname{Tr}A)I\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge v_0>0,
   \]
   then for every \(a\in\mathbb C^3\),
   \[
    \|K_nS^{-1}(a\otimes I_n)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge cn\|a\|^2.
   \]
   The same conclusion holds with the non-scalar condition imposed on \(B\).
   For actual Wigner minors of all sizes \(n\ge\theta N\), a single fixed \(v_0>0\) satisfies this condition for \(A\), simultaneously over all retained physical sets, except with probability \(Ce^{-cN^2}\).

3. **Common-complement identification and paired reconstruction.**
   Partition physical indices into \(I,J\), with \(|I|=r\), \(|J|=n\), \(n/N\ge\theta>0\).
   Condition on \(A_{JJ},B_{JJ}\) obeying item 2's norm and non-scalar bounds, and form its \(S_J,K_J\).
   With original cross blocks \(X_A=A_{JI},X_B=B_{JI}\), set
   \[
    Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&X_A&2X_B\\X_A&0&0\\2X_B&0&0\end{pmatrix},
    \qquad \mathcal C=K_JS_J^{-1}Y.
   \]
   For fixed \(K_1\), some \(\delta,c,C>0\) satisfy, whenever \(r\le\delta n\),
   \[
    \mathbb P\{\|X_A\|,\|X_B\|\le K_1,
       \ s_{\min}(\mathcal C)\le c\mid A_{JJ},B_{JJ}\}\le Ce^{-cn}.
   \]
   This holds uniformly for the conditioned minors.
   On the complementary identification event, the exact outside formula in [the paired reconstruction](#actual-companion-reconstruction) defines, for every prescribed full candidate \(u\), a vector \(v_*\) using only that candidate and the exposed outside.
   Every pair satisfying
   \[
    S_{\rm full}v-K_{\rm full}u=0,\qquad
    \|S_{\rm full}u-K_{\rm full}v\|\le\varepsilon
   \]
   then obeys \(\|v-v_*\|\le C\varepsilon\) on the fixed norm guards.
   Actual approximate kernels of \(Q\) produce such pairs by the exact formula in [the same proof](#actual-companion-reconstruction).
   After reducing a fixed maximum fraction of deletions, the guarded identification failure can also be bounded by \(Ce^{-cN}\) uniformly over all such physical subsets.

4. **Prescribed-root graph reconstruction.**
   Without a non-scalar condition on the outside, \(\mathcal R=[A_{JI},2B_{JI}]\) has
   \[
    \mathbb P\{\|A_{JI}\|,\|B_{JI}\|\le K_1,
       \ s_{\min}(\mathcal R)\le c\}\le Ce^{-cn}
   \]
   for sufficiently small fixed \(r/n\) and \(n/N\ge\theta\).
   For every prescribed root \(h\), the formula in [the direct root reconstruction](#root-image-reconstruction) recovers vectors \(p_I^*,q_I^*\) from the exposed outside and \(h\), before either \(I\times I\) input block is exposed.
   If \(\|(P_N-zI)h\|\le\varepsilon\), then
   \[
    \|(B_Nh)_I-p_I^*\|+\|(A_Nh)_I-q_I^*\|\le C\varepsilon.
   \]
   There is also a one-color version: condition on all \(B_N\), allow \(h\) to be \(B_N\)-measurable, and recover only \((A_Nh)_I\) through \(2B_{JI}\).
   Its identification exception, intersected with \(\|B_{JI}\|\le K_0\), is at most \(Ce^{-cN}\) under the original \(B_N\) law.
   For the resulting outside-measurable \(q^*\), the two exact necessary approximate equations are
   \[
    \|A_Nh-q^*\|+\|A_N(B_Nh)-zh+2B_Nq^*\|\le C\varepsilon.
   \]
   Thus the remaining \(A_{II}\) entries test the actual two-column coefficients \((h,B_Nh)\), as proved in [the one-color version](#one-color-root-reconstruction).

5. **Finite-family low-concentration exclusions.**
   Fix \(C_*<\infty\), \(s>0\), and deterministic families
   \[
    \mathcal H_N\subset\{h\in\mathbb C^N:1/2\le\|h\|\le2\},
    \qquad |\mathcal H_N|\le N^{C_*N+o(N)}.
   \]
   Define the original scalar concentrations
   \[
    \rho_a(h,t)=\sup_{w\in\mathbb C}
      \mathbb P\{|\sum_jh_j\xi_{a,j}-w|\le t\},
      \qquad a\in\{A,B\}.
   \]
   There is \(C_1<\infty\), independent of the final residual exponent \(p\), such that for every fixed \(p>s+2\),
   \[
    \mathbb P\left\{\Omega_{N,K_0},\ \exists h\in\mathcal H_N:
       \|(P_N-zI)h\|\le N^{-p},\quad
       \min_{a\in\{A,B\}}\rho_a(h,N^{-s})<N^{-C_1}\right\}
    \le Ce^{-cN}+N^{-cN}.
   \]
   The leading family entropy coefficient must be fixed independently of \(p\); any remainder for a specified family is deterministic and \(o(N)\).
   The corresponding one-color assertion holds for measurable finite families \(\mathcal H_N(B_N)\) satisfying the same deterministic uniform size and norm bounds, independent of \(A_N\), with the concentration condition replaced by
   \[
    \rho_{A,2}(h,B_Nh;N^{-s})<N^{-C_1},\qquad
    \rho_{A,2}(h,B_Nh;t)
      =\sup_{w\in\mathbb C^2}
       \mathbb P\{\|\sum_j(h_j,(B_Nh)_j)\xi_{A,j}-w\|\le t\}.
   \]
   The conclusion is the same guarded \(Ce^{-cN}+N^{-cN}\) bound.
   These are statements about the specified admissible finite families, not about an arbitrary row-adaptive singular vector.

6. **Exact triad and a lawful full-row product.**
   The original root equation is equivalent to
   \[
    A_Nh-q=0,\qquad B_Nh-g=0,\qquad A_Ng+2B_Nq-zh=r,
   \]
   where \(g=B_Nh,q=A_Nh\).
   For every deterministic candidate triple, after conditioning on diagonals, let \(\vartheta_i(t)\) be the concentration at radius \(t\) of
   \[
    \sum_{j>i}\bigl[(h_j,0,g_j)\xi_{A,ij}
                         +(0,h_j,2q_j)\xi_{B,ij}\bigr].
   \]
   The probability that all the corresponding raw three-coordinate row residuals have norm at most \(t\) is bounded by \(\prod_i\vartheta_i(t)\).
   This uses one ordered exposure of the original symmetric inputs.
   It does not supply an entropy cover for random actual triples.

The statements above neither produce an exhaustive structured family from a highly concentrated degree-six Schur numerator nor complete the high-concentration count.
The input norm guard remains a restriction, with no summable bound asserted for its complement.
The actual open scope, including the quantitative entropy deficit of a direct inside-block cover, is retained at [the final boundary](#remaining-entropy-obstruction).

### A cutoff lemma for a spread linear image

Let \(X_e\) be independent real variables, each drawn from one of a fixed finite list of centered variance-one finite-second-moment laws.
Let \(L:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb C^d\) be real-linear, with complex columns \(\ell_e\), such that
\[
 \|L\|\le C_0N^{-1/2},\qquad
 c_0\le\sum_e\|\ell_e\|^2\le C_0.
 \tag{Linear-image-data}
\]
Constants may be enlarged without changing the statement.
There are constants \(c,C>0\), depending only on these bounds and the fixed laws, such that for every deterministic \(f\in\mathbb C^d\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|LX-f\|\le c\}\le Ce^{-cN}.
 \tag{Linear-image-small-ball}
\]
The ambient dimensions \(m,d\) need not be fixed.

Choose a fixed cutoff \(R\) so large that every conditional law \((X_e\mid |X_e|\le R)\) has variance at least \(1/2\), and every discarded probability is at most a number \(p\) to be chosen below.
Write \(w_e=\|\ell_e\|^2\), so \(\max_e w_e\le C_0^2/N\) and \(\sum_e w_e\le C_0\).
For the independent discard indicators \(I_e\),
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_e w_e I_e\le pC_0,\qquad
 \sum_e w_e^2\le C/N.
\]
Choose \(pC_0<c_0/4\).
The bounded-variable exponential inequality, applied to the centered independent variables \(w_e(I_e-\mathbb EI_e)\), gives
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\sum_e w_eI_e>c_0/2\right\}\le Ce^{-cN}.
 \tag{Weighted-cutoff-mask}
\]
For example, the elementary estimate
\(\mathbb E e^{t(Y-\mathbb EY)}\le e^{t^2(b-a)^2/8}\) for \(a\le Y\le b\), followed by optimization, gives this bound directly from \(\sum_e w_e^2\le C/N\).

Condition on the entire mask and on the discarded values.
On the good mask, the retained coordinates are independent with their original bounded conditional laws, and their variance contribution is at least \(c_0/4\).
All discarded terms and conditional means may be absorbed into a deterministic shift \(f'\).
For the norm of the remaining affine image, denoted \(Y\), independence gives
\[
 \mathbb E(Y^2\mid\text{mask, discarded values})\ge c_0/4.
\]
The function is convex and at most \(C_0N^{-1/2}\)-Lipschitz in the retained real coordinates.
Integrating (Bounded-product-input) yields
\[
 \mathbb E[(Y-\operatorname{med}Y)^2\mid\text{mask, discarded values}]
 \le C_R/N.
\]
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality therefore gives
\(\operatorname{med}Y\ge\sqrt{c_0/4}-\sqrt{C_R/N}\).
For large \(N\) this is bounded below by a fixed positive constant, uniformly in the shift.
A second use of (Bounded-product-input), then addition of (Weighted-cutoff-mask), proves (Linear-image-small-ball).
Changing constants handles the finitely many remaining sizes.
No conditioned norm guard and no density assumption were used.

### The exact stable symmetric part of the actual companion

For real symmetric \(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb R)\), define the original companion and a fixed invertible auxiliary matrix by
\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\B&-I&0\\A&0&-I\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 U=\begin{pmatrix}I&0&0\\0&0&2I\\0&I&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 U^TQ=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\A&0&-I\\2B&-2I&0\end{pmatrix}=S+K,
\]
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\A&0&-3I/2\\2B&-3I/2&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&I/2\\0&-I/2&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Actual-additive-skew-decomposition}
\]
Here \(S^T=S\) and \(K^T=-K\); transpose, not adjoint, is the relevant symmetry.
The randomness lies entirely in the symmetric part, while \(K\) is deterministic and physically local.
The Schur complement of the auxiliary block of \(S\) is
\[
 H=\frac43(AB+BA)-zI.
 \tag{Stable-symmetric-root}
\]
The first term is selfadjoint.
Consequently, for fixed \(z\notin\mathbb R\) and \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le K_0\), both
\[
 \|S\|\le C_{K_0,z},\qquad \|S^{-1}\|\le C_{K_0,z}
 \tag{Stable-symmetric-inverse}
\]
hold deterministically.
Indeed \(\|H^{-1}\|\le |\operatorname{Im}z|^{-1}\), and block inversion expresses every other inverse block using \(H^{-1},A,B\) and fixed auxiliary constants.
These facts hold for every principal physical minor with its original normalization.
No least-value statement for \(AB+2BA-z\) has been used.

<a id="covariance-trace-gap"></a>
### A deterministic covariance trace gap

Assume in addition that
\[
 \frac1n\left\|A-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}A}{n}I\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge v_0>0.
 \tag{Non-scalar-input}
\]
The identical argument works with \(B\) in place of \(A\).
Put \(C=KS^{-1}\).
For every \(a=(a_0,a_1,a_2)\in\mathbb C^3\),
\[
 \|C(a\otimes I_n)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge c_{K_0,z,v_0}\,n\|a\|^2.
 \tag{Auxiliary-trace-gap}
\]
Here \(a\otimes I_n\) denotes the vertical matrix with blocks \(a_jI_n\).

To prove it, write
\(S^{-1}(a\otimes I_n)=(H_0,H_1,H_2)^T\) and
\(h^2=\|H_1\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\|H_2\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\).
The first block equation gives
\[
 H_0=-a_0z^{-1}I+E_0,
 \qquad \|E_0\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C_{K_0,z}h.
\]
The other two equations imply
\[
 \|a_1I+(a_0/z)A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 +\|a_2I+(2a_0/z)B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le C_{K_0,z}h.
\]
Projection in Hilbert--Schmidt space orthogonally to the scalar matrices, followed by (Non-scalar-input), gives \(|a_0|\sqrt n\le Ch\).
The same displayed inequalities then give \((|a_1|+|a_2|)\sqrt n\le Ch\).
Finally \(\|C(a\otimes I_n)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=h^2/4\), proving the claim.
This is a deterministic non-scalar covariance condition on an input minor; it is not an eigenvalue-modulus second-moment lower bound for the nonnormal product.

For actual Wigner minors, (Non-scalar-input) has an exponentially small failure without any diagonal assumption beyond the stated model.
Choose fixed \(t,p>0\) with \(\mathbb P\{|\xi_A|\ge t\}\ge p\).
For a prescribed retained physical set \(J\) of size \(n\ge\theta N\), the independent upper indicators and an elementary binomial lower-tail bound give, except with probability \(Ce^{-cn^2}\),
\[
 \frac1n\left\|A_{JJ}-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}A_{JJ}}nI\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \ge\frac1n\sum_{i\ne j\in J}|(A_N)_{ij}|^2
 \ge c\frac nN\ge c\theta.
 \tag{Actual-non-scalar-minor}
\]
Subtracting a scalar changes no off-diagonal entry, which proves the first inequality.
The raw original normalization supplies the factor \(N^{-1}\) in the middle sum.
A union over all at most \(2^N\) retained subsets still has failure \(Ce^{-cN^2}\).
Thus one can fix \(v_0>0\) simultaneously for all these actual minors outside that exceptional event.

<a id="common-complement-identification"></a>
### A common original-law identification matrix

Partition physical indices into a retained complement \(J\), of size \(n\), and an unexposed block \(I\), of size \(r\).
Assume \(n/N\ge\theta>0\).
Condition on the two original matrices restricted to \(J\times J\), denoted \(A,B\), satisfying (Stable-symmetric-inverse) and (Non-scalar-input).
Do not expose either original \(I\times I\) block.
Let the independent original cross blocks be
\[
 X_A=N^{-1/2}(\xi_{A,ij})_{i\in J,j\in I},\qquad
 X_B=N^{-1/2}(\xi_{B,ij})_{i\in J,j\in I}.
\]
The off-diagonal physical block in the symmetric companion is exactly
\[
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&X_A&2X_B\\X_A&0&0\\2X_B&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathcal C=KS^{-1}Y.
 \tag{Actual-identification-matrix}
\]
There are \(c,C,\delta>0\), depending only on the fixed laws, \(\theta,K_0,z,v_0\), such that whenever \(r\le\delta n\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|X_A\|,\|X_B\|\le K_1,
              \ s_{\min}(\mathcal C)\le c\mid A,B\}
 \le Ce^{-cn}.
 \tag{Common-identification-floor}
\]
The constants also depend on the fixed cross-norm bound \(K_1\).
The probability concerns the original cross law.
The norm bound is an intersection in the event, never part of its conditioning.

For a fixed unit \(\alpha=(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\in\mathbb C^{3r}\), the random vector \(Y\alpha\) has independent contributions at every physical output position.
Its Hermitian covariance is
\[
 \frac1N\Sigma_\alpha\otimes I_n,
 \qquad
 \Sigma_\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^r
 \left[
 \begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1j}\\\alpha_{0j}\\0\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1j}\\\alpha_{0j}\\0\end{pmatrix}^{\!*}
 +
 \begin{pmatrix}2\alpha_{2j}\\0\\2\alpha_{0j}\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}2\alpha_{2j}\\0\\2\alpha_{0j}\end{pmatrix}^{\!*}
 \right].
\]
Thus \(1\le\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma_\alpha\le5\).
Diagonalizing this positive three-by-three matrix and applying (Auxiliary-trace-gap) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\|\mathcal C\alpha\|^2
 =N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(C(\Sigma_\alpha\otimes I_n)C^*\bigr)
 \ge cn/N\ge c\theta.
\]
The corresponding upper bound is constant by (Stable-symmetric-inverse).
As a linear map of the \(2nr\) raw independent real entries, \(\mathcal C\alpha\) has operator norm at most \(C N^{-1/2}\): matrix multiplication of a raw rectangle by any \(\alpha_j\) is bounded by its Frobenius norm times \(\|\alpha_j\|\).
The cutoff lemma therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\mathcal C\alpha\|\le c_2\mid A,B\}\le Ce^{-c_3N}
 \tag{Fixed-identification-direction}
\]
uniformly over the complex unit sphere.

On the cross-norm event, \(\|\mathcal C\|\le C_{K_0,z,K_1}\).
Take a fixed sufficiently fine Euclidean net of the unit sphere in \(\mathbb C^{3r}\), of cardinality at most \((3/\eta)^{6r}\).
If a unit vector has image norm below \(c_2/2\), a net point has image norm below \(c_2\), provided \(\eta\) is chosen from the displayed norm bound.
Union over the net in (Fixed-identification-direction), then choose \(\delta\) sufficiently small, to prove (Common-identification-floor).
The argument preserves the dependence between repeated appearances of the same entry in \(Y\); those repetitions are exactly the covariance vectors above.
Combining this conditional statement with (Actual-non-scalar-minor) gives the same exponential guarded estimate for every prescribed actual partition, and for any fixed family of partitions by a finite union.
One may also union over every \(|I|\le\delta' N\) after reducing the fixed \(\delta'\): its subset entropy is \(\exp(o_{\delta'}(1)N)\), which is absorbed by the exponential probability above.
Here \(\delta'\) is chosen after the fixed net constants and before any candidate or precision; the global norm guard is still only an event intersection.

<a id="actual-companion-reconstruction"></a>
### Reconstruction from the same outside data

In physical block order, write the full symmetric and skew parts as
\[
 S_{\rm full}=\begin{pmatrix}S_I&Y^T\\Y&S_J\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad K_{\rm full}=\operatorname{diag}(K_I,K_J).
\]
Suppose \(u=(\alpha,w)\) and \(v=(t,v_J)\) satisfy
\[
 S_{\rm full}v-K_{\rm full}u=0,\qquad
 \|S_{\rm full}u-K_{\rm full}v\|\le\varepsilon.
 \tag{Actual-paired-equations}
\]
Set \(G_J=S_J^{-1}\), \(b=G_JK_Jw\), and \(c_J=S_Jw-K_Jb\).
The exact outside equation gives
\[
 v_J=b-G_JYt,
\]
and the other outside residual is
\[
 (S_{\rm full}u-K_{\rm full}v)_J
   =Y\alpha+c_J+(K_JG_JY)t.
\]
On (Common-identification-floor), define
\[
 t_*=-\mathcal C^\dagger(Y\alpha+c_J),\qquad
 v_*=(t_*,b-G_JYt_*).
 \tag{Outside-recovered-pair}
\]
Then
\[
 \|v-v_*\|\le C\varepsilon
 \tag{Outside-recovery-error}
\]
on the fixed input and cross-norm guards.
Indeed \(\mathcal C^\dagger\mathcal C=I\), so the error in \(t\) is at most \(c^{-1}\varepsilon\), and \(\|G_JY\|\) is bounded.
For each given \(u\), the reconstruction uses only the exposed outside, not either \(I\times I\) input block.
If \(u\) is itself selected using those unexposed blocks, this formula does not make it an admissible outside-measurable candidate.

The paired equations do apply to an actual approximate kernel of the original companion.
For \(r_0=(S_{\rm full}+K_{\rm full})u\), take \(v=S_{\rm full}^{-1}K_{\rm full}u\).
Then
\[
 v=-u+S_{\rm full}^{-1}r_0,\qquad
 S_{\rm full}u-K_{\rm full}v
     =(I-K_{\rm full}S_{\rm full}^{-1})r_0.
 \tag{Actual-near-kernel-pair}
\]
The constants are bounded on the original input norm guard.
For a root vector \(h\), the exact graph \(u=(h,Bh,Ah)^T\) satisfies
\[
 (S_{\rm full}+K_{\rm full})u
   =((AB+2BA-zI)h,0,0)^T.
\]
Thus the reconstruction is a lawful multiple-border consequence of a tiny original product residual.

<a id="root-image-reconstruction"></a>
### A smaller identification matrix when the root vector is prescribed

For a prescribed root vector \(h=(h_I,h_J)\), put \(p=Bh\), \(q=Ah\).
After exposing the original outside and cross blocks, both
\[
 p_J=B_{JJ}h_J+B_{JI}h_I,\qquad
 q_J=A_{JJ}h_J+A_{JI}h_I
\]
are known without exposing the two \(I\times I\) blocks.
The outside original product equation is
\[
 (ABh+2BAh-zh)_J
   =\mathcal R\binom{p_I}{q_I}-b_h,
\]
\[
 \mathcal R=[A_{JI},2B_{JI}],\qquad
 b_h=zh_J-A_{JJ}p_J-2B_{JJ}q_J.
 \tag{Root-outside-equation}
\]
For every fixed unit \((a,b)\in\mathbb C^{2r}\), the map of raw independent entries to
\(A_{JI}a+2B_{JI}b\) has operator norm at most \(CN^{-1/2}\) and covariance trace
\((n/N)(\|a\|^2+4\|b\|^2)\ge\theta\).
The cutoff lemma and a \(4r\)-real-dimensional net therefore give, for \(r\le\delta n\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|A_{JI}\|,\|B_{JI}\|\le K_1,
              \ s_{\min}(\mathcal R)\le c\}\le Ce^{-cn}.
 \tag{Root-identification-floor}
\]
This version does not even require an outside non-scalar condition.
On its good event, define
\[
 \binom{p_I^*}{q_I^*}=\mathcal R^\dagger b_h.
\]
If \(\|(AB+2BA-zI)h\|\le\varepsilon\), then
\[
 \|p_I-p_I^*\|+\|q_I-q_I^*\|\le C\varepsilon.
 \tag{Root-image-recovery-error}
\]
Every candidate root vector has exactly one reconstructed pair of inside images.
All remaining compatibility constraints, including the component of \(b_h\) perpendicular to \(\operatorname{Ran}\mathcal R\), remain necessary; reconstruction alone does not assert feasibility.

<a id="one-color-root-reconstruction"></a>
### Conditioning one color recovers the remaining graph image

There is a useful reduced version that does survive conditioning on an entire color.
Condition on all original \(B\), and let a prescribed root vector \(h\) be measurable with respect to \(B\).
Then \(p=Bh\) is already known.
After exposing only the original \(A\) entries outside \(I\times I\), the vector
\(q_J=A_{JJ}h_J+A_{JI}h_I\) is also known.
The outside product equation now reads
\[
 (ABh+2BAh-zh)_J
  =2B_{JI}q_I-\widetilde b_h,
\]
\[
 \widetilde b_h=zh_J-A_{JJ}p_J-A_{JI}p_I-2B_{JJ}q_J.
 \tag{One-color-outside-equation}
\]
For a fixed partition with \(|I|\le\delta |J|\), the same cutoff/net proof gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|B_{JI}\|\le K_0, s_{\min}(B_{JI})\le c\}
 \le Ce^{-cN}.
 \tag{One-color-identification-floor}
\]
This exceptional event is charged under the original \(B\) law before conditioning; the remaining argument can condition on each actual \(B\) satisfying it.
No randomness of \(A\) is involved in its definition.
Set
\[
 q_I^*=(2B_{JI})^\dagger\widetilde b_h,
 \qquad q^*=(q_I^*,q_J).
\]
Every root residual of norm at most \(\varepsilon\) satisfies
\[
 \|q-q^*\|\le C\varepsilon,\qquad
 \|Ah-q^*\|\le C\varepsilon,
 \|A(Bh)-zh+2Bq^*\|\le C\varepsilon
 \tag{One-color-reconstructed-equations}
\]
on the fixed \(B\) norm guard.
The entire \(q^*\) and both targets in the last two equations are fixed before any \(A_{II}\) entry is exposed.
Thus a fresh \(A\) row simultaneously tests the two complex coefficients \((h_j,(Bh)_j)\), with one original scalar multiplier and its actual coupled targets.
This is exactly the scalar-multiplier format of a two-column linear concentration problem, without claiming the remaining high-profile count.
It permits a finite candidate family \(\mathcal H_N(B)\) measurable in \(B\), provided its size obeys a deterministic uniform entropy bound and it is independent of \(A\).

For clarity, the same construction yields a complete low-joint-concentration theorem.
Define
\[
 \rho_{A,2}(h,Bh;t)=\sup_{w\in\mathbb C^2}
  \mathbb P\left\{\left\|\sum_j(h_j,(Bh)_j)\xi_{A,j}-w\right\|\le t\right\}.
\]
Replace the deterministic family in (Root-family-entropy) by \(\mathcal H_N(B)\) with a uniform bound, and impose
\(\rho_{A,2}(h,Bh;N^{-s})<N^{-C_1}\).
Then the probability of an original root residual at most \(N^{-a}\), \(a>s+2\), intersected with both input norm guards, is at most \(Ce^{-cN}+N^{-cN}\) for sufficiently large fixed \(C_1\).
The proof is literal at the following necessary-event level.
Use a fixed partition into sufficiently many blocks and charge all required (One-color-identification-floor) exceptions once.
The convolution inequality for \(\mathbb C^2\)-valued sums selects a reservoir \(R\) with concentration below \(N^{-C_1/M}\) at radius \(N^{-s}/M\).
Take \(I=R\cup T\), reconstruct \(q^*\), and expose \(A_{RR},A_{TT}\) while retaining the original \(A_{TR}\) rectangle.
For every \(j\in T\), (One-color-reconstructed-equations) forces its independent raw row sum with coefficients \((h_k,(Bh)_k)_{k\in R}\) into its already fixed center at radius \(C\sqrt N N^{-a}\).
The product over \(|T|\ge N/(2M)\) rows is at most \(N^{-C_1N/(2M^2)}\).
The uniformly bounded family size and finitely many choices are then absorbed just as below.
All estimates after the original \(B\) exception are conditional on \(B\), so arbitrary dependence of the allowed candidate family on \(B\) causes no hidden row adaptivity.

<a id="low-root-concentration"></a>
### A complete low-concentration exclusion for finite root families

Fix \(K_0,z\), an integer \(s>0\), and a deterministic family
\(\mathcal H_N\subset\{h\in\mathbb C^N:1/2\le\|h\|\le2\}\) with
\[
 |\mathcal H_N|\le N^{C_*N+o(N)}.
 \tag{Root-family-entropy}
\]
The leading coefficient \(C_*\) is fixed independently of the residual precision below.
For color \(a\in\{A,B\}\), write
\[
 \rho_a(h,t)=\sup_{w\in\mathbb C}
  \mathbb P\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^Nh_j\xi_{a,j}-w\right|\le t\right\}
\]
for independent original raw entries.
There is a constant \(C_1\), depending only on \(C_*\), the laws and norm bounds, such that for every fixed \(a>s+2\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P\{\|A_N\|,\|B_N\|\le K_0,\quad
 &\exists h\in\mathcal H_N:\ 
 \|(A_NB_N+2B_NA_N-zI)h\|\le N^{-a},\\
 &\min(\rho_A(h,N^{-s}),\rho_B(h,N^{-s}))<N^{-C_1}\}
 \le Ce^{-cN}+N^{-cN}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Low-root-family-exclusion}
\]
Constants in the threshold on \(a\) may be enlarged to absorb fixed factors; taking \(a>s+2\) suffices eventually.

Choose a fixed sufficiently large integer \(M\) so that the union of two balanced blocks in a partition \(P_1,\ldots,P_M\) has size at most \(\delta\) times its complement, with \(\delta\) from (Root-identification-floor).
Pay the identification exception for the finitely many ordered distinct block pairs once, before counting root candidates.
On the full norm guard, all relevant cross-norm guards hold.
For a vector with \(\rho_a(h,N^{-s})<N^{-C_1}\), independence of disjoint coordinate groups gives
\[
 \rho_a(h,N^{-s})\ge
     \prod_{\ell=1}^M\rho_{a,P_\ell}(h,N^{-s}/M).
\]
This follows by choosing maximizing centers to arbitrary accuracy and adding the independent events; their error radii sum to \(N^{-s}\).
Hence some block \(R=P_\ell\) satisfies
\[
 \rho_{a,R}(h,N^{-s}/M)<N^{-C_1/M}.
 \tag{Low-root-reservoir}
\]
Choose any distinct block \(T=P_k\) and take \(I=R\cup T\).
Expose the outside and cross blocks, reconstruct \(p_I^*,q_I^*\), and then expose entries internal to \(R\) and internal to \(T\), including diagonals, in both colors.
The two original \(T\)-by-\(R\) rectangles remain independent, and their different rows are independent.
The necessary graph equations and (Root-image-recovery-error) require, for \(j\in T\),
\[
 \left|\sum_{k\in R}\xi_{A,jk}h_k-f_{A,j}\right|
      \le C\sqrt N\,N^{-a},\qquad
 \left|\sum_{k\in R}\xi_{B,jk}h_k-f_{B,j}\right|
      \le C\sqrt N\,N^{-a},
\]
where the centers are the actual reconstructed images minus the already exposed contributions.
These centers are fixed under the permitted conditioning.
For large \(N\), the radius is below \(N^{-s}/M\).
Use the color in (Low-root-reservoir), and bound the other color's event by one.
The resulting conditional probability is at most
\[
 N^{-(C_1/M)|T|}\le N^{-C_1N/(2M^2)}.
\]
Union over both colors, the fixed ordered pairs and (Root-family-entropy) proves (Low-root-family-exclusion), upon choosing \(C_1>2M^2(C_*+1)\).
The estimate is uniform in the exposed outside, so no distributional assertion about a row-adaptive candidate is hidden in this conditioning.
The two rectangles could both be used when both concentrations are small, but that strengthening is unnecessary for this claim.

<a id="original-root-triad"></a>
### An exact three-vector formulation with a lawful full-row product

The original product equation has a smaller global linear realization than the six-coordinate paired formulation.
For \(g=Bh\) and \(q=Ah\), it is exactly
\[
 Ah-q=0,\qquad Bh-g=0,\qquad Ag+2Bq-zh=r.
 \tag{Original-root-triad}
\]
An actual residual of norm at most \(\varepsilon\) gives such a triple with \(\|r\|\le\varepsilon\).
Conversely, the first two exact equations identify the actual images, so the last equation is the original product residual.
On a norm guard, \(\|g\|,\|q\|\le K_0\|h\|\).

For a deterministic candidate triple \((h,g,q)\), condition on the original diagonals and expose upper entries in simultaneous increasing physical row order, in both colors.
The remaining fresh vector in row \(i\), at raw scaling, is
\[
 \sum_{j>i}
   \bigl[(h_j,0,g_j)\xi_{A,ij}
             +(0,h_j,2q_j)\xi_{B,ij}\bigr]\in\mathbb C^3.
 \tag{Triad-fresh-row}
\]
Every other term, including the actual target \(\sqrt N(q_i,g_i,zh_i)\), is fixed under this sequential exposure.
Let \(\vartheta_i(t)\) be the concentration at radius \(t\) of the vector sum in (Triad-fresh-row), with fresh independent original copies.
Then for this deterministic triple and prescribed row tolerance \(t\),
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{\left\|\sqrt N
       ((Ah-q)_i,(Bh-g)_i,(Ag+2Bq-zh)_i)\right\|\le t
       \text{ for all }i\right\}
 \le\prod_{i=1}^N\vartheta_i(t).
 \tag{Triad-legal-row-product}
\]
This follows by successive conditional expectations in one ordering; no whole-row events with incompatible conditionings are multiplied.
The empty last tail has concentration one, and its diagonal equation can simply be bounded by one.
The same bound after omitting any specified rows is the product over the remaining rows.

This identity makes the residual counting question concrete.
One needs an exhaustive low-entropy covering of actual triples compatible with the linked two-color coefficients in (Triad-fresh-row), whose entropy is paid by this same joint row product.
The existing single-scalar vector inverse theorem does not state that two-color covering result.
The three-vector formulation retains fewer unknowns than the additive-skew pair and may be the better interface for such an extension.
Neither the exact row product for a deterministic triple nor the existence of an actual random triple makes the latter deterministic before exposure.

<a id="remaining-entropy-obstruction"></a>
### The precise step still missing from the three-factor entropy proof

The preceding reconstruction removes the need for a fine net over the image vectors \(Ah,Bh\), or over the paired companion \(v\), once the root or full candidate has been legitimately fixed.
It does not itself create an admissible finite candidate family for an arbitrary actual least-singular vector.
An unrestricted root net at precision \(N^{-a}\) has logarithmic size of order \(aN\log N\), so its leading entropy coefficient depends on the final precision.
Using that net in (Low-root-family-exclusion) forces the threshold \(C_1\) to depend linearly on \(a\); it does not give an exhaustive argument by taking \(a\) large.

There is a second exact difference from C194v21's high-product proof.
In that theorem one fresh real scalar multiplies the entire pair coefficient \((u_j,v_j)\), and the same row concentration factor that controls a vector-valued inverse theorem occurs once in the ordered row probability.
For the present additive-skew pencil, a physical paired row has six complex coordinates, and the fresh coefficient vectors are instead
\[
 a_j=(u_{1j},u_{0j},0,v_{1j},v_{0j},0),\qquad
 b_j=(2u_{2j},0,2u_{0j},2v_{2j},0,2v_{0j}),
\]
multiplied by independent original \(\xi_{A,ij}\) and \(\xi_{B,ij}\).
The two colors may have different laws.
The concentration of their sum is at most each individual concentration, but is not bounded above by their product without an additional argument.
Thus applying the existing scalar-multiplier inverse theorem separately to the two coefficient lists does not reproduce the progression-entropy/probability cancellation in C194v21.
Nor does exposing one entire color preserve the full-column-rank identification proof: the remaining single-color cross map has a whole auxiliary input block on which it vanishes.
For example, if only \(X_A\) is fresh, its displayed \(Y\) annihilates every \(\alpha=(0,0,\alpha_2)\).
The reduced graph reconstruction in [the one-color subsection](#one-color-root-reconstruction) avoids that particular obstruction by recognizing that \(Bh\) is already fixed.
It consequently supplies a lawful \(B\)-measurable-family low-joint-concentration exclusion, rather than asserting that conditioning one color is intrinsically impossible.

In the smaller root reconstruction, the remaining high-concentration case consists of candidates for which both \(\rho_A(h,N^{-s})\) and \(\rho_B(h,N^{-s})\) exceed \(N^{-C_1}\).
The real-vector inverse theorem retained in C194v21 can compress such coefficient vectors at its exact source scope, but the reconstructed inside constraints use only a fixed small fraction of the physical rows.
The full high-product entropy cancellation therefore does not follow merely by quoting that theorem.
Here is a quantitative check on a direct two-color root-cover attempt.
Even in the largest dimensionally possible injective regime, \([A_{JI},2B_{JI}]\) requires \(2|I|\le|J|\), hence \(|I|\le N/3\).
Suppose, only as a bookkeeping model, that both relevant scalar profiles were flat at \(N^{-C}\).
A one-color inverse-theorem cover with its usual \(N^{-1/2}\) factor per coefficient has leading count \(N^{(C-1/2+o(1))N}\).
Testing every row of both inside blocks could at most supply the corresponding formal factor \(N^{-2C|I|}\) in this calculation, before any losses from triangular tails.
At \(|I|=N/3\) the product still has exponent \((C/3-1/2+o(1))N\), which is positive for \(C>3/2\).
This is a failure of that proposed entropy accounting, not a probability lower bound or an actual ensemble counterexample.
The one-color reconstruction reduces the unknown image dimension and allows the dimensional bound \(|I|\le N/2\), but the entire high-profile transfer and its missing compatibility constraints still require proof.
The unused outside compatibility condition
\((I-\mathcal R\mathcal R^\dagger)b_h\approx0\) is a concrete further constraint that an exhaustive count could exploit; its random target and projection are functions of the same original cross blocks, so independence cannot be assumed.

Finally, neither large matching in the complete degree-six Schur numerator nor the adopted MNV small-ball majorant asserts an inverse theorem producing a root-vector family with leading entropy independent of the final precision.
The complete raw tensor and physical-spread statements in C187v31 and C197v2 also do not supply that arithmetic conclusion.
A structural implication from an actual large Schur concentration to one of the admissible root or paired families would be a separate lemma.
Without it, even an exhaustive count for a specified structured family would not by itself prove the desired full original-law floor or the planar-defect estimate.

These are application gaps for a new actual-ensemble structured-vector proof, not counterexamples to a summable floor or to Brown convergence.
The present results establish exponentially unlikely common-identification failure and a complete original-law low-concentration family exclusion.
They leave the high-concentration class and the conversion to summable planar-defect deviations unresolved.
The fixed-norm guard also remains an event restriction, whose complement is not generally summable under the exact model of C186v27.
