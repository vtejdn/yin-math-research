# Conditional row concentration and small images of roots with few distinct coordinates

The [two-color root equations](Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#original-root-triad) lead to a rectangle on which the contribution of the reflected entries can be eliminated after conditioning on one row sum.
For roots with repeated coordinates, swaps preserve that sum and leave independent signs in the second sum.
The resulting estimate covers all feasible image vectors simultaneously.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Model and conclusions

Let \(A,B\) be independent real symmetric \(N\)-by-\(N\) Wigner matrices, with independent upper coordinates in each matrix, reflected strict upper coordinates, and normalization \(N^{-1/2}\).
For each color its raw strict upper law is fixed, centered and of variance one, and its raw diagonal law is fixed, centered and of finite second moment.
The two colors may have different laws.
These statements hold in particular under the additional finite off-diagonal fourth-moment hypotheses of F0012's current two-sided test.
Write \(P=AB+2BA\) and fix \(z\in\mathbb C\).

1. Let \(R,T\) partition the indices, with \(n=|T|\) and \(r_B=\operatorname{rank}B_{TR}\).
   Condition on \(B\) and \(A_{TT},A_{RR}\).
   Let \(h\) be measurable with respect to these outside data and put \(g=Bh\).
   For a fresh row of independent original raw \(A\)-entries \(\xi_j\), define
   \[
     X=\sum_{j\in R}\xi_j h_j,\qquad
     Y=\sum_{j\in R}\xi_j g_j,\qquad
     w(t)=\mathbb E_X\sup_{a\in\mathbb Q+i\mathbb Q}
          \Pr\{|Y-a|<t\mid X\}.
   \]
   If \(t>(1+\sqrt{r_B})\sqrt N\,\varepsilon\), then
   \[
     \Pr\{\|(P-zI)h\|\le\varepsilon\mid B,A_{TT},A_{RR}\}
       \le w(t)^{\,n-r_B}.
     \tag{Conditional-row-bound}
   \]
   A product over zero remaining rows is one.
   The conditional expectation defining \(w\) is over one original fresh row with the conditioned coefficients.

2. Suppose a deterministic \(h\) admits \(m=\lfloor N/5\rfloor\) disjoint pairs \((j,k)\) with \(h_j=h_k\).
   Use their endpoints as \(R\) and assume that at least \(\sigma N\) coordinates of \(h_T\) have magnitude at least \(\lambda/\sqrt N\), for fixed \(\sigma,\lambda>0\).
   For every fixed \(\gamma<1/10\), uniformly over these roots and \(z\),
   \[
    \Pr\{\|(P-zI)h\|\le 2N^{-3}\}\le N^{-\gamma N}
    \qquad (N\text{ sufficiently large}).
    \tag{Repeated-root-exclusion}
   \]
   The constants and cutoff dimension may depend on \(\sigma,\lambda,\gamma\) and the two fixed laws.

3. Fix \(0\le\alpha<1/10\), \(\sigma,\lambda>0\) and \(Z<\infty\).
   Let \(\mathcal H_N\) consist of the unit vectors \(h\in\mathbb C^N\) having at least \(\sigma N\) coordinates of magnitude at least \(\lambda/\sqrt N\), within Euclidean distance \(N^{-9}\) of some vector taking at most \(k_N=\max(1,\lfloor N^\alpha\rfloor)\) distinct complex coordinate values.
   For some \(C,c>0\) and all sufficiently large \(N\),
   \[
    \Pr\{\exists h\in\mathcal H_N,\ \exists |z|\le Z:
                     \|(P-zI)h\|\le N^{-3}\}
        \le C N^{-3}+N^{-cN}.
    \tag{Uniform-few-values-exclusion}
   \]
   This probability series is summable and first Borel--Cantelli gives an eventual exclusion for every cross-size coupling.
   Every unit root with \(\|h\|_\infty\le K/\sqrt N\), for fixed \(K\), satisfies the coordinate count requirement for suitable \(\sigma,\lambda>0\).

The [two-level root](../ledger/Yin_L_F0066_v20.md#bounded-flat-root-family) satisfies item 3 for every fixed \(0<\alpha<1/10\) at large \(N\).
The estimate tests the actual graph images \(Ah,Bh\) and includes every ordering of the two levels.
The coverage of arbitrary near-kernels and the summable planar defect under general finite fourth moments remain open.

## Complete argument

This is a project deduction from the matrix identities and original within-size independence.
Its comparison with the complementary reconstruction in [C194v31](Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#one-color-root-reconstruction) concerns the exposure order.
The proof derives its own row estimate and does not require the identification-matrix floor from that argument.

<a id="conditional-row-elimination"></a>
### Eliminating the reflected rectangle and weighting the image of each row

Put \(U=\sqrt N A_{TR}\), with rows indexed by \(T\) and columns by \(R\).
After the conditioning of item 1, only \(U\) and its forced transpose can vary.
The entries of \(U\) are independent with the original strict upper law, also when their physical indices are below the matrix diagonal, by real symmetry.
Write \(X_T=Uh_R,\ Y_T=Ug_R,\ V_R=U^T h_T\).
The image equations \(g=Bh\), \(q=Ah\) give exactly
\[
 \sqrt N((P-zI)h)_T
   =Y_T+2B_{TT}X_T+2 B_{TR} V_R+d_T,
 \quad
 d_T=\sqrt N\{A_{TT}g_T+2B_{TT}A_{TT}h_T+2B_{TR}A_{RR}h_R-zh_T\}.
 \tag{Projected-image-equations}
\]
The vector \(d_T\) is determined before \(U\) is exposed.
The possibly adaptive contribution \(2B_{TR}V_R\) takes values in the column space of \(B_{TR}\).

Let \(\mathcal S=\operatorname{Ran} B_{TR}\) and select \(r_B\) columns of \(B_{TR}\) forming a basis of \(\mathcal S\), using a deterministic choice given \(B\).
Select \(r_B\) rows \(H\subset T\) whose square minor has maximal absolute determinant.
Fix a deterministic tie-breaking rule.
For each \(i\in T\setminus H\) there is a row \(c_i\) with \(v_i=c_i v_H\) for every \(v\in\mathcal S\).
Cramer's rule writes each coefficient as a signed quotient of a minor obtained by replacing one pivot row by \(i\), divided by the selected determinant.
Maximality gives \(\|c_i\|_\infty\le1\), hence \(\|c_i\|_2\le\sqrt{r_B}\).
In rank zero \(H\) and the rows \(c_i\) are empty.

Condition further on all \(X_T\) and on the entries \(Y_H\).
Independence of the original rows implies that the laws of the remaining \(Y_i\) are independent conditional laws \(\mathcal L(Y\mid X=X_i)\).
One can use a regular conditional law on the Euclidean coordinate space.
The supremum over rational complex centers of probabilities of open disks is measurable.
If \(\|(P-zI)h\|\le\varepsilon\), write \(e=\sqrt N((P-zI)h)_T\), so \(\|e\|_2\le\sqrt N\varepsilon\).
Subtracting the pivot equations with coefficients \(c_i\) eliminates \(2B_{TR}V_R\) exactly.
Each nonpivot \(Y_i\) must therefore lie within \((1+\sqrt{r_B})\sqrt N\varepsilon\) of a center determined by \(X_T,Y_H,d_T,B\), since \(|e_i-c_i e_H|\le(1+\sqrt{r_B})\sqrt N\varepsilon\).
Every such closed disk fits in an open disk of radius \(t\) with rational center.
Bounding each conditional row probability by its supremum gives a product depending on the nonpivot \(X_i\)'s alone.
Before conditioning on \(X_T\) these \(X_i\) are iid under the given outside law, so integration gives (Conditional-row-bound).
This integration uses the distribution of the actual image constraints and requires no enumeration of \(q\) or \(g\).

<a id="sign-interval"></a>
### Sign interval estimate and original-law swaps

For independent fair signs and \(l\) real weights with absolute values at least \(b>0\), the concentration in any real open interval of radius \(b\) is at most \(2^{-l}\binom l{\lfloor l/2\rfloor}\le C/\sqrt l\).
This is [Erdős's Theorem 1](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1945-04.pdf) in the 1945 published paper, printed p. 898, after scaling.
The interval and its short proof on that page were inspected.
For completeness absorb the signs of the weights into the fair signs and encode their positive positions as a subset of \(\{1,\ldots,l\}\).
Two properly nested subsets yield sums at distance at least \(2b\), so subsets accepted by the open interval form an antichain.
A random maximal chain, obtained from a uniform permutation, contains at most one accepted subset.
Each specific subset of size \(j\) belongs to the chain with probability \(\binom lj^{-1}\).
Summing those probabilities gives the middle-binomial bound, and the standard central binomial estimate gives the last inequality.
Additional independent summands can be fixed by conditioning.

For each original strict upper law choose constants \(s_A,p_A,s_B,p_B>0\) such that \(\Pr\{|\xi_a-\xi'_a|\ge s_a\}\ge p_a\), with independent original copies for each \(a=A,B\).
This is possible because both laws have positive variance.
An iid real pair can be sampled by drawing its unordered multiset of values, followed by an independent fair ordering sign.
For equal values the sign leaves the ordering unchanged.
For \(l\) independent such pairs the number of gaps of size at least \(s_a\) dominates a binomial with parameters \(l,p_a\).
Its probability of falling below \(p_al/2\) is at most \(e^{-c_a l}\) by the elementary binomial Chernoff bound.

<a id="random-image-separation"></a>
### Generating separated coefficients from the actual second color

Use the pairs of item 2.
There is a phase \(\theta\in\{0,\pi/2\}\) such that at least \(\sigma N/2\) coordinates \(i\in T\) satisfy
\[
  |\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}h_i)|\ge\lambda/\sqrt{2N}.
\]
For each pair \(j,k\) in \(R\), conditional on \(B_{RR}\),
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(e^{-i\theta}(g_j-g_k)\bigr)
  =d_{jk}+N^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in T}
      \operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}h_i)(\xi_{B,ji}-\xi_{B,ki})
\]
with \(d_{jk}\) fixed.
Each difference is an independent fair sign times the absolute gap of two iid entries.
Among the visible coordinates a positive fraction of the gaps are at least \(s_B\) outside an event of probability \(e^{-cN}\).
For these gaps the real sign coefficients have magnitude at least \(s_B\lambda/(\sqrt2 N)\).
The preceding interval estimate implies, for some \(b_0>0\) and any fixed shift \(d_{jk}\),
\[
 \Pr\{|\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}(g_j-g_k))|
       \le b_0/N\mid B_{RR}\}\le C/\sqrt N.
 \tag{Small-second-color-gap}
\]
Choose \(b_0<s_B\lambda/\sqrt2\) to handle the closed interval.
The \(m\) differences are conditionally independent because the paired \(R\)-rows use disjoint entries in \(B_{RT}\).
For any fixed \(0<\eta<1\), the probability that fewer than \(\eta m\) pairs have gap greater than \(b_0/N\) in this chosen projection is bounded by
\[
 2^m(C/\sqrt N)^{\lfloor(1-\eta)m\rfloor}
     =N^{-((1-\eta)/10+o(1))N}.
 \tag{Second-color-exception}
\]
All constants are independent of the actual vector satisfying the stated visibility and matching conditions.

<a id="conditional-swap-count"></a>
### Conditional signs preserving the first image

Fix a realization of \(B\) having at least \(\eta m\) pairs with \(\left|\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}(g_j-g_k))\right|>b_0/N\), and call those pairs good.
For a fresh raw row of \(U\), expose the unordered entries at each good pair and the entries at all other indices of \(R\).
Call the exposed information \(F\).
Because \(h_j=h_k\) in every tested pair, \(X\) is \(F\)-measurable.
The as yet unrevealed orderings are independent signs.
In the projection \(\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}Y)\) their coefficients have magnitude
\[
 \tfrac12 |\xi_{A,j}-\xi_{A,k}|\,
              |\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}(g_j-g_k))|.
\]
Outside an \(F\)-measurable event of probability \(e^{-c_\eta N}\), at least a fixed positive multiple of \(N\) such coefficients exceed \(s_A b_0/(2N)\).
At \(t=s_A b_0/(4N)\) the real interval estimate, also bounding every complex disk by its projection, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_F\sup_{a\in\mathbb Q+i\mathbb Q}\Pr\{|Y-a|<t\mid F\}
    \le C_\eta/\sqrt N.
\]
Since \(X\) is \(F\)-measurable, conditioning first on \(F\), then on \(X\), and taking the countable suprema yields \(w(t)\le C_\eta/\sqrt N\).
This only controls the averaged conditional concentration needed in (Conditional-row-bound).
For \(\varepsilon=2N^{-3}\), \(t>(1+\sqrt{r_B})\sqrt N\varepsilon\) for sufficiently large \(N\).
Since \(n-r_B\ge N-4m\ge N/5\), the projected row bound contributes at most \(N^{-(1/10+o(1))N}\).
Adding (Second-color-exception) and choosing \(\eta\) sufficiently small for each prescribed \(\gamma<1/10\) proves (Repeated-root-exclusion).

<a id="few-value-cover"></a>
### Covering all root labels and all values at once

Use the event \(\max(\|A\|,\|B\|)\le N^2\).
For each input \(\mathbb E\|W\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=N-1+\mathbb E\zeta^2=O(N)\), so this event fails with probability \(O(N^{-3})\).
On this event \(\|P\|\le3N^4\).
The complement contributes once to the final probability bound.

Each unit \(h\in\mathcal H_N\) admits a comparison vector \(v\) with at most \(k_N\) complex values and distance at most \(N^{-9}\).
The set of such \(v\)'s is closed, as a finite union of coordinate-equality subspaces.
Every used value has modulus at most two for large \(N\).
Round the real and imaginary parts of all values on the same mesh \(N^{-10}\) in the square \([-2,2]^2\) to obtain \(h'\).
This gives \(\|h-h'\|\le N^{-9}+2\sqrt N N^{-10}\le2N^{-9}\) and \(\|h'\|\le2\) eventually.
At least \(\sigma N\) coordinates of \(h'\) have magnitude at least \(\lambda/(2\sqrt N)\).
The number of possible rounded \(h'\)'s is bounded by \(k_N^N(CN^{20})^{k_N}=N^{(\alpha+o(1))N}\).
The bound enumerates both the labels and the common dictionary.

For each such rounded root with the displayed visibility reserve \(\lfloor\sigma N/4\rfloor\) visible coordinates in \(T\), with \(\sigma\le1\) as the only nonempty case.
In the remaining positions, partition the indices by their equal values and greedily form disjoint pairs within each class.
At most \(k_N=o(N)\) positions remain unpaired there, so there are at least \((N-\lfloor\sigma N/4\rfloor-k_N)/2\ge\lfloor N/5\rfloor\) pairs for large \(N\).
Choose exactly \(m\) of them, with \(R\) consisting of their endpoints and \(T\) the complement.
Thus item 2 applies uniformly to all selected \(h'\)'s with visibility constants \(\sigma/5,\lambda/2\).
The partition and pairs may depend on the rounded vector and are chosen deterministically before either input is drawn.

Round \(z\) to a complex grid of mesh \(N^{-5}\) covering the disk of radius \(Z\) with \(O_Z(N^{10})\) points.
On the polynomial norm event and for large \(N\),
\[
 \|(P-z'I)h'\|
 \le\|(P-zI)h\|+(3N^4+Z)\|h-h'\|+|z-z'|\|h'\|
 \le2N^{-3}
\]
whenever \(\|(P-zI)h\|\le N^{-3}\).
Choose \(\gamma\) strictly between \(\alpha\) and \(1/10\).
Item 2 and a union bound over the two grids give \(N^{-(\gamma-\alpha+o(1))N}\).
Adding the single polynomial norm exception proves (Uniform-few-values-exclusion).
For a unit \(h\) with \(\|h\|_\infty\le K/\sqrt N\), the coordinates below \(1/\sqrt{2N}\) carry at most half its squared mass, so at least \(N/(2K^2)\) coordinates meet that threshold.
This proves the last assertion.

<a id="remaining-classes"></a>
### Remaining arithmetic and planar obligations

The fully exposed law of \(Y\) conditional on \(X\) can be a point mass for unequal coordinates of an atomic-law root.
For example a Rademacher row sum with distinct binary-power coefficients distinguishes all sign patterns exactly.
Obtaining a uniform gain for other classes therefore requires a quantitative conditional treatment at finite precision or additional structure of the actual feasible roots.
The low-cardinality cover here permits arbitrary distributions and mesh positions of its allowed levels, but leaves open the profiles that require larger dictionaries or are far from the covered vectors.
General finite-fourth-moment almost-sure Brown convergence still requires the [summable extreme planar loss](Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#extreme-planar-defect).
