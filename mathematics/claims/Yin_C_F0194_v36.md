# Fractional negative moments for the repeated interior determinant

<a id="statement"></a>
## Conditional and planar bounds

Use the independent real symmetric Wigner inputs of [C196v4](Yin_C_F0196_v4.md#statement), with finite fourth moments off the diagonal and finite second moments on the diagonal.
For this result assume that the fixed raw off-diagonal law of \(A\) has a decomposition \(p\nu+(1-p)\eta\), where \(0<p\le1\), and for some \(\theta,\ 0<\theta<1/4\),
\[
 K=\sup_{u\in\mathbb R}\int |x-u|^{-4\theta}\,d\nu(x)<\infty .
 \tag{Regular-component}
\]
The residual law \(\eta\), both diagonal laws, and the off-diagonal law of \(B\) need only the original model assumptions.
Every off-diagonal law with a nonzero absolutely continuous part meets this additional hypothesis for all \(0<\theta<1/4\), with a suitable fixed decomposition.
The condition also allows singular component laws.

Take a deterministic set \(I\) of any even size \(k=2m\ge2\), with \(U=A_{II}\), and reveal \(B\), all \(A\)-entries outside \(I\times I\), and the diagonal of \(U\), writing \(\mathcal E\) for this exterior information.
For a finite \(\mathcal E\)-measurable complex \(2k\)-by-\(2k\) matrix \(W\), put \(P(W)=\det(\operatorname{diag}(U,U)-W)\).
On an extension carrying the independent component labels there is one event \(G\), independent of \(\mathcal E\) and usable for all targets, such that
\[
 \Pr(G^c\mid\mathcal E)\le C_p e^{-c_p k},\qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf1_G|P(W)|^{-\theta}\mid\mathcal E\right]
 \le N^{\theta k}\max(1,K)^{k/2}.
 \tag{Matched-fractional-moment}
\]
A restricted integral means integration only on \(G\), including when the integrand is infinite on its complement.
If \(p=1\), one can take \(G\) to be the whole sample space.
There is \(C_\theta\), also depending on \(K\), such that for \(t\ge0\) the original mixture satisfies
\[
 \Pr\{\log^-|P(W)|>k\log N+C_\theta k+t\mid\mathcal E\}
 \le C_p e^{-c_pk}+ e^{-\theta t}.
 \tag{Absolute-conditional-tail}
\]
Here \(\log^-x=\max(0,-\log x)\) for positive \(x\) and \(\log^-0=+\infty\).

For a disk \(D_R=\{|z|<R\}\) take any jointly measurable exterior field \(W(z)\) finite for area-almost every \(z\), almost surely.
Define \(Y_R=|D_R|^{-1}\int_{D_R}\log^-|P(W(z))|\,dA(z)\), disregarding the exceptional parameters where \(W\) is undefined.
The same conditional bound (Absolute-conditional-tail) holds with \(Y_R\) in place of \(\log^-|P(W)|\).
It applies to the meromorphic [exterior target of the conditional determinant](../drafts/Yin_D_F0042.md#repeated-interior) wherever \(B_{I^cI}\) has full column rank.
That event belongs to \(\mathcal E\), and on it the finite pole set can be assigned arbitrary values for integration.
The estimates integrate over all realizations of \(U\) under its conditional law and permit the minimizing singular directions to depend on \(U\).

For a prescribed even \(k_N\le\delta N\), use \(\delta\) from [C196v4](Yin_C_F0196_v4.md#statement).
If \(k_N\log N=o(N)\) and \(\sum_N e^{-c_pk_N}<\infty\), then for the actual exterior target and every \(R,v>0\),
\[
 \sum_N\Pr\!\left\{ B_{I^cI}\text{ lacks full column rank, or }
     N^{-1}\int_{D_R}\log^-|P(W(z))|\,dA(z)>v\right\}<\infty .
 \tag{Mesoscopic-planar-tail}
\]
For \(p=1\) omit the condition on the series, including for bounded even \(k_N\).
For \(p<1\), a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(\log N\) suffices for the lower size condition, and \((\log N)^2\) rounded to an even integer is one admissible choice.
Every-coupling almost-sure vanishing of this normalized integral follows, also after restricting integration to \(F_N(z)^c\) or any measurable event depending on the whole sample and parameter.
The lower bound on the full characteristic determinant also involves its exterior compression.
An estimate for the interior factor alone leaves the planar loss \(D_N\) of [C186v29](Yin_C_F0186_v29.md#statement) unresolved.

## Exterior determinant at the actual random interior

Use the [square exterior map](Yin_C_F0196_v4.md#argument) for a prescribed \(I\) of size \(k\), and let \(P:\mathbb C^N\to\mathbb C^k\) be coordinate restriction.
Assume \(V=B_{JI}\) has full column rank and \(z\notin\sigma(C_H)\), where \(H=\ker X\), \(\Pi=\Pi_H\), \(C_H=\Pi(AB+2BA)|_H\), and \(Xh=(Ph,PBh)\).
Write \(D^e=A^eB+2BA^e-zI\), where the \(I\times I\) entries of \(A^e\) vanish and all other entries agree with \(A\).
For the actual real symmetric interior \(U=A_{II}\), put \(\mathcal U=\operatorname{diag}(U,U)\) and \(Z=(2BP^*,P^*)\).
Then \(D=M-zI=D^e+Z\mathcal U X\).
The map \(Z\) has image \(H^\perp\), since the two column spaces are the two rows of \(X\) under adjoint and the first has simply been scaled by two.
It is an isomorphism from \(\mathbb C^{2k}\) to \(H^\perp\).
All the following targets are determined before sampling the interior.
Define
\[
 T h=(\Pi D h,Xh),\qquad
 W(z)=-Z^{-1}(1-\Pi)D^e T^{-1}(0,\,\cdot\,).
\]
The inverse \(Z^{-1}\) here acts only on \(H^\perp\).
In orthonormal bases adapted to \(H\oplus H^\perp\), \(D T^{-1}\) has first output component equal to its first input component and lower-right block \(Z(\mathcal U-W(z))\).
Consequently
\[
 |\det D(z)|
 =2^k\,\det(V^*V)\,|\det(C_H-zI_H)|\,
       |\det(\mathcal U-W(z))|.
 \tag{Repeated-interior-determinant}
\]
Indeed the [exterior determinant identity](Yin_C_F0196_v4.md#argument) gives \(|\det T|=|\det(C_H-zI_H)|\det(V^*V)^{1/2}\).
The Gram matrix \(Z^*Z\) has blocks \(4P B^2P^*,2B_{II},2B_{II},I_k\).
Its Schur complement over \(I_k\) is \(4V^*V\).
Multiplying the two absolute Jacobians and the lower-right determinant proves the formula.

## Exposing a matching

Independently for every interior off-diagonal entry generate a Bernoulli label of parameter \(p\), then draw its raw value with law \(\nu\) on success and \(\eta\) otherwise.
Projection to the values recovers their original independent laws.
If the original entry measure has a nonzero density part \(f(x)\,dx\), restrict this part to a bounded set on which \(f\) is bounded and its integral is positive.
After normalization the restriction gives a bounded-density \(\nu\) with positive mixture weight.
For a density bounded by \(L\), splitting at distance one gives \(K\le 1+2L/(1-4\theta)\).
More generally a uniform interval probability bound \(\sup_u\nu([u-r,u+r])\le Cr^\alpha\) for \(0<r<1\) gives (Regular-component) for \(4\theta<\alpha\) by integrating the distribution of \(|x-u|^{-4\theta}\).

Split \(I\) into two deterministic halves of size \(m\).
Use the bipartite graph of successful labels across the halves and let \(G\) denote existence of a perfect matching.
Hall's criterion applied to the left neighborhoods gives a set with fewer neighbors than vertices whenever \(G\) fails [Hal35, Theorem 1, p. 27](../literature/pdf/Hal35.pdf).
For clarity, if a maximum matching misses a left vertex, following alternating paths from it reaches a left set \(S\) whose neighbor set consists only of matched right vertices.
Each of these right vertices has its matched partner in \(S\) and the original unmatched vertex lies outside that partner set.
Thus \(|N(S)|<|S|\).
For some \(a=|S|\) and some set of \(a-1\) right vertices containing its neighbors, all \(a(m-a+1)\) pairs to the remaining right vertices have failed labels.
Consequently
\[
 \Pr(G^c)\le\sum_{a=1}^m {m\choose a}{m\choose a-1}(1-p)^{a(m-a+1)}
 \le C_p e^{-c_pk}.
\]
For the last inequality there are at most two terms per \(b=\min(a,m-a+1)\), each at most \(m^{2b}\exp(-pbm/2)\).
Summing the resulting geometric bound for all large \(m\) and changing the constant for small \(m\) gives the claim.
If \(p=1\), every cross edge is successful.

## The top coefficient and its fractional inverse

After exposing the labels on \(G\), choose the lexicographically first perfect matching.
Disclose all entries except the matching variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\), which now denote the unnormalized raw values and are still independent with law \(\nu\).
The matrix uses each \(x_j/\sqrt N\) in two symmetric positions in each of its two diagonal copies.
As a polynomial in these variables, \(P=P(W)\) has degree at most four in each \(x_j\).
The coefficient of \(\prod_{j=1}^m x_j^4\) is \(N^{-k}\).
Indeed this monomial uses all \(4m=2k\) determinant factors, and every selected position is forced by the matching and its two copies.
The row permutation is the product of two identical perfect-matching involutions, of combined sign \(+1\).
No coefficient of \(W\), diagonal coordinate or nonmatching variable enters this term.

Set \(P_1=P\) and \(P_{j+1}=[x_j^4]P_j\), so \(P_{m+1}=N^{-k}\).
The uniform inverse moment implies that \(\nu\) has no atoms.
A nonzero polynomial in independent \(\nu\)-variables vanishes with probability zero, by induction using a nonzero univariate coefficient and finitely many roots.
In particular each \(P_{j+1}\) is nonzero for almost every remaining draw.
For fixed such values, factor \(P_j=P_{j+1}\prod_{\ell=1}^{4}(x_j-r_\ell)\) over the complex numbers.
For complex \(r\) one has \(|x-r|\ge|x-\operatorname{Re}r|\).
Hölder's inequality therefore gives
\[
 \int |P_j|^{-\theta}\,d\nu(x_j)
 \le |P_{j+1}|^{-\theta}
      \prod_{\ell=1}^{4}\left(\int|x_j-r_\ell|^{-4\theta}\,d\nu(x_j)\right)^{1/4}
 \le K|P_{j+1}|^{-\theta}.
\]
Tonelli and successive integration give \(\mathbb E[|P|^{-\theta}\mid\text{disclosed data}]\le K^m N^{\theta k}\).
Averaging the disclosed data on \(G\) proves (Matched-fractional-moment), uniformly over exterior sections and targets.

## Planar integration and scope

For a jointly measurable exterior field, Tonelli applies to the nonnegative integrands on almost every exterior section, assigning a finite target at exceptional null parameters.
The matching event is the same for every \(z\).
Since \(e^{\theta\log^-|P|}\le 1+|P|^{-\theta}\), Jensen's inequality for normalized disk area gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\mathbf1_G e^{\theta Y_R}\mid\mathcal E]
 \le 1+N^{\theta k}\max(1,K)^{k/2}.
\]
The same inequality holds for a single finite target.
Exponential Markov on \(G\), addition of its failure probability, and a choice of \(C_\theta\) absorbing the factor two prove both absolute tails.
These probabilities refer to original values because the event on the left after adding \(G^c\) depends only on the original matrices.

For the actual exterior field, extend it by \(W=0\) when the cross block lacks full rank.
The cross-block probability of failure is \(O_R(N^{-3})+O_R(\exp(-c_RN^{1/8}))\) by C196v4.
On its complement the characteristic polynomial of the exterior compression is monic up to sign and its zero set in the plane is finite.
For \(Y_R>vN/|D_R|\), the tail parameter \(vN/|D_R|-k\log N-C_\theta k\) is at least \(vN/(2|D_R|)\) eventually at the prescribed sizes.
Summing the conditional estimate and the cross-block exception proves (Mesoscopic-planar-tail).
The case \(p=1\) uses zero matching-failure probability throughout.
First Borel--Cantelli, using integer disks and positive rational thresholds, gives one event for local vanishing under each coupling.
Monotonicity permits any measurable parameter restriction.

The characteristic determinant contains the factors \(2^k\det(B_{I^c I}^* B_{I^c I})\) and \(|\det(C_H-zI_H)|\) in addition to \(|P(W(z))|\).
The latter compression acts on \(N-2k\) dimensions and can retain extensive negative logarithms.
The estimate above bounds the interior contribution in absolute terms at the stated sizes.
It supplies no bound comparing the exterior log potential with the clipped potential of the original matrix.
