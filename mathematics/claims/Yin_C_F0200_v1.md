# ACSY conjugate variables give dual operators on every countably generated holomorphic module

## Current assessment
[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\) generate a finite von Neumann algebra \(M\) with faithful normal probability trace \(\tau\).
Write \(H=L^2(M,\tau)\), identify \(M\) with its bounded vectors, and put \(P_1v=\tau(v)1\) for \(v\in M\).
Let \(R=\mathbb C\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle\) be the represented unital holomorphic polynomial algebra.
Assume the actual ACSY hypothesis of [T_F0011](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct).
Thus on the represented unital star algebra generated by \(X\) there are outer-bimodule derivations \(\partial_i\) into \(H\otimes_2 H\) with \(\partial_iX_j=\delta_{ij}1\otimes1\) and \(1\otimes1\) in the domain of each Hilbert adjoint \(\partial_i^*\).

For every finite or countable \(B\subseteq M\), the algebraic left \(R\)-module \(V=R1+\sum_{b\in B}Rb\) admits complex-linear maps \(E_i:V\to H\) satisfying
\[
 E_i(1)=0,\qquad E_i(X_jv)-X_jE_i(v)=\delta_{ij}\tau(v)1
 \quad(v\in V,\ 1\le i,j\le d).
\]
The maps \(D_i=-E_i\) have the generator-first sign of T_F0011.
The module sums comprise finite linear combinations, even when \(B\) is infinite.
Restriction also gives the commutators on a countably generated module omitting \(1\).

For a finite ordered list \(B\) starting with \(1\), let \(\mathcal E_U^i\) be the coordinate tuples of all such normalized solutions for the index \(i\) on \(V_U\), where \(U\) ranges over finite extensions by bounded inputs.
Write \(\pi_B\) for coordinate restriction and use the Hilbert direct-sum norm on \(H^{B}\).
The nonempty affine subspaces \(\overline{\pi_B\mathcal E_U^i}\) eventually coincide as \(U\) increases, with closure in that norm.
In particular \(\sup_{U\supseteq B}\inf_{z\in\mathcal E_U^i}\|\pi_B z\|<\infty\).
This bound concerns a fixed \(B\), and the countable-domain assertion requires no operator-norm-to-\(H\) bound.

For every compactly supported diffuse real probability law, the same finite-list stabilization and countable-domain conclusions hold for the coordinate multiplication operator on its scalar \(L^\infty\) space.
For the arcsine law on \((-1,1)\), the values of every normalized local solution on the first-kind Chebyshev polynomials satisfy \(\|E(T_n(X))\|_2=\sqrt n\) for \(n\ge1\), although \(\|T_n(X)\|=1\).
This law has neither a dual on all bounded inputs nor the ACSY hypothesis.
Thus the countable-domain conclusion alone leaves open the general implication from ACSY to a bare full-domain dual.

## Argument

### Finite modules and all their polynomial relations

By [ACSY24 Proposition 3.5](../audits/literature/ACSY24/Yin_U001.md#acsy24-u001), the given hypothesis implies \(\Delta(X)=d\).
The [maximal-Delta and free-field equivalence](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U001.md#exact-introductory-interfaces), using [MSY23 Theorem 3.1](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U002.md#msy23-u002) and the [affiliated realization](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U004.md#msy23-u004), identifies \(R\) with the free polynomial algebra in its skew field \(K\subset U(M)\).
Here \(U(M)\) is the algebra of affiliated operators, with its usual closed sums and products, and \(H=L^2(M,\tau)\subset U(M)\).
Fix \(i\) and suppress the index from \(\mathcal E_U^i\).
For a finite list \(b=(1,b_2,\ldots,b_m)^{\mathsf t}\) and a holomorphic polynomial matrix \(p\) with \(m\) columns, set
\[
 g(p,b)=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\tau)((\partial_i p)\cdot b).
\]
The product on the right contracts the matrix entries of the outer right tensor action with \(b\), followed by \(\operatorname{id}\otimes\tau\) in each resulting entry.
Every entry is a Hilbert vector because \(\partial_i\) on holomorphic polynomials has finite polynomial tensor values.

In the finite-dimensional left \(K\)-space \(K^{1\times m}\), choose from the polynomial rows \(q\) satisfying \(qb=0\) a basis of their left \(K\)-span.
Write \(P\) for the matrix of those rows.
By [C_F0155](Yin_C_F0155_v1.md#statement) choose \(z\in H^m\) with \(Pz=-g(P,b)\), using \(z=0\) if the basis is empty.
For each further polynomial relation \(qb=0\), the same result applied to the stack of \(P\) and \(q\) gives a vector \(z'\) solving both systems.
Writing \(q=cP\) over \(K\) and multiplying in \(U(M)\) yields \(q(z-z')=cP(z-z')=0\).
The bounded polynomial row acts on Hilbert columns, so \(qz=-g(q,b)\) in \(H\).
Thus the single choice \(z\) respects all polynomial relations.

For a polynomial row \(p\) define \(E(pb)=pz+g(p,b)\).
The relation calculation makes this a well-defined complex-linear map on \(V_b\).
The product rule gives \(g(X_jp,b)=X_jg(p,b)+\delta_{ij}\tau(pb)1\), hence the required commutators.
Replacing \(E(v)\) by \(E(v)-vE(1)\) normalizes it since left multiplication by \(v\in M\) acts on the Hilbert vector \(E(1)\), and the subtracted map is \(R\)-linear.
Conversely, for any such map, induction on word length gives \(E(pb)=pE(b)+g(p,b)\).
In particular its coordinate tuple determines the map everywhere on \(V_b\).

### Closed restriction images

The following argument uses only an embedding \(R\subset K\subset U(M)\) with \(K\) a division ring and existence of normalized solutions on finite modules containing \(1\).
Fix a finite ordered \(B\) with first entry \(1\) and length \(m\).
Use distinct entries, and index additional coordinates by their elements so that permutations leave restrictions unambiguous.
For each extension \(U\) let \(P_U\) be a polynomial-relation basis matrix as above for \(U\), augmented by \((1,0,\ldots,0)\).
The set \(\mathcal E_U\) is a nonempty affine translate of \(\ker_H P_U\): differences of solutions are \(R\)-linear and vanish at \(1\).
Conversely each \(z\in\ker_H P_U\) kills every polynomial relation by left row reduction in \(U(M)\), so \(pU\mapsto pz\) gives every such homogeneous perturbation.
In particular \(\mathcal E_U\) is closed in its finite Hilbert product.

Define \(W_U=\{q\in K^{1\times m}:(q,0)\in\operatorname{row}_K P_U\}\), with the left row span convention.
Then
\[
 \overline{\pi_B\ker_H P_U}
 =\{y\in H^m:qy=0\text{ in }U(M)\text{ for all }q\in W_U\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
For each affiliated row \(q\), \(s=(1+qq^*)^{-1/2}\) is invertible in \(U(M)\) and \(sq\) is a bounded row by polar decomposition.
Thus \(qy=0\) is the kernel of the bounded map \(y\mapsto(sq)y\) on \(H^m\).
This proves closedness on the right, and multiplication by the left row coefficients proves that it contains the projected kernel.

For the reverse inclusion split \(P_U=[P_1\ P_2]\) into old and new columns.
Over \(K\), left row and right column operations reduce \(P_2\) to a rectangular diagonal block with ones and zeros, applying the same row operations to \(P_1\).
The transformed old-coordinate rows opposite the zero rows of the reduced \(P_2\) belong to \(W_U\), so they vanish on \(y\).
Assign the pivot coordinates from the transformed right side, set the free coordinates to zero, and undo the column operations.
This gives \(w\) over \(U(M)\) with \(P_1y+P_2w=0\).
For \(e_n=1_{[0,n]}(\sum_j w_j^*w_j)\), we have \(e_n\uparrow1\) and \(\sum_j(w_je_n)^*(w_je_n)\le n1\), so every \(w_je_n\) is bounded.
Take \(e_n=1\) for an empty new column.
Right multiplication by \(e_n\) gives \((ye_n,we_n)\in\ker_H P_U\), since the matrix coefficients act on the left.
Moreover \(\|y-ye_n\|_2^2=\sum_k\tau(y_k^*y_k(1-e_n))\to0\) by normality of the finite positive functionals with densities \(y_k^*y_k\).
This proves (1).

Fix any prescribed set \(J\subseteq M\) containing \(B\), and let the finite extensions \(U\) be taken inside \(J\).
The \(W_U\) increase as more inputs are included, since extending a polynomial relation by zeros preserves it and the normalization row persists.
Choose \(U_0\subseteq J\) for which the integer \(\dim_KW_U\le m\) is maximal.
Above \(U_0\), all \(W_U\) coincide, so by (1) the nonempty nested affine restriction closures all have the same direction and equal \(\overline{\pi_B\mathcal E_{U_0}}\).
For arbitrary finite \(U\) inside \(J\), approximate any point of this common closed space by projections of solutions on \(U\cup U_0\), then restrict to \(U\).
With \(J=M\) this proves the claimed uniform bound on the infima for fixed \(B\).

### Construction on a countable module

For a countably infinite set of inputs, adjoin \(1\) and enumerate distinct elements as \(J=\{b_1=1,b_2,\ldots\}\).
Set \(B_n=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)\) and write \(\pi_{n,m}\) for restriction from \(B_m\) to \(B_n\) when \(m\ge n\).
These restrictions have operator norm at most one on the Hilbert products.
The preceding argument inside \(J\) shows that the spaces \(\overline{\pi_{n,m}\mathcal E_{B_m}}\) stabilize for each fixed \(n\), since the exhaustion eventually contains its finite stabilizing set.
Denote their eventual value by \(S_n\).
It is nonempty, closed, and lies in \(\mathcal E_{B_n}\) because this solution space is closed.

The restriction \(\pi_{n,n+1}\) takes \(S_{n+1}\) into \(S_n\), with dense image.
Indeed choose \(m\ge n+1\) so large that both levels have stabilized.
Continuity maps the closure of \(\pi_{n+1,m}\mathcal E_{B_m}\) into the closure of \(\pi_{n,m}\mathcal E_{B_m}\), and the image contains \(\pi_{n,m}\mathcal E_{B_m}\), which is dense in \(S_n\).
Choose \(y_1\in S_1\) and successively \(y_{n+1}\in S_{n+1}\) with \(\|\pi_{n,n+1}y_{n+1}-y_n\|<2^{-n}\).
For each \(k\), the sequence \(\pi_{k,n}y_n\), \(n\ge k\), belongs to \(S_k\) and is Cauchy by summability and contractivity.
Its limit \(z_k\in S_k\) satisfies \(\pi_{k,k+1}z_{k+1}=z_k\) by continuity.
The maps on \(V_{B_k}\) determined by these tuples agree on successive modules because their values on the module generators agree and the polynomial commutator recursion determines all other values there.
Their union is the required map on \(V\).
The finitely many indices \(i\) can be treated individually on this same domain.

### Diffuse scalar laws and the arcsine obstruction

Let \(X(t)=t\) in \(M=L^\infty(\mu)\) for a compactly supported diffuse real probability law \(\mu\), so \(R=\mathbb C[t]\).
Every nonzero polynomial has a \(\mu\)-null zero set, hence \(K=\mathbb C(t)\) embeds in \(U(M)\) and each finitely generated \(V_b\subset M\) is torsion-free as an \(R\)-module.
Such a module has a finite free basis over the PID \(R\), as in [The Stacks Project, Lemma 15.22.11(3)](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AUW).
Explicitly, torsion-freeness embeds \(V_b\) in \(K\otimes_R V_b\), a finite-dimensional \(K\)-space.
Clearing a single common denominator of the finitely many generator coordinates embeds \(V_b\) into a finite free \(R\)-module \(R^s\).
Every submodule of \(R^s\) is free by induction on \(s\): the image under the first-coordinate projection is a principal ideal, and lifting its generator when nonzero splits it off from the kernel in \(R^{s-1}\).

For a basis \(c_1,\ldots,c_l\) define \(E(\sum_k p_k c_k)=\sum_k(\operatorname{id}\otimes\tau)((\partial p_k)\cdot c_k)\), where \(\partial t=1\otimes1\) is the polynomial divided difference.
This produces Hilbert vectors and the polynomial Leibniz identity gives \([E,X]=P_1\).
Subtract \(vE(1)\) to normalize.
The finite-module hypothesis of the restriction and countable arguments now holds independently of any vacuum adjoint condition.

For the arcsine law \(d\mu(t)=dt/(\pi\sqrt{1-t^2})\) on \((-1,1)\), put \(T_n(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)\) and \(U_{n-1}(\cos\theta)=\sin(n\theta)/\sin\theta\).
The change of variables \(t=\cos\theta\) gives the uniform law \(d\theta/\pi\) on \((0,\pi)\), so \(\tau(T_n(X))=0\) for every \(n\ge1\).
Every normalized solution on a module containing \(1\) has \(E(T_0(X))=0\) and \(E(T_1(X))=1\).
The recurrence \(T_{n+1}=2tT_n-T_{n-1}\) and the commutator give \(E(T_{n+1}(X))=2XE(T_n(X))-E(T_{n-1}(X))\) for \(n\ge1\).
These initial values and recurrence give \(E(T_n(X))=U_{n-1}(X)\) for \(n\ge1\).
The elementary expansion \(U_{n-1}(\cos\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\exp(i(n-1-2k)\theta)\) has \(n\) orthogonal unit-norm terms in \(L^2((0,\pi),d\theta/\pi)\), since distinct frequencies differ by nonzero even integers.
Therefore \(\|E(T_n(X))\|_2=\sqrt n\) while \(\|T_n(X)\|=1\).
A full-domain solution could be normalized and would be operator-norm-to-\(H\) bounded by [C_F0069, Part A](Yin_C_F0069_v1.md#statement), contradicting these forced values.
[C_F0080](Yin_C_F0080_v1.md#statement) separately excludes the actual ACSY vacuum for this same law.
