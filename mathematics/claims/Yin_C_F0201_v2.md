# Continuous finite-moment tests for bounded duals and limits of normal ACSY laws

## Current assessment
[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Fix a positive integer \(d\) and a finite cutoff \(R>0\).
Let \(\mathcal L_{d,R}\) be the space of joint star-moment laws of \(d\)-tuples \(X\) with \(\|X_j\|\le R\) in finite von Neumann algebras with faithful normal probability trace.
Each law uses its generated tracial representation \(M_\tau=W^*(X)\), \(H_\tau=L^2(M_\tau,\tau)\), \(\Omega=1\).
Equip \(\mathcal L_{d,R}\) with pointwise convergence of moments of star words, and let \(\mathcal N_{d,R}\) be its subspace of coordinatewise normal laws.
Both are compact metrizable spaces.
For \(\tau\in\mathcal L_{d,R}\) let \(\tau^\sharp\) denote the joint law of the adjoint tuple.

Retain the two attained optima \(\alpha_i,\beta_i\), every original represented-norm word test, the iterated finite-moment formula, and the applicability conclusions at the full scope of [C_F0201 v1](Yin_C_F0201_v1.md#statement).
In particular \(\beta_i(\tau)\) is the infimum of \(\|E:M_\tau\to H_\tau\|\) over complex-linear maps satisfying \([E,X_j]a=\delta_{ij}\tau(a)\Omega\) on \(M_\tau\), with unrestricted \(E(1)\).
The value is \(+\infty\) if there is no bounded solution.
The new finite-word tests below use a fixed input norm common to all laws within the cutoff.

For every \(i,n\) and \(C\ge0\) there is a nonnegative continuous function \(D_{i,n,C}\) of finitely many moments, defined in (1), such that
\[
 \beta_i(\tau)\le C\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D_{i,n,C}(\tau)=0\quad\text{for every integer }n\ge0.
\]
If finite, the smallest feasible \(C\) is attained by an ultraweak-to-weak continuous full-domain witness.
Thus \(\alpha_i\) and \(\beta_i\) are lower semicontinuous on \(\mathcal L_{d,R}\).
The set \(\{\beta_i>C\}\) is the union of relatively open sets \(\{D_{i,n,C}>0\}\), each specified by a fixed degree.

For a sequence \(\tau_k\in\mathcal N_{d,R}\) admitting actual ACSY derivations, a uniform bound on their conjugate vectors \(\|\xi_{i,k}\|_2\) for each \(i\) makes every moment cluster law normal and ACSY.
For a sequence in the general tuple space, uniform bounds on \(\alpha_i(\tau_k)\) for every \(i\) imply that all its cluster laws satisfy ACSY, with the same joint bounds on the conjugate vector and the starred derivative values.
Write \(\tau_\omega=\lim_\omega\tau_k\) for a moment limit along a free ultrafilter on the sequence, and write \(\tau_k^+=\tau_k,\ \tau_k^-=\tau_k^\sharp\).
For the uniformly bounded normal ACSY sequence, \(\tau_\omega\) fails the strong condition \(\mathsf S\) of T_F0011 exactly when there is one fixed \((i,\sigma)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{+,-\}\) such that
\[
 \text{for every integer }C\ge1\text{ there is an integer }n(C)\ge0
 \text{ with }\lim_\omega D_{i,n(C),C}(\tau_k^\sigma)>0.
 \tag{A}
\]
An ordinary subsequential moment limit obeys the same equivalence with ordinary limits on that subsequence.
This is a criterion for a specified limit and requires positive limiting margins at fixed test degrees at each scale.

Equivalently, the sequence has *some* cluster law failing \(\mathsf S\) precisely when there exist a fixed \((i,\sigma)\), integers \(n_C\ge0\), and numbers \(\eta_C>0\) for \(C=1,2,\ldots\), such that for every \(m\) arbitrarily large \(k\) satisfy
\[
 D_{i,n_C,C}(\tau_k^\sigma)\ge\eta_C
 \qquad(1\le C\le m).
 \tag{B}
\]
Neither criterion asserts that a sequence satisfying these conditions exists.

## Argument

### One norm and representation for each cutoff

On formal star polynomials in \(d\) generators define the universal \(C^*\)-seminorm by taking the supremum of evaluation norms over all Hilbert-space operator tuples with generator norms at most \(R\).
It is finite by the sum of the absolute word coefficients weighted by \(R^{|w|}\).
The quotient by its null ideal and completion is a separable unital \(C^*\)-algebra \(B_R\), generated by letters \(x_j\) and their adjoints \(y_j\).
All tuples under consideration represent \(B_R\).
Its finite-dimensional polynomial subspaces carry a common input norm, and its bidual supplies the central summand needed below.

Every law determines a tracial state on \(B_R\), and conversely the tracial GNS representation of such a state yields its joint law in the generated von Neumann algebra with faithful normal vector trace.
Indeed the commuting right regular representation has the GNS trace vector as a cyclic vector, so this vector is separating for the left von Neumann algebra.
The trace identity extends from polynomial multipliers to the von Neumann algebra by separate ultraweak continuity.
Thus \(\mathcal L_{d,R}\) is the weak-star closed tracial state space in the dual unit ball of \(B_R\), compact by [weak-star compactness](../audits/literature/Keh25/Yin_U001.md#keh25-u001).
The countable words linearly span a norm-dense algebra, so coordinatewise word convergence on this ball is weak-star convergence and the topology is metrizable.
The normal laws are closed because for each \(j\) they are specified by the vanishing of the squared tracial \(L^2\)-norm of \(x_jy_j-y_jx_j\), a finite-moment condition.
The adjoint-law map is continuous because it interchanges word letters.

### Continuous defects in fixed finite dimensions

List all \(s=s_n\) formal star words of degree at most \(n+1\), including \(1\), in a fixed order.
Write \(P_{n+1}\subset B_R\) for the span of their images with its inherited universal norm.
Let \(G_\tau=(\tau(u^*v))_{u,v}\) be their \(s\)-by-\(s\) Gram matrix and \(v_\tau(h)=G_\tau^{1/2}e_h\) for a listed word \(h\), where \(e_h\) is its coordinate vector.
The identification \(h(X)\Omega\mapsto v_\tau(h)\) is an isometry of the represented word space onto \(\operatorname{ran}G_\tau^{1/2}\), by the Gram identity.
This includes laws where the words are linearly dependent.
Use the convention that Hilbert products are linear in their second argument.

Take the minimum over the compact ball \(\mathcal B_{n,C}\) of all complex-linear \(L:P_{n+1}\to\mathbb C^s\) with \(\|L\|\le C\), including the zero map.
For words \(p,h\) of degree at most \(n\) and \(1\le j\le d\), put
\[
 D_{i,n,C}(\tau)=
 \min_{L\in\mathcal B_{n,C}}\
 \max_{p,h,j}
 \left|
 \langle v_\tau(h),L(x_jp)\rangle
 -\langle v_\tau(y_jh),L(p)\rangle
 -\delta_{ij}\tau(p)\tau(h^*)\right|.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here the arguments of \(L\) are always taken in \(B_R\) and the two Gram vectors are evaluated for the given law.
The compact ball does not depend on \(\tau\).
The finite positive matrix square root depends continuously on its entries even when its rank changes, for instance by uniform polynomial approximation of the square root on a compact interval containing the spectra.
Such an interval is uniform here by the fixed number of words and their cutoff bounds.
The objective in (1) is jointly continuous in \(L\) and the finitely many displayed moments.
Taking its minimum on the fixed compact ball is continuous, as follows by evaluating at an old minimizer for the upper bound and extracting convergent minimizers for the lower bound.
In the law variables this uses only moments of words of length at most \(2n+2\).
The universal polynomial norm in the parameter ball is a fixed constant structure depending on \(d,R,n\).

If a full-domain solution \(E\) has norm at most \(C\), apply \(E\) to the evaluations of \(P_{n+1}\), project its values orthogonally onto the represented word space, and use the Gram isometry for \(L\).
Then \(\|L\|\le C\) by contractivity of evaluation.
Both test rows of (1) are in the represented word space, so projection preserves their pairings and (1) vanishes.
When \(\beta_i\le C\), attainment in [v1](Yin_C_F0201_v1.md#statement) supplies such an \(E\).

### From zero defects to the same global optimum

Conversely assume all defects in (1) vanish for a fixed law and finite \(C\).
Choose a minimizing \(L_n\) at every level, project its values onto \(\operatorname{ran}G_\tau^{1/2}\), and pull back by the Gram isometry.
This gives maps \(T_n:P_{n+1}\to H_\tau\) of norm at most \(C\).
They satisfy (1) with zero residual, with Gram pairings now expressed as pairings in \(H_\tau\).
By weak compactness of Hilbert balls and diagonal extraction on the countable words, take a subsequence with weak limits for all the word values.
One may obtain the subsequence by scalar diagonal extraction on a countable dense subset of \(H_\tau\), followed by Riesz representation, since the polynomial vectors are dense and countable in linear span.
For every finite linear combination \(p\) the limit has norm at most \(C\|p\|_{B_R}\) by weak lower semicontinuity.
Consequently the limits define a linear map \(T:B_R\to H_\tau\) of norm at most \(C\), including well-definedness on universal polynomial relations.
Testing (1) on every fixed word \(h\) gives by Hilbert density and linear extension
\[
 T(x_jb)-X_jT(b)=\delta_{ij}\tau(b)\Omega\qquad (b\in B_R).
 \tag{2}
\]
This step uses \(b\) in the universal algebra and does not require \(T\) to kill the kernel of the law's representation.

Let \(\pi:B_R^{**}\to M_\tau\) be the normal extension of the GNS representation and \(e\) its representing central projection.
The [normal representation and central-summand theorem](../audits/literature/Bla06/Yin_U002.md#bla06-u002) gives an inverse \(j:M_\tau\to eB_R^{**}\) to that summand restriction.
Let \(\iota\) denote the canonical embedding of \(B_R\) into its bidual.
Reflexivity gives an ultraweak-to-weak continuous \(T^{**}:B_R^{**}\to H_\tau\) of the same norm.
The functional \(\tau\) on \(B_R\) extends normally as the trace of \(\pi(\,\cdot\,)\) in \(M_\tau\).
Extend (2) by weak-star density of \(\iota(B_R)\) in \(B_R^{**}\) and separate normality of multiplication by \(\iota(x_j)\).
Using \(\iota(x_j)j(a)=j(X_ja)\) then shows that \(E=T^{**}j\) satisfies the full commutators on \(M_\tau\), with norm at most \(C\).
This proves the equivalence including the constant.
The detection of strict failure follows by taking complements, and shows lower semicontinuity of \(\beta_i\).
The represented tests of v1 use the operator norm in the given law and take an inner approximation limit at each fixed level.
Formula (1) proves a separate equivalence at the global optimum and makes no identification of finite-level minima in the two constructions.

### Uniform conjugate bounds and simultaneous separation

For fixed \(A\ge0\), the condition \(\alpha_i(\tau)\le A\) is [v1, inequality (1)](Yin_C_F0201_v1.md#statement) for all formal star polynomials.
For each individual polynomial both sides of that inequality depend continuously on finitely many moments, since the outer word cuts have finite tensor values.
Intersecting the closed sublevel constraints proves lower semicontinuity of \(\alpha_i\), and hence inheritance of a uniform full-witness norm bound by every cluster law.
For normal ACSY tuples the [forced normal phase](Yin_C_F0076_v1.md#statement) gives \(\partial_i X_j^*=0\) for \(j\ne i\) and \(\partial_i X_i^*=k_i\), where \(k_i\) has modulus one almost everywhere on the diffuse marginal product law.
All starred-generator values are therefore uniquely determined.
The word rule then determines the whole derivation and its vacuum \(\xi_i=\partial_i^*(1\otimes1)\).
Thus \(\alpha_i^2=\|\xi_i\|_2^2+1\) on every normal ACSY law.
The normal subspace is closed, and finiteness of all \(\alpha_i\) is equivalent to the actual ACSY condition by v1.
This proves the normal cluster-law assertion under the stated bounds on the conjugate variables alone.

For a cluster law in the normal subspace, v1 says that failure of \(\mathsf S\) means \(\beta_i(\tau^\sigma)=\infty\) for at least one fixed \((i,\sigma)\).
At each positive integer cap \(C\) this is precisely a strict positive defect at some finite \(n(C)\).
Compactness gives the ultrafilter limit within the law space, and the adjoint-law map and every fixed defect in (1) are continuous.
Passing their values along that ultrafilter proves (A) in both directions, with the same proof for a convergent subsequence.

If a subsequence converges to a law failing \(\mathsf S\), take \(\eta_C\) to be half the positive limiting defect at the chosen degree and index for each cap.
Every finite initial segment of inequalities (B) holds eventually along that subsequence.
Conversely choose increasing indices \(k_m\) satisfying the first \(m\) inequalities and extract a moment-convergent subsequence by compact metrizability.
Every fixed cap then retains its positive lower bound by continuity, proving failure of \(\mathsf S\) at the cluster law.
Taking a supremum of degrees before passing to a limit does not provide these common positive margins.
Lower semicontinuity by itself gives only \(\beta_i(\tau)\le\liminf_k\beta_i(\tau_k)\) for a convergent sequence.
