# A free semicircular coordinate reduces the unrestricted bare-dual implication to bounded finite-word tests

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-candidate)

## Statement

Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\) be any bounded generating tuple in a finite von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau)\) with faithful normal probability trace.
In the tracial free product \(\widetilde M=M*W^*(s)\) freely adjoin a standard variance-one semicircular element \(s=s^*\) and put \(Y=(X,s)\).
Use the [original conditions](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct), and the optimal constants \(\alpha_i,\beta_i\) of [C_F0201](Yin_C_F0201_v1.md#statement).
The latter are respectively the joint Hilbert norm of the vacuum and starred-generator derivatives for actual ACSY witnesses, minimized over those witnesses, and the least operator-norm-to-Hilbert bound of a full-domain dual with unrestricted value at the vacuum.
An infeasible bound is infinite.

For \(1\le i\le d\),
\[
  \alpha_i(Y)=\alpha_i(X),\qquad \beta_i(X)\le\beta_i(Y).
\]
For the new coordinate, \(\alpha_{d+1}(Y)=\sqrt2\) and \(\beta_{d+1}(Y)\le1\).
Thus \(\mathsf A(Y)\) is equivalent to \(\mathsf A(X)\), and \(\mathsf F(Y)\) is equivalent to finiteness of the \(\beta_i(Y)\) for \(1\le i\le d\).

The following three assertions, each quantifying over **all finite tuple lengths**, are equivalent.

1. Every tuple with \(\mathsf A\) has algebraic bare duals on every bounded vector.
2. Every tuple with \(\mathsf A\) has bounded \(M\to L^2(M)\) full-domain duals for all coordinates.
3. Every tuple with \(\mathsf A\) and a distinguished freely independent standard semicircular coordinate has bare full-domain duals.

In the second assertion an ultraweak-to-weak continuous witness of optimal norm can be chosen for each coordinate.
In particular the general implication fails if and only if there exist a bounded tuple \(X\) with every \(\alpha_j(X)<\infty\) and an index \(i\) with \(\beta_i(X)=\infty\).
Given any such word-test law, its free extension \(Y\) has \(\mathsf A(Y)\) and fails the actual algebraic condition \(\mathsf F(Y)\).
This last conclusion is existential across tuple lengths and does not assert automatic continuity for a given unaugmented tuple.

For a fixed common cutoff \(R\ge\max(2,\max_j\|X_j\|)\), there is also a test on the original law for whether its particular extension fails \(\mathsf F\).
With \(D_{i,n,C}\) as in [C_F0201 v2](Yin_C_F0201_v2.md#statement), define \(\widetilde D_{i,n,C}(\tau_X)=D_{i,n,C}(\tau_{(X,s)})\).
Each is continuous in finitely many moments of \(X\).
The extension fails \(\mathsf F\) exactly when for some \(1\le i\le d\), for every integer \(C\ge1\) there exists \(n\) with \(\widetilde D_{i,n,C}(\tau_X)>0\).

## Argument

### Actual starred derivations extend through a free factor

Write \(B=\mathbb C\langle X,X^*\rangle\), \(C=\mathbb C[s]\), \(H=L^2(M)\), and \(\widetilde H=L^2(\widetilde M)\).
The represented algebra generated by \(B,C\) is their algebraic unital free product.
Indeed a centered alternating word represents the corresponding tensor of centered letters in the [pointed free-product Hilbert representation](../audits/literature/Voi98/Yin_U005.md#voi98-u005).
The algebraic tensor products inject into the Hilbert tensors by faithfulness, and distinct factor patterns give orthogonal summands.
This also verifies absence of extra relations even when the original tuple satisfies star relations.

Fix an old coordinate and an actual witness \(\delta\) on \(B\) with vacuum \(\xi\) and values \(K_j=\delta(X_j^*)\).
Inside the coarse space of \(\widetilde M\), extend \(\delta\) by the product rule with \(\delta(C)=0\).
This is well defined on the algebraic free product since its separately prescribed derivations respect the factor relations and the images of \(1\) are zero.
For an alternating centered word \(w\) of length at least two, each differentiated \(B\)-letter with prefix \(u\) and suffix \(v\) contributes the trace pairing of \(u(\delta b)v\) with \(1\otimes1\).
If the prefix is nonempty it ends with a centered \(C\)-letter, and \(\widetilde\tau(u h)=0\) for every \(h\in H\), by reduction first for \(h\in B\), then by Hilbert continuity.
If the suffix is nonempty it starts with a centered \(C\)-letter and \(\widetilde\tau(hv)=0\) in the same way.
At least one of these occurs, so the pairing vanishes, also for a completed tensor \(\delta b\) by density of finite tensors in \(H\otimes_2 H\).
The pairing with \(\xi\) vanishes on this reduced word by orthogonality.
On either single factor and the vacuum the identity follows from the original adjoint pairing, centeredness of \(\xi\), and freeness.
Consequently the same \(\xi\) is the actual adjoint vacuum in \(\widetilde H\), the old starred values retain their Hilbert norms, and the new starred value is zero.
This proves \(\alpha_i(Y)\le\alpha_i(X)\) whenever the latter is finite.

Conversely, let \(Q:\widetilde H\to H\) be the trace-preserving conditional-expectation projection.
It commutes with the left and right actions of \(M\).
On restricting an augmented witness to \(B\), projection by \(Q\otimes Q\) therefore preserves the derivation rule and the prescribed old generator values.
The vacuum becomes \(Q\widetilde\xi\) by adjoint testing and the old starred values are contractions of their augmented values.
This proves the opposite inequality including infeasible cases.
The same centered-word argument with the two factors interchanged extends the scalar semicircular difference quotient, killing all of \(B\).
The scalar adjoint vacuum is \(s\): its moments satisfy \(\tau(s^{k+1})=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\tau(s^r)\tau(s^{k-1-r})\) for \(k\ge1\) by pairing the first letter in the [semicircular moment formula](../audits/literature/NS06/Yin_U004.md#ns06-u004), and \(\tau(s)=0\).
The new coordinate thus has a witness with vacuum norm one and sole nonzero starred value \(\delta s^*=1\otimes1\).
For any such witness, testing at \(s\) gives \(1=|\langle\xi,s\rangle|\le\|\xi\|_2\), and \(s=s^*\) forces the same starred value.
This proves the asserted optimum.

### Bounded witnesses and the three universal assertions

A bounded witness for an old coordinate on \(\widetilde M\) restricts and compresses to \(a\mapsto Q E(a)\) on \(M\).
The projection commutes with each \(X_j\) and fixes the vacuum.
Thus the commutator equations and the input norm bound pass to \(M\).
The same compression of an algebraic witness is defined even before proving boundedness.

On the scalar semicircular Hilbert space use the orthonormal basis \(u_n=U_n(s/2)\), \(n\ge0\), of rescaled second-kind Chebyshev polynomials, with \(u_0=1\).
With \(s=2\cos\theta\), orthonormality follows by sine orthogonality against the semicircle density \(2\sin^2\theta\,d\theta/\pi\), and polynomials are dense on the compact support.
Let \(t u_n=u_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge1\) and \(tu_0=0\).
Then \(s=t+t^*\) and \([t,s]=P_1\).
Its norm-one right-factor lift on the pointed free-product Hilbert space commutes with every left \(M\) multiplier, and has \([t^{\mathrm{lift}},s]=P_1\) on the entire space by the [free-product end-action identity](../audits/literature/Voi98/Yin_U005.md#voi98-u005).
With the \([E,s]=P_1\) convention for \(\beta\) this gives \(\beta_{d+1}(Y)\le1\).

Since \(s\) is selfadjoint, [C_F0201 v1](Yin_C_F0201_v1.md#statement) applies its nonconstant normal affine-combination criterion to the full tuple \(Y\).
Thus a bare system on \(Y\) exists exactly when every \(\beta_i(Y)\) is finite, and finite optima admit the stated normal representatives.
Equivalently, if \(E_{d+1}\) is any witness at \(s\) and \(E_i\) any member of a simultaneous bare system at an old coordinate, both \(E_{d+1}+E_i\) and \(E_{d+1}\) have the prescribed nonzero commutator with \(s\).
Their difference inherits operator-norm-to-Hilbert boundedness.

Assertion 2 implies 1 by forgetting the bound, and 1 implies 3 by restriction of the quantified class.
Under assertion 3, take an arbitrary \(X\) with actual ACSY, extend the witnesses as proved above, apply 3 to \(Y\), and compress its bounded witnesses to \(M\).
This proves 2 at the unrestricted scope.
The optimal bounded representatives on \(M\) follow from [C_F0201 v1](Yin_C_F0201_v1.md#statement).
A law with all finite \(\alpha_j\) and any infinite \(\beta_i\) retains actual ACSY on augmentation while its divergent bound persists by compression, precluding any augmented algebraic system.
Conversely an actual counterexample already has some infinite bounded-dual optimum.

Finally every mixed moment after free augmentation is a polynomial in finitely many original star moments.
To see this, group consecutive same-factor letters, center the factors, apply the vanishing trace of each alternating centered word, and expand the terms with at least one removed scalar factor by induction on the number of alternating blocks.
The semicircular moments are constants independent of the original law.
Composition with the continuous finite-moment discrepancies of C_F0201 v2 now proves the final criterion at the common cutoff.
