# A uniform one-generator criterion for producing an ACSY tuple without bare full-domain duals

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Use the [ACSY and bare full-domain hypotheses](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct) in finite tracial von Neumann algebras generated by the indicated tuples.
Write \(\beta(T)\) for the least norm of any bounded \(M\to L^2(M)\) witness of \([E,T]=P_\Omega\) on \(M\), with unrestricted value at \(1\), and \(+\infty\) when none exists.
Choose a sequence of bounded single generators \(T_n\in(M_n,\tau_n)\) with \(\|T_n\|\le1\) and actual coordinate derivations \(\delta_n\) on the represented star algebras.
Let \(\xi_n=\delta_n^*(1\otimes1)\) and \(K_n=\delta_n(T_n^*)\), measured in the respective normalized Hilbert spaces.

If
\[
 \sup_n\frac{\beta(T_n)}{1+\|\xi_n\|_2}=\infty,                    \tag{1}
\]
then there exists a bounded single generator \(X\) satisfying actual ACSY and \(\beta(X)=\infty\).
There then exists a tuple of length two with actual ACSY and no algebraic full-domain dual system.
The individual \(K_n\) have no common size restriction in this assertion.
In particular if the universal implication \(\mathsf A\Rightarrow\mathsf F\) holds for all finite lengths, there is a numerical \(C<\infty\) such that every single ACSY generator of norm at most one and every actual witness on it satisfy
\[
                   \beta(T)\le C(1+\|\xi\|_2).                      \tag{2}
\]
The theorem does not assert the existence of a sequence satisfying (1).
Equivalently, the quantitative necessity can be stated without an operator cutoff: under that universal implication one numerical constant \(C'\) satisfies \(\beta(T)\le C'\|\xi\|_2\) for every bounded single generator with an actual ACSY witness.
Consequently, an unbounded ratio \(\beta(T_n)/\|\xi_n\|_2\) at arbitrary finite cutoffs already suffices to produce the two-coordinate counterexample.

## Argument

### Scaling and placing the corners

If a term has infinite cost, take that single generator and apply the [free semicircular extension theorem](Yin_C_F0205_v1.md#statement) to obtain the conclusion.
Otherwise pass to a subsequence, indexed again by \(n\ge1\), such that the ratios in (1) are at least \(4^n\).
Choose centers \(\lambda_n=2^{-n}\) and positive weights \(t_n\) so small that
\[
  t_n\le2^{-3n-4},\qquad
  t_n\|K_n\|_2\le 2^{-n},\qquad
  r_n=2^n t_n^{3/2}(1+\|\xi_n\|_2)\le 2^{-n-4}.
\]
This is possible independently at each \(n\), since the chosen witness has finite \(\|K_n\|_2,\|\xi_n\|_2\).
The remaining weight \(t_0=1-\sum_{n\ge1}t_n\) is positive.
At index zero take a fixed standard semicircular scaled to norm at most one, with its actual difference quotient and vacuum, and set \(\lambda_0=2,\ r_0=1/16\).
These scalar vacuum and scaling facts follow from the scalar construction in [C_F0205](Yin_C_F0205_v1.md#argument).

On the von Neumann product \(M=\prod_{n\ge0} M_n\) use the probability trace \(\tau=\sum t_n\tau_n\) and set
\[
               X=(X_n)_n,\qquad X_n=\lambda_n1_n+r_n T_n.
\]
The disk of radius \(r_n\) about \(\lambda_n\) contains \(\sigma(X_n)\).
Each disk is separated by a positive distance from the closure of the union of all the other disks, including the accumulation point zero.
A contour close to that disk yields its individual component unit by holomorphic calculus on \(X\): resolvents outside the closure of the disks are uniformly bounded on the product by the Neumann series, with bound the reciprocal distance to their union.
The resolvents lie in the generated von Neumann algebra by inverse closedness, so the individual units belong to \(W^*(X)\).
In each corner the compressed generator generates \(M_n\), and bounded finite truncations converge strongly to every element of \(M\).
This proves \(W^*(X)=M\).

### The actual derivation on countably many corners

Write \(H=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}H_n\) with \(H_n=L^2(M_n,t_n\tau_n)\) in the inherited norm and unit of the algebra denoted by \(1_n\), of Hilbert norm \(\sqrt{t_n}\).
On an evaluated star polynomial \(p(X)\) define the diagonal block of \(\delta p(X)\) to be \(r_n^{-1}\delta_n(p(X_n))\), viewed in \(H_n\otimes_2H_n\).
The expression inside \(\delta_n\) is a represented polynomial in \(T_n,T_n^*\).
The diagonal values give \(1_n\otimes1_n\) at \(X\) and \(K_n\) at \(X^*\), since all \(r_n,\lambda_n\) are real.
These diagonal families are square summable in the inherited coarse space because \(\sum t_n^2<\infty\) and \(\sum t_n^2\|K_n\|_2^2<\infty\).
By the word rule and boundedness of \(X\) they are square summable for every fixed polynomial.
On this diagonal sum its pairing with \(1\otimes1\) has representing vector with component \(t_n r_n^{-1}\xi_n\).
Indeed the tensor trace carries the weight \(t_n^2\) and the Hilbert pairing the weight \(t_n\).
The squared Hilbert norm is finite since
\[
            \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{t_n^3}{r_n^2}\|\xi_n\|_2^2
               \le \sum_{n\ge1}4^{-n}.
\]

For \(a\ne b\) let \(k_{ab}\in H_a\otimes_2H_b\) solve
\[
                  X_a k_{ab}-k_{ab}X_b=1_a\otimes1_b
\]
by applying the inverse of the scalar \(\lambda_a-\lambda_b\) plus the outer multiplier \(r_a L_{T_a}-r_b R_{T_b}\).
The Neumann series converges since \(r_a+r_b\le|\lambda_a-\lambda_b|/2\), and gives
\(\|k_{ab}\|\le2\sqrt{t_at_b}/|\lambda_a-\lambda_b|\).
For \(1\le a<b\) the denominator is at least \(2^{-a-1}\); the terms with index zero have a uniform positive gap.
Hence
\[
 \sum_{a\ne b}\|k_{ab}\|^2
 \le C_0\!\left(1+\sum_{a\ge1}4^a t_a\sum_{b>a}t_b\right)<\infty .
\]
Put \(k=\bigoplus_{a\ne b}k_{ab}\) in the mixed coarse summands and prescribe there \(\delta p=p(X)k-kp(X)\).
The construction agrees on represented polynomials, respects the outer Leibniz rule, and gives \(1\otimes1\) on the generator when all the blocks are combined.
The mixed vacuum pairing on any \(p\) is bounded by \(2\|k\|_2\|p(X)\|_2\), by moving each outer multiplier onto \(1\otimes1\).
Together with the diagonal vector this supplies the actual Hilbert adjoint vacuum on the dense star algebra.
Thus \(X\) has actual ACSY.

### Simultaneous testing of diagonal duals

For any bounded \(E:M\to H\) with \([E,X]=P_\Omega\), restrict to inputs supported in the \(n\)-th corner and project its output to \(H_n\).
Call the result \(B_n:M_n\to L^2(M_n,\tau_n)\), now measured in normalized norms.
Compressing the generator identity gives \([B_n,T_n]=(t_n/r_n)P_{1_n}\).
Consequently \(\|B_n\|\ge(t_n/r_n)\beta(T_n)\).

There is also a simultaneous estimate
\[
                \|E\|^2\ \ge\ \sum_n t_n\|B_n\|^2.                 \tag{3}
\]
For a finite set \(J\) choose inputs \(a_n\) in the unit balls of the indicated corners, embedded by zero off their own corners.
Allow independent symmetric signs \(\epsilon_n\) on \(J\).
The element \(\sum_{n\in J}\epsilon_n a_n\) always has norm at most one.
The mean squared Hilbert norm of its image under \(E\) equals \(\sum_{n\in J}\|E a_n\|_H^2\) by cancellation of the cross terms.
Projecting each term onto its own corner leaves at least \(\sum_{n\in J}t_n\|B_n a_n\|_{2,\tau_n}^2\) as a lower bound for that mean.
Taking the separate suprema on the finite set and then the supremum over finite sets gives (3), independently of the off-diagonal blocks of \(E\).

In the present construction this forces
\[
 \|E\|^2\ge\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{t_n^3}{r_n^2}\beta(T_n)^2
     =\sum_{n\ge1}4^{-n}
            \frac{\beta(T_n)^2}{(1+\|\xi_n\|_2)^2}=\infty .
\]
No bounded full-domain witness can exist.
By [C_F0205](Yin_C_F0205_v1.md#statement) the freely augmented pair has actual conjugate variables and fails the bare full-domain condition.
If (2) fails, choosing generators and witnesses with arbitrarily large ratios gives the premise of (1), proving the stated consequence by contraposition.
Indeed \(1=\langle\xi,T\rangle\) by testing the actual vacuum at its generator, so \(\|\xi\|_2\ge1\) for \(\|T\|\le1\).
For a general single generator with actual ACSY put \(L=\|T\|>0\).
The coordinate \(T/L\) has actual derivation \(L\delta\) with vacuum \(L\xi\) and optimal cost \(L\beta(T)\), since multiplying a witness by \(L\) and then reversing this scaling gives both inequalities for the infima.
This proves the cutoff-free bound with \(C'=2C\) and the stated ratio criterion.
The construction amalgamates separate one-generator laws; it does not combine arbitrary multivariable twists, which would require simultaneous mixed equations for all coordinates.
