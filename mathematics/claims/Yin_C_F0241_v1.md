# Gaussian column bounds under measurable relabelling and a corner with an unbounded Schur multiplier

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Use the [finite-channel Gaussian model](Yin_C_F0209_v1.md#statement).
Thus \(Z\) is bounded normal with diffuse compact law \(\mu\), the base is \(N=W^*(Z)\), and the finite semicircular noise family is free from \(N\).
The \(q\) bounded selfadjoint first-chaos fields \(T_j\) are specified by a row-bounded measurable \(q\)-by-\(r\) matrix \(A(z,w)\) with \(A(w,z)=\overline{A(z,w)}\).
Use the generated tracial representation of \(X=(Z,T_1,\ldots,T_q)\).

1. If \(X\) satisfies the [actual starred ACSY condition](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct), every canonical right slice for \(X\) and for the adjoint tuple has finite column cost.
   Precisely, for each such slice \(E_i=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta_i\) on its represented starred polynomial core, there is a finite \(C_i\) such that
   \[
       \sum_j\|E_i b_j\|_2^2\le C_i^2\Big\|\sum_j b_j^*b_j\Big\|
   \]
   for all finite polynomial columns.
2. Suppose \(h\) is bounded and Borel on a conull Borel subset of the base spectrum, is injective there, and has a Borel inverse on its image.
   In \(X_h=(h(Z),T_1,\ldots,T_q)\) the exact criterion for actual ACSY is the scalar actual vacuum for \(h_*\mu\), full row rank of \(A\) almost everywhere, and
   \[
     \int {\rm tr}(G(z,w)^{-2})\,d\mu^2<\infty,\qquad
     \int \frac{\|P^{-1}Q-PQ^{-1}\|_{\rm HS}^2}{|h(z)-h(v)|^2}\,d\mu^3<\infty ,
     \quad G=(AA^*)^{1/2},\quad P=G(z,w),\quad Q=\overline{G(w,v)} .
   \]
   Here \({\rm tr}\) is the unnormalized matrix trace and bars are entrywise conjugation.
   When these conditions hold, \(X_h\) satisfies \(\mathsf S\) and all its column costs are finite in both orientations.
   For bi-Lipschitz \(h\) the second integral is comparable to the one for \(Z\), with the squared reciprocal Lipschitz bounds as factors.
3. For an explicit correlated example, let \(Z(s)=s\) on \([-1,1]\) with probability \(d\mu=ds/2\).
   For real \(a\ne0\) take \(h(s)=s+ia|s|\) and the one-channel Hermitian edge kernel \(b(s,t)=1+(s+t)/4\).
   The normal pair \((Y,T_b)\), \(Y=h(Z)\), has actual ACSY, both strong domains and finite column costs in both orientations.
   Its inverse divided difference
   \[
                  k(s,t)=\frac{s-t}{h(s)-h(t)}
   \]
   fails to be a measurable Schur multiplier on \(B(L^2(\mu))\), already between the two opposite open half-intervals.
   In particular \(k\) has no absolutely projectively summable representation by bounded separated functions on this square.

## Argument

### Two Gaussian channels supply the column test inside the model

Realize the original noises together with two further free standard semicirculars \(S',S''\), jointly free from the original ambient algebra.
Appended to the fields as two additional coordinates, these give the same class of Gaussian systems with coefficient matrix
\(\widetilde A=\operatorname{diag}(A,I_2)\).
The intrinsic algebra of the enlarged tuple is generated by the old intrinsic algebra and \(S',S''\), including in the rectangular-noise case.
The old ACSY criterion ensures full row rank almost everywhere and finiteness of both integrals for \(A\).
For \(\widetilde A\) the first integral increases by \(2\).
In the second integral both matrix products gain the same identity summand, which cancels in their difference.
The scalar marginal vacuum and the old row bounds are unchanged.
The [matrix covariance theorem](Yin_C_F0209_v1.md#statement) supplies the enlarged actual derivations and the actual full domains of their canonical closures and adjoints.
For an old coordinate \(i\) let \(\widetilde E\) be that closed canonical right slice restricted to all bounded elements.
It is bounded from operator norm to Hilbert norm by the closed graph theorem, since operator norm convergence of the input implies Hilbert norm convergence.
It agrees with \(E_i\) on all old starred polynomials: the old coarse space embeds isometrically, and the [forced normal starred values](Yin_C_F0076_v1.md#statement) for every old generator, including the own marginal phase, are identical in the two representations.
Leibniz and the unchanged trace then give agreement word by word.

On the enlarged starred core the slice commutes with left multiplication by \(S',S''\), since both of their derivatives at \(i\) vanish.
Each bounded multiplier preserves the closed slice domain and commutes there, by taking graph approximants to a vector in that domain.
In particular the commutation holds on all bounded inputs for polynomials in the two added generators.
Norm-continuity on bounded inputs extends it to their generated \(C^*\)-algebra.
Write \(F_{\rm sc}\) for the continuous distribution function of the standard semicircle, strictly increasing on its supporting interval.
The two continuous functions \(U=\exp(2\pi i F_{\rm sc}(S'))\), \(V=\exp(2\pi i F_{\rm sc}(S''))\) are Haar unitaries by the probability integral transform.
They are free from each other and jointly free from the old intrinsic algebra as elements of the corresponding free factors.
They already belong to \(C^*(S',S'')\).

For each finite old polynomial column \(b_1,\ldots,b_m\) put \(u_j=U^jVU^{-j}\).
The conjugate words, one for each index, are the free Haar family used in [the column encoding](Yin_C_F0208_v1.md#encoding-finite-columns-by-two-haar-unitaries).
It gives \(\|\sum_j u_j b_j\|\le2\|\sum_j b_j^*b_j\|^{1/2}\).
Commutation on the full bounded domain gives \(\widetilde E(\sum_j u_j b_j)=\sum_j u_j E_i b_j\).
The summands on the right are orthogonal across \(j\), by freeness of the \(u_j\) from the old Hilbert-valued coefficients, first for bounded coefficients and then by \(L^2\) approximation.
Thus \(C_i=2\|\widetilde E\|_{M_{\rm enlarged}\to L^2(M_{\rm enlarged})}\) is sufficient.
No additional Haar extension beyond these two Gaussian channels is needed.
For the adjoint tuple, the marginal coordinate becomes \(Z^*\), the selfadjoint fields stay fixed, and their kernels are written in the conjugated base variables.
The normalized actual derivations for this tuple exist by the flip-adjoint companion formula in C_F0076, item 1.
Applying the same Gaussian argument in this orientation gives its column bounds.

### Relabelling the spectral measure

Set \(\nu=h_*\mu\).
The inverse hypothesis gives \(W^*(h(Z))=N\), and diffuseness is preserved by injectivity.
The same first-chaos fields over this base have coefficient matrix
\(A_h(y,y')=A(h^{-1}(y),h^{-1}(y'))\) almost everywhere.
Indeed composition is an isometry on the one-edge coefficient Hilbert spaces that identifies every separated left and right base coefficient, hence identifies the kernel of each field by density.
The row bounds are preserved by change of variables and the noises remain free from the same base.
In the matrix theorem the rank condition and the inverse covariance integral are consequently unchanged.
Substitution \(y=h(z),\ y'=h(w),\ y''=h(v)\) gives exactly the second integral stated in item 2, with no change in the matrix order at either endpoint.
If \(l|z-v|\le|h(z)-h(v)|\le L|z-v|\), the new integral is bounded below and above by \(L^{-2}\) and \(l^{-2}\) times the old one.
The scalar vacuum condition is the actual adjoint condition for the divided difference on the new represented marginal core.
This proves the exact ACSY criterion by C_F0209, its strong-domain conclusion by the same theorem, and the column conclusion by item 1.

### The vacuum and correlation on the two meeting segments

In item 3, \(|s-t|\le |h(s)-h(t)|\le\sqrt{1+a^2}|s-t|\), and \(h^{-1}\) is the real part on its image.
For \(0<\epsilon<1\) define
\[
             C_\epsilon(s)=\int_{|s-t|>\epsilon}
                             \frac{d\mu(t)}{h(s)-h(t)} .
\]
For \(0<s<1\), integration over its own positive half-interval gives
\[
    \frac{\log_+(s/\epsilon)-\log_+((1-s)/\epsilon)}{2(1+ia)},
    \qquad \log_+x=\max(0,\log x).
\]
The absolute value of the numerator is at most \(|\log s-\log(1-s)|\), because taking the positive part is a contraction on the real line.
It converges to \(\log s-\log(1-s)\).
The opposite half-interval contributes at most \(\frac12\log((1+s)/s)\) in absolute value, since \(|h(s)-h(t)|\ge s+|t|\).
That contribution converges pointwise by absolute integration at each \(s>0\).
Reflection yields the same absolute estimates at \(s<0\), with \(s\) replaced by \(|s|\).
These logarithmic majorants are square integrable, so \(C_\epsilon\) converges in \(L^2(\mu)\) to a function \(C\).
For any starred polynomial function \(f\) on the \(Y\)-spectrum, the kernel
\((f(h(s))-f(h(t)))/(h(s)-h(t))\) is bounded, by Lipschitz continuity of \(f\) on the compact containing disk.
Antisymmetry of the truncated Cauchy kernel gives the integral of that divided difference, off \(|s-t|\le\epsilon\), as \(2\int f(h(s))C_\epsilon(s)\,d\mu(s)\).
Passing to the limit exhibits \(2\overline C\) as the actual scalar vacuum in the original parameter coordinates.
The divided difference itself is well defined on the represented marginal algebra by continuity on the support and diffuseness on the square.

The one-channel positive matrix \(G=b\) lies between \(1/2\) and \(3/2\), so it is row bounded, of full rank and has square-integrable inverse.
For \(P=b(s,t)\), \(Q=b(t,v)\) we have
\[
       |P^{-1}Q-PQ^{-1}|=\frac{|Q-P|(P+Q)}{PQ}
           \le 3|s-v|.
\]
Thus the second covariance integral is finite after relabelling.
The conclusions for the pair follow from items 1 and 2.
The conditional second moment of the centered field is
\[
      \mathbb E_{N}(T_b^2)(s)=\int b(s,t)^2\,d\mu(t)
                            =(1+s/4)^2+1/48 .
\]
Indeed the matrix element of this expectation against a base coefficient is the inner product of the corresponding first-chaos vectors, by the one-edge isometry in [the Gaussian realization](Yin_C_F0157_v2.md#bounded-coordinates-phase-and-generation).
This conditional second moment is nonconstant, precluding freeness from \(W^*(Y)=N\).

### A logarithmic lower bound for the inverse divided difference

By a bounded measurable Schur multiplier we mean that pointwise multiplication of square-integrable integral kernels by \(k\) is bounded for the operator norm of their integral operators, uniformly over those kernels.
Failure already on finite-rank tests also excludes a bounded extension to all bounded operators.
Use the opposing points \(s_i=4^{-i}\) and \(t_j=-4^{-j}\), \(1\le i,j\le n\).
Set \(A_+=(1+ia)^{-1}\), \(A_-=(1-ia)^{-1}\).
The matrix entries are
\[
 k_{ij}=\frac{4^{-i}+4^{-j}}{(1+ia)4^{-i}+(1-ia)4^{-j}} .
\]
For \(i<j\) the difference from \(A_+\) has absolute value bounded by \(C_a4^{-(j-i)}\).
For \(i>j\) the corresponding estimate holds with \(A_-\) and \(4^{-(i-j)}\).
This follows by subtraction and the bound of the denominator below by its positive real part.

Test the entrywise product on the matrix \(D_{ij}=1/(i-j)\) for \(i\ne j\), \(D_{ii}=0\).
This matrix has operator norm at most \(\pi\) because it is the compression to frequencies \(1,\ldots,n\) of multiplication by \(i(\pi-\theta)\) on \(L^2([0,2\pi],d\theta/(2\pi))\).
The multiplier has mean zero and integration by parts gives its \(m\)-th Fourier coefficient \(1/m\) for \(m\ne0\).
With \(x=n^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)\), summing by diagonals gives
\[
 \langle x,(k_{ij}D_{ij})x\rangle
       =(A_--A_+)\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-d/n}{d}+O_a(1).
\]
The absolute error is at most \(2C_a\sum_{d\ge1}4^{-d}/d\).
Since \(A_-\ne A_+\), these tests force the Schur norms of the sampled matrices to grow at least as \(c_a\log n-O_a(1)\).
Kwapień and Pełczyński studied the triangular projection that gives this lower bound. [Kwapień and Pełczyński, *The main triangle projection in matrix spaces and its applications*](https://doi.org/10.4064/sm-34-1-43-67)

To pass from samples to the diffuse measure, at each fixed \(n\) choose disjoint small intervals around the positive nodes and disjoint small intervals around the negative nodes.
Use the two sets of \(L^2\)-normalized indicator functions as orthonormal row and column bases.
The input operator with matrix \(D\) between their spans and zero on the orthogonal complement has norm \(\|D\|\) and finite rank.
After pointwise multiplication of its kernel by \(k\), compression between the same spans gives \(D_{ij}\) times the product-measure average of \(k\) on the \(ij\)-th rectangle.
The kernel is continuous at each of these finitely many pairs, so the compressed matrices converge in matrix norm to \((k_{ij}D_{ij})\) as the intervals shrink.
Thus the same lower bound excludes the measurable Schur estimate.
Every absolutely projectively summable expansion in \(L^\infty(\mu)\otimes_\pi L^\infty(\mu)\) would give such an estimate by summing the bounds for left and right multiplication of the input operator.
This proves the asserted failure of that representation.
