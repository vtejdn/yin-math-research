# Subtracting the free Haar right slice can give an unbounded correction at every cutoff

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Fix \(0<s<1\).
Let \(U\) be Haar and free from a unitary \(V\) of Poisson-kernel law \(d\mu_s(z)=(1-s^2)|1-sz|^{-2}\,dm(z)\), where \(m\) is Haar probability on the unit circle.
In their tracial free product set \(A=U\), \(B=U^*V\), and write \(\tau\) for the trace.
Both \(A\) and \(B\) are Haar unitaries, \(\tau(AB)=s\), and the pair has bounded Hilbert-space duals. [Correlated Haar pair](Yin_C_F0057_v1.md#3-correlated-haar-coordinates-retain-bounded-duals)
For comparison, let \(a,b\) be two free Haar unitaries with trace \(\tau_0\).
Use the canonical coordinate-\(A\) right slice \(E^{\rm c}\) of the original pair and the coordinate-\(a\) right slice \(E^{\rm f}\) of the free pair, both normalized by \(\delta A=1\otimes1\) or \(\delta a=1\otimes1\).
Write \(E^{\rm c}_\epsilon,E^{\rm f}_\epsilon\) for the corresponding [liberation cutoffs](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#statement) at any fixed \(\epsilon>0\).

The two polynomial cores are identified as group algebras by the word evaluation \(\iota(p(a,b))=p(A,B)\).
Define the transported free slice on the correlated core by \(F(p(A,B))=\iota(E^{\rm f}p(a,b))\).
Define \(F_\epsilon\) in the same way using \(E^{\rm f}_\epsilon\), re-evaluating its output words as well as its input words.
For the free unitary pair these cutoff outputs on the group algebra are finite sums of words, as shown below.

On the correlated core \(F\) and every \(F_\epsilon\) are unbounded from operator norm to \(L^2(\tau)\).
Their differences \(E^{\rm c}-F\) and \(E^{\rm c}_\epsilon-F_\epsilon\) are likewise unbounded, already on single polynomial inputs.
This holds for each fixed \(s\), although the correlated original and cutoff slices have finite column costs and the actual liberation gradient has finite inverse-gap energy.
The word identification \(\iota\) and its inverse are also nonclosable between the two tracial Hilbert spaces.

## Argument

### The correlated and free word norms

The Poisson-kernel moment \(\tau(V)=s\) and the bounded dual construction hold at each fixed \(s\) in [C_F0057, Sections 2–3](Yin_C_F0057_v1.md#2-a-free-reference-pair-with-bounded-duals).
The two single-factor Laurent polynomial algebras generated by \(U,V\) each represent the Laurent polynomials faithfully because their marginal measures have full support.
Freeness represents their algebraic free product faithfully, as seen by decomposing into the vacuum and centered alternating tensors. [Reduced free-product Hilbert space](../audits/literature/Voi98/Yin_U005.md#free-products-55-58)
The substitution \((U,V)\mapsto(A,B)=(U,U^*V)\) has inverse \((A,B)\mapsto(A,AB)\) in the group algebra.
The same substitution shows that \(a\) and \(ab\) are a free Haar pair in the reference representation, since it takes each nonidentity group word to a nonidentity group word.
Thus the word evaluation is well defined in both directions.

In formal unitary variables put
\[
  w_k=a^k(ab)a^{-k},\qquad P_n=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n w_k,\qquad
  q_n=a(P_n-s1).
\]
In the correlated representation the conjugates \(A^k W^*(V)A^{-k}\) form a free family.
Indeed, in testing an alternating product of centered elements from these algebras, cyclically moving the first power of \(A\) to the end leaves centered \(V\)-blocks separated by nonzero powers of the free Haar unitary \(A\).
If the final power is zero the product still starts and ends in centered \(V\)-blocks and its trace vanishes by freeness.
The same argument applies with \(a,ab\) in the free representation.

If \(x_k\) are conjugate copies of a centered bounded element \(x\) in \(n\) free factors, the reduced-word representation gives
\[
    \left\|\sum_{k=1}^n x_k\right\|\le 2\sqrt n\,\|x\|_2+\|x\|.
\]
Let \(P_k^0\) project onto words beginning in factor \(k\) in the free-product Hilbert space of these factors.
The creation term \(P_k^0 L_{x_k}(1-P_k^0)\) appends the centered vector \(x_k\) on the left and has norm \(\|x\|_2\).
The ranges are orthogonal across \(k\), so the sum of these terms has norm at most \(\sqrt n\|x\|_2\).
The annihilation sum is the adjoint of the creation sum for \(x_k^*\), with the same bound.
The diagonal terms \(P_k^0L_{x_k}P_k^0\) have disjoint supports and norms at most \(\|x\|\), while the remaining compression to \(1-P_k^0\) is zero by centeredness.
The trace is faithful, so this estimates the operator norm in the ambient representation.
This is the length-one case of the free-product creation, annihilation and diagonal decomposition. [Ricard–Xu source passage](Yin_C_F0105_v1.md#1-precise-statement-and-reuse-boundary)

Applying the estimate to \(V-s1\) and to \(ab\), respectively, gives
\[
  P_n(A,B)\longrightarrow s1,\qquad P_n(a,b)\longrightarrow0
  \quad\text{in their respective operator norms}.
\]
In particular \(\|q_n(A,B)\|\to0\).
The vectors \(P_n\) witness nonclosability from the free Hilbert space to the correlated one, and \(P_n-s1\) witness nonclosability in the reverse direction.

### Free right slices at a fixed cutoff

On the Haar marginal the inner multiplier in \(E^{\rm f}_\epsilon\) acting on the coordinate derivation is
\[
 f_\epsilon(z/w)=\frac{|z-w|^2}{|z-w|^2+\epsilon^2}
       =\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}d_m(z/w)^m .
\]
This smooth even function on the circle has absolutely summable Fourier coefficients, by integrating twice by parts.
They satisfy \(d_m=d_{-m}\), \(d_0>0\) and \(\sum_m d_m=f_\epsilon(1)=0\).
For a cut \(h\otimes t\) of a group word, the \(m\)-term after slicing is \(d_m h a^m\,\tau_0(a^{-m}t)\).
The group trace can be nonzero only if \(t=a^m\), which selects at most one \(m\) for each cut.
There are finitely many cuts, proving the asserted algebraic re-evaluability.

The word \(aw_k=a^{k+2}b a^{-k}\) has all its positive \(a\)-cuts before \(b\), so each corresponding suffix contains the single letter \(b\) and its traced term vanishes.
In the negative tail use \(\delta(a^{-k})=-\sum_{j=1}^k a^{-j}\otimes a^{-(k-j+1)}\).
The surviving index for cut \(j\) is \(m=-(k-j+1)\).
The output word after insertion is \(a^{k+2}b a^{-j+m}=w_{k+1}\), independently of \(j\).
Since \(E^{\rm f}_\epsilon(a)=d_0\,1\), writing \(S_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^k d_{-\ell}\) gives
\[
 F_\epsilon(q_n(A,B))
     =-\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n S_k\,w_{k+1}(A,B)-s d_0\,1 .
\]
The coefficients satisfy \(S_k\to-d_0/2\).
As \(\tau(w_{k+1}(A,B))=s\), the correlated trace of the right side tends to \(-s d_0/2\).
Its Hilbert norm therefore stays bounded away from zero.
For the unregularized reference slice the multiplier is the constant one, so \(d_0=1\), all negative Fourier coefficients vanish and \(F(q_n(A,B))=-s1\) for every \(n\).

### Original costs and the obstruction

The bounded Hilbert-space dual \(D_A\) of C_F0057 has the prescribed generator commutators on the correlated pair.
After subtracting right multiplication by \(D_A1\) on polynomial inputs, its commutators and value at \(1\) agree with the canonical right slice.
For unitary generators the commutators with their inverses follow from the inverse identity, so induction on group words gives
\[
          E^{\rm c}p=D_Ap-pD_A1 .
\]
For a finite column the first term has Hilbert column norm bounded by \(\|D_A\|(\sum_j\|p_j\|_2^2)^{1/2}\), and the second by \(\|D_A1\|_2\|\sum_jp_j^*p_j\|^{1/2}\).
Since the trace is a probability, \(c(E^{\rm c})\le\|D_A\|+\|D_A1\|_2\).
The pair has the actual coordinate vacuum by the same bounded-dual construction.
The [inverse-gap and cutoff formulas](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#statement) give finite inverse-gap energy and \(\sup_{\epsilon>0}c(E^{\rm c}_\epsilon)\le3c(E^{\rm c})\).
Thus the original slices send \(q_n(A,B)\) to zero, including at every fixed cutoff, whereas the transported free slices have the nonzero trace limits above.
This excludes decomposition into a word-evaluated free slice and an operator-norm-to-\(L^2\) bounded correction even for this fixed law.
It leaves open estimates for the original slice that use the correlated representation throughout.
