# Marginal weights fail to dominate a mixed slice at a semicircular coordinate

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

There exists a bounded normal pair \((Z,T)\) generating a finite von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau)\) with faithful normal probability trace.
Its first coordinate has a compactly supported planar density, and its second coordinate \(T\) has the standard semicircular law.
The coordinates are noncommuting and their marginal algebras are correlated.
The pair satisfies actual starred ACSY and the strong dual condition. [Task hypotheses](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct)

Let \(\mathcal P=\mathbb C\langle Z,Z^*,T\rangle\) in its represented algebra, and let \(E_T=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta_T\) be the original right slice at \(T\).
It has finite column cost and admits a probability density \(d\in L^1(M)_+\) and a finite constant \(C\) such that
\[
                    \|E_Tp\|_2^2\le C^2\tau(d p^*p)
                    \qquad(p\in\mathcal P).                                  \tag{1}
\]
For every \(w\in L^1(W^*(T))_+\) with \(\tau(w)=1\), the same inequality with \(d=w\) fails for every finite \(C\), even on individual polynomials of the form \(p=u(Z,Z^*)T v(Z,Z^*)\).
Thus the optimal-density commutator and domination theorem can require a density outside the distinguished marginal algebra.
The full-domain duals and both actual adjoint domains exist in this example.

## Argument

### A Markov covariance with a small transition probability

Let \(D_0=\{z:|z|\le1\}\), \(D_1=\{z:|z-4|\le1\}\), and use uniform area probability on the two disks with mixing weights \(5/8\) and \(3/8\), respectively, to define \(\mu\).
In \(N=L^\infty(\mu)\) let \(Z(z)=z\).
Define on the support
\[
 b(z)=
 \begin{cases}
      -1+\tfrac12\sqrt{|z|}, &z\in D_0,\\
       1, &z\in D_1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 P(z,w)=1+b(z)b(w),\qquad a(z,w)=\sqrt{P(z,w)} .
\]
On the uniform unit disk \(\int\sqrt{|z|}=2\int_0^1 s^{3/2}ds=4/5\), so \(\int b\,d\mu=(5/8)(-3/5)+3/8=0\).
The symmetric kernel \(P\) lies in \([0,2]\), is strictly positive almost everywhere, and \(\int P(z,w)\,d\mu(w)=1\).
Write \(Qg(z)=\int P(z,w)g(w)\,d\mu(w)\).
Set \(m(z)=\sqrt{|z|}/2\) on \(D_0\) and \(m(z)=1\) on \(D_1\).
On the same disk \(P(z,w)\ge1\), and on opposite disks \(P(z,w)\) equals \(m\) at the point in \(D_0\).
It follows that \(P(z,w)\ge m(z)m(w)\) and
\[
                     \int m^{-2}\,d\mu=(5/8)\,8+3/8=43/8.                   \tag{2}
\]
Also \(|b(z)-b(v)|\le2|z-v|^{1/2}\), by the square-root bound on \(D_0\), constancy on \(D_1\), and the separation of the disks.
For the bounded area density \(d\mu/dA\le 5/(8\pi)\), integration over \(|z-v|\le1\) and its complement gives
\[
                 \sup_z\int |z-v|^{-1}\,d\mu(v)\le 5/4+1=9/4.                \tag{3}
\]

Take the [one-channel Gaussian construction](Yin_C_F0209_v1.md#statement) over \(N\) and one free standard semicircular noise, with Hermitian coefficient \(a\) and field \(T=T_a\).
Work in the algebra generated by \(Z,T\).
The row norm of \(a\) is one, so \(\|T\|\le2\).
By (3) the scalar Cauchy row \(B(z)=\int (z-v)^{-1}\,d\mu(v)\) is absolutely defined and bounded.
For each starred polynomial \(p\), interchange of the variables and Fubini identify the double trace of its difference quotient with \(2\int p B\,d\mu\), giving the actual vacuum \(2\bar B\).
The first covariance integral \(\int a^{-2}\,d\mu^2\) is bounded by \((\int m^{-1}\,d\mu)^2<\infty\).
For the second one, off the null sets where a denominator vanishes,
\[
 \frac{|a(w,v)/a(z,w)-a(z,w)/a(w,v)|^2}{|z-v|^2}
   =\frac{|b(w)(b(v)-b(z))|^2}{|z-v|^2P(z,w)P(w,v)}
   \le\frac{4}{|z-v|m(z)m(v)m(w)^2}.
\]
Since \((m(z)m(v))^{-1}\le(m(z)^{-2}+m(v)^{-2})/2\), (2) and (3) bound the integral by \(9(43/8)^2\).
Strict positivity almost everywhere is the single-channel rank condition.
The Gaussian criterion therefore gives actual ACSY and the strong domain condition on the original represented tuple.
Its [Gaussian column theorem](Yin_C_F0241_v1.md#statement), item 1, gives finite column cost for the canonical slices in both orientations.
The [optimal-density theorem](Yin_C_F0211_v1.md#statement) supplies (1), including attainment by an ambient faithful density and the corresponding bounded rank-one commutator.

### The semicircular marginal and the conditional square

Use the [Gaussian path representation](Yin_C_F0209_v1.md#colored-paths-and-the-rank-obstruction).
In relative degree \(n\) put \(A_n(z_0,\ldots,z_n)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}a(z_j,z_{j+1})\) and \(A_0=1\).
The path norm is one, because successive integrations of \(a^2=P\) give one.
Creation at the left gives \(A_{n+1}\), and left annihilation on \(A_n\) gives \(A_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge1\), by the same integral of \(P\).
Thus \(T A_0=A_1\), and \(T A_n=A_{n+1}+A_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge1\).
The distinct degrees are orthogonal.
Define \(U_0(t)=1,\ U_1(t)=t,\ U_{n+1}(t)=tU_n(t)-U_{n-1}(t)\).
The polynomial vectors \(U_n(T)\Omega=A_n\) constitute an orthonormal basis of \(L^2(W^*(T))\), since they span all polynomial vectors of \(T\).
For the probability density \((2\pi)^{-1}\sqrt{4-t^2}\) on \([-2,2]\), the substitution \(t=2\cos\theta\) and \(U_n(2\cos\theta)=\sin((n+1)\theta)/\sin\theta\) give the same polynomial integrals, namely zero for \(U_n,\ n>0\).
Agreement on polynomials for these compactly supported laws identifies the marginal of \(T\).
In particular \(U_2(T)=T^2-1\) has spectrum in \([-1,3]\).

For Borel projections \(e,f\in N\) put \(p=eTf\), and identify the projections with their indicator functions on the two disks.
Using \(e=e^2=e^*\) and \(f=f^*\) gives
\[
                        p^*p=fT eT f .
\]
The degree-zero component of its vector is \(f Qe\), with integral \(A=\int e Qf\,d\mu\) by symmetry of \(P\).
Its only other component is in degree two, with path
\[
                  f(z)a(z,w)e(w)a(w,v)f(v).
\]
Indeed the rightmost \(T\) creates one edge, the intermediate \(e\) multiplies its left endpoint, and the remaining \(T\) either annihilates this edge or creates a second one.
The inner product of the two-edge path with \(A_2\) is \(B=\int e(Qf)^2\,d\mu\).
Projection onto the orthonormal basis above gives the following conditional expectation and bounds.
\[
                  \mathbb E_{W^*(T)}(p^*p)= A1+B(T^2-1),\qquad
                0\le B\le A,\qquad 0\le\mathbb E_{W^*(T)}(p^*p)\le4A1 .        \tag{4}
\]
The inequalities use \(0\le Qf\le1\) and positivity of conditional expectation.
For every positive \(w\in L^1(W^*(T))\), pairing with bounded truncations of \(w\) in (4) and passing to the limit gives \(\tau(wp^*p)\le4A\tau(w)\).

### Tests on the represented polynomial core

Take \(f=1_{D_1}\) with mass \(q=3/8\), and \(e=e_r=1_{\{|z|\le r\}}\), \(0<r<1\).
Then \(\mu(e_r)=(5/8)r^2\) and \(Qf(z)=q\sqrt{|z|}/2\) on \(e_r\).
In particular \(A\le q\sqrt r\,\mu(e_r)/2\), and the limiting slice computed below has squared norm \(\|e_r\tau(f)\|_2^2=q^2\mu(e_r)\).
The mixed moment \(\tau(e_r T f T)=A<q\mu(e_r)\) shows correlation, since freeness would give \(\mu(e_r)\mu(f)\) using \(\tau(T)=0,\ \tau(T^2)=1\).
Also \(\|[Z,T]\|_2^2=\iint|z-w|^2P(z,w)\,d\mu(z)d\mu(w)>0\) by the one-edge representation and diffuseness.

To pass (1) with a putative marginal density \(w\) from actual polynomials to this test, approximate \(e_r,f\) by uniformly bounded real starred polynomials \(u_n,v_n\) in \(Z\) converging strongly-star to the respective projections.
For \(f\), its values are continuous on the separated support.
For \(e_r\), first use continuous cutoffs converging pointwise off the null circle \(|z|=r\), and then uniformly approximate on the compact support by polynomials in the two real coordinates to error at most \(1/n\).
Left multiplication by these uniformly bounded approximants converges strongly-star on \(L^2(M)\), first on bounded vectors by the tracial \(L^2\) convergence and then by density.
Thus \(p_n=u_nTv_n\to e_rTf\) strongly-star with uniform bounds, and \(\|p_n\sqrt w\|_2^2\to\tau(wp^*p)\).
The distinguished derivation kills \(Z,Z^*\), and \(E_Tp_n=u_n\tau(v_n)\) on the original core by the outer Leibniz rule.
These outputs converge in Hilbert norm to \(e_r\tau(f)\).

Consequently, if (1) held for these polynomials with \(d=w\), \(\tau(w)=1\), then at every \(0<r<1\),
\[
     q^2\mu(e_r)\le4 C^2 A\le 2q C^2\sqrt r\,\mu(e_r),
                      \qquad C^2\ge\frac{3}{16\sqrt r}.
\]
Each \(\mu(e_r)>0\), and the lower bound diverges.
All tests belong by approximation to the same original core on one fixed pair, with the same finite column cost and actual vacua throughout.
