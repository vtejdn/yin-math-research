# Local secant variation under regularly varying singular concentration

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \(Z=X_i\) belong to a bounded normal generating tuple with actual starred conjugate variables in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal probability trace.
For a fixed \(b\) in the algebra generated by the other coordinates, put \(P=|Z-b|\) and \(F(s)=\tau(1_{[0,s]}(P))\).
Suppose \(F\) is positive at small radii and, for some \(0<\alpha<1\), is regularly varying at zero of index \(\alpha\), meaning
\[
                      F(st)/F(s)\longrightarrow t^\alpha
                                  \qquad(t>0,\ s\downarrow0).
\]
Assume \(\int_0^1 F(s)^3ds/s^3=\infty\).
Use the local phase spread \(e(z)\) on the distinguished spectrum from the [local secant theorem](Yin_C_F0273_v1.md#statement).
At every common concentration point \(z_0\) along a concentrating sequence from the [two-resolvent theorem](Yin_C_F0269_v1.md#statement),
\[
                      e(z_0)\ \ge\ 2\big(\csc(\pi\alpha/2)-1\big).       \tag{1}
\]
Consequently these assumptions require \(1/3\le\alpha\le2/3\).
The separate [singular-mass estimate](Yin_C_F0229_v1.md#statement) improves the lower restriction to \(\alpha\ge1/2\), irrespective of divergence.
For \(1/2\le\alpha<2/3\) the cubic divergence assumption is automatic.
At \(\alpha=2/3\) it remains an explicit condition.
In particular (1) applies to regularly varying critical laws with a divergent cubic integral even when \(F(s)/s^{2/3}\to0\).

## Argument

### Rescaling the singular law

Put \(H(s)=\tau(s^2/(P^2+s^2))\) and \(A(s)=\tau(sP/(P^2+s^2))\).
The rescaled positive measure on \([0,\infty)\) with distribution \(F(st)/F(s)\) converges on compactly supported continuous tests to \(\alpha t^{\alpha-1}dt\).
Indeed the distribution functions converge at every positive endpoint and the limiting distribution is continuous with no atom at zero.
Approximation by step functions on a compact interval gives the asserted convergence there, including at zero by taking a small positive endpoint.
The singular-law theorem excludes an atom at zero in the initial law as well.

For each \(0<\gamma<\min(\alpha,1-\alpha)\) regular variation at dilation two gives, for small enough \(x\),
\[
             2^{\alpha-\gamma}\le F(2x)/F(x)\le2^{\alpha+\gamma}.
\]
It follows by iteration and monotonicity that \(c_\gamma s^{\alpha+\gamma}\le F(s)\le C_\gamma s^{\alpha-\gamma}\) at sufficiently small \(s\).
To see this choose a fixed small \(s_*>0\) and iterate \(2^n s\) until it first reaches \([s_*/2,s_*]\).
The value of \(F\) on this interval is bounded below by \(F(s_*/2)>0\) and above by one.
The same iteration gives
\[
         F(2^j s)\le K_\gamma\,2^{(\alpha+\gamma)j}F(s)
                         \qquad(j\ge0,\ 0<s<s_*/2).                     \tag{2}
\]
Up to the first crossing of \(s_*/2\) this is the iterated doubling bound.
Thereafter use \(F\le1\) and divide the lower bound \(F(s_*/2)\) at the crossing into the constant.

On \(t\ge1\), the two kernels \(1/(1+t^2),t/(1+t^2)\) are bounded by \(1/t\).
Their tail integrals past \(2^m\) against the rescaled measure are therefore bounded uniformly in small \(s\) by a constant times \(\sum_{j\ge m}2^{-(1-\alpha-\gamma)j}\), using (2).
Thus the compact convergence gives
\[
 \frac{H(s)}{F(s)}\longrightarrow
       \int_0^\infty\frac{\alpha t^{\alpha-1}}{1+t^2}dt
       =\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\csc(\pi\alpha/2),\qquad
 \frac{A(s)}{F(s)}\longrightarrow
       \int_0^\infty\frac{\alpha t^\alpha}{1+t^2}dt
       =\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\sec(\pi\alpha/2).
\]
The two evaluations follow from the [Mellin integral](Yin_C_F0261_v2.md#a-positive-scale-average-of-the-signed-resolvent-test) by \(x=t^2\).
In particular \(A(s)/H(s)\to\tan(\pi\alpha/2)\) on the full scale axis.

### The local trace constraints and the two endpoint regimes

Take any common concentration point along the stated selected sequence.
Property (2), with \(\alpha+\gamma<1\), yields the vanishing normalized tail beyond \(P>2^m s\) as \(m\to\infty\) uniformly along that sequence, since \(H(s)/F(s)\) stays bounded below.
On \(P\le 2^m s\) one has \(a_s(P)\le 2^m h_s(P)\).
The [variation localization proof](Yin_C_F0273_v1.md#concentration-of-the-complex-measures-in-total-variation-away-from-the-point) therefore applies at every such point without discarding a further class of scales.
The [two scalar trace inequalities](Yin_C_F0273_v1.md#the-two-scalar-trace-inequalities-in-a-local-cone) imply
\[
                1\le \big(e(z_0)+e(z_0)^2/4\big)\tan^2(\pi\alpha/2).
\]
Solving the quadratic inequality and using \(1+\cot^2 x=\csc^2 x\) gives (1).

Since \(e(z_0)\le2\), (1) requires \(\alpha\ge1/3\).
The separate pointwise estimate \(F(s)^2\le\pi s\|\xi_i\|_2\) excludes \(\alpha<1/2\) by the lower power bound with \(\alpha+\gamma<1/2\).
If \(\alpha>2/3\), choose \(\alpha-\gamma>2/3\) in the upper power bound.
Then \(F(s)^3 s^{-3}\) is integrable at zero.
If \(\alpha<2/3\), choose \(\alpha+\gamma<2/3\) and use the lower power bound to see divergence.
At equality write \(F(s)=s^{2/3}L(s)\).
The cubic test is exactly \(\int_0^1 L(s)^3ds/s\), which may diverge even when \(L(s)\to0\); for instance \(L(s)=(\log(e/s))^{-1/3}\) has this property.
This example describes a scalar tail and supplies no realization by a shifted coordinate.
