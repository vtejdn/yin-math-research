# A free selfadjoint noise with measurable location and scale over a normal coordinate

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \(Z(z)=z\) generate \(A=L^\infty(\mu)\) for a compactly supported diffuse planar probability measure, and let \(v=v^*\) be free from \(A\) in their tracial free product \(M\).
Assume \(v\) is bounded with diffuse law and its scalar divided difference has an actual conjugate variable \(g\).
Let \(k,h\in A\) be bounded real Borel functions, \(h>0\) almost everywhere.
Set \(b=1/h\), \(T=k+hvh\), and \(Y=(Z,T)\).
The following are equivalent.

1. \(Y\) has [actual conjugate variables](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct) on its represented starred polynomial core.
2. \(Y\) has a strong dual system whose operators and Hilbert adjoints are defined on every bounded vector.
3. \(Z\) has an actual scalar conjugate variable \(\zeta\), \(b\in L^2(\mu)\), and
   \[
   \iint_{z\ne w}\frac{|K(z,w)|^2+|L(z,w)|^2}{|z-w|^2}\,d\mu(z)d\mu(w)<\infty,
   \quad
   K(z,w)=\frac{h(w)}{h(z)}-\frac{h(z)}{h(w)},\quad
   L(z,w)=\frac{k(w)-k(z)}{h(z)h(w)}.                                  \tag{1}
   \]

When these conditions hold, both right contractions on the original core and on the adjoint tuple have finite [column costs](Yin_C_F0208_v1.md#statement).
The negatives of the original right-contraction closures witness the strong condition.
Write \(H=vg-1\) and let \(J_s(a\otimes d)=a s d\) for \(a,d\in A\) and centered \(s\in L^2(W^*(v))\), extended by continuity.
With the inner product linear in the second variable, the target vacua satisfy
\[
 \xi_T=J_g(b\otimes b),\qquad
 \|\xi_Z\|_2^2=\|\zeta\|_2^2+
       \iint_{z\ne w}\frac{\|K(z,w)H+L(z,w)g\|_2^2}{|z-w|^2}
                                                    \,d\mu(z)d\mu(w).     \tag{2}
\]
The Gram matrix of \(H,g\) is positive definite for every noise allowed here.
For a standard semicircular \(v\) these two vectors lie in the first and second Wick degrees, as in the [Gaussian drift and covariance criterion](Yin_C_F0260_v1.md#statement).
Formula (2) retains their cross term for general noise.

For normalized area on the unit disk, \(h(z)=|z|^\alpha\) and \(k(z)=|z|^\beta\), \(\alpha,\beta>0\), the pair has both regularity properties exactly when \(\alpha<1\) and \(\beta>2\alpha-1\).

## Argument

### A necessary inverse scale and the conjugate variable of the new coordinate

Write \(B=W^*(v)\), \(P=\mathbb C\langle Z,Z^*,T\rangle\), and \(\mathcal Q=A*\mathbb C[v]\), the algebra of finite words in these two factors inside \(M\).
The centered reduced-word representation embeds the algebraic free product, and diffuseness makes \(\mathbb C[v]\) a polynomial algebra.
The von Neumann algebra generated by \(Y\) contains \(b1_{\{b\le n\}}(T-k)b1_{\{b\le n\}}=1_{\{b\le n\}}v1_{\{b\le n\}}\), converging strongly to \(v\).
Thus \(W^*(Y)=M\).
For centered \(s,t\in L^2(B)\), freeness gives
\[
 \langle J_s(f),J_t(f')\rangle=\langle s,t\rangle\langle f,f'\rangle
       \quad(f,f'\in L^2(\mu^2)).
\]
This follows first for bounded centered \(s,t\) and simple bounded tensors from
\(\mathbb E_A(s^*a t)=\tau(a)\tau(s^*t)1\), then by \(L^2\) approximation.
The ranges of these maps are orthogonal to \(L^2(A)\).

Suppose \(Y\) is actual.
The [forced derivatives for a normal tuple](Yin_C_F0076_v1.md#statement) restrict the \(Z\)-derivation to the scalar divided difference, so projection of its vacuum onto \(A\) gives \(\zeta\).
The \(T\)-derivation kills \(Z,Z^*\), and its vacuum \(\psi\) is orthogonal to \(L^2(A)\).
For marginal polynomials \(a,d\), testing \(aTd\), subtracting its \(A\)-component and putting \(v_0=v-\tau(v)\) gives
\[
       \langle\psi,J_{v_0}(ah\otimes hd)\rangle=\tau(a)\tau(d).
\]
Uniformly bounded strongly-star marginal polynomial approximants extend this identity to \(a,d\in A\), by the norm formula for \(J_{v_0}\).
With \(d=1\) it gives \(|\tau(a)|\le C\|a h\|_2\) with \(C=\|\psi\|_2\|v_0\|_2\|h\|_2\).
Taking \(a=b^2 1_{\{b\le n\}}\) gives \(\int_{\{b\le n\}} b^2\,d\mu\le C^2\).
Thus \(b\in L^2(\mu)\).

For the remainder take \(b\in L^2(\mu)\).
Let \(d_0,d_R,d_L\) be polynomial derivations on \(\mathbb C[v]\) taking \(v\) to \(1\otimes1,1\otimes v,v\otimes1\), respectively.
Extend them to \(\mathcal Q\) by zero on \(A\).
For a finite bounded tensor \(\beta=\sum x_\ell\otimes y_\ell\) with both factors in \(A\), let \(d_{\sigma,\beta}=I_\beta d_\sigma\), where \(I_{x\otimes y}(a\otimes d)=ax\otimes yd\).
For \(\beta\in L^2(\mu^2)\) define \(d_{\sigma,\beta}\) by its values at \(v\), respectively \(\beta,\beta\cdot v,v\cdot\beta\), with the outer actions, and by zero on \(A\).
The product rule on each fixed word gives convergence in coarse Hilbert norm under bounded finite-tensor approximation in \(L^2(\mu^2)\).
It also identifies these derivations with the finite insertions for bounded separated symbols.

The following two consequences of the [free terminal-block estimate](Yin_C_F0268_v1.md#inserting-an-arbitrary-base-tensor-at-the-selfadjoint-coordinate) will be used for all three derivations.
If \(e_\sigma=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)d_\sigma|_{\mathbb C[v]}\) is bounded for the operator-norm to Hilbert-norm map with bound \(a_\sigma\), and \(\tau(e_\sigma f)=\langle s_\sigma,f\rangle\), then
\[
   c((\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)d_{\sigma,\beta}|_{\mathcal Q})
       \le (2\sqrt{4/\pi}\,a_\sigma+2\|s_\sigma\|_2)\|\beta\|_2,
   \qquad d_{\sigma,\beta}^*(1\otimes1)=J_{s_\sigma}(\overline\beta).    \tag{3}
\]
Here \(c\) is the column supremum with inputs in the indicated algebra, and the bar conjugates both base tensor coefficients.
To verify the hypotheses of the terminal calculation, take a reduced word \(q f a\) ending with its last centered \(B\)-block \(f\) and bounded \(a\in A\), allowing \(a=1\).
All earlier derivatives leave a centered suffix with a later noise block after taking the right trace, and give zero by freeness.
Insertion of a finite tensor in the last block gives \(\sum_\ell q(e_\sigma f)x_\ell\tau(a y_\ell)\), since each right noise coefficient has trace against \(a y_\ell\) equal to the product of traces.
This is exactly formula (4) of the cited terminal calculation with \(e_\sigma,s_\sigma\) in place of its slice and its vacuum.
Its centered estimate uses only \(e_\sigma 1=0\), the bound \(a_\sigma\), and the commutative finite-family inequality on \(C^*(v)\).
Its scalar estimate there, formula (5), uses only \(\tau(e_\sigma f)=\langle s_\sigma,f\rangle\), centering of \(s_\sigma\), and freeness.
They give the asserted constant.
For the vacuum, the trace of \(d_\sigma\) on a reduced word of length at least two vanishes by the centered free segment on at least one trace leg.
For finite separated tensors traciality then gives the double trace of \(d_{\sigma,\beta}p\) as \(\sum_\ell\langle s_\sigma,x_\ell p y_\ell\rangle\).
Taking the limit on fixed words proves both conclusions for every Hilbert tensor \(\beta\).

The [scalar estimate](Yin_C_F0055_v1.md#statement) bounds \(e_0\), with scalar vacuum \(s_0=g\).
The scalar difference kernels give \(e_R f=e_0(fv)-f\) and \(e_L f=v e_0 f\), so both maps are bounded as required.
The left and right weighted differences have the same double trace by swapping the variables, since the scalar divided difference is symmetric.
Thus \(s_R=s_L=vg-1=H\).
The vacuum \(g\) is real by testing adjoints in the scalar identity.
Both \(g\) and \(H\) are centered, since \(\tau(g)=0\) and \(\tau(vg)=1\).
If \(H=\lambda g\), reality and \(g\ne0\) make \(\lambda\) real.
For \(u=v-\lambda\) one then has \(ug=1\).
Testing \(u^3\) with the scalar vacuum gives \(\tau(u^2)=2\tau(u^2)+\tau(u)^2\), which contradicts diffuseness.
This proves the positive Gram assertion.

The restriction of \(d_{0,b\otimes b}\) to \(P\) kills \(Z,Z^*\) and takes \(T\) to \(1\otimes1\).
Equation (3) gives its actual vacuum and its column bound.
In particular \(T\) has diffuse law.
Indeed restriction to polynomials in \(T\) and projection of this vacuum onto \(W^*(T)\) give the usual scalar divided-difference vacuum there, with represented derivative normalized at \(T\).
The [diffuse marginal conclusion](Yin_C_F0076_v1.md#statement) applies to that one-element selfadjoint system, by orthogonal projection of both tensor legs onto its scalar Hilbert space.

### Liberation at the base coordinate and the two weighted differences

Define \(\gamma^0\) on \(\mathcal Q\) by the inner difference \(a\otimes1-1\otimes a\) on \(A\) and by zero on \(v\).
It has zero vacuum, by traciality in \(A\) and the centered free segments on mixed reduced words.
Put \(B_R(z,w)=b(z)h(w)-1\), \(B_L(z,w)=1-h(z)b(w)\).
Then \(B_R+B_L=K\).
These symbols, and \(L=b(z)b(w)(k(w)-k(z))\), are real Hilbert tensors because \(h,k\) are bounded and \(b\in L^2\).
Set
\[
      \gamma=\gamma^0+d_{0,L}+d_{R,B_R}+d_{L,B_L}.                      \tag{4}
\]
This is a represented derivation on \(\mathcal Q\) with \(\gamma a=a\otimes1-1\otimes a\).
To compute \(\gamma T\), its \(d_{0,L}\) contribution is \(1\otimes k-k\otimes1\).
The next two contributions are \(1\otimes hvh-h\otimes vh\) and \(hv\otimes h-hvh\otimes1\), respectively.
Their sum cancels \(\gamma^0 T=k\otimes1-1\otimes k+ h\otimes vh-1\otimes hvh+hvh\otimes1-hv\otimes h\).
Thus \(\gamma|_P\) is precisely the partial liberation derivation at \(Z\).
By (3) its vacuum on the dense target core is
\[
                      \eta=J_g(L)+J_H(K).
\]
The base bimodule action on \(J_s(f)\) is multiplication on the first and last arguments of \(f\).
Consequently the joint left-right spectral measure of \(\eta\) at \(Z\) is \(\|L(z,w)g+K(z,w)H\|_2^2\,d\mu(z)d\mu(w)\), by the inner-product identity for the maps \(J_s\).
Its density lies between strictly positive constant multiples of \(|L|^2+|K|^2\) by positive definiteness of the fixed Gram matrix.
The [marginal and liberation recovery theorem](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#statement) now applies on \(P\) with both marginals diffuse, proving that actuality at \(Z\) is equivalent to its marginal vacuum and (1).
Its norm formula is (2).
Together with the \(T\)-coordinate this proves equivalence of items 1 and 3.

### Column bounds on the original starred words

Assume item 3 and write \(u=z-w\), \(t_r=\bar u/(|u|^2+r^2)\) for \(r>0\), \(C=\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau\), and \(\delta_Z\) for the recovered derivative.
By the same recovery theorem \(I_u\delta_Z=\gamma|_P\).
For fixed words in \(\mathcal Q\) the bounded inner multiplier \(I_{t_r}\) commutes with the outer actions.
Thus \(I_{t_r}d_{\sigma,\beta}=d_{\sigma,t_r\beta}\), first on \(v\), and then by the product rule and finite-tensor approximation.
The right slice of \(I_{t_r}\gamma\) equals
\[
     CI_{t_r}\gamma^0+Cd_{0,t_rL}+Cd_{R,t_rB_R}+Cd_{L,t_rB_L}.
\]
Since \(h(w)/h(z)>0\), the numbers \(B_R,B_L\) have the same sign.
Thus \(|B_R|,|B_L|\le |K|\).
Equation (1) bounds \(\|t_rL\|_2,\|t_rB_R\|_2,\|t_rB_L\|_2\) uniformly, so (3) controls these three terms on the entire target core.

For the free reference term use the [reference and cutoff argument](Yin_C_F0265_v1.md#removing-the-cutoff-on-the-target-polynomial-core).
On \(P_0=\mathbb C\langle Z,Z^*,v\rangle\), the free reference slice at \(Z\) has finite cost \(c_0\) from its scalar vacuum and freeness, as in that argument with a free selfadjoint noise factor.
Its centered-word extension, the right-module column calculation and the soft estimate require only free normal coordinates with scalar vacua, which are present here.
Hence \(CI_{t_r}\gamma^0\) has cost at most \(3c_0\) on \(P_0\), uniformly in \(r\).
To apply this bound on \(P\), replace \(h,k\) by uniformly bounded marginal star-polynomials converging strongly-star to them.
Their \(\gamma^0\)-values converge in the coarse norm by the explicit inner difference.
The product rule gives bounded strong-star and entrywise graph approximation to every finite target column \(p\), including its adjoints.
As in the cited cutoff argument a column of norm at most one has approximants of limiting column norm at most \(1+\epsilon\), for arbitrary \(\epsilon>0\).
Indeed for an approximating column \(q\) multiply by a smooth function of \(q^*q\) that equals one near \([0,1]\) and whose product with \(\sqrt{s}\) has supremum at most \(1+\epsilon\) for \(s\ge0\).
Polynomial approximation of this function and its first derivative on the common bounded spectral interval gives column approximants in \(P_0\).
The divided difference acting on \(\gamma^0(q^*q)\) converges to zero, because its limiting outer arguments lie in \([0,1]^2\).
Hence the approximants retain entrywise \(\gamma^0\)-graph convergence.
For each fixed \(r\), boundedness of \(I_{t_r}\) passes the estimate to the target column, then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) gives the same \(3c_0\).

Finally \(t_r u\to1\) off the diffuse spectral diagonal, with \(|t_ru|\le1\).
The coarse inner spectral measures are absolutely continuous with respect to \(\mu^2\) as in the recovery theorem, giving \(I_{t_r}\gamma p\to\delta_Zp\) in Hilbert norm on every target polynomial.
The uniform bounds therefore control the original right column at \(Z\).
They apply also to the pair \((Z^*,T)\) by conjugating the base spectral parameter in \(h,k\) and in the marginal law.
The marginal vacuum at \(Z^*\) is \(\zeta^*\) by the flip-adjoint derivation, and all the absolute kernels in (1) are unchanged under that substitution.
The adjoint tuple's right-slice estimate gives the original left-slice estimate by applying the flip-adjoint derivation to \(p^*\).
The [two-contraction domain theorem](Yin_C_F0072_v1.md#statement) then gives bounded vectors in both the original right-slice closure and its adjoint domains at each coordinate, with the generator-first commutators after negating the closures.
This proves item 2, whose converse to actuality is the [normal strong-to-vacuum theorem](Yin_C_F0070_v1.md#statement).

### The radial thresholds on the disk

Normalized disk area has an [actual scalar vacuum](Yin_C_F0267_v1.md#a-disk-with-vanishing-one-dimensional-density).
For \(r>s\) the angular average of \(|z-w|^{-2}\) is \((r^2-s^2)^{-1}\), by expanding the two geometric series with ratio \(s/r\).
Symmetry of the integrands and substitution \(x=s/r\) express the two parts of (1) as
\[
 \begin{split}
 8\int_0^1 r\,dr\,\int_0^1
      \frac{x(x^\alpha-x^{-\alpha})^2}{1-x^2}\,dx,\qquad
 8\int_0^1 r^{\,1+2\beta-4\alpha}\,dr\,\int_0^1
      \frac{x^{1-2\alpha}(1-x^\beta)^2}{1-x^2}\,dx .
 \end{split}
\]
Both angularly integrated factors are integrable near \(x=1\) and, for the stated positive exponents, both are finite near zero exactly when \(\alpha<1\).
The inverse scale is square integrable under the same restriction.
The remaining radial factor is integrable precisely for \(\beta>2\alpha-1\), with logarithmic divergence at equality.
For \(1/2<\alpha<1\) and \(\beta=2\alpha-1\), each of the pairs obtained by setting \(h=1\) or \(k=0\) has both properties, while their combined location and scale fails both.
