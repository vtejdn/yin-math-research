# Product density and a weighted test for ordered two-block outputs

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let a finite generating tuple of bounded normal elements in a finite tracial von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau)\) have [actual starred conjugate variables](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct), with \(\tau(1)=1\).
Fix a coordinate \(Z\), its scalar marginal probability \(\mu\), \(B=W^*(Z)\), the algebra \(N\) generated by the other coordinates and their adjoints, and the joint represented starred polynomial algebra \(P\).
Write \(\delta\) for the distinguished derivation, \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\), \(E=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta\), and \(P_K\) for projection onto \(K=\overline{\operatorname{span}NB}^{\,2}\) in \(L^2(M)\).
Inner products are linear in the second variable.
On \(P_B=\mathbb C[Z,Z^*]\) let \(L=JE_BJ\), where \(J\) is scalar conjugation and \(E_B\) is the scalar right slice.
For a finite test \(u=\sum n_j\otimes a_j\in N\odot P_B\) write \(Uu=\sum n_j a_j\) and \(L_0u=\sum n_j\otimes La_j\), with \(U L_0u=\sum n_j(La_j)(Z)\).
Put \(Qp=P_K(p\xi^*-Ep)\) for \(p\in P\).

1. The product-trace functional on \(N\odot P_B\) is represented in \(K\) by \(v=P_K(1+\xi^* Z-Z\xi^*)\).
   In particular, \(\langle Uu,v\rangle=\sum\overline{\tau(n_j)\tau(a_j)}\).
   If \(N\) is abelian, let \(\eta\) denote its trace probability in a standard measure space realization, and let \(\gamma\) be the ordered spectral law of left \(N\) and right \(B\) on \(1\).
   Then \(\lambda=\eta\otimes\mu\ll\gamma\) and its density \(r=d\lambda/d\gamma\) satisfies
   \[
           \int r^2\,d\gamma-1
             =\|P_K(\xi^*Z-Z\xi^*)\|_2^2
             \le4\inf_{c\in\mathbb C}\|Z-c1\|^2\,\|\xi\|_2^2.             \tag{1}
   \]
   Writing \(\gamma=\eta(dy)\nu_y(dz)\), one has \(\mu\ll\nu_y\) with density \(r(y,\cdot)\) for almost every \(y\), including when \(\gamma\) has an additional part singular to \(\lambda\).

2. For any scalar \(d\in L^1(\mu)\) positive almost everywhere, the least \(C\) in \(\|Qp\|_2\le C\|p\sqrt d\|_2\) on the entire original \(P\) equals the least \(C\) in
   \[
               \|(U L_0u)d^{-1/2}\|_2\le C\|Uu\|_2
                      \qquad(u\in N\odot P_B).                            \tag{2}
   \]
   An infinite norm or the absence of a finite constant is allowed.
   For abelian \(N\) its square is
   \[
       \mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_{y\,[\eta]}\,
       \sup_{a\in P_B}
       \frac{\displaystyle\int |La(z)|^2\,d(z)^{-1}\,d\nu_y(z)}
            {\displaystyle\int |a(z)|^2\,d\nu_y(z)},                     \tag{3}
   \]
   with \(0/0=0\) and a positive numerator over zero interpreted as infinity.
   A finite \(C\) gives the weight \(2(\xi^*\xi+C^2d)\) for the projected right slice \(P_KE\) on all original columns.

3. The following sufficient condition for (2) uses an integrable inverse in place of a uniform lower bound.
   On \(H_N=L^2(N)\) suppose there is a bounded field \(A(z)\ge0\) on \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\), Lipschitz in operator norm with constant \(b\), such that for bounded \(n,n'\in N\)
   \[
             \mathbb E_B(n^*n')(z)=\langle A(z)n,A(z)n'\rangle_{H_N}
                      \quad\text{for almost every }z.
   \]
   Suppose \(A(z)\) is invertible almost everywhere and \(D^2=\int\|A(z)^{-1}\|^2d\mu(z)<\infty\).
   Then (2) holds for \(d=1+W\) with \(C=\sqrt 2+bD\), where \(W\) is the [scalar curvature weight](Yin_C_F0267_v1.md#statement).
   In parts 2 and 3 the same bound for \(P_KE\) holds for the slice of the closed derivation on bounded inputs admitting uniformly bounded strong-star and derivation-graph approximation from \(P\).
   It bounds the entire slice on the linear span of \(n a n'\), \(n,n'\in N,\ a\in P_B\).

## Argument

### The product law in the ordered representation

For \(a\in P_B\), the conjugated scalar divided difference gives \(La(z)=\int (a(z)-a(w))/(\bar z-\bar w)\,d\mu(w)\).
The integrand is bounded off the null diagonal because \(a\) is Lipschitz on the compact spectrum.
In particular \([L,M_{\bar z}]a=\tau(a)1\).
The [two-block adjoint pairing](Yin_C_F0271_v1.md#the-finite-column-on-ordered-two-block-outputs), before any Gram bound is used, gives
\[
                     \langle Uu,Qp\rangle=\langle U L_0u,p\rangle .       \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the product rule on \(a^*n^*p\) and the vacuum identity give the term from differentiating \(p\) and the conjugated scalar slice from differentiating \(a^*\).
Bounded strong-star approximation of \(n\) by omitted polynomials gives the same equality for all \(n\in N\).
Apply (4) to \(u(1\otimes\bar z)\) at \(p=1\) and subtract (4) for \(u\) at \(p=Z\).
Since \(U(u(1\otimes\bar z))=(Uu)Z^*\), \(E1=0\) and \(EZ=1\), the left side is \(\langle Uu,1+\xi^* Z-Z\xi^*\rangle\).
The right side is \(\langle\sum n_j\tau(a_j),1\rangle\) by the scalar commutator and traciality.
This proves the first assertion for arbitrary \(N\).

For abelian \(N\), the [ordered two-block isometry](Yin_C_F0239_v1.md#the-ordered-product-space-and-disintegrated-test) identifies \(K\) with \(L^2(\gamma)\), taking \(U(n\otimes a)\) to \(n(y)a(z)\).
The omitted tuple is finite, so its joint abelian spectral realization may be taken on a compact metrizable space with its trace probability.
Testing the finite measure represented by \(v\) against separated continuous tests shows that it equals \(\lambda\), by uniqueness of finite measures and uniform density of their span.
Consequently the representative of \(v\) is \(r\), nonnegative almost everywhere, with integral one.
Its centered squared norm is \(\|v-1\|_2^2\), giving the equality in (1).
Subtracting \(c1\) from \(Z\) inside the commutator gives the bound there.
Disintegrate the equality of the two measures against a countable uniformly dense scalar test set in \(C(\operatorname{supp}\mu)\), including \(1\).
For almost every \(y\), \(r(y,\cdot)\nu_y\) then has mass one and its integrals equal those of \(\mu\) on all continuous tests, giving the conditional assertion.

### Duality against the weighted original core

Write \(t=U L_0u\), which is bounded for each test by the bounded scalar divided differences.
If \(td^{-1/2}\in L^2(M)\), then \(\langle t,p\rangle=\langle td^{-1/2},p\sqrt d\rangle\), by spectral truncation and the trace property.
Hence (2) bounds (4) by \(C\|Uu\|_2\|p\sqrt d\|_2\), proving the estimate for \(Q\) by density of the tests in \(K\).

Conversely let that estimate hold.
For each \(u\), (4) defines a functional of norm at most \(C\|Uu\|_2\) on the vectors \(p\sqrt d\), \(p\in P\).
These vectors are dense in \(L^2(M)\).
Indeed uniformly bounded strong-star approximation from \(P\) gives all \(M\sqrt d\) in their norm closure, and bounded elements times \(1_{[1/k,k]}(d)\) have the form \(m\sqrt d\) and are dense as \(k\to\infty\).
Let \(h\) be the Riesz representative with \(\langle h,p\sqrt d\rangle=\langle t,p\rangle\).
Traciality for the two Hilbert factors gives \(h\sqrt d=t\) in \(L^1(M)\), since the resulting normal functionals agree on the weakly dense starred algebra \(P\).
Multiplying on the right by the bounded inverse square root on \(1_{[1/k,k]}(d)\) identifies \(h=td^{-1/2}\) and proves the same constant in (2).

In the abelian realization both norms in (2) are integrals over \(y\) of the corresponding squared fiber norms.
For necessity test \(u=1_S(y)a(z)\) with arbitrary measurable \(S\).
This gives the fiber inequality outside a null set for each \(a\).
Use first polynomials with rational complex coefficients, then arbitrary coefficients in each finite monomial list.
The denominator converges under coefficient approximation and Fatou's lemma gives the asserted numerator inequality, since both polynomial values and their \(L\)-images converge pointwise.
This also realizes the supremum using a countable determining family, by the same density and lower semicontinuity argument for each finite proposed upper bound.
Conversely apply the simultaneous fiber inequalities pointwise to \(a=\sum n_j(y)a_j\) and integrate, giving (2).
Combining \(\|Qp\|_2\le C\|p\sqrt d\|_2\) with \(\|P_K(p\xi^*)\|_2\le\|p\xi^*\|_2\) gives the asserted weight, also after termwise summation.

### The commutator of the scalar transform with a varying Gram field

For every finite test write \(u(z)=\sum n_j a_j(z)\) in \(H_N\).
The Gram assumption and traciality give
\[
      \|Uu\|_2^2=\int\|A(z)u(z)\|^2d\mu(z),\qquad
      \|(U L_0u)d^{-1/2}\|_2^2=
         \int\frac{\|A(z)(L_0u)(z)\|^2}{d(z)}\,d\mu(z).
\]
The second identity follows first after bounded truncation of \(d^{-1/2}\); the finitely many functions \(La_j\) are bounded and measurable.
Here \(L_0u(z)\) denotes the vector of divided differences integrated against \(\mu\).

We need a scalar bound in the orientation of (2).
The core adjoint \(JT_BJ\) of \(L\) has norm at most \(\sqrt2\) from \(L^2(W\mu)\) to \(L^2(\mu)\) on polynomials by the [scalar adjoint estimate](Yin_C_F0267_v1.md#statement).
By adjoint pairing on polynomials and their density for the finite measure \(d(z)\,d\mu(z)\) with \(d=1+W\), it follows that \(\|Lf/\sqrt d\|_{2,\mu}\le\sqrt2\|f\|_{2,\mu}\) on scalar polynomials.
For Lipschitz \(f\) on the compact support the same estimate holds with \(Lf\) given by its ordinary divided-difference integral.
Indeed extend the real and imaginary parts to Lipschitz functions on the plane, mollify them, and approximate each mollification in \(C^1\) on a rectangle by real-coordinate polynomials as in the [Lipschitz extension of the scalar test](Yin_C_F0239_v1.md#a-lipschitz-density-and-the-atomic-terms).
This gives uniform approximation on the support with uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants.
Dominated convergence off the diffuse diagonal identifies the divided differences and Fatou's lemma gives the bound.
For \(H_N\)-valued Lipschitz \(f\), its divided-difference integral exists in norm and the same inequality follows by taking scalar coefficients along an orthonormal basis and using Parseval.
One may work in the separable closed span of the range, since the support is compact metrizable and \(f\) is continuous.

Apply this bound to \(f(z)=A(z)u(z)\), a Lipschitz vector function.
Subtracting the two divided differences gives
\[
     Lf(z)-A(z)(L_0u)(z)
          =\int\frac{A(z)-A(w)}{\bar z-\bar w}\,u(w)\,d\mu(w).
\]
Its pointwise norm is at most \(b\int\|u(w)\|d\mu(w)\le bD\|A u\|_{2,\mu}\).
Since \(d\ge1\), its \(L^2(d^{-1}d\mu)\) norm has the same bound.
The triangle inequality proves (2) with the stated constant.
Finally (4) passes to the indicated uniformly bounded strong-star and graph approximants, including convergence of their products with \(\sqrt d\) and \(\xi^*\) in Hilbert norm.
The [one-marginal-block domain and range](Yin_C_F0271_v1.md#the-finite-column-on-ordered-two-block-outputs) give these approximants and \(E(n a n')\in K\), proving the last assertion.
