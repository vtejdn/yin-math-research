# Canonical slices of alternating matrix products and a sharp projection beyond their bounded-length approximation

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \(X\) be a finite bounded normal generating tuple in a finite von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau)\) with faithful normal probability trace and [actual starred conjugate variables](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct).
For a distinguished coordinate \(Z\), let \(\delta\) be its derivation on the original represented starred polynomial algebra \(P\), let \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\), and write \(E_0=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta\) on \(P\subset H=L^2(M,\tau)\).
Let \(B=W^*(Z)\), let \(N\) be generated by the omitted coordinates and their adjoints, and put \(b=(2+\sqrt2)\|\mathbb E_B\xi\|_2\).
For \(r>0\) let \(E_r\) be the [normal right cutoff](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#statement) obtained by inner spectral multiplication of \(\delta\) by \(m_r(z,w)=|z-w|^2/(|z-w|^2+r^2)\) before taking its right slice.
Use \(E_0^\#,E_r^\#\) for the corresponding operators of the adjoint tuple distinguished at \(Z^*\).

Consider a finite rectangular matrix product with compatible sizes, one input column and \(q\ge1\) output rows,
\[
              p=A_0 B_1 A_1\cdots B_m A_m .
\]
Here \(A_j\) has entries in \(N\), \(B_j\) has entries in \(B\), and \(m=0\) is allowed.
Write \(d(p)\) for the product of the operator norms of the displayed rectangular factors in their natural matrix spaces.
For a finite sum \(v=\sum_\alpha p_\alpha\) of such products of the same output size, set \(K=\sum_\alpha m_\alpha d(p_\alpha)\) for this particular expression.
Let \(\|\cdot\|_{H^q}\) denote the Hilbert sum norm, and let \(\|v\|=\|\sum_i v_i^*v_i\|^{1/2}\) denote the column operator norm.
Every entry of \(v\) belongs to the intersection of the domains of the original canonical dual \(-\overline{E_0}\) and its Hilbert adjoint.
Acting entrywise, one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
   \|\overline{E_0}v\|_{H^q},\quad \|\overline{E_0^\#}v\|_{H^q}
       &\le 2bK,\\
   \sup_{r>0}\|E_r v\|_{H^q},\quad \sup_{r>0}\|E_r^\# v\|_{H^q}
       &\le 6bK,\\
   \|E_0^*v\|_{H^q},\quad \|(E_0^\#)^*v\|_{H^q}
       &\le \|\xi\|_2\|v\|+2bK .
\end{aligned}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
All constants are independent of the intermediate matrix sizes and \(q\).
If a net of these finite sums converges entrywise strongly-star to \(v\in M_{q,1}(M)\), with bounded column operator norms and expression costs at most \(K\), the same conclusions hold with the limit column \(v\) in (1).

In the free Haar pair \(X=(u,v_0)\) both canonical domains are full.
Nevertheless, on every projection layer of fixed trace \(0<t<1\), a dense \(G_\delta\) of projections admits no bounded strong-star approximation by finite sums of the displayed products at any uniformly finite cost \(K\).
For \(t=1/2\) this includes the spectral projection of \(u v_0\) onto the open upper semicircle.
Thus even for this pair, (1) alone cannot establish the [nonmeagre cutoff test](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#statement) by exhibiting such factorizations on a nonmeagre set of sharp projections.

## Argument

### The bridge from a marginal factor to the rest of the word

Use \(D\) for the closed scalar right slice of the marginal \(Z\), restricted to \(B\).
The marginal vacuum is \(\xi_B=\mathbb E_B\xi\) by restriction of the double-trace identity, and the scalar derivative is its [forced divided difference](Yin_C_F0076_v1.md#statement).
The [scalar weight estimate](Yin_C_F0267_v1.md#statement) supplies a positive integrable \(W\) of integral \(\|\xi_B\|_2^2\) with \(\|Da\|_2\le(2+\sqrt2)\|a\sqrt W\|_2\) on marginal polynomials.
The bound extends strongly along bounded strong-star approximations by such polynomials, since bounded convergence in measure implies convergence in \(L^2(W\,d\mu)\) for the marginal law \(\mu\).
Completeness and closedness of \(D\) identify the limit with its actual value on every \(a\in B\).
Consequently every finite column \(h\) over \(B\) obeys \(\|Dh\|_{H^q}\le b\|h\|\), by summing the weighted squares.
For the marginal of \(Z^*\) the scalar vacuum has the same norm by [adjoint conjugation](Yin_C_F0073_v1.md#statement), so the same column constant applies.

Write \(D_r\) for the marginal cutoff.
The [separated multiplier and Gaussian average](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#removing-the-cutoff-in-the-column-norm) applied in the scalar marginal bounds the column norm of \(D_r\) by three times that of \(D\), uniformly in \(r\).
This extends to all bounded marginal columns by matrix Kaplansky approximation and weak lower semicontinuity, using the normal cutoff at fixed \(r\).
Equivalently, \(D_r\) has scalar kernel \((f(z)-f(w))\overline{(z-w)}/(|z-w|^2+r^2)\) under the integral in \(w\).
It is Hilbert-space bounded for each fixed \(r\) since its integral kernel coefficient is bounded.

Take an individual matrix product with polynomial entries in the factors, and factor it as \(L B_j R\) at the \(j\)-th marginal occurrence, keeping all earlier and later factors in \(L\) and \(R\).
Then \(R\) is a column and \(h=\mathbb E_B(R)\) is a column of the same size with \(\|h\|\le\|R\|\).
Indeed \(\sum_k|\mathbb E_B R_k|^2\le \mathbb E_B(\sum_kR_k^*R_k)\) by the conditional expectation Schwarz inequality.
The contribution of this occurrence to \(E_0p\) is exactly
\[
                         L\,\{D(B_j h)-B_jD(h)\}.                       \tag{2}
\]
For a marginal polynomial \(f\), multiplying its divided-difference tensor on the outer right by an entry \(y\) of \(R\) and slicing replaces \(y\) by \(\mathbb E_B y\), by tracial conditional expectation in the second leg.
For \(h'\) a marginal polynomial the resulting expression is \(D(f h')-fD(h')\), by the scalar Leibniz rule.
For bounded \(h'\in B\) use bounded strong-star polynomial approximants.
Both \(D\)-terms converge by the preceding weighted estimate, and the integral against the bounded divided difference of \(f\) converges in \(L^2(\mu)\) by Cauchy–Schwarz.
Summing over the matched matrix indices gives (2).
The omitted factors have zero derivative by the forced starred values.
With \(E_r\) in place of \(E_0\), (2) holds with \(D_r\) in place of \(D\), since \(m_r\) multiplies each scalar divided difference at this same occurrence.

Left multiplication by a rectangular matrix \(L\) on the corresponding Hilbert columns has norm at most \(\|L\|\).
The two terms in braces in (2) therefore cost together at most \(2b\|L\|\|B_j\|\|R\|\) after multiplying by \(L\).
The cutoff costs at most \(6b\|L\|\|B_j\|\|R\|\) there.
Since \(\|L\|\|B_j\|\|R\|\le d(p)\), summing over the occurrences and then over the products gives the first two estimates of (1) on these polynomial expressions.
At \(m=0\) both right slices are zero.
In the adjoint tuple \(B\) and \(N\) are the same algebras, and the proof with the scalar slice of \(Z^*\) gives the two other right slice bounds.

### Closure and the Hilbert adjoint on measurable products

The [actual adjoint on the original core](Yin_C_F0271_v1.md#exact-energy-and-affine-line-rigidity), with \(\delta^\#(a)=(\delta(a^*))^\dagger\) for the flip-adjoint \(\dagger\), gives \(E_0^*p=p\xi-E_0^\#p\).
Indeed \(E_0^\#p=((\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(p^*))^*\).
The corresponding identity for \((E_0^\#)^*\) uses the vacuum \(\xi^*\) and \(E_0\).
For any bounded column \(p\), left multiplication of a vector \(\zeta\) entrywise gives \(\|p\zeta\|_{H^q}\le\|p\|\|\zeta\|_2\).
These facts give the adjoint estimates on polynomial products and sums.

For factors with bounded measurable entries, approximate the finitely many rectangular factors strongly-star by matrices of marginal or omitted starred polynomials, with limsup operator norms bounded by their respective norms, using [Kaplansky density](../audits/literature/AP18/Yin_U001.md#ap18-u001) in matrix corners and polynomial norm approximation.
The products and their finite sums converge entrywise strongly-star, since all factors stay bounded.
Their right slices in both orientations have weak cluster points by the bounds already proved.
The weak closure of the two Hilbert graphs gives membership and the asserted right slice bounds at the limit.
Every cluster point has its unique graph value, hence both image nets converge weakly.
Left multiplication of \(\xi\) by the approximating column converges strongly, by uniformly bounded strong convergence, also with \(\xi^*\) in place of \(\xi\).
The two core adjoint identities pass to the limit by weak graph closure, giving the identities and adjoint bounds with the actual norm of the limiting column.
At fixed \(r>0\) normality of the two cutoff maps gives weak convergence of their images and preserves the estimates.
All these weak graph and normality arguments apply a second time to any bounded net of finite sums of uniformly bounded cost.
This proves (1) and the stated closure assertion.

### Sharp projections in the free Haar pair

Realize \(u,v_0\) as the two group unitaries in \(M=L(\mathbb F_2)\) with its group trace.
Their scalar Haar slices send \(z^k\) to \(z^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge1\) and to zero otherwise, so they are Hilbert-space bounded with actual scalar vacua.
The [free-product vacuum and slice formula](Yin_C_F0208_v1.md#vacuum-preservation-and-the-right-slice-on-reduced-words) gives the joint vacua and the [free normal theorem](Yin_C_F0162_v1.md#statement) gives both full canonical domains.
For \(g\in\mathbb F_2\), let \(\ell(g)\) count the maximal nonzero powers of the individual generators in its reduced word, with \(\ell(1)=0\).
On the orthonormal group basis take the closed diagonal operator
\[
                    T\lambda_g=(\ell(g)-1)_+\lambda_g
\]
with its maximal weighted square-summable domain in \(H\).
For each scalar output product with \(m\) marginal factors the Fourier support lies in \(\{\ell\le 2m+1\}\), for all rectangular factor sizes.
For Laurent-polynomial entries this follows by expanding the finitely many matrix indices, since multiplication of \(2m+1\) single-generator powers cannot yield more than \(2m+1\) syllables.
Bounded strong-star matrix approximations of measurable entries by Laurent polynomials give Hilbert-norm convergence of the products, and the indicated Fourier subspace is closed.
Consequently \(\|Tp\|_2\le 2m\|p\|_2\le2m d(p)\), also for omitted-only products.
Finite sums of cost at most \(K\) obey \(\|Tv\|_2\le2K\), and every \(L^2\) limit of such sums lies in the domain with the same bound by weak closedness of the graph.

Set \(w=uv_0\).
The nonidentity powers of \(w\) have zero trace, so it is Haar and \(e=1_{\{e^{i\theta}:0<\theta<\pi\}}(w)\) has trace \(1/2\).
The Fourier coefficients of the semicircle indicator have modulus \(1/(\pi |k|)\) at odd \(k\), by integration over \((0,\pi)\).
Because \(\ell(w^k)=2|k|\), one obtains
\[
                 \sum_{k\ {\rm odd}}
                  \frac{(2|k|-1)^2}{\pi^2 k^2}=\infty .
\]
Thus \(e\) lies outside \(\operatorname{Dom}T\).
The [fixed-trace domain dichotomy](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#statement) applies to \(T\) on this diffuse finite algebra, since \(T1=0\) and a bounded vector outside its domain was just exhibited.
On every trace layer the complement of its domain is a dense \(G_\delta\).
Every projection in that complement lies outside all the uniformly finite cost approximations above, even when only \(L^2\) convergence of the approximating sums is required.
