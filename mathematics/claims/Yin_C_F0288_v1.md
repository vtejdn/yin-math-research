# Dense polynomial spectral cuts of prescribed trace in the canonical domains

## Current assessment

[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\), \(d\ge2\), be bounded normal generators of a finite tracial von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau)\) with [actual starred conjugate variables](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct).
Write \(P=\mathbb C\langle X,X^*\rangle\), and fix a coordinate \(i\) and \(0<t<1\).
Use the two derivations \(D=\delta_i,\delta_i^\#\), where \(\delta_i^\# x=(\delta_i(x^*))^\dagger\) and \(\dagger\) flips and takes adjoints on the two legs.
For each \(D\) let \(E_D=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)D\) on \(P\), and let \(E_{D,r}\) be its [normal cutoff](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#canonical-cutoffs-on-the-original-algebra) of radius \(r>0\).
Give \(\mathcal P_t=\{e=e^*=e^2:\tau(e)=t\}\) its \(L^2\) metric.

There exists a selfadjoint polynomial \(h\) of degree at most one in an omitted coordinate and its adjoint with \(\ker h=0\) and \(\tau(1_{(-\infty,0)}(h))=t\).
Fix any such \(h\).
For every \(a\in P\) invertible in \(M\), define
\[
       p_a=aha^*,\qquad e_a=1_{(-\infty,0)}(p_a).
\]
These projections all have trace \(t\), lie in both closed coarse derivation domains and both forward and adjoint slice domains, and satisfy
\[
     E_{D,r}e_a\longrightarrow\overline E_D e_a\quad\text{in }L^2
          \quad (r\downarrow0),\qquad
     \|\overline D e_a\|_2\le
          \|a^{-1}\|\big(\|Da\|_2+\|D(a^*)\|_2\big).                     \tag{1}
\]
The threshold needs no small-ball estimate.
The set of these \(e_a\) is dense in \(\mathcal P_t\) even if \(a\) ranges only over polynomials with coefficients in \(\mathbb Q+i\mathbb Q\) satisfying \(\|a\|,\|a^{-1}\|\le2\).
Denote this countable family by \(Q\).
For either \(D\), put \(b_D=\sup_{e\in Q}\|\overline E_D e\|_2\) and \(a_D=\sup_{x\in P,\ \|x\|\le1}\|E_Dx\|_2\).
With \(m=\min(t,1-t)\) one has in the extended reals
\[
   b_D=\sup_{e\in\mathcal P_t}\|\overline E_D e\|_2,\qquad
   b_D\le a_D\le4\max\{(1-m)^{-1},(2m)^{-1}\}\,b_D,                     \tag{2}
\]
where the cost outside the domain is infinite.
If \(a_D=\infty\), the values \(\|\overline E_D e\|_2\) for \(e\in Q\) are unbounded in every nonempty relatively open subset of \(\mathcal P_t\).
Thus uniform bounds for these explicit limiting graph values in both orientations give the full canonical dual and adjoint domains at the distinguished coordinate.
For each of the two coarse derivatives \(D\), the unsliced values \(\|\overline D e\|_2\), \(e\in Q\), are unbounded in every nonempty relatively open subset of \(\mathcal P_t\), for every actual tuple in the statement.
In particular (2) cannot be obtained by uniformly bounding those coarse norms, even on a single patch.

In any diffuse finite tracial algebra generated by a finite tuple, the set of **all** strict lower spectral cuts of represented selfadjoint starred polynomials lying in \(\mathcal P_t\) is meagre there.
For nonscalar polynomials this still holds when any subprojection of the threshold eigenspace may be included.
In particular the original mixed polynomial cuts are dense and meagre in each trace layer of the normal tuples above, even though the dense countable family \(Q\) already consists of domain vectors for the selected coordinate.

## Argument

### The cuts and their coarse derivatives

The [normal starred symbols](Yin_C_F0076_v1.md#statement) give diffuse marginal laws, closability of the two derivatives and their vanishing on all omitted generators and adjoints.
For an omitted \(X_j\) of law \(\mu\), consider \(z\mapsto\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\theta}z)\).
For each distinct \(z,w\) equality of their two images holds for only two angles modulo \(2\pi\).
The diagonal has zero \(\mu^2\) mass, so Fubini gives zero probability of equality for two independent draws for almost every angle.
At one such angle the image law is atom-free.
Its distribution function is continuous and attains \(t\), so subtracting a corresponding real threshold gives the required \(h\).

Write \(p=aha^*\), \(e=1_{(-\infty,0)}(h)\) and \(q=1_{(-\infty,0)}(p)\).
Invertibility of \(a\) and injectivity of \(h\) give \(\ker p=0\).
The closed range of \((a^*)^{-1}e\) has projection \(f\) equivalent to \(e\) by polar decomposition.
The form of \(p\) is strictly negative on each nonzero vector in this range, since the form of \(h\) is strictly negative on \(eH\), where \(H=L^2(M,\tau)\).
Consequently \(f\wedge(1-q)=0\), whence \(\tau(f)\le\tau(q)\) by the [projection dimension inequality](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#a-bounded-patch-in-a-fixed-trace-layer).
Applying the same argument to \(h=a^{-1}p(a^*)^{-1}\) gives the reverse inequality.

Use the outer left and right actions \(L_p,R_p\) on the coarse correspondence \(H\otimes_2 H\).
For either \(D\) the product rule and \(Dh=0\) give
\[
      Dp=A\cdot p+p\cdot B,\qquad A=(Da)\cdot a^{-1},\quad
                     B=(a^*)^{-1}\cdot D(a^*).
\]
For a real \(C^1\) function \(f\) on an interval containing \(\sigma(p)\), the derivative of \(f(p)\) is \(f^{[1]}(L_p,R_p)Dp\), with the derivative on the diagonal of the divided difference.
Indeed this follows first by the product rule for polynomials, and uniform polynomial approximation of \(f'\) followed by integration gives polynomial approximants in \(C^1\).
Their values converge in norm and their divided differences converge uniformly, giving convergence on the original coarse graphs in both orientations.

Take \(f_\epsilon(x)=1/2-\pi^{-1}\arctan(x/\epsilon)\).
Both \(v f_\epsilon^{[1]}(u,v)\) and \(u f_\epsilon^{[1]}(u,v)\) are bounded by an absolute constant independent of \(\epsilon\).
If \(|u-v|\ge\max(|u|,|v|)/2\), use \(0\le f_\epsilon\le1\).
Otherwise \(u,v\) have the same sign and all intermediate \(x\) satisfy \(|x|\ge\max(|u|,|v|)/2\), so use \(|x f_\epsilon'(x)|\le 1/(2\pi)\).
Off the two axes the two multipliers converge to \(v\chi^{[1]}(u,v)\) and \(u\chi^{[1]}(u,v)\), respectively, for \(\chi=1_{(-\infty,0)}\), assigning zero on the nonzero diagonal.
The limiting multipliers vanish at same-sign pairs and have modulus at most one at opposite-sign pairs.
The joint spectral measure of \(L_p,R_p\) gives the axes zero projection since \(p\) has zero kernel.
Bounded convergence in joint spectral calculus on \(A,B\) therefore gives the actual coarse graph value
\[
       \overline D q=(v\chi^{[1]})(L_p,R_p)A+(u\chi^{[1]})(L_p,R_p)B.     \tag{3}
\]
Here \(f_\epsilon(p)\to q\) strongly and in \(L^2\) by scalar spectral calculus.
This proves the norm estimate (1) and, using the \(C^1\) approximants at successive \(\epsilon\), supplies one uniformly bounded selfadjoint polynomial sequence converging strongly to \(q\) with convergence in both coarse graphs.

Slicing these graph approximants gives the two closed forward values.
The [original polynomial adjoint identities](Yin_C_F0271_v1.md#exact-energy-and-affine-line-rigidity) express the two adjoint values as \(x\xi-E_{\delta_i^\#}x\) and \(x\xi^*-E_{\delta_i}x\) on those approximants, where \(\xi=\delta_i^*(1\otimes1)\).
The bounded strong convergence gives convergence of both vacuum products, hence membership in both actual adjoint domains.
For fixed \(r>0\) the cutoff is the right slice of the inner spectral multiplier \(g_r(z,w)=|z-w|^2/(|z-w|^2+r^2)\) applied to \(D\).
Its bounded normal extension on \(M\) identifies the value on \(q\) by weak convergence along the same approximants, while the coarse graph convergence gives the norm limit \((\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)I_{g_r}\overline Dq\).
The inner spectral variables have zero diagonal projection by diffuseness of \(X_i\): in the coarse correspondence the two leg spectral measures are absolutely continuous with respect to the marginal on each leg, and this assertion on simple tensors extends by density.
Thus \(I_{g_r}\to1\) strongly by bounded spectral convergence, proving the cutoff limit in (1).

### Exact trace, density and the norm test

The two-coordinate [relative commutant theorem](Yin_C_F0202_v1.md#statement) makes \(M\) a factor.
For any \(q\in\mathcal P_t\), both \(q,e\) and their complements have equal traces.
In a factor with faithful tracial state equal-trace projections are equivalent, by Anantharaman–Popa, Corollary 2.4.11, printed p. 39 (PDF p. 45), whose statement and hypotheses apply here. [Inspected author draft](../literature/pdf/AP18.pdf)
Adding the two partial isometries gives a unitary \(u\in M\) with \(ueu^*=q\).
Write \(u=\exp(ik)\) for a bounded selfadjoint \(k\) by Borel functional calculus.
Choose uniformly bounded selfadjoint \(k_n\in P\) converging strongly to \(k\) using [Kaplansky density](../audits/literature/AP18/Yin_U001.md#ap18-u001), taking a sequence by evaluation on the trace vector and bounded \(L^2\)-to-strong convergence in the tracial representation.
Their exponentials converge strongly-star to \(u\) by uniform polynomial approximation on a common compact interval.
Each exponential can be approximated in operator norm to error less than \(1/(n+2)\) by a starred polynomial \(a_n\) with rational complex coefficients, by polynomial approximation of the exponential and then of the finitely many coefficients.
Thus \(a_n\) is invertible, \(\|a_n\|,\|a_n^{-1}\|\le2\), and \(p_{a_n}\to uhu^*\) strongly with a common norm bound.
These inputs are selfadjoint and the limit has no kernel.
To see convergence of the cuts on a vector, bound the squared error in replacing the step by a continuous ramp of width \(\eta\) by the vector expectation of a continuous cutoff equal to one on \([-\eta,\eta]\) and zero outside \([-2\eta,2\eta]\).
Continuous functional calculus converges strongly at fixed \(\eta\), and the cutoff on the limit tends strongly to zero as \(\eta\downarrow0\).
Consequently \(e_{a_n}\to q\) strongly and in \(L^2\), proving density with the same \(h\) at every target \(q\).

For the closed forward operator, the sublevel \(\{q\in\mathcal P_t:\|\overline E_Dq\|_2\le K\}\) is closed by weak compactness of the output ball and weak graph closure.
If \(b_D<\infty\), this sublevel for \(K=b_D\) contains the dense set \(Q\) and hence all of \(\mathcal P_t\).
If \(b_D=\infty\), the layer supremum is already infinite.
When \(a_D<\infty\), bounded strong approximation of each contraction by core contractions and weak graph closure give \(\|\overline E_D q\|_2\le a_D\) on the layer.
Conversely the [fixed-trace layer estimate](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#statement) applies to \(\overline E_D\) with \(\overline E_D1=0\) and bounds \(a_D\) as in (2).
A uniform bound on \(Q\) in one relative open patch would give that same bound on the patch by density and closedness, and the bounded-patch argument of that theorem would force \(a_D<\infty\).
Both orientations together give the asserted canonical domain conclusion by [normal domain comparison](Yin_C_F0075_v1.md#statement).

### The unsliced norms on each patch

Suppose for one orientation that the coarse graph values on \(Q\) have a common bound on a nonempty relatively open patch.
The weakly closed graph of \(\overline D\) and density of \(Q\) give this bound on the entire patch.
Apply the [bounded-patch theorem](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#a-bounded-patch-in-a-fixed-trace-layer) with target \(H\otimes_2H\) and \(\overline D1=0\).
It gives \(\|\overline D x\|_2\le C\|x\|\) on \(M\) for some \(C<\infty\).

Let \(\mu\) be the compactly supported diffuse law of \(Z=X_i\).
On marginal starred polynomials the generator symbols and product rule give the divided difference with denominator \(z-w\) for \(\delta_i\) and \(\bar z-\bar w\) for \(\delta_i^\#\).
Their squared coarse norms therefore have the same formula.
For any scalar Borel sign function \(f\) on the marginal, Kaplansky density in \(W^*(Z)\) gives marginal polynomial contractions converging to \(f\) in \(L^2(\mu)\), hence almost everywhere along a subsequence.
Fatou's lemma in the marginal product law and the putative bound give
\[
    \iint_{z\ne w}\frac{|f(z)-f(w)|^2}{|z-w|^2}\,d\mu(z)d\mu(w)\le C^2.
\]
Assign independent symmetric signs to the cells of a finite dyadic square partition of a box containing the support, and use its resulting sign function.
The expected squared difference equals two on pairs from distinct cells and zero on pairs from the same cell.
Averaging the inequality and refining these partitions, monotone convergence gives \(2\iint_{z\ne w}|z-w|^{-2}\,d\mu(z)d\mu(w)\le C^2\).
The [critical planar energy calculation](Yin_C_F0204_v1.md#the-diagonal-value-of-the-differentiated-central-relation) makes this integral infinite for every nonzero compactly supported diffuse planar measure.
This excludes the assumed coarse bound at either orientation.

### Why polynomial cuts form a meagre set

Work now in any diffuse finite tracial \(M\).
For a finite-dimensional real space \(V\) of centered selfadjoint elements, let \(S\) be its \(L^2\) unit sphere and put \(\beta(v)=\sup_{q\in\mathcal P_t}\tau(vq)\).
Let \(K_V\) consist of \(q\in\mathcal P_t\) attaining \(\beta(v)\) for some \(v\in S\).
The zero-dimensional space gives the empty set.
Otherwise \(K_V\) is closed by compactness of \(S\) and continuity of \(\beta\), which is Lipschitz in \(L^2\).
For each \(v\in S\) take a projection of trace \(t\) on the largest spectral values of \(v\), splitting a threshold eigenspace if needed by [diffuseness of corners](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#a-bounded-patch-in-a-fixed-trace-layer).
The average of \(v\) on this projection exceeds its average on the complement: the two laws lie on opposite sides of the threshold, and equality of their means forces both laws to be concentrated at that threshold, contrary to \(v\) being nonscalar.
Since \(\tau(v)=0\), this gives \(\beta(v)>0\) and hence \(\min_{v\in S}\beta(v)>0\).

Fix \(e_0\in\mathcal P_t\) and \(0<\lambda<1\).
In a diffuse unital abelian algebra in each of \(e_0Me_0,(1-e_0)M(1-e_0)\) choose sequences of projections converging ultraweakly to \(\lambda(1-t)e_0\) and \(\lambda t(1-e_0)\), respectively, with exactly the traces of these limits at every index.
Indeed each corner contains a copy of the Lebesgue probability algebra by dyadic splitting.
To approximate a constant fraction \(\alpha\) on \([0,1]\), choose the initial \(\alpha\) fraction of each dyadic interval at level \(n\).
These indicators pair eventually with each dyadic step function as \(\alpha\) does, which proves convergence against all \(L^1\) tests and hence against restrictions of normal functionals on \(M\).
Subtract the first projections from \(e_0\) and add the second projections.
The resulting \(q_n\) have trace \(t\), satisfy \(\|q_n-e_0\|_2^2=2\lambda t(1-t)\) and converge ultraweakly to \(x=(1-\lambda)e_0+\lambda t1\).
For every \(v\in S\),
\[
        \beta(v)-\tau(vx)\ \ge\ \lambda\beta(v).
\]
The pairings against \(q_n\) converge uniformly on the compact sphere \(S\), using the \(L^2\) Lipschitz bound for these pairings.
Thus all sufficiently large \(q_n\) are outside \(K_V\).
Choosing \(\lambda\) arbitrarily small proves that the closed set \(K_V\) has empty interior.

For a nonscalar selfadjoint polynomial \(y\) of degree at most \(n\), take \(V\) to be the centered selfadjoint represented polynomial space of degree at most \(n\).
Every strict lower cut of \(y\) enlarged by any subprojection of its threshold eigenspace, if of trace \(t\), maximizes the pairing for the centered and normalized \(-y\).
Indeed for threshold \(s\) and the resulting cut \(q\) one has \(\tau((s-y)q)=\tau((s-y)_+)\ge\tau((s-y)q')\) for every \(q'\in\mathcal P_t\).
These cuts all belong to \(K_V\).
A scalar polynomial has no strict lower cut of trace \(0<t<1\).
There are countably many degree bounds, so the asserted families are meagre.
Their dense subfamily \(Q\) in the tuple application has finite cost at each member by (1), while (2) still requires a uniform bound.
