# Testing a deficient slice in one marginal neighborhood

## Current assessment
[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \(X\) be a finite bounded normal generating tuple with [actual starred conjugate variables](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct), of length \(d\ge2\).
Fix a coordinate \(Z\) with law \(\mu\), its right slice \(E=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta\) on the original starred polynomial algebra \(P\), its vacuum \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\), and any positive sequence \(r_j\to0\).
Write \(T_j\) for the original normal cutoff with inner multiplier \(m_j(z,w)=|z-w|^2/(|z-w|^2+r_j^2)\).
Set \(B=W^*(Z)\), let \(N\) be the algebra generated by all omitted coordinates, and put \(e_U=1_U(Z)\) for a relative open neighborhood in the marginal spectrum.
The local quantity in this statement tests only the compression of both input and output to the same corner:
\[
 k(U)=\sup_j\ \sup_{\|y\|\le1,\ y=e_U y e_U}\|e_U T_j(y)e_U\|_2.
\]

If \(d\ge3\), the slice is bounded on core contractions exactly when every marginal spectral point has a neighborhood \(U\) with \(k(U)<\infty\).
If the slice is unbounded, there is a **single** spectral point such that in every neighborhood of that point one finds orthogonal projections in disjoint marginal annuli having both oriented graph and cutoff properties of the [two-point obstruction](Yin_C_F0295_v1.md#statement): every finite subsum belongs to the domains and every infinite subsum misses the deficient forward domain and has unbounded cutoffs along the prescribed sequence.
All assertions also apply to the other orientation, with its own spectral point.

Here is a weighted alternative for a pair.
Set \(C=N'\cap M,\ A=Z(C)\) (the center of \(C\)), \(\eta=[Z^*,\xi]\), \(w=1-\eta\), and \(h=\mathbb E_A(ww^*)\).
Then \(h\ge1\) and \(\tau(h)=1+\|\eta\|_2^2\).
If every point has a neighborhood with \(k(U)<\infty\), then
\[
 \sup_j\|h^{-1/2}T_j\|_{M\to L^2}<\infty,\qquad
 \sup_j\|T_j\|_{M\to L^1}<\infty .                                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular the one-point equivalence and projection conclusion also hold for a pair if \(h\) is bounded, for instance if the designated joint conjugate variable is bounded.
Under the local finiteness premise, an unbounded slice for a pair must retain unbounded cutoff norms even on inputs with left support in \(1_{(L,\infty)}(h)\), for every finite \(L\).
The opposite orientation uses its own \(h\).

For the universal implication on finite tuples it suffices to prove the local boundedness assertion at every coordinate and both orientations of all tuples of length at least three.
This reduction allows adjoining free Haar coordinates while testing the original question.

## Argument

### Omitted multipliers and a lower bound for the averaged spectral projection

Write \(\mathbb E_D\) for trace-preserving conditional expectation onto \(D\), also acting on the tracial \(L^1\) space.
Use \(G=\mathcal U(N)\) for \(d\ge3\), with commutant in \(M\) equal to \(\mathbb C1\) by the [two-coordinate commutant theorem](Yin_C_F0202_v1.md#statement).
For \(d=2\) use \(G=\mathcal U(C)\).
Its commutant in \(M\) is \(A\): since \(N\) is abelian it is contained in \(C\), so an element commuting with \(C\) belongs to \(C\) and is central there.
For either group every \(T_j\) is left \(G\)-modular, meaning \(T_j(ux)=uT_j(x)\) for \(u\in G,\ x\in M\).
For omitted multipliers this follows first on the two polynomial cores since both starred derivatives vanish at the omitted letters, then by the separate normality of multiplication and of the fixed-radius slice.
Normality and core convergence are supplied by the [cutoff theorem](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#statement).

For a pair, choose a Haar unitary \(v\in N\) by marginal diffuseness.
Every bounded \(c\in C\) has bounded polynomial approximants \(c_l\to c\) strongly-star with \(\delta c_l\to0\) in the coarse Hilbert space.
Here are the details of the bounded approximation, using the [diffuse commutant argument](Yin_C_F0202_v1.md#the-diffuse-coordinate-and-the-graph-of-the-derivation).
Start with bounded polynomial approximants to \(c\) using [Kaplansky density](../audits/literature/AP18/Yin_U001.md#ap18-u001).
For each approximant average its conjugates by the first \(m\) powers of \(v\).
Their derivatives are the corresponding conjugates in the outer coarse correspondence, in which the conjugation by \(v\) has no fixed vectors: equal-length spectral partitions for \(v\) give diagonal coarse projections tending strongly to zero, by the same diffuse commutant argument.
The Cesaro averages of those derivatives tend to zero by telescoping on the dense range of \(1-\operatorname{Ad}v\).
The averaged inputs stay bounded and their \(L^2\) distances to \(c\) do not increase.
For each fixed finite average replace the omitted powers and their adjoints by bounded omitted polynomial approximants converging strongly-star.
Their derivatives vanish and their outer multipliers converge strongly on the coarse correspondence, so the resulting polynomials have the required simultaneous input and derivative limits.
The bounded \(L^2\) convergence also gives strong-star convergence in the tracial representation.
For \(x\in P\), the extra product-rule term in \(T_j(c_lx)\) has norm at most \(\|\delta c_l\|_2\|x\|\).
Normality and a second bounded approximation in \(x\) now give \(T_j(cx)=cT_j(x)\) on \(M\).

In the pair case we also need
\[
               \mathbb E_A(e)\ \ge\ \tau(e)^2 h^{-1}
                          \qquad(e\text{ a projection in }B).              \tag{2}
\]
Let \(\gamma=I_{z-w}\delta\) be partial liberation, where \(I\) acts in the two inner marginal legs.
Its vacuum is \(\eta\) by the [liberation identity](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#recovering-the-divided-difference-on-the-represented-core).
On marginal polynomials \(\gamma b=b\otimes1-1\otimes b\).
The preceding bounded approximants for \(c\in C\) also have \(\gamma c_l\to0\), since \(I_{z-w}\) is bounded.
Test the product rule at \(c_l b\) against the vacuum and pass to the limit to obtain \(\langle w,cb\rangle=\tau(c)\tau(b)\).
The formula extends to any \(b\in B\) by bounded strong-star approximation in that marginal and tracial continuity.
Taking \(c\in A\) identifies \(\mathbb E_A(e w^*)=\tau(e)1\), and in particular \(\mathbb E_A(w^*)=1\).
Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz on the abelian algebra \(A\) gives \(\tau(e)^2\le\mathbb E_A(e)\,h\) and \(h\ge1\).
For clarity, the matrix of \(L^1(A)\) entries obtained by applying \(\mathbb E_A\) to the products \(v_i v_j^*\), \(v_1=e,v_2=w\), is positive pointwise: integrate each scalar rational complex quadratic form against positive indicators, then use density in \(\mathbb C^2\).
The formula for \(\tau(h)\) follows from \(\langle\eta,1\rangle=0\).

### A bound from the compressed tests

Write \(B_0=\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\gamma\|_{\infty\to2}\) on the core, finite by the [liberation bound](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#removing-the-cutoff-in-the-column-norm), and \(L_0=1+3B_0\).
For a relative ball \(U=B(\lambda,2s)\cap\sigma(Z)\) of positive mass put \(F=e_U,\ b=e_{B(\lambda,s)}\).
With \(D=G'\cap M\) and \(H_F=\mathbb E_D F\), we claim that for \(x=xF,\ \|x\|\le1\),
\[
        \|H_F^{1/2} T_j(x)b\|_2 \ \le\ k(U)+6b_0,\qquad
        b_0=(2+\sqrt2)\|\mathbb E_B\xi\|_2 .                             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the [marginal bridge and scalar cutoff bounds](Yin_C_F0284_v1.md#the-bridge-from-a-marginal-factor-to-the-rest-of-the-word) give, for any marginal contraction \(f\) and \(x\in M\),
\[
 T_j(fx)-fT_jx=S_j(f\,\mathbb E_Bx)-f S_j(\mathbb E_Bx),\qquad
 \|S_j\|_{B\to L^2(B)}\le3b_0 ,
\]
where \(S_j\) is the scalar marginal cutoff.
For core polynomials this is the outer product rule followed by tracing the second leg of the marginal derivative.
Both sides extend in each argument by normality at fixed radius, including for \(f=F\); the scalar cutoff agrees with the restriction of \(T_j\) by the same marginal derivative formula.
Applying this identity at \(F,ux\), compressing the output on the left by \(F\) and on the right by \(b\le F\), and using \(Fux\in FMF\), gives \(\|FuT_j(x)b\|_2\le k(U)+6b_0\) for every \(u\in G\).
The \(L^2\)-closed convex hull of \(u^*Fu\) contains \(H_F\): its minimum-norm vector is invariant and has the same projection on the invariant subspace \(L^2(D)\) as \(F\), as in the [omitted averaging proof](Yin_C_F0276_v1.md#averaging-over-the-omitted-algebra).
Convex averages stay in the operator unit ball, so their \(L^2\) approximation gives convergence against the integrable density of \(T_j(x)b\) by bounded truncation.
Averaging the preceding squared inequality proves (3).

For arbitrary input discard \(x(1-F)\) before applying (3).
The [right off-support bound](Yin_C_F0295_v1.md#localization-before-taking-graph-limits) gives \(\|T_j(x(1-F))b\|_2\le L_0\|x\|/s\).
For \(d\ge3\), \(H_F=\tau(F)1\); for pairs use (2).
Writing \(W=1\) in the first case and \(W=h^{-1/2}\) in the second, we have consequently
\[
 \|WT_j(x)b\|_2\le
 \big((k(U)+6b_0)/m_F+L_0/s\big)\|x\|,
 \qquad
 m_F=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\tau(F)}& d\ge3,\\ \tau(F)& d=2.\end{cases}     \tag{4}
\]
A neighborhood with finite local cost contains a doubled ball of finite cost about its center by monotonicity of corner compression.
Compactness then supplies finitely many smaller balls covering the spectrum.
Refine them to disjoint measurable sets, multiply each bound (4) on the right by the projection of its assigned set, and sum squares using orthogonality of right supports.
This proves the uniform weighted \(L^2\) estimate.
For a pair, tracial Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\|T_jx\|_1\le\|h^{1/2}\|_2\|h^{-1/2}T_jx\|_2\), which proves (1).
With \(q=1_{[1,L]}(h)\), \(q\in A\subset C\) and left modularity gives \(T_j(qx)=qT_jx\); these maps are uniformly bounded in \(L^2\) by \(\sqrt L\) times the weighted bound for \(L\ge1\).
Thus if the total norms are unbounded, the complementary left inputs still have unbounded norms, including trivially when \(L<1\).

### The one-point obstruction

When \(W^{-1}\) is bounded, local finiteness everywhere therefore gives a uniform unweighted cutoff bound and hence boundedness of \(E\) on the core by convergence.
Conversely finite core norm bounds every cutoff by three times that norm by the [single-input cutoff estimate](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#statement).
A failure must give a point with \(k(U)=\infty\) for every neighborhood.
In particular the uncompressed norms on the unit ball of every such \(e_U M e_U\) are unbounded.
Use the [punctured-neighborhood and subsum construction](Yin_C_F0295_v1.md#recovering-large-actual-graph-values-in-punctured-neighborhoods) with just that one point.
Its input is precisely unbounded cutoff norms on those corners and absence of atoms at the points removed: the smooth approximations, large good spectral cuts and shrinking-tail normal functionals in that proof therefore supply the claimed sequence.

Finally one coordinate alone satisfies the scalar bound in both orientations.
Any two-coordinate failure has an infinite original single-input cost in at least one orientation by the [normal comparison](Yin_C_F0075_v1.md#statement).
Freely adjoining a Haar unitary preserves actuality and the old slices on old polynomials by [free extension](Yin_C_F0278_v1.md#statement), so the enlarged three-coordinate tuple still has this failure.
This justifies the stated reduction without imposing a hypothesis on the relative commutant of the original pair.
