# The relative conjugate variable and the canonical slice in a spectral corner

## Current assessment
[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let a finite tuple of bounded normal generators in a finite von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau)\) with faithful normal probability trace satisfy the [actual starred conjugate-variable condition](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#three-hypotheses-that-must-remain-distinct).
Fix a coordinate \(Z\), its marginal law \(\mu\), \(B=W^*(Z)\), its distinguished derivation \(\delta\), and its vacuum \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\).
Put \(\xi_B=\mathbb E_B\xi\), \(\eta=[Z^*,\xi]\), \(u=z-w\) and \(k_r(z,w)=\bar u/(|u|^2+r^2)\).
Here \(I_g\) acts on the inner two marginal legs of the coarse correspondence, \(C=\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau\), \(\gamma=I_u\delta\) is partial liberation, and \(T_r=CI_{k_r}\gamma\) is the [normal fixed-radius slice](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#canonical-cutoffs-on-the-original-algebra) on \(M\).

Take any Borel set \(F\) with \(e=1_F(Z)\) of trace \(t>0\), give \(M_e=eMe\) the probability trace \(\tau_e=\tau/t\), and write \(Z_e=eZ,\ \mu_e=t^{-1}\mu|_F\).
Let \(A_{\rm out}\) be the unital starred algebra generated by \(e,(1-e)Z\) and the omitted coordinates.
Set \(P_e=\operatorname{alg}^*(Z_e,e A_{\rm out} e)\) with unit \(e\); \(eA_{\rm out}e\) is an algebra since \(e\in A_{\rm out}\).
It is the coefficient algebra for a relative distinguished derivation on \(P_e\).
With \(H_e=L^2(M_e,\tau_e)\), there is a represented outer derivation \(\delta_e:P_e\to H_e\otimes_2 H_e\) killing these coefficients, taking \(Z_e\) to \(e\otimes e\), and having the actual vacuum
\[
       \xi^e=\delta_e^*(e\otimes e)=\xi_{\mu_e}+t^{-1}e(\xi-\xi_B)e.       \tag{1}
\]
The scalar term is the conjugate variable of the divided difference for the restricted probability \(\mu_e\), evaluated on \(Z_e\).
The two terms are orthogonal, and partial liberation \(\gamma_e=I_u\delta_e\) has vacuum \(t^{-1}e\eta e\).
For any family of disjoint spectral sets of positive mass,
\[
               \sum_F t^3\|\xi^e\|_{2,\tau_e}^2\le C_0\|\xi\|_{2,\tau}^2  \tag{2}
\]
with an absolute constant \(C_0\).
In addition \(t^3\|\xi^e\|_{2,\tau_e}^2\to0\) whenever \(t\to0\) in this fixed law.

Write \(C_e=\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau_e\), \(T_r^e=C_e I_{k_r}\gamma_e\).
Each \(T_r^e\) has a normal bounded extension to \(M_e\), and on that algebra
\[
                eT_r(x)e=tT_r^e(x)+x\,eT_r(e)e.                          \tag{3}
\]
For any positive sequence \(r_j\to0\), define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 k(F)&=\sup_{j,\ x\in M_e,\ \|x\|\le1}\|eT_{r_j}(x)e\|_{2,\tau},\\
 a_e&=\sup_{p\in P_e,\ \|p\|\le1}\|C_e\delta_e(p)\|_{2,\tau_e},&
 B_e&=\sup_j\|eT_{r_j}(e)e\|_{2,\tau}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Let \(a_\mu\) be the corresponding single-input norm of the original scalar slice on marginal polynomials.
Then \(B_e\le3a_\mu<\infty\) and
\[
             t^{3/2}a_e\le k(F)+B_e,\qquad
             k(F)\le3t^{3/2}a_e+B_e.                                     \tag{4}
\]
In particular the same-corner test \(k(F)<\infty\) is equivalent to boundedness of the relative single-input slice on \(P_e\).
For tuples of length at least three with an unbounded original slice, there is one marginal point such that every open neighborhood of it has infinite relative single-input cost.
The same statements hold with the conjugate coordinate and its adjoint tuple in place of the original orientation.
These assertions concern the distinguished relative derivative on the corner.

## Argument

### Extending liberation and restricting its two inner legs

The coordinating agent proposed compressing liberation with both inner marginal legs before recovering the divided difference.
Let \(Q\) be the original starred polynomial algebra with \(e\) adjoined.
Taking bounded strong-star marginal polynomial approximants \(e_n\to e\) also gives convergence of their liberation derivatives to \(e\otimes1-1\otimes e\), since liberation on the marginal is the commutator with \(1\otimes1\).
The product rule gives graph approximation on words in \(Q\): all undifferentiated factors converge boundedly strongly-star and the differentiated factors converge in the coarse Hilbert norm.
The [measurable spectral block argument](Yin_C_F0242_v1.md#the-cost-of-a-measurable-spectral-block) and the closable liberation graph therefore give a well-defined derivation on \(Q\) with these values.
Also \(P_e=eQe\), since substituting \(Z=Z_e+(1-e)Z\) into words in \(eQe\) leaves only products of \(Z_e,Z_e^*\) and corner compressions of words from \(A_{\rm out}\).
The reverse inclusion follows from \(e\in Q\).
Bounded strong-star density of the original polynomial algebra gives weak density of \(P_e\) in \(M_e\), so it is a dense Hilbert domain.

Inner multiplication by \(e\otimes e\) commutes with both outer actions.
Thus \(\theta=I_{e\otimes e}\gamma\) is a derivation on \(Q\) whose coarse values have right support \(e\) on the first leg and left support \(e\) on the second leg.
It kills \(e\), the marginal element \((1-e)Z\) and its adjoint, and the omitted starred coordinates.
For \(x=exe\) the two other supports of \(\theta x=e\cdot\theta(x)\cdot e\) also lie under \(e\).
Identify this four-sided corner linearly with \(H_e\otimes_2H_e\), retaining the element and changing the product trace to \(\tau_e\otimes\tau_e\).
The restriction \(\gamma_e=\theta|_{P_e}\) takes \(Z_e\) to \(Z_e\otimes e-e\otimes Z_e\), has the same marginal commutator action on \(Z_e^*\), and kills \(e A_{\rm out}e\).
For the [global liberation adjoint](Yin_C_F0077_v1.md#the-marginal-restriction-of-the-actual-liberation-adjoint) the two marginal correction terms at \(e\otimes e\) cancel.
For \(x\in P_e\) this gives
\[
    (\tau_e\otimes\tau_e)(\gamma_e x)
     =t^{-2}\langle e\eta e,x\rangle_\tau
     =\langle t^{-1}e\eta e,x\rangle_{\tau_e}.
\]
The fixed global adjoint can be paired on \(Q\) by the graph approximation just proved.

### The actual relative derivation and its vacuum

The [scalar geometric criterion](Yin_C_F0255_v1.md#statement) compares \(\|\xi_B\|_{2,\tau}^2\) in both directions with
\[
 G(\mu)=\int(M_1\mu)^2d\mu+\iiint \kappa^2\,d\mu^3,
 \qquad M_1\mu(z)=\sup_{\rho>0}\mu(B(z,\rho))/\rho .
\]
Here \(\kappa\) is the reciprocal circumradius, set to zero on degenerate triples.
By restriction and scaling, \(t^3G(\mu_e)=G(\mu|_F)\le G(\mu)\), using the same integral expression on the finite restricted measure.
In particular \(\mu_e\) is diffuse and has an actual scalar vacuum.

On formal corner words put \(DZ_e=e\otimes e\), \(DZ_e^*=\bar u/u\) on the marginal product away from the null diagonal, and \(D=0\) on every coefficient.
The formula is interpreted in the coarse marginal calculus for \(Z_e\).
The product rule gives \(I_u D=\gamma_e\) on every expression.
Inner multiplication by \(u\) on the corner coarse space is injective, since the two marginal spectral variables there have joint measure class dominated by the product of the diffuse \(\mu_e\) with itself, as in [divided-difference recovery](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#recovering-the-divided-difference-on-the-represented-core).
Every relation in the represented algebra thus has zero \(D\)-value, so this gives a derivation \(\delta_e\) on \(P_e\).
In particular no freeness or normal generating set is required for the coefficient algebra.

On \(H=L^2(M,\tau)\) let \(S_g\) denote the joint calculus for left and right multiplication by \(Z\), and let \(B_r^F(z)=\int\overline{k_r(z,w)}\,d\mu_e(w)\).
Use the global adjoint formula at \(h=(1_F\otimes1_F)\bar k_r\), a bounded Borel marginal symbol.
The formula for \(\gamma^* h\) is \(S_h\eta+\operatorname{row}(h)-\operatorname{col}(h)\), valid by the [marginal adjoint graph formula](Yin_C_F0077_v1.md#the-marginal-restriction-of-the-actual-liberation-adjoint) with the factor \(i\) removed.
Since \(\bar k_r\) is antisymmetric, the row minus column is \(2t e B_r^F\).
Consequently on \(x\in P_e\)
\[
  (\tau_e\otimes\tau_e)(I_{k_r}\gamma_e x)
       =t^{-2}\langle eS_{\bar k_r}\eta e+2t eB_r^F,x\rangle_\tau .    \tag{5}
\]
The left side tends to the corner double trace of \(\delta_e x\) by dominated inner convergence of \(k_r u\) to one.
The [inverse-gap and scalar adjoint limits](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#the-scalar-correction-in-the-liberation-adjoint) give \(S_{\bar k_r}\eta\to\xi-\xi_B\) in global Hilbert norm and \(2B_r^F\rightharpoonup\xi_{\mu_e}\) in the scalar corner Hilbert space.
Pairing the latter in (5) uses only the orthogonal marginal projection of the test.
Converting the remaining global trace to \(\tau_e\) proves (1).
The nonmarginal term stays perpendicular to the spectral diagonal after compression because the global term is perpendicular to it and the corner multiplication commutes with the diagonal projection.
In particular it is perpendicular to the scalar term.

Its squared corner norm multiplied by \(t^3\) is exactly \(\|e(\xi-\xi_B)e\|_{2,\tau}^2\).
These contributions sum over disjoint \(F\) to at most \(\|\xi-\xi_B\|_{2,\tau}^2\), since the two-sided compressions are mutually orthogonal projections on the global Hilbert space.
The scalar contributions are bounded by an absolute multiple of \(\sum_F G(\mu|_F)\le G(\mu)\): the restricted maximal linear density is bounded by the original one on \(F\), and the integrals of the nonnegative triple kernel are over disjoint cubes \(F^3\).
This proves (2).
For arbitrary shrinking masses the same estimates tend to zero by absolute continuity of the integrals of the original integrable density and triple kernel, and \(\|e(\xi-\xi_B)e\|_2\le\|e(\xi-\xi_B)\|_2\to0\) by normality of the corresponding vector functional.

### The slice in the corner

For fixed \(r>0\) the cutoff \(T_r\) on \(Q\) agrees with the slice of \(I_{k_r}\gamma\): the bounded graph approximants converge weakly under the original normal cutoff and in Hilbert norm under the graph formula.
For \(x\in P_e\), \(t T_r^e(x)=C I_{e\otimes e}I_{k_r}\gamma(x)\) is supported on both sides by \(e\).
Right multiplication of a sliced vector by \(e\) inserts \(e\) on the inner side of the first leg.
The difference from \(eT_r(x)e\) therefore equals \(e C I_{e\otimes(1-e)}I_{k_r}\gamma(x)\).
Apply the product rule at \(x=xe\).
In the term \(\gamma(x)\cdot e\), the second leg after inner insertion belongs to \((1-e)H e\), with trace zero.
The other term gives \(x C I_{e\otimes(1-e)}I_{k_r}\gamma(e)\).
Since \(I_{e\otimes e}\gamma(e)=0\), and the original slice of \(e\) is marginal, this is \(x eT_r(e)e\).
This proves (3) on \(P_e\), and its right-hand rearrangement defines the unique normal bounded extension of \(T_r^e\) to \(M_e\).
The original marginal cutoff equals the scalar cutoff on \(B\), first on marginal polynomials and then by normality.
Thus the [uniform scalar cutoff bound](Yin_C_F0282_v1.md#canonical-cutoffs-on-the-original-algebra) gives \(B_e\le3a_\mu\), with \(a_\mu<\infty\) by the scalar geometric theorem.

For each \(p\in P_e\), \(T_r^e(p)\to C_e\delta_e(p)\) in normalized Hilbert norm because \(I_{k_ru}\to1\) strongly on the corner coarse space.
Applying (3) to each core contraction along the chosen sequence and taking the supremum proves the first inequality of (4), since converting a corner output norm from \(\tau_e\) to \(\tau\) introduces \(\sqrt t\).
For the other inequality assume \(a_e<\infty\).
For separated marginal multipliers \(v,w\), the right slice of \(I_{v\otimes w}\delta_e(p)\) equals
\[
        (C_e\delta_e)(pw)v-p(C_e\delta_e)(w)v .
\]
The identity follows from the product rule and traciality for marginal polynomials, and extends to \(v,w\in C^*(Z_e)\) by uniform approximation on either side, using the bounded core cost on the right.
The identity bounds the sliced insertion of any pair of marginal unitaries on a core contraction by \(2a_e\).
As in the [cutoff multiplier estimate](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#removing-the-cutoff-in-the-column-norm), \(1-k_r u=r^2/(|u|^2+r^2)\) is the Fourier transform of a probability on the plane, hence the corresponding inner multiplier is an average of such separated unitaries.
Subtracting the averaged slice from the original one gives \(\|T_r^e\|\le3a_e\) on \(P_e\), and the same norm bound holds on \(M_e\) by normality and bounded strong-star approximation.
Equation (3) now gives the other inequality of (4).
If the original slice is unbounded and \(d\ge3\), the [same-corner localization theorem](Yin_C_F0299_v1.md#statement) yields a point with \(k(U)=\infty\) for every neighborhood.
Its open neighborhoods have positive marginal mass, and (4) gives the assertion there.
Applying the same argument to the adjoint tuple gives the other orientation.
