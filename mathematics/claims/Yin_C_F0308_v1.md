# A trace-half projection as the only coefficient in the uniform normal slice problem

## Current assessment
[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Take a finite tracial von Neumann algebra \((R,\tau)\) generated by a bounded normal \(Z\) and finitely many bounded selfadjoint coefficients \(H_l\).
On their represented starred polynomial algebra suppose a distinguished outer derivation \(\delta\) kills the coefficients, satisfies \(\delta Z=1\otimes1\), and has actual vacuum \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\).
Write \(E=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta\).
Let \(K_{\mathrm{rel}}\) be the supremum of \(\|Ep\|_2/\|\xi\|_2\) over all such inputs and all starred polynomial contractions \(p\).
Let \(K_{\mathrm{proj}}\) be this supremum restricted to the case of one coefficient \(P=P^*=P^2\) of trace \(1/2\).
Then, in the extended reals,
\[
                K_{\mathrm{proj}}\le K_{\mathrm{rel}}\le2K_{\mathrm{proj}} .
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
More precisely, every finite witness transfers, up to an arbitrarily small additive error, to at least half its ratio in one such projection model.
The normal generator can be required to be a contraction in both suprema.

Consequently the [general normal implication](../tasks/Yin_T_F0011_v1.md#contract) \(\mathsf A\Rightarrow\mathsf S\) holds if and only if \(K_{\mathrm{proj}}<\infty\).
A sequence of diverging ratios in projection models would give a single actual normal pair, with a selfadjoint second coordinate, admitting no bare dual system.
The projection in (1) is a coefficient for the derivative at the normal generator.
An outer derivation cannot take a nontrivial projection \(P\) to \(1\otimes1\), since differentiating \(P^2=P\) would require \(P\otimes1+1\otimes P=1\otimes1\).
The value or finiteness of \(K_{\mathrm{proj}}\) remains open.

## Argument

### Encoding the coefficients in a spectral projection of a noisy operator

Fix an input and a polynomial witness \(p\), \(\|p\|\le1\).
Rescale its \(m\) selfadjoint coefficients to contractions, adapting its expression, and put \(k=m+1,\ d=2k,\ t=1/d\).
Use the [finite matrix construction](Yin_C_F0306_v1.md#matrix-derivations-and-the-off-diagonal-correction) in \(d\) rows, adjoining \(d-1\) freely independent standard semicirculars \(s_2,\ldots,s_d\) freely to \(R\).
With normalized matrix trace and diagonal matrix units \(e_r\), set
\[
 W=e_1\alpha Z+\sum_{r=2}^{d}e_r(10r+s_r),\qquad
 B=\sum_{r=2}^k(E_{1r}+E_{r1})(2+H_{r-1})^{1/2}
\]
where \(0<\alpha\|Z\|\le1\) and \(B\) is regarded as a \(k\)-by-\(k\) matrix.
Choose \(c>\max(1,\|B\|)\).
In the doubled matrix algebra use
\[
 P_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}
 I+B/c & (I-(B/c)^2)^{1/2}\\
 (I-(B/c)^2)^{1/2} & I-B/c
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The two entries \(B/c\) and \((I-(B/c)^2)^{1/2}\) commute and their squares sum to \(I\).
Thus \(P_0\) is a projection and cancellation on its diagonal gives it trace \(1/2\).

Recall the derivative before the cross-spectrum correction.
It lifts the relative derivative divided by \(\alpha\) into the first matrix slot and the free difference quotients at \(s_r\) into slots \(r\ge2\).
On the matrix core it kills all matrix units and all entries of \(P_0\).
Indeed these entries belong to the matrix norm closure of the starred coefficient algebra: approximate the first square roots by polynomials in individual \(H_l\) and the final matrix square root by polynomials in \(B/c\).
The approximants have derivative zero, and products with the original letters converge in the derivation graph by norm convergence of their undifferentiated factors.
The actual vacuum gives closability by the core tensor adjoint formula, as in the finite matrix construction.
The lifted vacuum norm is at most \(t^{3/2}\|\xi\|_2/\alpha+t^{3/2}\sqrt{d-1}\).
Add the inner derivation with kernel \(1/(z-v)\) on distinct summand spectra of \(W\), zero elsewhere, to get \(L W=1\otimes1\).
That kernel has Hilbert norm at most one by separation, so the correction costs at most two in vacuum norm and \(\|LP_0\|_2\le2\).

Adjoin one more freely independent standard semicircular \(S\) and put \(Y=P_0+\varepsilon S\) with \(0<2\varepsilon<1/4\).
The [free-noise correction](Yin_C_F0306_v1.md#making-the-coefficient-an-actual-selfadjoint-coordinate) extends \(L\) by zero on \(S\), then inserts \(-LP_0/\varepsilon\) into the free difference quotient at \(S\).
The resulting derivation kills \(Y\), retains the generator values at \(W,W^*\), and has vacuum norm at most
\[
                     t^{3/2}\|\xi\|_2/\alpha+t^{3/2}\sqrt{d-1}+2+2/\varepsilon.
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
Now take \(P=1_{(1/2,\infty)}(Y)\).
The spectrum of \(Y\) lies within \(2\varepsilon\) of \(\{0,1\}\), and \(P\) is a norm limit of polynomials in \(Y\), all with zero derivative.
Taking the same fixed continuous real function, equal to zero on \([-1/4,1/4]\) and one on \([3/4,5/4]\), at \(P_0\) and \(Y\) shows \(\|P-P_0\|\to0\) by uniform polynomial approximation.
When this norm is less than one each projection is subequivalent to the other: the compression of one projection to the range of the other is bounded below, so the polar isometry of their product embeds that range into the opposite range.
Thus \(\tau'(P)=1/2\), where \(\tau'\) denotes the ambient trace.
The pair \(W,P\) carries the relative derivative \(\delta'\) in \(M'=W^*(W,P)\).
Indeed the spectral law of \(W\) is diffuse, and its adjoint-generator value is its forced normal phase in the marginal coarse space. [Normal phase](Yin_C_F0076_v1.md#1-diffuseness-the-phase-and-closability)
The product rule therefore places the restricted values inside the coarse space of \(M'\), and Hilbert projection of the ambient vacuum gives the actual relative vacuum \(\xi'\) with bound (2).

### Direct comparison of the original and compressed slices

Put \(e=e_1\), \(H'_l=(2c)^2eP e_{l+1}Pe-2e\).
For \(2\le r\le k\), the off-diagonal \((1,r)\)-entry of the upper-left block of \(P_0\) is \((2+H_{r-1})^{1/2}/(2c)\).
Consequently
\[
              \|H'_l-E_{11}H_l\|\le 2(2c)^2\|P-P_0\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
These coefficients are independent of \(\alpha\) in the common ambient corner.
Each \(e_r\) is a smooth function of \(\operatorname{Re}W\) which is constant, with the appropriate value zero or one, on neighborhoods of the disjoint summand spectra of \(\operatorname{Re}W\).
Approximate the derivative of this function uniformly on a containing interval by polynomials and integrate, obtaining polynomial approximants in \(C^1\).
On the marginal, the derivative of each polynomial in \(\operatorname{Re}W\) is its divided difference with denominator \(z-v\), by the forced phase and Leibniz rule.
The difference between two such tensors is bounded by the sup norm of the difference of the polynomial first derivatives.
Thus the cuts \(e_r\) are limits in operator norm and coarse graph on the original core, with derivative \((e_r(z)-e_r(v))/(z-v)\); the diagonal is product-null.

On the algebra formed by adjoining these cuts to the core, restrict both inner legs by setting \(\theta x=I_{e\otimes e}\delta' x\), where \(I_{e\otimes e}(v\otimes w)=ve\otimes ew\).
The product rule persists by joint norm and coarse approximation, and inner insertion commutes with outer actions.
Since the cuts are constant on the first spectral piece, \(\theta e_r=0\), and \(\theta P=0\).
In particular \(\theta H'_l=0\).
On elements \(x=exe\) of this algebra the four supports of \(\theta x\) are in \(e\), by the product rule and \(\theta e=0\).
Identify its values with the coarse corner furnished with normalized trace \(\tau_e=\tau'/t\), keeping the same tensor elements.
On \(eWe/\alpha=E_{11}Z\) the derivative \(\alpha\theta\) has value \(e\otimes e\).
On its adjoint it has the original phase of \(Z\), since \(\alpha>0\).

Let \(E'=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau')\delta'\) and \(a'\) be its single-input cost on the original core.
If \(a'<\infty\), the same bound holds at each of the above norm and graph limits.
For every such \(x=exe\), applying the product rule to \(xe\) and tracing the second tensor leg gives
\[
 (\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau')I_{e\otimes e}\delta' x=(E'(xe)-xE'(e))e.
\]
For elementary tensors this is the identity \(ve\,\tau'(ew)=v\tau'(we)e\); linearity and Hilbert continuity give the general case.
Since \(\|xe\|\le\|x\|\), the right side has Hilbert norm at most \(2a'\|x\|\).
The left side has ambient Hilbert norm \(t^{3/2}\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau_e)\theta x\|_{2,\tau_e}\).
This factor consists of \(t\) from the trace in the second leg and \(\sqrt t\) from the Hilbert norm in the first.

Evaluate \(p\) at \(eWe/\alpha,H'_1,\ldots,H'_m\) with unit \(e\), obtaining \(x\) in this enlarged graph core.
Under the normalized corner identification \(x\to E_{11}p\) in operator norm as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
Expanding the finite product rule for \(\alpha\theta x\), the zero coefficient derivatives and the two fixed original tensors at the distinguished letters give
\[
                  (\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau_e)\alpha\theta x
                              \longrightarrow E_{11}Ep
                       \quad\text{in }L^2(\tau_e).
\]
These two limits are independent of \(\alpha\), since all elements and tensors in the finite expressions after scaling are independent of it.
For arbitrarily small \(\eta>0\), choose \(\varepsilon\) first so that \(\|x\|\le1+\eta\) and the last norm is at least \(\|Ep\|_2-\eta\).
The direct comparison yields \(2\alpha a'(1+\eta)\ge t^{3/2}(\|Ep\|_2-\eta)\), also trivially if \(a'=\infty\).
Using (2) and then choosing \(\alpha\) sufficiently small gives \(a'/\|\xi'\|_2\) arbitrarily close from below to \(\|Ep\|_2/(2\|\xi\|_2)\) after letting \(\eta\) tend to zero.
Both vacuum norms are positive by testing the corresponding normal generator.
Since \(a'\) is a supremum over finite core witnesses, a further arbitrarily small additive loss suffices for a single such witness.
This proves the upper comparison in (1), and the lower comparison follows from inclusion.
Positive rescaling of a normal generator preserves the ratio, since both its derivative and vacuum scale by the inverse factor on the same represented core.

### Equivalence to the general comparison

If \(K_{\mathrm{proj}}\) is finite, (1) bounds the cost uniformly for all finite relative models.
The [assembly theorem](Yin_C_F0305_v1.md#statement) then gives the universal normal implication.
If \(K_{\mathrm{proj}}\) is infinite, the same theorem for \(m=1\) assembles projection-coefficient witnesses with unbounded ratios into a single fully actual normal pair with selfadjoint second coordinate and infinite distinguished single-input cost.
This pair has no bare dual system, as asserted there.
