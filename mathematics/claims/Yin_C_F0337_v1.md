# Local spectral mass and the signed curvature estimate for normal coordinates

## Current assessment
[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \(Z\) be a bounded normal variable in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal probability trace.
On the represented weakly dense starred algebra generated by its marginal polynomials and bounded omitted coefficients assume an actual distinguished outer derivation with \(\delta Z=1\otimes1\), zero on the omitted coefficients and their adjoints, and \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\).
Use the normal divided difference on \(Z^*\).
In a tuple satisfying actual starred ACSY these are the original prescriptions.
Let \(\mu\) be the marginal probability, \(\zeta=(i\gamma)^*(1\otimes1)\) the real partial liberation vacuum, and \(\nu\) its spectral measure for left and right multiplication by \(Z\).
For every disk \(B=B(a,r)\) put
\[
 T(B)=\int \min(1,r^2/|a-v|^2)\,d\mu(v).
\]
There is an absolute \(K\) such that all disks with \(T(B)>K\mu(3B)\), taken together in the positive disk representation of the curvature kernel, give an absolutely bounded signed pairing on the analytic unitary tests, of cost at most \(C\|\xi\|_2^2\).
Thus for the unrestricted normal implication it suffices to bound the negative signed contribution of the other disks at each coordinate.
The assertion concerns signed pairings after collecting the angular kernels on each disk.

Suppose in addition that for a bounded Borel map \(\phi\) into a separable real Hilbert space with \(\mathcal H^2\)-null essential range in the sense of an image of a full-measure set, one has
\[
 d\nu(z,w)\le\|\phi(z)-\phi(w)\|^2d\mu(z)d\mu(w),\qquad
 J_\phi=\iint_{z\ne w}\frac{\|\phi(z)-\phi(w)\|^2}{|z-w|^2}\,d\mu(z)d\mu(w)<\infty .
 \tag{1}
\]
No growth assumption on \(\mu\) beyond its actual scalar vacuum is required.
Both oriented original slices have operator-norm-to-trace-Hilbert-norm cost at most \(C(\|\xi\|_2+\sqrt{J_\phi})\) on the original core, with an absolute constant.
The canonical operator and its adjoint have all bounded vectors in their domains.
If (1) holds at every coordinate of an actual normal tuple, it admits the strong system of F0011.
In particular the bound is \(C\|\xi\|_2\) whenever the density is exactly a squared feature difference as in (1).
Real or circle-valued maps and finite sets of values satisfy the range condition.

The analytic estimate on which this reduction rests is the following smooth density square inequality for any compactly supported diffuse planar probability.
Fix a smooth decreasing cutoff \(\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\) equal to one on \([0,1]\) and zero on \([2,\infty)\).
With \(\theta(x,r)=r^{-1}\int\psi(|x-y|/r)d\mu(y)\) and \(G(\mu)=\int(M_1\mu)^2d\mu+\iiint\kappa^2d\mu^3\), one has
\[
  \int\!\int_0^\infty|\theta(x,r)-\theta(x,2r)|^2\,\frac{dr}{r}d\mu(x)
       \le C_\psi G(\mu).                                             \tag{2}
\]
Here \(M_1\mu(x)=\sup_{s>0}\mu(B(x,s))/s\) and \(\kappa\) denotes reciprocal circumradius with value zero on repeated or collinear triples.

## Argument

### A change of measure for the density square function

It suffices to consider finite \(G(\mu)\).
Write \(b(x)^2=\iint \kappa(x,y,z)^2d\mu(y)d\mu(z)\), \(f=M_1\mu+b\) and \(d\sigma=f^{-1}d\mu\) on the full-measure set with finite \(f\).
As \(\mu\) is a probability of bounded support and positive diameter, \(M_1\mu\) has a strictly positive lower bound there.
Thus \(\sigma\) is finite and \(\int f^3d\sigma=\int f^2d\mu\le 2G(\mu)\).
For \(x\in B(a,r)\), \(M_1\mu(x)\ge\mu(B(a,r))/(2r)\), so \(\sigma(B(a,r))\le2r\).
For every Borel set \(Q\) there is also a uniform local curvature bound
\[
                         \iiint_{Q^3}\kappa^2\,d\sigma^3\le 3\sigma(Q).
\]
Indeed assign each triple to a point of smallest \(f\), bounding its reciprocal product by the inverse cube at that point.
Integration with respect to \(\mu^3\) then costs at most \(3\int_Q b(x)^2 f(x)^{-3}d\mu(x)\).

Apply [Tolsa, Theorem 19.1](../audits/literature/Tol17/Yin_U001.md#the-global-square-function-under-uniform-linear-growth) to \(\sigma|_Q\).
The global integral of its squared sharp density differences against itself is at most \(C\sigma(Q)\).
To pass to the smooth differences, put \(\alpha(s)=-\psi'(s)\) for \(1\le s\le2\).
For any finite measure the smooth density at \(r\) is the average of the sharp densities at \(sr\) with weights \(s\alpha(s)ds\).
Cauchy–Schwarz in \(s\) and the substitution \(r\mapsto sr\) bound the smooth square integral by a fixed multiple of the sharp one.
For the linear operator \(U\) from functions on \(\sigma\) to functions taking values in \(H=L^2((0,\infty),dr/r)\), let the \(r\) component be the smooth density difference of the input measure at \(r,2r\).
The last bound tests \(U1_Q\) on \(Q\) itself over all radii, hence over \(0<r<\ell(Q)\) for every square.

The scalar kernel of the \(r\) component is supported on \(|x-y|\le4r\), bounded by \(C/r\), and has gradient in either variable bounded by \(C/r^2\).
These give the size and \(y\)-regularity estimates of [Lacey and Martikainen, Theorem 1.3](../audits/literature/LM17/Yin_U001.md#indicator-testing) with \(m=\alpha=1\).
If \(|y-y'|<r/2\) and either value is nonzero, \(|x-y|\le(9/2)r\), as required for the off-support part of the regularity inequality.
The measure has uniform linear growth and \(b_Q=1_Q\) has integral and squared norm \(\sigma(Q)\).
Consequently \(U:L^2(\sigma)\to L^2(\sigma;H)\) is bounded with a constant depending only on the fixed cutoff and the absolute testing constants.

For \(d=|x-y|>0\), integrating the size estimate over \(r\ge d/4\) bounds the \(H\)-norm of the operator kernel by \(C/d\).
When \(|x-x'|\le d/2\), the difference has norm at most \(C|x-x'|/d^2\), using \(r\ge d/8\) and the gradient bound.
The same estimate holds in the second variable.
Summing over doubling annuli using the upper linear growth gives both integral Hörmander conditions with uniform constants.
The integral represents \(U\) away from the support of an input by the component formula, using positive separation, finiteness of \(\sigma\), and the displayed \(H\)-valued size estimate.
These are the hypotheses of [García-Cuerva and Martell, Definition 2.1](../audits/literature/GM00/Yin_U001.md#operator-kernel).
Their [Theorem 2.7](../audits/literature/GM00/Yin_U001.md#strong-type) gives the strong \(L^3(\sigma)\to L^3(\sigma;H)\) estimate, with the same uniformity for this fixed exponent.
Since \(\sigma\) is finite, \(f\in L^3(\sigma)\subset L^2(\sigma)\); the components of \(Uf\) are those of \(\mu=f\sigma\), by approximation on compact scale intervals.
Hölder gives
\[
             \int\|Uf\|_H^2 f\,d\sigma\le\|Uf\|_{L^3(\sigma;H)}^2\|f\|_{L^3(\sigma)}
                      \le C_\psi\int f^3d\sigma .
\]
This proves (2) with no uniform linear growth assumption on the original measure.

### Disks of small local mass

Write \(\mathcal B=\{B(a,r):T(B)>K\mu(3B)\}\).
For \(x\in B(a,r)\), decomposition into annuli around \(x\) gives
\[
 \frac{T(B)}{r}\le C_0\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\theta(x,2^j r).
\]
Indeed \(\min(1,r^2/|a-v|^2)\le C\min(1,r^2/|x-v|^2)\).
Also \(r\theta(x,r)\le\mu(3B)\) since \(\psi\) is supported at distances at most \(2r\).
Subtract \(\theta(x,r)\) from each density in the series.
For \(K>4C_0\), the constant part contributes at most half the left side on \(\mathcal B\).
Telescoping the differences at the intervening scales and summing the tail of the geometric series yields
\[
 {\bf1}_{\mathcal B}\frac{T(B)^2}{r^2}
 \le C\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}|\theta(x,2^j r)-\theta(x,2^{j+1}r)|^2 .
\]
Here the last step uses weighted Cauchy–Schwarz; on the complement the left side is zero.
For \(t=\mu(B)\), integrating \(x\in B\) against \(\mu\) and using that the centers within \(r\) of \(x\) have area \(\pi r^2\), (2) gives
\[
             L=\int_{\mathcal B}t\,T(B)^2\,dA(a)dr/r^5\le C G(\mu).
\tag{3}
\]
The identical integration with two endpoints in \(B\), forcing \(r\ge|z-w|/2\), gives
\[
             N=\int \nu(B^2)\,dA(a)dr/r^5
                  \le C\int_{z\ne w}|z-w|^{-2}d\nu(z,w).
\tag{4}
\]
The [actual vacuum decomposition](Yin_C_F0255_v1.md#statement) bounds \(G(\mu)\) and the inverse-gap integral by \(C\|\xi\|_2^2\).
For a single relative coordinate this uses the same [liberation commutator at that coordinate](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#statement) and the scalar criterion for its projected vacuum.
In particular the diagonal has zero \(\nu\)-mass.

For fixed \(B\) collect in the [disk representation](Yin_C_F0329_v1.md#integrating-the-two-block-estimate) the contributions of the angles and of the third vertex \(v\).
Up to an absolute constant the resulting kernel on \(B^2\) is
\[
                q_B(z,w)=1_B(z)1_B(w)\int |h_v(z)-h_v(w)|^2d\mu(v),
       \qquad h_v(z)=(z-v)/(\bar z-\bar v).
\]
The values at coincidences are immaterial.
On the disk its angular oscillation is bounded by \(C\min(1,r/|a-v|)\), using the gradient away from the disk and the bound two otherwise.
Thus \(\|q_B\|_\infty\le C T(B)\).
The [localized signed kernel bound](Yin_C_F0297_v1.md#a-localized-kernel-in-the-liberation-energy) costs at most \(CT(B)(\sqrt{t\nu(B^2)}+t^2)\) on each disk, uniformly in the analytic test.
Since \(t\le T(B)\), its integral on \(\mathcal B\) is at most \(C(\sqrt{LN}+L)\) by Cauchy–Schwarz.
This proves the unrestricted reduction in the statement.

### The remaining disks under feature domination

For a disjoint angular threshold rectangle \(E,F\subset B\) let \(t_E=\mu(E)\ge\mu(F)=t_F>0\).
Assign the points of \(F\) to the first member of a countable Borel cover of the full-measure range of \(\phi\) with sum of squared diameters below \(\varepsilon\).
For the resulting positive-mass sets \(F_i\), (1) gives \(\sum_i\nu(F_i^2)/\mu(F_i)^2\le\varepsilon\).
The [expanded rectangle bound](Yin_C_F0332_v1.md#subdivision-by-cubes) then gives, after summing the disjoint kernels and letting \(\varepsilon\) tend to zero,
\[
  -F_{1_E\otimes1_F+1_F\otimes1_E}(u)
  \le 2t_F\nu(E^2)/t_E^2+[\nu(E\times F)+\nu(F\times E)]/t_E
  \le\frac{10}{t_E}\int_{E\times F}\|\phi(z)-\phi(w)\|^2d\mu(z)d\mu(w).
\]
For the last inequality, insert \(w\in F\) between two points of \(E\) and average with \(d\mu(w)/t_F\).
A second squared triangle inequality, using an arbitrary constant vector \(c\), bounds the last term by \(20\int_B\|\phi-c\|^2d\mu\).
The estimate is symmetric in the order of the masses and is valid if either vanishes.
It requires no measurable selection of the cover as the parameters vary.

For a fixed \(B,v\), the area of level pairs giving nonempty endpoints is at most half the squared oscillation on \(B\) of the real or imaginary component of \(h_v\).
After integration in the levels and \(v\) the cost is at most \(C T(B)\int_B\|\phi-c\|^2d\mu\).
On \(\mathcal B^c\) with positive mass take \(c=\phi(w)\) and average against normalized \(\mu\) on \(3B\).
The bound becomes \(CK\int_{B\times3B}\|\phi(z)-\phi(w)\|^2d\mu(z)d\mu(w)\).
For a fixed pair the centers have area at most \(\pi r^2\) and the radii satisfy \(r\ge |z-w|/4\).
Integration therefore costs at most \(C K J_\phi\).
Together with (3) and (4) this bounds the negative matched curvature form by \(C(\|\xi\|_2^2+J_\phi)\).

At truncated radii all kernel evaluations above pass under the integrals by the fixed-test bounded insertion formula.
The kernels from any selected set of disks are nonnegative and their sum is bounded pointwise by the full curvature kernel up to its fixed normalization.
The [gap-weighted convergence and real-component energy inequality](Yin_C_F0292_v1.md#the-signed-estimate-on-analytic-unitaries) therefore pass to full scales on each original analytic test in either orientation and bound both original slices by the claimed constant.
That calculation uses only the distinguished derivation on \(Z,Z^*\) and its zero values on the omitted letters.
The [original-domain argument](Yin_C_F0078_v2.md#the-original-canonical-operators) gives both domains and the generator commutators.
In the exact density case \(J_\phi=\int |z-w|^{-2}d\nu\le\|\xi\|_2^2\).
The hypotheses leave open the negative contribution on \(\mathcal B^c\) for a general joint spectral density with no known domination (1).
