# Uniform slice estimates on any fixed number of parallel spectral lines

## Current assessment
[Selected decision](#decision-reviewed)

## Statement

Let \(Z\) be a bounded normal coordinate in a von Neumann algebra with faithful normal probability trace.
Assume an actual distinguished outer derivation on a weakly dense represented starred core generated by \(Z,Z^*\) and bounded coefficients, with \(\delta Z=1\otimes1\) and vacuum \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\).
The derivation vanishes on the coefficients and their adjoints.
If the marginal law \(\mu\) is carried by \(m\ge1\) parallel straight lines in \(\mathbb C\), then both original oriented right slices satisfy
\[
             \|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta p\|_2
                        \le C m^2\|\xi\|_2\|p\|
\]
and the same estimate holds for the flip-adjoint derivation.
The canonical negative right-slice closure and its adjoint have all bounded vectors in their domains, with the distinguished generator-first commutators.
The constant \(C\) is absolute, independently of all distances between lines, their relative masses, and the killed coefficients.

In the finite-label setting of [the lattice reduction](Yin_C_F0339_v1.md#statement), if all labels are on \(m\) parallel lines, then
\[
                           \|E_c p\|_2\le C m^2 B_c\|p\|
\]
on the entire represented word algebra.
In fact this holds for any finite labels with separation at least \(h>0\), with that lower scale used in their budget.
In particular any two lattice rows obey an absolute bound regardless of their separation measured in mesh units.
The [collinear estimate and consecutive-row result](Yin_C_F0340_v1.md#statement) are special cases.

## Argument

### Curvature at the heights of a triangle

Rotate the plane so the supporting lines are horizontal.
This conjugates the normal derivative normalization and the inverse gaps by unit phases and preserves all norms.
Write \(\mathcal L\) for the finite set of positive differences between two supporting heights, with at most \(m(m-1)/2\) elements.
For \(L\in\mathcal L\) put \(b_L(z,w)=(1+|z-w|^2/L^2)^{-1}\).
For three distinct vertices, order their heights as \(y_j\le y_i\le y_k\) and write \(x_h,y_h\) for the coordinates of vertex \(z_h\).
In the determinant
\[
 (x_j-x_i)(y_k-y_i)-(x_k-x_i)(y_j-y_i)
\]
bound the two horizontal displacements by the corresponding side lengths.
Using the squared determinant formula for the reciprocal circumradius gives
\[
 \kappa^2\le
 \frac{8(y_k-y_i)^2}{|z_i-z_k|^2 |z_j-z_k|^2}
 +\frac{8(y_j-y_i)^2}{|z_i-z_j|^2 |z_j-z_k|^2}.
\]
For the first summand both denominator sides are at least \(L=y_k-y_i\) since \(i\) is the middle height, and for the second they are at least \(L=y_i-y_j\).
Omit terms with \(L=0\).
If all heights coincide, curvature vanishes.
Each nonzero term is bounded by \(32 L^{-2}\) times the product of the \(b_L\)'s on the two indicated sides.
Thus the marginal kernel \(g(z,w)=\int\kappa(z,w,v)^2d\mu(v)\) is bounded by \(\sum_{L\in\mathcal L}G_L(z,w)\), where
\[
 G_L(z,w)=64 L^{-2}\!\int\!
 [ b_L(z,v)b_L(w,v)+b_L(z,w)(b_L(z,v)+b_L(w,v))]\,d\mu(v).
\]
The measure is diffuse by the scalar actual vacuum, so the coincident-site convention does not affect this bound.
Use the nonnegative symmetric kernels \(g_L=gG_L/(\sum_{L'}G_{L'})\), taking zero when the denominator is zero.
They add up to \(g\) and satisfy \(g_L\le G_L\) individually.
For a single line \(g=0\) directly.
This decomposition will permit absolute estimates on actual signed kernel pairings.

### Localization on two cells of one fixed length

Fix \(L>0\) from this decomposition.
Partition each supporting line separately into half-open horizontal cells of length \(L\), and retain the positive-mass cells \(E_i\) of masses \(t_i\) and geometric centers \(c_i'\).
Bounded spectrum ensures there are finitely many at each fixed \(L\).
Let
\[
 d_{ij}=|c_i'-c_j'|/L,\qquad
 \lambda_{ij}=(1+d_{ij}^2)^{-1},\qquad S_i=\sum_j\lambda_{ij}t_j .
\]
The symmetric matrix \(\lambda\) has row and column sums at most \(K=C_0m\), taking \(K\ge1\): on each line the horizontal indices alone give the summable sequence \((1+k^2)^{-1}\), using the same horizontal grid origin for all lines.
Consequently \(\|\lambda t\|_{\ell^3}\le K\|t\|_{\ell^3}\), by Jensen on each row and summation using the column bound.
On \(E_i\times E_j\) one has \(b_L\le C_1\lambda_{ij}\) by the triangle inequality.
Also
\[
       \lambda_{ik}\lambda_{jk}
          \le 4\lambda_{ij}(\lambda_{ik}+\lambda_{jk}),
\]
since at least one of \(d_{ik},d_{jk}\) is at least \(d_{ij}/2\).
Thus \(\|g_L\|_{\infty,E_i\times E_j}\le C_2 L^{-2}\lambda_{ij}(S_i+S_j)\).

Write \(\nu\) for the spectral measure of the real partial liberation vacuum and \(\sigma=|z-w|^{-2}\nu\) off the diagonal, extended by zero on it.
The [relative liberation adjoint and inverse-gap calculation](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#recovering-the-divided-difference-on-the-represented-core) gives \(\sigma(\mathbb C^2)=I\le\|\xi\|_2^2\) and zero diagonal mass of \(\nu\).
Its application with killed coefficients uses only the distinguished normalization, normal phase and original vacuum.
Also, since \(E_i\subset B(z,2L)\) for every \(z\in E_i\),
\[
        L^{-2}\sum_i t_i^3
           \le 4\int(M_1\mu)^2d\mu\le C_3\|\xi\|_2^2
\]
by the [scalar geometric estimate](Yin_C_F0255_v1.md#statement) for the marginal vacuum.

Take the portion of \(g_L\) on the rectangle \(E_i\times E_j\) and its transpose together, taking a diagonal cell square just once.
It is a bounded real symmetric kernel supported on \((E_i\cup E_j)^2\).
The [localized signed kernel bound](Yin_C_F0297_v1.md#a-localized-kernel-in-the-liberation-energy) holds for this disconnected Borel set.
Writing \(F_q\) for the real matched curvature pairing on any strict analytic unitary, its absolute value for this portion is at most
\[
 C_4 L^{-2}\lambda_{ij}(S_i+S_j)
        \big(\sqrt{t_i+t_j}\sqrt{\nu((E_i\cup E_j)^2)}
                         +(t_i+t_j)^2\big).
\]
For the relative version, the finite-kernel adjoint, row and composition identities in the cited proof act only on the distinguished marginal and are zero on the killed coefficients; the pairing is therefore the same original-core estimate. [Relative signed-energy application](Yin_C_F0337_v1.md#disks-of-small-local-mass)

We give the summation, in particular keeping diagonal vacuum masses separate from the off-diagonal rectangles.
For positive integers \(p,q\) with \(p+q=3\),
\[
 \sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}(S_i+S_j)^p(t_i+t_j)^q
             \le C_5 K\|S\|_{\ell^3}^p\|t\|_{\ell^3}^q .
\]
This follows by Holder on pairs for the measure \(\lambda_{ij}\) and the row and column bounds on each cubed factor.
The mass part hence costs at most \(C_6 L^{-2}K^2\sum t_i^3\).

Put \(\sigma_{ij}=\sigma(E_i\times E_j)\).
On each rectangle \(\sqrt{\nu(E_i\times E_j)}\le L(1+d_{ij})\sqrt{\sigma_{ij}}\).
For \(i\ne j\), the off-diagonal portions of the square root of \(\nu((E_i\cup E_j)^2)\) are bounded by the sum of the square roots for the two ordered cross rectangles.
For either order, Cauchy--Schwarz over all pairs bounds the cost by
\[
 \frac{C_7\sqrt I}{L}
       \left[\sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}^2(1+d_{ij})^2
                      (S_i+S_j)^2(t_i+t_j)\right]^{1/2}
       \le \frac{C_8 K^{3/2}\sqrt I}{L}\,\|t\|_{\ell^3}^{3/2}.
\]
Here \(\lambda_{ij}^2(1+d_{ij})^2\le2\lambda_{ij}\).
For the repeated diagonal contribution from \(E_i^2\), first sum over \(j\); its cost is at most
\[
 \frac{C_9}{L}\sum_i\sqrt{\sigma_{ii}}\,
          \sum_j\lambda_{ij}(S_i+S_j)\sqrt{t_i+t_j}
      \le\frac{C_{10}K^2\sqrt I}{L}\,\|t\|_{\ell^3}^{3/2}.
\]
Indeed apply weighted Cauchy--Schwarz in each \(j\)-sum with weight \(\lambda_{ij}\) of mass at most \(K\), then Cauchy--Schwarz in \(i\) and the preceding pair inequality at \((p,q)=(2,1)\).
The \(E_j^2\) contribution obeys the same bound and this also covers the diagonal cell squares.
Thus at one length \(L\) the total absolute cost is at most \(C_{11}m^2\|\xi\|_2^2\).
Summing this over the at most \(m(m-1)/2\) lengths bounds \(|F_g|\) uniformly by \(C_{12}m^4\|\xi\|_2^2\).

The [real-component energy inequality](Yin_C_F0292_v1.md#the-signed-estimate-on-analytic-unitaries), with [its original relative-domain passage](Yin_C_F0337_v1.md#the-remaining-disks-under-feature-domination), gives on each analytic test
\[
       V^2\le 2\|\xi\|_2 V+C_{12}m^4\|\xi\|_2^2/2 ,
\]
where \(V^2\) is the sum of the squared right slices of the two real components.
For one line the curvature term vanishes.
Solving the inequality, averaging the two analytic signs and decomposing a polynomial into its selfadjoint parts gives \(Cm^2\|\xi\|_2\) for both oriented original right slices.
The two-slice adjoint and graph argument yields the two full domains and the generator-first relative commutators.

### Returning to exact complex lattice gaps

For a finite label set on \(m\) parallel lines rotate and translate the configuration so that the lines are horizontal.
The norm ratios and budget are unchanged.
Replace each point \(c_i\) by the uniform horizontal segment of half-length \(h/8\) in its own spectral corner as in the [free-noise construction](Yin_C_F0339_v1.md#free-noise-in-each-spectral-corner).
The [scalar and inverse-gap budget estimates](Yin_C_F0339_v1.md#the-scalar-vacuum-of-the-spread-measure) give an actual relative coordinate with vacuum norm at most \(C B_c\).
They only require separation at least \(h\), since the kernel comparison errors \(Ch/|c_i-c_j|^2\) are summed over dyadic annuli using \(M_i\).
The spread marginal lies on the same parallel lines, so its mixed right slice costs at most \(C m^2 B_c\).

On each slot test the two-leg conditional expectation onto the old generated von Neumann algebra averages the reciprocal gap to
\[
      A_h(z)=\mathbb E\{(z+T_h)^{-1}\},\qquad |z|\ge h ,
\]
at the distinct center differences \(z=c_i-c_j\), with \(T_h\) the difference of independent scalar uniforms on \([-h/8,h/8]\).
It sends the derivative of each original slot cut to its indicator difference times this kernel.
The projected derivative on words is determined by that rule and zero at \(P\), and its right slice inherits the same bound by the [norm and graph approximation of the cuts](Yin_C_F0339_v1.md#recovering-the-discrete-derivative-on-circles).

To restore the exact reciprocal, at \(h=1\) put \(A=A_1\).
For \(|z|\ge1/2\), symmetry and \(|T_1|\le1/4\) give
\[
        z A(z)=\mathbb E(1-T_1^2/z^2)^{-1},\qquad
                 |z A(z)-1|\le 1/3 .
\]
In particular \(z A(z)\) is nonzero on that region.
Its expansion in even reciprocal powers converges with derivatives through order two uniformly there, with \(zA(z)-1=O(|z|^{-2})\) at infinity and each derivative gaining the appropriate inverse power.
The same decay holds for \((zA(z))^{-1}-1\) and its derivatives.
Extend the latter by multiplication with a fixed smooth radial cutoff equal to one for \(|z|\ge1\) and zero near \(|z|\le1/2\).
The extended \(g_0\) has bounded \(H^2(\mathbb R^2)\) norm, giving \(\|\widehat g_0\|_1\le C\) by Plancherel and the integrability of \((1+|\theta|^2)^{-2}\) on the plane.
The dilated multiplier \(1+g_0(z/h)\) has uniformly bounded Fourier measure and equals \(1/(z A_h(z))\) for every required gap.
Every planar Fourier character on center differences is the separated product of two unitary functions of the labels, which lie in \(D\).
By the [separated trace-product identity](Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#removing-the-cutoff-in-the-column-norm), each such insertion costs at most twice the one-input bound for the projected derivative.
The resulting derivative agrees exactly with \(d_c\) on the slots and on \(P\), and hence on all represented words.
This proves the claimed lattice estimate independently of the spacing ratios.
