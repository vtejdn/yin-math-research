# Quadratic polynomials with one missing square and the proportional commutator case

<a id="statement"></a>
## Real finite-variance inputs

Use the independent real fixed-law Wigner colors of [the oblique quadratic theorem](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#statement), with centered finite second moments and unit strict upper variances.
The planar conclusions of that theorem hold for its entire family
\[
 P=AB+CA+CU+VB+\ell(A,Y)
\]
whenever \(b,c\ne0\), including \(C=-B\) and \(\ell_A=0\).
Here \(A\) is an independent distinguished color, \(B,C,U,V\) are real linear forms of the other inputs \(Y\), and \(\ell\) is real affine.
The planar conclusions are almost-sure convergence of the ordinary normalized potential in local planar \(L^1\), almost-sure weak convergence of the empirical eigenvalue law to the free Brown measure under every coupling, and summability of the disk-integrated bottom-\(h_N\) negative-log deviations, \(h_N=\lceil\log(N+3)\log\log(N+3)\rceil\).
In the added \(C=-B,\ \ell_A=0\) case with nonconstant commutative scalar evaluation of \(P\), the pointwise nonreal log limits of the earlier theorem also hold.

In particular, for two independent real Wigner inputs the planar conclusions hold for every degree-two real polynomial with a missing square in its original colors:
\[
 P(x,y)=\alpha xy+\beta yx+\gamma y^2+\ell_1 x+\ell_2y+c
 \tag{1}
\]
and likewise with the variables exchanged.
At least one of the degree-two coefficients is nonzero.
No absolute moment above two or assumption on the support of the laws is imposed.
For example \(xy-yx+y^2\) is included although its commutative evaluation has no dependence on the first color.

<a id="argument"></a>
## A two-site test when scalar evaluation has no distinguished-variable slope

Only the case \(C=-B,\ \ell_A=0\) needs a new rational test.
Write \(P=[A,B]+P_0(Y)\), let \(f(y)\) be its commutative scalar evaluation, and first assume \(f\) is nonconstant.
The four kernels needed after rescaling are \(K_{ef}=\operatorname{sym}(B^e R_z B^f)\), \(e,f\in\{0,1\}\), \(R_z=(P-z)^{-1}\).
Take any finite tuple of distinct upper-half-plane shifts together with their conjugates.
If a complex linear combination of those four kernels over the tuple were scalar in the free field, its evaluations would satisfy the same identity wherever all denominators are invertible.
Use independent two-site diagonal background values \(y^{(1)},y^{(2)}\) with \(b_i=B(y^{(i)})\), \(s_i=f(y^{(i)})\); take \(A=t(E_{12}+E_{21})\) and set \(d=b_2-b_1\).
All background polynomials are diagonal at this specialization.
The actual resolvent is
\[
 R_z=\frac{1}{(s_1-z)(s_2-z)+t^2d^2}
 \begin{pmatrix}s_2-z&-td\\ td&s_1-z\end{pmatrix}.
\]
On a nonempty real open set of background choices \(d\ne0\) and \(s_1+s_2\ne z+z'\) for every distinct pair of nodes.
Indeed \(b\ne0\) and \(f\) nonconstant exclude an identity in each of the finitely many polynomial tests; nonreal sums that cannot equal a real number impose no constraint.
Also \(s_i-z\ne0\) for these nonreal nodes.
Write \(\alpha_z,\beta_z,\gamma_z,\delta_z\) for the coefficients of \(K_{00},K_{01},K_{10},K_{11}\).
The \(11\) site entry of their sum is
\[
 \sum_z\frac{(s_2-z)(\alpha_z+(\beta_z+\gamma_z)b_1+\delta_z b_1^2)}
                  {(s_1-z)(s_2-z)+t^2d^2}.
\]
It tends to zero for real \(t\to\infty\); hence the putative scalar is zero.
The poles as rational functions of \(t^2\) are distinct by the excluded-sum condition.
Residues at each node, then variation of \(b_1\) over an interval in the open set, give \(\alpha_z=\delta_z=0\), \(\beta_z+\gamma_z=0\).
The off-diagonal entry of \(K_{01}-K_{10}=[R_z-R_z^{\mathsf T},B]/2\) is \(-td^2/((s_1-z)(s_2-z)+t^2d^2)\).
Dividing by \(t\ne0\) and taking the same residues gives \(\beta_z=\gamma_z=0\).
The argument establishes the required independence modulo scalars on the entire compact separated family, by the same continuity in measure and compactness as in the [physical-corner transfer](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#kernels).
The proportional flag uses a nonzero multiple of \(\operatorname{sym}R_z\), which is among these directions.

All the other inputs to the [oblique restoration](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#restoration) hold at this coefficient.
The normal columns are proportional with nonzero factor, the two-dimensional update still has nonsingular exchange matrix, and the background quadratic tensor still vanishes on the prescribed exterior spaces.
The [initial trims and overlaps](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#statement) hold for the original real quadratic.
The separated-parameter quadratic and quartic tests therefore give the same area bounds as in [the terminal comparison](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#terminal).
This proves the planar statements; the single-parameter test, continuity of the undivided determinant inequality at exceptional poles and dyadic Borel--Cantelli prove the indicated prescribed nonreal assertions.

<a id="normal"></a>
## Constant commutative image

For a scalar real polynomial of degree at most two, constant commutative image means its linear coefficients and its symmetric quadratic coefficients vanish.
Thus \(P-c\) is a sum of real multiples of commutators and is skew-adjoint at every Hermitian tuple, including the free evaluation.
The selfadjoint polynomial \(-i(P-c)\) has almost-sure weak spectral convergence in the finite-variance model by the [selfadjoint polynomial weak law](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#finite-variance-weak).
Push the real measures forward by \(t\mapsto c+it\); the free evaluation is normal and its Brown measure is its spectral measure.
The [general real quadratic trim and terminal equivalence](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#statement) now gives the series.
To see local planar convergence directly from the same statements, the original and trimmed log sums differ by the bottom negative logarithms and the omitted positive logarithms.
The former tend to zero in disk integral by first Borel--Cantelli, and the positive contribution is \(O(h_N\log N/N)\) uniformly on a disk on the eventual Frobenius norm guard.

<a id="binary"></a>
## The binary classification with one diagonal quadratic coefficient zero

When \(\alpha\beta\ne0\) in (1), take the distinguished color \(A=x\), \(B=\alpha y,\ C=\beta y\).
The background square \(\gamma y^2\) has the required tensor form, for instance \(VB\) with \(V=(\gamma/\alpha)y\).
All real affine terms are allowed after the preceding two-site and normal cases.

If \(\alpha\ne0,\ \beta=0\), write \(P=x g(y)+f_0(y)\) with \(g(y)=\alpha y+\ell_1\) and \(f_0(y)=\gamma y^2+\ell_2 y+c\).
Polynomial division writes \(f_0(y)=q(y)g(y)+c_0\), so \(P=(x+q(y))g(y)+c_0\).
For \(\alpha=0,\ \beta\ne0\) the same computation places the factor \(g(y)\) on the left.
The [symmetric carrier theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v7.md#statement) applies since \(q,g\) depend on one real variable, \(g\) is a scalar multiple of \(y+\mathrm{const}\), and \(x\) is independent.
It proves weak Brown convergence for either product and its translate under each coupling.
If both mixed terms vanish, \(P\) is selfadjoint and the finite-variance selfadjoint weak law applies.
In each branch the same terminal equivalence and comparison with the trimmed logarithm give the planar series and the local \(L^1\) conclusion.
This covers exactly the stated missing-square condition on a binary polynomial; the step of eliminating an arbitrary fresh \(A^2\) coefficient with the two exterior normals remains open for the general polynomial.
