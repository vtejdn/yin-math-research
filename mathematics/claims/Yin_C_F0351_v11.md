# Brown convergence for the sum of two squares and their commutator

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two real finite-variance Wigner inputs

Let \(A,B\) be independent real symmetric Wigner inputs with the fixed centered finite-second-moment laws, unit strict upper variances and \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization of [the quadratic trim theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#statement).
Put \(P_N=A^2+B^2+AB-BA\), let \(p\) denote the same polynomial in two free standard semicirculars, and put \(u(z)=\tau\log|p-z|\).
Under every coupling across dimensions, almost surely \(N^{-1}\log|\det(P_N-\cdot I)|\to u\) in local planar \(L^1\) and the empirical eigenvalue measures converge weakly to the Brown measure of \(p\).
For \(h_N=\lceil\log(N+3)\log\log(N+3)\rceil\), decreasing singular values, and any \(R,\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \sum_{N:h_N<N}\Pr\left\{N^{-1}\int_{|z|<R}
       \sum_{j>N-h_N}\log^-s_j(P_N-zI)\,dA(z)>\epsilon\right\}<\infty .
\]
For each prescribed nonreal \(z\) the ordinary normalized log determinant converges to \(u(z)\) in probability in all dimensions and almost surely along \(N=2^j\); its all-dimension limsup is \(u(z)\) almost surely under every coupling.
The [missing-square family](Yin_C_F0351_v10.md#statement) retains its earlier scope.

## Keeping the fresh square as a second coordinate

Write
\[
 K(z)=\begin{pmatrix}AB-BA+B^2-zI&A\\A&-I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Schur complementation gives \(\det K=(-1)^N\det(P_N-zI)\).
Choose \(k=2\lfloor\log^3(N+3)/2\rfloor\) sites \(I\), let \(J=I^c,\ n=N-k,\ h=h_n\), and write \(A_0=A_{JJ},\ B_0=B_{JJ},\ V=B_{JI},\ T=P(A_0,B_0)-zI\) with the original scaling.
Use the two left normals \(F_{L,i}=[(e_i,0),(-Be_i,e_i)]\) and the two right normals \(F_{R,i}=[(e_i,0),(Be_i,e_i)]\) at each unexposed site.
For all \(i\in I\) their common left orthogonal complement has coordinates \(h_I=0,\ l_I=V^{\mathsf T}h_J\) on pairs \((h,l)\); on the right \(l_I=-V^{\mathsf T}h_J\).
In the resulting graph coordinates \((h_J,l_J)\), the full compressed bilinear form has top-left block
\[
 A_0B_0-B_0A_0+B_0^2-zI+2VV^{\mathsf T},
\]
off-diagonal blocks \(A_0\) and lower block \(-I\).
Indeed, with \(U=A_{JI}\), the physical top-left block contributes \(UV^{\mathsf T}-VU^{\mathsf T}+VV^{\mathsf T}\), the two off-diagonal graph insertions contribute \(-UV^{\mathsf T}+VU^{\mathsf T}\), and the lower block contributes \(VV^{\mathsf T}\).
Both graph Gram matrices are \(\operatorname{diag}(I+VV^{\mathsf T},I)\), so the absolute determinant of the initial compression in orthonormal frames is exactly
\[
     |\det(T+2VV^{\mathsf T})|/\det(I+V^{\mathsf T}V).
\]
The denominator costs at most \(O(k\log N)\) in the logarithm on the Frobenius norm guards \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le N^2\), of failure probability \(O(N^{-3})\).
The full normal maps are \((a,b)\mapsto (E_I a\pm BE_I b,E_I b)\), where \(E_I\) embeds the site coordinates.
Each map has least singular value at least \(c/(1+\|B\|)\), since its lower component determines \(b\) and the top \(I\) component then controls \(a\).
This bound persists for the distance of a site's normal pair from the span of all future pairs by taking the infimum over their coefficients.

<a id="initial"></a>
## The first positive rank perturbations

Put \(T_s=T+2V_sV_s^{\mathsf T}\), where \(V_s\) contains the first \(s\) columns of \(V\).
Work initially with invertible \(T\), and write \(t_1\le\cdots\le t_n\) for its singular values.
Use the largest \(1\le r\le2h\) such that \(t_r<N^{-a h}t_{r+1}\), taking \(r=0\) if there is none, and use the constants \(b=20D+10,\ a=10(b+2),\ D=12\) of the [initial-overlap proof](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#initial-overlap).
Write \(p_j(T)\) for the product of the largest \(j\) singular values.
On the [finite-variance rectangular floor](Yin_C_F0351_v8.md#cross-block), \(\sigma_{\min}(V)\ge c>0\) with summable exceptions, and this bound holds also for \(V_r\).
The overlap theorem allows identical real columns on both sides.
Let \(F_R,F_L\) be the bottom singular frames of \(T\), and \(Q_r\) an orthonormal frame for \(V_r\).
Except on the stated initial-overlap failures, the least singular values of \(F_L^*Q_r,Q_r^{\mathsf T}F_R\) are at least \(N^{-(b+2)r}\), since their other singular values are at most one; choose \(Q_r\) real.
Consequently the least singular values after replacing \(Q_r\) by \(V_r\) are at least \(cN^{-(b+2)r}\).
Moreover their two determinant moduli are each at least \(c^r N^{-(b+2)r}\) by the polar factorization of \(V_r\).

For \(r>0\) write \(T^{-1}=F_R\operatorname{diag}(t_i^{-1}:i\le r)F_L^*+H\), \(\|H\|\le t_{r+1}^{-1}\).
In \(\det T_r=\det T\det(I+2V_r^{\mathsf T}T^{-1}V_r)\), call the contribution of the first summand to the small matrix \(S\).
It is invertible, with \(\|S^{-1}\|\le C t_rN^{2(b+2)r}\) and
\[
 \|S^{-1}(I+2V_r^{\mathsf T}HV_r)\|
 \le C N^{2(b+2)r-a h}(t_{r+1}+2\|V_r\|^2)\le 1/2
\]
for large \(N\) on each bounded shift disk, since \(t_{r+1}\le C_R N^4,\ \|V_r\|\le N^2,\ r\le2h\).
This comparison includes the constant term and permits arbitrarily small \(t_{r+1}\).
Thus \(|\det T_r|\ge2^{-r}|\det T|\,|\det S|\ge c^{2r}N^{-2(b+2)r}p_{n-r}(T)\).
For \(r=0\) the last comparison is equality.
The adjacent gaps beyond \(r\) give
\[
 p_{n-r}(T)\ge p_{n-2h}(T)\min(1,t_{2h+1})^{2h}
                 \exp(-C_a h^3\log N).
\]
For subsequent columns \(v_i\), the determinant ratio is \(1+2v_i^{\mathsf T}T_{i-1}^{-1}v_i\) when the incoming determinant is nonzero.
Use the filtration of the old inputs and the successive columns of \(V\).
The adaptive \(r(z)\) depends only on the old minor.
The inverse at a regular incoming step differs from \(T^{-1}\) by rank at most \(k\).
On two disjoint site classes the mixed second difference in the raw \(N^{1/2}v_i\) removes the constant and tests the physical corner of \(\operatorname{sym}T^{-1}\), up to the rank correction and a fixed nonzero factor.
For \(d\) separated shifts assigned to that step, ratios below \(N^{-7}\) give raw mixed targets \(O(N^{-6})\); the [two-panel Fourier test](Yin_C_F0351_v6.md#border-tests) gives \(O(n^{-d/8})\) plus exponentially small exceptions using the kernel independence below.
The rectangular floor is charged once outside these tests, without conditioning on the future columns.

<a id="restoration"></a>
## Schur restoration of a whole physical row

Reveal \(B,A_0,A_{II}\), then expose \(u=A_{Ji}\), padded by zero outside \(J\), one site at a time.
With \(F_L,F_R\) the current normal pairs, put
\[
 G_R=[(Bu,u),(u,0)],\qquad G_L=[(-Bu,u),(u,0)] .
\]
Adding the new row and column to \(A\) gives exactly \(\Delta K=F_LG_R^{\mathsf T}+G_LF_R^{\mathsf T}\).
The four terms in its upper-left block give the commutator with \(e_i u^{\mathsf T}+u e_i^{\mathsf T}\); its off-diagonal blocks give this symmetric row update itself.
Future updates vanish between the spaces orthogonal to their normal pairs.
Write \(W_L,W_R\) for old orthonormal frames orthogonal to the current and future pairs, and \(J_L,J_R\) for orthonormal completions by the two directions restored at this site.
When the incoming compression is invertible, put \(R_{\rm old}=W_R(W_L^{\mathsf T}K W_R)^{-1}W_L^{\mathsf T}\); here the compression is measurable before exposing \(u\).
The new compressed determinant divided by the old one is a two-dimensional Schur determinant.
Its degree-four term in the normalized row is
\[
 \det(J_L^{\mathsf T}F_L)\det(J_R^{\mathsf T}F_R)
 \det(G_R^{\mathsf T}R_{\rm old}G_L).
\]
Indeed the quadratic part of the Schur block is \(-(J_L^{\mathsf T}F_L)G_R^{\mathsf T}R_{\rm old}G_L(F_R^{\mathsf T}J_R)\); the new diagonal block is affine.
The product of the normal factors has modulus at least \(cN^{-8}\) by the full normal-map bound, independently of \(u\).
In raw row coordinates the quartic carries the additional \(N^{-2}\).

Let \(K_0\) denote the two-channel matrix evaluated on the original \(J\) inputs.
The inverse is
\[
 K_0^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}R&RA_0\\A_0R&A_0RA_0-I\end{pmatrix},\qquad R=T^{-1}.
\]
Padded by identities on the two copies of \(I\), \(K_0\) differs from the full \(K\) by rank \(O(k)\), by replacing \(A,B\) with padded \(J\) blocks in each term and accounting for the \(I\) diagonal.
The full \(K\) can instead be taken at any values of the current and future rows since those updates vanish on the old compression.
One has \(\operatorname{rank}(I-KR_{\rm old})\le2k\) because the range of this residual is orthogonal to \(W_L\).
Thus \(R_{\rm old}\) differs from the padded \(K_0^{-1}\) by rank \(O(k)\), even if \(K\) is singular.
In the maps \(u\mapsto Bu\), replacing \(B\) by the padded \(B_0\) also costs at most \(O(k)\) rank.
After exchanging both column orders, the four symmetric quadratic kernels of \(G_R^{\mathsf T}R_{\rm old}G_L\) are therefore, modulo \(O(k)\) rank,
\[
 \operatorname{sym}R,\quad \operatorname{sym}(R(A_0-B_0)),\quad
 \operatorname{sym}((A_0+B_0)R),\quad
 \operatorname{sym}((A_0+B_0)R(A_0-B_0)-I).
\]
Transpose in \(\operatorname{sym}H=(H+H^{\mathsf T})/2\) retains the parameter.

## Four independent kernel directions

At commuting scalar values \(x,y\), put \(c=x+y,\ d=x-y,\ f=(c^2+d^2)/2\).
Modulo the scalar contribution \(-1\) in the last kernel, their specializations are \(1,d,c,cd\) divided by \(f-z\).
For distinct nodes, including their conjugates when nonreal, these denominators are pairwise relatively prime in \(\mathbb C[c,d]\).
Multiply any purported scalar relation by their product and reduce modulo \(f-z\) at each node.
The span of \(1,d,c,cd\) contains no nonzero multiple of \(f-z\), by comparison of the pure square terms and the degree.
All coefficients vanish, including when \(z=0\) occurs once.
A scalar relation in the semicircular free field would hold at regular commuting specializations, so this also proves independence modulo scalars there.
In particular for separated upper-half-plane tuples the entrywise real and imaginary symmetric kernels have independent real directions modulo scalars.

The [physical-corner argument](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#kernels) now applies with these four words.
The sole rational denominator is the fixed scalar polynomial \(P-z\), which is a nonzero free-field element.
Continuity in measure gives uniform free corner spread on compact separated tuples away from the real axis.
Clipping the two old input colors, regularizing the inverse and returning the clipped factors on the two sides to the original \(A_0\pm B_0\) use the same fixed-polynomial rank telescopes and finite-variance row concentration as in that argument.
The gain is a fixed positive fraction of singular values on every physical corner for every unit real combination, with bounded approximants and arbitrarily small error ranks relative to that fraction outside exponentially small old-minor exceptions.
The additional \(O(k)\) ranks from the changing restorations or initial positive perturbations fit inside this tolerance.
For the latter only the first kernel is needed.

At a restoration step use the [four-panel determinant test](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#restoration) with these four independent kernels.
Its quartic contraction has coefficients \(v_{00}w_{11}+v_{11}w_{00}-v_{01}w_{10}-v_{10}w_{01}\); all three pairings of the four independent resampled site panels occur.
The \(00\) corner controls how many first pair coefficients are small, and the \(11\) corner gives conditional spread for the other shifts after projecting away the images of the first two differences in the fourth panel.
Four Cartesian Cauchy--Schwarz differences eliminate all lower degrees under the original independent row law.
For \(d\) assigned separated shifts with regular incoming compression, a threshold \(N^{-12}\) for the ratios gives raw mixed targets \(O(N^{-2})\) after division by the normal factors and the quartic scaling.
The cited test bounds their joint probability by \(O(n^{-d/32})\) plus exponentially small exceptions.

## From the two filtrations to the ordinary potential

Define a small ratio event only with nonzero incoming determinant; a first zero after a good initial determinant is included.
In the initial perturbation filtration charge only steps above each shift's \(r\).
In the restoration filtration the guard for the normal factors uses \(B\) only, and the old-minor corner exceptions are charged separately in both filtrations.
For each filtration apply the [separated-tuple argument](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#terminal) with 64 upper-half-plane shifts, assigning each bad shift to one step.
Chronological conditioning and summation over assignments give \(Ck^{64}n^{-2}\) or smaller, plus summable guard failures; the two filtrations are combined by a union bound on their bad areas.
Reflection and removal of an arbitrarily thin real strip then give summability at every fixed positive area tolerance, including the separate initial overlap failures.
The finitely many poles of the original minor per realization have zero planar area.
On the remaining points, telescoping the \(k-r\) positive perturbations and the \(k\) paired restorations across their exact common graph determinant gives
\[
 |\det(P_N-zI)|\ \ge\
 e^{-C_R(h^3+k)\log N}\,p_{n-2h}(T)\min(1,t_{2h+1})^{2h}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The two [original quadratic trims](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#statement) now give the local \(L^1\) limit as in [the characteristic-potential argument](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#terminal).
In detail, the ordinary characteristic potential's negative part and the free potential's negative part have uniformly bounded local \(L^2\) norms by Jensen applied to the translated log kernel.
The free potential is bounded above on compact sets.
Cauchy--Schwarz thus controls the lower deficit on exceptional sets of vanishing area; on their complement (1) bounds it by the trimmed deficits and the adjacent log loss.
The full-matrix upper trim and its \(O(h_N\log N/N)\) omitted positive logarithms give the reverse bound.
Distributional Laplacians and total mass one give almost-sure weak convergence.
The [terminal equivalence](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#statement) gives the asserted series, by applying the coupling-uniform result to independent dimensions.

For a fixed nonreal parameter in a compact neighborhood away from the axis, the one-node [initial-overlap estimate](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#initial-overlap) costs \(O(h\sqrt{\log K_N/n})\), \(K_N=C\log^3(N+2)\), plus summable norm, rectangular and old-minor exceptions.
The remaining steps have total one-node error \(O(k n^{-1/32})\).
These bounds hold at almost every prescribed parameter by Fubini, uniformly on that compact neighborhood.
Both sides of (1) are continuous for every realization.
Taking nearby full-area parameters and using Fatou on the strict violation therefore transfers the same probability bound to every prescribed nonreal parameter.
All these errors vanish and are summable along powers of two.
At the specified point the moving two-trim bounds, the fixed-clip all-dimension upper bound and first Borel--Cantelli now give the pointwise conclusions.
