# Brown measures of nonsymmetric binary quadratics with invertible coefficient matrix

<a id="statement"></a>
## Planar and prescribed-parameter limits

Let \(A,B\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices with the fixed centered laws, unit strict upper variances, finite second moments and original \(N^{-1/2}\) scaling of the [quadratic trim theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#statement).
Let
\[
 P(x,y)=ax^2+bxy+cyx+dy^2+ex+fy+g,\qquad b\ne c,\quad ad-bc\ne0 ,
\]
with all coefficients real.
Write \(p=P(s_1,s_2)\) for its free standard semicircular evaluation, \(P_N=P(A,B)\), and \(u(z)=\tau\log|p-z|\).
Under every coupling across sizes, almost surely the ordinary potentials \(N^{-1}\log|\det(P_N-zI)|\) converge to \(u\) in local planar \(L^1\), and the empirical eigenvalue measures converge weakly to the Brown measure of \(p\).
With \(h_N=\lceil\log(N+3)\log\log(N+3)\rceil\) and decreasing singular values, for every \(R,\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \sum_{N>h_N}\Pr\left\{N^{-1}\int_{|z|<R}\sum_{j>N-h_N}\log^-s_j(P_N-zI)\,dA(z)>\epsilon\right\}<\infty .
\]

If the commutative evaluation of \(P\) is nonconstant, at each prescribed \(z\in\mathbb C\) its ordinary normalized log determinant tends to the finite \(u(z)\) in probability in all dimensions and almost surely along \(N=2^j\), and its almost-sure all-dimension limsup is \(u(z)\).
The event may depend on \(z\).
These assertions pass to the standard three-channel affine companion with the normalized trace factor \(1/3\).
If the commutative evaluation is constant, \(P=g+b[x,y]\), the planar assertions still hold and the ordinary potentials converge almost surely at each point off \(g+i\mathbb R\).
At \(z=g\) every odd-dimensional determinant vanishes.

## The initial graph and its rank perturbations

If one original square occurs, exchange the two input names when necessary so \(a\ne0\).
Set \(D=bB+eI/2,\ C=cB+eI/2,\ \lambda=d-bc/a\ne0\) and use
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}AD+CA+dB^2+fB+(g-z)I&A\\ A&-I/a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its absolute determinant equals \(|a|^{-N}|\det(P_N-zI)|\).
Choose the \(k=2\lfloor\log^3(N+3)/2\rfloor\) deleted sites \(I\), put \(J=I^c,\ n=N-k,\ h=h_n,\ A_0=A_{JJ}, B_0=B_{JJ}, V=B_{JI}, U=A_{JI}\) and \(T=P(A_0,B_0)-zI\).
The left normal pair at site \(i\) is \((e_i,0),(Ce_i,e_i)\), and the right pair uses \(D\) instead of \(C\).
In each common orthogonal complement the physical coordinate satisfies \(x_I=0\), while the auxiliary \(I\) coordinate equals \(-cV^{\mathsf T}x_J\) on the left and \(-bV^{\mathsf T}x_J\) on the right.
The extra top block before restriction is \(bUV^{\mathsf T}+cVU^{\mathsf T}+dVV^{\mathsf T}\).
The two off-diagonal graph terms cancel its mixed terms, and the lower block contributes \(-(bc/a)VV^{\mathsf T}\).
Thus the initial absolute determinant in orthonormal frames is
\[
 \frac{|a|^{-n}|\det(T+\lambda VV^{\mathsf T})|}
 {\{\det(I+c^2V^{\mathsf T}V)\det(I+b^2V^{\mathsf T}V)\}^{1/2}}.
\]
The denominator costs \(O(k\log N)\) in the logarithm on the common guard \(\|A\|,\|B\|\le N^2\), whose complement costs \(O(N^{-3})\).

For regular \(T\), use the largest adjacent singular gap among its first \(2h\) increasing singular values, with the adaptive rule and exponents \(A_*,B_*\) of the [first rank perturbation argument](Yin_C_F0351_v11.md#initial).
More precisely \(r\) is the largest \(1\le r\le2h\) with \(t_r<N^{-A_*h}t_{r+1}\), or zero if there is none.
The first \(r\) columns \(V_r\) have the required two marginal overlaps from [the quadratic theorem](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#initial-overlap) and the [rectangular lower bound](Yin_C_F0351_v8.md#cross-block).
In the determinant lemma let \(S\) be the contribution of the bottom \(r\) singular modes to \(\lambda V_r^{\mathsf T}T^{-1}V_r\), and \(H\) the complementary part of \(T^{-1}\).
For \(r>0\), \(\|S^{-1}\|\le C_\lambda t_r N^{2(B_*+2)r}\) and
\[
 \|S^{-1}(I+\lambda V_r^{\mathsf T}H V_r)\|
 \le C_\lambda N^{2(B_*+2)r-A_*h}(t_{r+1}+|\lambda|\|V_r\|^2)<1/2.
\]
Both overlap determinants have modulus at least \(c_0^rN^{-(B_*+2)r}\) in the unorthogonalized columns.
The leading determinant consequently gives \(|\det(T+\lambda V_rV_r^{\mathsf T})|\ge c_1^rN^{-2(B_*+2)r}p_{n-r}(T)\), where \(p_j\) denotes the largest \(j\) singular-value product.
For \(r=0\) the starting product is exact.
The remaining adjacent gaps give the factor \(p_{n-2h}(T)\min(1,t_{2h+1})^{2h}\) at a cost \(O(h^3\log N)\).
At every subsequent column the ratio is \(1+\lambda v^{\mathsf T}(T+\lambda V_sV_s^{\mathsf T})^{-1}v\) on a regular incoming determinant.
Its mixed second difference tests a nonzero fixed multiple of the corner of \(\operatorname{sym}T^{-1}\) up to rank \(O(k)\).

## Restoring a physical row

Expose \(B,A_0,A_{II}\), then the individual \(A_{Ji}\).
For the current row \(u=A_{Ji}\) padded by zero outside \(J\), use the displayed normal pairs as the column matrices \(F_L,F_R\), and put
\[
 G_R=[(Du,u),(u,0)],\qquad G_L=[(Cu,u),(u,0)] .
\]
The exact update is \(F_LG_R^{\mathsf T}+G_LF_R^{\mathsf T}\), by substituting \(e_i u^{\mathsf T}+u e_i^{\mathsf T}\) for the increment of \(A\) in \(K\).
Future updates vanish in the old compression.
Each full normal map \((v,w)\mapsto(E_Iv+CE_Iw,E_Iw)\), or its right version using \(D\), has least singular value at least \(c_2/(1+\|B\|)\).
The auxiliary component recovers \(w\) and the physical \(I\) component then controls \(v\).
Minimizing over future normal coefficients preserves this floor for the current pair.
The [two-dimensional Schur calculation](Yin_C_F0351_v11.md#restoration) therefore gives the quartic term of the determinant ratio as the product of two normal determinants and \(\det(G_R^{\mathsf T}R_{\rm old}G_L)\), where \(R_{\rm old}\) is its framed incoming inverse.
The product of normal determinants has modulus at least \(c_3N^{-8}\) on the background guard, and the four raw entries contribute \(N^{-2}\).

Put \(D_0=bB_0+eI/2,\ C_0=cB_0+eI/2,\ H_L=aA_0+D_0,\ H_R=aA_0+C_0,\ R=T^{-1}\).
The inverse of the original \(J\)-site pencil \(K_0\) has blocks \(R,aRA_0,aA_0R,a^2A_0RA_0-aI\) in row order.
Padding \(K_0\) by identities on the removed sites changes the full pencil by rank \(O(k)\).
For the incoming inverse \(\operatorname{rank}(I-KR_{\rm old})\le2k\), since the range is orthogonal to the incoming left frame.
Thus replacing \(R_{\rm old}\) by the padded inverse, and the factors \(C,D\) by their old blocks on \(J\), costs rank \(O(k)\) in each quadratic kernel.
After interchanging both column orders their four kernels are
\[
 \operatorname{sym}R,\quad\operatorname{sym}(RH_R),\quad
 \operatorname{sym}(H_LR),\quad\operatorname{sym}(H_LRH_R-aI),
 \qquad \operatorname{sym}M=(M+M^{\mathsf T})/2 .
\]

## Independent kernels and the determinant comparison

At scalar inputs write \(L=ax+by+e/2,\ H=ax+cy+e/2,\ F=P(x,y)\) in commuting variables.
The four numerators, after dropping the last additive scalar, are \(1,H,L,LH\) over \(F-z\).
They are linearly independent because the affine coordinates \(L,H\) are independent.
Their span contains no nonzero multiple of \(F-z\).
Indeed such a multiple has constant multiplier by degree, and comparing the \(x^2\) coefficients forces its quadratic part to identify \(aF_2\) with \(L_1H_1\), where subscripts denote homogeneous components.
Their difference is \((ad-bc)y^2\ne0\).
For distinct nodes multiply a potential scalar relation by the product of the \(F-z\) and reduce modulo each \(F-z\), using their pairwise constant differences.
All coefficients must vanish, also for one real node and at a reducible fiber.
Regular scalar specializations thus exclude the free rational relations required by the [physical corner test](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#kernels).
Use all nodes and conjugates on separated upper-half-plane tuples and just the four real kernels at a real node.
The inverses exist in the free field since \(P-z\ne0\).
The same physical proof applies to the displayed fixed affine factors, by clipping and regularizing \(R\), replacing each factor at the cost of its rank discrepancy, and allowing the \(O(k)\) incoming rank errors.
Compactness and continuity in measure give uniform corner thresholds for the prescribed separated tuples or single real nodes in a compact interval.

The [two-panel and four-panel tests](Yin_C_F0351_v11.md#kernels) therefore give \(O(n^{-s/8})\) and \(O(n^{-s/32})\), respectively, for \(s\) assigned separated shifts at one regular transition, up to exponentially small exceptions.
The former tests the nonzero \(\lambda\) quadratic coefficient, and the latter tests the same determinant contraction with its \(00\) and \(11\) kernels in the displayed order.
Cutoffs \(N^{-7}\) and \(N^{-12}\) give raw mixed targets \(O(N^{-6})\) and \(O(N^{-2})\), respectively.
The initial column tests condition on the old minor and earlier columns, and the restorations condition on the background and earlier restored rows.
Pay the common rectangular error separately and charge the first zero outgoing determinant as a small ratio.
By the [separated-tuple argument](Yin_C_F0351_v11.md#terminal), 64 shifts give \(O(k^{64}n^{-2})\) in each filtration plus summable guard errors, implying summable positive bad-area deviations.
Telescoping across the graph, including \(|a|^{k}\), yields outside those bad sets
\[
 |\det(P_N-zI)|\ \ge e^{-C_R(h^3+k)\log N}\,
 p_{n-2h}(T)\min(1,t_{2h+1})^{2h}. \tag{1}
\]
The [quadratic trims](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#statement), the local \(L^2\) bound for the negative characteristic logarithms and the [terminal comparison](Yin_C_F0351_v11.md#terminal) give both planar limits and the series.
For a single nonreal node the same comparison gives the stated pointwise conclusions by the one-node estimates there.

## Principal trims at every real shift

We extend the [bounded-center joint gap](Yin_C_F0357_v1.md#joint) to the possible Rayleigh quotients of all unit vectors in the deterministic principal minors of size \(l=N-o(N)\) used to grow the two trims.
For either single input \(X\) at that size the expected normalized count in \([\alpha-2t,\alpha+2t]\) is \(O(t)\) at \(t=N^{-\gamma}\) for sufficiently small fixed \(\gamma>0\), uniformly over all real \(\alpha\).
To see the uniformity, the [scalar dual-system inequality (2)](Yin_C_F0352_v1.md#dual) gives \(r^2(l/N)\le 4s^2/\sigma^2\) for the free singular mass \(r\) at \(s\) with arbitrary scalar drift \(-\alpha\) and bounded frozen background, since that drift commutes with right creation.
Here \(\sigma>0\) is the fixed noise strength from the conditional replacement.
Layer integration gives the smoothed free mass \(O(t)\), uniformly in the drift and truncation.
In the [conditional finite comparison](Yin_C_F0195_v4.md#bounded-core) the derivative and covariance errors use only the variable coefficients and the selfadjoint resolvent bound, as does the [free-background comparison](Yin_C_F0195_v4.md#free-background).
Thus in [the polynomial-window inequality (2)](Yin_C_F0186_v40.md#argument) one can take the background cutoff \(M=t^{-1}\) and use the preceding uniform scalar free bound.
The finite errors \(O(l^{-1/2}t^{-3}+l^{-1}t^{-4}+t^2)\) are \(O(t)\) for sufficiently small \(\gamma\), also at \(2t\).

On the single Frobenius guard all Rayleigh quotients lie in \([-N^2,N^2]\).
Use a \(t\)-mesh there for \(\alpha\) and a sufficiently small fixed mesh for \(\beta\) on the same interval.
Row-rank concentration on the first mesh gives ranks at most \(l/\log^2N\) in every slice of width \(4t\) except with \(O(N^{3+\gamma}e^{-cN/\log^4N})\) over the nested minors.
Conditioning on the first input, apply the [independent-rectangle test and net argument](Yin_C_F0357_v1.md#joint) for every \(\beta\) on the enlarged mesh.
Each fixed real unit vector in a slice has a fixed escape probability on each of a positive fraction of independent rows, uniformly in their shifts.
The \(N^{-3}\)-nets cost \(\exp(O(l/\log N))\), and the added center meshes cost only polynomial factors.
The net extension on the Frobenius guard still costs \(O(N^{-1})\) because \(\|Y-\beta I\|\le2N^2+O(1)\).
Real and imaginary parts give the same norm lower bound on the complexified slice.
A unit vector with \(\|(X-\alpha)v\|\le N^{-3}\) lies within \(N^{-3}/t\) of the slice at the nearest center, costing \(O(N^{-1+\gamma})\) in the second residual.
Consequently, outside summable failures, for all these minors and unit complex \(v\),
\[
 \|(I-vv^*)Xv\|+\|(I-vv^*)Yv\|\ge N^{-3}.
\]

For the standard companion take \(Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)=UV,\ U=I_2,V=Q\), with first diagonal entry \(eX+fY+(g-z)I\), first block row \( (X,Y)U\), first block column \(V(X,Y)^{\mathsf T}\) below it, and auxiliary block \(-I_2\).
Both factors and their transposes have bounded fixed inverses.
The [directional calculation](Yin_C_F0357_v1.md#real-trim) now gives the same \(N^{-8}\) raw floor with residual \(N^{-12}\) for the two companion orientations.
Projecting off the physical line removes all affine scalar-direction terms, and invertibility of \(Q\) transfers the displayed lower bound to the two column combinations.
For the small leading direction the injective factor \(U\) dominates on \(\|X\|,\|Y\|\le N^2\), also with the fixed linear coefficients.
The calculation no longer requires a bound on the Rayleigh quotients from the quadratic form of \(P\).
The [paired border induction and initialization](Yin_C_F0186_v42.md#old-trim) therefore prove both real trims and the adjacent log loss at \(n=N-k,N\), just as in the cited real-trim proof.
The compact real shift family consists of full companions since \(P-z\ne0\).

## One-node restoration and the two absent squares

At a regular real node the [initial real overlap test](Yin_C_F0357_v1.md#real-restoration) applies with \(P-z\) and its reversal, whose fixed quadratic coefficient supplies the top word for the sparse-vector bound.
Its error is \(O(h\sqrt{\log K_N/n})\), \(K_N=C\log^3(N+2)\), plus summable guards.
The just-proved real kernel independence gives one-node errors \(O(kn^{-1/32})\) for the subsequent two filtrations.
Almost every real shift is regular almost surely for the old minor by Fubini, and continuity of both sides of (1) transfers its strict-violation probability bound by Fatou to each exceptional prescribed point as well.
The errors vanish and are summable on powers of two.
The real trims and the fixed-clip upper bound now imply all three assertions at each prescribed real shift, by [the pointwise comparison](Yin_C_F0357_v1.md#limits).

If both original squares are absent, \(bc\ne0\) and the [missing-square theorem](Yin_C_F0351_v10.md#statement) gives all planar assertions.
When \(F\) is nonconstant, choose the distinguished color \(x\) so that \(b+c\ne0\) or \(e\ne0\), interchanging the names if necessary.
Then the [proportional exterior argument](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#statement) gives the nonreal statements.
At an individual real \(z\), apply its [kernel proof](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#kernels) at the scalar pole in \(x\) with fixed \(y\) satisfying \((b+c)y+e\ne0\).
Varying \(y\) forces the coefficients of \(1,y,y^2\) to vanish, leaving the difference of the two middle kernels after rescaling.
Their commutator derivative at a diagonal two-site input with distinct background values is a nonzero multiple of \((y_1-y_2)^2/((F_1-z)(F_2-z))\) when \(b\ne c\), choosing both denominators regular.
This gives four real directions modulo scalars and a nonscalar flag kernel \(\operatorname{sym}R\).
The same real sparse-word overlap test, the [two-direction determinant comparison](Yin_C_F0351_v9.md#terminal), and the real trims just proved apply with these kernels, with the same one-node errors and continuation at exceptional shifts.
For the standard companion, finite and free unit triangular Schur elimination identifies its absolute determinant with the scalar one in physical normalization, giving the factor \(1/3\) for the normalized log.
Fullness and the [free power bound](Yin_C_F0352_v1.md#statement) give finiteness at every shift.

For constant \(F\), \(P_N-gI\) is real skew-symmetric, as is its transpose-negative polynomial identity in the free evaluation.
Off the indicated vertical line the singular values of either normal evaluation are bounded below by \(|\operatorname{Re}z-g|\), so the [fixed-clip limit](Yin_C_F0186_v4.md#regularized-potentials) gives ordinary convergence.
Odd skew-symmetric matrices have zero determinant by transposition.
