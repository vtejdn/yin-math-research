# Uniform lower-tail estimates for full affine pencils under free semicircular perturbations

<a id="statement"></a>
## A power bound with a tracial second-moment constant

Fix \(m,g\ge1\) and a compact family \(\mathcal A\) of complex coefficient tuples \((A_0,\ldots,A_g)\) for which each \(L_A=A_0+\sum_{j=1}^g A_jx_j\) is full as an \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrix over the complex free polynomial algebra.
Fullness means that no polynomial factorization passes through fewer than \(m\) columns.
Take bounded selfadjoint \(D_1,\ldots,D_g\) in any finite tracial von Neumann algebra with faithful normalized trace \(\tau\).
In its reduced tracial free product with a standard free semicircular system \(s_1,\ldots,s_g\), put \(X_j=D_j+\sigma_j s_j\) for positive \(\sigma_j\).
Let \(K=1+\max_j(\|D_j\|_{2,\tau}+\sigma_j+\sigma_j^{-1})\).
There exist \(C,h>0\) depending only on the coefficient family and the fixed sizes, and \(\beta_m>0\) depending only on \(m\), such that for \(0<t\le1\),
\[
 (\operatorname{Tr}_m\otimes\tau)\big(\mathbf1_{[0,t]}(|L_A(X)|)\big)
 \le C K^h t^{\beta_m}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Tr}_m\) is unnormalized.
One choice is \(\beta_1=1/2\) and \(\beta_m=\beta_{m-1}/(4\beta_{m-1}+4)\) for \(m\ge2\).
The \(D_j\) may be dependent and their operator norms need not be bounded in terms of \(K\).
The normalized smoothed mass \((\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau)(t^2/(|L_A(X)|^2+t^2))\) obeys the same bound with a different constant.
In particular the free logarithmic determinant is finite.
The estimate applies when the \(D_j\) are deterministic matrices of growing size with a common normalized Hilbert--Schmidt bound.
It supplies a free comparison input for [the Wigner determinant task](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract).

<a id="dual"></a>
## A bounded commutator in the larger free product

Use the reduced free-product Hilbert space with vacuum vector \(\Omega\), taking the first algebra to be generated by the \(D_j\).
The semicircular Hilbert space is full Fock space, with \(s_i\) acting by left creation plus left annihilation.
A convenient reduced word consists of semicircular letters in their Fock basis and centered first-algebra letters from \(L^2(W^*(D),\tau)\ominus\mathbb C\Omega\), with no consecutive first-algebra letters.
Consecutive semicircular letters encode one Fock word.
Right appending a semicircular letter \(j\) defines an isometry \(R_j\), by the orthogonal tensor decompositions of these word spaces.
It commutes with left multiplication by every \(D_i\).
Indeed on a word beginning with a first-algebra letter this multiplication separates its product into the centered and scalar parts, with either result unaffected by the appended letter.
On a word beginning with a semicircular letter, or on the vacuum, the left scalar and centered creation terms commute with appending as well.
The left creation in \(s_i\) commutes with \(R_j\).
Left annihilation removes a leading semicircular \(i\), vanishes on words starting with a first-algebra letter, and its commutator with \(R_j\) is \(\delta_{ij}P_\Omega\), as seen on the empty word.
These operations can first be checked on bounded first-algebra letters, a dense subspace.
Thus \([X_i,R_j]=\sigma_i\delta_{ij}P_\Omega\), where \(P_\Omega\) is the rank-one vacuum projection.
This realizes the [bounded dual system relation](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U005.md#msy23-u005) in the ambient free product, including the arbitrary deterministic shift algebra.

Write \(L=L_A(X)\), \(p=\mathbf1_{[0,t]}(|L|)\), \(q=\mathbf1_{[0,t]}(|L^*|)\), and \(P=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)p,\ Q=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)q\).
Both projections have the same unnormalized trace \(r=\operatorname{Tr}P=\operatorname{Tr}Q\), by polar decomposition including the kernels.
Let \(J:\mathbb C^m\to\mathbb C^m\otimes L^2\) be vacuum inclusion.
Then \(J^*pJ=P,\ J^*qJ=Q\).
Because \(\|Lp\|,\|qL\|\le t\), the identity \([L,I\otimes R_j]=\sigma_j J A_j J^*\) yields \(\|q J A_j J^* p\|\le2t/\sigma_j\).
This operator has Hilbert-space rank at most \(m\).
Its squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(P A_j^* Q A_j)\le 4m\,t^2/\sigma_j^2 .
 \tag{2}
\]
Only \(L,p,q\) belong to the amplified tracial algebra in this argument; the auxiliary isometry and vacuum projection act on its Hilbert space.

<a id="full-minors"></a>
## Compact families of restrictions

The dimension induction below quantifies the full-submatrix argument in [MSY23, Theorem 3.1, implication (ii) to (iii)](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U002.md#ii-implies-iii).
For any \(m\)-by-\((m-1)\) complex isometry \(V\), the rectangular pencil \(L_A V\) has full column rank over the free field because \(L_A\) is invertible there.
The [full submatrix criterion](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U001.md#definitions-and-elementary-matrix-arguments) gives a row selector \(E:\mathbb C^m\to\mathbb C^{m-1}\) such that \(E L_A V\) is a full square affine pencil.
Equivalently, complete \(V\) to a unitary and apply MSY23, Proposition 2.7 to the first \(m-1\) columns.
Full square affine tuples form an open set.
Indeed the [linear hollow criterion](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U001.md#msy23-u001) characterizes nonfull tuples by a pair of subspaces of dimensions with sum larger than the coefficient size, between which the compression of every coefficient, including the constant, vanishes.
For fixed dimensions this is a closed incidence condition on the tuple and two compact Grassmannians.
Projecting away the Grassmannians preserves closedness and there are finitely many dimension pairs.
For each pair \((A,V)\) the same selector consequently works in a neighborhood whose closure in the product of \(\mathcal A\) and the isometry space still works.
Finitely many smaller such neighborhoods cover that compact product, so the resulting selected subpencils range over finitely many compact families of full pencils.
The same argument applies to the compact adjoint family \((A_0^*,\ldots,A_g^*)\), which remains full.
In particular the constants from any uniform induction hypothesis of size \(m-1\) can be chosen once for all these selections.

<a id="mass-transfer"></a>
## A missing auxiliary direction gives small values of a smaller pencil

Use \(\mathcal T=\operatorname{Tr}_m\otimes\tau\).
Compactness of the coefficients and the tracial triangle inequality give \(\|L\|_{2,\mathcal T}\le C_0 K\), also for the adjoint.
Suppose the theorem holds at size \(m-1\) with exponent \(b=\beta_{m-1}\), and write \(h_0\) for an upper bound on the finitely many powers of \(K\) needed there.
Increase the constants so the inductive bounds also hold for thresholds larger than one by the trivial total-mass estimate.

Fix a unit auxiliary vector \(u\), set \(O=uu^*\otimes1\), and put \(\lambda=u^*Pu=\mathcal T(pOp)\).
Use \(V\) with range \(u^\perp\), suppressing tensor identities on finite-dimensional maps, and choose the full row restriction \(B=E L V\) above.
If \(r=0\) there is nothing to bound.
For \(r>0\) choose \(M=2C_0 K/\sqrt r\) and \(0<\delta\le1/2\).
Remove from \(p\) the spectral subspace of \(pOp\) above \(\delta^2\) and intersect the remaining range with \(\ker(\mathbf1_{(M,\infty)}(|L|)O)\).
The resulting projection \(p'\le p\) has trace at least \(r-\lambda/\delta^2-r/4\).
Indeed polar decomposition bounds the trace codimension of the second kernel by \(\mathcal T(\mathbf1_{(M,\infty)}(|L|))\le C_0^2 K^2/M^2\), and intersection costs at most the sum of the removed traces.
On \(p'\) one has \(\|Op'\|\le\delta\) and \(\|LOp'\|\le M\delta\), since \(Op'\) lies in the spectral subspace of \(|L|\) at most \(M\).
The map \(V^*\) on this range is bounded below by \(\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\).
Its closed range has trace \(\mathcal T(p')\) in the smaller matrix algebra, by polar decomposition with unnormalized matrix traces.
On this range the norm of \(B\) is at most \((t+M\delta)/\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\), from \(VV^*=1-O\).
The spectral dimension bound follows by intersection with the spectral subspace of \(|B|\) strictly above this threshold.
Consequently
\[
 r\le r/4+\lambda/\delta^2+C_1 K^{h_0}\{2(t+M\delta)\}^b. \tag{3}
\]

If \(r\le C_2 K^{h_0}t^b\), the target power in size \(m\) already holds because \(\beta_m\le b\).
Otherwise choose \(\delta=c (r/K^{h_0})^{1/b}/M\).
The constant \(c>0\) can be fixed small enough that \(\delta\le1/2\), since \(r\le m\) and \(K\ge1\).
For \(C_2\) large and \(c\) small, the last term in (3) is at most \(r/2\).
It follows for every unit \(u\) that
\[
 u^*Pu\ge c_1 K^{-2-2h_0/b}r^{2+2/b}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Apply the same reasoning to \(L^*\) to obtain (4) for \(Q\), enlarging \(C_2,C_1\) and decreasing \(c_1\) for both compact families.
The estimate uses only the tracial second moment of the original pencil to control \(LOp'\), even when a shift has large operator norm.

<a id="induction"></a>
## Quantitative induction

Write \(a=\max_{j\ge1}\|A_j\|\).
Uniformly over the compact full family, either \(a\ge a_0>0\) or the least singular value of \(A_0\) is at least a fixed \(c_0>0\).
Otherwise a convergent subsequence would give a nonfull constant pencil in the family.
For \(t\) above a sufficiently small fixed threshold the conclusion follows from \(r\le m\).
For small \(t\), if \(a<\sqrt t\) then \(a<a_0,\ t<c_0/2\).
On the range of \(p\), the operator \(L-A_0\) is bounded below by \(c_0-t\).
As \(\|L-A_0\|_{2,\mathcal T}\le C aK\), this gives \(r\le C K^2 t\).

It remains to treat \(a\ge\sqrt t\).
At size one, \(P=Q=r\), so (2) for a maximizing color gives \(r\le 2t/(\sigma_j a)\le2K t^{1/2}\).
This proves the base case.
At higher sizes, unless the small-\(r\) alternative immediately following (3) applies, use (4) for \(P,Q\) on the left of (2).
Positivity gives
\[
 c K^{-4-4h_0/b}r^{4+4/b}a^2
 \le \operatorname{Tr}(P A_j^* Q A_j)
 \le 4m K^2 t^2
\]
for a maximizing color.
As \(a^2\ge t\), this gives (1) with \(\beta_m=1/(4+4/b)\) and a finite enlarged power of \(K\).
All constants depend only on the stipulated family and sizes.
To obtain the smoothed bound, integrate the distribution function in (1) against \(2t^2x/(x^2+t^2)^2\), using the trivial count above one.
Since \(0<\beta_m<2\) the result is \(O(CK^h t^{\beta_m})\).
Integration of the lower tail against \(dx/x\) gives a finite negative logarithm; boundedness of the evaluated tuple gives a finite positive logarithm.
Neither weak convergence of random matrices nor a comparison with their individual small singular values is part of this free estimate.
