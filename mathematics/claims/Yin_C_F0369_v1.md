# Anticommutator duals and obstructions to quadratic selfadjoint simplifications

Task: [T_F0003](../tasks/Yin_T_F0003_v2.md#contract).
Provenance: project deduction from the standard tracial free-product model and the bounded-defect affiliated rationality criterion.
The statements concern specific quadratic candidates and specific classes of witnesses; they neither establish rational-closure equality for the anticommutator pair nor exclude every quadratic counterexample.

## Statements and scope

<a id="statement"></a>

Let h have distribution dmu(t)=4t^3 dt on (0,1), let b be a standard variance-one semicircular element free from h, and let M=W*(h,b), with its faithful normal tracial probability state tau.
Write H=L2(M,tau), Omega=1, Y=hb+bh, and A=C<h,Y>.
Rat denotes matrix rational closure in its specified ambient algebra, and Div denotes scalar division closure.
All tuple coordinates considered below are selfadjoint; the polynomial algebras generated by them are therefore already closed under adjoints.

1. The pair (h,Y) generates M and has bounded operators D_h,D_Y on all H satisfying
   \[
   [h,D_h]=[Y,D_Y]=P_\Omega,\qquad
   [h,D_Y]=[Y,D_h]=0,
   \]
   with norm bounds 4pi+8 and 2, respectively.
   In fact C*(h,Y)=C*(h,b).
2. The bounded element b does not belong to Rat_{L0(M)}(A).
   More generally, let V_1 be the linear space of polynomials
   \[
   p(h)+\sum_{j=1}^m p_j(h)bq_j(h),
   \qquad p,p_j,q_j\in\mathbb C[t].
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \operatorname{Rat}_{L^0(M)}(A)\cap V_1=A\cap V_1.
   \]
   For the displayed expression, membership is equivalent to divisibility of Q(s,t)=sum_j p_j(s)q_j(t) by s+t in C[s,t].
3. The pair (h,i[h,b]) admits no bounded dual system on H.
   Indeed there is no bounded D with [h,D]=0 and [i[h,b],D]=P_Omega.
4. For every selfadjoint quadratic expression
   \[
   Z=p(h)+\alpha(hb+bh)+i\beta(hb-bh)+\gamma b+\delta b^2,
   \]
   where p is a real polynomial of degree at most two and alpha,beta,gamma,delta are real, let
   \[
   \xi_\varepsilon=
   \frac{1_{(0,\varepsilon]}(h)\Omega}
        {\sqrt{\tau(1_{(0,\varepsilon]}(h))}}.
   \]
   For every c in C,
   \[
   \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\|(Z-c)\xi_\varepsilon\|_2^2
   =|p(0)+\delta-c|^2+
     \int|(\alpha+i\beta)t+\gamma|^2\,d\mu(t)+\delta^2.
   \]
   Thus these vectors can be a common approximate scalar vector for h and Z only if all b-dependent coefficients vanish.
   This excludes the original sandwich example's particular small-spectral-vector mechanism in this quadratic class, not all mechanisms for quadratic counterexamples.

## Complete argument

<a id="argument"></a>

### The anticommutator pair

Let h have law dmu(t)=4t^3 dt on (0,1), let b be a standard semicircular element free from h, and put Y=hb+bh.
Use the standard tracial model of [C_F0012 Section 1](Yin_C_F0012_v1.md#1-an-explicit-tracial-fock-model): K=L2(mu), H_n=K^{bar tensor(n+1)}, H the orthogonal sum of the H_n, h acting on the first factor, b=ell+ell*, and Omega=1 in H_0.
Let rho denote bounded right multiplication.

Define
\[
 F(s,t)=\frac{1_{t\le s}}{s+t},\qquad
 (T_F\zeta)(s)=\int F(s,t)\zeta(s,t)\,d\mu(t).
\]
Since
\[
 \int |F(s,t)|^2\,d\mu(t)
 \le s^{-2}\int_0^s4t^3\,dt=s^2\le1,
\]
T_F is bounded from K bar tensor K to K with norm at most one.
Let R vanish on H_0 and act as I^{bar tensor(n-1)} bar tensor T_F on H_n, n>=1.
Different input grades have orthogonal output grades, so R is bounded with norm at most one.
It commutes with h: at grade one the multiplication by h stays on the retained variable s, and on higher grades the first factor is untouched.

The commutator [R,ell] is zero on positive grades and is the integral operator B_F with kernel F on grade zero.
Both orders in [R,ell*] vanish on grades zero and one; on higher grades first-factor contraction commutes with the terminal merge.
Consequently, for the bounded skewadjoint operator K_Y=R-R*,
\[
 [K_Y,h]=0,\qquad [K_Y,b]=B,
 \qquad B(s,t)=\frac1{s+t}\quad\hbox{on }H_0,
\]
and B is zero on the other grades.
The diagonal of the kernel is irrelevant because mu has no atoms.
It follows that
\[
 [K_Y,Y]=hB+Bh=P_\Omega.
\]

Let Dhat_h be the last-factor lift of the scalar bounded dual from [C_F0012 Section 3](Yin_C_F0012_v1.md#3-a-bounded-dual-for-h-and-its-bounded-correction).
Thus [Dhat_h,h]=P_Omega, [Dhat_h,b]=0, and its norm is at most 4pi.
Set
\[
 E_Y=K_Y,\qquad
 E_h=\widehat D_h-\rho(b)K_Y-K_Y\rho(b).
\]
Then [E_h,h]=P_Omega, [E_Y,h]=0, [E_Y,Y]=P_Omega, and
\[
 [E_h,Y]=P_\Omega b+bP_\Omega
             -\rho(b)P_\Omega-P_\Omega\rho(b)=0.
\]
The two vacuum identities use rho(b)Omega=bOmega and traciality.
The required generator-first duals are D_h=-E_h and D_Y=-E_Y.
They are bounded on all H, with norm bounds 4pi+8 and 2, respectively.

### The anticommutator already recovers b in the generated C*-algebra

For a bounded real function a of h, the Fock creation formula gives
\[
 \|a\ell a\|\le\|a\|_2\|a\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 \|aba\|\le2\|a\|_2\|a\|_\infty.
\]
Indeed a ell a sends an elementary tensor to a prepended a followed by the first input factor multiplied by a; its adjoint is a ell* a.
For T>0, norm integration gives an element of C*(h,Y):
\[
 b_T=\int_0^T e^{-th}Y e^{-th}\,dt
     =b-e^{-Th}b e^{-Th}.
\]
Here the identity is the integral of the norm derivative of e^{-th}b e^{-th}.
Since h has no zero spectral projection, dominated convergence gives ||e^{-Th}||_2 ->0.
The preceding estimate implies b_T ->b in operator norm.
Therefore C*(h,Y)=C*(h,b), and in particular W*(h,Y)=M.
This is a norm-closure fact, not a bounded rational representation of b.

### The witness b is not affiliated rational in the anticommutator pair

The integral operator B with kernel 1/(s+t) has infinite rank.
To see this without a formal point evaluation, fix t_0 in (0,1) and average over shrinking intervals I around t_0.
For eta_I=1_I/mu(I), B eta_I converges uniformly on [0,1] to s ->1/(s+t_0), hence converges in K.
If B had finite rank its range would be closed, so it would contain this function for every t_0.
For distinct t_1,...,t_m, the functions 1/(s+t_j) are linearly independent in K: an a.e. vanishing rational combination vanishes identically on (0,1), and its distinct poles at -t_j force every coefficient to vanish.
Thus the range cannot be finite dimensional.

The pair h,Y is selfadjoint, generates M, and has the bounded duals above.
[The bounded-element consequence (5) of C_F0013](Yin_C_F0013_v1.md#1-statement-and-exact-scope) therefore applies, after its recorded sign conversion:
\[
 \operatorname{Div}_{L^0(M)}\mathbb C\langle h,Y\rangle\cap M
 =\{f\in M:\operatorname{rank}[E_h,f],
                   \operatorname{rank}[E_Y,f]<\infty\}.
\]
Since [E_Y,b]=B has infinite rank, b is not in this affiliated division closure.
The same accepted input identifies this closure F with a division subring of L0(M).
It is matrix rationally closed: if a matrix over F were singular over F, a nonzero F-kernel vector would contradict its invertibility over L0(M).
Thus any matrix over F invertible over L0(M) has its inverse over F, and
\[
 b\notin\operatorname{Rat}_{L^0(M)}\mathbb C\langle h,Y\rangle.
\]
This decisively excludes b as a counterexample witness for this pair, rather than merely failing to find its rational expression.
It does not decide the closure equality for this pair.

### An exact obstruction for witnesses of b-degree at most one

Take a polynomial
\[
 f=p(h)+\sum_{j=1}^m p_j(h)bq_j(h),
 \qquad Q(s,t)=\sum_jp_j(s)q_j(t).
\]
Its commutator [K_Y,f] is zero off H_0 and on H_0 has kernel Q(s,t)/(s+t).
Divide in C[s,t] to write Q(s,t)=(s+t)G(s,t)+r(s).
The polynomial kernel G has finite rank.
If r is nonzero, multiplication by r(h) is injective on K, because mu is diffuse and r has finitely many zeros.
Therefore the residual operator M_r B has infinite rank, by the infinite rank of B and injectivity.
Consequently [K_Y,f] has finite rank exactly when s+t divides Q.
In that case, write \(G(s,t)=\sum_{k,l}g_{kl}s^kt^l\).
The identity \(Q=(s+t)G\) gives explicitly
\[
 f=p(h)+\sum_{k,l}g_{kl}h^kYh^l\in A.
\]
The preceding affiliated criterion implies
\[
 \operatorname{Rat}_{L^0(M)}\mathbb C\langle h,Y\rangle
 \cap\bigl\{\text{polynomials in h,b of b-degree at most one}\bigr\}
 \subseteq\mathbb C\langle h,Y\rangle.
\]
Thus no witness in this explicit polynomial class can give the desired strict inclusion.
Conversely every element of A belongs to its affiliated matrix rational closure, so the asserted intersection equality follows.
The same formula proves the divisibility criterion in the statement.

### The commutator pair cannot have bounded duals

Let Z=i(hb-bh).
Suppose a bounded D on H obeys [h,D]=0 and [Z,D]=P_Omega.
For p=sum_j a_j(h) Z c_j(h), its commutator has vacuum expectation sum_j tau(a_j)tau(c_j).
On the other hand,
\[
 |\langle\Omega,[p,D]\Omega\rangle|
 \le2\|D\|\,\|p\Omega\|_2,
\]
using ||p*Omega||_2=||pOmega||_2 and the two bounded vacuum vectors D Omega and D* Omega.
Writing Q(s,t)=sum_j a_j(s)c_j(t), the first free layer identifies pOmega with i(s-t)Q(s,t).
Hence
\[
 \left|\int Q\,d\mu\,d\mu\right|
 \le2\|D\|\,\|(s-t)Q\|_{L^2(\mu\otimes\mu)}
\]
for every polynomial Q.
The subspace \(\{i(s-t)Q:Q\in\mathbb C[s,t]\}\) is dense in \(L^2(\mu\otimes\mu)\).
Indeed, a vector orthogonal to it gives, after multiplication by the bounded function \(s-t\), a vector orthogonal to every polynomial; polynomial density makes that product zero, and the product-null diagonal makes the original vector zero.
Riesz representation of the displayed functional therefore supplies \(g\in L^2(\mu\otimes\mu)\) such that
\[
 \int \overline{g(s,t)}\,i(s-t)Q(s,t)\,d\mu(s)d\mu(t)
 =\int Q(s,t)\,d\mu(s)d\mu(t)
\]
for all polynomials Q.
The difference \(i(s-t)\overline g-1\) belongs to L2 and pairs to zero with all polynomials, so it vanishes a.e.
In particular \(|g(s,t)|=|s-t|^{-1}\) a.e.
This is impossible, since the density 4t^3 is bounded below on every interior compact interval and the square of 1/(s-t) is not locally integrable along its diagonal.
Thus (h,i[h,b]) has no bounded dual system.

### The small-spectral-vector obstruction for quadratic expressions

Let
\[
 Z=p(h)+\alpha(hb+bh)+i\beta(hb-bh)+\gamma b+\delta b^2,
\]
where p is a real polynomial of degree at most two and alpha,beta,gamma,delta are real.
This is every selfadjoint polynomial of total degree at most two in h,b.
For xi_epsilon=1_(0,epsilon](h)/sqrt(tau(1_(0,epsilon](h))), the zero-, one-, and two-b Fock layers of (Z-c)xi_epsilon are orthogonal.
They are respectively
\[
 (p(h)+\delta-c)\xi_\varepsilon,
\]
\[
 ((\alpha+i\beta)h+\gamma)\otimes\xi_\varepsilon
 +(\alpha-i\beta)1\otimes h\xi_\varepsilon,
\]
and delta times 1 tensor 1 tensor xi_epsilon.
It follows that
\[
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\|(Z-c)\xi_\varepsilon\|_2^2
 =|p(0)+\delta-c|^2
  +\int| (\alpha+i\beta)t+\gamma|^2\,d\mu(t)
  +\delta^2.
\]
The right side can vanish only when delta=alpha=beta=gamma=0 and c=p(0).
Thus any genuinely b-dependent selfadjoint quadratic fails the particular common-small-vector mechanism used by the sandwich example.
This is not a lower bound on the polynomial degree of every possible counterexample.
It does not exclude other spectral vectors, other input models, more generators, or another mechanism for bounded rational nonmembership.
