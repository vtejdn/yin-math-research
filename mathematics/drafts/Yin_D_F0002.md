## Bounded vectors and the entire Hilbert space are different domain targets

On 2026-10-16 Yin asked whether A and normality extend the domain to the L2 space generated by X.
This clarification concerns extension of the given derivation or canonical contraction, with its original Hilbert-space target and with closability retained.
The starred polynomial vectors are already dense in H=L2(W*(X),tau), and [ACSY24-U001, Section 2](../audits/literature/ACSY24/Yin_U001.md#2-theorem-34-construction-and-adjoint) gives closability of the canonical contractions without normality.
Density and existence of a closed densely defined operator do not give an operator whose domain equals H.
An everywhere-defined closable linear map between Hilbert spaces is bounded: its closure still has domain equal to the entire source space, so the closed graph theorem applies.

A scalar specialization of the existing source and adjoint formulas distinguishes these requirements.
Let M=L-infinity([-1,1],mu), dmu(x)=dx/2, X(x)=x, and H=L2(mu).
On the polynomial core define
\[
 (\partial p)(x,y)=\frac{p(x)-p(y)}{x-y},\qquad
 E_0p(x)=\int\frac{p(x)-p(y)}{x-y}\,d\mu(y).
\]
The diagonal is null for the product measure.
The generator X is selfadjoint and its law has compactly supported L3 density.
[Voi98-U002, Proposition 3.5](../audits/literature/Voi98/Yin_U002.md#proposition-35-scalar-hilbert-transform-realization), checked against printed pp.196-197, gives the actual conjugate variable
\[
 \partial^*(1\otimes1)=\xi,\qquad
 \xi(x)=\log\frac{1+x}{1-x}\in L^2(\mu)\setminus L^\infty(\mu).
\]
Indeed its regularized vector is
\[
 g_\varepsilon(x)
 =2\int\frac{x-y}{(x-y)^2+\varepsilon^2}\,d\mu(y)
 =\frac12\log\frac{(1+x)^2+\varepsilon^2}{(1-x)^2+\varepsilon^2}.
\]
It converges to xi in L2(mu), by the source or directly by a square-integrable logarithmic majorant for 0<epsilon<=1.
Symmetrizing the regularized difference quotient gives the actual vacuum identity against every polynomial.
Thus this is an instance of A with a selfadjoint generator, not merely a formal vacuum pairing.

The scalar adjoint identity in [C_F0055, Section 7](../claims/Yin_C_F0055_v1.md#7-actual-adjoints-and-original-graph-restoration) reduces on this real support to
\[
 E_0^*p=p\xi-E_0p,\qquad p\in\mathbb C[x].
\]
If E_0 had a bounded extension B on H, then E_0*=B* and multiplication by xi would satisfy
\[
 \|\xi p\|_2=\|(B+B^*)p\|_2
 \le 2\|B\|\,\|p\|_2
 \quad(p\in\mathbb C[x]).
\]
Density of the polynomials and closedness of multiplication by xi extend this estimate to H, contradicting xi not in L-infinity(mu).
Hence E_0 is unbounded in the Hilbert norm.
Since E_0=(id tensor tau)partial and the tensor contraction is bounded, partial is unbounded in that norm as well.
Neither the given partial nor the canonical D=-closure(E_0) admits a closable extension with domain equal to all H.
This assertion concerns extension of these specified operators; it makes no assertion against other, differently normalized dual systems.

Nevertheless [C_F0055](../claims/Yin_C_F0055_v1.md#statement) applies to this scalar actual-vacuum law and gives M Omega subset Dom D intersect Dom D*.
Thus the example satisfies S, and does not separate A from S.
For arbitrary correlated normal tuples, the bounded-vector domain question remains the unresolved target above; failure of an entire-H extension cannot decide it.
The displayed specialization and domain distinction were checked by root for this answer; no new independent review or Claim acceptance is recorded.

## Realification is legitimate but does not import the Dabrowski estimate

Write \(\delta=\partial_i\), \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\), and

\[

are real outer derivations, with actual vacuum vectors
\[

However, the exact source interface [Dab10-U002](../audits/literature/Dab10/Yin_U002.md#dab10-u002) is the free-difference-quotient energy calculation and the closed-real-derivation functional calculus.

For a general normal marginal, the unique starred derivative is the phase
\[
 \delta(X_i^*)=k_i,\qquad
 k_i(z,w)=(\bar z-\bar w)/(z-w).
\]
The conjugate derivation has \(\delta^\#(X_i)=\overline{k_i}\) and \(\delta^\#(X_i^*)=1\otimes1\).
Thus the realified generator values involve \(k_i\) and \(\overline{k_i}\), which need not be algebraic tensors.
The actual iterated-domain and coassociativity issue is now resolved by C_F0076.
A controlled mixed-defect estimate is still required for the realified energy argument.

This identifies the remaining failed shortcut: actual vacua survive realification, but realification by itself gives no uniform contraction bound.
Actual second-derivative domains have been established by a separate phase approximation.

There is a direct check that reality and an actual vacuum alone do not give the zero-defect Dabrowski estimate.
On any nontrivial finite tracial algebra take
\[
 \beta(a)=\mathrm i(a\otimes1-1\otimes a).
\]
This is a real outer derivation, since the flip-star reverses the sign of \(a\otimes1-1\otimes a\) and conjugates \(\mathrm i\).
Its vacuum functional is identically zero, so its actual vacuum is \(0\).
But its right contraction is \(\mathrm i(a-\tau(a)1)\), which is nonzero on a nonzero centered element.
Its exact coassociativity defect is
\[
 (\beta\otimes\mathrm{id})\beta
 -(\mathrm{id}\otimes\beta)\beta
 =\mathrm i\kappa\beta,\qquad
 \kappa(a\otimes b)=a\otimes1\otimes b.
\]
Thus an estimate with constant depending only linearly on the vacuum norm and vanishing at vacuum zero cannot be imported for all real derivations.
This example does not have the prescribed rank-one generator values and is not a counterexample to the Task.

 (ap_n\Omega,E_{i,0}(ap_n\Omega))

## Inner phase unitary and the real structure of the closed form

Fix the coordinate and phase as above.
For a marginal bounded tensor h, let I_h denote inner insertion: for a finite tensor h=sum r tensor s, I_h(x tensor y)=sum x r tensor s y.

Since |k|=1 almost everywhere, K=I_k is a coarse-bimodule unitary with inverse I_bar(k).
The two derivations agree on every starred generator in the identity

    epsilon = I_bar(k) delta.

Let J_2 be flip-star on the coarse space.
Then J_2 I_bar(k)=I_k J_2, because the phase is symmetric in its variables.
Consequently

    delta(a*) = R delta(a),     R=K J_2,

## Direct complex coassociativity gives a cross identity, not coercivity

The ordinary coassociativity of \(\delta\) gives a useful identity without the mixed defect.
Keep \(E=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\bar\delta\), \(F=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\bar\epsilon\), and \(\xi=\delta^*(1\otimes1)\).
Write \(\Theta(a\otimes b)v=a\tau(bv)\).
On the original polynomial core, the actual second-application domains and bounded graph approximants already retained in C_F0056 and L_F0011 give
\[
 E^2p=\Theta(\delta p)\xi^*,\qquad
 F^2p=\Theta(\epsilon p)\xi.
 \tag{C1}
\]
Indeed coassociativity moves the iterated derivative to the second leg, and its double trace is \(\tau(\xi^*b)\).
The bounded spatial approximants justify the product with the fixed \(L^2\) vector; this is an actual-domain calculation, not a formal interchange of unbounded operators.

Using \(E_0^*p=p\xi-Fp\) and (C1), with inner products linear in the second variable, gives
\[
 \langle Fp,Ep\rangle
 =\langle p\xi,Ep\rangle-\langle p,\Theta(\delta p)\xi^*\rangle.
 \tag{C2}
\]
For a unitary \(u\), the first-order derivation rule gives \(\epsilon u=-u(\delta u)^\dagger u\).
If \(\delta u=\sum a\otimes b\), this means
\[
 Fu=-\sum ub^*\tau(a^*u),\qquad
 \langle Fu,u\xi^*\rangle
 =-\langle u,\Theta(\delta u)\xi^*\rangle.
\]
The latter formula extends from algebraic tensors by Hilbert--Schmidt continuity.
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\langle Fu,Eu\rangle
 =\langle u\xi,Eu\rangle+\langle Fu,u\xi^*\rangle},
 \qquad
 |\langle Fu,Eu\rangle|
 \le \|\xi\|_2(\|Eu\|_2+\|Fu\|_2).
 \tag{C3}
\]
This is valid for core unitaries and for the analytic-core unitaries used in L_F0011.
For the latter, pass (C2) through the norm and first-derivative graph approximants: \(\Theta(\delta p)\xi^*\) converges by the Hilbert--Schmidt bound.
No assertion that every analytic-core unitary lies in the actual domain of \(E^2\) is needed.
The identity obtained from \(F^2\) is the conjugate of the same constraint.

The cross form in (C3) is not coercive, even for a single Haar unitary.
For scalar Haar \(U\), the actual difference quotient has
\[
 \xi=U,\quad k=-U^*\otimes U^*,\quad
 E(U^n)=U^{n-1},\quad F(U^n)=0\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Here \(\epsilon U=-U\otimes U\), and every second tensor exponent in \(\epsilon(U^n)\) is strictly positive, so its right trace vanishes.
The right side of (C3) vanishes as well.
Thus ordinary complex coassociativity and the unitary inner-phase relation do not turn this particular cross identity into a bound for \(\|Eu\|_2^2+\|Fu\|_2^2\).
This is a failure of the proposed energy argument, not a counterexample to the Task: the Haar case already has both canonical bounded-vector domains.

 \tag{V5}
\]

 \tag{V6}
\]
The usual actual Gaussian tensor-adjoint identity proves these directly: partial_k of the degree-one leg is delta_kl times its two marginal endpoints, and the partial trace removes its middle marginal factor.

 \tag{V7}

## The power-iteration mechanism from Mai15 and the missing quadratic estimate

The directed source audit of 2026-10-16 separates the power-growth part of Mai15 Lemma 4.11 from the real/coassociative identity used in Theorem 4.9.

The Hilbert-valued interpretation of the power-iteration lemma is essential: its proof uses only Hilbert norms of the outputs, although the source writes its target as M.

Let P be a weakly dense unital star subalgebra of a finite tracial M and let delta:P -> H tensor_2 H be an outer derivation with actual xi=delta*(1 tensor 1) in H.
No reality, normal generator or algebraic-tensor range is assumed for this calculation.
The two maps
\[
 T_\ell(a)=\delta^*(a\otimes1),\qquad
 T_r(a)=\delta^*(1\otimes a),\qquad a\in P,
\]
are actually defined, by the general adjoint-domain lemma.
Writing star componentwise on the Hilbert tensor product, their formulas are
\[
 T_\ell(a)=a\xi-(\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})\bigl(\delta(a^*)^*\bigr),
 \qquad
 T_r(a)=\xi a-(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\bigl(\delta(a^*)^*\bigr).
\]
For either map T and every positive integer m, the Leibniz rule and bounded tensor contraction give
\[
 \|T(a^m)\|_2
 \le \|a\|^m\|\xi\|_2
       +m\|a\|^{m-1}\|\delta(a^*)\|_2.
\]
For a=0 the conclusion is immediate; otherwise taking m-th roots shows
\[
 \limsup_{m\to\infty}\|T(a^m)\|_2^{1/m}\le\|a\|.
\]
Thus the growth hypothesis of the power-iteration lemma is automatic under the actual vacuum condition.

The remaining sufficient inequalities would be finite positive constants C_ell,C_r such that
\[
 \|T_\ell(a)\|_2^2\le C_\ell\|T_\ell(a^*a)\|_2,
 \qquad
 \|T_r(a)\|_2^2\le C_r\|T_r(aa^*)\|_2
 \quad(a\in P).
\]
For the left inequality put b=a*a, and for the right inequality put b=aa*.
For either map T with its constant C, the chosen b is positive; iterate on b,b^2,b^4,... .
For every n at least one,
\[
 \|T(b)\|_2
 \le C^{1-2^{-n}}\|T(b^{2^n})\|_2^{2^{-n}}.
\]
The automatic power-growth bound gives ||T(b)||2 <= C||b||, and one more use of the assumed quadratic inequality gives ||T(a)||2 <= C||a||.
This argument does not multiply outputs of T or require them to lie in M.

Taking stars in the two actual-adjoint formulas yields
\[
 (\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau)\delta(a)
   =a\xi^*-T_r(a^*)^*,\qquad
 (\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(a)
   =\xi^*a-T_\ell(a^*)^*.
\]
The two hypothetical quadratic estimates would therefore imply
\[

    \le(\|\xi\|_2+C_r)\|a\|,
 \qquad

    \le(\|\xi\|_2+C_\ell)\|a\|.
\]

## Angular modes of the three-point curvature form

Fix a vertex \(z_i\) of a finite set of distinct complex locations and write \(z_j-z_i=r_j e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}\) for \(j\ne i\).
The shifted block identity (U4) groups the actual form by unordered triples, with one cross Gram at each of the three vertices.
At the fixed vertex its contribution summed over unordered endpoint pairs is \(\sum_{j\ne k} \mathfrak c(z_i,z_j,z_k)^2\operatorname{Re}\langle B_{ij},B_{ik}\rangle\), where both endpoints differ from \(i\).
To examine positivity at this vertex alone, allow arbitrary Hilbert vectors \(B_{ij}\) and normalize them by \(\beta_j=B_{ij}/r_j\).
For distinct endpoints, the area formula gives the coefficient of their normalized Gram as
\[
 F_{r,s}(\theta)=rs\,\mathfrak c(0,r,s e^{\mathrm i\theta})^2
 =\frac{4rs\sin^2\theta}{r^2+s^2-2rs\cos\theta}.
 \tag{P11}
\]
For \(r=s\) this equals \(2+2\cos\theta\) away from the repeated point, with continuous diagonal value four.
The actual convention in (U4) omits the diagonal.
For \(r\ne s\), put \(q=\min(r,s)/\max(r,s)\) and use the Fourier series with coefficients multiplying \(e^{\mathrm i n\theta}\).
Multiplying \(\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{|n|}e^{\mathrm i n\theta}=(1-q^2)/(1-2q\cos\theta+q^2)\) by \(4q\sin^2\theta/(1-q^2)\) gives
\[
 \widehat F(0)=2q,\qquad
 \widehat F(\pm1)=q^2,\qquad
 \widehat F(\pm n)=-(1-q^2)q^{n-1}\quad(n\ge2).
 \tag{P12}
\]
Thus a common second angular mode on distinct radii can produce a negative cross Gram even for genuine three-point curvature.

For an explicit count, put equally spaced \(N\)-gons at each of two fixed radii about \(z_i=0\), with the same angles and \(N\ge4\) divisible by four.
Take \(\beta_j=e^{2\mathrm i\theta_j}v\) with a fixed unit vector \(v\) on both polygons.
The completed kernel on each individual ring has zero quadratic form on this mode, by discrete orthogonality to the harmonics \(0,\pm1\).
In each direction between rings the sum is \(N^2\sum_{n\equiv2\, (\mathrm{mod}\,N)}\widehat F(n)\), by absolute convergence and the finite geometric sum over angles.
Every coefficient in that sum is negative and the \(n=2\) term equals \(-q(1-q^2)\).
The off-diagonal form at the fixed vertex is consequently at most \(-2N^2q(1-q^2)-8N\), after removing \(2N\) diagonal values of four.
Since \(\sum_{j\ne i}\|B_{ij}\|^2/r_j^2=2N\), even the completed anchored form admits no lower bound by minus a universal constant times this sum for arbitrary block vectors.
These are counting sums, exactly as in (U4) where the actual block vectors already include their trace normalization.

The sign also occurs with probability measures on the fixed scalar actual-vacuum law \(\mu=\sum_{l\ge1}p_l\sigma_l\) of [C_F0143](../claims/Yin_C_F0143_v1.md#argument), where \(\sigma_l\) is normalized Haar measure on the circle of radius \(r_l=2^{-l}\).
Take the bounded field \(b(r_l e^{\mathrm i\theta})=r_l e^{2\mathrm i\theta}/p_l\) on \(l=1,2\) and zero elsewhere.
For \(z\) near zero define
\[
 H(z)=\iint \mathfrak c(z,v,w)^2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{b(v)}b(w))\,d\mu(v)d\mu(w).
 \tag{P13}
\]
The masses cancel in each selected pair in \(H(0)\).
The same-ring integrals vanish and the two ordered cross-ring integrals give \(H(0)=-2q(1-q^2)\) with \(q=r_2/r_1\).
For \(|z|<r_2/2\) the distance from \(z\) to each selected endpoint exceeds \(r_2/2\), and the circumradius bound gives \(\mathfrak c^2\le16/r_2^2\).
For distinct selected endpoints the area formula is continuous in \(z\) there, and pairs with equal endpoints are null for \(\mu^2\).

The actual scalar contraction for this law has positive quadratic form by C_F0143.

For the finite polygons the other vertices give zero off-diagonal Grams if all outgoing legs there vanish except possibly the reciprocal leg pointing to the center.
This extension across all vertices still fails the actual triangle identities.
Indeed put \(x_{jk}=q_{jk}/(m_jm_k)\) in the notation of (U4) and take all leaf masses equal.
Any three leaves give \(x_{jk}+x_{kl}+x_{lj}=0\).
With at least five leaves, differences of two such equations imply \(d_a+d_b=0\) for distinct \(a,b\notin\{k,l\}\), where \(d_a=x_{ak}-x_{al}\).

On either ring the pair values at angles \((0,\pi/2),(\pi,3\pi/2)\) have negative sum, while the pairs \((0,\pi),(\pi/2,3\pi/2)\) have positive sum, contradicting additivity.

For \(w\) on either outer circle and endpoints on those two circles the curvature squared is at most 16, since a mixed-radius triangle has an edge at least \(1/2\) long and a same-circle triangle has curvature at most two.
As \(\int|b|\,d\mu=3/2\), this gives \(|H(w)|\le36\) on the two outer circles.
Fubini now gives the strictly negative quadratic form of the integrated scalar curvature kernel,
\[
 \iint g(z,v)\overline{b(z)}b(v)\,d\mu(z)d\mu(v)
 =\int H(w)\,d\mu(w)
 \le-\tfrac12(1-\varepsilon)+36\varepsilon<0.
 \tag{P18}
\]
The left side is real by symmetry.
Any representation by the positive mixture in (P16) would give \(\int_T|\int_{U_s} b\,d\mu|^2\,d\pi(s)\ge0\) for that same left side.
Fubini applies even without a cost bound, since its absolute majorant integrates to \(\iint g(z,v)|b(z)b(v)|\,d\mu^2<\infty\).
Thus actual scalar \(\mathsf A\) allows curvature kernels with no exact positive mixture of measurable projection blocks.
The actual scalar contraction still has the nonnegative jump form \(\tfrac12\iint g(z,v)|u(z)-u(v)|^2\,d\mu^2\) on core tests, by symmetry and inner scalar liberation.
The signed coefficients in (P14) and the lower-bound question for correlated tuples remain open.

## Localizing a shift outside the omitted algebra

For the shift \(X_i-b\) with \(b\in N=W^*(X_j:j\ne i)\), [the one-corner and summed estimates](../claims/Yin_C_F0227_v1.md#intertwining-and-small-omitted-algebra-corners) rule out a diverging packing test using projections in \(N\) itself.
The spectral projections of \(|X_i-b|\) can lie outside \(N\); estimates on those projections or on their conditional expectations are still needed.
The same section excludes a sequence approximating Haar intertwining equations or the span of normalizing unitaries with vanishing Hilbert error and uniformly bounded vacuum norms.

### The remaining shift-distribution question

The [conditional matrix covariance inequality](../claims/Yin_C_F0227_v1.md#conditional-matrix-covariance) bounds the integrable inverse of the conditional second moment \(\mathbb E_N((X_i-b)^*(X_i-b))\), including noncentral shifts \(b\).
Relating this quantity to the distribution of \(|X_i-b|\) near zero would require control before taking \(\mathbb E_N\).

<a id="polar-defect-and-four-point-arcs"></a>
## Line directions on corners of small polar phase loss

The [fixed four-point theorem](../claims/Yin_C_F0230_v1.md#statement) gives a straight-line carrier in every subsequential compactness limit of weighted laws with phase loss tending to zero, a positive lower trace, and bounded omitted shifts.
The following additional observations track the corresponding component of the omitted shifts themselves.

Use the notation of the [positive-trace argument](../claims/Yin_C_F0230_v1.md#weights-with-a-common-positive-lower-trace-bound) and a subsequence on which the two weighted laws converge.
Write their common limiting line as \(\ell=c+d\mathbb R\), \(|d|=1\).
Since \(k(z,w)=\bar d^{\,2}\) for distinct points on \(\ell\), the weighted product \(L^2\)-distance of \(k\) from this constant tends to zero by diffuseness and weak convergence, using the same diagonal cutoff as in the fixed proof.
Thus \(1-d(e_n)=\operatorname{Re}(\bar d^{\,2}\tau(e_n U_n)^2/t_n^2)+o(1)\).
Since \(d(e_n)\to0\), the numbers \(\bar d\,\tau(e_nU_n)/t_n\) of modulus at most one approach the set \(\{1,-1\}\).
After passing to a subsequence and taking its sign \(\sigma=\pm1\), we get
\[
   t_n^{-1}\|(U_n-\sigma d)e_n^{1/2}\|_2^2
     =2-2\sigma\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\bar d\,\tau(e_nU_n)/t_n\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently \((U_n^*-\sigma\bar d)e_n^{1/2}\to0\) in \(L^2\), by left multiplying the preceding difference by \(U_n^*\) and a scalar of modulus one.
The normalized squared norm of \(\operatorname{Im}(\bar d(X-c))e_n^{1/2}\) is the \(\nu_{e_n}\)-integral of the squared distance to \(\ell\), so this norm tends to zero.
Writing \(X-b_n=U_nP_n\), the difference \((\bar d U_nP_n-dP_n U_n^*)e_n^{1/2}\) also tends to zero in \(L^2\), since \(P_n\) commutes with \(e_n\) and is uniformly bounded.
It follows by subtraction that
\[
       \|\operatorname{Im}(\bar d(b_n-c))e_n^{1/2}\|_2\longrightarrow0 .
\]
For a single positive \(e=g(P)\) with exact phase loss zero, use its constant sequence after normalizing \(\|e\|\) to one.
The support of \(e\) is then bounded by both \(1_\ell(X)\) and \(1_{\{0\}}(\operatorname{Im}(\bar d(b-c)))\).
This tracks a direction for each fixed positive lower trace.
For the shrinking spectral projections \(e_n=1_{[0,r_n]}(P_n)\) with \(r_n\downarrow0\), the [uniform shift estimate](../claims/Yin_C_F0229_v1.md#statement) forces \(\tau(e_n)\to0\), so this compactness argument supplies no law-independent line for such projections.

<a id="holomorphic-resolvent-near-a-supporting-half-plane"></a>
## Singular mass near a supporting half-plane of the numerical range

The [omitted-unitary supporting-half-plane theorem](../claims/Yin_C_F0231_v1.md#statement) gives the small-radius estimate and its \(L^p\) improvement.
Its one-sided formulation already has the full scope of its two-unitary formulation.
Indeed if \(V,W\in N\) and \(D=V^*(X-b)W\), then \(WDW^*=WV^*(X-b)\).
Writing \(v=VW^*\), the one-unitary condition is \(\operatorname{Re}(v^*(X-b))\ge-\kappa r\,1\).
The associated normalized test also reduces to one factor, since \(v(v^*(X-b)+s)^{-1}=V(D+s)^{-1}W^*\).

For each shift \(b\) define the supporting tolerance
\[
   a_b=\inf_{v\in\mathcal U(N)}
      \|(\operatorname{Re}(v^*(X-b)))_-\|.
\]
The fixed little-order estimate applies uniformly to families \(a_b\le Kr\) with fixed \(K\), since for \(r>0\) one may take an approximating unitary with tolerance \((K+1)r\).
The infimum need not be presumed attained.
For the single-coordinate uniform mass problem, this reduces any sequence with \(t^3/r^2\) bounded away from zero as \(r\to0\) to \(a_b/r\to\infty\): every bounded-ratio subsequence would contradict the [uniform estimate](../claims/Yin_C_F0231_v1.md#statement).
For the [orthogonal packing problem](../claims/Yin_C_F0211_v1.md#statement), even a sequence for which all individual ratios tend to zero can have unbounded partial sums.
A bound for that sum still requires control of the normalized test vectors or another estimate using the orthogonality of the input projections.

<a id="localization-between-separated-spectral-components"></a>
## Reducing the column problem to same-component cuts

The [separated spectral component theorem](../claims/Yin_C_F0237_v1.md#decision-reviewed) gives finite column cost between distinct components and within each component supported on a regular arc or closed curve.
Its multiplier argument moves a continuous coefficient \(w\) past the right trace using \(L(pw)v-pL(w)v\).
This works for the liberation slice and for the original slice whenever its column cost is finite.
In the latter case a finite-column application to \(v=e_a,w=e_b\) bounds each individual cut by twice the full cost, since the spectral projections belong to the continuous marginal algebra.
Together with the theorem's off-diagonal bounds and finite recombination, this gives equivalence between finite full cost and finite cost for every same-component cut of any fixed separated partition.
The [cutoff column criterion](../claims/Yin_C_F0238_v1.md#removing-the-cutoff-in-the-column-norm) gives a corresponding test without a separation hypothesis, using smooth functions of spectral distance.
