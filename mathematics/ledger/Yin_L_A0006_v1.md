# Kernel-preserving normalizations of unmatched rational Schur complements

Date: 2026-11-11 (Asia/Hong_Kong).
The main research agent and two development helpers derived and checked these calibration examples for Yin.
This investigation serves [Yin_T_A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract) by testing a concrete right multiplier on an actual rational Schur complement.
The current question is whether a value outside every phase-rotated positive matched realization can nevertheless be moved into the existing energy criterion using only an independently established unit.
The primary worked family below answers that restricted question affirmatively and identifies precisely why its bordered rank is already covered by existing criteria.
A secondary reciprocal-ratio family records another legal transfer and its parameter boundary.
All affiliated sums and products are the closed operations in the actual affiliated algebra, rather than unrestricted compositions on arbitrary Hilbert-space domains.
The complete investigation has an [independent whole-artifact review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_A0008_v1.md#whole-artifact-finding), covering both calibration families, the domain-sensitive rank argument and the stated coverage limits.

## Coupled-pencil normalization

Let \(G\) be an arbitrary torsion-free discrete group, let \(g,h,k\in G\) with \(g\ne h\), and put \(M=\mathcal N(G)\), \(U=\mathcal U(G)\), and \(R=\operatorname{Rat}_{U}(\mathbb CG)\).
Write each group element for its left regular unitary when it occurs in an operator formula.
Use the unnormalized matrix trace for analytic ranks and kernel dimensions.
Define
\[
K=k+k^{-1},\qquad a=2+iK,\qquad b=g+h,\qquad q=gh^{-1},
\]
\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\-b^*&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P=2a-bb^*=2-q-q^{-1}+2iK.
\tag{1}
\]
The order \(q=gh^{-1}\) is part of the formula: it comes from \(bb^*\), whereas the cyclic factorization \(b=g(1+g^{-1}h)\) involves \(g^{-1}h\).

The elements \(b,P\in\mathbb CG\) are units of \(U\), and \(L\in GL_2(U)\), by the cyclic regular-representation argument and the positive-energy criterion, without any Atiyah integrality hypothesis.
Consequently, the actual rational value
\[
r=e_1^*L^{-1}e_2
\]
is defined in \(R\), and it satisfies the exact ordered identities
\[
r=P^{-1}b,
\qquad
\widehat r:=rb^{-1}=P^{-1}
=\frac12 e_1^*L^{-1}e_1.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the single right factor \(b^{-1}\in R\cap U^\times\), whose invertibility is established independently of \(r\), moves \(r\) to a matched positive-energy realization.
In particular \(r,\widehat r\in R^\times\), with \(r^{-1}=b^{-1}P\) and \(\widehat r^{-1}=P\).

The original and transformed constant-border pencils over exactly the same group labels are
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
0&a&-b\\
1&-b^*&2
\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&e_1^*\\e_2&L\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Q_+=\begin{pmatrix}
0&\frac12&0\\
1&a&-b\\
0&-b^*&2
\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac12e_1^*\\e_1&L\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]
They satisfy
\[
\operatorname{rk}_GQ=\operatorname{rk}_GQ_+=3,
\qquad
\dim_G\ker Q=\dim_G\ker Q_+=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Here \(Q_+\) has the matched data \(W=I_2\), \(v=e_1\), and \(\gamma=1/2\).
Its denominator is the same actual invertible \(L\); no enlargement of the finite group tuple is needed for (3).

The scalar realization also gives the smaller constant-border pencil
\[
Q_P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&P\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]
It is affine after adjoining the actual group labels \(q,q^{-1}\) to the finite tuple, with their actual relation \(q=gh^{-1}\) retained.
It has rank 2, and the general rank comparison for these three realizations is
\[
\operatorname{rk}_GQ
=2+\operatorname{rk}_Gr
=\operatorname{rk}_GQ_+
=1+\operatorname{rk}_GQ_P.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, removing the denominator state changes the matrix rank by one while preserving the zero kernel dimension.

For the specific group \(G=F(g,h,k)\) freely generated by these three letters, the raw value has the stronger obstruction
\[
\operatorname{Re}(zr)\not\geq0
\qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb C^\times.
\tag{7}
\]
Therefore \(r\) has no exact-value realization of any size satisfying the positive matched-energy hypothesis, even with an arbitrary scalar phase and a different denominator.
This obstruction concerns the raw value; it does not preclude the independently invertible right multiplication in (2).
The positive conclusion (1)--(6) holds for every torsion-free \(G\) with \(g\ne h\), whereas (7) is asserted here only for the specified free-group example.

## Proof of the coupled-pencil normalization

### Independent admissibility of the factors and denominators

For every nonidentity \(x\in G\), its infinite order makes the left regular action on each orbit \(\langle x\rangle t\) a bilateral shift.
Such a shift has no eigenvector: an eigenvector for a unit-modulus eigenvalue has constant absolute value on the infinite orbit and hence vanishes if square summable.
Thus \(1+x\) and \(1-x\) are injective on \(\ell^2(G)\).
The finite-trace polar-decomposition principle then supplies their affiliated inverses.
This is exactly the [previous cyclic regular-representation input](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#cyclic-rational-functions), with no analytic zero-divisor assertion about an arbitrary group-ring element.

Since \(g^{-1}h\ne e\), the factorization \(b=g(1+g^{-1}h)\) proves that \(b\) is injective and affiliated-invertible.
The inverse need not be bounded; its unboundedness causes no difficulty in the ring \(U\).
Because \(b\in\mathbb CG\) is actually invertible in \(U\), \(b^{-1}\in R\) by the definition of the actual rational closure.
No inverse of \(r\), no rank of \(Q\), and no group-wide Atiyah assertion enters this step.

The nonidentity element \(q=gh^{-1}\) also has infinite order, and
\[
\operatorname{Re}P
=2-q-q^{-1}
=(1-q)^*(1-q)\geq0
\]
has zero kernel.
For a vector \(\xi\in\ker P\), the equality
\[
0=\operatorname{Re}\langle P\xi,\xi\rangle
=\|(1-q)\xi\|^2
\]
gives \(\xi=0\).
Thus \(P\) is a bounded injective square operator and a unit of \(U\), independently of \(r\).
This is the scalar case of the [positive-energy argument](../claims/Yin_C_A0003_v1.md#bounded-energy-calculation).
It does not require \(q\) to commute with \(k\), a positive lower spectral bound, or a bounded inverse of \(P\).

The same denominator admissibility can be checked directly at the original size.
Set
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}1&-g\\1&-h\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since \(K=K^*\),
\[
\operatorname{Re}L
=\begin{pmatrix}2&-g-h\\-g^{-1}-h^{-1}&2\end{pmatrix}
=D^*D.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(D(w_1,w_2)=0\), then \(w_1=gw_2=hw_2\), so \(w_2=g^{-1}hw_2\).
The no-eigenvector argument forces \(w_2=0\), and then \(w_1=0\).
Hence \(D^*D\) is injective, and the bounded energy argument makes \(L\) injective and affiliated-invertible.
This is the [existing two-edge graph certificate](../claims/Yin_C_A0003_v1.md#a-coupled-example-without-a-spectral-gap-premise), specialized to the selfadjoint diagonal perturbation \(\operatorname{diag}(K,0)\).

### Ordered block elimination and the matched target

All ensuing identities take place in \(M_2(U)\), after the preceding independent admissibility checks.
Scalar-pivot elimination gives
\[
L
=\begin{pmatrix}1&-b/2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}P/2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-b^*/2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{9}
\]
Multiplication verifies the upper-left entry as \(P/2+bb^*/2=a\), so (9) retains the required noncommutative order.
Taking the inverse yields
\[
L^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}
2P^{-1}&P^{-1}b\\
b^*P^{-1}&\frac12+\frac12b^*P^{-1}b
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (2) follow from the indicated two entries and multiplication on the right by \(b^{-1}\).
No commutation of \(P\) with \(b\) has been used.

Equation (8) and the matched data of \(Q_+\) place that entire bounded pencil in the existing positive-energy criterion.
The smaller \(Q_P\) also satisfies that criterion with \(N=1\), \(W=u=v=1\), and \(H=2-q-q^{-1}\).
These are two actual representations of the same transformed value \(\widehat r\), rather than substitutions into an abstract free field.
The same-size representation (3) shows that the normalization does not need to treat the word \(gh^{-1}\) as a formally independent generator.

### Affiliated rank and the kernel-dimension statement

All products of affiliated operators mean their closed affiliated products in this fixed algebra.
For an affiliated matrix \(Z\) and actual units \(A,B\) of the appropriate square sizes,
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G(AZB)=\operatorname{rk}_G Z.
\]
The accepted [domain-aware rank inequalities](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U004.md#lemma-59-a-local-domain-aware-proof) give one inequality from the displayed product and the reverse inequality from \(Z=A^{-1}(AZB)B^{-1}\).
No boundedness of the four multipliers is required.
For square matrices of the same size, subtracting rank from that size gives
\[
\dim_G\ker(AZB)=\dim_G\ker Z.
\]
This is a dimension statement for closed affiliated kernels, not an assertion that an unbounded multiplier maps every Hilbert-space kernel vector pointwise to another kernel vector.
In particular, the expression \(B^{-1}\ker Z\) requires domain qualifications if used as a Hilbert-space set and is not used in this proof.
The unit premises must be established independently of the target's own invertibility.

For a closed affiliated scalar operator \(t\in U\) and a unit \(c\in U^\times\), equality of principal right ideals gives
\[
(tc)U=tU.
\]
In the finite affiliated algebra, the unique projection generating this ideal is the left support projection of \(t\), and its trace is the analytic rank.
Thus
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G(tc)=\operatorname{rk}_Gt.
\tag{11}
\]
The initial and final support projections of a closed affiliated operator are equivalent by polar decomposition, so
\[
\dim_G\ker(tc)
=1-\operatorname{rk}_G(tc)
=1-\operatorname{rk}_Gt
=\dim_G\ker t.
\tag{12}
\]
These are the affiliated-rank facts retained in [Lin93, Lemma 2.3](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#affiliated-closure-and-rank-conventions), not Atiyah integrality for a group-ring matrix.
In particular, (11)--(12) apply to \(t=r\) and \(c=b^{-1}\).
They do not assert a pointwise formula for Hilbert-space kernels obtained by applying the potentially unbounded \(b^{-1}\) to every kernel vector.
The product \(rb^{-1}\) is the affiliated product, including its closure, and the ideal and support argument handles that domain issue.

For any actually invertible denominator \(A\in M_N(U)\), elementary triangular operations over \(U\) reduce
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\v&A\end{pmatrix}
\]
to \(\operatorname{diag}(-uA^{-1}v,A)\).
These operations use \(A^{-1}\) and elementary triangular units, without assuming invertibility of the Schur value.
Affiliated rank invariance therefore gives the exact rank shift
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\v&A\end{pmatrix}
=N+\operatorname{rk}_G(uA^{-1}v).
\]
Applying this formula to (3) and (5), and using (11), proves (6) before needing the already obtained full-rank conclusion.
Subtracting the respective matrix sizes proves equality of all three kernel dimensions.
Since \(\widehat r=P^{-1}\) is a unit, its rank is one, and (4)--(6) follow.

There is also an elementary bounded proof that the original bordered rank was already settled.
For \(Q(f,w_1,w_2)=0\), the first row gives \(w_1=0\), the second then gives \(bw_2=0\), and the third finally gives \(f=0\).
The independently proved injectivity of \(b\) forces \(w_2=0\), so \(Q\) is injective without any Schur-complement calculation.
This is precisely the [existing complementary-block reduction](../ledger/Yin_L_A0005_v1.md#complementary-block-reduction-and-the-scalar-obstruction): the complementary scalar entry for these unmatched borders is \(-b\), giving \(\operatorname{rk}_GQ=2+\operatorname{rk}_Gb=3\).
The normalization supplies a useful certificate in the matched class, but it adds no new rank obstruction for this particular \(Q\).

### Failure of matching and the stronger exact-value obstruction

The original constant borders cannot themselves be matched by any positive definite constant \(W\).
Indeed, \(u=e_1^*\) and \(v=e_2\) satisfy \(uv=0\), whereas \(u=\gamma v^*W\) with \(\gamma\ne0\) and \(W>0\) would imply
\[
uv=\gamma v^*Wv\ne0.
\]
This observation alone concerns that realization; the free-group symmetry proves the stronger obstruction (7) for the value itself.

Now specialize to \(G=F(g,h,k)\).
In this group \(q=gh^{-1}\) and \(k\) do not commute, so the positive-energy calculation is not relying on a simultaneous scalar functional calculus for the two terms of \(P\).
For each \(t\in\mathbb R\), the assignment
\[
\chi_t(g)=\chi_t(h)=e^{it},\qquad \chi_t(k)=1
\]
extends to a unitary character of the free group.
The diagonal unitary \(V_t\delta_x=\chi_t(x)\delta_x\) implements a trace-preserving \(*\)-automorphism \(\alpha_t\) of \(M\), given by \(\alpha_t(x)=\chi_t(x)x\) on group operators, and hence of \(U\).
It preserves positivity and actual affiliated inverses.
Because \(\alpha_t(a)=a\), \(\alpha_t(b)=e^{it}b\), and \(\alpha_t(q)=q\), one has
\[
\alpha_t(P)=P,
\qquad
\alpha_t(L)=E_tLE_t^*,
\qquad
E_t=\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},1),
\]
\[
\alpha_t(r)=e^{it}r,
\qquad
\alpha_t(\widehat r)=\widehat r.
\tag{13}
\]
The value \(r=P^{-1}b\) is nonzero because \(P^{-1}\) is a unit and \(b\ne0\); this nonvanishing does not appeal to any general free-group Atiyah theorem.

Suppose \(A=\operatorname{Re}(zr)\geq0\) for some \(z\ne0\).
Then \(\alpha_\pi(A)=-A\geq0\), so \(A=0\).
Applying \(\alpha_{\pi/2}\) to this zero equality gives \(\operatorname{Re}(izr)=0\), or \(\operatorname{Im}(zr)=0\).
Both real and imaginary parts therefore vanish, giving \(zr=0\), a contradiction.
This proves (7) using positivity in the actual affiliated algebra.

To connect this with arbitrary positive matched realizations, suppose the same value had a representation
\[
r=\gamma v^*W A^{-1}v,
\qquad W>0,
\qquad H=\operatorname{Re}(WA)\geq0,
\]
with constant \(v\ne0\), \(\gamma\ne0\), and \(A\) actually affiliated-invertible.
The retained [half-plane necessity](../ledger/Yin_L_A0005_v1.md#a-positive-realization-restricts-the-scalar-value) is the affiliated identity
\[
\operatorname{Re}(\gamma^{-1}r)
=v^*A^{-*}HA^{-1}v\geq0.
\]
It contradicts (7), independently of the size of \(A\) and even without imposing the stronger energy-kernel condition.
Thus the right multiplier really changes the raw value's membership in this class; changing only a realization while retaining its value cannot achieve that membership.

## Coverage and limitation of the primary test

For the concrete \(F(g,h,k)\) example, full complex-coefficient Atiyah is already covered by [Lin93, Theorem 1.3, printed p. 562](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#group-class-and-exact-theorems), by taking its normal free subgroup to be the whole group and its elementary amenable quotient to be trivial.
The more general class-\(\mathcal C\) theorem also includes this group.
That source coverage is a comparison, not a premise in the derivation above.
The derivation uses only cyclic shift injectivity, finite affiliated support and rank facts, elementary block elimination, and the previously proved energy criterion.

The same explicit positive conclusion applies to the indicated family in every torsion-free group, without assuming that the whole group belongs to Linnell's class, is locally indicable, is amenable, or satisfies Atiyah.
It is therefore an expression-level sufficient condition of universal torsion-free scope, while the chosen group's complete Atiyah conclusion is already known.
The exact-value symmetry obstruction uses characters of \(F(g,h,k)\) and is not silently extended to a torsion-free quotient whose additional relations may obstruct those characters.

This test supplies a concrete successful normalization and a sharp separation between a raw-value obstruction and a kernel-dimension obstruction.
It does not establish a new constraint excluding fractional rank beyond the previous complementary-block and cyclic certificates for this example.
It provides no algorithm that finds an independently invertible multiplier for an arbitrary rational Schur complement, and it gives no extension of the known positive group classes.
No historical novelty or resolution of the general torsion-free Atiyah conjecture is asserted.

## Secondary calibration: a reciprocal-ratio Schur complement

Notation is reset in this secondary calibration: \(a,b,r\) and the later scalar parameter \(q\) are local to the following sections.

Let \(G\) be torsion-free, choose \(g,h\in G\setminus\{e\}\), and initially assume \(g\ne h\).
Identify group elements with their left regular unitaries and put
\[
a=1-g,\qquad b=1-h,\qquad c=b-a=g-h,
\qquad r=a^{-1}b-b^{-1}a.
\]
The [cyclic regular-representation argument](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#cyclic-rational-functions) independently gives the units \(a,b\).
It also gives the unit \(c=g(1-g^{-1}h)\), since \(g^{-1}h\ne e\) has infinite order.
These are units in the actual affiliated algebra, not merely in an abstract division-ring model.

An affine constant-border realization of \(r\) is
\[
L_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-b&0&0\\
0&1&0&0\\
0&0&b&-a\\
0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u_0=(1,0,-1,0),
\qquad
v_0=(0,1,0,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Indeed its two diagonal triangular blocks have inverses
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a^{-1}&a^{-1}b\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}b^{-1}&b^{-1}a\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
so \(L_0\) is actually affiliated-invertible before making any assertion about \(r\), and \(u_0L_0^{-1}v_0=r\).
The entries of \(L_0\) are affine in \(g,h\), with their inverses allowed in the fixed group tuple.
Since \(u_0v_0=0\), no positive definite constant \(W\) and nonzero scalar \(\gamma\) can give \(u_0=\gamma v_0^*W\).
Such an equality would imply \(u_0v_0=\gamma v_0^*Wv_0\ne0\).
Thus this specified realization lies outside the matched positive-energy hypotheses; no assertion is made that every possible realization of its scalar value has that failure.

### Exact ordered transformation

The identity
\[
b r b^{-1}
=b a^{-1}-a b^{-1}
=(b-a)(a^{-1}+b^{-1})
\]
holds without commuting \(a\) and \(b\).
Set
\[
A=c^{-1}b,\qquad B=b^{-1},\qquad t=a^{-1}+b^{-1}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{ArB=t.}
\]
Both multipliers and their inverses are finite products of the independently proved units \(a,b,c\) and their inverses.
The target \(r^{-1}\) is neither assumed nor used in constructing them.
The general affiliated-unit rank argument above gives
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G r=\operatorname{rk}_G t,
\qquad
\dim_G\ker r=\dim_G\ker t.
\]

The transformed element has the matched realization
\[
L_+=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u_+=(1,1),\qquad v_+=(1,1)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad t=u_+L_+^{-1}v_+.
\]
Its real part is
\[
\operatorname{Re}L_+
=\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(2-g-g^{-1},\,2-h-h^{-1}),
\]
which is positive and injective by the cyclic argument.
The [proved energy criterion](../claims/Yin_C_A0003_v1.md#statement) therefore gives \(\operatorname{rk}_G Q_+=3\) for its three-by-three border and makes \(t\) an actual unit.
It follows that \(r\) is an actual unit and the original five-by-five border \(Q_0\) has rank \(5\).
Both scalar kernel dimensions are zero, and both bounded bordered matrices have zero kernel.

More generally, the Schur identities give
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G Q_0-4
=\operatorname{rk}_G r
=\operatorname{rk}_G t
=\operatorname{rk}_G Q_+-2.
\]
Thus their kernel dimensions agree despite their different sizes.
After adjoining an identity block of size two to \(Q_+\), the two borders are equivalent by invertible affiliated matrices: eliminate their denominators to \(\operatorname{diag}(-r,I_4)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(-t,I_4)\), then use \(\operatorname{diag}(A,I_4)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(B,I_4)\).
Every factor in these eliminations is justified by the already known inverses of \(L_0,L_+,A,B\).

### What the successful transfer adds

The endpoint \(t\) is already covered by the [mixed resolvent-sum calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#mixed-resolvent-sums-with-a-scalar-real-part), since \(t+t^*=2\).
In particular \(t^{-1}\) is bounded, with norm at most \(1\).
The energy theorem supplies a literal matched linear-border certificate for that same established conclusion.
The new local calculation is the explicit ordered transfer from the nonmatched reciprocal-ratio realization to that known endpoint, together with verification that all multipliers are independently actual units.
It is not a new positivity principle or a new Atiyah group-class theorem.
The special case \(G=F(g,h)\) already lies in the [free-group case of Lin93](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#group-class-and-exact-theorems), independently of this calculation.
The direct expression-level proof holds for the stated elements in any torsion-free group without asserting the Atiyah property for all matrices of that group.

The transfer does not provide a bounded inverse for \(r\).
For example, in \(G=\mathbb Z=\langle g\rangle\) with \(h=g^{-1}\), one has \(r=g-g^{-1}\), whose cyclic inverse is affiliated and unbounded near its circle zeros.
The preserved statement is analytic rank and kernel dimension, not spectral gap or bounded conditioning.

## A positive real parameter family

For \(q>0\), keep \(g,h\ne e\) and set
\[
r_q=a^{-1}b-q^2b^{-1}a,
\qquad
c_q=b-qa,
\qquad
t_q=a^{-1}+qb^{-1}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
b r_q b^{-1}
=b a^{-1}-q^2a b^{-1}
=(b-qa)(a^{-1}+qb^{-1}).
\tag{S1}
\]
If \(c_q\) is an independently known unit, this gives the valid transformation
\[
c_q^{-1}b\,r_q\,b^{-1}=t_q.
\tag{S2}
\]
For real \(q>0\), the unit condition follows from the previous cyclic and positive-energy tests except for the explicit zero case below.

If \(0<q<1\), write
\[
c_q=-h\bigl[1-(1-q)h^{-1}-q h^{-1}g\bigr].
\]
The real part of the bracket is
\[
\frac{1-q}{2}(2-h-h^{-1})
+\frac q2\bigl(2-h^{-1}g-g^{-1}h\bigr).
\]
Both summands are positive, and the first has zero kernel because \(h\ne e\) and its coefficient is strictly positive.
Thus the bracket is injective and has an affiliated inverse independently of \(r_q\).
If \(q>1\), write instead
\[
c_q=qg\bigl[1-(1-q^{-1})g^{-1}-q^{-1}g^{-1}h\bigr].
\]
Its bracket has the same positive-real-part proof, now using the nontrivial element \(g\) and positive coefficient \(1-q^{-1}\).
These are explicit instances of the [earlier coefficient-dominance boundary argument](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#the-first-additive-scalar-tests), not new group-level inputs.
If \(q=1\) and \(g\ne h\), the already verified cyclic factorization of \(c=g-h\) applies.

For every nonexceptional case, the endpoint in (S2) is matched by
\[
L_+=\operatorname{diag}(a,b),
\qquad
v_+=(1,\sqrt q)^{\mathsf T},\qquad u_+=v_+^*.
\]
The same positive-real-part proof applies, and also
\[
t_q+t_q^*=(1+q)I,
\qquad
\|t_q^{-1}\|\le\frac{2}{1+q}.
\]
Using \(u_0=(1,0,-q^2,0)\) in the original four-state denominator realizes \(r_q\) with a nonmatched border.
Its analytic rank is one and the corresponding five-by-five border has rank five.

If \(g=h\), direct cancellation gives \(r_q=(1-q^2)I\).
For \(q=1\) this is exactly zero, with original bordered rank four; (S2) is not used because \(c_q=0\).
For every other \(q>0\) it is a nonzero scalar unit, agreeing with the preceding proof.
Thus the parameter family has only the displayed zero case and no fractional-rank case under its stated hypotheses.

## The complex-parameter bottleneck

For this comparison assume again that \(g\ne h\), so \(e,g,h\) are distinct.
Identity (S1) remains a formal identity for complex \(q\), but (S2) is allowed only after proving \(c_q\) to be an actual unit.
For example, at \(q=i\),
\[
c_i=(1-i)+ig-h,
\qquad
t_i=a^{-1}+ib^{-1}.
\]
The cyclic positive-real-part argument above no longer certifies \(c_i\), and its three coefficient moduli do not satisfy the earlier dominance condition.
The endpoint is the previously retained complex-phase resolvent type rather than the positive-weight matched family.
For a group covered by Lin93, the known whole-group theorem may supply invertibility of a nonzero numerator, but invoking that theorem would be a different proof input from the requested independent multiplier test.
The present calculation supplies no new independent certificate for this complex-parameter branch and makes no general claim that none exists.
It must not use \(c_i^{-1}\) as an admissible multiplier solely because the formal expression can be written.

The successful positive-real transfer is therefore a controlled calibration of kernel-preserving transformations.
Its source and mathematical inputs were reused at their stated local scopes, and no external literature theorem is needed for the displayed elementary identities.
Further progress requires another independently certified multiplier or a genuinely different rank constraint for a selected expression outside this transferred family.
