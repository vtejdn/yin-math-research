# Character-orbit certificates after complementary-block screening

Date: 2026-11-11 (Asia/Hong_Kong).
The main research agent and two development helpers derived and checked this investigation for Yin under [Yin_T_A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract).
The question is whether an original complementary block surviving the cyclic, coefficient-dominance and positive-energy screens admits a kernel-preserving normalization using independently certified actual units.
The complex-phase resolvent and anticommutator examples below survive the explicitly checked direct tests, whereas the commutator is removed by its complete cyclic reduction.
An additional common integer grading supplies the missing units by the existing finite-eigenspace dimension formula and a character orbit.
This gives a usable certificate beyond the three elementary screens, with known cyclic-extension coverage and no claim of a new Atiyah group class or historical novelty.
All affiliated operations mean closed operations in the actual finite affiliated algebra; matrix traces and kernel dimensions are unnormalized.
The entire investigation has an [independent whole-artifact review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_A0009_v1.md#whole-artifact-finding), including the general affiliated companion argument, every parameter case, the supporting-subgroup interface and the exact known-coverage limits.

## Actual setting and the first complementary block

Let \(G\) be torsion-free and let \(g,h\in G\setminus\{e\}\).
Work in \(M=\mathcal N(G)\), its actual affiliated algebra \(U\), and the actual rational closure of \(\mathbb CG\) in \(U\), using the unnormalized matrix trace.
Every unbounded product below is the closed affiliated product.
For these finite group-ring expressions, one may work in the subgroup generated by their displayed group labels and then use the [coset decomposition](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#reduction-to-finitely-generated-groups) to preserve the resulting kernel dimensions in an overgroup.
Accordingly, a grading used below is needed on that supporting subgroup; it need not extend to an unrelated larger ambient group.
Set
\[
a=1-g,\qquad b=1-h,\qquad
t=a^{-1}+ib^{-1},
\]
\[
s=b+ia=(1+i)-ig-h,\qquad
c=b-ia=(1-i)+ig-h.
\]
The cyclic regular-representation argument independently certifies \(a,b\) as actual units.
It does not yet certify \(s,c\).
The exact denominator clearing is
\[
a t b=s.
\tag{C1}
\]
Thus \(t\) and \(s\) have the same analytic rank and kernel dimension by the [affiliated-unit rank argument](../ledger/Yin_L_A0006_v1.md#affiliated-rank-and-the-kernel-dimension-statement).

Use the two-state realization
\[
L=\operatorname{diag}(a,b),\qquad u=(1,i),\qquad v=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The constant matrices
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
satisfy \(uT=e_1^*\), \(Sv=e_1\), and
\[
SLT=\begin{pmatrix}a&-ia\\-a&ia+b\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently the [complementary-block formula](../ledger/Yin_L_A0005_v1.md#complementary-block-reduction-and-the-scalar-obstruction) gives
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G Q_t=2+\operatorname{rk}_G s,
\qquad
\dim_G\ker Q_t=\dim_G\ker s.
\tag{C2}
\]
The original complementary block and the ordered polynomial numerator are the same scalar \(s\); they are not two independent reductions of difficulty.

## Exact screening in the concrete free-group example

Now take \(G=F(g,h)\), with the indicated free basis.
The support \(\{e,g,h\}\) is not contained in a cyclic subgroup, and no translation of that support can be contained in a cyclic coset: its pairwise differences generate a noncyclic subgroup.
Thus the original numerator is not a scalar cyclic rational function or a monomial translate of one.
This does not assert that every possible factorization into cyclic units has been excluded.
Its nonzero coefficient lengths are \(\sqrt2,1,1\), which satisfy all three strict triangle inequalities.
Hence no coefficient is dominant in the [previous scalar test](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#the-first-additive-scalar-tests).
The same statements hold for \(c\).

There is an explicit finite-support witness excluding every scalar-phase positive-real-part test for \(s\).
Let \(A=g+g^{-1}+h+h^{-1}\), and let \(e_j\) be the normalized sum of the reduced words of length \(j\), for \(0\le j\le3\).
These are orthonormal vectors in \(\ell^2(F(g,h))\).
The consecutive radial matrix coefficients of \(A\) are \(2,\sqrt3,\sqrt3\).
For
\[
\xi=2e_0-3e_1+3e_2-2e_3,
\]
one obtains directly
\[
\|\xi\|^2=26,\qquad
\frac{\langle \operatorname{Re}(g+h)\xi,\xi\rangle}{\|\xi\|^2}
=-\frac{12+15\sqrt3}{26}<-\sqrt2.
\tag{C3}
\]
Only the displayed finite tree edges enter this quadratic-form calculation; no formula for the full free-group spectrum is needed.

For any \(\zeta\in\mathbb T\), a unitary character of the free group can multiply \(g,h\) by the independently chosen phases \(i/\zeta,-1/\zeta\).
The resulting trace-preserving automorphism \(\alpha\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Re}\alpha(\zeta s)
=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\zeta(1+i)\bigr)I
  +\operatorname{Re}(g+h).
\]
Since the scalar term is at most \(\sqrt2\), (C3) gives a negative quadratic form.
Positivity is preserved by \(\alpha\), so
\[
\operatorname{Re}(z s)\not\ge0
\qquad(z\in\mathbb C^\times).
\tag{C4}
\]
Complex conjugation of the standard group basis exchanges \(s\) and \(c\), so it proves the same statement for \(c\).
Thus neither original scalar block has a phase-rotated nonnegative real part, even before checking injectivity of that real part.
This excludes the direct scalar positive-energy test; it does not exclude every possible larger realization or independently certified multiplication.

## No constant positive weight repairs the displayed diagonal realization

For this section it is enough that \(G\) is torsion-free and \(h\notin\langle g\rangle\).
Suppose that a positive definite constant matrix
\[
W=\begin{pmatrix}x&z\\\overline z&y\end{pmatrix}
\]
made \(H=\operatorname{Re}(W\operatorname{diag}(a,b))\) nonnegative.
Compress \(H\) to \(\ell^2(\langle g\rangle)^2\); the compression \(H_0\) remains positive and deletes the \(h,h^{-1}\) coefficients because \(h\notin\langle g\rangle\).
In the cyclic Fourier representation the compression is the continuous matrix function
\[
H_0(e^{i\theta})=
\begin{pmatrix}
x(1-\cos\theta)&z(1-e^{-i\theta}/2)\\
\overline z(1-e^{i\theta}/2)&y
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{C5}
\]
At \(\theta=0\) its determinant is \(-|z|^2/4\).
If \(z\ne0\), continuity gives a negative eigenvalue on a positive-measure arc, contradicting positivity of the compression.
Therefore \(z=0\).
But the matching equation \((1,i)=\gamma(1,1)W\) would then imply \(i=y/x\), which is impossible because \(x,y>0\).
There is consequently no positive constant weight that simultaneously supplies the matching and positive-real-part hypotheses for this particular two-state realization.
When \(h\in\langle g\rangle\), the expressions instead lie in the already covered cyclic field.

## The reciprocal-ratio complementary block and an independent rank bound

Return to arbitrary torsion-free \(G\) with \(g,h\ne e\), and put
\[
r=a^{-1}b+b^{-1}a.
\]
This is the \(q=i\) member of the reciprocal-ratio family.
The known four-state denominator is
\[
L_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-b&0&0\\
0&1&0&0\\
0&0&b&-a\\
0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
u_0=(1,0,1,0),\qquad
v_0=(0,1,0,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Its two triangular blocks are independently invertible through the cyclic units \(a,b\), and its compression is \(r\).
The equality \(u_0v_0=0\) rules out matching for this realization, as in the preceding investigation.

Choose a constant column change with columns \(e_1,e_2,e_3-e_1,e_4\), and a constant row change with rows \(e_2^*,e_1^*,e_3^*,e_4^*-e_2^*\).
They send \(u_0\) and \(v_0\) to the first coordinate row and column.
The resulting complementary block is
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}
-b&-a&0\\
0&b&-a\\
-1&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Eliminating its lower-right constant pivot gives
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G C
=1+\operatorname{rk}_G J,
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}-b&-a\\-a&b\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Left multiplication by \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\) makes
\[
\begin{pmatrix}b&a\\-a&b\end{pmatrix}
=bI_2+a\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The last constant matrix has eigenvectors \((1,i)^{\mathsf T}\) and \((1,-i)^{\mathsf T}\), so a constant unitary change of basis gives \(\operatorname{diag}(s,c)\).
No commutation between \(a,b\) is used.
For the five-by-five border \(Q_r\), the exact identities are therefore
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G Q_r
=3+\operatorname{rk}_G s+\operatorname{rk}_G c,
\]
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G r
=\operatorname{rk}_G s+\operatorname{rk}_G c-1,
\qquad
\dim_G\ker r
=\dim_G\ker s+\dim_G\ker c.
\tag{C6}
\]

Coordinatewise complex conjugation is an antiunitary fixing the real permutation matrices of all regular group elements.
It exchanges the bounded operators \(s,c\), hence their kernel projections, and preserves the canonical trace evaluated at the real vector \(\delta_e\).
Thus \(\operatorname{rk}_G s=\operatorname{rk}_G c\), and (C6) gives
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G r=2\operatorname{rk}_G s-1,
\qquad
\dim_G\ker r=2\dim_G\ker s,
\qquad
\operatorname{rk}_G s\ge\tfrac12.
\tag{C7}
\]
The last inequality is independent of any Atiyah integrality assumption.
It still allows every \(\operatorname{rk}_G s\in[1/2,1)\), and thus does not exclude a proper nonzero rational rank.
This bound is an elementary consequence of the paired scalar blocks and conjugation; it is not a resolution of their unit problem.

## The character-orbit certificate

Let \((M,\tau)\) be finite with faithful normal tracial state, and let \(T\in M_n(\mathcal U(M))\).
Suppose trace-preserving automorphisms \(\alpha_\theta\) of \(M_n(M)\), extended to its actual affiliated algebra, satisfy
\[
\alpha_\theta(T)=e^{id\theta}T
\qquad(\theta\in\mathbb R),
\qquad d\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.
\tag{G1}
\]
Then, for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times\),
\[
\ker(T-\lambda I_n)=\{0\},\qquad
\operatorname{rk}_M(T-\lambda I_n)=n,
\qquad T-\lambda I_n\in GL_n(\mathcal U(M)).
\tag{G2}
\]
Only the infinite phase orbit matters; continuity of the automorphisms is not needed for this conclusion.

For distinct complex eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\), let \(p_j\) be their kernel projections.
The accepted [MSY23 Lemma 5.19 and its domain-correct bounded-row proof](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U006.md#lemma-519-domain-repair) give
\[
\sum_{j=1}^m(\operatorname{Tr}_n\otimes\tau)(p_j)
=(\operatorname{Tr}_n\otimes\tau)\left(\bigvee_jp_j\right)\le n.
\tag{G3}
\]
That source interface requires no maximal-Delta, freeness, integrality or generating-tuple assumption.
To recall the domain issue precisely, the bounded row \(B=(p_1\ \cdots\ p_m)\) is injective on the direct sum of the eigenspaces by a scalar Vandermonde argument.
Each genuine eigenvector belongs to every power domain of \(T\), and the argument applies powers only to finite sums of such vectors.
The rectangular polar decomposition of \(B\) then gives (G3); it neither assumes orthogonality nor passes an unbounded operator through a norm limit.

The automorphism in (G1) sends \(p_\lambda\) to \(p_{e^{-id\theta}\lambda}\), preserving its trace \(\delta\).
For \(\lambda\ne0\), choose arbitrarily many distinct points of this orbit and apply (G3).
The inequalities \(m\delta\le n\) for every positive integer \(m\) imply \(\delta=0\).
Faithfulness gives a zero kernel projection, and finite-trace polar decomposition gives the actual affiliated inverse in (G2).
No inverse or full-rank conclusion for the target was assumed.
The same argument with only an orbit of \(m\) distinct values gives the weaker bound \(\delta\le n/m\), which by itself need not exclude fractional dimension.
The zero eigenvalue has no nontrivial phase orbit and is not controlled by this argument.

For a group homomorphism \(\phi:G\to\mathbb Z\), the diagonal unitary
\[
V_\theta\delta_x=e^{i\theta\phi(x)}\delta_x
\]
implements \(\alpha_\theta(\lambda(x))=e^{i\theta\phi(x)}\lambda(x)\) and preserves the canonical trace.
Thus any finite group-ring sum supported in one nonzero degree satisfies (G1).
This construction and conclusion do not require torsion-freeness of the whole group.
In the applications below, the additional condition \(\phi(g)=\phi(h)=1\) itself ensures that \(g,h\) have infinite order.
Torsion-freeness alone does not guarantee that specified elements admit such a common grading.
For example, the Klein bottle relation \(xyx^{-1}=y^{-1}\) forces every homomorphism to \(\mathbb Z\) to vanish on \(y\); the pair \(x,y\) therefore has no such common grading, even though its separate [finite-index abelian calibration](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#a-noncommutative-calibration) is already covered.

## A companion reduction for one-sided graded polynomials

Let \(\alpha_\theta\) be a trace-preserving action on \(M\), extended entrywise and to affiliated operators.
Let \(A_0,\ldots,A_d\in M_n(\mathcal U(M))\), with \(d\ge1\), satisfy
\[
\alpha_\theta(A_j)=e^{ij\theta}A_j,
\qquad
A_0\in GL_n(\mathcal U(M)),
\]
where the unit premise on \(A_0\) has been independently justified.
Then \(P=A_0+\cdots+A_d\) is an actual affiliated unit.
There is no hypothesis that the coefficient algebra is a division ring.

Put \(B_j=A_0^{-1}A_j\) and form the actual affiliated companion matrix
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}
-B_1&-B_2&\cdots&-B_d\\
I_n&0&\cdots&0\\
0&I_n&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D_\theta=\operatorname{diag}(I_n,e^{-i\theta}I_n,\ldots,e^{-i(d-1)\theta}I_n).
\tag{G4}
\]
For \(d=1\), this means simply \(T=-B_1\).
Checking each nonzero block gives
\[
\alpha_\theta(T)=e^{i\theta}D_\theta T D_\theta^{-1}.
\]
Consequently \(\beta_\theta=\operatorname{Ad}(D_\theta^{-1})\circ\alpha_\theta\) is trace preserving and satisfies \(\beta_\theta(T)=e^{i\theta}T\).
The character-orbit certificate gives \(I_{dn}-T\in GL_{dn}(\mathcal U(M))\).

In \(I_{dn}-T\), the lower-right \((d-1)n\)-square block is lower bidiagonal with diagonal \(I_n\) and subdiagonal \(-I_n\), so it has an explicit finite triangular inverse.
Its Schur complement is \(I_n+B_1+\cdots+B_d=A_0^{-1}P\).
Thus the justified affiliated elimination gives
\[
\operatorname{rk}_M(I_{dn}-T)
=(d-1)n+\operatorname{rk}_M(A_0^{-1}P),
\qquad
\dim_M\ker P=\dim_M\ker(I_{dn}-T)=0.
\tag{G5}
\]
For \(d=1\) the same formula is immediate without a lower block.
All eliminations use \(A_0^{-1}\) and triangular units, not \(P^{-1}\).
If \(A_0^{-1}\) or the coefficients are unbounded, (G5) remains a matrix identity in the affiliated algebra; no unrestricted Hilbert-space eigenvector substitution is used.
An already justified invertible extreme homogeneous coefficient can likewise be normalized to degree zero by an actual homogeneous unit, after reversing the grading when needed.
Neither the existence of that extreme coefficient nor its invertibility follows from the mere presence of some nontrivial group character.

## Certified multipliers for the complex-phase examples

The ordered algebraic identities are
\[
t=a^{-1}s b^{-1},\qquad
r=b^{-1}c a^{-1}s.
\tag{C8}
\]
The second follows by expanding \(c a^{-1}s=b a^{-1}b+a\), with cancellation of the middle scalar terms.
Assume now that \(\phi(g)=\phi(h)=1\).
The homogeneous elements \(ig+h\) and \(h-ig\) have nonzero scalar shifts \(1+i\) and \(1-i\), so (G2) independently proves that \(s,c\) are actual units before either target inverse is used.
The valid normalizations are
\[
t\,(b s^{-1})=a^{-1},
\qquad
(c^{-1}b)\,r\,s^{-1}=a^{-1}.
\tag{C9}
\]
Their endpoint has the scalar matched realization \(L_+=a\), \(u_+=v_+=1\), whose positive real part is \((2-g-g^{-1})/2\) and is injective.
Every multiplier in (C9) is then independently justified, and affiliated rank invariance preserves the exact scalar kernel dimension.
The second identity also transports the original five-by-five bordered kernel dimension to that of the two-by-two scalar matched border, with the corresponding rank shift of three.

The mathematical work in such a successful application is the independent scalar certificate for \(s,c\); the subsequent multiplication is elementary denominator clearing.
For the concrete free group, complete Atiyah coverage is already known from [Lin93](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#group-class-and-exact-theorems).
Using that theorem would settle the example but would not supply the requested new independent mechanism.
The character certificate above discharges the scalar-unit premises in (C9) under the common grading.
None of the raw screening arguments above derives that extra hypothesis from torsion-freeness alone.

## The full complex parameter range under the common grading

Keep \(a=1-g\), \(b=1-h\), and assume \(G\) torsion-free with \(\phi(g)=\phi(h)=1\).
For \(q\in\mathbb C\), write
\[
t_q=a^{-1}+qb^{-1},\qquad
r_q=a^{-1}b-q^2b^{-1}a,
\]
\[
s_q=b+qa=(1+q)-qg-h,
\qquad
c_q=b-qa=(1-q)+qg-h.
\tag{G6}
\]
For \(q\ne-1\), the degree-one element \(qg+h\) and nonzero scalar \(1+q\) make \(s_q\) a unit by (G2).
For \(q\ne1\), the same argument with \(h-qg\) and \(1-q\) makes \(c_q\) a unit.
At \(q=-1\) or \(q=1\), the remaining zero-constant numerator is \(g-h\), which is a cyclically certified unit if \(g\ne h\).
The other numerator has constant term \(2\) and is covered by the preceding argument or the old positive-energy test.

The exact identities
\[
t_q=a^{-1}s_qb^{-1},\qquad
r_q=b^{-1}c_qa^{-1}s_q
\tag{G7}
\]
give the complete classification in this family.
If \(g\ne h\), both \(t_q,r_q\) are actual rational units for every \(q\in\mathbb C\).
If \(g=h\), then \(t_q=(1+q)a^{-1}\) and \(r_q=(1-q^2)I\), so the only zero cases are respectively \(q=-1\) and \(q=\pm1\).
Every other case is a unit.
Whenever the respective numerators are units, the independently certified normalizations are
\[
t_q(bs_q^{-1})=a^{-1},\qquad
(c_q^{-1}b)r_qs_q^{-1}=a^{-1}.
\tag{G8}
\]
This extends the positive-real parameter range of the [preceding calibration](../ledger/Yin_L_A0006_v1.md#a-positive-real-parameter-family) under the stated additional grading.
It makes no assertion that the complex branch over every torsion-free group is now settled.

## A common four-state realization and its actual complementary block

Let \(G\) be torsion-free, let \(g,h\in G\setminus\{e\}\), and put
\[
a=1-g,\qquad b=1-h,\qquad
r_\eta=a^{-1}b^{-1}-\eta b^{-1}a^{-1}.
\]
The [cyclic regular-representation argument](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#cyclic-rational-functions) gives the actual affiliated units \(a,b\) before any assertion about \(r_\eta\).
For \(\eta\in\mathbb C\), use the affine denominator
\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-1&0&0\\
0&b&0&0\\
0&0&b&-1\\
0&0&0&a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u_\eta=(1,0,-\eta,0),
\qquad
v=(0,1,0,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Its two triangular inverse blocks are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a^{-1}&a^{-1}b^{-1}\\0&b^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}b^{-1}&b^{-1}a^{-1}\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus \(L\) is independently invertible and \(u_\eta L^{-1}v=r_\eta\).
The specified borders satisfy \(u_\eta v=0\), so they cannot satisfy \(u_\eta=\gamma v^*W\) with \(W>0\) and \(\gamma\ne0\).

For the two cases \(\eta=1,-1\), the original bounded border \(Q_\eta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&u_\eta\\v&L\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has the complementary block
\[
A_\eta=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-1&0\\
\eta b&0&-1\\
0&b&-a
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{A1}
\]
One direct derivation is to impose the first border equation \(w_1-\eta w_3=0\), hence \(w_3=\eta w_1\), and eliminate the border coordinate \(f\) by subtracting the second and fourth denominator equations.
The remaining equations on \((w_1,w_2,w_4)\) are exactly \(A_\eta(w_1,w_2,w_4)^{\mathsf T}=0\).
These are bounded constant coordinate operations of the [established complementary-block reduction](../ledger/Yin_L_A0005_v1.md#complementary-block-reduction-and-the-scalar-obstruction), so
\[
\operatorname{rk}_GQ_\eta=2+\operatorname{rk}_GA_\eta.
\tag{A2}
\]

Permuting the columns of \(A_\eta\) to \((w_2,w_4,w_1)\) gives
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0&a\\
0&-1&\eta b\\
b&-a&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Replacing the third row by its sum with \(b\) times the first row minus \(a\) times the second row produces the diagonal pivots \(-1,-1\) and the remaining entry \(ba-\eta ab\).
All of these pivots and triangular operations are invertible already over the group ring.
For \(\eta=1,-1\), the last entry is \(-\eta p_\eta\), where
\[
p_\eta=ab-\eta ba
=(1-\eta)(1-g-h)+gh-\eta hg.
\tag{A3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{rk}_GA_\eta=2+\operatorname{rk}_Gp_\eta,
\qquad
\operatorname{rk}_GQ_\eta=4+\operatorname{rk}_Gp_\eta.
\tag{A4}
\]
This calculation checks the actual complementary block rather than merely inspecting the original denominator.

There is also an exact scalar transfer for every \(\eta\):
\[
\boxed{(ba)r_\eta(ab)=ab-\eta ba=p_\eta.}
\tag{A5}
\]
The left and right factors are products of the independently certified units \(a,b\).
No inverse of \(r_\eta\) or \(p_\eta\) is used.
The [affiliated rank-invariance argument](../ledger/Yin_L_A0006_v1.md#affiliated-rank-and-the-kernel-dimension-statement) therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{rk}_Gr_\eta=\operatorname{rk}_Gp_\eta,
\qquad
\dim_G\ker r_\eta=\dim_G\ker p_\eta.
\tag{A6}
\]
This is an equality of closed affiliated kernel dimensions, not a pointwise bijection obtained by applying an unbounded inverse to arbitrary kernel vectors.

## The commutator is removed by the completed old screening

At \(\eta=1\),
\[
p_1=gh-hg=gh\bigl(1-h^{-1}g^{-1}hg\bigr).
\]
If \(g,h\) commute, \(p_1=r_1=0\).
Otherwise the group element \(h^{-1}g^{-1}hg\) is nonidentity and has infinite order, so the old cyclic criterion makes \(p_1\) a unit.
Equations (A4)--(A6) give
\[
\operatorname{rk}_GQ_1=
\begin{cases}
4,&gh=hg,\\
5,&gh\ne hg.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the complete complementary-block screening already resolves this commutator over every torsion-free group.
The ordered rational identity is useful expression-level evidence, but it supplies no additional rank principle after the existing cyclic certificate is used.
Stopping the screening at the raw three-by-three block would have incorrectly classified this as a remaining case.

## The anticommutator and the exact limits of the old tests

Now take \(\eta=-1\), initially in the concrete group \(G=F(g,h)\), and abbreviate
\[
r=r_{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-1}+b^{-1}a^{-1},
\qquad
p=p_{-1}=2-2g-2h+gh+hg.
\]
The five displayed support elements of \(p\) are distinct.
Their coefficient moduli are \(2,2,2,1,1\), so no coefficient dominates the sum of the others.
The support is not contained in a translate of a cyclic subgroup: because it contains \(e,g,h\), such a containment would force the noncommuting \(g,h\) into one cyclic subgroup.
These facts exclude the direct cyclic-membership and coefficient-dominance tests, without asserting that every possible further rational factorization has been classified.

The scalar positive-energy test fails even after an arbitrary nonzero complex rotation.
Define the exact finitely supported vector
\[
\begin{aligned}
\xi={}&3\delta_e
 +2(\delta_g+\delta_{g^{-1}}+\delta_h+\delta_{h^{-1}})\\
&+\delta_{g^2}+\delta_{g^{-2}}+\delta_{h^2}+\delta_{h^{-2}}
 +\delta_{gh^{-1}}+\delta_{g^{-1}h}
 +\delta_{hg^{-1}}+\delta_{h^{-1}g}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A7}
\]
Free-word cancellation gives the following exact finite sums:

| Operator \(x\) | \(\langle x\xi,\xi\rangle\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(1\) | \(33\) |
| \(g\) | \(20\) |
| \(h\) | \(20\) |
| \(gh\) | \(6\) |
| \(hg\) | \(6\) |

For example, each entry is obtained from the explicit coefficient function \(c\) of (A7) by the finite sum \(\sum_w c(w)c(xw)\).
Thus
\[
\langle p\xi,\xi\rangle
=2\cdot33-2\cdot20-2\cdot20+6+6=-2,
\qquad
\tau(p)=2.
\tag{A8}
\]
If \(\operatorname{Re}(zp)\ge0\), its trace gives \(\operatorname{Re}z\ge0\), whereas (A8) gives \(\operatorname{Re}z\le0\).
Hence its trace is zero, and faithfulness forces \(\operatorname{Re}(zp)=0\).
For nonzero \(z\) with zero real part this would make \(p\) selfadjoint, contrary to its unequal coefficients at \(g\) and \(g^{-1}\).
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Re}(zp)\not\ge0
\qquad(z\in\mathbb C^\times).
\tag{A9}
\]
The [half-plane necessity](../ledger/Yin_L_A0005_v1.md#a-positive-realization-restricts-the-scalar-value) consequently excludes every exact-value positive matched realization of this particular \(p\), at any denominator size.
This assertion concerns \(p\); no identical every-realization assertion about the rational value \(r\) is needed here.

The original complementary block \(A_{-1}\) also fails the displayed constant-weight energy test, even with an arbitrary scalar rotation.
Entrywise group trace sends it to
\[
A_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&0\\
-1&0&-1\\
0&1&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}A_0=0,
\qquad
\det(\lambda I-A_0)=\lambda^3-\lambda-2.
\tag{A10}
\]
The cubic has one nonzero real root and two nonreal roots: its two real stationary values, at \(\pm1/\sqrt3\), are both negative.
Suppose \(\operatorname{Re}(zWA_{-1})\ge0\) for a positive definite constant \(W\) and \(z\ne0\).
The positive entrywise-trace map gives \(\operatorname{Re}(zWA_0)\ge0\).
For every eigenvalue \(\mu\) and eigenvector \(v_\mu\) of \(A_0\),
\[
v_\mu^*\operatorname{Re}(zWA_0)v_\mu
=\operatorname{Re}(z\mu)\,v_\mu^*Wv_\mu\ge0.
\]
Since the three eigenvalues sum to zero, all three real parts \(\operatorname{Re}(z\mu)\) must vanish.
They would all lie on one line through the origin, which is impossible for one nonzero real root and a nonreal conjugate pair.
Thus no such \(z,W\) exists.
This excludes the stated direct constant-weight test on \(A_{-1}\), not arbitrary nonconstant or rational equivalences of that block.

## An additional grading resolves the remaining numerator

Assume now, more generally, that the selected \(g,h\) in a torsion-free \(G\) admit a homomorphism
\[
\phi:G\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
\qquad \phi(g)=\phi(h)=1.
\tag{A11}
\]
The free-group example has this homomorphism.
It is additional structure and is not supplied by torsion-freeness alone.
The character \(\chi_t(x)=e^{it\phi(x)}\) gives a trace-preserving automorphism \(\alpha_t\) of \(\mathcal N(G)\).
For \(\eta\ne1\), normalize
\[
P_\eta(z)=1-z(g+h)+z^2\frac{gh-\eta hg}{1-\eta},
\qquad
p_\eta=(1-\eta)P_\eta(1).
\tag{A12}
\]
The bounded companion matrix
\[
T_\eta=
\begin{pmatrix}
g+h&-\dfrac{gh-\eta hg}{1-\eta}\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{A13}
\]
is defined without the inverse of any group-ring numerator.
For \(z\ne0\), its eigenvalue equation has the exact kernel parametrization
\[
\ker(T_\eta-z^{-1}I_2)
=\{(v,zv):v\in\ker P_\eta(z)\}.
\tag{A14}
\]
For \(|z|=1\), the map \(v\mapsto 2^{-1/2}(v,zv)\) is a bounded equivariant isometry, so these two kernels have equal von Neumann dimensions.

By (A11)--(A12),
\[
P_\eta(e^{it})=\alpha_t(P_\eta(1)),
\]
and therefore all the kernels \(\ker P_\eta(e^{it})\) have the same dimension \(\delta\).
For any finite collection of distinct nonzero complex numbers \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\), the corresponding eigenspaces of the fixed bounded \(T_\eta\) are linearly independent.
Indeed, applying \(\prod_{k\ne j}(T_\eta-\lambda_k I)\) to a relation \(\sum_j x_j=0\), with \(T_\eta x_j=\lambda_jx_j\), isolates a nonzero scalar multiple of \(x_j\).
The bounded equivariant sum map from the Hilbert direct sum of these eigenspaces to \(\ell^2(G)^2\) is consequently injective.
Finite-trace polar decomposition gives the dimension bound
\[
\sum_{j=1}^m\dim_G\ker(T_\eta-\lambda_jI)\le2.
\tag{A15}
\]
Using \(m\) distinct unit-circle values in (A14) gives \(m\delta\le2\) for every \(m\), hence \(\delta=0\).
Thus \(p_\eta\) is injective and has an actual affiliated inverse for every \(\eta\ne1\) under (A11).
This proof uses bounded operators and bounded graph maps throughout; it requires no cutoff argument for unbounded eigenvectors.

For the anticommutator \(\eta=-1\), this proves the unit \(p\) independently of \(r^{-1}\), from the explicit grading, companion construction and finite trace.
Together with (A5), it gives
\[
r^{-1}=(ab)p^{-1}(ba),
\qquad
\operatorname{rk}_G A_{-1}=3,
\qquad
\operatorname{rk}_G Q_{-1}=5.
\tag{A16}
\]
Every scalar and bordered kernel dimension in this example is zero.
The same conclusion for \(r_\eta\) holds under (A11) for \(\eta\ne1\); the case \(\eta=1\) has already been classified by the universal cyclic argument.

If a literal endpoint in the old matched class is desired after this independent proof, there is the legal transformation
\[
(ba)r\bigl(abp^{-1}\bigr)=1.
\tag{A17}
\]
The endpoint \(1\) has the scalar matched realization \(L=u=v=W=1\).
The right factor in (A17) is justified by the newly established unit \(p\), rather than by a merely formal inverse.
Equation (A17) adds no rank information beyond that independent unit proof.
The substantive transfer before that proof is (A5), using only the old cyclic units; the additional rank input is the grading argument, not the final normalization to \(1\).

## Scope and continuation

The commutator control is already exhausted by the cyclic criterion after its full complementary-block reduction.
The anticommutator passes the specific old screening above, and its success uses (A11) in addition to torsion-freeness.
The support and positivity obstructions in (A7)--(A10) describe the specified actual regular free-group example, without proving the absence of every other possible old-factor decomposition.
The positive graded conclusion does not require that the full group satisfy Atiyah, but it settles only the indicated expressions.
For the chosen \(F(g,h)\), complete complex-coefficient Atiyah is already covered by [Lin93, Theorem 1.3](../audits/literature/Lin93/Yin_U001.md#group-class-and-exact-theorems).
That whole-group theorem is a coverage comparison, not an input to (A11)--(A16).
The graded conclusion is also covered by the known [natural-extension mechanism](../audits/literature/JZLA20/Yin_U001.md#natural-extensions) and [JZLA20 Proposition 3.11](../audits/literature/JZLA20/Yin_U001.md#hughes-free), independently of whether the entire group satisfies Atiyah.
Indeed, put \(H=\langle g,h\rangle\), \(N=\ker(\phi|_H)\), \(t=g\), \(n=hg^{-1}\in N\), and let \(\sigma\) denote conjugation by \(g\).
Then \(H=N\rtimes\langle t\rangle\), and the normalized numerator is the skew polynomial
\[
\frac{p_\eta}{1-\eta}
=1-(1+n)t+\frac{\sigma(n)-\eta n}{1-\eta}\,t^2
\qquad(\eta\ne1).
\]
Its multiplication matrix on every finite truncation modulo \(t^m\) is triangular with identity diagonal, so its normalized truncated rank is exactly one.
Proposition 3.11 identifies the actual von Neumann rank on \(\mathbb C[H]\) with this natural extension, giving the same full-rank conclusion.
The audited Proposition 3.5 criterion \(r'(I_d+At)=d\) describes the corresponding regular-coefficient matrix interface.
Thus the character-companion argument gives an explicit independent certificate for the selected examples, while its positive conclusion is already covered by known cyclic-extension theory.
No new general rank principle or historical novelty is asserted.
Without an applicable grading or another independent argument for the surviving numerator, the formal factor \(p^{-1}\) is unavailable and (A17) cannot be used.
