# Common-power reciprocal arrays through the next Schur elimination

This investigation continues [route four](../strategies/Yin_S_A0004.md) under the exact [A0001 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract): every torsion-free group and every finite rectangular matrix over its complex group ring.
The [accepted common-power factorization](../claims/Yin_C_A0009_v6.md#argument) supplies the initial scalar inverses.
The [existing inverse-difference calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0011_v1.md#one-inverse-difference-schur-step) then certifies two pivots of a reciprocal array, and an explicit rational expression isolates the third obstruction.
A noncommutative common-power example below has three independently certified pivots, while another has an exact final zero pivot despite all initial binomial gates being units.
Both examples belong to the already controlled normalized-coefficient mechanism; neither gives new positive group coverage or a reduction of arbitrary Task inputs.
The literal arbitrary-coefficient construction in [C_A0006](../claims/Yin_C_A0006_v1.md#statement) does not satisfy the new exact common-power condition in general, so its all-size equivalence cannot simply be transferred to this restricted family.

## The attempted complete chain and its first gap

Start with an arbitrary torsion-free \(H\) and \(A\in M_{r,n}(\mathbb CH)\), using unnormalized matrix traces.
Its finite support permits restriction to a finitely generated subgroup, with unchanged kernel dimension, as in the Task contract.
The intended route would exhibit actual independently invertible matrices \(P,Q\), an integer \(s\ge0\), and a reciprocal common-power array \(K\) such that
\[
P\operatorname{diag}(A,I_s)Q=K,
\qquad
K\in M_{r+s,n+s}(\mathcal U(\mathcal N(H))),
\tag{1}
\]
or an equally explicit stabilization with all added zero and identity coordinates accounted for.
An enlargement to a specified torsion-free group containing \(H\) is also admissible if the embedding and preservation of the original canonical kernel dimension are proved; no such enlargement or reduction is assumed here.
Equation (1) would give
\[
\dim_H\ker A=\dim_H\ker K=(n+s)-\operatorname{rk}_H K.
\]
The required multiplier certificates cannot assume the integer-rank assertion for \(A\).
No such representation is presently available for arbitrary \(A\).
The retained [three-monomial row splitting](../ledger/Yin_L_A0010_v1.md#an-elementary-row-splitting-step) adds independently eliminable identity coordinates, but supplies neither common powers nor an inverse-difference rectangle.
This is the first gap before any induction on the proposed class begins.

Even assuming (1), a second independent problem remains: every successive scalar residual must be zero or have an independently certified actual inverse, with zero rows and columns handled by their exact rank contributions.
The third-pivot calculation below identifies a concrete failure of automatic certification after the first two steps.
Thus a positive small array is a mechanism test, not completion of either missing implication.

## Actual ambient algebra and the initial gates

Let \(G\) be torsion-free, and let \(a_1,\ldots,a_p,b_1,\ldots,b_q\in G\) satisfy actual identities
\[
a_i^{m_i}=b_j^{n_j}=z\ne e,
\qquad m_i,n_j\ge1.
\tag{2}
\]
Work in the subgroup generated by these labels; \(z\) is central in that subgroup because it is a power of every generator.
The subgroup restriction preserves the canonical kernel dimensions, and no centrality in an unrelated larger ambient group is required.
All further relations in the actual subgroup remain allowed.
Set \(U=\mathcal U(\mathcal N(G))\), with the canonical trace, and interpret all products, sums and inverses as closed affiliated-ring operations.
The [retained finite-affiliated Schur interface](../ledger/Yin_L_A0009_v1.md#the-exact-size-reducing-step) supplies rank invariance under actual matrix units and the rank increment at a certified scalar pivot.
It does not assert an everywhere-defined Hilbert-space change of variables by an unbounded multiplier.

Write
\[
c=z-1,
\qquad x_i=(a_i-1)^{-1},
\qquad y_j=(b_j-1)^{-1},
\]
\[
F_{ij}=\sum_{u=0}^{m_i-1}a_i^u-\sum_{v=0}^{n_j-1}b_j^v
       =c(x_i-y_j).
\tag{3}
\]
Every inverse defining \(x_i,y_j,c^{-1}\) is independently justified by the [cyclic regular-shift certificate](../ledger/Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#cyclic-rational-functions), since (2) makes all labels nonidentity.
Assume the row labels are mutually distinct, the column labels are mutually distinct, and \(a_i\ne b_j\) for every pair used below.
Then every cross difference and every same-side difference among the \(x_i,y_j\) is independently a unit.
For example,
\[
x_i-y_j=(a_i-1)^{-1}(b_j-a_i)(b_j-1)^{-1},
\tag{4}
\]
and \(b_j-a_i=a_i(a_i^{-1}b_j-1)\) has its own cyclic unit certificate.
The same ordered difference-of-inverses identity handles \(x_i-x_k\) and \(y_j-y_l\).
If an initial label equality occurs, it must be treated explicitly; (4) supplies no inverse of a zero difference.

The additive matrix \((F_{ij})\) is not a useful unrestricted-rank test.
Indeed
\[
(F_{ij})
=c\begin{pmatrix}x_1&1\\ \vdots&\vdots\\x_p&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\-y_1&\cdots&-y_q\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \operatorname{rk}_G(F_{ij})\le2.
\tag{5}
\]
Under the distinct-label gates, any two-square additive subarray has rank two: its Schur residual, after removing \(c\), is
\[
(x_2-x_1)(x_1-y_1)^{-1}(y_2-y_1),
\]
whose factors are already units.
Thus for \(p,q\ge2\) these gates give rank exactly two, with kernel dimension \(q-2\).
The common numerator and the additive rectangle already explain that outcome.

Instead consider the entrywise reciprocal array
\[
K_{ij}=F_{ij}^{-1}=c^{-1}C_{ij},
\qquad C_{ij}=(x_i-y_j)^{-1}.
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(c\) commutes with every displayed entry, and multiplication by \(c^{-1}I_p\) is an independently certified matrix unit.
Consequently \(K\) and \(C\) have identical ranks and closed-kernel dimensions.
The entrywise reciprocal operation itself is not a matrix inverse and supplies no assertion about the rank of \(K\).

## The first two certified pivots and the exact third residual

For a three-square subarray set
\[
E_{ij}=x_i-y_j,
\qquad A_i=x_i-y_1,
\qquad B_j=x_1-y_j,
\qquad c_i=x_1-x_i,
\qquad d_j=y_j-y_1.
\]
The symbol \(c_i\) is a row separation, whereas \(c=z-1\) remains the common numerator.
Every one of these factors with \(i,j>1\), and every \(E_{ij}\), has an independent unit certificate from (4).
The first pivot of \(C\) is \(E_{11}^{-1}\).
The retained ordered inverse-difference identity gives the remaining entries
\[
S_{ij}
=E_{ij}^{-1}-E_{i1}^{-1}E_{11}E_{1j}^{-1}
=A_i^{-1}d_jE_{ij}^{-1}c_iB_j^{-1},
\qquad i,j>1.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular every \(S_{ij}\) here is independently a unit, and
\[
S_{22}^{-1}=B_2c_2^{-1}E_{22}d_2^{-1}A_2
\tag{8}
\]
is justified before the second elimination.
This controls an actual sum of two noncommuting rational paths.
It is a direct use of L_A0011, not an additional unrestricted common-power theorem.

After the second pivot, the final scalar is
\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=S_{33}-S_{32}S_{22}^{-1}S_{23}\\
 &=A_3^{-1}\bigl[d_3E_{33}^{-1}c_3
 -d_2E_{32}^{-1}c_3c_2^{-1}E_{22}d_2^{-1}d_3E_{23}^{-1}c_2\bigr]B_3^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Define the explicitly ordered unit
\[
\Omega=
c_3^{-1}E_{33}d_3^{-1}d_2E_{32}^{-1}c_3c_2^{-1}
E_{22}d_2^{-1}d_3E_{23}^{-1}c_2.
\tag{10}
\]
Then adjacent multiplication, with no commutation beyond the stated common central factor, gives
\[
R=A_3^{-1}d_3E_{33}^{-1}c_3(1-\Omega)B_3^{-1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Every factor outside \(1-\Omega\) is already certified.
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rk}_G K=2+\operatorname{rk}_G(1-\Omega),
\qquad
\dim_G\ker K=\dim_G\ker(1-\Omega).}
\tag{12}
\]
If \(\Omega=1\), the last pivot is exactly zero and the rank is two.
If \(1-\Omega\) has an independently established inverse, the rank is three.
Knowing that \(\Omega\) itself is a product of units does not establish either alternative.
In particular (10) is a rational product involving original binomial inverses; it is not thereby an original group element whose difference from one has a cyclic certificate.
Nor has it been identified as another difference of geometric sums with actual group labels and the premises of C_A0009_v6.
Equation (12) is the decisive later gap for this general three-square test.
For a rectangular array each of the two certified pivots removes one row and one column and contributes one to rank; every entry of the residual uses the same ordered Schur operation, but its remaining rank is not fixed by the size decrease.

## Central columns expose the next row-separation condition

Suppose additionally that every \(b_j\) is central in the supporting subgroup.
Put \(D_{ij}=(a_i-b_j)^{-1}\).
The actual common-power inverse then gives the useful unit equivalence
\[
K=-\operatorname{diag}(a_i-1)\,D\,
\operatorname{diag}(b_j-1)c^{-1}.
\tag{13}
\]
The order in (13) follows because each central \(b_j\) commutes with \(a_i\), so \(a_i-1\) commutes with \((a_i-b_j)^{-1}\).
All outside factors in (13) have their previously certified inverses.

For \(D\), set \(u_i=a_1-a_i\) and
\[
a_i'=u_i^{-1}a_i u_i\quad(i>1).
\tag{14}
\]
The [central-column shape formula](../ledger/Yin_L_A0011_v1.md#a-shape-invariant-with-central-column-parameters) writes its first Schur block as
\[
(a_i-b_1)^{-1}u_i\,(a_i'-b_j)^{-1}
(b_j-b_1)(a_1-b_j)^{-1},
\qquad i,j>1.
\tag{15}
\]
Every outside factor is a known unit, and \(a_i'-b_j=u_i^{-1}(a_i-b_j)u_i\) keeps each transformed cross difference a known unit.
The common powers are also retained as exact affiliated identities:
\[
(a_i')^{m_i}=u_i^{-1}zu_i=z.
\tag{16}
\]
However \(a_i'\) is generally a rational conjugate, not an original group element or a unitary.
Therefore a nonzero \(a_i'-a_k'\) is not automatically an original binomial.
For three rows and three columns the existing calculation specializes to
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G K=2+\operatorname{rk}_G(a_2'-a_3'),
\qquad \dim_G\ker K=\dim_G\ker(a_2'-a_3').
\tag{17}
\]
After removing the known first-Schur row and column units in (15), the final scalar factorization of the transformed array is
\[
(a_3'-b_2)^{-1}(b_3-b_2)(a_3'-b_3)^{-1}
(a_2'-a_3')(a_2'-b_3)^{-1}.
\tag{18}
\]
All factors except the displayed new row separation are independently units before (18) is used.
Equation (16) is additional real information, but the cyclic proof for original group binomials does not apply to arbitrary rational conjugates merely because their powers are central.

There is also a direct size screen when the rows satisfy a stronger common-degree identity \(a_i^d=w\) with central \(w\).
For central \(b_j\ne a_i\), torsion-freeness gives \(b_j^d\ne w\): otherwise the commuting quotient \(a_i b_j^{-1}\) would have finite order.
Thus \(w-b_j^d\) has an independent binomial inverse, and
\[
(a_i-b_j)^{-1}
=\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}a_i^{d-1-\ell}b_j^\ell\right)
(w-b_j^d)^{-1}.
\tag{18a}
\]
This factors \(D\), and hence \(K\), through \(d\) coordinates and proves \(\operatorname{rk}_G K\le d\).
It is a finite geometric identity, not a zero-or-unit certificate for every minor below that bound.
The following two examples attain the respective bounds \(d=3\) and \(d=2\), so larger arrays of these same types cannot supply more than three and two nonzero scalar pivots.

## A noncommutative third pivot that survives by original binomial factors

Let
\[
G=\langle h,k\rangle\rtimes_\alpha\langle t\rangle,
\qquad \langle h,k\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad \alpha(h)=k,\quad\alpha(k)=(hk)^{-1}.
\tag{19}
\]
The displayed automorphism has order three, as direct substitution on \(h,k\) verifies.
The semidirect product is torsion-free: a finite-order element projects trivially to the infinite cyclic \(t\)-coordinate, then lies in the torsion-free subgroup \(\mathbb Z^2\).
Put \(w=t^3\ne e\), which is central.
Choose
\[
(a_1,a_2,a_3)=(t,ht,h^{-1}t),
\qquad (b_1,b_2,b_3)=(w,w^2,w^3),
\qquad z=w^6.
\tag{20}
\]
The norm products \(h\alpha(h)\alpha^2(h)\) and \(h^{-1}\alpha(h^{-1})\alpha^2(h^{-1})\) both equal one.
Hence \(a_i^3=w\), so
\[
a_i^{18}=z,
\qquad b_1^6=b_2^3=b_3^2=z.
\tag{21}
\]
These are actual common-power identities of the precise type (2).
All six labels are distinct: the rows have \(t\)-degree one with distinct \(\mathbb Z^2\) coefficients, while the columns have \(t\)-degrees \(3,6,9\).
Thus every initial cross and same-side difference has its original binomial unit certificate.

Write \(a_i=d_i t\), with \(d_1=1,d_2=h,d_3=h^{-1}\).
For \(e_i=1-d_i\), the actual similarity in (14) has the ordered coefficient formula
\[
a_i'=d_i't,
\qquad d_i'=\alpha^{-1}(e_i^{-1}d_i)e_i.
\tag{22}
\]
This is the formula already proved in the [normalized-coefficient component](../ledger/Yin_L_A0011_v1.md#a-complete-conditional-invariant-in-one-normalized-coefficient-component).
Here all its specific inverses can be checked using only binomials, without assuming that a larger unknown coefficient ring is a division ring.
Since \(\alpha^{-1}(h)=(hk)^{-1}\), substitution gives
\[
d_2'=\frac{1-h}{hk-1},
\qquad
d_3'=k\frac{1-h}{hk-1},
\]
\[
\boxed{
a_2'-a_3'=(1-k)(1-h)(hk-1)^{-1}t.}
\tag{23}
\]
Fractions in (23) lie in the commuting coefficient subalgebra generated by \(h,k\); the final \(t\) remains on the right.
Each of \(1-h,1-k,hk-1,t\) is an independently certified actual unit because \(h,k,hk\) are nonidentity in the specified \(\mathbb Z^2\).
Thus (23) certifies the last pivot (18) before any inverse of it is used.
Together with (13), the two earlier pivots give
\[
\operatorname{rk}_G K=3,
\qquad \dim_G\ker K=0.
\tag{24}
\]
The rows genuinely involve noncommuting group elements, since \(tht^{-1}=k\ne h\).
The reciprocal array therefore survives the next elimination in a noncommutative example, and its full rank also excludes the additive rank-two explanation (5).
Nevertheless the argument is a concrete specialization of the retained normalized-coefficient mechanism.
The subgroup generated by \(h,k,t^3\) is abelian of index three, so this example remains within virtually abelian group coverage and establishes no new whole-group Atiyah case.
Its third-pivot certificate depends on the explicit extra relations in (19), not on common powers alone.

## Actual common powers also permit an exact third-pivot merger

Use the torsion-free Klein bottle group
\[
\Gamma=\langle t,g\mid tgt^{-1}=g^{-1}\rangle,
\qquad w=t^2.
\]
Its torsion-freeness and normal forms are retained in [L_A0011](../ledger/Yin_L_A0011_v1.md#an-actual-torsion-free-collision-after-one-step).
Take
\[
(a_1,a_2,a_3)=(t,tg,tg^2),
\qquad (b_1,b_2,b_3)=(w,w^2,w^3),
\qquad z=w^6.
\tag{25}
\]
Now \(a_i^2=w\), hence \(a_i^{12}=z\), while the column exponents are again \(6,3,2\).
All initial cross and same-side labels are distinct by their normal forms and \(t\)-degrees, so every initial gate is an independently known unit.
For \(u_i=a_1-a_i\), the common-square identities give
\[
u_i(a_i'+a_1)=a_i u_i+u_i a_1=a_1^2-a_i^2=0.
\]
Since \(u_i\) is already a unit, \(a_2'=a_3'=-a_1\).
Equations (17)--(18) therefore give
\[
\operatorname{rk}_\Gamma K=2,
\qquad\dim_\Gamma\ker K=1.
\tag{26}
\]
This is a permitted integer defect and an exact row merger after two certified pivots.
It refutes a claim that distinct initial common-power group labels force every later pivot to be nonzero or invertible.
It does not refute a possible integer-rank induction that detects these mergers and separately controls every nonzero later residual.
The calculation is a common-power reciprocal realization of the existing Klein bottle collision, not a newly discovered spectral obstruction.

## What the arbitrary-coefficient obstruction does and does not imply here

The accepted [C_A0006 construction](../claims/Yin_C_A0006_v1.md#the-next-separation-is-the-original-coefficient-kernel) has rational row parameters
\[
x_1=tI_m,
\quad x_2=tgI_m,
\quad x_3=q\,tg^2,
\quad q=(I_m+i\varepsilon a)(I_m-i\varepsilon a)^{-1},
\]
for an arbitrary selfadjoint \(a\in M_m(\mathbb CH)\).
Its next separation has exactly the rank of \(a\), even though all original differences are independently units.
This proves that the larger inverse-difference class with unrestricted rational Haar-unitary parameters and no exact common-power constraint already retains the complete all-size Task.

It does not prove the same equivalence for (2).
Indeed the product-group \(t\)-degree would force equal positive exponents in a common-power identity for these three rows.
Equality of the first two powers forces that exponent \(N\) to be even, and equality with the third then requires \(q^N=I_m\).
The arbitrary Cayley coefficient does not satisfy that finite-order equation in general.
In addition \(x_3\) is generally not an actual group element.
Consequently neither the exact block rank reduction nor its arbitrary-input quantifier is inherited by the common-power reciprocal family without a further construction.

Conversely exact common powers do not justify replacing the actual group by a relation-free parent or assuming known whole-group coverage for all its torsion-free quotients.
For every torsion-free \(H\), the semidirect product
\[
\widetilde G=(H\times H)\rtimes_\sigma\mathbb Z,
\qquad \sigma(h,k)=(k,h),
\]
is torsion-free by projection to \(\mathbb Z\), and with generator \(t\) the actual elements
\[
r_h=(h,h^{-1})t
\qquad\text{satisfy}\qquad r_h^2=t^2\ne e.
\tag{27}
\]
The element \(t^2\) is central and distinct \(h\) give distinct roots.
Thus common-square group labels can be formed in ambient constructions over arbitrary torsion-free groups.
Formula (27) is not a kernel-preserving encoding of an arbitrary matrix over \(H\), and supplies no claim that its reciprocal arrays retain the arbitrary coefficient obstruction of C_A0006.
It only prevents treating the displayed power relation as evidence that all further actual group structure is already controlled.

## Scope of reuse and the useful next question

The actual unit, rank, closed-domain and inverse-difference interfaces are reused at the exact L_A0002, L_A0009 and L_A0011 locators given above.
The common numerator and initial inverse are the fixed C_A0009_v6 result.
The general three-square residual (9)--(12), the explicit substitutions (19)--(26), and the elementary semidirect-product check (27) are direct calculations from those interfaces.
The source attribution for Schur expressions and their actual-ring inverse premises remains the [existing GGRW05 passage check](../ledger/Yin_L_A0011_v1.md#source-attribution-and-remaining-scope), whose scope does not claim a source theorem proving the present common-power array assertion.
No formal free-field evaluation, general group-ring Ore property, whole-group Atiyah theorem, or newly recognized external theorem is used as a missing inverse certificate.
No historical novelty is asserted.

The bounded test has therefore produced both an explicit successful third pivot and an explicit permitted merger, while locating the unresolved general residual precisely.
A useful continuation needs an actual constraint on \(1-\Omega\) in (10), or on the central-column separation in (17), that follows from the original group labels and survives all further relations.
It must allow exact mergers, certify nonzero residuals without assuming division of the unknown actual rational closure, and extend to arbitrary rectangular Task inputs through an explicit independently certified transformation such as (1).
The existing normalized-coefficient class already completes the induction when its known division coefficient ring and normalizing unit are supplied; repeating examples inside that class does not discharge the remaining general question.
