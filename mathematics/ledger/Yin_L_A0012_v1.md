# Atomic probability formulations of the complex Atiyah property

Date: 2026-11-11 (Asia/Hong_Kong).
This note answers the bounded question of whether [Yin_T_A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract) has a completely equivalent formulation using atoms of probability distributions.
It synthesizes the existing spectral-measure and rational-closure inputs below with direct spectral-calculus consequences.
The main agent checked the normalization, both directions of the reformulations, and the bounded transform used in the scalar converse.
This is an ordinary in-discussion check, not a new independent review or a resolution of A0001.
No mathematical novelty is claimed.
The [Cauchy-transform continuation](Yin_L_A0015_v1.md) develops boundary-residue, reciprocal-slope, free-heat and entropy formulations, with separate geometric and return-word strategy tests.

## Inputs and probability setting

Use [MSY23 Theorem 5.25](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U005.md#theorem-525-the-complete-equivalence) for the equivalence between the strong Atiyah property and division of the actual rational closure.
The [spectral source unit](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U006.md#normal-atomic-masses) fixes normalized atom masses and distinguishes them from unnormalized analytic ranks.
Its scalar rational atomlessness argument uses maximal Delta and a free-field realization; the argument below instead applies its elementary spectral-projection mechanism to the actual closure, with division supplied exactly by the general Atiyah property.
The [linear-pencil synthesis](Yin_L_A0004_v1.md#setting-and-exact-criteria) supplies the all-size affine-linear rank reduction.
These are the retained source and mathematical inputs, not new assumptions about arbitrary torsion-free groups.

Fix a discrete group \(G\), put \(M:=\mathcal N(G)\), and use its faithful normal tracial state
\[
\tau_G(T):=\langle T\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle.
\]
Write \(u_g:=\lambda_G(g)\).
The noncommutative joint distribution is specified by
\[
\tau_G(u_{g_1}^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots u_{g_k}^{\varepsilon_k})
=
\begin{cases}
1,&g_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots g_k^{\varepsilon_k}=e,\\
0,&g_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots g_k^{\varepsilon_k}\ne e,
\end{cases}
\qquad \varepsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
The original conjecture quantifies over every torsion-free \(G\).
The equivalences for a fixed \(G\) do not themselves require torsion-freeness.

For a selfadjoint affiliated operator \(H\in M_n(L^0(M))\), define its scalar probability law by
\[
\mu_H(B):=(\operatorname{tr}_n\otimes\tau_G)(1_B(H)),
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}_n:=\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}_n.
\]
Here \(B\subseteq\mathbb R\) is Borel and the spectral projections belong to \(M_n(M)\).
An atom at \(t\) means \(\mu_H(\{t\})>0\).
In particular,
\[
\mu_H(\{t\})
=\frac1n\dim_M\ker(H-tI_n).
\tag{1}
\]
An individual selfadjoint law is an ordinary probability measure; a simultaneous commutative joint model for the group generators is not being assumed.

## Matrix atoms: an exact formulation without rational inverses

For this fixed \(G\), the following are equivalent.

1. Every \(A\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb CG)\), at every finite size, has integer von Neumann kernel dimension.
2. For every such \(A\),
   \[
   \mu_{A^*A}(\{0\})\in
   \left\{0,\frac1n,\ldots,\frac{n-1}{n},1\right\}.
   \tag{2}
   \]
3. For every \(n\), every selfadjoint \(H\in M_n(\mathbb CG)\), and every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
   \[
   \mu_H(\{t\})\in
   \left\{0,\frac1n,\ldots,\frac{n-1}{n},1\right\}.
   \tag{3}
   \]

Indeed \(\ker(A^*A)=\ker A\), so (1) immediately identifies conditions 1 and 2.
Condition 1 gives condition 3 by applying it to \(H-tI_n\), whose entries remain in \(\mathbb CG\).
Condition 3 gives condition 2 by taking \(H=A^*A\) and \(t=0\).
All sizes and all complex coefficients are essential to the stated formulation.

Matrix laws need not be atomless.
For example, \(H=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\in M_2(\mathbb CG)\) has
\(\mu_H=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1\) for every group.
A nonzero atom in an \(n\)-by-\(n\) test has mass at least \(1/n\), and its mass must be an integer multiple of \(1/n\).
The assertion imposes no absolute continuity or density regularity on the remaining measure.

For bounded selfadjoint \(H\), the atom can equivalently be recovered by
\[
\mu_H(\{t\})
=
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
(\operatorname{tr}_n\otimes\tau_G)
\left[
\varepsilon^2\bigl((H-tI_n)^2+\varepsilon^2I_n\bigr)^{-1}
\right].
\tag{4}
\]
This follows by dominated convergence in the scalar spectral measure.
Thus a probabilistic analysis could target these limiting masses directly, without requiring a bounded inverse for \(H-tI_n\).

## Selfadjoint affine-linear tests suffice

Suppose \(G\) is finitely generated by \(g_1,\ldots,g_d\).
It is enough in condition 3 to test all sizes of the selfadjoint affine pencils
\[
H=C_0\otimes1+\sum_{j=1}^d
\left(C_j\otimes u_{g_j}+C_j^*\otimes u_{g_j}^*\right),
\qquad C_0=C_0^*,\quad C_j\in M_n(\mathbb C).
\tag{5}
\]
There is no fixed upper bound on \(n\).

To check the converse precisely, let \(P\) be any square affine pencil in the generators and their inverses and form
\[
K_P:=
\begin{pmatrix}
-I_n&P\\
P^*&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is a selfadjoint affine pencil of size \(2n\).
Its kernel is exactly \(\{0\}\oplus\ker P\): the equations give \(x=Py\) and \(P^*Py=0\), hence \(Py=x=0\).
Consequently the atom condition for \(K_P\) at zero implies
\[
\dim_M\ker P
=2n\,\mu_{K_P}(\{0\})\in\mathbb Z.
\]
The retained all-size affine-linear rank equivalence then supplies condition 1.
The converse from condition 1 is immediate from (1).
For arbitrary \(G\), each tested matrix is supported in a finitely generated subgroup, and the Task's coset argument preserves its kernel dimension.

The zero-diagonal block matrix
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&P\\P^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
alone would have kernel dimension \(2\dim_M\ker P\).
Integer nullity of that particular matrix would only give half-integer nullity of \(P\).
The block \(-I_n\) above avoids that loss.

## Scalar rational laws: either a point mass or no atoms

Let
\[
R_G:=\operatorname{Rat}_{L^0(M)}(\mathbb CG)
\]
be the actual rational closure in the fixed affiliated algebra, as in MSY23.
Concretely it is the smallest subalgebra containing \(\mathbb CG\) and containing all entries of the inverse of each of its square matrices that is invertible in the ambient affiliated matrix algebra.
It is inverse closed at scalar level and is a star algebra: taking adjoints commutes with these actual inverse operations.
This definition exists without assuming the Atiyah property or making every nonzero denominator invertible.
A formal expression whose required inverse does not exist is not an element by that expression.

The matrix conditions above are also equivalent to each of the following scalar statements.

4. Every selfadjoint \(h\in R_G\setminus\mathbb R1\) has an atomless spectral law.
5. Every bounded selfadjoint \(h\in R_G\cap M\setminus\mathbb R1\) has an atomless spectral law.
6. For every \(r\in R_G\), the bounded positive contraction
   \[
   q_r:=(1+r^*r)^{-1}\in R_G\cap M
   \]
   satisfies
   \[
   \mu_{q_r}(\{1\})\in\{0,1\}.
   \tag{6}
   \]

Equivalently, in conditions 4 and 5 every permitted selfadjoint law is either a Dirac mass or atomless.
The exclusion of scalars concerns the actual evaluated operator.
A formally nonconstant expression can evaluate to a scalar because of the group relations.

For the forward implication, MSY23 identifies condition 1 with \(R_G\) being a division ring.
If \(h=h^*\in R_G\) is not scalar, then \(h-t1\ne0\) for every real \(t\), so it is invertible in \(R_G\) and in \(L^0(M)\).
Its kernel is zero, and (1) for \(n=1\) proves condition 4.
Condition 4 implies condition 5.
Condition 5 implies condition 6, since a scalar \(q_r\) has a Dirac law and a nonscalar \(q_r\) is atomless.

For the converse, spectral calculus for the positive selfadjoint affiliated operator \(r^*r\) gives
\[
1_{\{1\}}(q_r)=1_{\{0\}}(r^*r)=p_{\ker r},
\qquad
\mu_{q_r}(\{1\})=\dim_M\ker r.
\tag{7}
\]
The inverse defining \(q_r\) always exists and is bounded, since \(1+r^*r\ge1\).
It therefore belongs to the actual rational closure.
If the mass in (7) is one, faithfulness gives \(p_{\ker r}=1\), hence \(r=0\).
For \(r\ne0\), condition 6 forces that mass to be zero.
An injective square affiliated operator in a finite von Neumann algebra is affiliated invertible; scalar inverse closure puts \(r^{-1}\) in \(R_G\).
Thus \(R_G\) is a division ring, and MSY23 gives the original matrix condition.

The bounded testing version still uses the rational closure formed in \(L^0(M)\).
Intermediate inverses may be unbounded.
It does not replace that closure with a ring formed using bounded inverses only, and does not identify the closure with a formal free field.

## What the reformulation does and does not supply

Checking only selfadjoint scalar polynomials in \(\mathbb CG\) yields the scalar analytic injectivity problem.
For a nonscalar selfadjoint polynomial \(h\), scalar injectivity applied to \(h-t\) gives atomlessness.
Conversely, for nonzero \(a\in\mathbb CG\), apply scalar polynomial atomlessness to \(a^*a\) if it is nonscalar; if it is scalar, it is a positive nonzero scalar and already injective.
This does not supply the missing passage to every actual rational element or every matrix size.
No converse from the scalar polynomial property to the complete group-matrix Atiyah property is being asserted.

Torsion-freeness makes each \(u_g\), for \(g\ne e\), a Haar unitary, since \(\tau_G(u_g^k)=0\) for every nonzero integer \(k\).
The generating tuple need not be freely independent.
Its mixed moments retain the actual group relations.
The [existing direct-sum example](Yin_L_A0011_v1.md#individual-diffuse-path-laws-do-not-suffice) has Haar generators of a faithful abstract torsion-free group but a noncanonical trace, and produces an arbitrary fractional kernel mass.
It shows why separate generator laws and abstract torsion-freeness cannot replace the canonical joint distribution.

The exact scalar probabilistic target for A0001 is therefore
\[
r\in R_G,\ r\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_{(1+r^*r)^{-1}}(\{1\})=0
\]
for every torsion-free \(G\).
This is a complete reformulation, not an independent anti-concentration estimate or a proof that torsion-freeness enforces it.
For the current bordered route \(r=uL^{-1}v\), it names precisely the atom whose exclusion is still needed.
The all-size matrix formulation can be used when one wishes to avoid rational-domain conditions.
