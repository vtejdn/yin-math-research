# Global rational descent for the actual Promislow module

This development serves [T_A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract) through [route seven](../strategies/Yin_S_A0007.md).
The fixed inputs are the actual matrices and cocycles in [L_A0024](../ledger/Yin_L_A0024_v1.md#the-actual-left-matrices-and-right-semilinear-symmetries), the formal-lifting limitation in [L_A0028](../ledger/Yin_L_A0028_v1.md), and the actual index-two criterion in [L_A0003](../ledger/Yin_L_A0003_v1.md#complete-norm-criterion-and-the-half-rank-witness).
The question is whether a global rational constraint removes the extra left commutation from L_A0028 without discarding any original cocycle.

There are two complete reductions below and one complete negative conclusion about a proposed invariant.
The full rational problem is reduced both to an explicit mixed norm equation and to anisotropy of a displayed six-variable quadratic form over a three-variable rational field.
The determinant-line constraints themselves admit rational rank-two data with every original cocycle, so those constraints cannot establish divisibility by four.
The missing step remains an independent proof of anisotropy, equivalently exclusion of the mixed norm equation.
Known division coverage of the Promislow group is comparison only and is not used in these derivations.

## The actual rational algebra and its full commutant

Use
\[
 P=\langle a,b\mid b^{-1}a^2b=a^{-2},\ a^{-1}b^2a=b^{-2}\rangle,
 \quad x:=a^2,\quad y:=b^2,\quad z:=(ab)^2,
 \quad F:=\mathbb C(x,y,z).
\]
The exact group facts and normal forms are retained in [L_A0003](../ledger/Yin_L_A0003_v1.md#retained-candidate-beyond-the-ambient-cyclic-quotient-test) and directly checked against Gardam's arXiv v4, Section 3.1, pp. 4--5, in the fixed prerequisite chain.
Let \(\mathscr A\) be the actual algebra generated by the coefficient field of the translation subgroup and by P in the affiliated algebra.
Every nonzero translation Laurent polynomial has an actual affiliated inverse by the abelian Fourier model.
Coset independence and the original action make \(\mathscr A\) four-dimensional as a right F-vector space, with basis \((1,a,b,ab)\).
All inverses and products here are actual affiliated-ring operations, not unrestricted pointwise operations on Hilbert-space vectors.

In this basis the right translations are exactly \(C_a\sigma_a,C_b\sigma_b\), where
\[
 \sigma_a(x,y,z)=(x,y^{-1},z^{-1}),\qquad
 \beta:=\sigma_b:(x,y,z)\mapsto(x^{-1},y,z^{-1}),
 \qquad\gamma:=\sigma_a\beta.
\]
An F-linear endomorphism commuting with these two right translations is left multiplication by its value at the basis vector 1.
Indeed, the other basis vectors are its right translates by a, b and ab, so all four columns are determined by the first column and the commuting equations.
Conversely, associativity makes each actual left multiplication satisfy those equations.
Thus the full rational covariance algebra in \(M_4(F)\) is exactly the left regular image of \(\mathscr A\), and the corresponding algebra in \(M_{4n}(F)\) is \(M_n(\mathscr A)\).
This identifies the actual rational algebra rather than an abstract split matrix algebra with the same dimension.

If \(\mathscr A\) is a division ring, the image of an idempotent in \(M_n(\mathscr A)\) is a free right \(\mathscr A\)-module.
Restriction of scalars to F therefore makes its field rank a multiple of four.
The next section gives the converse obstruction without assuming that division conclusion.

## An elementary coefficient division ring and the exact mixed equation

Let \(H:=\ker(P\to C_2)\), where a maps to the nonidentity class and b maps to the identity class.
The normal forms give
\[
 H=\langle x,z,b\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\rtimes_{-I}\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 \mathscr D:=F\oplus Fb,\quad bf=\beta(f)b,\quad b^2=y.
\]
The displayed sum is the actual subgroup algebra after inverting the nonzero translation polynomials.
For \(f,g\in F\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 (f+gb)(\beta(f)-gb)
 =(\beta(f)-gb)(f+gb)
 =f\beta(f)-yg\beta(g).
 \tag{1}
\]
When f and g are nonzero, the two terms on the right have respectively even and odd valuations at y = 0.
Here \(v_y(\beta(f))=v_y(f)\), because beta fixes y and acts invertibly on its coefficient field.
They cannot cancel.
The cases f = 0 or g = 0 are immediate.
Thus every nonzero element of \(\mathscr D\) is invertible by (1), proving that \(\mathscr D\) is a division ring without using a division theorem for P or H.
Minimality of the actual division closure also identifies \(\mathscr D=D_{\mathbb C}(H)\): its generators lie in that closure, and (1) makes it an actual division subring containing \(\mathbb CH\).

For the extension by a, put \(\alpha:=\operatorname{Ad}_a\).
The exact twisting data are
\[
 \alpha|_F=\sigma_a,\qquad
 \alpha(b)=xz b^{-1}=\frac{xz}{y}b,
 \qquad a^2=x,
 \qquad\alpha^2=\operatorname{Ad}_x.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, alpha is not an involution on \(\mathscr D\), although its restriction to F is one.
For example, (2) gives \(\alpha^2(b)=x^2b=xbx^{-1}\).
The actual algebra is \(\mathscr A=\mathscr D\oplus\mathscr D a\).
The full criterion in L_A0003 applies with precisely this subgroup, automorphism and square:
\[
 \mathscr A\text{ is a division ring}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \nexists k\in\mathscr D:\ \alpha(k)k=x.
 \tag{3}
\]
It follows directly by factoring a general nonzero element into a coefficient unit times \(a+k\) and using
\[
 (a-\alpha(k))(a+k)=x-\alpha(k)k.
\]
No division statement for the full group is inserted into this factorization.

Writing \(k=f+gb\), equation (3)'s prohibited norm equation is exactly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_a(f)f+xz\,\sigma_a(g)\beta(g)&=x,\\
 \sigma_a(f)g+\frac{xz}{y}\sigma_a(g)\beta(f)&=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
All coefficients are arbitrary rational functions in F.
For g = 0, the first equation has even x-valuation on its left and odd x-valuation on its right, because sigma_a fixes x.
For f = 0, it reduces to \(\sigma_a(g)\beta(g)=z^{-1}\); applying sigma_a gives \(g\gamma(g)=z\), impossible by z-valuation because gamma fixes z.
Thus any solution must have both f and g nonzero.
These exclusions do not settle the mixed case, where addition occurs before a valuation is taken.

One exact elimination makes that remaining condition explicit.
Put \(v:=f/g\) and \(N:=v\beta(v)\).
Equation (1)'s parity argument ensures \(N-y\ne0\).
The mixed system (4) is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{\beta(g)}{g}
 =-\frac{y}{xz}\frac{\sigma_a(v)}{\beta(v)},
 \qquad
 \sigma_a(g)g
 =\frac{x\beta(v)}{\sigma_a(v)(N-y)}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Taking the beta norm of the first equation gives the additional necessary compatibility
\[
 N=y^2\sigma_a(N).
 \tag{6}
\]
Conversely, the two equations (5), with nonzero v and g, reconstruct (4) by setting f = vg.
No solvability of (5) is asserted.

A solution of (4) would make the two-dimensional \(\mathscr D\)-matrix of \(a+k\) have rank one, by the exact factorization in L_A0003.
Its F-rank would therefore be two.
Its rational orthogonal kernel projection, formed by the Gram construction from L_A0022, has F-rank two and both original covariances.
Conversely, such a nontrivial rational equivariant idempotent belongs to \(\mathscr A\), makes that algebra nondivision, and hence supplies a solution of (3).
Therefore absence of solutions to (4) is equivalent both to absence of rational rank-two equivariant projections and to divisibility by four for every rational equivariant idempotent of every matrix size.
Prescribing the particular specialization E_y is an additional condition not included in this equivalence.

## Why a single invariant parity valuation is unavailable

Suppose \(\nu:\mathscr D^\times\to\mathbb Z\) is a multiplicative-group homomorphism invariant under alpha.
Its restriction to H, together with
\[
 \chi(h):=2\nu(h)\quad(h\in H),\qquad
 \chi(a):=\nu(x),
\]
respects the extension relations and defines a homomorphism \(P\to\mathbb Z\).
The presentation gives \(P_{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_4\oplus C_4\), so this homomorphism is zero.
In particular \(\nu(x)=0\), and it cannot produce an odd valuation of the target in (3).
This is the invariant-valuation obstruction from L_A0003 specialized to the actual Promislow extension.
The ordinary b-degree is not a substitute: alpha sends b to a nonzero coefficient times \(b^{-1}\).
On a skew Laurent polynomial, alpha changes the lowest b-exponent to minus the original highest exponent.
Thus the one-sided lowest-exponent valuation and its formal completion are not preserved by alpha, and L_A0003's invariant leading-coefficient residue step cannot be copied here.

## Full determinant data still allow rank two

On the determinant line of a hypothetical invariant d-dimensional subspace, right translation by n in the translation subgroup acts as the scalar \(n^d\).
For d = 2, define the rational semilinear line maps
\[
 L_a:=p\sigma_a,\qquad L_b:=q\beta,
 \qquad p:=xy^{-1}z^{-1},\quad q:=y.
 \tag{7}
\]
Their squares are
\[
 p\sigma_a(p)=x^2,\qquad q\beta(q)=y^2.
\]
The right-translation order gives
\[
 L_{ab}=L_bL_a=(x^{-1}z)\gamma,
 \qquad (x^{-1}z)\gamma(x^{-1}z)=z^2.
 \tag{8}
\]
The presentation relations also hold: conjugating the x-squared scalar by L_b inverts it, and conjugating the y-squared scalar by L_a inverts it.
Thus these maps define the entire original right group action on a line with every translation scalar squared, not merely three unrelated square equations.
They supply the full cocycle data required of a rank-two determinant line.
For comparison, \(\det C_a=p^2\) and \(\det C_b=q^2\), so these are rational square roots of the actual four-dimensional determinant multipliers.

For an arbitrary hypothetical rank d, taking x-valuation in the determinant square equation \(m_a\sigma_a(m_a)=x^d\) forces d even.
Equations (7)--(8) demonstrate that the full determinant-line system cannot sharpen that conclusion to divisibility by four.
They do not themselves construct a two-dimensional invariant subspace; decomposability in the exterior square is the missing condition.

## The complete exterior-square equation

On \(\Lambda^2 F^4\), define
\[
 T_a:=p^{-1}(\Lambda^2 C_a)\sigma_a,
 \qquad T_b:=q^{-1}(\Lambda^2 C_b)\beta.
 \tag{9}
\]
Because (7) realizes the squared cocycle, the scalar translations cancel in (9).
These are commuting semilinear involutions, giving an actual action of \(C_2^2\).
Write a bivector as \(\eta=\sum_{i<j}\eta_{ij}e_i\wedge e_j\).
Direct multiplication of the six basis bivectors gives the following complete invariant equations:
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 \eta_{12}=-yz\,\sigma_a(\eta_{12}),&
 \eta_{13}=\sigma_a(\eta_{24}),&
 \eta_{14}=x\sigma_a(\eta_{23}),\\
 \eta_{34}=-(yz)^{-1}\sigma_a(\eta_{34}),&
 \eta_{24}=\sigma_a(\eta_{13}),&
 \eta_{23}=x^{-1}\sigma_a(\eta_{14}),\\[2pt]
 \eta_{12}=y\beta(\eta_{34}),&
 \eta_{13}=-\beta(\eta_{13}),&
 \eta_{14}=-\beta(\eta_{23}),\\
 \eta_{34}=y^{-1}\beta(\eta_{12}),&
 \eta_{24}=-\beta(\eta_{24}),&
 \eta_{23}=-\beta(\eta_{14}).
 \end{array}
 \tag{10}
\]
Equivalently, choose h, r and s satisfying
\[
 h=-yz\,\sigma_a(h),\qquad r=-\beta(r),\qquad s=-x\gamma(s),
 \tag{11}
\]
and put
\[
 (\eta_{12},\eta_{13},\eta_{14},\eta_{23},\eta_{24},\eta_{34})
 =(h,r,s,-\beta(s),\sigma_a(r),y^{-1}\beta(h)).
 \tag{12}
\]
A nonzero bivector in four dimensions is decomposable if and only if
\[
 \eta_{12}\eta_{34}-\eta_{13}\eta_{24}+\eta_{14}\eta_{23}=0.
 \tag{13}
\]
This follows by eliminating a nonzero coordinate to express it as a wedge of two vectors; equivalently its skew-symmetric matrix has rank two rather than four.
For (12), condition (13) becomes
\[
 y^{-1}h\beta(h)-r\sigma_a(r)-s\beta(s)=0.
 \tag{14}
\]

Let
\[
 u:=\frac{x-1}{x+1},\quad v_0:=\frac{y-1}{y+1},\quad w:=\frac{z-1}{z+1},
 \qquad U:=u^2,\quad V:=v_0^2,\quad T:=uv_0w,
 \qquad W:=w^2=\frac{T^2}{UV}.
 \tag{15}
\]
The invariant field is
\[
 L:=F^{\langle\sigma_a,\beta\rangle}=\mathbb C(U,V,T).
\]
Indeed, sigma_a changes the signs of \(v_0,w\), beta changes the signs of \(u,w\), and \(F=L(u,v_0)\) has degree four with these four distinct automorphisms.
The three fixed quadratic subfields are \(L(u),L(v_0),L(w)\).
Every solution of (11) therefore has the unique form
\[
 h=(1-yz)(H_1+H_2u),\qquad
 r=u(R_1+R_2v_0),\qquad
 s=(1-x)(S_1+S_2w),
 \tag{16}
\]
where all six capital-letter coefficients lie in L.
For example, dividing the first relation by its nonzero solution \(1-yz\) gives the sigma_a-fixed field; the other two relations work in the same way.

Define the nonzero elements of L
\[
 d:=y+y^{-1}-z-z^{-1}
   =\frac{4(V-W)}{(1-V)(1-W)},
 \qquad
 e:=x+x^{-1}-2=\frac{4U}{1-U}.
\]
Substituting (16) into (14) gives exactly
\[
 Q(H_1,H_2,R_1,R_2,S_1,S_2)
 :=d(H_1^2-UH_2^2)-U(R_1^2-VR_2^2)+e(S_1^2-WS_2^2)=0.
 \tag{17}
\]
For example,
\((1-yz)(1-yz^{-1})/y=d\), while
\(-(1-x)(1-x^{-1})=e\); these fix the signs in (17).
The parameterization (16) is injective, so a nonzero six-tuple yields a nonzero bivector.

## Why the quadratic condition is necessary and sufficient

An invariant decomposable bivector gives an invariant two-dimensional F-subspace by taking its wedge factors, or equivalently the vectors whose wedge with it vanishes.
The normalization in (9) is scalar and does not change that subspace's invariance under the original right translations.
Conversely, let a two-dimensional F-subspace be invariant under both original semilinear maps.
Its one-dimensional exterior line is preserved by both T_a and T_b.
These two maps are commuting L-linear involutions on its underlying L-vector space.
The four elementary projectors
\[
 \frac{(I+\epsilon_aT_a)(I+\epsilon_bT_b)}4,
 \qquad \epsilon_a,\epsilon_b\in\{1,-1\},
\]
sum to the identity, so one projector applied to a nonzero vector in that line is nonzero.
Multiplying the resulting vector by one of \(1,u,v_0,uv_0\), according to its two signs, makes it invariant under both maps.
This remains a scalar multiple of a decomposable bivector.
Thus every invariant two-plane supplies a nonzero invariant solution of (13), and hence a nonzero solution of (17).
This explicit sign argument supplies the needed descent without assuming that a determinant multiplier already selects a rational two-plane.

Finally, any rational invariant two-plane has a rational orthogonal projection by the generic Gram construction.
The Gram determinant is nonzero because a nonzero rational minor is nonzero on a generic set of torus points.
The original right semilinear matrices are unitary on that torus and preserve the two-plane fibers, so uniqueness of orthogonal projection gives both rational covariance identities.
Consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q\text{ is anisotropic over }L
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 \text{there is no rational rank-two equivariant projection over }F\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 \text{the mixed norm system (4) has no solution}\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 4\mid\operatorname{rank}_F\Pi
 \text{ for every rational equivariant idempotent of every size}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{18}
\]
Here anisotropic means that the only six-tuple solving (17) is the zero tuple.
This equivalence keeps all original square translations and uses no added commutation with B.
It is a reduction of the global obstruction, not a proof of the first condition in (18).

There is a concrete rational invariant bivector already satisfying all the linear equations: take \(H_1=1\) and the other five coefficients zero.
It has \(h=1-yz\) and the two nonzero coordinates specified by (12), but its Plucker value is \(d\ne0\).
Thus linear exterior descent itself is nonempty; the unresolved obstruction is exactly the nonlinear decomposability equation.

## Source comparison and bounded conclusion

Local comparison first reused L_A0003's full norm criterion and the fixed L_A0024/L_A0028 calculations.
No retained local treatment supplied the mixed Promislow norm equation or its explicit exterior-square form.
For the recognized quadratic-form mechanism, the publisher edition of [Lew06, Section 1.2, Theorem 6, p. 47](../literature/pdf/Lew06.pdf#page=7) was inspected.
It states the classical equivalence between division of a biquaternion algebra and anisotropy of its six-dimensional Albert form.
This is related-method and attribution evidence, not a proof premise for (18).
No explicit identification of (17) with the Albert form of a particular tensor decomposition is asserted here, and the cited theorem's original proof was not re-audited.

The full rational problem now has an explicit global six-variable equation, with an independently justified coefficient division ring and exact original-algebra interfaces.
The determinant-line invariant is rigorously insufficient, and the pure-coefficient cases of the norm equation are excluded.
The remaining concrete task is to prove anisotropy of (17), or equivalently exclude the mixed solutions of (4)/(5), by an argument independent of known division coverage for P.
The displayed reductions do not discharge that task, do not exclude only a bounded class of rational expressions while claiming all expressions, and do not complete T_A0001.
