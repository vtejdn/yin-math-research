# Fourth moments across double cosets of a cyclic subgroup

Suppose a nonzero \(a\in\mathbb CG\) has exactly four occupied right cosets of an infinite cyclic subgroup \(H=\langle h\rangle\) in a torsion-free group \(G\).
The [four-coset zero-product problem](Yin_L_A0037_v1.md#the-exterior-cyclic-covers) asks whether both factors can have rank \(1/2\).
The fourth moment of a normalized operator gives a coefficient test for this rank that also applies when two differences belong to the same double coset.
All ranks and traces below refer to the left regular representation with its canonical scalar trace.

## Normalization

Write \(a=\sum_{i=1}^4 f_i(h)x_i\) with \(f_i\) nonzero Laurent polynomials and all \(Hx_i\) distinct.
On the circle with probability Haar measure \(dz\), put \(s(z)=(\sum_i|f_i(z)|^2)^{1/2}\) and \(g_i=f_i/s\) almost everywhere.
Then \(s(h)\) is invertible in the affiliated algebra, since a nonzero Laurent polynomial has only finitely many circle zeros.
The operator \(A=\sum_i g_i(h)x_i\) is bounded, \(a=s(h)A\), and affiliated invertibility gives \(r(A)=r(a)\).
The more general normalization used below has bounded coefficients \(g_i\) with \(\sum_i|g_i|^2=1\) and is obtained by invertible affiliated multipliers on both sides.
Put \(u_i=|g_i|^2\) and \(w_i=\int u_i\,dz\).
In \(AA^*\) the diagonal terms sum to \(1\), and the off-diagonal terms are orthogonal to \(\ell^2(H)\) since \(x_ix_j^{-1}\notin H\) for \(i\ne j\).
In particular \(\tau(AA^*)=1\).

## Exact correction for repeated double cosets and nontrivial intersections

Partition the twelve ordered pairs \(i\ne j\) according to \(D=Hx_ix_j^{-1}H\), with cell set \(I_D\).
For each \(D\) fix \(v_D\in D\) and choose \(a_{ij},b_{ij}\in\mathbb Z\) such that \(x_i x_j^{-1}=h^{a_{ij}}v_D h^{b_{ij}}\).
Set
\[
 R_D=\{(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2:h^m v_D h^n=v_D\},\qquad
 \Omega_D=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb T^2:z^m w^n=1\ \text{for every }(m,n)\in R_D\}.
\]
The subgroup \(R_D\) is trivial or generated by a pair \((m,-n)\) with \(mn\ne0\), since it is the graph of an isomorphism between subgroups of \(H\).
Use probability Haar measure \(\mu_D\) on the entire \(\Omega_D\), including all components when the generating pair is nonprimitive.
Both circle marginals are Haar: in the nontrivial case each coordinate projection of \(z^m=w^n\) is onto.
Define on this group
\[
 V_{ij}(z,w)=z^{a_{ij}}w^{b_{ij}}g_i(z)\overline{g_j(w)} .
\]
Changing the two exponents by an element of \(R_D\) leaves this function unchanged.
Under Fourier transformation on \(\mathbb Z^2/R_D\), the vector in \(\ell^2(D)\) supported at \(h^j v_Dh^k\) with value one corresponds to the character \(z^j w^k\).
Thus the term \(g_i(h)x_i x_j^{-1}g_j(h)^*\) has Fourier function \(V_{ij}\) on \(D\).
For Laurent polynomials this follows term by term.
For bounded circle coefficients, use uniformly bounded Fejér sums converging in \(L^2\); the two marginal identities and the bounded factors give \(L^2\)-convergence of both products.

Orthogonality of different double cosets now yields
\[
 M_4:=\tau((AA^*)^2)=1+\sum_D\int_{\Omega_D}\left|\sum_{(i,j)\in I_D}V_{ij}\right|^2d\mu_D
       =2-\sum_iw_i^2+\Gamma .
 \tag{1}
\]
Explicitly, for any fixed order on each \(I_D\),
\[
 \Gamma=\sum_D\left[
 \sum_{(i,j)\in I_D}\left(\int u_i(z)u_j(w)\,d\mu_D-w_iw_j\right)
 +2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{\beta<\gamma\ \mathrm{in}\ I_D}
                 \int V_\beta\overline{V_\gamma}\,d\mu_D\right].
 \tag{2}
\]
For a trivial intersection \(\Omega_D=\mathbb T^2\); the first sum within that class vanishes, and each cross integral factors into two one-circle Fourier integrals.
For a nontrivial relation \((m,-n)\) the diagonal contribution minus \(w_iw_j\) is \(\sum_{k\ne0}\widehat u_i(km)\widehat u_j(-kn)\), with \(\widehat u(j)=\int u(z)z^{-j}\,dz\).
Indeed only exponent pairs in \(R_D\) survive Haar integration, and the displayed sum converges absolutely by Cauchy–Schwarz.

If \(P\) is the range projection of \(A\), then \(\tau(PAA^*)=1\).
Trace Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(r(a)\ge1/M_4\).
In particular, \(r(a)\le1/2\) requires \(\Gamma\ge\sum_iw_i^2\).
When \(r(a)=1/2\), the exact excess is
\[
                 \Gamma-\sum_iw_i^2=\|AA^*-2P\|_2^2 .
\]
For example, if all off-diagonal double cosets are distinct and all intersections trivial, then \(\Gamma=0\), \(M_4=2-\sum_i w_i^2\le7/4\) and \(r(a)\ge4/7\).
The same conclusion holds with collisions if the distinct functions in each class are pairwise orthogonal and every diagonal covariance in (2) vanishes.
More generally (2) tests the *signed sum*: any value strictly less than \(\sum_iw_i^2\) excludes half rank regardless of the number of repeated double cosets.

For the first, left normalization there is a further strictness statement if \(G\) is amenable.
First divide the Laurent polynomials by their common greatest common divisor, a scalar affiliated unit whose removal preserves rank.
If this divisor is \(d\), the normalized operators before and after division differ by the left unitary \((d/|d|)(h)\), defined almost everywhere. Their fourth moments and the four coefficient weights agree.
The resulting coefficient polynomials have no common circle zero, so \(s>0\) on the circle and all \(g_i\) are smooth.
Their Fourier coefficients are absolutely summable, for instance by integrating twice by parts.
Hence \(A\in\ell^1(G)\).
If \(r(a)=1/2\) and the correction in this normalization attained \(\sum_iw_i^2\), the identity above would give \(P=AA^*/2\in\ell^1(G)\).
Its trace would contradict the [scalar idempotent theorem for torsion-free amenable groups](../audits/literature/BCM04/Yin_U001.md#amenable-projective-trace).

## A \(4/7\) bound when all differences normalize the cyclic subgroup

Suppose all twelve off-diagonal double cosets are distinct, and all \(x_i x_1^{-1}\) normalize \(H\).
All pair intersections are then the full cyclic subgroup.
Right multiplication by \(x_1^{-1}\) lets us use \(n_i=x_ix_1^{-1}\) as representatives.
Define \(\varepsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}\) by \(n_i h n_i^{-1}=h^{\varepsilon_i}\).
For \(v_D=n_i n_j^{-1}\), the two circle variables satisfy \(w=z^{\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j}\).
If the two orientation classes are both present, put
\[
 F_\pm(z)=\sum_{\varepsilon_i=\pm1}|f_i(z)|^2,\qquad
 R(z)=\left(\frac{F_+(z)F_-(z^{-1})}{F_-(z)F_+(z^{-1})}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Both class sums are positive almost everywhere, and \(R(z^{-1})=1/R(z)\).
Multiply on the right by the positive affiliated unit \(\psi(h)\) with \(\psi(z)^2=R(z)^{-1/2}\).
Normalize on the left by the reciprocal square root of \(\sum_i |f_i(z)\psi(z^{\varepsilon_i})|^2\), which is positive and finite almost everywhere.
The resulting coefficients \(g_i\) are bounded and \(\sum_i |g_i|^2=1\).
This multiplication preserves rank even if the two scaling operators have unbounded inverses.

The new total \(U_+(z)=\sum_{\varepsilon_i=+1}|g_i(z)|^2\) equals \(F_+(z)/(F_+(z)+F_-(z)R(z))\).
Writing \(A_0=F_+(z), B_0=F_-(z), C_0=F_-(z^{-1}), D_0=F_+(z^{-1})\), its value at \(z^{-1}\) is \(D_0 R(z)/(C_0+D_0 R(z))\).
These agree because \(R(z)^2=A_0 C_0/(B_0 D_0)\).
Thus both \(U_+\) and \(U_-=1-U_+\) are invariant under inversion.
If only one orientation class occurs, ordinary left normalization already gives this invariance.

Consequently \(\sum_{i,j}u_i(z)u_j(z^{\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j})=1\) pointwise almost everywhere, by summing within the two orientation classes.
For distinct off-diagonal double cosets there are no cross integrals, so
\[
                   M_4=2-\int_{\mathbb T}\sum_i u_i(z)^2\,dz\ \le\ 7/4 .
\]
This proves \(r(a)\ge4/7\) also in this case.
The bound applies to the adjoint of a factor supported on four left cosets by taking inverses and adjoints.
For intermediate commensurations and colliding double cosets, the covariance and interference terms in (2) can persist, and the general four-coset rank problem remains open.
