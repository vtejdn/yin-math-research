# A simultaneous constraint from all finite two-group images of the original factors

Let \(G\) admit \(\eta:G\to C_7\), and let \(a,b\in\mathbb F_2[G]\) be two factors with \(ab=0\) and the four-line odd patterns \(D,-D\), where \(D=\{0,1,2,4\}\).
Let \(a_i\) be the actual part of \(a\) of degree \(i\), and \(b_j\) the actual part of \(b\) of degree \(-j\).
For every finite two-group \(P\) and every homomorphism \(\psi:G\to P\), the combined map \((\eta,\psi)\) gives a refinement by a direct product.
If all six even colors disappear in \(G\), then
\[
      \psi_*a_0=\psi_*a_1=\psi_*a_2=\psi_*a_4,\qquad
      \psi_*b_0=\psi_*b_1=\psi_*b_2=\psi_*b_4
                    \qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[P].
\]
Thus a disagreement detected just among the four odd colors on either side rules out simultaneous erasure.
The test concerns arbitrary finite two-group images of \(G\) itself.
A relative two-group kernel on which \(C_7\) acts nontrivially has different multiplication rules. [Relative homology and fine bins](Yin_L_A0053_v1.md)

## The six coefficient equations

Each of the eight displayed images has augmentation one and hence is a unit.
For \(P=1\) its augmentation ideal \(I\) is zero, and otherwise quotienting by a central involution \(s\) proves nilpotence by induction on \(|P|\).
The kernel is generated by the central square-zero element \(s-1\), so \(I^m\subseteq(s-1)\) gives \(I^{2m}=0\).
It follows that \(1+I\) is a group of order a power of two.
Normalize the left pieces on the left by \((\psi_*a_0)^{-1}\), and the right pieces on the right by \((\psi_*b_0)^{-1}\).
Write the remaining three left pieces as \(x,y,z\), indexed by \(1,2,4\), and the right pieces as \(u,v,w\) in the same forward index order.
Under complete erasure the two products contributing to each nonzero difference of elements of \(D\) agree, since their sum is zero in characteristic two.
The \(C_7\) coordinate is central in the direct product, so the six equations are
\[
          x=yu,\quad y=zv,\quad z=xw,\qquad
          u=xv,\quad v=yw,\quad w=zu .
\]
In an abelian image, written additively, substitution gives \(v=-y,\ w=-z,\ u=-x\).
It follows that \(y=2x,\ z=2y,\ x=2z\), hence \(7x=0\) and all six generators vanish in every abelian two-group image.
The subgroup generated by these six units is a finite two-group.
If nontrivial it would have a quotient of order two.
Thus all six units are the identity, proving the assertion without assuming that \(P\) is abelian.

## Necessary coincidences on the four odd classes

Set \(R_2=\bigcap_\psi\ker\psi\) over finite two-group images.
On the finite union of the eight original supports, choose one detecting map for every pair of distinct \(G/R_2\) labels and take the finite product of these two-group images.
The preceding equalities thus hold in \(\mathbb F_2[G/R_2]\) as well.
Their common image on each side has odd augmentation.
In particular there must be some \(R_2\)-coset which meets **each of its four odd classes in an odd number of actual support labels** on that side.
One can choose a support representative in each of those four classes with identical image under every finite two-group homomorphism.
If \(G\) is residually a finite two-group, the four classes have disjoint images in \(G/R_2=G\).
Hence the two sparse patterns cannot both occur.
For a general group these equalities are necessary and the given examples need not be residually a finite two-group.
They neither establish the single left color-\(3\) erasure nor show how to produce a distinguishing finite map on every four-line tuple.
