# Exact-support characteristic transfer and tests for torsion-free realizability

## Scope and relation to existing work

This exploration concerns the [complex-coefficient Kaplansky task](../tasks/Yin_T_A0002_v1.md).
It retains consequences of the coefficient-table methods in [C25](../claims/Yin_C_A0025_v1.md) and [C26](../claims/Yin_C_A0026_v1.md), together with an effective arithmetic comparison.
The Smith normal form and one-direction gcd criteria already belong to those claims.
The additional content here is their cross-characteristic interpretation and explicit coefficient-field bounds.
The complementary [projective rank test](../claims/Yin_C_A0093_v1.md#statement) uses a multiplication matrix of Sylvester type and allows supports to shrink.
For the classical multigraded construction see [Sturmfels and Zelevinsky](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.1994.1007) and [Dickenstein and Emiris](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(03)00086-5).
These arguments have been locally checked but have not received independent mathematical review.

Let \(A,B\) be fixed finite nonempty subsets of a group \(G\), with distinct elements in each support.
For a field \(K\), an exact-support solution means coefficients \(a_x,b_y\in K^\times\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{xy=g}a_xb_y=0\qquad(g\in AB).
\]
Thus both group-ring factors are nonzero and retain every element of their specified supports.
All statements below use the actual product fibers of these subsets in this same group.
They hold without assuming torsion-freeness; that additional hypothesis is required when interpreting a solution as a counterexample to the task.
An arbitrary combinatorial partition of \(A\times B\) is not automatically realizable in a torsion-free group.

Put \(M=\mathbb Z^{A\sqcup B}\), use distinct row and column basis vectors, and set \(v_{xy}=e_x+e_y\).
A full coefficient assignment is a character \(z:M\to K^\times\), with \(z(v_{xy})=a_xb_y\).
For each two-term fiber choose an orientation and a difference \(d_j=v_{xy}-v_{x'y'}\).
Write \(L=\langle d_j\rangle_{\mathbb Z}\) and \(S=(L\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q)\cap M\).

## Pair-only fibers: one odd characteristic suffices

Suppose every product fiber has exactly two terms.
The coefficient equations are precisely \(z(d_j)=-1\).
They have a solution over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from \(2\) if and only if
\[
 \sum_j n_jd_j=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_j n_j\equiv0\pmod2
 \qquad(n_j\in\mathbb Z).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, multiplying the equations proves necessity over any field of odd characteristic.
Conversely, (1) makes
\[
 \chi\!\left(\sum_j n_jd_j\right)=(-1)^{\sum_j n_j}
\]
a well-defined character on \(L\).
Choose Smith bases so that \(L\) is generated by \(s_i f_i\), \(1\le i\le\ell\), in an integral basis \(f_1,\ldots,f_{\operatorname{rank}M}\) of \(M\).
For each \(i\le\ell\), choose \(z(f_i)\) with \(z(f_i)^{s_i}=\chi(s_if_i)\), and assign \(1\) to the remaining basis vectors.
Such roots exist in an algebraically closed field, and the resulting character solves all equations.
This is the character-extension mechanism already used in C25/C26; compare [Eisenbud–Sturmfels, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 2.3](https://eisenbud.github.io/papers/pdfs/1996-002.pdf).

Consequently, a single exact-support solution over any field of odd characteristic already gives an exact-support solution over \(\mathbb C\), on the same \(A,B\subset G\).
Neither infinitely many characteristics nor a bound on the support size is needed for this special class.
At characteristic \(2\), all coefficients equal to \(1\) solve every pair-only system, even when (1) fails.
Thus this implication cannot use characteristic \(2\) alone.

The relation test is over the integer kernel, not the kernel after reducing the lattice matrix modulo \(2\).
For example, the abstract single equation \(z(2e)=-1\) has no nonzero integer relation among its exponent vectors and is solved by \(z(e)=i\).
Its exponent vector vanishes modulo \(2\), so using the modular kernel would give an incorrect obstruction.
This example calibrates the lattice test; it is not asserted to be an actual product table.

There is also a coefficient-field bound.
Set \(t=\max_{1\le i\le\ell}v_2(s_i)\), with \(t=0\) when \(\ell=0\).
If \(\chi(s_if_i)=1\), take \(z(f_i)=1\).
Otherwise write \(s_i=2^{t_i}u_i\), with \(u_i\) odd, and take \(z(f_i)\) to be any primitive \(2^{t_i+1}\)-st root of unity.
Its \(s_i\)-th power is \(-1\).
Hence one can choose all coefficients in
\[
 \mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^{t+1}}),\qquad
 [\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^{t+1}}):\mathbb Q]=2^t.                    \tag{2}
\]
For every odd prime \(p\), the identical construction works in
\[
 \mathbb F_{p^f},\qquad
 f=\operatorname{ord}_{2^{t+1}}(p)\le 2^t.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{ord}_2(p)=1\).
Existence over \(\mathbb F_p\) itself is not part of this conclusion.

## Mixed fibers with at most one remaining lattice direction

Now allow larger fibers and retain \(L,S\) from the two-term fibers.
Let \(e\) be the exponent of the finite group \(S/L\), with \(e=1\) if it is trivial, and put \(N=2e\).
For any solution and \(u\in S\), one has \(eu\in L\), so
\[
 z(u)^N=1.                                                    \tag{3}
\]
Assume that all within-fiber differences span a subspace of dimension at most one in \((M/S)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\).
In the one-dimensional case choose a lift \(w\in M\) of a primitive generator of the saturation of that direction in \(M/S\).
For a base cell \(v_{g,0}\) in each fiber write uniquely
\[
 v_{xy}-v_{g,0}=u_{xy}+k_{xy}w,\qquad u_{xy}\in S,\quad k_{xy}\in\mathbb Z.
\]
For a branch \(\rho:S\to K^\times\) solving the pair equations define
\[
 P_g(T)=\sum_{xy=g}\rho(u_{xy})T^{k_{xy}},\qquad
 D=\max_g\left(\max_{xy=g}k_{xy}-\min_{xy=g}k_{xy}\right).
\]
The fiber equation is \(z(v_{g,0})P_g(z(w))=0\), with a nonzero prefactor.
For a fixed branch combine equal powers, discard identically zero polynomials, and divide each remaining Laurent polynomial by its lowest surviving power of \(T\).
The resulting polynomials have nonzero constant terms and degree at most \(D\).
An exact-support solution exists for this branch precisely when either every \(P_g\) vanishes identically or their gcd has positive degree.
This is the [one-direction test in C26](../claims/Yin_C_A0026_v1.md#exact-test-with-one-remaining-direction).

To justify reconstruction over a specified field, note that \(S+\mathbb Zw\) is saturated in \(M\): its quotient is the torsion-free group \((M/S)/\mathbb Z\bar w\).
It is therefore a direct summand of the free abelian group \(M\).
Choose \(z|_S=\rho\), choose a nonzero common root \(z(w)=\theta\), and set the values on a complementary basis equal to \(1\).
This extends the character to \(M\) without extracting further roots.
Every original coefficient is then nonzero and lies in the field generated by the branch values and \(\theta\).
If all polynomials vanish identically, take \(\theta=1\).

For a complex solution, (3) puts its branch in \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)\).
If there is a nonzero polynomial, choose an irreducible factor of the nonconstant gcd and a root \(\theta\); then \([E(\theta):E]\le D\).
Thus whenever a complex exact-support solution exists under this one-direction hypothesis, some such solution has coefficients in a number field \(F\) satisfying
\[
 [F:\mathbb Q]\le \varphi(N)\max(1,D).                         \tag{4}
\]
In dimension zero the branch itself must make every fiber sum vanish, and \(\varphi(N)\) is sufficient.

In characteristic \(p\), let \(N_p=N/p^{v_p(N)}\).
Every \(N\)-th root of unity in a field of characteristic \(p\) has order dividing \(N_p\).
Consequently all branch values lie in
\[
 E_p=\mathbb F_p(\mu_{N_p}),\qquad
 [E_p:\mathbb F_p]=\operatorname{ord}_{N_p}(p)\le\varphi(N_p),
\]
using degree \(1\) when \(N_p=1\).
Apply the same gcd argument in \(E_p[T]\).
An irreducible common factor of degree \(k\le D\) has a root in the degree-\(k\) extension of \(E_p\).
Thus a geometric exact-support solution gives a solution in some finite field satisfying
\[
 \mathbb F_{p^f},\qquad
 f\le\varphi(N_p)\max(1,D).                                   \tag{5}
\]
This proof works at small primes as well and uses no generic choice of a rational hyperplane over a finite field.
At \(p=2\) the pair branches must be computed with \(-1=1\).
At every prime, coefficients must be combined and the gcd recomputed after reduction.
The characteristic-zero gcd cannot simply be carried unchanged across all characteristics.
Equations (4) and (5) are existence bounds; they do not claim that the displayed upper bound is a divisibility bound for the minimal extension degree.

The distinction between \(\mathbb F_p\) and its algebraic closure is real.
For \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), \(X^2+1\) has no root in \(\mathbb F_p\) but has roots in \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\).
The [finite-group calibration in C39](../claims/Yin_C_A0039_v1.md) contains a forced square-root-of-\(-1\) relation; its ambient group has torsion, so it supplies no torsion-free counterexample.
More generally, arbitrary mixed fibers need not satisfy the one-direction hypothesis, and (4)–(5) supply no general extension-degree bound for them.

## A general effective transfer that preserves every coefficient

Let \(r=|A|\), \(s=|B|\), with \(r,s\ge2\).
A one-element support cannot occur in a zero product of nonzero factors because a scalar multiple of a group element is a unit.
Normalize one coefficient of each factor to \(1\).
For each of the remaining \(r+s-2\) coefficient variables \(X\), introduce a separate inverse variable \(X'\) and the equation \(XX'-1=0\).
Together with the normalized fiber equations this is an integral affine scheme whose points over any field are precisely normalized exact-support solutions.
It has
\[
 n=2(r+s-2)
\]
variables, degree at most \(2\), and coefficient height \(0\), where height is the logarithm of the maximum absolute coefficient.
The normalized fiber polynomials have distinct monomials, each with coefficient \(1\): normalization of one row and one column preserves the distinction between constants, the two sets of linear variables, and bilinear monomials.
The inverse equations have coefficients \(1,-1\).
A singleton fiber makes an exact-support solution impossible in every field.
If a solution exists in some characteristic, the number of fiber equations is therefore at most \(\lfloor rs/2\rfloor\), and the total equation count is at most
\[
 S_0=\lfloor rs/2\rfloor+r+s-2.
\]

[Krick–Pardo–Sombra, Theorem 1, p. 2 of arXiv:math/9911094v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9911094) applies to integral polynomials having no common complex zero.
It produces a nonzero integer \(a\) in their integral polynomial ideal, with simultaneous degree and height estimates.
For the quadratic height-zero system above, the following deliberately enlarged version of its height estimate is sufficient:
\[
 \log|a|\le H(r,s):=
 4n(n+1)2^n
 \left(\log S_0+2(n+7)\log\!\left(2(n+1)\right)\right).          \tag{6}
\]
The prefactor \(4n(n+1)d^n\), equation-count term \(\log S_0\), and the hypotheses were checked against the source theorem.
The logarithmic contribution in (6) is enlarged; no sharpness or optimal-constant claim is made.
All logarithms are natural.

If this scheme were empty over \(\mathbb C\), take its integer certificate \(a=\sum_i g_i f_i\).
Evaluating in any field of characteristic \(p\) at an exact-support solution gives \(a=0\), so \(p\mid a\).
For a prime \(p>\exp H(r,s)\) this contradicts \(0<|a|\le\exp H(r,s)\).
Thus a solution in just one such characteristic gives a complex solution with exactly the same supports in the same group.
The characteristic-\(p\) coefficient field need not be \(\mathbb F_p\).
This is a standard effective Nullstellensatz consequence, with an explicit encoding of nonvanishing.
It quantitatively complements the qualitative finite-pattern transfer in [the MD26 source audit, Proposition 8.3](../audits/literature/MD26/Yin_U001.md).
It is not an elementary replacement for arithmetic Nullstellensatz and does not assert an improvement over certificates that permit shrinking the supports.

## Finite-field functional certificates and limits

[Moshkovitz–Yu, Theorem 1, arXiv:2111.09305v3, pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09305) gives degree bounds for finite-field functional Nullstellensatz certificates.
The equality is modulo \(X_i^{|K|}-X_i\), so it concerns values on \(K^n\), not on \(\overline K^n\).
For polynomials of degree at most \(d\), the degree bound is \(md(|K|-1)\) for \(m\) input equations.
Taking the target polynomial to be \(1\) certifies that there is no \(K\)-point.
It does not certify that the coefficient scheme is geometrically empty and cannot replace the integral certificate in the preceding transfer.
This source is comparison-only here.

The results distinguish three obligations: realizing the product table in a torsion-free group, producing an exact-support point of its coefficient scheme, and controlling its characteristic and coefficient field.
They resolve none of these for general tables by themselves.
For an actual pair-only table in a torsion-free group, a full-support solution in odd characteristic already yields a complex counterexample.
For mixed fibers, small characteristic alone remains insufficient; even the one-direction gcd can change with characteristic.
The effective general threshold is extremely large and gives no present construction with uniformly bounded supports.

## Partial transport obstructions to torsion-free realization

Let \(I,J\) be finite sets of distinct row and column labels, with \(|I|=m\) and \(|J|=n\).
A partial product pattern prescribes equalities between selected cells of \(I\times J\).
A group realization consists of elements \(x_i\) and \(y_j\), distinct on each respective side, satisfying those equalities.
If complete fibers are prescribed, their distinctness from other fibers is an additional requirement.
The following necessary condition uses only the prescribed equalities, so it also applies to selected equalities inside mixed-size fibers.
An equality between distinct cells in the same row or in the same column is already impossible by cancellation and the distinctness of the labels.

For \(k\ne i\), define a partial map \(P_{ki}:J\rightharpoonup J\) by
\[
 P_{ki}(j)=\ell
 \quad\hbox{whenever}\quad
 x_i y_j=x_k y_\ell
\]
is prescribed.
In a group realization this map is the restriction of left multiplication by \(h_{ki}=x_k^{-1}x_i\).
It must therefore be well defined and injective.
A violation already excludes realization in any group with the stated distinct labels.
Its reverse map is \(P_{ik}=P_{ki}^{-1}\).

For any composable word of these partial maps, let
\[
 P_w=P_{k_d i_d}\circ\cdots\circ P_{k_1 i_1},
 \qquad
 h_w=h_{k_d i_d}\cdots h_{k_1 i_1}.
\]
Whenever \(P_w(j)=\ell\), successive substitution gives \(h_w y_j=y_\ell\).
Consequently every torsion-free realization must satisfy the following condition:
each \(P_w\) is either a restriction of the identity or has a directed graph consisting only of directed paths, including isolated vertices.
In particular, the following are explicit obstruction certificates.

1. If \(P_w\) has a fixed point and also a moving arrow, the pattern has no realization in any group.
   Indeed the fixed point gives \(h_w y_j=y_j\), hence \(h_w=1\) by cancellation, contradicting the moving arrow and column distinctness.
2. If \(P_w\) contains a directed cycle of length \(q>1\), the pattern has no realization in a torsion-free group.
   Going around the cycle gives \(h_w^q y_j=y_j\), hence \(h_w^q=1\), while its first arrow gives \(h_w\ne1\).
   This proves actual finite order in every realization; it does not infer group torsion from torsion in an abelianization.

These assertions prove the stated dichotomy because a partial injective map on a finite set has only path and cycle components.
The same criterion applies to partial maps on the row labels induced by right multiplication by \(y_j y_\ell^{-1}\).
No unique-root property for arbitrary torsion-free groups is used.

There is also a sound elementary closure before applying the criterion.
The identity \(h_{ki}h_{i\ell}=h_{k\ell}\) implies that every arrow of \(P_{ki}\circ P_{i\ell}\) is a forced arrow of \(P_{k\ell}\), where \(P_{kk}\) is the identity.
Adjoin such arrows and their inverses repeatedly.
Reject conflicting images, conflicting preimages, a nonidentity arrow in \(P_{kk}\), or a newly forced equality between cells declared to lie in distinct complete fibers.
Each adjoined arrow has a derivation from the initial equalities.
There are at most \(m^2n\) possible arrows before a functional conflict occurs, so naive iteration over all index triples and column labels terminates in polynomial time.
This closure is optional and is not asserted to derive every equality forced by the presentation.

For a fixed positive integer \(d\), one can check all words of length at most \(d\) exactly in polynomial time in the explicit table size.
After constructing the maps from the prescribed equalities, use \(r=m(m-1)\) possible ordered row-pair generators, including inverse maps.
Each composition and its path/cycle check require \(O(n)\) elementary map operations.
Breadth-first word enumeration therefore uses
\[
 O\!\left((mn)^2+n\sum_{j=1}^{d}r^j\right)
\]
operations with a direct quadratic preprocessing of the equality blocks.
The implicit constant may depend on fixed \(d\); this is not a polynomial bound uniform in \(d\).
An obstruction is certified by a word of length at most \(d\) together with its fixed/moving arrows or a cycle of at most \(n\) vertices.
The displayed bound uses the direct input maps; performing the optional closure first adds its own polynomial preprocessing cost.

There is even a terminating, generally much larger, test of all composed-map obstructions on the given finite label set.
Partial injections on \(n\) labels form a finite monoid of size
\[
 T(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk^2 k!
 \ \le\ (n+1)^n.
\]
Starting from the identity, saturate under composition by the generators, retaining one representative word per distinct partial map.
Every transport word is reached, and at most \(T(n)\) states occur.
With a dictionary of states this requires at most \(O(rnT(n))\) elementary map operations, apart from dictionary overhead.
Thus unrestricted saturation decides whether this particular kind of obstruction occurs.
It does not decide torsion-free realizability: the represented intermediate points stay within the selected finite column set, and other consequences of the group relations are not covered.
Passing either test is only a necessary consistency condition.

The following \(4\times4\) pattern shows that composition adds information beyond checking the individual transports.
Prescribe four double fibers
\[
 x_0y_0=x_1y_1,\qquad
 x_0y_2=x_1y_3,\qquad
 x_2y_1=x_3y_2,\qquad
 x_2y_3=x_3y_0.
\]
The nonempty row-pair maps, apart from inverses, are
\[
 P=P_{10}:\ 0\mapsto1,\ 2\mapsto3,
 \qquad
 Q=P_{32}:\ 1\mapsto2,\ 3\mapsto0.
\]
Every one of these maps and its inverse consists of paths.
Every directly prescribed column-pair map on the rows has a single arrow, so the individual tests on that side also pass.
However,
\[
 QP:\ 0\mapsto2,\ 2\mapsto0.
\]
Thus every group realization has an element
\[
 h=x_3^{-1}x_2x_1^{-1}x_0,\qquad
 h^2=1,\qquad h y_0=y_2\ne y_0.
\]
The four prescribed collisions therefore exclude every torsion-free realization, even if they occur inside a larger table or inside larger fibers.

This is a group-realizability sanity check with an actual realization in a group having torsion.
Take
\[
 G=\langle a,t,q\mid q^2=1\rangle
   =F(a,t)*C_2,
 \qquad
 (x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)=(a,1,tqa^{-1},t),
 \qquad
 (y_0,y_1,y_2,y_3)=(1,a,q,aq).
\]
The rows and columns are distinct by free-product normal forms.
The full product table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &1&a&q&aq\\ \hline
 a&a&a^2&aq&a^2q\\
 1&1&a&q&aq\\
 tqa^{-1}&tqa^{-1}&tq&tqa^{-1}q&t\\
 t&t&ta&tq&taq
\end{array}.
\]
The reduced normal forms show that its only repeated entries are \(a,aq,tq,t\), each appearing in the prescribed pair.
The other eight entries are singleton fibers.
Here \(h_{10}=a\) and \(h_{32}=qa^{-1}\) each have infinite order: their positive powers have nonempty reduced free-product normal forms.
Their product is \(h_{32}h_{10}=q\), which has order two.
Hence the obstruction genuinely appears after composing different transports.

The four displayed binomial coefficient equations nevertheless have a solution with all row and column coefficients nonzero:
\[
 (u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3)=(1,-1,1,-1),
 \qquad
 (v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3)=(1,1,1,1).
\]
Each prescribed double fiber has coefficient sum zero.
This verifies compatibility of that partial binomial system, independently of the obstruction to torsion-free realization.
It is not a zero product for the full table: the eight singleton fibers have nonzero coefficients.
More generally, a binomial equation may be imposed from a collision only when the two cells constitute its entire actual fiber; the transport obstruction requires no such assumption.

The gain relative to the coefficient-lattice tests in C_A0022, C_A0025 and C_A0026 is therefore a separate exact rejection test for abstract support patterns, not another Smith-normal-form criterion or a new zero-divisor example.
It can be applied before or alongside coefficient-branch elimination in a bounded-support search.
No claim is made that the surviving patterns admit a torsion-free realization, that the original Mghirbi–Douglas construction contains this forbidden configuration, or that the test supplies a uniform support bound.
