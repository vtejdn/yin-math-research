# Preserving rational rank tests while matching a prescribed free word ball

## Scope

The [commuting tracial comparison](../claims/Yin_C_A0100_v1.md#statement) matches finitely many moments by a finite torus covering.
Here the fixed marking is that of the free group on five generators, and it is the ball in that marking that can be prescribed.
This development concerns the distinction between a finite check of the canonical group trace and the regular-trace requirement of [A0001](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract).

Suppose \(U,V\) are unitaries in a finite tracial von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau)\) with \(\tau(1)=1\).
Fix \(j\ge1\).
For every \(k\le j\) suppose that the group algebra over \(\mathbb Q\) generated by the two unitaries and their inverses in each of \(k\) separate coordinates lies in an actual division subring \(D_k\) of the affiliated algebra of \(M^{\bar\otimes k}\).
Suppose a fixed finite matrix \(P\) over a coefficient field \(K\subset\mathbb C\) in the free group on the two letters has noninteger tracial rank in the \(U,V\) representation.

For every integer \(R\ge1\), there is a tracial representation of the free group on five marked generators for which each nonidentity word of length at most \(R\) has trace zero.
Every rational matrix in its first \(j\) spatial powers has integer tracial rank.
An explicitly substituted matrix over the same field \(K\) has exactly the fractional rank of \(P\).
The substituted words grow with \(R\); the construction makes no claim about matching the trace on a ball in those substituted words as a new marking.

## Three auxiliary group generators

In the free group on \(z,c,d\), take \(L>2R\) and form
\[
                          a=z^L c z^{-L},\qquad b=z^L d z^{-L}.
\]
Every nontrivial freely reduced word of length at most \(R\) in the five formal symbols \(z,c,d,a,b\) remains nontrivial after this substitution.
To check this, move each \(z\) to the right, recording the current exponent as the height on each of the other four letters.
The kernel of the exponent map from a free group to \(\mathbb Z\) is free on the conjugates of the other letters at all integral heights.
This follows directly by reducing their product: two consecutive conjugated letters cancel only when their heights agree and their underlying letters are inverse.
Conversely this rewriting covers every word of exponent zero.
For the given short word all recorded heights lie between \(-R\) and \(R\).
The letters \(c,d\) keep those heights, and \(a,b\) become \(c,d\) at heights between \(L-R\) and \(L+R\).
The two intervals are disjoint, hence the free kernel word keeps its reductions, and a nonzero final \(z\)-exponent also survives.

There is a single finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent quotient \(Q\) of the three-generator free group in which these finitely many nonidentity images are still nonidentity.
For a direct construction write each image in its reduced syllables \(h_{i_1}^{e_1}\cdots h_{i_l}^{e_l}\), with neighboring generators different and all \(e_s\ne0\).
Send the three generators \(h_i\) to \(1+X_i\) in the noncommutative truncated integer polynomial algebra on three letters, killing monomials of degree greater than the maximum syllable length of the finite list.
Negative powers use the finite geometric inverse.
The coefficient of \(X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_l}\) is \(e_1\cdots e_l\): because there are no adjacent equal symbols in this monomial, each of the \(l\) syllables must contribute exactly one symbol.
Thus all the chosen nonidentity words survive in the subgroup \(Q\) generated by the images.
The positive-degree ideal \(J\) is nilpotent, and commutators of \(1+J^a,1+J^b\) lie in \(1+J^{a+b}\).
The lowest nonzero part of \(w-1\) for \(w\in1+J\) is multiplied by \(n\) in \(w^n-1\), giving torsion-freeness.
The successive induced subquotients for \(Q\) embed in the finite-rank free abelian groups \(J^a/J^{a+1}\), hence refine to a subnormal series with infinite cyclic factors after omitting trivial ones.
The same is true for every finite direct power of \(Q\).

## Trace and rational ranks

Represent \(z,c,d\) by \(1\otimes\lambda_Q(z),1\otimes\lambda_Q(c),1\otimes\lambda_Q(d)\), and \(a,b\) by \(U\otimes\lambda_Q(a),V\otimes\lambda_Q(b)\) in \(M\bar\otimes\mathcal N(Q)\).
A nonidentity word in the specified ball has nonidentity \(Q\)-coordinate and therefore has product trace zero.
The five-generator words \(a z^Lc^{-1}z^{-L}\) and \(b z^Ld^{-1}z^{-L}\) represent \(U\otimes1,V\otimes1\).
Replacing the two letters throughout \(P\) by these words preserves its tracial rank.

For clarity, adjoining \(Q^k\) to the original \(D_k\) retains an affiliated division ring for the rational matrix tests.
Start the cyclic series at the trivial group with \(D_k\).
At an intermediate subgroup \(N\) use the division closure of the algebra generated by \(D_k,N\), which is a division ring by induction inside \(U(M^{\bar\otimes k}\bar\otimes\mathcal N(N))\).
It is preserved by the automorphism induced by the next cyclic generator, acting trivially on the first tensor factor.
For a nonzero skew Laurent polynomial in that generator, a common bounded left clearing of its finite coefficient list leaves both extreme coefficients injective.
The [two-ended slice proof](../claims/Yin_C_A0007_v8.md#the-fraction-ring-in-the-regular-representation) applies with coefficient algebra \(M^{\bar\otimes k}\bar\otimes\mathcal N(N)\) and its normalized trace.
In detail, for a polynomial of width \(h>0\), restriction to \(h\) consecutive slices is injective on its kernel, since the end coefficients propagate \(h\) consecutive zero slices in both directions.
Polar decomposition bounds the dimension over the coefficient algebra by \(h\).
Right translation identifies all cyclic slices and preserves the kernel, so the diagonal trace on each slice equals the original product trace of the kernel projection.
Summing over all slices forces that trace to be zero.
The width-zero case follows from the coefficient being a unit.
Finite traciality gives an affiliated inverse for every nonzero skew polynomial, and the division ring of skew fractions embeds by these actual inverses.
This contains the next division closure and completes the induction.
All rational input matrices on the \(k\)-fold group power have entries in the resulting division ring, so their ranks are integers.

This operation preserves the rank hypotheses of the input tracial model and tests any chosen finite free word ball.
It also preserves a specific noninteger rank with the indicated longer support.
It neither gives such an input with the canonical group trace nor makes the constructed representation the regular one.
