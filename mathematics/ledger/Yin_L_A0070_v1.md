# Detecting Schur kernel dimension by direct sums and scalar inverses

For the [complex Atiyah problem](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract), let \(B=\mathbb CG\), \(N=\mathcal N(G)\) with its canonical trace, and \(U=\mathcal U(N)\).
Set \(D_0=B\), let \(D_{j+1}\) be generated by \(D_j\) and the inverses of its elements invertible in \(U\), and put \(D=\bigcup_jD_j\).
For \(A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(a\) an affiliated unit let \(S=d-ca^{-1}b\), let \(p\) be the kernel projection of \(S\), and put \(\delta=\tau(p)\).
The experiment asks when a direct sum of \(m\) copies of \(A\), whose ordinary von Neumann nullity is \(m\delta\), factors through fewer than \(2m\) free coordinates over a finite \(D_j\).

## The least affiliated factorization width

Let \(X\) be a rectangular affiliated matrix over any finite von Neumann algebra, and let \(q\) be its final support projection.
Write \(\operatorname{Tr}^{Z}(q)\) for the unnormalized matrix extension of the center-valued trace, normalized by \(\operatorname{Tr}^{Z}(1_N)=1\).
The least width of a factorization \(X=BC\) over \(U\) is the least nonnegative integer \(r\) satisfying \(\operatorname{Tr}^{Z}(q)\le r1\).
Indeed the final support of a product of width \(r\) is contained in the final support of its left factor, which is equivalent by polar decomposition to a subprojection of \(I_r\).
Conversely the projection comparison theorem for finite von Neumann algebras gives a rectangular partial isometry \(V\) with \(VV^*=q\) and \(V^*V\le I_r\) under the displayed center-valued inequality.
Then \(X=V(V^*X)\) over \(U\).

Schur elimination gives invertible row and column operations carrying \(A\) to \(\operatorname{diag}(1,S)\) over \(D_1\).
They preserve center-valued dimension of the image and kernel modules.
With \(h=\operatorname{Tr}^{Z}(p)\), the least affiliated width for \(A^{\oplus m}\) is consequently the least \(r\) satisfying \(m(2\,1-h)\le r1\).
A loss of at least one coordinate is equivalent to \(mh\ge1\) on the whole center.
In a factor the exact width is \(2m-\lfloor m\delta\rfloor\), including the case \(m\delta=1\).
In the presence of a center the scalar trace condition alone can miss a summand on which \(p=0\).

## Cancellation of the identity blocks in finite inverse layers

For a fixed matrix with entries in \(D\), a factorization over \(D\) uses finitely many entries and therefore belongs to some \(D_j\) containing that matrix.
An identity block can be cancelled in the computation of inner rank over \(D\).
The following scalar perturbation supplies the needed column operations even when the kernel projections of matrices over \(D\) have no known expressions in \(D\).

For any \(x\in U\), at most countably many complex numbers \(t\) have \(\ker(x-t)\ne0\).
Indeed the right \(U\)-modules given by distinct eigenvalues of left multiplication by \(x\) form a direct sum over any finite selection, by applying the scalar interpolation polynomials in \(x\).
The sum of their trace dimensions is at most one, and each nonzero one has positive dimension.
Injectivity of an affiliated scalar in a finite von Neumann algebra is equivalent to its invertibility there.

Suppose a column \(v\in D^r\) has a left inverse \(l\), so \(\sum l_i v_i=1\).
Choose \(t\in\mathbb C\) so that \(l_1+t\) and \(1+t v_1\) are both invertible in \(U\) by the countability just proved.
Both inverses lie in \(D\).
Adding \(\sum_{i>1}(l_1+t)^{-1}l_i v_i\) to \(v_1\) by row operations produces the unit \((l_1+t)^{-1}(1+t v_1)\).
Scaling this entry to one and eliminating the others carries \(v\) to the first standard column by an invertible matrix over \(D\).

If \(\operatorname{diag}(1,T)=PQ\) factors through \(r\) coordinates, the first column of \(Q\) has the first row of \(P\) as a left inverse.
Change the intermediate basis as above.
The transformed factors have first columns \(e_1\) in their respective spaces, so their lower-right blocks multiply to \(T\) through \(r-1\) coordinates.
Conversely adjoining one identity to each factor of \(T\) increases the width by one.
Permutation of coordinates, Schur elimination, and iteration thus give
\[
\rho_D(A^{\oplus m})=m+\rho_D(SI_m).
\]
A finite-layer loss on either side gives a finite-layer loss relative to its full size on the other side, possibly at greater depth.
For \(m=2\), writing \(SI_2=(u,v)^{\mathsf T}(w,z)\) gives \(uw=vz=S\) and \(uz=vw=0\).
When \(S\ne0\), these equations yield nonzero zero divisors in \(D\).
More generally a unital map from \(D\) to a division ring carrying \(S\) to a nonzero element forces \(\rho_D(SI_m)=m\) for all \(m\).

## A finite tracial comparison inside one factor

Use the [direct-sum calculation from MSY23 Example 5.24](Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#why-infinite-orbits-and-scalar-injectivity-do-not-finish-the-argument).
Take a free semicircular family \(s,t,z_1,z_2,z_3\) in one finite factor \(M\), for instance the von Neumann algebra generated by that family.
Put \(Y=(t^2,tst,ts^2t)\), \(Z=(z_1,z_2,z_3)\), and \(X_i=\operatorname{diag}(Y_i,Z_i)\in M_2(M)\) with normalized trace.
Every nonzero noncommutative polynomial in the three \(X_i\) is injective.
For \(P=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X_1&X_2\\X_2&X_3\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) the two diagonal components have ranks one and two, respectively, and the pivot \(X_1\) is an affiliated unit.
The resulting Schur complement has nullity \(1/2\).

The ambient algebra \(M_2(M)\) is a factor, so the affiliated width of \(P^{\oplus m}\) is exactly \(\lceil3m/2\rceil\).
Start the scalar inverse layers at the unital algebra generated by the \(X_i\) inside this same factor.
Every entry of each layer commutes with the projection onto the second diagonal coordinate, since an invertible element commuting with that projection has inverse commuting with it.
Taking its second diagonal entry is consequently a unital homomorphism into \(\mathcal U(M)\) on the entire union.
It takes \(P^{\oplus m}\) to a matrix of analytic rank \(2m\), so any factorization over the union has width at least \(2m\).
Thus every finite layer retains width \(2m\), including for \(m\ge3\) when the kernel dimension exceeds one.
The tuple uses the indicated block representation of a polynomial algebra.
The example tests the combination of scalar polynomial injectivity, finite trace and an ambient factor, and leaves the canonical regular group-ring question open.

There is also a commutative test for descent of a specific projection from a free extension.
Let \(x\) be the real coordinate with law \(\frac12\delta_0+\frac12\operatorname{Unif}[1,2]\) and start the inverse layers at \(\mathbb C[x]\).
Every allowed inverse is taken componentwise, so the union consists of rational functions regular at zero, with poles on \([1,2]\) allowed on null sets.
The kernel projection \(p=1_{\{0\}}\) is outside this union, since a rational function vanishing almost everywhere on \([1,2]\) has zero numerator.
After adjoining a free Haar unitary \(t\) in the tracial free product, set \(y=t^*x^2t\).
The free supports of \(x^2\) and \(y\) both have trace \(1/2\), so \(x^2+y\) is an affiliated unit and the [two Schur eliminations](../claims/Yin_C_A0124_v1.md#the-kernel-projection-and-its-approximants) give \(p=1-x(x^2+y)^{-1}x\).
Thus a projection in the original von Neumann algebra can first acquire a rational formula after free extension.
This last scalar model has an atom at zero and serves only to test descent.

## Conversion to a group-ring kernel

Under scalar injectivity for \(\mathbb CG\), a nonzero polynomial column \(v\) with \(Av=0\) excludes a fractional Schur kernel by the [two-by-two zero-pair criterion](../claims/Yin_C_A0113_v1.md#the-two-by-two-schur-complement).
A nonzero polynomial kernel vector for \(A^{\oplus m}\) supplies such a column by selecting any nonzero block.
This deduction also applies to \(A^{\oplus m}=PQ\) if its shorter right factor \(Q\) has a nonzero kernel column over \(D\) whose entries admit a common right multiplier into \(\mathbb CG\) preserving a nonzero entry.
The width inequality by itself supplies neither the polynomial column nor that multiplier.
For instance, over the torsion-free free group \(F_2=\langle u,v\rangle\) the group-ring row \((u-1,v-1)\) is injective on finite columns, since a tree has no nonzero finitely supported one-cycle.
The [right Ore argument](Yin_L_A0002_v1.md#denominator-clearing-and-finite-propagation) supplies common denominators and integer rank when the group ring is a right Ore domain and its nonzero elements are affiliated units.
For the original group in general, both the existence of a finite-layer compression and the conversion of such a compression to a contradiction with scalar injectivity require additional information about regular convolution.
