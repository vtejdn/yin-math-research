# A rational kernel projection whose polynomial annihilators have unbounded widths

In [A0003](../tasks/Yin_T_A0003_v3.md#contract), finite coset matrices give bounded rational annihilators approaching the von Neumann dimension of the kernel when the subgroup has regular division closure. [Finite coset estimates](../claims/Yin_C_A0134_v1.md#statement)
For a cyclic quotient, one might seek a uniform bound on the widths in the cyclic coordinate of the skew Laurent polynomials needed to generate that kernel.
The example below has an explicit rational kernel projection and a strictly positive rank deficit at every finite width.
It also gives a scalar non-finitely-generated right annihilator in a skew Laurent ring over a regular commutative group algebra.

## The lamp and its finite paths

Let \(K\) be a finitely generated group, \(H=\bigoplus_{\mathbb Z}K\), and \(G=K\wr\mathbb Z=H\rtimes\langle t\rangle\), where \(t\) shifts the copies.
Write \(U_J\) and \(D_J\) for the affiliated algebra and the division closure of \(\mathbb CJ\) in \(U_J\), respectively.
Assume \(D_K\) contains a projection \(p\) with \(0<\theta=\tau_K(p)<1\), and embed it in the zeroth lamp.
For \(K=C_2=\langle a\rangle\) one takes \(p=(1+a)/2\), so \(\theta=1/2\).
The translates \(t^jpt^{-j}\) commute, and their joint trace is the product of the Bernoulli distributions of parameter \(\theta\).
This follows from the tensor-product trace on the commuting group von Neumann algebras of the distinct copies of \(K\).
The affiliated inclusion sends \(D_K\) into \(D_H\) and \(D_H\) into \(D_G\).

Set \(S=D_H[t,t^{-1};\alpha]\subseteq U_G\), with \(\alpha\) conjugation by \(t\), and put
\[
 v=ptp,\qquad x=(1-p)+v+v^* \ \in S .
\]
In the coset model \(\ell^2(G)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}t^n\ell^2(H)\), \(t\) advances the integer coordinate and \(p\) in coordinate \(n\) acts by \(t^{-n}pt^n\) on \(\ell^2(H)\).
Decompose \(\ell^2(H)\) over the abelian von Neumann algebra generated by these translates, retaining its multiplicities.
The faithful trace has the Bernoulli measure on this abelian algebra.
For almost every configuration the open sites form a union of finite runs, since the probability that a specified site begins or ends an infinite string of open sites is zero.
On a closed site \(x\) acts by the identity, and on each finite open run \(x\) is path adjacency tensored with the identity on its multiplicity space.
On an odd run of length \(\ell=2m+1\), its kernel consists of multiples, with that multiplicity, of the vector with coefficients \(1,0,-1,0,\ldots,(-1)^m\).
On an even run the endpoint condition forces the kernel to vanish.
These statements follow from the adjacency recurrence at successive sites including the two endpoints.
The [odd-path trace calculation](../claims/Yin_C_A0005_v2.md#path-blocks-and-the-exact-kernel-dimension) and the [Bernoulli realization](../claims/Yin_C_A0005_v2.md#torsion-free-realization-in-the-von-neumann-algebra) supply the previous path model.
For finite lamps the link between the trace and the random finite clusters also appears in [Lehner, Neuhauser and Woess, Section 2.B](../literature/pdf/LNW08/arxiv-v2.html#S2.SS2).

## A rational formula on all odd runs

Put \(f=p-vv^*\), \(l=p-v^*v\), the start and end projections of the runs.
Since \(v\) is the forward shift cut off at either end, it is nilpotent on every finite run.
Consequently \(1+v^2\) has zero kernel and is an affiliated unit, even though its inverse need not be bounded.
Define
\[
 R=(1+v^2)^{-1}f,\qquad e=(lR)^*(lR),\qquad Y=Re .
\]
On each run \(R\) sends the start coordinate to the vector with alternating signs on all even offsets and is zero on the other inputs.
The operator \(lR\) maps starts of odd runs isometrically up to sign onto their ends and kills the remaining inputs, so \(e\) is the projection onto starts of odd runs.
These computations can be made on the increasing union of subspaces of bounded run length, and determine the closed affiliated operators on the full space by their direct-integral descriptions.
Thus \(xY=0\) and the closure of the range of \(Y\) is exactly \(\ker x\), including every finite odd run.
The positive affiliated operators \(x^*x\) and \(YY^*\) have complementary supports, which gives
\[
     p_{\ker x}=YY^*(x^*x+YY^*)^{-1}\in D_G .
\]
All inverses in this expression and the construction of \(Y\) are inverses of affiliated units.

## Exact loss under a bound on Laurent width

For nonzero \(y=\sum_{j=a}^b t^jy_j\in S\) with the end coefficients nonzero define its width as \(b-a+1\).
The uniqueness of these coefficients follows first for bounded \(y_j\) by the disjoint cosets, and for affiliated coefficients by multiplying on the right by \(d=(1+\sum_j y_j^*y_j)^{-1}\in D_H\).
Here \(d\) is an affiliated unit and \(y_jd\) is bounded, since \(d(\sum_j y_j^*y_j)d\leq 1\).
Consequently \(yd\) is bounded, has the same width as \(y\), and has the same closed range by the affiliated right-ideal identity \(yU_G=ydU_G\).

Fix \(W\geq1\) and let \(P_W\) project onto the closed span of the ranges of all \(y\in S\) of width at most \(W\) satisfying \(xy=0\), including the zero range.
A bounded such \(y\) maps every vector supported at a fixed integer height to an interval of at most \(W\) heights.
Every value of \(y\) belongs to \(\ker x\).
After decomposition over the Bernoulli algebra, a kernel vector on an odd run must have nonzero components at both endpoints whenever its component on that run is nonzero.
Hence the image of a vector supported at one input height vanishes on runs longer than \(W\).
Finite sums of such inputs are dense, and the resulting constraint is a closed subspace condition.
The denominator argument gives the same constraint for an unbounded polynomial \(y\).
This argument permits the coefficients of \(y\) to mix the fibers of the Bernoulli algebra.

For odd \(\ell=2m+1\), let \(f_\ell\) be the start projection of runs of length exactly \(\ell\).
It is a finite product in \(D_H\), imposing the closed site before the start, the \(\ell\) open sites and the following closed site.
The polynomial
\[
 Y_\ell=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^j v^{2j}f_\ell
\]
has width \(\ell\), satisfies \(xY_\ell=0\) and has closed range equal to the entire kernel on runs of this length.
For \(\ell\le W\) these initial and final spaces are orthogonal across distinct lengths, so the sum over all such odd \(\ell\) has width at most \(W\) and range equal to \(P_W\ell^2(G)\).
In particular \(P_W\) is the kernel projection restricted to odd runs of length at most \(W\).

The partial isometry obtained by dividing \(Y_\ell\) by \(\sqrt{m+1}\) has initial projection \(f_\ell\), whose trace is \((1-\theta)^2\theta^\ell\).
Normality of the trace gives
\[
 \tau_G(p_{\ker x})=\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}{1+\theta},\qquad
 \tau_G(p_{\ker x}-P_W)
 = (1-\theta)^2\sum_{\substack{\ell>W\\ \ell\ {\rm odd}}}\theta^\ell>0 .
\]
The ranges of bounded-width annihilators therefore miss positive dimension for every \(W\), although the increasing union of these closed spans is dense in \(\ker x\).
More precisely, the algebraic right annihilator \(\operatorname{ann}_S(x)\) cannot be finitely generated.
If \(y_1,\ldots,y_r\) generated it, choose \(W\) bounding their widths.
All their right multiples and finite sums have left support dominated by \(P_W\), whereas \(Y_\ell\in\operatorname{ann}_S(x)\) has nonzero left support orthogonal to \(P_W\) for odd \(\ell>W\).
The right annihilator of this particular \(x\) in \(D_G\) is \(p_{\ker x}D_G\).

For \(K=C_2\), \(H\) is locally finite abelian and \(D_H=\mathbb CH\), since every element of \(\mathbb CH\) is in a finite group algebra, where an invertible affiliated element has its inverse in the same finite group algebra.
Thus \(D_H\) is commutative and regular, while \(S=\mathbb CG\) fails right coherence by the scalar annihilator above.
For torsion-free \(K\) satisfying the stated projection hypothesis, both \(H\) and \(G\) are torsion-free and \(G\) is finitely generated.
The calculation in this case requires a supplied nontrivial rational lamp projection and makes no assumption about regularity of \(D_H\).
It gives a conditional obstruction to bounded Laurent widths and no torsion-free counterexample to the unrestricted equation.
