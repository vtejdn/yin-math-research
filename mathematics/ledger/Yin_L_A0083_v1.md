# Original-group calibrations of the order-twenty-six homology obstruction

For the [four-site problem](../tasks/Yin_T_A0001_v1.md#contract), suppose \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) is torsion-free with abelianization \(C_{26}\), \(N=[H,H]\), and \(J\) is the preimage of the subgroup of order two.
The [dihedral test](../claims/Yin_C_A0203_v1.md#statement) excludes a half-dimensional kernel when \(H_1(N;\mathbb F_{13})\ne0\) under scalar group-ring injectivity.
The two examples below have the same marking \((1,10)\) on \(C_{26}\) and exhibit opposite outcomes for the homology premise.
The subgroup tests given for the second example address the [necessary odd-dimensional norm](../claims/Yin_C_A0199_v1.md#subgroup-coordinates-and-the-odd-dimensional-norm).

## Why a global invariant degree does not detect the norm

Let \(u\) lift a generator of \(H/N\), put \(g=u^{13}\) and \(\alpha=\operatorname{Ad}_g\).
On the finitely generated abelian group \(B=N_{\rm ab}\), the operator \(S=\operatorname{Ad}_u\) satisfies \(S^{26}=1\) and \(S-1\) is an automorphism by the [coinvariant argument](../claims/Yin_C_A0203_v1.md#from-homology-to-an-actual-rotation-and-reflection).
In particular \(u^{26}\in[N,N]\).
The entire metabelian quotient is \(B\rtimes C_{26}\).
For a different lift \(g'=nu^{13}\) of degree thirteen, \([(g')^2]=(1+\alpha)[n]\) in \(B\).
Thus the norm equation \(V\alpha(V)=(g')^{-2}I\), considered just over \(\mathbb Z[B]\), has the scalar matrix solution associated with \([n]^{-1}\) in multiplicative notation.
This solves the norm after abelianization; a fixed affiliated expression for \(V\) need not admit that specialization.

On \(B\) modulo its torsion subgroup the determinant of \(S-1\) is a unit in \(\mathbb Z\).
The rational representation is a direct sum of cyclotomic summands with orders dividing 26.
The values at one of \(\Phi_1,\Phi_2,\Phi_{13},\Phi_{26}\) are \(0,2,13,1\), respectively, so only \(\Phi_{26}\) can occur.
In particular \(S^{13}=-1\) on \(B\otimes\mathbb Q\); there is no nonzero \(\alpha\)-invariant integer character on \(N\).
There is at most one copy of the cyclotomic summand.
Indeed the map from the free group on the two labels induces a surjection on the first rational homology of the two cyclic kernels.
For the free group its kernel is the fundamental group of the 26-sheeted cover of the two-petal rose.
At every nontrivial complex character the boundary from two edge coordinates to one vertex coordinate has rank one because the two marked images generate the deck group.
Thus the first homology has multiplicity one there, and passing to the quotient cannot increase multiplicity.
It follows that the first Betti number of \(N\) is either zero or twelve.

A related limitation holds for finite-dimensional complex specializations of the norm alone.
In any equivariant complex algebra in which the image \(z\) of \(g^2\) is invertible and algebraic over \(\mathbb C\), some polynomial \(f\) satisfies \(f(z)^2=z^{-1}\).
This follows by expanding a square root of \(w^{-1}\) at each nonzero root of the minimal polynomial of \(z\), keeping the finite jet required by its multiplicity, and applying the Chinese remainder theorem.
Conjugation by \(g\) fixes \(z\) and hence \(f(z)\).
Thus \(f(z)I\) solves the bare norm in any matrix size, even for a noncommutative finite group quotient.
The [dihedral phase proof](../claims/Yin_C_A0203_v1.md#a-phase-dependent-dihedral-representation) uses the specific original rectangle and tensor twists of the regular representation.

## A permutation lattice with first Betti number twelve in the kernel

Take the augmentation lattice \(E=\{(x_i)\in\mathbb Z^{15}:\sum x_i=0\}\).
Let \(a=(1,14)(2,15)\) and \(b=(1,\ldots,13)\) act by permuting the coordinates, and write \(P=\langle a,b\rangle\).
Put \(z_2=e_3-e_4\) and \(z_{13}=e_{14}-e_{15}\).
These vectors are fixed by \(a,b\), respectively.
Form two groups generated by \(E\) and a new element \(u\) or \(v\), with
\[
       \operatorname{Ad}_u=a,\quad u^2=z_2,\qquad
       \operatorname{Ad}_v=b,\quad v^{13}=z_{13},
\]
using additive notation on \(E\).
Each has its indicated cyclic quotient and embeds in the rational affine group \((E\otimes\mathbb Q)\rtimes P\) by mapping \(u,v\) to \((z_2/2,a)\), \((z_{13}/13,b)\), respectively.
Indeed reducing a word leaves one translation and a representative of the cyclic quotient, whose permutation detects its residue.
The first group is torsion-free because the square of a nontrivial coset representative times a translation has odd coordinate at site 3.
For the second, the thirteenth power of a translate of \(v^j\), \(1\le j<13\), has coordinate congruent to \(j\bmod 13\) at site 14.
The pure translation subgroups are both torsion-free.

Amalgamate the two groups along \(E\) to form \(H\).
Its action on the amalgam tree, or the reduced-word normal form, implies torsion-freeness.
Conjugates of \(u^2\) under \(v\) yield the adjacent differences on the 13-cycle.
Two further conjugates under \(u\) join site 14 to site 13 and site 15 to site 3, since \(a(e_1-e_{13})=e_{14}-e_{13}\) and \(a(e_2-e_3)=e_{15}-e_3\).
Differences along a connected graph generate \(E\), hence \(u,v\) generate \(H\).

In the coinvariants \(E_P\) the class of \(e_{pi}-e_i\) is independent of \(i\) for each \(p\in P\).
Each of the generators \(a,b\) has a fixed point, so this class vanishes for both generators and hence for every product.
Transitivity then kills every coordinate difference and \(E_P=0\).
As \(H/E=C_2*C_{13}\), it follows that \(H_{\rm ab}=C_2\oplus C_{13}=C_{26}\).
Take quotient degrees \(13,2\) on \(u,v\) and mark \(s=uv^7,\ t=vs^8\).
This pair generates \(H\) and has degrees \(1,10\).

The quotient \(N/E\) is the kernel of \(C_2*C_{13}\to C_2\times C_{13}\).
It acts freely on its amalgam tree, with quotient graph having 26 edges and \(13+2\) vertices.
It is free of rank twelve, so \(b_1(N)\ge12\) and the preceding bound gives equality.
This group meets the homology obstruction for the stated marking.
It also belongs to a known class: the two vertex embeddings into the affine group agree on \(E\), and the kernel of the resulting map from \(H\) meets no conjugate of a vertex group.
Its action on the tree is free, so \(H\) is free-by-virtually-abelian and satisfies the full complex conjecture by the [Linnell-class theorem](Yin_L_A0001_v1.md#coefficients-and-permanence).
Thus the example verifies the assumptions and the marking independently of any conjectural group.

## An unmarked prime-index norm and its available subgroups

For a torsion-free \(J\) containing \(N\) at index two, the [prime-index test](../claims/Yin_C_A0036_v1.md#an-abelianized-norm-obstruction-and-the-limit-of-integer-valuations) treats invariant degrees on a division closure.
In applications to a particular matrix \(V\), one can restrict the coefficient group first.
Let \(K\le N\) be generated by all group elements in fixed scalar inverse expressions for the entries of \(V\), their \(\alpha\)-images and \(g^2\).
This group is \(\alpha\)-invariant since \(\alpha^2=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^2}\), and subgroup regular embeddings reflect the required inverses and all the equalities in the expression.
If \(D(K)\) is a division ring and there is an \(\alpha\)-invariant integer character of \(K\) taking \(g^2\) to an odd integer, the cited prime-index argument extends it to an \(\alpha\)-invariant integer valuation \(v\) on \(D(K)^\times\).
The Dieudonne determinant on invertible matrices over a division ring is multiplicative with values in its unit abelianization and sends a scalar diagonal of size \(d\) to the \(d\)-th power of that scalar class.
Applying \(v\) to the determinant of \(V\alpha(V)=g^{-2}I_d\) gives \(2v(\det V)=-d\,v(g^2)\), which is impossible for odd \(d\).
A local character of \(K\) need not extend to \(N\).

Another sufficient condition is that \(\langle K,g\rangle\) have a subgroup \(L\) of finite odd index with \(D(L)\) a division ring.
The matrix \(X=V(gI_d)\) over \(D(\langle K,g\rangle)\) then satisfies \(X^2=I_d\).
The index-two sign character fixes \(V\), sends \(X\) to \(-X\), and preserves regular trace, so the two complementary idempotents \((I_d\pm X)/2\) have dimension \(d/2\).
Finite-index restriction sends the ambient division closure into matrices over \(D(L)\) by [coset coordinates and matrix inversion](../claims/Yin_C_A0129_v1.md#finite-index-transfer-with-a-depth-bound).
Their dimensions after restriction would be half of the odd integer \(d[\langle K,g\rangle:L]\), contradicting skew-field rank.

## A perfect kernel passing the homology test

Use the [full triangle lift](../audits/literature/Mil75/Yin_U001.md#lifted-triangle-presentation) \(\Gamma\) for \(\Delta=\Sigma(3,5,7)\) and denote its central full turn by \(c\).
The triangle group is perfect: in its abelianization the vertex orders are pairwise coprime and the sum of the three generators is zero.
As \(\Gamma/\langle c\rangle=\Delta\), \(\Gamma_{\rm ab}\) is cyclic generated by \(c\); the determinant of the abelianized four-generator presentation is \(105-35-21-15=34\).
For \(N=[\Gamma,\Gamma]\) this gives \(N\cap\langle c\rangle=\langle z\rangle\) with \(z=c^{34}\).
Moreover \(\Gamma=N\langle c\rangle\) and \(c\) is central, so \(N=[N,N]\).
It maps onto \(\Delta\).
Choose \(x,y\in N\) lifting the first two triangle generators.
Since \(N=\langle x,y,z\rangle\) and \(N\) is perfect, \(x,y\) already generate \(N\).

Adjoin a central \(26\)-th root \(v\) of \(z\) by setting \(H=(N\times\langle v\rangle)/\langle(z^{-1},v^{26})\rangle\).
The subgroup \(N\) embeds, \(H/N=C_{26}\), \(v\) has infinite order, and \(H/\langle v\rangle=\Delta\).
The group \(N\) is torsion-free: any finite order element projects to a conjugate of a vertex rotation in the triangle action, and its preimage in the full lift belongs to a conjugate of the infinite cyclic lifted rotation group.
A nontrivial finite-order element in \(H\) would, since \(N\) is torsion-free and \(H/N\) has order 26, give an element of prime order 2 or 13.
Its projection to \(\Delta\) would be trivial, as neither prime divides a vertex order; the kernel \(\langle v\rangle\) is infinite cyclic.
This proves torsion-freeness.
Centrality and perfectness give \(H_{\rm ab}=C_{26}\) with kernel \(N\).
The two elements \(vx,v^{10}y\) have the required degrees and generate \(H\): commutators of words in them yield all commutators of the same words in \(x,y\), and these generate \(N\).

Here \(H_1(N;\mathbb F_{13})=0\), so the positive-homology test leaves this group.
There is still an odd-index subgroup of \(J=\langle N,v^{13}\rangle\) with a division-ring closure.
Take a normal finite-index torsion-free \(\Delta_0\le\Delta\), using the [linear triangle-group construction](Yin_L_A0082_v1.md#the-surface-subgroup-and-its-central-extension).
In \(\Delta/\Delta_0\) choose a Sylow 2-subgroup and let \(\Delta_1\) be its inverse image.
It has odd index and is torsion-free, since any nontrivial torsion in \(\Delta\) has odd order and retains its order modulo \(\Delta_0\).
Thus \(\Delta_1\) is a closed hyperbolic surface group.
The inverse image of \(\Delta_1\) in \(J\) has odd index and is a central extension of \(\Delta_1\) by \(\langle v^{13}\rangle\).
It is locally indicable: any nontrivial finitely generated subgroup either projects onto a nontrivial finitely generated subgroup of the orientable surface group, which maps onto \(\mathbb Z\), or lies in the cyclic fiber.
The [locally indicable complex theorem](../audits/literature/JZLA20/Yin_U001.md#atiyah-proof) gives the required division closure.
In particular the odd-matrix norm in the original group is excluded even though its abelian quotient has an explicit solution.
This example illustrates two separate group tests and does not extend known integrality for central lifts of surface groups.
