# Rationality Conventions and the Scope of Yin_C_F0013

<a id="rationality-conventions"></a>
Date: 2026-09-09.
Context: the user requested exact hypotheses, denominator independence and a complete definition of bounded scalar rational expressions.
This note clarifies the existing [Yin_C_F0001 definition](../claims/Yin_C_F0001_v1.md#statement-and-scope) and [Yin_C_F0013 theorem](../claims/Yin_C_F0013_v1.md#yin_c_f0013); their authoritative statements and original proof content remain unchanged.

## What Existence of a Dual System Means Here

The retained setting is a finite von Neumann algebra $(M,\tau)$ with faithful normal tracial state, $H=L^2(M,\tau)$, $\Omega=1$, finitely many bounded normal generators $X_i$ with $M=W^*(X)$, and bounded operators

\[
D_i\in B(H),\qquad [D_i,X_j]=\delta_{ij}P_\Omega.
\]

Within this setting, existence of one such system suffices for Yin_C_F0013.
No freeness, prescribed distribution, factoriality, selfadjointness of the duals or vacuum normalization is needed.
Normality of the generators and boundedness of the duals have not been removed.
The proof uses normality to deduce that $\sum_i[\rho(X_i),D_i]+P_\Omega$ commutes with all of $M$.
Merely having conjugate variables, or unbounded duals on a dense domain, is not an established substitute.

## Bounded Fractions Always Exist

For every $f\in U(M)=L^0(M,\tau)$, regardless of rationality or duals, set

\[
s=(1+ff^*)^{-1/2},\quad a=sf,\qquad
t=(1+f^*f)^{-1/2},\quad b=ft.
\]

Polar decomposition and spectral calculus give $s,a,b,t\in M$, all contractions; $s,t$ are injective with dense range and

\[
f=s^{-1}a=bt^{-1}.
\]

The products are the closed affiliated products.
The denominators need not have bounded inverses, and these four bounded elements are not required to be rational functions of $X$.
This existence statement is a general finite-tracial foundation, not part of the unknown analytic regularity problem.

## Exactly What Is Independent of Choices

The bounded operator

\[
\Delta_i(f;s,a,b,t)=sD_i b-aD_i t
\]

does depend on the fraction representatives.
For $f=X_j$, the fractions $(1,X_j,X_j,1)$ give $\Delta_j=P_\Omega$, whereas $(2,2X_j,X_j,1)$ give $2P_\Omega$.

Its finite-rank property is independent, as proved in Yin_C_F0013.
In fact its finite numerical rank is independent as well.
Yin_C_F0013's common-multiple identity reads

\[
y\Delta_i' y'=x\Delta_i x',
\]

where $x,y,x',y'$ are bounded regular elements.
If $T$ has finite rank, $A$ is bounded injective and $B$ is bounded with dense range, then

\[
\operatorname{rank}(ATB)=\operatorname{rank}T.
\]

Injectivity preserves range dimension.
Also $T(BH)$ is dense in $T(H)$ and is a linear subspace of that finite-dimensional space, hence equals it.
Applying this fact to both sides proves the rank equality.
If either defect has infinite rank, the previously proved finite-rank equivalence rules out finite rank of the other.
Thus rank with all infinite ranks denoted by $\infty$ is an invariant.

For fixed fractions, the actual defect is also independent of the chosen bounded dual system.
If $D_i'$ is another system, $B_i=D_i-D_i'$ commutes with all $X_j$.
Fuglede and normality put $B_i$ in $M'$.
Consequently

\[
sB_i b-aB_i t=(sb-at)B_i=0.
\]

These are different assertions: changing fractions can change the operator, while changing the bounded dual system at fixed fractions does not.

## Complete Definition of Bounded Scalar Rational Expressions

Terminology clarification, 2026-09-09: this is exactly the standard division closure in $M$, described through admissible finite expressions.
[Yin20 definition interface](../audits/literature/Yin20/Yin_U001.md#general-definition-homes-and-primary-locators), Definition IV.5.5 and Remark IV.5.6(iv), printed p. 60 / PDF p. 70, gives the same recursive construction.
"Bounded scalar rational expressions" is explanatory wording, not a distinct class.
Prefer "division closure in $M$" when naming the algebra.

Fix $X\in M^d$ and let

\[
A_X=\mathbb C\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle.
\]

Start with scalar multiples of $1$ and the listed $X_i$.
Form a finite expression using addition, subtraction, multiplication and parentheses.
An inverse subexpression $r^{-1}$ is admissible only if its already evaluated operand $r(X)$ is invertible in $M$: there is $y\in M$ with $r(X)y=yr(X)=1$.
Evaluate that subexpression as this bounded inverse.
Apply the rule at every inverse node, including nested ones.
The final value of any such admissible expression is called a bounded scalar rational expression in $X$ here.

The collection of all values is precisely

\[
\mathcal R_X=\operatorname{Div}_M A_X.
\]

Equivalently, with $\operatorname{alg}_1$ denoting the unital complex algebra generated by a set,

\[
R_0=A_X,\qquad
R_{n+1}=\operatorname{alg}_1\!\left(
R_n\cup\{r^{-1}:r\in R_n,\ r\in M^\times\}\right),\qquad
\mathcal R_X=\bigcup_{n\ge0}R_n.
\]

The union contains $A_X$ and is inverse closed inside $M$, because each of its elements belongs to one finite stage.
Every inverse-closed unital subalgebra containing $A_X$ contains each stage by induction.
This proves equality with the smallest such subalgebra and with the finite-expression definition.

Scalar refers to inversion of individual algebra elements (a $1$-by-$1$ expression), not to commutativity.
Coefficients are complex scalars; arbitrary elements of $M$ are not allowed as coefficients.
Adjoint is not an additional expression operation: $X_i^*$ is available only when already expressible under the rules, for example for selfadjoint generators or $X_i^*=X_i^{-1}$ for unitaries.
No norm, strong, weak or measure closure is taken.

For example,

\[
(1-X_1X_2)^{-1}X_1(2-X_2)^{-1}
\]

is admissible exactly when both displayed operands are invertible in $M$.
Boundedness of a final affiliated value does not retroactively validate an intermediate unbounded inverse.

## Distinguish Three Ambient Constructions

| Construction | Allowed inverses and operations | Relevant distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\operatorname{Div}_M A_X$ | Finite scalar operations; each inverse exists in $M$ | The bounded scalar expression class above |
| Matrix rational closure in $M$ | Entries of inverses of finite matrices over $A_X$ that are invertible in the corresponding matrix algebra over $M$ | An entire bounded inverse is required; reduction to scalar inverses needs a theorem |
| $F_X=\operatorname{Div}_{U(M)}A_X$ | Finite scalar operations; each inverse exists in $U(M)$ | Intermediate values and inverses can be unbounded |

Under Yin_C_F0013's hypotheses, MSY23 identifies the last algebra with the embedded free skew field generated by $X$.
It is not the whole affiliated algebra.
The allowed generator algebra is still $A_X$, with no implicit adjoints.

In the selfadjoint Yin_C_F0012 model, $q=h^{-1}$ is unbounded and $b=q(hbh)q$ is bounded.
This is a valid affiliated rational expression, but its two inverse nodes are inadmissible in $M$.
Yin_C_F0012's separate norm-exclusion argument proves that no alternative bounded scalar expression recovers $b$; the failure of this one expression alone would not prove nonmembership.

## How Yin_C_F0013 Avoids the Bounded Rationality Intermediate

Let $\mathscr D$ be the class of affiliated elements with finite bounded denominator defects, and let $F=\operatorname{Div}_{U(M)}A_X$.
The [Miy22-U007 reconstruction](../audits/literature/Miy22/Yin_U007.md#miy22-u007) identifies the source's bounded intermediate precisely:

\[
u\in\mathscr D
\ \Longrightarrow\
s=(1+uu^*)^{-1},\ a=su\in\mathscr D\cap M
\ \Longrightarrow\
s,a\in\operatorname{Div}_M A_X
\ \Longrightarrow\
u=s^{-1}a\in F.
\]

The middle implication uses Miy22's bounded Theorem 3.1 through Remark 4.7; putting the regularizers in $\mathscr D$ uses adjoint closure.
This is a valid source proof in its semicircular setting.

Yin_C_F0013 takes a different route for $\mathscr D\subseteq F$.
Its essential algebraic input is MSY23's theorem that $F$ is a division ring under the bounded-dual hypotheses.
For arbitrary bounded fractions $u=s^{-1}a=bt^{-1}$ it sets

\[
K=\sum_i[\rho(X_i),D_i],\qquad C=K+P_\Omega.
\]

The dual relations and exact Fuglede commutation for normal generators give $C\in M'$.
Since $sb=at$ and all four fraction coefficients are bounded,

\[
\sum_i[\rho(X_i),\Delta_i]
=sKb-aKt=aP_\Omega t-sP_\Omega b.
\]

Writing the finite-rank $\Delta_i$ as finite tensors converts this bounded identity into

\[
a\otimes t-s\otimes b
=\sum_{i,\alpha}
(g_{i\alpha}X_i\otimes h_{i\alpha}
-g_{i\alpha}\otimes X_ih_{i\alpha}).
\]

In the algebraic balanced tensor product $U(M)\otimes_F U(M)$, the right side vanishes because $X_i\in F$.
Multiplication on the outer sides by $s^{-1}$ and $t^{-1}$ therefore gives

\[
u\otimes_F1=1\otimes_Fu.
\]

This forces $u\in F$.
Otherwise $1,u$ are right-$F$ linearly independent; extending them to a basis gives a right-$F$ linear functional with values $0,1$ on these two vectors.
Applying it to the first tensor factor gives $1=0$.

The other inclusion, $F\subseteq\mathscr D$, follows by the generic sum/product/inverse formulas for bounded fractions.
In particular, inversion swaps numerator and denominator and negates the defect.
Those formulas need neither adjoint closure nor the bounded rationality theorem.

Thus Yin_C_F0013 still uses bounded representatives for legitimate everywhere-defined operator calculations, but it never proves that the representatives individually belong to $\operatorname{Div}_M A_X$.
Its tradeoff is the strong algebraic division-ring input.
It yields affiliated rationality, not the generally false bounded-intersection equality.
