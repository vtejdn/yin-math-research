## 1. Question, source reuse and accepted inputs

For projections \(p,q\) in a finite tracial von Neumann algebra, let
\[
 \mathcal M=W^*(p,q),\qquad A=\mathbb C\langle p,q\rangle.
\]
The equality under consideration is
\[
 \operatorname{Div}_{L^0(\mathcal M)}A\cap\mathcal M
   =\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal M}A.
 \tag{1}
\]
Both closures use scalar inverses in their indicated ambient algebras.
All scalar expressions reconstructed in C_F0100 use only bounded inverses in \(\mathcal M\); passing merely to a matrix or C*-closure would not establish (1).

## 2. Algebra and the generic matrix model

Put
\[
 T=1-p-q+pq+qp=1-(p-q)^2=(1-p-q)^2,
 \qquad b=pq(1-p).
 \tag{2}
\]
Direct multiplication gives \([T,p]=[T,q]=0\), \(Tp=pqp\), and
\[
 q=Tp+(1-T)(1-p)+b+b^*.
 \tag{3}
\]
In the generic part of the two-projection representation,
\[
 p(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 q(t)=\begin{pmatrix}t&s(t)\\s(t)&1-t\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad s(t)=\sqrt{t(1-t)},\qquad 0<t<1.
 \tag{4}
\]
Here \(T(t)=tI_2\) and \(b(t)=s(t)E_{12}\).
On the remaining summands \((p,q)=(i,j)\in\{0,1\}^2\); write their central projections as
\[
 e_{11}=p\wedge q,\quad e_{10}=p\wedge(1-q),\quad
 e_{01}=(1-p)\wedge q,\quad e_{00}=(1-p)\wedge(1-q),
 \qquad e=1-\sum_{i,j}e_{ij}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The parameter \(T\) is 0 on \(e_{10},e_{01}\) and 1 on \(e_{11},e_{00}\).
Each nonzero character corner of \(\mathcal M=W^*(p,q)\) is one-dimensional.
Merely knowing (5) as spectral projections does **not** put them in \(\operatorname{Div}_{\mathcal M}A\).

On a generic part with diffuse parameter law, every scalar affiliated rational expression in \(p,q\) has the form
\[
 F(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
  a(t)&s(t)c(t)\\ s(t)d(t)&h(t)
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad a,c,d,h\in\mathbb C(t).
 \tag{6}
\]
For clarity, this is an algebraic induction on the expression, not an identification with arbitrary measurable matrix entries.
The displayed class is closed under sums and products.
Its inverse, when defined, is
\[
 F(t)^{-1}=\frac1{a(t)h(t)-t(1-t)c(t)d(t)}
 \begin{pmatrix}h(t)&-s(t)c(t)\\-s(t)d(t)&a(t)\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{7}
\]
An actual affiliated inverse has nonzero determinant almost everywhere.
On a diffuse parameter measure with interval support this determinant is a nonzero rational function, so (7) is valid off a finite null set.
Compression to the central generic corner preserves every inverse used in the original expression.
No inverse is newly evaluated on a character where it was undefined.
