# Actual inverse rectangles and a complete directional Schur estimate

This ordinary development for Yin, dated 2026-11-06T11:30 UTC, studies the actual good-minor geometry for [F13](../tasks/Yin_T_F0013_v1.md#contract).
The proof and its limitation were checked during authoring, without independent review or Claim adoption.
The [D10 investigation](../drafts/Yin_D_F0010.md#actual-inverse-rectangles-and-directional-schur) is development provenance; the complete argument is retained here.
It uses the actual good rectangles to obtain an original-law estimate for every auxiliary direction selected before the fresh border is sampled.
The estimate applies to the complete Schur quadratic form, including the pole, but it does not yet control a kernel direction selected by that same border.
In particular it neither supplies a determinant floor nor settles the same-sign definite single-pole range \(N\lesssim|\lambda|\lesssim N^4\).

<a id="inverse-cut-transmission"></a>
## A commutator transfers the actual rectangles through the inverse

Use the real symmetric size-three restriction of the [original positive-frame model](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v17.md#statement), with fixed real symmetric \(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_d\), complex-direction frame \(\chi_A>0\), and a deterministic real scalar shift \(\theta_N\).
Each real off-diagonal input law is fixed, centered and has variance one, each real diagonal law is fixed, centered and has finite second moment, and all scalar upper-triangular coordinates and input families are independent within each dimension.
Cross-size coupling is arbitrary, and no density or moment above two is imposed.
For each specified physical deletion, let \(M\) be an actual invertible retained minor on \(n=N-O(1)\) physical indices, keeping the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization, and let \(Z=M^{-1}\).
The conditioning sigma-field \(\mathcal F\) is generated by this minor and the exposed diagonal of the removed physical block, so the fresh border retains its independent original coordinate laws.
Fix \(B_0\ge1\), and impose only the following minor-measurable guards for the present argument:
\[
 \|M\|\le B_0\sqrt N,\qquad
 s_{\min}(M[J,I])\ge\frac a{\sqrt N}
 \quad\text{for every }I\subset[n],\ |I|=k:=\lfloor\delta n\rfloor,\ J=[n]\setminus I.
 \tag{Inverse-cut-actual-guards}
\]
Here \(a,\delta>0\), with \(\delta\le1/4\), are the constants in the [accepted actual positive-frame rectangle theorem](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v17.md#statement).
Applying that theorem in dimension \(n\) to \(\sqrt{N/n}\,M\) gives exactly the displayed threshold, since its deterministic shift may depend on \(N\) and its off-diagonal normalization is then \(n^{-1/2}\).
For each specified minor the simultaneous rectangle exception is at most \(Ce^{-cN}\); no simultaneous union over all removed minors is being taken here.
The norm guard remains a minor event, and no full-parent norm event is inserted into the fresh-coordinate conditional distribution.

Fix one such \(I\), put \(r=3k\), and let \(P\) project onto its \(r\) auxiliary coordinates.
The exact inverse identity is
\[
 [P,Z]=-Z[P,M]Z.
 \tag{Inverse-cut-commutator}
\]
In the \(I,J\) ordering, \([P,M]\) has off-diagonal blocks \(M[I,J]\) and \(-M[J,I]\), with zero diagonal blocks.
Because \(M\) is real symmetric, the nonzero singular values of this commutator are the \(r\) singular values of \(M[J,I]\), each occurring twice.
The same assertion holds for \(Z\), including possible zero singular values.
For singular values in decreasing order, multiplication on both sides by the invertible \(Z\) therefore gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 s_r(Z[J,I])
 &=s_{2r}([P,Z])\\
 &\ge s_{\min}(Z)^2s_{2r}([P,M])
 =\|M\|^{-2}s_r(M[J,I])
 \ge \frac a{B_0^2}N^{-3/2}
 =:t_N.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Inverse-cut-full-rank}
\]
Thus the inverse cut retains all \(r\) singular directions at this polynomial scale, simultaneously for the actual supports in (Inverse-cut-actual-guards).
This is a deterministic consequence of their geometry and is not an independence assertion about the entries of \(Z\).

On a single-pole branch write \(Z=E+\lambda ee^T\), with \(e\) a real unit vector, \(E=E^T\) and \(Ee=0\), selecting these spectral data measurably from the minor.
Rank-one singular-value interlacing gives, for \(r\ge2\),
\[
 s_{r-1}(E[J,I])\ge s_r(Z[J,I])\ge t_N.
 \tag{Inverse-cut-residual-rank}
\]
The index loss is exactly one on this rectangular cut, not two.
Neither the sign or size of \(\lambda\), definiteness of the background, nor a bound on \(\|E\|_F\) is used in these two inverse-cut conclusions.

## The actual frame turns a cut into a matching of original quadratic coefficients

Choose a real unit auxiliary vector \(u\) measurably from \(\mathcal F\), before the fresh border is sampled.
For every physical index use the \(3\)-by-\(d\) matrix
\[
 G(u)=(A_1u,\ldots,A_du).
\]
The frame condition implies \(G(u)G(u)^T\succeq\chi_AI_3\), since its definition over complex directions includes the real unit test directions used here.
Let \(G_I(u)\) and \(G_J(u)\) be the corresponding block-diagonal maps onto the auxiliary coordinates of \(I\) and \(J\).
For \(D=Z\) or \(D=E\), put
\[
 K_D(u)=G_J(u)^TD[J,I]G_I(u),\qquad
 R_Z=r,\quad R_E=r-1.
\]
Both block maps are surjective with their nonzero singular values at least \(\sqrt{\chi_A}\), and their transposes are injective with the same lower bound.
Their polar decompositions, or the singular-value min-max principle on their ranges, consequently give
\[
 s_{R_D}(K_D(u))\ge\chi_A s_{R_D}(D[J,I])
 \ge \chi_At_N=:\sigma_N.
 \tag{Inverse-cut-directional-rank}
\]
All these deterministic constants are uniform over the unit vector \(u\); the probabilistic use below still fixes \(u\) before sampling.

Form a bipartite graph on the \(d|J|\) and \(d|I|\) original scalar coordinates, placing an edge at each entry of \(K_D(u)\) with modulus at least
\[
 \tau_N:=\frac{\sigma_N}{4dN}.
\]
If a maximum matching had \(m<R_D/2\) edges, its \(2m\) endpoints would cover every edge.
Indeed an uncovered edge could be added to that matching.
Retaining all entries in those covered rows or columns gives a matrix \(L\) of rank at most \(2m<R_D\).
Every remaining entry has modulus below \(\tau_N\), so
\[
 \|K_D(u)-L\|\le\|K_D(u)-L\|_F
 \le dN\tau_N=\sigma_N/4,
\]
contradicting \(s_{R_D}(K_D(u))\ge\sigma_N\) and the singular-value distance to matrices of rank below \(R_D\).
There is therefore a matching of at least \(R_D/2\ge cN\) disjoint original coordinate pairs.
The finite choices may be made measurably from \(\mathcal F\), for example by choosing the first maximum matching in a fixed enumeration.

Write the actual fresh border as
\[
 R_k=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^dA_j\xi_{j,k},
\]
and retain the complete scalar quadratic polynomial
\[
 Q_D(u):=u^T(A_0+\theta_NI_3+D_i-R^TDR)u.
\]
Its cross-\(I,J\) part is exactly \(-2N^{-1}\xi_J^TK_D(u)\xi_I\), with the factor two coming from the symmetry of \(D\).
The selected disjoint quadratic monomials consequently have coefficient moduli at least
\[
 \frac{2\tau_N}{N}
 =\frac{\chi_Aa}{2dB_0^2}N^{-7/2}.
 \tag{Inverse-cut-original-coefficient-scale}
\]
All other quadratic, linear and constant terms remain in \(Q_D(u)\); no residual proxy or coefficient-wise expectation has been substituted.
For \(D=Z\), this is exactly \(u^TSu\) for the complete Schur matrix \(S=A_0+\theta_NI_3+D_i-R^TZR\).

## Original-law small-ball control and the remaining adaptive-direction step

Use the exact original-law two-copy representation
\[
 \xi_{j,k}=\frac{\xi'_{j,k}+\xi''_{j,k}}2
       +\varepsilon_{j,k}\frac{\xi'_{j,k}-\xi''_{j,k}}2,
\]
with independent original-law copies and independent fair signs, separately for all fresh coordinates.
Choosing either copy with an independent fair sign retains the original joint product law.
The fixed nonzero variances and finite number of laws give \(b_0,p_0>0\) such that every copy difference has modulus at least \(b_0\) with probability at least \(p_0\).
The matching uses disjoint coordinates, so a fixed positive fraction of its pairs survive these two difference tests outside an error \(Ce^{-cN}\).
After conditioning on all copies, the corresponding quadratic sign coefficients have modulus at least \(b_0^2/4\) times (Inverse-cut-original-coefficient-scale).
Squares become affine in their signs, and total degree at most two prevents higher-degree collisions from changing these selected quadratic coefficients.
All linear and constant sign coefficients may be arbitrary.
The accepted [MNV16 degree-two bound and interval convention](../audits/literature/MNV16/Yin_U001.md#quadratic-specialization) therefore give fixed \(c_*,c,C>0\) such that
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
 \mathbb P\left\{|Q_D(u)-t|\le c_*N^{-7/2}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F\right\}
 \le Ce^{-cN}+C\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/9}
 =:\varepsilon_N,
 \qquad D\in\{Z,E\}.
 \tag{Inverse-cut-complete-directional-small-ball}
\]
Take \(c_*\) small enough that the closed interval has length strictly below the surviving coefficient scale.
This reuses that source's recorded inspection scope and source-proof limitation; it asserts no new source-proof review.
The center and \(u\) may depend on the minor and exposed diagonal, but neither may depend on the fresh border.
The estimate has no loss depending on \(\lambda\), requires no convergence rate for the residual approximation error, and uses no moment above two.

For any minor-measurable good event \(\mathcal G_i\) carrying the stated guards and any allowed choices \(u_i,t_i\), integration gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\mathcal G_i,\ |u_i^TS_iu_i-t_i|\le c_*N^{-7/2}\}
 \le\varepsilon_N\mathbb P(\mathcal G_i).
\]
Its average over physical indices is therefore at most \(\varepsilon_N\), without a factor \(N\).
Intersecting a full-parent norm guard can only decrease these unconditioned events; conditioning on that fresh-border-dependent guard is not allowed.

The first unsupported step toward the desired determinant estimate is replacing the preselected \(u_i\) by an approximate kernel direction of \(S_i\), which itself depends on the fresh border.
A unit sphere net at the polynomial precision demanded by this quadratic window has polynomially many directions, and the displayed \(N^{-1/9}\) bound does not pay that union.
On the single-pole branch, set \(S_E=A_0+\theta_NI_3+D_i-R^TER\) and \(z=\sqrt N R^Te\).
A bound on one complete quadratic form does not control the full determinant
\[
 \frac N\lambda\det S_E-z^T\operatorname{adj}(S_E)z
 \quad\text{or its graph denominator}\quad
 \sqrt{\det(I_3+R^TZ^2R)}.
\]
The actual good rectangles have thus ruled out the low-rank cut obstruction of [L63v7](Yin_L_F0063_v7.md#fixed-law-realization-and-rectangle-boundary) and supplied a complete original-law directional estimate, but no good-minor counterexample or full determinant small-ball conclusion has been obtained.
A discriminating continuation would have to make a kernel direction independent of an unused physical subblock while retaining enough inverse-cut rank, or extract a surviving matching from the complete determinant using several cuts jointly.
Neither adaptive exposure nor that complete determinant matching is supplied by the present argument.
