# A uniformly invertible actual minor still permits a nonsummable conditional atom

This ordinary investigation for Yin on 2026-11-06 starts from the [remaining polynomially invertible branch](Yin_L_F0066_v10.md#remaining-polynomially-invertible-minors).
It adapts the actual second-and-third-factor background of [L66v9](Yin_L_F0066_v9.md#actual-high-matching-background), changing the first principal block and full diagonal entries so that the complete nonzero center cancels on a definite event of the original first row.
The resulting minor has smallest singular value greater than one, yet the complete normalized numerator has a conditional zero atom of order \(N^{-1}\), with linear-size matching.
The fixed-law realization uses bounded off-diagonal laws and unbounded diagonal laws with every finite moment, on exponentially rare backgrounds.
This rules out a uniform conditional shortcut on the remaining branch; it does not refute the averaged high-concentration target, the bounded-diagonal pilot, or F0012.
The calculations were developed through ordinary reasoning and self-checks; the separate [independent whole-material review R200v19](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_F0200_v19.md#invertible-minor-atom-whole-material) checks the complete construction and its scope.

## Actual model, conditioning and the unresolved average

The input class is the real three-factor specialization of the fixed-law [finite-variance Task](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract).
Write \(A:=W_1\), \(B:=W_2\), \(C:=W_3\); all upper coordinates within each factor and all three factors are independent at each size, with the raw fixed laws specified below and the normalization \(N^{-1/2}\).
The laws are fixed for every dimension, while the particular backgrounds below use the subsequence \(N=4k+1\), \(k\ge2\).
No assumption on coupling across dimensions is needed for the conditional statements.

On the factor-invertibility event, put \(M:=A-zC^{-1}B^{-1}\), \(D_i:=M_{\widehat i,\widehat i}\), \(T_i:=\operatorname{adj}D_i\), \(Q_i:=(T_i+T_i^T)/2\), and
\[
 \mathcal F_i:=\sigma(A_{\widehat i,\widehat i},A_{ii},B,C).
\]
On \(T_i\ne0\), use \(Z_i:=\det M/\|T_i\|_F\) and \(\kappa_i(\tau):=\mathbb P\{|Z_i|\le\tau\mid\mathcal F_i\}\).
The event \(\mathcal H_i(a,s)\) requires \(T_i\ne0\) and means that the graph on the retained coordinates with edges \(|(Q_i)_{jk}|\ge a\|T_i\|_F\), \(j\ne k\), contains a matching of at least \(s\) edges.
The [remaining target](Yin_L_F0066_v10.md#remaining-polynomially-invertible-minors) asks for control of the mean over \(i\) of the probability of backgrounds with polynomial factor guards, \(t_1(D_i)>N^{-J}\), high matching, and \(\kappa_i(N^{-\Theta})>N^{-B_*}\), where \(B_*>1\).
The construction below prevents replacing this mean-probability obligation by a bound on \(\kappa_i\) uniform over all such backgrounds.

## Actual three-factor construction and the nonzero center

Let \(n\ge4\) be even, \(N:=2n+1\), \(m:=2n\), and \(r:=m/N\).
Split the \(m\) retained coordinates into two groups of size \(n\), with normalized indicator vectors \(p,q\in\mathbb R^m\).
Write \(\mathbf1\in\mathbb R^m\) for the all-ones vector and put
\[
 P:=pp^T+qq^T,\qquad R:=I_m-P+qp^T-pq^T,
\]
\[
 B_0:=I_m-2qq^T,\qquad C_0:=I_m-P+pq^T+qp^T.
\]
Thus \(B_0,C_0\) are real symmetric orthogonal matrices, \(C_0^{-1}B_0^{-1}=R\), \(Rp=q\), and \(Rq=-p\), as checked in L66v9.
Take
\[
 B:=\operatorname{diag}(1,B_0),\qquad
 C:=\operatorname{diag}(-5/r,C_0),
\]
\[
 A_0:=2I_m+N^{-1/2}\mathbf1\mathbf1^T,\qquad
 A:=\begin{pmatrix}2r/5&x^T\\x&A_0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad x:=\varepsilon/\sqrt N,
 \tag{Invertible-atom-inputs}
\]
where the coordinates of \(\varepsilon\) are independent fair signs.
Every strict off-diagonal entry of \(A_0\) is \(+1/\sqrt N\), an allowed value of the first input's original Rademacher law.
Fix \(z:=i\).
The actual inverse-product shift and row-exposed matrix are
\[
 C^{-1}B^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(-r/5,R),\qquad
 M:=A-iC^{-1}B^{-1}
   =\begin{pmatrix}\mu&x^T\\x&D\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
 \mu:=\frac{(2+i)r}{5}=\frac r{2-i},\qquad
 D:=A_0-iR,\qquad MBC=ABC-iI_N.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-actual-shift}
\]
Thus \(f=g=0\) and \(\mu\ne0\); the complete \(\mu\det D\) term is retained below.

Put \(c:=n/\sqrt N\) and \(\Delta:=3+4c\).
Since \(\mathbf1=\sqrt n(p+q)\), orthogonal decomposition gives
\[
 D\big|_{\operatorname{span}\{p,q\}}
 =\begin{pmatrix}2+c&c+i\\c-i&2+c\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 D\big|_{\operatorname{span}\{p,q\}^{\perp}}=(2-i)I.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-minor-blocks}
\]
The plane block is Hermitian with eigenvalues \(2+c\pm\sqrt{c^2+1}\).
Its smaller eigenvalue is greater than one because \(c>0\) and \(\sqrt{c^2+1}<c+1\), while the complementary singular values are \(\sqrt5\).
Therefore, writing \(t_1\) for the smallest singular value,
\[
 t_1(D)>1,\qquad \|D^{-1}\|<1,\qquad
 0<\|D^{-1}\|_F<\sqrt m.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-inverse-guard}
\]
The factor bounds are
\[
 \|B\|=\|B^{-1}\|=\|C^{-1}\|=1,\qquad
 \|C\|=5/r\le45/8.
\]
Also \(\|A_0\|=2+m/\sqrt N=O(\sqrt N)\) and \(\|M\|=O(\sqrt N)\), uniformly over the fresh signs.
Thus the polynomial norm and factor-inverse guards hold, and \(t_1(D)>N^{-J}\) for every fixed \(J>0\).

## Complete normalized polynomial, its exact atom and its graph

Write \(h:=(2-i)^{-1}=(2+i)/5\).
Inverting the plane block gives
\[
 D^{-1}\big|_{\operatorname{span}\{p,q\}}
 =\frac1\Delta\begin{pmatrix}2+c&-c-i\\-c+i&2+c\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
 \operatorname{sym}D^{-1}
 =h(I_m-P)+\frac{2+c}{\Delta}P
       -\frac c\Delta(pq^T+qp^T).
 \tag{Invertible-atom-symmetric-inverse}
\]
Symmetrization here uses ordinary transpose.
For the actual sign row, \(\|x\|^2=m/N=r\) surely.
Set \(u:=p^Tx\), \(v:=q^Tx\), and \(\eta:=\det D/|\det D|\).
The complete Schur numerator and its actual normalization satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mu-x^TD^{-1}x
 &=\left(h-\frac{2+c}{\Delta}\right)(u^2+v^2)
       +\frac{2c}{\Delta}uv,\\
 Z(\varepsilon)
 &:=\frac{\det M}{\|\operatorname{adj}D\|_F}\\
 &=\frac{\eta}{\|D^{-1}\|_F}
       \left[\left(h-\frac{2+c}{\Delta}\right)(u^2+v^2)
       +\frac{2c}{\Delta}uv\right].
 \end{split}
 \tag{Invertible-atom-complete-normalization}
\]
Indeed the complementary contribution is \(h(r-u^2-v^2)\), and the actual center \(\mu=hr\) cancels its \(hr\) term exactly.
This uses the fixed norm of the original Rademacher row, not an omitted center or a changed input law.
The normalization follows from \(\operatorname{adj}D=(\det D)D^{-1}\), with every determinant magnitude cancelled.
Since \(u,v\) are real,
\[
 \operatorname{Im}(\mu-x^TD^{-1}x)=\frac{u^2+v^2}{5}.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-imaginary-part}
\]
Hence \(Z=0\) if and only if \(u=v=0\).
The independent group sums each vanish with probability
\[
 b_n:=2^{-n}\binom n{n/2}
      =\sqrt{\frac2{\pi n}}\bigl(1+O(n^{-1})\bigr),
\]
so the exact conditional zero probability is
\[
 b_n^2=\frac2{\pi n}\bigl(1+O(n^{-1})\bigr)\asymp N^{-1}.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-zero-probability}
\]
The binomial asymptotic reuses the precise Stirling statement and applicability inspection retained in [L66v9](Yin_L_F0066_v9.md#fixed-laws-and-background-probability).
The inspected NIST DLMF equations 5.11.1 and 5.11.3 apply at the positive arguments \(n+1\) and \(n/2+1\), and give
\[
 \log b_n=-n\log2+\log\Gamma(n+1)-2\log\Gamma(n/2+1)
          =-\tfrac12\log(\pi n/2)+O(n^{-1}).
\]
Only those formula statements, their sector and the application are reused; no inspection of their original proofs or new external random-matrix input is claimed.
The existing bibliography key is DLM26, with display key DLMF.

A nonzero group sum has modulus at least two because \(n\) is even.
Thus, away from the zero event,
\[
 |Z|\ge\frac4{5nN\sqrt m}.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-nonzero-gap}
\]
This follows from (Invertible-atom-imaginary-part), \(u,v\) being group sums divided by \(\sqrt{nN}\), and \(\|D^{-1}\|_F<\sqrt m\).
For every fixed \(\Theta>5/2\), the conditional probability of \(|Z|\le N^{-\Theta}\) is therefore exactly \(b_n^2\) for sufficiently large sizes.
For every nonnegative window it is at least \(b_n^2\).

The actual graph uses \(Q:=\operatorname{sym}\operatorname{adj}D\).
For distinct coordinates \(j,k\) in the same group,
\[
 (\operatorname{sym}D^{-1})_{jk}
   =\frac1n\left(\frac{2+c}{\Delta}-h\right),\qquad
 \left|\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{sym}D^{-1})_{jk}\right|
   =\frac1{5n}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{|Q_{jk}|}{\|\operatorname{adj}D\|_F}
 =\frac{|(\operatorname{sym}D^{-1})_{jk}|}{\|D^{-1}\|_F}
 \ge\frac1{5n\sqrt m}.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-normalized-edges}
\]
The graph contains two disjoint complete \(n\)-vertex subgraphs and hence a matching of \(n\) edges.
For every fixed \(H>3/2\), it belongs to \(\mathcal H_1(N^{-H},\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor)\) eventually, including the fine thresholds of the existing low-branch assembly.
This bound uses the actual cofactor denominator and is independent of the fresh first row.

The raw quadratic matrix is nevertheless full rank.
On the complementary space \(\operatorname{sym}D^{-1}\) acts by the nonzero scalar \(h\); on the plane its eigenvalues are \(2/\Delta\) and \((2+2c)/\Delta\), also nonzero.
Thus the complete polynomial in unrestricted real coordinates has a rank-\(m\) quadratic part, while its sign-cube values reduce to the displayed two-sum quadratic.
This is an actual-model realization of the [raw versus two-point reduction boundary](Yin_L_F0066_v9.md#invertible-minor-and-sign-boundary).

## A single fixed-law realization and its background probability

Use the Rademacher off-diagonal law for the first input at every size.
For the second and third inputs, reuse the single fixed bounded symmetric variance-one off-diagonal law of [L66v9](Yin_L_F0066_v9.md#fixed-laws-and-background-probability).
For each even \(n\ge4\), writing \(t_n:=2^{-n-20}\), that law has positive mass at zero and mass at least \(t_n/4\) at each nonzero location
\[
 \pm\frac{\sqrt{2n+1}}n,\qquad
 \pm\frac{2\sqrt{2n+1}}n.
\]
It realizes every strict off-diagonal coordinate of the displayed \(B,C\), including their zero first borders.

For all three diagonal families use the following single fixed symmetric law.
For each even \(n\ge4\), with \(N=2n+1\) and \(m=2n\) only as notation for its support values, assign mass \(t_n/12\) to each signed value in
\[
 \pm(2\sqrt N+1),\quad
 \pm\frac{2m}{5\sqrt N},\quad
 \pm\sqrt N,\quad
 \pm(1-2/n)\sqrt N,\quad
 \pm(1-1/n)\sqrt N,\quad
 \pm\frac{5N\sqrt N}{m}.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-fixed-diagonal-law}
\]
Add masses at coincident locations and put the remaining probability at zero.
The total prescribed mass is \(\sum_{n\ge4,\ n\ {\rm even}}t_n<1\).
All support magnitudes are \(O(\sqrt n)\), so exponential weights give finite moments of every order.
The law is centered by symmetry and unbounded, and its support is fixed before varying the matrix dimension.
At each size, sample the three Wigner inputs independently with these laws and with independent upper coordinates within each matrix.

Let \(\Gamma_N\) prescribe \(A_{\widehat1,\widehat1}=A_0\), \(A_{11}=2r/5\), and the full \(B,C\) above.
Every prescribed coordinate lies in its original normalized support.
In particular the raw first-principal diagonal is \(2\sqrt N+1\), the raw first exposed diagonal is \(2m/(5\sqrt N)\), and the raw first diagonal of \(C\) is \(-5N\sqrt N/m\).
The event belongs to \(\mathcal F_1:=\sigma(A_{\widehat1,\widehat1},A_{11},B,C)\) and leaves the first border independent with its original normalized Rademacher law.
It has positive probability and, for fixed constants \(c_0,C_0'>0\),
\[
 e^{-C_0'N^3}\le\mathbb P(\Gamma_N)\le e^{-c_0N^2}.
 \tag{Invertible-atom-background-probability}
\]
For the upper bound, all \(m(m-1)/2\) first-principal off-diagonal signs are prescribed to be \(+1\).
For the lower bound, there are \(O(N^2)\) prescribed independent coordinates, each required atom having mass at least a fixed constant times \(2^{-n-20}\).
These are bounds, not an exact exponential asymptotic.

On \(\Gamma_N\), the actual conditional concentration is
\[
 \kappa_1(\tau):=\mathbb P\{|Z(\varepsilon)|\le\tau\mid\mathcal F_1\}.
\]
For every fixed \(B_*>1\) and every nonnegative window \(\tau_N\),
\[
 \kappa_1(\tau_N)\ge b_n^2>N^{-B_*}
 \tag{Invertible-atom-conditional-concentration}
\]
eventually along the displayed sizes.
For \(\tau_N=N^{-\Theta}\) with \(\Theta>5/2\), equality \(\kappa_1(\tau_N)=b_n^2\) holds eventually.
All the factor-inverse, minor-inverse and high-matching bounds above hold on \(\Gamma_N\).

## Scope and remaining probability obligation

This is in the residual \(t_1(D)>N^{-J}\) branch, with the stronger fixed inverse bound \(\|D^{-1}\|<1\).
Polynomial invertibility, a nonreal parameter, raw full quadratic rank and high matching therefore cannot imply a summable small-ball bound uniformly over all legal finite-variance backgrounds.
Here the original sign law and actual nonzero center create exact cancellation, so raw irreducibility or omission of lower-degree terms does not repair that implication.
The fixed diagonal law is unbounded; the construction does not settle the bounded-diagonal pilot.

The background probability is exponentially small, so the construction remains compatible with the averaged high-concentration target on typical backgrounds.
It supplies neither a macroscopic planar deviation nor a Brown-convergence counterexample.
The next probability gain must still use the distribution of the actual background coefficients and center.
No such typical-background estimate or full F0012 conclusion is established here.
