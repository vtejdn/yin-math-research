## Exact one-row conditioning and scalar Schur parameter

Fix a deterministic candidate \(u\in\mathbb C^n\), \(1/2\le\|u\|\le2\), independently of X after conditioning on \(B,C\).
Choose h as a largest-modulus coordinate, breaking ties deterministically, and write
\[
 u=\binom{\alpha}{w},\qquad |\alpha|\ge(2\sqrt n)^{-1}.
\]
With h first, put \(p:=n-1=N-2\) and write
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}s&y^T\\y&S_0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}0&k^T\\-k&K_0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{SR-original-blocks}
\]
Here \(K_0^T=-K_0\), and
\(y=\xi/\sqrt N+f_h\) has the original iid real raw law plus a deterministic complex shift.
The new exposure sigma-field \(\mathcal H_h\) contains \(B,C\), the original X minor with h deleted and the exposed X diagonal.
It fixes \(S_0,s,K_0,k,f_h\) and leaves the p entries of \(\xi\) independent with their original law.
It is not the earlier \(\mathcal F_i\), which fixes the whole first-input principal block when defining \(\kappa_i\).
The present exposure estimates the distribution of that background block after the inverse-concentration event has been reduced to a deterministic candidate family.

On \(S_0\) invertible, let \(G:=S_0^{-1}\), write \(v=\binom t{v_J}\), and set
\[
 b:=G(-k\alpha+K_0w).
\]
The exact equations \(Sv=Ku\) give
\[
 v_J=b-tGy,\qquad
 t(s-y^TGy)=k^Tw-y^Tb.
 \tag{SR-scalar-Schur-parameter}
\]
If S is also invertible, its scalar Schur complement is nonzero and the second equation determines t.
The retained part of the other coupled equation is
\[
 (Su-Kv)_J=A(t)y+c+tk,
 \qquad
 A(t):=\alpha I_p+tK_0G,\qquad
 c:=S_0w-K_0b.
 \tag{SR-retained-row-equations}
\]
The exposed component is
\[
 (Su-Kv)_h=s\alpha-k^Tb+y^Tw+t\,k^TGy.
 \tag{SR-exposed-equation}
\]
Thus the companion's entire fresh-row dependence is encoded by one complex scalar t and the original vector y.
Neither t nor \(v_J\) is independent of y.
All transposes in these formulas are ordinary algebraic transposes.

For an upper bound one may enlarge the event by allowing any t in its proved deterministic disk and by omitting the scalar relation in (SR-scalar-Schur-parameter) and the exposed constraint.
That is an event inclusion, not conditioning on t or replacing its distribution.
The retained equations still impose the original coupled constraints on all but at most \(C_0\) of the p retained rows.

## A uniform half-rank singular-value bound for every scalar parameter

Fix an arbitrary polynomial failure power q.
The accepted [C188v2 symmetric-shift theorem](../claims/Yin_C_F0188_v2.md#statement), at failure power \(q+2\) for the actual dimension \(N-2\) and unchanged \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization, gives an exponent \(L_0>0\) such that
\[
 s_{\min}(S_0)\ge N^{-L_0}
 \tag{SR-internal-minor-floor}
\]
fails, after a union over all choices of h in the fixed original principal minor, with probability \(O(N^{-q-1})\) on \(\Lambda_N\).
Its proof applies conditionally on \(B,C\), since the symmetric part of their full inverse-product shift is independent of X.
The first-input finite-second-moment norm estimate also gives the common event \(\|S\|\le N^{k_0}\), with probability failure \(O(N^{-q})\), after choosing the fixed \(k_0\) sufficiently large as in L66v12.
In the row calculation only
\[
 \|S_0\|\le N^{k_0},\qquad \|G\|\le N^{L_0}
 \tag{SR-measurable-minor-guards}
\]
is conditioned upon; this event is \(\mathcal H_h\)-measurable.
The larger \(\|S\|\) event is used below only as an intersection in an estimated probability, never as a change of the y law.

For every complex t,
\[
 A(t)=(\alpha S_0+tK_0)G,\qquad
 (\alpha S_0+tK_0)+(\alpha S_0+tK_0)^T=2\alpha S_0.
\]
Let \(r_0:=\lfloor(p+1)/2\rfloor\).
The singular-value sum inequality, together with equality of the singular values of a complex matrix and its transpose, gives
\[
 s_{r_0}(\alpha S_0+tK_0)\ge|\alpha|s_{\min}(S_0),
\]
where singular values are ordered decreasingly.
Indeed \(2r_0-1\le p\), so the singular value of \(2\alpha S_0\) at that index is at least \(2|\alpha|s_{\min}(S_0)\) and at most twice \(s_{r_0}(\alpha S_0+tK_0)\).
Right multiplication by G yields
\[
 s_{r_0}(A(t))
 \ge\frac{|\alpha|s_{\min}(S_0)}{\|S_0\|}
 \ge\lambda_N,\qquad
 \lambda_N:=\tfrac12N^{-L_0-k_0-1/2}.
 \tag{SR-uniform-spectral-mass}
\]
This bound is uniform in t, including values where A(t) is singular.
Deleting at most \(C_0\) rows loses at most \(C_0\) singular values by the rank perturbation inequality.
The retained map therefore has at least \(r_0-C_0\ge p/3\) singular values at least \(\lambda_N\) for sufficiently large N.
No structural or independence assumption on \(K_0G\) is needed for this deterministic fact.

<a id="original-law-projection"></a>
## The finite-second-moment projection estimate used here

The [accepted fixed-cutoff distance proof C194v3](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#linear-codimension) gives an exponential small-ball bound for a real iid vector near an arbitrary complex subspace of linear codimension.
Its displayed threshold is p/2; the following constant change supplies the p/3 case needed above.

Choose a fixed cutoff \(R\ge1\) so that
\(\mathbb P\{|\xi|>R\}\le1/128\) and the variance conditioned on \(|\xi|\le R\) is at least \(1/2\).
The probability of more than \(p/16\) excluded coordinates is \(O(e^{-cp})\) by exponential Markov and independence.
On every remaining coordinate set, projecting a complex subspace of codimension at least p/3 and adjoining the deterministic mean and shift leaves codimension at least
\[
 p/3-p/16-1\ge p/4
\]
eventually.
The squared projected norm of the centered conditional vector has expectation at least p/8.
The [bounded-product convex concentration input](../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v2.md#argument) used by C194v3 puts its median at order \(\sqrt p\) and its lower deviation at \(O(e^{-cp})\), with R fixed.
Thus, for a constant \(c_\xi>0\), uniformly over complex subspaces V of codimension at least p/3 and arbitrary deterministic shifts f,
\[
 \mathbb P\{\operatorname{dist}(\xi+f,V)\le c_\xi\sqrt p\}
 \le C_\xi e^{-c_\xi p}.
 \tag{SR-original-law-projection}
\]
The fixed cutoff is a proof device integrated back to the original law; no cutoff is imposed on the row's conditional law in (SR-original-blocks).
Only the existing accepted concentration input is reused, with this explicit codimension adjustment.
No new external source or source-proof review is claimed.

## A polynomial scalar net gives an exponential bound for a fixed u

Retain the L66v12 coupled tolerance
\[
 \delta_N:=N^{-a+Q_0},\qquad Q_0:=2k_0+L,
\]
where L is the full S floor exponent and a is the adjustable fixed precision.
The target coupled event includes
\[
 \|v\|\le2N^{L+k_0};
\]
therefore its scalar t lies in the deterministic disk of radius \(R_N:=2N^{L+k_0}\).
This disk does not depend on the fresh y.

On the event \(\|S\|\le N^{k_0}\), one has \(\|y\|\le N^{k_0}\).
Together with (SR-measurable-minor-guards) and \(\|K\|\le N^{k_0}\), this gives
\[
 \|K_0Gy+k\|\le2N^{2k_0+L_0}.
\]
Choose an integer \(C_t>2k_0+L_0+3\) and a deterministic complex lattice net of the disk at mesh \(N^{-a-C_t}\).
It has at most \(N^{C_a}\) points, where \(C_a=O(a+L+k_0+C_t+1)\).
Replacing an actual t by a net point changes the entire vector in (SR-retained-row-equations) by at most \(N^{-a-1}\) eventually.
Hence the original coordinatewise bound \(\delta_N\) outside E implies, for one net point and the same omitted rows, a Euclidean residual at most \(2\sqrt p\,\delta_N\).

For each fixed net point, the retained map has at least p/3 singular values at least \(\lambda_N\).
Projecting onto those singular directions converts the residual event into
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(\xi+f',V)
 \le\frac{2\sqrt{Np}\,\delta_N}{\lambda_N},
 \qquad \operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb C}V\ge p/3,
 \tag{SR-projected-radius}
\]
for a deterministic complex shift \(f'\) and subspace V measurable under \(\mathcal H_h\).
If the fixed precision satisfies
\[
 a>Q_0+L_0+k_0+3,
 \tag{SR-sufficient-precision}
\]
the right-hand side is \(o(\sqrt p)\).
Equation (SR-original-law-projection) therefore applies uniformly in the net point, the conditioned minor, the coefficient vector u and the deterministic inverse-product shift.
There are at most \(N^{C_0}\) choices of omitted retained rows.
After these polynomial unions,
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
 \begin{array}{l}
 \|S\|\le N^{k_0},\
 \exists\,v,\ \|v\|\le2N^{L+k_0},\ Sv=Ku,\\
 |(Su-Kv)_j|\le\delta_N\ \text{outside some }|E|\le C_0
 \end{array}
 \,\middle|\,\mathcal H_h\right\}
 \le N^{C_a+C_0}C_\xi e^{-c_\xi p}
 \tag{SR-fixed-candidate-bound}
\]
on the measurable minor guards.
The event may be further restricted to the full D inverse and S inverse guards without weakening this upper bound.
No conditioning on the scalar Schur parameter or the full-row norm has occurred.
The omitted scalar and exposed equations only enlarge the estimated event, so the estimate remains valid for the exact companion \(v=S^{-1}Ku\).

This bound is uniform over every deterministic u in the stated norm range.
It is not yet a bound for a u selected from the observed X.
The latter selection must be handled by an actual family count.

## Exact entropy obstruction and consequence for the averaged target

For any deterministic family \(\mathcal V_N\), independent of X conditional on \(B,C\), with
\[
 |\mathcal V_N|=\exp(o(N)),
\]
the last estimate, the common internal-minor guards and the original norm guard imply
\[
 \mathbb P\{\text{some }u\in\mathcal V_N
        \text{ satisfies the guarded coupled event}\mid B,C\}
 \le O(N^{-q})+e^{-cN}
 \tag{SR-subexponential-family-bound}
\]
uniformly on \(\Lambda_N\).
The largest-coordinate choice h is deterministic for each candidate, and the common internal-minor floor was obtained before counting candidates.
Thus its \(O(N^{-q})\) failure is paid once, not once per candidate.
One may also allow families of size \(e^{c'N}\) for a sufficiently small fixed \(c'>0\).

The inverse-theorem class supplied by [L66v12](Yin_L_F0066_v12.md#polynomial-row-combination-floor) only has the available count
\[
 |\mathcal U_N(a,C_0)|
 \le N^{C_*N+O_a(N^\varepsilon)+O_a(1)},
 \tag{SR-existing-entropy}
\]
where \(C_*\) is fixed before a.
Combining this bound directly with (SR-fixed-candidate-bound) does not tend to zero: the available logarithmic counting bound has leading term \(C_*N\log N\), whereas the probability gain established here is only \(cN\).
Refining the scalar net or increasing a does not change that comparison.
In particular an atomic original row law has no uniform radius-dependent improvement supplied by the projection estimate.
Repeating the fixed-u argument at several rows does not justify multiplying the bounds, since each conditioning contains the other rows and the same coupled event is being reused.
A valid profile argument would need an additional conditional multiplication or entropy reduction, not a fresh independent copy of v.

If an X-independent family of the admissible size could cover the inverse-theorem output outside an \(O(N^{-q})\) background exception, (SR-subexponential-family-bound) would feed directly into the [L66v12 mean bridge](Yin_L_F0066_v12.md#sufficient-mean-probability-obligation).
Choosing \(q>B_*+1\), conditioning on \(B,C\), and averaging over the outer index i would then yield the desired \(O(N^{-B_*})\) upper tail.
No such covering statement is available here; the current bound on the GAP class is (SR-existing-entropy).
