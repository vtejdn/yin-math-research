# A joint long-row profile compresses both columns, but the unrestricted companion net breaks the parameter order

This ordinary research for Yin on 2026-11-06 tests the [actual coupled equations](Yin_L_F0066_v12.md#coupled-near-kernel-interface) directly on pairs \((u,v)\), without conditioning on \(Yt\) or replacing the companion by independent randomness.
The [Ngu12a real-vector inverse theorem](../audits/literature/Ngu12a/Yin_U001.md#real-scalar-geometric-inverse) does apply to their joint coefficients in \(\mathbb C^2=\mathbb R^4\).
The [C194v1 long-tail argument](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v1.md#structured-vector-counting) can then compress both columns on a specified high-product class, and its degenerate branch has a joint-column version.
The first uncovered part is the low joint-product class with an initially unrestricted continuous v.
A direct fine net for that part cannot satisfy the accepted count's scale choices, even when the original equation precision and the net mesh are named independently.
This is a limitation of the tested adaptation, not a proof that every joint-profile method fails or a counterexample to F0012.
The fixed account retains ordinary exploration and comparison with authoring self-checks; it does not claim independent review, a new accepted Claim or a complete background-probability theorem.

## Actual equations, normalization and legal triangular exposure

Keep the real fixed-law three-factor pilot, \(n=N-1\),
\[
 X=(W_1)_{\widehat i,\widehat i},\quad
 F=-z(C^{-1}B^{-1})_{\widehat i,\widehat i},\quad
 S=X+\operatorname{sym}F,\quad K=\operatorname{skew}F.
\]
F is the principal block of the full inverse product.
Condition on actual B,C with \(\Lambda_N\) retained.
The source reduction gives a structured u with \(1/2\le\|u\|\le2\), an omission set E of size at most \(C_0\), and
\[
 v=S^{-1}Ku,\qquad \|v\|\le2N^{L+k_0},
 \qquad Sv-Ku=0\quad\hbox{on every row},
\]
\[
 |(Su-Kv)_i|\le N^{-a_0+Q_0}\quad(i\notin E),
 \qquad Q_0=2k_0+L.
 \tag{TC-actual-equations}
\]
The S inverse guard is a restriction on the matrix event, not a conditioning on its rows.

Pass to raw scaling
\[
 R:=\sqrt N X,\qquad
 \mathsf F:=\sqrt N\,\operatorname{sym}F,\qquad
 \mathsf K:=\sqrt N K.
\]
Then the actual paired row residual is
\[
 \mathcal L_i(u,v)
 :=\left(
 (Ru+\mathsf Fu-\mathsf Kv)_i,\,
 (Rv+\mathsf Fv-\mathsf Ku)_i
 \right)\in\mathbb C^2.
 \tag{TC-raw-residual}
\]
Its second component is zero on all rows for the original pair.
Dropping the paired constraint on E enlarges the counted event by omitting only \(C_0\) rows; the exact original companion relation is not changed.
For fixed u,v, the two deterministic shift terms in (TC-raw-residual) remain their actual values.
They enter row small-ball bounds as fixed centers, not newly chosen centers defining a different feasible event.

For any fixed failure power \(q>B_*+1\), reuse the [C194v1 raw truncation](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v1.md#truncation) at a sufficiently large exponent \(\kappa\).
The probability of changing any original first-input entry is \(O(N^{-q})\).
Conditional on the independent truncations, the strict upper entries have the original conditional law \(\xi_n\), are iid, and satisfy the uniform source anti-concentration condition.
No centering or higher moment is added.
Condition on the independent diagonal values.
Choose a fixed integer exponent \(k\ge1\) so that entries of R and of both deterministic raw shifts have modulus at most \(n^k\) on these events.
Choose a fixed integer \(m\) with \(\|v\|\le n^m\).
These choices precede every fine precision below.
All bounds below are uniform in the conditioned diagonal and actual deterministic shifts in this range.

For a deterministic candidate pair write
\[
 a_j:=(u_j,v_j)\in\mathbb C^2,\qquad
 \rho_i(\mathbf a,t)
 :=\sup_{z\in\mathbb C^2}
    \mathbb P\left\{\left\|\sum_{j=i}^n a_j\xi_{n,j}-z\right\|\le t\right\}.
 \tag{TC-joint-tail-profile}
\]
There is one original real scalar \(\xi_{n,j}\) multiplying each coefficient vector.
This is a real-vector linear form in ambient dimension four, not two independently sampled scalar rows and not a theorem for correlated complex scalar inputs.
The tail concentrations are nondecreasing in i.

Whenever a tail has norm
\(\sigma=(\sum_{j=i}^n(|u_j|^2+|v_j|^2))^{1/2}>0\), apply the source to \(a_j/\sigma\) at radius \(t/\sigma\).
Its concentration is exactly \(\rho_i(\mathbf a,t)\).
The norm bound is \(\sigma\le2+n^m\); the zero tail has concentration one.
The source normalization therefore costs only fixed polynomial exponents and does not replace v by a bounded unit vector while leaving u's scale unchanged.
In the rational-generator counting clause used in C194v1, rescaling a generator of form \((t/\sigma)p_{ij}/p\) returns \(tp_{ij}/p\).
The variable norm cancels.
At polynomial t, the four real coordinates of the rescaled generators have polynomial height, with constants allowed to depend on the fixed precision and on m.
Thus the same finite descriptor argument applies in dimension four.

Expose the symmetric original upper entries in increasing row order after a deterministic simultaneous permutation.
At row i the remaining random sum is
\(\sum_{j=i+1}^n a_j\xi_{n,ij}\).
Every other contribution, including both actual shifts, is fixed by that exposure.
Covering the joint support ball of polynomial radius in \(\mathbb R^4\) gives, for \(t\ge n^{-s}\),
\[
 \rho_i(\mathbf a,t)\ge n^{-D_s},
 \qquad D_s:=4(s+k+m+3)+1.
\]
The same holds for the bounded rounded pairs below.
Consequently the legal exposure bound is
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\mathcal L_i(u,v)\|\le t\text{ on prescribed }n-C_0
                    \text{ rows}\}
 \le n^{D_s(C_0+1)}\prod_{i=1}^n\rho_i(\mathbf a,t).
 \tag{TC-triangular-product}
\]
The missing diagonal term shifts the product from \(\rho_1\) to \(\rho_2\), and the displayed polynomial factor pays for that one factor and the bounded omitted rows.
This is one ordered exposure of the original symmetric matrix.
It never multiplies mutually conditioned whole-row events.

Place a largest u-coordinate last, with
\[
 |u_n|\ge(2\sqrt n)^{-1}.
\]
Every tail then contains it.
Projection onto that complex coordinate direction and the source anti-concentration condition give a fixed \(p_0<1\) for every tail at \(t\le c/\sqrt n\).
In particular the elementary fixed-pair bound is \(n^{O_s(1)}p_0^{n-C_0-1}\).
It remains essential to count an actual deterministic family; the existence of the random companion is not covered by this bound alone.

<a id="joint-degenerate-count"></a>
## The joint degenerate class is compressible without a fine net for v

Fix \(\varepsilon=1/100\), let
\[
 q_n=\lceil n^{1-\varepsilon}\rceil,\qquad
 \theta=\varepsilon/10,\qquad t_*=n^{-k-5}.
\]
Call a pair degenerate if every \(q_n\)-coordinate subset has joint concentration at least
\(q_n^{-1/2+\theta}\) at radius \(t_*\).
For each such subset, normalize its coefficient vectors as above and use the real-four-dimensional inverse theorem with exception count \(\lfloor q_n/2\rfloor\).
Its exponent B can be fixed, for example B=1, independently of the eventual fine precision.
The allowed source exception exponent can also be fixed, since \(\lfloor q_n/2\rfloor\) lies between \(q_n^\eta\) and \(q_n\) for any fixed \(0<\eta<1\) and large n.
The progression-size bound becomes
\[
 \max\{Cq_n^{-\theta},1\}=1
\]
eventually.
A proper symmetric progression of size one is \(\{0\}\).
Thus at least half of every such subset has \(\|a_j\|\le t_*\).
It follows that at most \(\lfloor q_n/2\rfloor\) coordinates of the full pair have norm greater than \(t_*\).

Round those coordinates to an \(\mathbb R^4\) lattice of mesh \(t_*\), and set every other coordinate to zero.
The possible rounded pairs number at most
\[
 \exp(O_{k,m}(q_n\log n))=\exp(o(n)).
 \tag{TC-degenerate-count}
\]
This covers continuous v directly; no fine grid with \(n^{O(a_0n)}\) cost is used.
The coordinatewise rounding error is \(O(t_*)\) in both columns.
Because (TC-raw-residual) is linear in both columns with entry bounds \(n^k\), the row tolerance increases by at most \(O(n^{k+1}t_*)=O(n^{-4})\).
The largest u-coordinate survives.
The fixed-pair triangular bound and (TC-degenerate-count) therefore give \(e^{-cn}\) for existence of such a degenerate feasible pair, whenever the original raw row tolerance is smaller than \(n^{-4}\).
The omission choices are only polynomially many.
This is a uniform upper bound for a genuine subclass of the actual event, with the original companion equation still supplying each feasible pair.

<a id="high-joint-product-count"></a>
## A fixed high joint-product class also compresses both columns

For a nondegenerate pair select a \(q_n\)-subset with joint concentration below \(q_n^{-1/2+\theta}\), add a largest u-coordinate if needed, and put this reservoir last.
Its size is \(q_0\in\{q_n,q_n+1\}\).
The reservoir and prescribed permutation choices cost \(\exp(O(q_n\log n))=\exp(o(n))\).
Let \(p=n-q_0\).
For each fixed such ordering, every tail starting at \(i\le p\) contains the reservoir, so
\[
 \rho_i(\mathbf a,t)\le n^{-b_0},\qquad
 b_0=1/2-3\varepsilon/4,
 \qquad t\le t_*.
 \tag{TC-long-tail-upper}
\]
The ordering can change the product; no earlier product classification is transported across a permutation.

Fix a constant \(C_1>0\) before choosing the integer fine profile precision s.
The subclass considered here satisfies, in the ordering actually used,
\[
 \pi_{\beta_0}(\mathbf a):=\prod_{i=1}^p\rho_i(\mathbf a,\beta_0)
       \ge n^{-C_1n},\qquad \beta_0=n^{-s}.
 \tag{TC-high-product-class}
\]
Take
\[
 R_n=n^{k+3},\qquad t_\ell=R_n^\ell\beta_0,\qquad
 L_1=\lceil C_1/\varepsilon\rceil+2.
\]
Choose s after \(C_1,k,m,\varepsilon\), large enough that
\[
 s\ge(k+3)(L_1+1)+k+5,
 \quad\hbox{so}\quad t_{L_1+1}\le t_*.
 \tag{TC-available-scale-range}
\]
At some \(0\le\ell\le L_1\),
\[
 \pi_{t_{\ell+1}}(\mathbf a)
       \le n^{\varepsilon n}\pi_{t_\ell}(\mathbf a),
 \tag{TC-stable-scale}
\]
since otherwise iteration from (TC-high-product-class) would exceed one.

Partition the possible \(\rho_i(\mathbf a,t_\ell)\) into bins
\([r_h,n^\varepsilon r_h)\) between \(n^{-D_s}\) and \(n^{-b_0}\).
There are \(H=O_{s,k,m}(1)\) bins and at most \((n+1)^H\) monotone profiles.
At the first index of each block apply the same real-four-dimensional inverse theorem to the whole corresponding tail with exception count
\[
 n'=\lfloor n^{1-2\varepsilon}\rfloor.
\]
Each tail has length at least \(q_n\), so this is within the source's permitted range.
Its polynomial concentration exponent is fixed after s is fixed.
The normalized-tail and rational-generator calculation above applies, including when v has polynomial norm.
Both coordinates of all but \(n'\) pairs in the block are approximated together within \(t_\ell\) by a single proper symmetric progression \(Q_h\subset\mathbb R^4\), with
\[
 |Q_h|\le C r_h^{-1}n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}.
 \tag{TC-joint-progression-size}
\]
The maximum with one is harmless by (TC-long-tail-upper).
The inverse theorem therefore does compress v as well as u; it is not merely a theorem for the first column.

The rank and generator choices contribute only a polynomial number of descriptors for fixed s.
Round the remaining \(Hn'+q_0=o(n)\) coordinate pairs on a four-real-coordinate \(t_\ell\) lattice.
Their positions and values cost \(\exp(o(n))\), since their norm is polynomially bounded.
The number of rounded pairs in a fixed profile is at most
\[
 \exp(O_s(n)+o(n))\,
 n^{-(1/2-\varepsilon)p}\prod_h r_h^{-m_h}.
 \tag{TC-joint-profile-count}
\]
This construction is finite even if the initial pair class is continuous.
For each rounded image choose one original witness from the deterministic profile class, before imposing any random row event.
Neither that witness nor the descriptor choice is selected from an observed R.

A feasible original pair gives row tolerance at most
\(t'=\beta_0+Cn^{k+1}t_\ell\) for its rounded image.
Its actual deterministic shift terms change by the same polynomially controlled error.
For every tail the rounded coefficient sum differs from its witness by at most \(Cn^{k+1}t_\ell\), hence
\[
 \rho_i(\mathbf a',t')\le\rho_i(\mathbf a,t_{\ell+1}).
\]
Use (TC-triangular-product), discard the last \(q_0\) factors, then use (TC-stable-scale) and the bin upper endpoints.
The probability of a rounded feasible pair is at most
\[
 n^{O_s(1)+2\varepsilon n}\prod_h r_h^{m_h}.
 \tag{TC-joint-profile-probability}
\]
Multiplying (TC-joint-profile-count) and (TC-joint-profile-probability) cancels the concentration factors and gives
\[
 \exp(O_s(n)+o(n))\,
 n^{-(1/2-\varepsilon)p+2\varepsilon n}\le n^{-n/3}
\]
for large n.
The bounded scale choices, polynomial profiles and \(\exp(o(n))\) reservoir choices preserve a bound \(n^{-cn}\).
The original raw residual must be at most \(\beta_0\), which is achieved by choosing \(a_0\) sufficiently large after s.
No initial unrestricted fine net for v is used in this high-product subclass.

These two subclass estimates did not need u's original GAP restriction beyond its norm and large coordinate.
That restriction can be retained, but it is needed to address the remaining low-product class.
The result does not say that the inverse-quadratic output lies in either controlled subclass.

<a id="two-precision-grid-obstruction"></a>
## The first uncovered class and two genuinely distinct precision choices

The [L66v12 inverse reduction](Yin_L_F0066_v12.md#polynomial-row-combination-floor) supplies u in a deterministic structured family of size
\[
 n^{C_*n+o_{a_0}(n)},\qquad C_*\ \hbox{independent of }a_0.
\]
It supplies no lower bound such as (TC-high-product-class) for the pair \((u,S^{-1}Ku)\).
Joint concentration is at most its first marginal concentration; the marginal structure of u gives no reverse comparison.
Thus one must still control feasible structured-u pairs for which the joint product is smaller than \(n^{-C_1n}\) in the selected ordering.

To test the direct coarse-count union without conflating scales, let the integer A denote the original raw row precision, so the tolerance is at most \(n^{-A}\), and let the integer b denote a separate uniform grid exponent for v, with mesh \(n^{-b}\).
The grid covers \(\|v\|\le n^m\) and has at most
\[
 n^{2(b+m)n+O(n)}
 \tag{TC-free-companion-grid}
\]
points.
The coefficient of b in this logarithmic entropy is intrinsic to a plain mesh of an unrestricted complex n-ball: a coordinate cube of side comparable to \(n^{m-1/2}\) lies in that ball and already has \(n^{(2b+O_m(1))n}\) such cells.
This statement concerns the unrestricted norm ball, not the actual companion set determined by \(Sv=Ku\).

The entry bounds only give a uniform rounded row error \(O(n^{k+1-b})\).
Thus this tested rounding scheme supplies a profile row precision s when, for example,
\[
 s\le A-2,\qquad s\le b-k-3.
 \tag{TC-effective-grid-precision}
\]
Taking A arbitrarily large does not recover the original fine row window after a coarser v grid.
For this attempted full split, classify the rounded grid pair at its effective row window; a high- or low-product label of an unrounded pair is not assumed to survive the rounding.
At this effective precision, multiplying the original u count by (TC-free-companion-grid) and the low-product probability bound requires
\[
 C_1>C_*+2(b+m)+10
 \tag{TC-low-product-union-requirement}
\]
to make this particular crude union decay.

But the high-product branch beginning at the same effective precision needs (TC-available-scale-range):
\[
 s\ge(k+3)(\lceil C_1/\varepsilon\rceil+3)+k+5.
\]
Together with \(s\le b-k-3\) and (TC-low-product-union-requirement), these inequalities cannot hold for \(k\ge1\) and \(\varepsilon=1/100\).
The right side grows by more than \(2(k+3)b/\varepsilon\), while the available s is at most b minus a fixed constant.
This is the failure of the tested unrestricted-grid plus accepted multiscale count.
It persists with independent original precision A and grid precision b, so merely renaming a single precision does not fix it.

The available uniform support-cover lower bound
\(\rho_i\ge n^{-D_s}\), with \(D_s=4(s+k+m+3)+1\), does not replace a fixed \(C_1\) independent of s.
Starting the stable-scale argument from that bound asks for a number of polynomial scale enlargements proportional to s with a larger constant than the interval below \(t_*\) supplies.
No stable scale with the needed gain is obtained from that estimate alone.

This comparison does not rule out a multilevel covering that uses the equations between the coarse and fine stages.
In particular \(Sv=Ku\) determines v uniquely on the S floor, but through the same random matrix whose remaining rows would need exposure.
Fixing a suitable subset of original rows first might reduce the admissible v entropy before a finer count.
No such conditioning scheme has been established or excluded here.
It would have to retain the remaining original upper-coordinate independence and use the exact equation, rather than regard the unrestricted norm-ball grid as the actual companion set.

<a id="mean-target-and-structure-boundary"></a>
## Consequence for the actual mean target and the stopping point

The high-product and degenerate estimates apply uniformly in the actual deterministic inverse-product shift, and the original truncation and guard failures cost \(O(N^{-q})\).
Their union therefore controls the contribution of backgrounds whose inverse-theorem output has a feasible pair in one of those subclasses.
For a fixed \(C_1\), choose the source and guard constants, then s as above, then \(a_0\), and finally the numerator precision \(\Theta\) required by the [L66v12 mean bridge](Yin_L_F0066_v12.md#sufficient-mean-probability-obligation).
With \(q>B_*+1\), their averaged contribution is \(O(N^{-q})+e^{-cN}\).
High matching and the remaining D floor may be kept as further event restrictions.
No extra Markov factor is needed for this background-event inclusion.

The unresolved part is the low joint-product feasible pair with structured u and the exact companion v.
The current source supplies the four-dimensional joint inverse theorem; an unverified extension of that theorem is not the first missing input.
The first missing mechanism is a probability count for this low-product part that avoids the unrestricted \(n^{2bn}\) companion mesh, or a justified reduction of that mesh using actual equations and original-row exposure.
The [random-graph tube formulation](Yin_L_F0066_v15.md#averaged-tube-and-mean-bridge) is another exact description of a remaining probability obligation, not an estimate supplied by this trial.

<a id="actual-unstructured-feasible-pair"></a>
The role of u's structure cannot be dropped altogether.
On every actual background where D and S are invertible, \(\mathcal T=DS^{-1}D^T\) is invertible.
For a coordinate vector \(e_h\), take
\[
 u=\frac{\mathcal T^{-1}e_h}{\|\mathcal T^{-1}e_h\|},
 \qquad v=S^{-1}Ku.
\]
Then \(\|u\|=1\), v has the same polynomial bound on the S and K guards, \(Sv-Ku=0\), and \(Su-Kv\) is supported on the single row h.
One omitted row is allowed in the current inverse-theorem interface.
This is an exact construction within the actual algebraic model, but it supplies no GAP structure for u.
It explains why an argument that silently discards that structure cannot make the whole feasible-pair event rare.
It does not refute the structured-u event, the averaged high-concentration target or F0012.

The useful new evidence is the valid simultaneous long-profile compression and its two controlled subclasses, together with the explicit incompatible precision requirements for the plain companion net.
Further work is warranted only with a mechanism for the remaining structured-u/low-joint-product class; increasing precision inside the same unrestricted grid argument is not progress.
This record is ordinary exploration and self-checking, with no independent review, accepted Claim or complete probability conclusion.
