# The one-sided adaptive fiber for AB + 2BA is always feasible

This bounded ordinary proof-development test for Yin, fixed at 2026-11-06T11:34:48Z UTC, tests the [two-sided exposure proposal](../drafts/Yin_D_F0011.md#two-sided-linear-exposure-profile-test) on the original polynomial \(P(x,y)=xy+2yx\).
It addresses the missing adaptive-near-kernel-to-predetermined-family step by trying an all-vector count on the exact outside fiber.
The result is a deterministic rejection of that particular one-sided-rectangle relaxation: its existence event has conditional probability one at tolerance zero.
This is ordinary authoring and mathematical self-checking, not an independent project review, a new accepted Claim, or a counterexample to F12 or F14.
The separate joint-image calculation below preserves what a full two-sided continuation would still have to control.

For each \(N\), let \(A=W_1^{(N)}\) and \(B=W_2^{(N)}\) be independent real symmetric Wigner matrices in the [unchanged F12 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_F0012_v3.md#contract).
Their respective strict upper entries are independent copies of \(\xi_A/\sqrt N,\xi_B/\sqrt N\), where the fixed real variables \(\xi_A,\xi_B\) each have mean zero and variance one.
Their diagonal entries have independent fixed centered real finite-second-moment laws divided by \(\sqrt N\), independent of the strict upper entries; the lower entries mirror the upper entries.
The two off-diagonal laws may differ, and no density, non-atomicity or higher moment is assumed.
Couplings across sizes may be arbitrary; every rejection below already holds conditionally at each sufficiently large single size.
Fix any \(z\in\mathbb C\), including zero and the real axis, and consider the actual equations
\[
 Bu=v,\qquad Au=t,\qquad Av+2Bt-zu\approx0.
 \tag{TS-original-relations}
\]
The only reused non-elementary estimate is the accepted original-law rectangular distance input identified below.
No new literature result or stochastic replacement model is used.

<a id="two-sided-fiber-and-law"></a>
## The full outside information leaves at least two blocks of vector freedom

Put \(|R|=|T|=m=\lfloor N/8\rfloor\), \(I=R\cup T\), \(r=|I|=2m\), and \(J=I^c\), of size \(p=N-r\).
Condition first on all of \(B\), \(A_{JJ}\), and \(A_{JI}\).
The original symmetric block \(A_{II}\) remains independent of this sigma-field; its upper coordinates retain their original laws and their original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
Retain the common event \(s_{\min}(B_{JI})\ge N^{-1}\), whose complement has probability at most \(Ce^{-cN}\).
To justify its use at the original law, the columns of \(B_{JI}\) are independent because they cross the disjoint sets \(J,I\).
Conditional on the other columns, the remaining complex codimension is at least \(p-r+1=N-2r+1\ge p/2\).
The accepted [C194v3 fixed-cutoff distance estimate](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v3.md#linear-codimension), applied to the raw column with law \(\xi_B\), gives constant-order normalized column distance outside \(Ce^{-cN}\).
A union over the \(r\) columns preserves an exponential failure bound.
The column-distance inverse-Gram identity then gives \(s_{\min}(B_{JI})\ge c/\sqrt r\ge N^{-1}\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
No Gaussian invariance, bounded density or higher moment enters this rectangular guard.
Let
\[
 \Pi=I_J-B_{JI}B_{JI}^{\dagger},\qquad
 h(u)=zu_J-(ABu)_J-2B_{JJ}(Au)_J.
\]
Here the two \(J\)-row expressions use only the exposed rows of \(A\), so \(h:\mathbb C^N\to\mathbb C^J\) is a fixed linear map under this conditioning.
Define
\[
 V=\ker(\Pi h),\qquad
 t_J(u)=(Au)_J,\qquad
 t_I^*(u)=\tfrac12 B_{JI}^{\dagger}h(u),\qquad v(u)=Bu.
 \tag{TS-outside-fiber}
\]
Since \(B_{JI}\) has full column rank \(r\), \(\operatorname{rank}\Pi=p-r=N-2r\).
Therefore
\[
 \dim_{\mathbb C}V\ge2r.
 \tag{TS-fiber-dimension}
\]
For every \(u\in V\), the vectors \(v(u),t^*(u)\) satisfy exactly
\[
 Bu=v,\qquad (Au-t^*)_J=0,\qquad
 (Av+2Bt^*-zu)_J=0.
\]
The outside consistency condition has been retained, not discarded after applying the pseudoinverse.
If \(z\) is real, all maps are real and the same argument gives a real solution space of dimension at least \(2r\).
For an actual unit vector with \(e=(AB+2BA-zI)u\) and \(\|e\|\le\varepsilon\), put \(t=Au\).
The exact outside identity is \(h(u)=2B_{JI}t_I-e_J\), and hence
\[
 \Pi h(u)=-\Pi e_J,\qquad
 t_I^*(u)=t_I-\tfrac12 B_{JI}^{\dagger}e_J,\qquad
 \|t_I-t_I^*(u)\|\le\tfrac12N\varepsilon.
 \tag{TS-approximate-reconstruction}
\]
The full remaining necessary equations are
\[
 (Au-t^*)_I\approx0,\qquad
 (Av+2Bt^*-zu)_I\approx0,
\]
with tolerances at most \(C N^{\gamma+1}\varepsilon\) on \(\|B\|\le N^\gamma\).
Passing from normalized to raw row entries costs another factor \(\sqrt N\).
Thus an actual approximate near-kernel vector only supplies \(\|\Pi h(u)\|\le\varepsilon\), not a quantitative distance to \(V\).
No polynomial lower bound for the nonzero singular values of \(\Pi h\) is supplied here.
The corresponding approximate outside tube contains \(V\) exactly, so the obstruction below already holds on a subset of that tube and does not rely on an unjustified projection onto its exact kernel.

These dimensions are not caused by removing polynomial companion guards.
Write \(A^0\) for \(A\) with its entire \(I\)-by-\(I\) block set to zero.
The conditions \(\|A^0\|\le N^K\) and \(\|B\|\le N^\gamma\) are measurable at this stage and have arbitrary prescribed polynomial failure probabilities after choosing \(K,\gamma\).
Indeed \(\mathbb E\|A^0\|_F^2+\mathbb E\|B\|_F^2\le C N\), so the two failure probabilities are bounded by \(C N^{1-2K}+C N^{1-2\gamma}\).
For any prescribed \(q\), choose fixed \(K,\gamma\ge1\) with \(2K-1,2\gamma-1\ge q\), before choosing the fine approximation exponent.
For every unit \(u\), the formulas above give
\[
 \|v(u)\|\le N^\gamma,\qquad
 \|t^*(u)\|\le C_zN^{K+\gamma+1}.
\]
Thus all the fake directions constructed below have polynomially bounded companions on these common outside guards.

<a id="two-sided-relaxed-event-certain"></a>
## One retained side admits adaptive exact solutions for every rectangle realization

Next expose \(A_{RR}\) and \(A_{TT}\), including their diagonals.
The only unexposed part of \(A_{II}\) is the original rectangle \(C=A_{TR}\), with independent original real entries divided by \(\sqrt N\); \(A_{RT}=C^T\).
The subspace \(V\) and the maps \(v,t^*\) remain fixed before \(C\) is sampled.

For each realized \(C\), impose only the two \(T\)-side row families that supplied the independent-row product in the previous fixed-family argument:
\[
 (Au-t^*(u))_T=0,\qquad
 (Av(u)+2Bt^*(u)-zu)_T=0.
 \tag{TS-one-sided-relaxation}
\]
For this realized \(C\), these are \(2m=r\) complex linear equations in \(u\in V\).
Hence their common kernel inside \(V\) has dimension at least
\[
 \dim_{\mathbb C}V-2m\ge2r-r=r.
 \tag{TS-surviving-adaptive-dimension}
\]
There is a unit solution for every possible realization of the original rectangle, with all the polynomial companion bounds above.
Consequently the event asserting existence of an adaptive unit solution of the complete outside equations and (TS-one-sided-relaxation) has conditional probability exactly one.
This remains true at tolerance zero and for every finite-second-moment input law.
For real \(z\) a real unit solution exists by the identical real dimension argument.

The discarded \(R\)-side constraints are explicitly
\[
 C^T u_T+A_{RR}u_R+A_{RJ}u_J-t_R^*(u)=0,
\]
\[
 C^T v_T+A_{RR}v_R+A_{RJ}v_J
       +2(Bt^*(u))_R-zu_R=0.
 \tag{TS-discarded-R-side}
\]
Together with the retained equations these would imply the actual exact kernel relation; the dimension argument does not impose them.
The vectors produced above need not satisfy (TS-discarded-R-side) and need not be near-kernel vectors of \(AB+2BA-zI\).
For every positive tolerance, the relaxed event with \(\|\Pi h(u)\|\le\varepsilon\) and the necessary \(T\)-side row tolerances contains these exact fake solutions, so it also has conditional probability one.
That distinction is precisely the failure of this implementation.
A prescribed finite family cannot adapt to the realized rectangle, so its valid earlier low-concentration bound is unaffected.
But replacing that family by all vectors in the outside fiber creates an identically feasible relaxation.
Any exhaustive split of this relaxed event into poor and rich profiles must therefore leave substantial probability in at least one class.

<a id="two-sided-precision-entropy"></a>
## Precision and profile thresholds do not repair the same relaxation

There is also an explicit entropy budget.
Let the desired residual scale be \(\varepsilon=N^{-a}\), with all norm and transfer exponents chosen before the fine precision \(a\).
On the polynomial guards a covering mesh of order \(N^{-a-K_1}\), for a fixed \(K_1\), is sufficient to preserve the necessary row tolerances.
Any full \(A\)-norm guard used for this error comparison is an event restriction only: pay its \(O(N^{-q})\) complement from the original Frobenius moment, then drop it before applying a row-probability bound.
It is not an additional conditional law on \(C\), and \(V\) and its chosen net are fixed before \(C\) is sampled.
The unit directions of a complex \(d_V\)-dimensional subspace, even after quotienting the irrelevant common phase, have metric dimension \(2d_V-2\ge4r-2\).
For real vectors the corresponding dimension is at least \(2r-1\).
Ordinary volumetric covers therefore carry precision terms at least
\[
 a(4r-2)\log N\quad\text{or}\quad a(2r-1)\log N
\]
in their logarithmic size, up to coarse-exponent terms.

Even granting the favorable hypothetical small-ball factor \((C N^{K_2}\varepsilon)^4\) for each complex paired row, the \(m\) independent \(T\)-rows contribute only \(4am=2ar\) powers of \(N^{-1}\) coming from precision.
The resulting free-net expression has the positive precision loss
\[
 a(2r-2)\log N+O_{\rm coarse}(N\log N).
 \tag{TS-complex-net-loss}
\]
For real \(z\) and real vectors, the analogous ideal two-real-coordinate row factor leaves
\[
 a(r-1)\log N+O_{\rm coarse}(N\log N).
 \tag{TS-real-net-loss}
\]
These are failures of the proposed net expressions, not assumed small-ball theorems for discrete laws.
The exact probability-one statement above is stronger and requires no density heuristic.

More concretely, fix the profile radius \(N^{-s}\) and poor-profile threshold \(N^{-D}\) before \(a\), as required by the existing precision-independent counting organization.
Classify each fixed net point, before \(C\) is sampled, by the concentration
\[
 \rho_R=\sup_{w\in\mathbb C^2}
 \mathbb P\left\{\left\|\sum_{j\in R}
      (u_j,(Bu)_j)\xi_j-w\right\|\le N^{-s}\right\},
\]
with the original real scalar law.
After \(a\) is large enough for the row-transfer errors, the poor branch \(\rho_R\le N^{-D}\) gives at most \(N^{-Dm}\) for a fixed candidate.
Covering the whole adaptive fiber already costs at least \(N^{a(8m-2)+O_{\rm coarse}(N)}\) in the complex case, or \(N^{a(4m-1)+O_{\rm coarse}(N)}\) in the real case.
These are lower precision costs; a larger actual dimension of \(V\) can only worsen the unrestricted cover.
They do not assert a lower bound for the entropy of a separately proved structured poor-profile subset, which is precisely an input not supplied by this construction.
The same calculation would require \(D\) to grow at least like \(8a\), or \(4a\), respectively.
It cannot yield the existing form “choose all profile/entropy constants first, then every sufficiently large fixed \(a\).”
The [earlier joint-profile precision test](Yin_L_F0066_v16.md#two-column-long-profile-precision-test) explains the same coarse-before-fine requirement in its actual counting context.
Allowing \(D\) to grow with \(a\) has no supplied inverse-profile argument whose leading entropy remains independent of \(a\); that would be a new input, not a substitution in the earlier estimate.
The rich branch also cannot be removed solely by invoking independence of \(B\).
For zero-diagonal Rademacher \(B\), take \(u=N^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)\) and write \((Bu)_j=k_j/N\), where \(k_j\in\mathbb Z\) and \(|k_j|\le N-1\).
For fresh independent Rademacher scalars \(\eta_j\), the pair
\[
 \left(\sum_{j\in R}u_j\eta_j,\ \sum_{j\in R}(Bu)_j\eta_j\right)
\]
has at most \((m+1)(2m(N-1)+1)\le(N+1)(2N^2+1)\) possible values, hence an exact atom at least \(cN^{-3}\) for every such \(B\).
This arithmetic witness is not asserted to belong to \(V\), or to satisfy the near-kernel equations.
It refutes only the shortcut that the independent image \(Bu\) alone makes every paired profile arbitrarily poor.
No unsupported conditional variance or Gaussian rotation is used in this conclusion.

## Restoring the other side requires a joint matrix-image estimate

One cannot obtain the missing constraints by treating the \(R\)-side rows as new independent randomness after the \(T\)-side rows.
For a fixed \(u\), the fresh outputs of both sides are
\[
 C u_R,\quad C v_R,\quad C^T u_T,\quad C^T v_T,
 \qquad v=Bu.
 \tag{TS-full-rectangle-image}
\]
They use the same original entries.
Conditioning on the first two outputs generally destroys the product law needed for the last two.

The exact size of this dependence can be computed without changing the sampled law.
Let \(k_R\) be the real dimension of the span of \(\Re u_R,\Im u_R,\Re v_R,\Im v_R\), and define \(k_T\) similarly.
The real-linear map from \(M_m(\mathbb R)\) in (TS-full-rectangle-image) has rank
\[
 m(k_R+k_T)-k_Rk_T.
 \tag{TS-joint-image-rank}
\]
Indeed its kernel consists of matrices annihilating the \(k_R\)-dimensional right space whose transpose also annihilates the \(k_T\)-dimensional left space.
Its dimension is \((m-k_R)(m-k_T)\).
Choosing orthonormal bases to calculate this rank does not rotate or replace the original independent-entry probability law.

For real vectors with two independent coefficient directions on each side, the rank is \(4m-4\), not \(4m\).
For complex vectors with four real directions on each side, it is \(8m-16\), not \(8m\).
For real orthonormal basis matrices \(Q_R\in\mathbb R^{m\times k_R}\) and \(Q_T\in\mathbb R^{m\times k_T}\), write the equivalent fresh images as \(Y_T=CQ_R\) and \(Y_R=C^TQ_T\).
Their \(k_Rk_T\) compatibility equations are
\[
 Q_T^T Y_T=Y_R^T Q_R.
 \tag{TS-center-compatibility}
\]
When the original row equations prescribe these images, this identity involves their actual reconstructed centers and the candidate \(u\), rather than freely chosen centers.
Their quantitative geometry has not been supplied here, and it would be unjustified to remove these dimensions from the adaptive cover without proving that those center conditions impose the claimed restrictions.

The tested one-sided all-vector substitute is therefore rejected at the original law, not merely left with an unspecified arithmetic gap.
A continuation must retain the two-sided joint image and its actual center compatibility, or produce a genuine adaptive-to-structured reduction before discarding rows.
Neither the earlier fixed-family poor-profile estimate nor multiplying two mirrored row products provides that missing input.
This test establishes no new least-value floor, middle negative-logarithm estimate, F14 upper limit or F12 Brown theorem.
