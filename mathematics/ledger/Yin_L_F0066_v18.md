## The centers retain the original equations and the original law

Keep the original two independent real fixed-law finite-second-moment inputs, the polynomial \(AB+2BA\), and \(|R|=|T|=m=\lfloor N/8\rfloor\), \(I=R\cup T\), \(J=I^c\).
First condition on \(B,A_{JJ},A_{JI}\), retaining the original-law common guard \(s_{\min}(B_{JI})\ge N^{-1}\), and then on \(A_{RR},A_{TT}\).
Exactly as in [L66v17](Yin_L_F0066_v17.md#two-sided-fiber-and-law), \(C=A_{TR}\) retains independent original real entries divided by \(\sqrt N\), while \(A_{RT}=C^T\) is the same randomness.
The outside fiber and maps are
\[
 \begin{split}
 \Pi&=I_J-B_{JI}B_{JI}^{\dagger},\\
 h(u)&=zu_J-(ABu)_J-2B_{JJ}(Au)_J,\\
 V&=\ker(\Pi h),\qquad v=Bu,\\
 t_J^*&=(Au)_J,\qquad t_I^*=\tfrac12 B_{JI}^{\dagger}h(u).
 \end{split}
 \tag{TS2-outside}
\]
All these maps are fixed before sampling \(C\).
For this first algebraic test take real \(z\) and real \(u\in V\).
Define the actual centers
\[
 \begin{split}
 \alpha_T&=t_T^*-A_{TT}u_T-A_{TJ}u_J,&
 \alpha_R&=t_R^*-A_{RR}u_R-A_{RJ}u_J,\\
 \beta_T&=zu_T-2(Bt^*)_T-A_{TT}v_T-A_{TJ}v_J,&
 \beta_R&=zu_R-2(Bt^*)_R-A_{RR}v_R-A_{RJ}v_J.
 \end{split}
 \tag{TS2-centers}
\]
The full remaining exact relations, with none of the \(R\)-side equations discarded, are
\[
 C[u_R,v_R]=[\alpha_T,\beta_T],\qquad
 C^T[u_T,v_T]=[\alpha_R,\beta_R].
 \tag{TS2-both-sides}
\]
When both displayed coefficient pairs have real rank two, their necessary and sufficient algebraic compatibility for some real matrix \(C\) is the following system of four homogeneous quadratic equations in the actual vector \(u\):
\[
 \begin{split}
 F_{11}&=u_T^T\alpha_T-\alpha_R^Tu_R=0,\\
 F_{12}&=u_T^T\beta_T-\alpha_R^Tv_R=0,\\
 F_{21}&=v_T^T\alpha_T-\beta_R^Tu_R=0,\\
 F_{22}&=v_T^T\beta_T-\beta_R^Tv_R=0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{TS2-four-quadrics}
\]
Indeed full column rank allows a change to bases of the two coefficient spaces, and these four equations identify the common two-by-two corner of the prescribed images of \(C\) and \(C^T\).
The prescribed images fix the remaining row and column blocks, while the block between the two orthogonal complements is free.
This basis argument proves only solvability over real matrices; it neither changes the sampled entry law nor ensures that a solution lies in its support.
The image rank remains \(4m-4\), as in [the fixed joint-image calculation](Yin_L_F0066_v17.md#two-sided-mirror-joint-map).

One relation among the displayed expressions identifies their actual meaning, without making one of them identically zero:
\[
 F_{12}-F_{21}=z\,u^Tu-3(Bu)^Tt^*\qquad(u\in V).
 \tag{TS2-global-compatibility}
\]
To verify it, put \(\overline A=A\) with just its \(R\)-by-\(T\) and \(T\)-by-\(R\) blocks set to zero.
The global center vectors are \(\alpha=t^*-\overline A u\) and \(\beta=zu-2Bt^*-\overline A v\), and their \(J\) components vanish on \(V\).
Thus the left side is \(u^T\beta-v^T\alpha\); symmetry of \(\overline A\), symmetry of \(B\), and \(v=Bu\) give the identity.

## One explicit family distinguishes transverse and degenerate centers

For \(m\ge2\), put \(r=2m\), \(p=|J|\), \(H=[I_r;0]\in\mathbb R^{p\times r}\), and fix \(z=1\).
Consider the deterministic outside and already exposed blocks
\[
 B_{JI}=H,\qquad B_{II}=B_{JJ}=0,\qquad
 A_{JJ}=A_{JI}=0,\qquad A_{RR}=A_{TT}=\lambda I_m.
 \tag{TS2-background-family}
\]
The remaining blocks are fixed by symmetry or are the unexposed \(C\).
Here \(s_{\min}(B_{JI})=1\), \(\|B\|=1\), and the exact linear outside constraint has all its nonzero singular values equal to one.
Writing \(u_I=x\), the outside fiber and reconstructed vectors are
\[
 V=\{u_I=x,\ u_J=Hy:\ x,y\in\mathbb R^r\},\qquad
 t_I^*=y/2,\quad t_J^*=0,\quad v_I=y.
 \tag{TS2-family-fiber}
\]
In particular its real dimension is exactly \(4m\), so no extra outside degeneracy explains the comparison below.
Set
\[
 \Delta_{xy}=x_T^Ty_T-x_R^Ty_R,\quad
 \Delta_x=\|x_T\|^2-\|x_R\|^2,\quad
 \Delta_y=\|y_T\|^2-\|y_R\|^2.
\]
The four actual quadrics reduce to
\[
 \begin{split}
 F_{11}&=\tfrac12\Delta_{xy}-\lambda\Delta_x,\\
 F_{12}&=\|x_T\|^2-\tfrac12\|y_R\|^2-\lambda\Delta_{xy},\\
 F_{21}&=\tfrac12\|y_T\|^2-\|x_R\|^2-\lambda\Delta_{xy},\\
 F_{22}&=\Delta_{xy}-\lambda\Delta_y.
 \end{split}
 \tag{TS2-family-quadrics}
\]
At
\[
 x_R=x_T=e_1,\qquad y_R=y_T=e_1+e_2,
 \tag{TS2-common-point}
\]
all four expressions vanish for every \(\lambda\), and both coefficient pairs have rank two.
The Jacobian submatrix on the variables \((x_{R1},x_{R2},x_{T1},y_{R1})\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 2\lambda-\tfrac12&-\tfrac12&\tfrac12-2\lambda&-\tfrac12\\
 \lambda&\lambda&2-\lambda&\lambda-1\\
 \lambda-2&\lambda&-\lambda&\lambda\\
 -1&-1&1&2\lambda-1
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is \(-8\lambda(2\lambda^2-1)\).
A complete hand calculation is short: replace row two by row two minus row three, and row four by row four minus twice row one.
The new fourth row is \(2\lambda(-2,0,2,1)\), so factor out \(2\lambda\).
After adding that factored fourth row to row two, the second row is \((0,0,4,0)\).
Expansion in that row gives
\[
 -8\lambda\det\begin{pmatrix}
 2\lambda-\tfrac12&-\tfrac12&-\tfrac12\\
 \lambda-2&\lambda&\lambda\\
 -2&0&1
 \end{pmatrix}
 =-8\lambda(2\lambda^2-1).
\]
These are polynomial determinant identities, so the factorization also holds at \(\lambda=0\).
At \(\lambda=1\) it is \(-8\), proving that the four actual constraints can be independent.
The vector in (TS2-common-point) has norm \(\sqrt6\); normalization preserves rank, and the four-by-four minor becomes \(-2/9\).
Since the quadrics are homogeneous and vanish there, their gradients are perpendicular to the radial direction.
The real implicit function theorem therefore gives local dimension \(4m-5\) for the unit compatible vectors at this normalized transverse point.

At \(\lambda=0\), however, \(F_{22}=2F_{11}\) identically.
The first three Jacobian rows on the variables \((x_{R1},x_{R2},x_{T1})\) have determinant \(2\) at the same point, so the Jacobian rank is exactly three.
The local unit compatible set then has dimension \(4m-4\).
Thus full coefficient-pair rank, a perfect rectangular inverse guard and bounded exposed blocks do not themselves imply four independent center constraints.
