# One-row conditioning gives a sharp quartic quotient, with a necessary rank branch

This bounded ordinary development for Yin on 2026-11-06 continues the [exact physical Schur calculation in D11](../drafts/Yin_D_F0011.md#two-sided-physical-schur-continuation), using the [adopted C187v28 gap and covariance](../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v28.md#statement).
It produces a complete conditional algebraic reduction and tests its limitations; it does not establish an original-law small-ball estimate.
All matrices, rows and conditional laws below are the actual ones for \(P=AB+2BA\), with real finite-second-moment Wigner inputs and the original \(N^{-1/2}\) scaling.
The parameter \(z\) may be complex.
The formulas use direct block elimination, determinant expansion and explicit minors, so no numerical run or unretained program is evidence for this section.
No new external theorem or source is invoked.
The authoring arrangement included a delegated direct algebra calculation and ordinary authoring checks; this is neither an independent project review nor adoption of a Claim proof.


<a id="two-sided-quartic-physical-setup"></a>
## Original model, physical blocks and the tested implication

The model is exactly the real-input part of the [C187v28 statement](../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v28.md#statement): two independent Wigner matrices with fixed centered variance-one real off-diagonal laws and fixed centered finite-second-moment real diagonal laws, all upper-triangular coordinates independent within each size and arbitrary dependence across sizes.
All minors retain the original \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization.
This test concerns the still-unresolved representative \(P=AB+2BA\) of the original F12/F14 goals, not a statement for every repeated-variable polynomial.
The attempted improvement is to use one entire original row as the remaining independent randomness, lowering the degree of the complete physical determinant while retaining its normalization and every rank branch.
The failed stronger assertions tested below are a universal reduction to degree at most three in those original row coordinates, and a uniform conditional small-ball bound from only the current individual minor joint-gap and norm conditions.
The result is a conditional quartic reformulation with explicit counterexamples to those stronger assertions, leaving the additional original-law probability input open.

Write, after moving the selected physical position to the first coordinate,
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}a&x^T\\x&A_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}b&y^T\\y&B_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad n=N-1,
\]
and use the actual companion
\[
 \mathcal L(A,B;z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}-zI&A&2B\\B&-I&0\\A&0&-I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Eliminating its last two block equations gives
\(\det\mathcal L(A,B;z)=\det(AB+2BA-zI)\).
Deleting the selected physical coordinate in all three channels gives
\[
 Q=\mathcal L(A_0,B_0;z),\qquad M_0=A_0B_0+2B_0A_0-zI_n.
\]
The same equations identify \(\ker Q\) with \(u\mapsto(u,B_0u,A_0u)\) on \(\ker M_0\), and show \(Q\) invertible exactly when \(M_0\) is invertible.
The full companion, ordered with the three selected physical coordinates first, has blocks
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}D_i&R_i\\C_i&Q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D_i=\begin{pmatrix}-z&a&2b\\b&-1&0\\a&0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
 R_i=\begin{pmatrix}0&x^T&2y^T\\y^T&0&0\\x^T&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C_i=\begin{pmatrix}0&x&2y\\y&0&0\\x&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both borders use the same original \(x,y\).
On the \(Q\)-invertible branch the exact Schur and projected-column matrices are
\[
 S_i=D_i-R_iQ^{-1}C_i,\qquad
 F_i=(I_3+R_iQ^{-1}Q^{-*}R_i^*)^{-1/2}S_i.
\]
The isometric rows \((I_3+R_iQ^{-1}Q^{-*}R_i^*)^{-1/2}[I_3,-R_iQ^{-1}]\) annihilate the outside columns, which verifies this normalization directly.
These definitions fully specify the numerator and denominator used below without an independent copy or a changed Gaussian row law.

## Actual exposure and a rank-four update

At physical position \(i\), condition on \(A_0,B_0\), both diagonal entries \(a,b\), and the entire second-input row \(y\).
The first-input row \(x\) remains a vector of independent original entries \(\xi_1/\sqrt N\), independent of this sigma-field.
This conditioning fixes all of \(B\), as well as \(\bar A=\operatorname{diag}(a,A_0)\) after moving \(i\) to the first position.
No adaptive vector has been frozen, and no independent copy of a reused row is introduced.
Write \(e=e_i\), \(h=(0,x)^T\), so \(A=\bar A+eh^T+he^T\), and set
\[
 \bar M=\bar AB+2B\bar A-zI_N,\qquad
 U=[e,Be,h,Bh],\qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&1\\0&0&2&0\\0&1&0&0\\2&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The exact identity is
\[
 P-zI_N=\bar M+UJU^T,
 \qquad UJU^T=e h^TB+h e^TB+2Be h^T+2Bh e^T.
 \tag{Conditional-rank-four-update}
\]
The perturbation is linear in \(x\) and has rank at most four.
In the determinant expansion, any term involving more than four columns from this perturbation vanishes.
Thus
\[
 f_i(x):=\det(P-zI_N)
\]
is a complex polynomial of total degree at most four in the original real coordinates of \(x\), for every conditioned background, including singular \(\bar M\).
The following bordered identity retains that branch without division:
\[
 f_i(x)=\det\begin{pmatrix}\bar M&U\\-JU^T&I_4\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{Conditional-bordered-determinant}
\]
If \(\bar M\) is invertible, eliminating its block also gives
\[
 f_i(x)=\det\bar M\,\det(I_4+JU^T\bar M^{-1}U).
\]
This last expression has an additional inverse hypothesis; invertibility of the actual \(Q=\mathcal L(A_0,B_0;z)\) does not provide it.
For example, \(A_0=0\), \(y=0\), \(z=1\), and \(a=b=1/\sqrt3\) give invertible \(M_0=-I\) but a zero first diagonal entry of \(\bar M\).
Neither inverse branch may be silently discarded.
On the original \(Q\)-invertible branch, \(f_i=\det Q\det S_i\), so \(\det S_i\) also has conditional degree at most four.

<a id="two-sided-quartic-denominator"></a>
## The same-row denominator also has degree four

Retain the actual row border \(R_i\) and physical minor \(Q\) from the [physical setup](#two-sided-quartic-physical-setup).
After fixing \(y\), only its first and third rows depend on \(x\), and that dependence is linear.
If \(Q\) is invertible, define
\[
 H_i(x)=\det(I_3+R_iQ^{-1}Q^{-*}R_i^*)\ge1.
\]
This is the Gram determinant of the three rows of \([I_3,R_iQ^{-1}]\).
Expanding it as the sum of the squared moduli of all three-column minors shows that \(H_i\) is a nonnegative real polynomial of degree at most four in \(x\): each such minor is at most quadratic because only two rows vary.
The exact normalized determinant is therefore
\[
 \det F_i(x)=\frac{\det S_i(x)}{\sqrt{H_i(x)}}.
 \tag{Conditional-quartic-Schur-quotient}
\]
Both numerator and denominator use this same original row \(x\).
The reduction from total degree six to conditional degree four is genuine, but it neither gives a quadratic form nor separates independent noise.

There is also a version that does not assume \(Q\) invertible.
Let \(\mathcal B_i\) be the three physical columns and let \(T_i\) be all other columns of the full companion matrix, after the same row and column rearrangement:
\[
 \mathcal B_i=\begin{pmatrix}D_i\\C_i\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T_i=\begin{pmatrix}R_i\\Q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathscr D_i(x)=\det(T_i^*T_i).
\]
The rectangular matrix \(T_i\) has \(3N\) rows and \(3N-3\) columns.
Every maximal minor of \(T_i\) has degree at most two in \(x\), again because only two rows vary.
Consequently
\[
 \mathscr D_i(x)=\sum_{|I|=3N-3}|\det(T_i)_I|^2
 \tag{Conditional-exterior-denominator}
\]
is a nonnegative real polynomial of degree at most four.
If \(\mathscr D_i>0\), choose any orthonormal basis of \(\ker T_i^*\), which has dimension three, and let \(\widehat F_i\) be the matrix of the orthogonal projection of \(\mathcal B_i\) into this basis.
Orthogonal block elimination gives
\[
 |\det\widehat F_i|^2
 =\det(\mathcal B_i^*(I-T_i(T_i^*T_i)^{-1}T_i^*)\mathcal B_i)
 =\frac{|\det\mathcal L_N(z)|^2}{\det(T_i^*T_i)}.
\]
Using \(\det\mathcal L_N(z)=f_i(x)\), this yields
\[
 |\det\widehat F_i(x)|=\frac{|f_i(x)|}{\sqrt{\mathscr D_i(x)}}.
 \tag{Conditional-quartic-exterior-quotient}
\]
On the \(Q\)-invertible branch, \(\mathscr D_i=|\det Q|^2H_i\), so this agrees exactly with the previous quotient in modulus.
If instead \(\mathscr D_i=0\), the outside columns are dependent and the full companion, hence \(P-zI_N\), is already singular.
Equivalently there exists nonzero \(w\) with \(Qw=0\) and \(R_iw=0\).
This branch is preserved as an actual exceptional event, not converted into a quotient or assumed absent.
A singular \(Q\) alone need not force \(\mathscr D_i=0\), since its null vectors may be separated by \(R_i\).

## A fourth-order term survives the original coordinate structure

Take \(N=5\), \(a=b=0\), \(A_0=0_{4\times4}\), \(y=e_1\),
\[
 B_0=E_{12}+E_{21}+E_{34}+E_{43},\qquad
 x=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)^T,
\]
and any fixed \(z\ne0\).
Here \(\bar A=0\), \(P=UJU^T\), \(\det J=4\), and \(M_0=-zI_4\) is invertible.
The fourth elementary coefficient of \(P\) is the sum of its four-dimensional principal minors.
Each is \(4\det(U_I)^2\).
The only possibly nonzero minors of \(U\) use the first two physical rows, because its first two columns are \(e_0,e_1\).
The three remaining two-by-two determinants are
\[
 x_2x_4-x_1x_3,\qquad x_2x_3-x_1x_4,\qquad x_3^2-x_4^2.
\]
Thus
\[
 e_4(P)=4\bigl[(x_2x_4-x_1x_3)^2+
                   (x_2x_3-x_1x_4)^2+(x_3^2-x_4^2)^2\bigr].
\]
Since \(\operatorname{rank}P\le4\), the degree-four homogeneous part of \(f_i=\det(P-zI_5)\) is \(-z e_4(P)\).
Also \(\det Q=\det M_0=z^4\), so that of \(\det S_i\) is
\[
 -\frac4{z^3}\bigl[(x_2x_4-x_1x_3)^2+
                   (x_2x_3-x_1x_4)^2+(x_3^2-x_4^2)^2\bigr].
 \tag{Conditional-distinct-quartic-layer}
\]
The coefficient of \(x_1x_2x_3x_4\) is \(16/z^3\ne0\).
In particular it remains a fourth-order Walsh term when the four row coordinates are Rademacher; replacing \(x_j^2\) by one does not remove it.
This establishes sharp conditional degree four, including nonreal \(z\), without saying that this background satisfies the joint-gap guard.

There is an exact original-law single-size support realization.
Choose the fixed off-diagonal law with masses \(1/10\) at each of \(\pm\sqrt5\) and mass \(4/5\) at zero, and choose zero diagonals.
It is centered with variance one and bounded, and at size five the required normalized entries lie in its support.
The displayed minor and \(y\) conditioning has positive probability.
Conditioning further on each fresh row coordinate being nonzero preserves independence and makes the four signs Rademacher, so the surviving Walsh term is not an artifact of a continuous row model.
This single-size support fact gives no asymptotic bad-event probability and no Task counterexample.
The full background is used only to refute a universal algebraic reduction to a quadratic or cubic original-coordinate polynomial.

## The current minor gap does not control the necessary rank branch

A separate deterministic test shows that C187v28's minor-measurable gap and norm conditions alone cannot give a uniform conditional small-ball estimate for the complete object.
Let \(n=2m\), \(m\ge4\), and take
\[
 A_0=\bigoplus_{j=1}^m\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B_0=\bigoplus_{j=1}^m\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad z=i.
 \tag{Conditional-gap-corank-background}
\]
Then \(A_0^2=B_0^2=I\), \(A_0B_0=-B_0A_0\), and both operator norms equal one.
For a real unit \(u\), put \(\alpha=u^TA_0u\), \(\beta=u^TB_0u\).
The identity \((\alpha A_0+\beta B_0)^2=(\alpha^2+\beta^2)I\) and the expectation in \(u\) give \(\alpha^2+\beta^2\le1\).
Minimizing over real centers therefore yields
\[
 \inf_{a,b\in\mathbb R}
 \bigl(\|(A_0-aI)u\|^2+\|(B_0-bI)u\|^2\bigr)
 =2-\alpha^2-\beta^2\ge1.
\]
Hence this minor has the all-real-center maximum gap at least \(1/\sqrt2\), and also satisfies the complex sum extension.
However \(P_0=A_0B_0+2B_0A_0=-A_0B_0\) has eigenvalues \(i,-i\), each with multiplicity \(m\).
Thus \(\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker(P_0-iI)=m\), and the exact companion equations identify \(\ker Q\) with \(u\mapsto(u,B_0u,A_0u)\) on this kernel.
For every choice of the fresh rows and diagonals, the three-row map \(R_i:\ker Q\to\mathbb C^3\) has a nonzero kernel because \(m\ge4\).
Consequently \(\mathscr D_i=0\) and \(f_i=0\) identically in the fresh row variables, despite the stated minor gap and norm bounds.
Positive Hermitian covariance for each fixed minor-measurable graph vector does not make this random linear map injective on a domain of dimension four or more.

These deterministic backgrounds can occur at arbitrarily large sizes under one fixed original finite-second-moment law, while remaining extremely rare.
For odd integers \(r\ge9\), put \(w_r=384\,2^{-r}/r\), give each of \(\pm\sqrt r\) mass \(w_r/2\), and put the remaining mass at zero.
The sum of \(2^{-r}\) over odd \(r\ge9\) is \(1/384\), so the law is centered with variance \(\sum_rw_rr=1\), and \(\sum_rw_r\le1/9\).
Use this same fixed law for both off-diagonal and diagonal coordinates, with all the required independence.
At every odd size \(N=2m+1\ge9\), the normalized entries \(0,\pm1\) needed for (Conditional-gap-corank-background) are in support, and the exact minor event has positive probability.
Its probability is at most \((w_N/2)^{N-1}\), already from the prescribed nonzero diagonal entries of \(A_0\), so these examples are summably rare and refute no Task limit or marginal asymptotic estimate.
They show only that a conditional estimate uniform over all backgrounds satisfying the current individual minor gap and polynomial norm guard is false.
No assertion is made that the example satisfies the simultaneous all-parent-deletion good event or any additional spectral regularity guard.

More precisely, for this one fixed law and each odd \(N\ge9\), let \(\mathfrak G_N\) contain all positive-probability conditioning backgrounds with individual minor real gap at least \(1/\sqrt2\) and both minor norms at most one.
For every \(\tau\ge0\), the example gives
\[
 \sup_{\omega\in\mathfrak G_N}
 \mathbb P_x\{\mathscr D_i=0\ \text{or}
   (\mathscr D_i>0,\ |f_i|\le\tau\sqrt{\mathscr D_i})\mid\omega\}=1.
\]
It is this vanishing uniform conditional estimate that fails, not an averaged estimate after additional quantitative background exclusions.

## Exact floor implication and the first missing input

The reduction has an explicit interface to the original least-value question.
Put \(H_N(K)=\{\max(\|A\|,\|B\|)\le N^K\}\), with \(K\ge1\).
On this full-parent norm guard, the companion norm and every projected physical column block satisfy \(\|\widehat F_i\|\le C_zN^K\).
If \(s_{\min}(P-zI)\le\varepsilon\), the graph vector \((u,Bu,Au)\), after normalization, gives a unit companion approximate kernel with residual at most \(\varepsilon\).
Some physical block has norm at least \(N^{-1/2}\).
For its index \(i\), if \(\mathscr D_i>0\), orthogonal projection away from the outside columns gives \(s_{\min}(\widehat F_i)\le\sqrt N\,\varepsilon\).
Therefore the complete deterministic event inclusion is
\[
 \{s_{\min}(P-zI)\le\varepsilon\}\cap H_N(K)
 \subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^N\left(
 \{\mathscr D_i=0\}\ \cup
 \{\mathscr D_i>0,\ |f_i|\le C_z^2N^{2K+1/2}\varepsilon\sqrt{\mathscr D_i}\}
 \right).
 \tag{Conditional-quartic-floor-interface}
\]
The norm-guard complement is at most \(CN^{1-2K}\) by the [exact Frobenius calculation in C187v28](../claims/Yin_C_F0187_v28.md#companion-normalization-and-average).
For each index the conditioning above leaves a genuine product original law in \(x\); the events for different indices are not asserted independent.
An elementary union bound costs \(N\), so arbitrary-power bounds for the complete conditional event, with the extra power allocated in advance, would supply a least-value floor at this interface.
No such bound is proved, and the formula by itself is not a principal-minor average estimate.

The first unsupported input is original-law control of the background and quartic pair \((f_i,\mathscr D_i)\) that includes the exact rank branch, excludes conditional identically-zero numerators when necessary, and proves small concentration for the same-row quotient at polynomial precision.
The corank test shows why C187v28's current individual minor gap cannot alone be that background hypothesis; its common exception is also only \(o(1)\), not the summable or arbitrary-power error a floor assembly would require.
Even after an adequate rank exclusion, the explicit fourth-order term prevents simply importing a scalar quadratic inverse-concentration theorem.
A new coefficient/structure estimate or another lawful exposure would have to control the complete surviving quartic layer and its random denominator.
This conditional reformulation bypasses the earlier free-vector net bookkeeping, but establishes neither that new probabilistic estimate nor a middle negative-logarithm profile, so the original F12/F14 conclusions remain open for this representative polynomial.
