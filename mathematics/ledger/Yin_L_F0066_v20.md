# A shared nonzero root does not give a one-factor progression count

This ordinary algebraic comparison tests a possible entropy reduction in the [fixed C194v31 root-triad formulation](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#original-root-triad) and its [remaining structured-count boundary](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#remaining-entropy-obstruction).
The exact fresh coefficients have the form
\[
 a_j=(h_j,0,g_j),\qquad b_j=(0,h_j,2q_j)\in\mathbb C^3.
\]
When both entry laws coincide, these can lawfully be concatenated as coefficients of independent copies of one real scalar law.
The tested further inference is that membership of both lists in one proper generalized arithmetic progression, together with the shared root \(h\), leaves only one progression factor of choices per physical index.
The construction below disproves that inference from coefficient geometry alone.
It retains two progression factors even when every root coordinate is nonzero, the root is flat, its real and imaginary parts have equal orthogonal mass, and all three vector norms remain uniformly bounded.

No inverse Littlewood--Offord theorem, probability estimate or numerical calculation is used to prove the obstruction.
In particular, this is not an actual Wigner-triad realization or a counterexample to a high-probability estimate.
The original equations relating \(h,g,q\) remain additional constraints that could eliminate the constructed assignments.

<a id="coincident-law-concatenation"></a>
## Exact scope of concatenating the two colors

For deterministic coefficient vectors and a fixed fresh physical row, the original two-color sum is
\[
 \sum_{j\in J}\bigl[a_j\xi_{A,j}+b_j\xi_{B,j}\bigr],
 \qquad a_j=(h_j,0,g_j),\quad b_j=(0,h_j,2q_j).
 \tag{Two-color-triad-row}
\]
Suppose the two fixed real off-diagonal laws coincide, and keep the original within-size independence across colors and physical entries.
Then \((\xi_{A,j},\xi_{B,j})_{j\in J}\), listed in any fixed order, consists of \(2|J|\) independent copies of that common real law.
Consequently (Two-color-triad-row) is exactly a scalar-multiplier vector sum with the concatenated coefficient list
\[
 (a_{j_1},b_{j_1},a_{j_2},b_{j_2},\ldots)
 \quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb C^3\simeq\mathbb R^6.
 \tag{Coincident-law-concatenation}
\]
This identity preserves the original law and does not introduce independent copies of mirrored entries.
It is used only at the legitimate fresh-row exposure, where those original upper coordinates are independent.

Thus equality of the two laws removes the identically-distributed-input obstacle to comparing this sum with the scalar-vector inverse-theorem format discussed in [C194v21](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v21.md#original-law-joint-exposure).
It does not verify that theorem's concentration and scale hypotheses or establish any particular progression output.
If the two laws differ, concatenation gives independent but not identically distributed real multipliers, and no extension of the scalar iid theorem is asserted here.
The counterexample below starts directly with a specified proper progression; it does not claim that an inverse theorem selects that progression.

<a id="shared-root-fiber-count"></a>
## The exact fiber count and an explicit proper progression

Let \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) be the standard complex coordinate vectors.
The two coefficient subspaces are
\[
 V_A=\{(x,0,y):x,y\in\mathbb C\},\qquad
 V_B=\{(0,x,y):x,y\in\mathbb C\}.
\]
For a finite set \(Q\subset\mathbb C^3\) and a prescribed shared root value \(h\), define
\[
 A_h(Q)=\{g:(h,0,g)\in Q\},\qquad
 B_h(Q)=\{q:(0,h,2q)\in Q\}.
\]
The number of permitted coefficient pairs at that fixed physical index is exactly
\[
 |A_h(Q)|\,|B_h(Q)|.
 \tag{Exact-shared-root-fiber}
\]
If the root value is also allowed to vary, the total is
\(\sum_h|A_h(Q)||B_h(Q)|\), with the sum over the finitely many compatible projections.
These are literal Cartesian-fiber counts; equality of the two projections fixes \(h\) but does not identify \(g\) with \(q\).

Fix an even integer \(N\ge2\) and any integer \(m\ge1\), and set
\[
 a=N^{-1/2},\qquad \delta=\frac{a}{m}.
\]
Consider the symmetric generalized arithmetic progression
\[
 \begin{split}
 Q_{N,m}=\bigl\{&a(k_1e_1+k_2e_2+k_3ie_1+k_4ie_2)\\
              &+\delta(\ell_1e_3+\ell_2ie_3):
       k_\nu\in\{-1,0,1\},\ -m\le\ell_1,\ell_2\le m\bigr\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{Explicit-shared-root-GAP}
\]
All parameters are integers in the displayed symmetric intervals.
Its six generators are linearly independent over \(\mathbb R\) in \(\mathbb C^3\simeq\mathbb R^6\).
Therefore its parameterization is injective, so this is a proper symmetric progression of fixed rank six and exact cardinality
\[
 M:=|Q_{N,m}|=3^4(2m+1)^2=81(2m+1)^2.
 \tag{Progression-cardinality}
\]

Take either nonzero root value \(h=a\) or \(h=ia\).
For \(a_j=(h,0,g)\) to belong to \(Q_{N,m}\), the first two complex coordinates fix all four \(k_\nu\), while \(g\) may independently be any of
\[
 g=\delta(\ell_1+i\ell_2),\qquad -m\le\ell_1,\ell_2\le m.
\]
Hence \(|A_h(Q_{N,m})|=(2m+1)^2\).
For \(b_j=(0,h,2q)\), the first two coordinates instead fix the corresponding four \(k_\nu\), while
\[
 q=\frac\delta2(\ell_1'+i\ell_2'),\qquad
 -m\le\ell_1',\ell_2'\le m
\]
is arbitrary and independent of the preceding choice.
Hence \(|B_h(Q_{N,m})|=(2m+1)^2\), and (Exact-shared-root-fiber) is
\[
 |A_h(Q_{N,m})||B_h(Q_{N,m})|
     =(2m+1)^4=\left(\frac M{81}\right)^2.
 \tag{Quadratic-nonzero-fiber}
\]
This already rules out a bound \(C|Q|\) with a constant uniform over such progressions and nonzero shared root values.
Indeed the ratio of the left side of (Quadratic-nonzero-fiber) to \(M\) is \((2m+1)^2/81\), which tends to infinity with \(m\).
More generally, even a factor \(N^D|Q|\), for a prescribed fixed \(D\), does not follow uniformly from this geometry: take \(m=\lfloor N^\kappa\rfloor\) with \(2\kappa>D\).
All these progressions still have the same fixed rank six.

The missing information is visible directly in the fibers.
Both subspaces contain the entire third-coordinate line \(\mathbb Ce_3\), and changing either third coordinate leaves its root projection unchanged.
The shared-root condition imposes no relation between the two independent choices along that common line.
A bound on at least one of those fibers, or another condition coupling their assignments, would be additional information beyond the displayed subspaces and common root.

<a id="bounded-flat-root-family"></a>
## Uniform norms, flatness and two nonzero real-imaginary directions

Fix the root vector once and for all by
\[
 h_j=a\quad(1\le j\le N/2),\qquad
 h_j=ia\quad(N/2<j\le N).
 \tag{Fixed-flat-complex-root}
\]
Every coordinate is nonzero, and
\[
 \|h\|^2=1,\qquad \max_j|h_j|^2=\frac1N.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \|\operatorname{Re}h\|^2=\|\operatorname{Im}h\|^2=\frac12,
 \qquad \langle\operatorname{Re}h,\operatorname{Im}h\rangle=0.
 \tag{Two-real-directions}
\]
Thus this root is not a scalar complex phase times a real vector.
The obstruction is not confined to a zero root projection or a globally one-dimensional real root direction.

At every physical coordinate choose \(g_j\) and \(q_j\) independently from the two grids in the preceding construction, with this fixed \(h_j\).
All resulting coefficient vectors \((h_j,0,g_j)\) and \((0,h_j,2q_j)\) belong to the same \(Q_{N,m}\).
Since the choices are independent across physical positions, their exact number is
\[
 (2m+1)^{4N}=\left(\frac M{81}\right)^{2N}.
 \tag{Whole-vector-assignment-count}
\]
These are distinct triples \((h,g,q)\), because \(h\) is fixed and the grid parameterizations of both other vectors are injective.

The grids remain bounded as \(m\) grows:
\[
 |g_j|\le\sqrt2\,a,\qquad |q_j|\le\frac a{\sqrt2},
\]
and hence
\[
 \|g\|\le\sqrt2,\qquad \|q\|\le\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad
 \max_j\bigl(|h_j|^2+|g_j|^2+|q_j|^2\bigr)\le\frac7{2N}.
 \tag{Uniform-triad-norms-and-flatness}
\]
Thus neither bounded vector norms nor vanishing maximal coordinate mass removes the second progression factor.
Large \(m\) only refines the grid; it does not enlarge these norm bounds.
The choice \(m=\lfloor N^\kappa\rfloor\) for any fixed \(\kappa>0\) gives polynomial precision \(\delta\asymp N^{-\kappa-1/2}\) and the exact same qualitative obstruction.

For comparison with unit-energy coefficient conventions, the squared norm of the concatenated list is
\[
 \sigma^2:=\sum_j(\|a_j\|^2+\|b_j\|^2)
   =2\|h\|^2+\|g\|^2+4\|q\|^2\in[2,6].
\]
Dividing the full coefficient list and its progression by the same \(\sigma\) gives unit coefficient energy and preserves properness, cardinality and every fiber count.
The shared scaled root remains nonzero and flat, with its real and imaginary directions still orthogonal and of comparable fixed norms.
The value of \(\sigma\) may depend on the chosen triple; the whole-family count above uses the single common unscaled progression, while this paragraph checks individual normalization only.
This is only a normalization check; it asserts no inverse-theorem applicability or output.

<a id="exact-failure-scope"></a>
## Exact failed implication and the remaining probabilistic question

The failed statement is a uniform one-factor descriptor bound inferred solely from these premises:
both concatenated coefficient lists lie in a proper progression of fixed rank, their vectors lie in \(V_A,V_B\), their root projections agree and are nonzero, and the complete vectors have the bounded norms and flatness above.
The exact count (Whole-vector-assignment-count) retains two factors per physical coordinate under all of those premises.
Coincidence of the two scalar laws makes the iid concatenation lawful but imposes no new deterministic relation on \(g_j,q_j\), so it does not change that count.

The construction does not prescribe real symmetric matrices \(A,B\) satisfying
\[
 Ah=q,\qquad Bh=g,\qquad Ag+2Bq-zh\approx0.
\]
It does not claim that the triples arise with positive probability, are typical, or witness a small singular value of the actual Wigner product.
The original first two equations and the coupled third residual may eliminate most or all assignments in (Whole-vector-assignment-count).
Exploiting those actual equations in a lawful entropy/probability cancellation is a remaining possible method.
Likewise, an inverse theorem might produce additional useful properties of its particular progression; none is disproved by this example unless it is a consequence only of the tested geometric premises.

The conclusion is therefore restricted to the proposed shortcut in the [C194v31 counting boundary](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#remaining-entropy-obstruction).
It supplies no probability lower bound, no obstruction theorem for the original Wigner ensemble, and no negative answer to the summable planar-defect or almost-sure Brown questions.
No formal review or Claim adoption is recorded by this ordinary exploration.
