# Many exactly feasible two-color triples with the same root and progression

The question is whether the exact image equations \(Ah=q,\ Bh=g\), the product equation \(Ag+2Bq=zh\) and all complement range conditions suffice to reduce the support-wide number of two-color coefficient assignments to one progression factor per physical position.
The construction below realizes two progression factors to leading order across different matrices in the support of a single bounded real Wigner law.
It strengthens the coefficient-space test in [L66v20](Yin_L_F0066_v20.md#shared-root-fiber-count) by imposing all three equations from [C194v31](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#original-root-triad) at the nonreal parameter \(z=i\).
Every recovered image in this construction is an actual matrix image.
Both input norms are bounded by a fixed constant, and both inputs satisfy a fixed lower bound on the centered Hilbert--Schmidt variance of every macroscopic principal minor.
The count concerns deterministic triples feasible somewhere in the product support.
Conditional probabilities on a fixed random complement impose additional restrictions, so the resulting counting limit leaves the probability question open.

<a id="feasible-image-system"></a>
## A fixed flat complex root and symmetric lifts of its two images

Let \(N=16n\) tend to infinity through powers of two, and put \(a=N^{-1/2}\).
Split the physical indices into four consecutive quarters.
Let \(E\) be the \(N\)-by-two real matrix whose first column is \(\sqrt2a\) on the first half and zero elsewhere and whose second column is \(\sqrt2a\) on the second half and zero elsewhere.
Define \(F\) by multiplying each row of \(E\) by the sign \(+,-,+,-\) on the four successive quarters.
Then \(E^TE=F^TF=I_2\) and \(E^TF=0\).
Set
\[
 c_0=2^{-1/2}(1,i)^T,\qquad h=Ec_0,\qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We have \(Jc_0=ic_0\), \(\|h\|=1\), and all entries of \(h\) equal \(a\) or \(ia\).
Its real and imaginary parts have disjoint supports and squared norms \(1/2\).

Choose real \(N\)-by-two matrices \(X,Y\) satisfying
\[
 E^TX=E^TY=F^TX=F^TY=0,\qquad X^TY=0 .
\]
Write
\[
 U=F+X,\quad V=-FJ+Y,\quad
 A_0=UE^T+EU^T,\qquad B_0=VE^T+EV^T .
\]
In particular \(E^TU=E^TV=0\), \(U^TV=-J\) and \(V^TU=J\).
Both input matrices are real symmetric and satisfy
\[
 A_0E=U,\quad B_0E=V,\quad A_0V=E(U^TV),\quad B_0U=E(V^TU).
\]
Consequently \((A_0B_0+2B_0A_0)E=EJ\).
Setting \(q=Uc_0,\ g=Vc_0\) gives the exact three equations and zero residual at \(z=i\).
Given the fixed \(E,F,J\), the real and imaginary parts of \(g,q\) recover all four real columns of \(X,Y\).
Hence distinct pairs \(X,Y\) give distinct feasible triples.

## Counting orthogonal, uniformly conditioned image pairs

Fix an integer \(m\ge1\) and put \(b=\sqrt2a/m\).
Split each quarter into four equal blocks of \(n\) rows.
An unscaled block string has entries \(x_{j1},x_{j2},y_{j1},y_{j2}\) in \(\{-m,\ldots,m\}\) at each position.
Associate to a string its four column sums, the four sums of products \(x_{ju}y_{jv}\), and the three independent entries of each of the two Gram matrices \(\sum_j x_jx_j^T,\ \sum_j y_jy_j^T\), with \(x_j,y_j\) real two-vectors.
There are at most
\[
 H=(2nm+1)^4(2nm^2+1)^{10}
\]
such lists of aggregate integers.

At least half the strings have both Gram matrices bounded below by \(nm^2 I_2/6\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly in \(m\).
Indeed under the uniform counting distribution each coordinate divided by \(m\) is centered, bounded by one and has variance \((m+1)/(3m)\ge1/3\).
Each of the six normalized Gram entries divided by \(n\) has variance at most \(1/n\).
Chebyshev and a union bound put all entries within \(1/12\) of their expectations with probability at least \(1-864/n\).
The operator norm error of each normalized two-by-two matrix is then at most \(1/6\) by its row sums.
Choose one aggregate list satisfying these Gram bounds whose class has at least \((2m+1)^{4n}/(2H)\) strings.
This single list is used in all sixteen blocks.

Choose one string in this class independently at each block position, in the sense of Cartesian counting.
In each quarter multiply the two \(x\)-columns and two \(y\)-columns by the respective sign pairs \((+,+),(+,-),(-,+),(-,-)\) in the four blocks.
Multiplying the resulting entries by \(b\) defines \(X,Y\).
Equality of the original block column sums forces both signed column sums to vanish in each quarter.
Equality of each original cross-product sum forces \(X^TY=0\), since the four products of signs sum to zero in each quarter.
The vanishing quarter sums give the claimed orthogonality to \(E,F\).
The Gram lower bound becomes \(X^TX,Y^TY\succeq I_2/3\) since \(16nb^2m^2/6=1/3\).
The absolute value of every entry of \(X,Y\) is at most \(\sqrt2a\), whence their norms are bounded by their Frobenius norms, at most two.

The signed and scaled string assignments are injective.
Therefore the number of exactly feasible triples with this single fixed \(h\) is at least
\[
 \frac{(2m+1)^{4N}}{(2H)^{16}} .
\]
For each \(j\), the actual \(g_j,q_j\) are complex grid points with real and imaginary parts in \((a/m)\mathbb Z\) and bounded in absolute value componentwise by \(2a\).
Let \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) denote the complex coordinate vectors in \(\mathbb C^3\).
The proper symmetric progression
\[
 Q=\left\{a(k_1e_1+k_2e_2+k_3ie_1+k_4ie_2)
          +\frac{a}{m}(\ell_1e_3+\ell_2ie_3):
       k_j\in\{-1,0,1\},\quad \ell_j\in\mathbb Z,\ |\ell_j|\le4m\right\}
\]
contains every coefficient \((h_j,0,g_j)\) and \((0,h_j,2q_j)\) for every assignment.
The six real generators are independent, giving \(|Q|=81(8m+1)^2\).
The vectors are uniformly bounded and each coordinate has modulus at most \(2\sqrt2a\) for \(g,q\).
For \(m=\lfloor N^\kappa\rfloor\) with any fixed \(\kappa>0\), the logarithm of the count is at least \(4\kappa N\log N+O_\kappa(N)\), while \(N\log|Q|=2\kappa N\log N+O(N)\).
The assignments are distinguishable at coordinate precision below \(a/(2m)\), because the real and imaginary coordinate differences lie on the grid of spacing \(a/m\).
Thus even an approximation allowing that precision requires the same number of centers in the maximum coordinate norm.

## Bounded Wigner support and simultaneous macroscopic minor variances

Let \(P\) be the real orthogonal projection onto the span of all columns of \(E,F,X,Y\).
These four column spaces are pairwise orthogonal and each two-column Gram matrix has smallest eigenvalue at least \(1/3\).
For a two-column block \(Z\), its projection is \(Z(Z^TZ)^{-1}Z^T\), so each diagonal entry is bounded by \(3\|Z_{j,:}\|^2\).
The row bounds therefore give \(\max_j P_{jj}\le C/N\) with an absolute constant \(C\).
Let \(H_N\) be a real symmetric orthogonal Sylvester matrix with all entries of modulus \(N^{-1/2}\), as given by the recursion in [C198v1](../claims/Yin_C_F0198_v1.md#actual-polynomial-bounded-support-obstruction).
For a fixed \(\gamma>0\) to be chosen, set
\[
 W=\gamma(I-P)H_N(I-P),\qquad A=A_0+W,\quad B=B_0+W .
\]
Since \(W\) annihilates \(E,U,V\), the padded matrices realize the identical \(h,g,q\) and satisfy \((AB+2BA)h=ih\).
In particular, for any physical sets \(I,J=I^c\) the outside right side in [C194v31's reconstruction](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#root-image-reconstruction) equals \([A_{JI},2B_{JI}](g_I,q_I)^T\), interpreting the last factor as a block column.
Its projection perpendicular to that range is exactly zero for every split, including when the cross matrix has a kernel.

The operator norms of \(A_0,B_0\) are bounded since \(E,U,V\) have bounded norms, and \(\|W\|\le\gamma\).
Every entry of \(A_0,B_0\) is \(O(1/N)\) by the row bounds and their fixed number of columns.
For every \(j,k\), the three correction terms in \(H_N-(I-P)H_N(I-P)\) obey
\[
 |(PH_N)_{jk}|\le\sqrt{P_{jj}},\quad
 |(H_NP)_{jk}|\le\sqrt{P_{kk}},\quad
 |(PH_NP)_{jk}|\le\sqrt{P_{jj}P_{kk}} .
\]
It follows that \(\max_{jk}|W_{jk}|\le C'\gamma N^{-1/2}\).
Choose \(\gamma>0\) so that \(C'\gamma<\sqrt3/2\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\), every raw independent coordinate of \(A,B\), including their diagonals, lies in the interior of \([-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]\).
Thus each pair belongs to the support of two independent real Wigner inputs with fixed centered variance-one uniform laws for all independent coordinates.
The deterministic padding may be the same in both matrices because the support of the two-color law is Cartesian.

The difference \(H_N-(I-P)H_N(I-P)\) has rank at most \(2\operatorname{rank}P\le16\), since it equals \(PH_N+(I-P)H_NP\), and its operator norm is at most two.
The rank and operator norm of \(A_0,B_0\) are uniformly bounded, so \(\|A-\gamma H_N\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\|B-\gamma H_N\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C''\) uniformly.
For any subset \(J\) of size \(k\), the off-diagonal part of \(\gamma(H_N)_{JJ}\) has Hilbert--Schmidt norm \(\gamma\sqrt{k(k-1)/N}\).
Taking a submatrix and deleting its diagonal can only decrease the Hilbert--Schmidt norm of the error.
Consequently for either matrix \(M=A,B\) and every fixed \(\theta>0\), eventually and simultaneously for all \(k=|J|\ge\theta N\),
\[
 \frac1k\left\|M_{JJ}-k^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(M_{JJ})I_k\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \ \ge\ \frac1k\left(\gamma\sqrt{k(k-1)/N}-C''\right)^2
 \ \ge\ c_\theta>0 .
\]
The expression in parentheses is positive in this range for large \(N\).
This verifies the macroscopic non-scalar minor condition from [C194v31](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v31.md#covariance-trace-gap) under the same actual assignments.

## Scope of the support-wide test

The two-factor leading entropy survives the exact image equations, the coupled nonreal root equation, all complement range conditions and both simultaneous macroscopic covariance bounds.
For increasing \(m\), each feasible triple is realized at a potentially different pair of matrices.
For one fixed pair of matrices and one fixed \(h\), the two image equations already specify \(q,g\) uniquely.
A cover depending on the exposed complement can therefore have much smaller fibers on an event of high probability.
The construction quantifies only the count pooled over supports and uses a single parameter \(z=i\).
It gives no estimate for the probability of these exact backgrounds and imposes no lower bound on the least singular value of the cross identification matrix.
The remaining arithmetic problem is to charge the sizes of conditional feasible fibers against row probabilities while keeping the entries used in the test unexposed.
The almost-everywhere planar integral and the every-coupling Brown question retain their scopes in [C186v27](../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v27.md#extreme-planar-defect).
