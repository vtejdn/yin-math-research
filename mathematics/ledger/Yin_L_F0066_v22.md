# A fixed Wigner background with a full-dimensional family of feasible low-product roots

The [large interior product count](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v35.md#statement) leaves a low-product branch and handles that branch when given a sufficiently small dictionary in the fixed color.
The [earlier exact triple construction](Yin_L_F0066_v21.md#feasible-shared-root-count) pooled matrix pairs over both colors.
The calculation below keeps one color fixed, realizes the full root equation, and forces the reverse of the large-product inequality.
The example is a deterministic support test, checked in ordinary exploration.

Consider independent real symmetric Wigner colors with every raw upper coordinate, including the diagonals, uniform on \([-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]\), divided by \(\sqrt N\).
Fix an integer \(d\ge2\), which may be taken arbitrarily large, and use dimensions \(N=2m\) with \(m\) a power of two divisible by \(2^d\).
Set \(k=m/2^d\), so \(k/N=2^{-d-1}\) can meet the small fixed block fraction in the interior theorem.
Let \(W_m\) be the normalized real symmetric Sylvester matrix with entries \(m^{-1/2}(-1)^{u\cdot v}\) for binary index vectors \(u,v\).
Its square is the identity by character orthogonality and its trace is zero since the binary Hamming-weight signs cancel.
Use the fixed background \(B=\operatorname{diag}(W_m,0_m)\).
All its raw entries are \(0\) or \(\pm\sqrt2\), strictly inside the chosen support.
Its normalized eigenvalue multiplicities at \((-1,0,1)\) are \((1/4,1/2,1/4)\), and its norm is one.
In the nonzero block let \(I\) be the indices whose first \(d\) bits vanish, ordered lexicographically.
For \(J=I^c\), one has \(B_{II}=2^{-d/2}W_k\) and \(B_{JI}^*B_{JI}=(1-2^{-d})I_k\).

<a id="positive-volume-roots"></a>
## A positive-volume set of roots

Write \(h=x+iy\), \(H=[x,y]\), and give the complex unit sphere its normalized rotation-invariant surface measure in \(\mathbb R^{2N}\).
Let \(P=[H,BH,B^2H]\), \(D=[H,BH]\), and let \(S\) be the final \(\lfloor k/10\rfloor\) sites in \(I\).
There exist constants \(c_d,C_d>0\) such that a set of sphere measure \(1-o(1)\) has all three properties
\[
 P^TP\succeq c_d I_6,\qquad D_S^TD_S\succeq c_d I_4,\qquad
 \max_{0\le r\le3}\max_{j,\ell}|(B^r H)_{j\ell}|
 \le C_d\sqrt{\log N/N}.
\]

Indeed take independent standard real Gaussian vectors \(g_1,g_2\), put \(T^2=\|g_1\|^2+\|g_2\|^2\), and take \(H=[g_1,g_2]/T\).
Then \(T^2/(2N)\to1\) in probability.
For any deterministic real \(N\)-by-\(N\) matrix \(U\) of norm at most one, \(N^{-1}g_i^TUg_j\) differs from \(\delta_{ij}\operatorname{Tr}(U)/N\) by a quantity tending to zero in probability.
Independence and the Gaussian fourth moments bound its variance by \(2/N\), using the symmetric part when \(i=j\).
Apply this to the finitely many products \(B^r B^s\) in the first Gram matrix.
Its limiting form on the three powers is half the moment Gram of \(1,t,t^2\) for the displayed three-point spectral measure, tensored with the two-dimensional identity.
This is positive definite since a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two cannot vanish at all three points.
For \(D_S^T D_S\) the scalar trace inputs are \(|S|/N\), \(\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf1_S B)/N=O(N^{-1/2})\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf1_S B^2)/N=|S|/N\), using the coordinate projector \(\mathbf1_S\).
The limiting Gram is a positive scalar multiple of \(I_4\).
Each coordinate of \(B^r g_i\) has variance at most one, so a Gaussian tail bound, a union over \(8N\) tests and \(T\ge\sqrt N\) give the last property.

<a id="symmetric-interpolant"></a>
## Real symmetric realization at an arbitrary bounded parameter

Fix \(R>0\) and choose any \(h\) from the preceding sphere set.
For \(z=u+iv\), \(|z|\le R\), put
\[
 J_z=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\-v&u\end{pmatrix},\qquad S_0=H^TH,\qquad S_1=H^TBH.
\]
Let \(K\) solve \(2K+K^T=S_0J_z\).
This real linear equation is invertible, with multipliers three and one on the symmetric and skew parts respectively.
Define
\[
 Q=P(P^TP)^{-1}
       \begin{bmatrix}0\\ K\\ S_1 J_z/2\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad Y=HJ_z-2BQ,\qquad C=[Q,Y].
\]
Then \(H^TQ=0\), \((BH)^TQ=K\), and \((B^2H)^TQ=S_1J_z/2\).
Consequently \(H^TY=S_0J_z-2K=K^T\) and \((BH)^TY=0\), so \(D^TC\) is real symmetric.
With \(D^\dagger=(D^TD)^{-1}D^T\), set
\[
 A=C D^\dagger+(C D^\dagger)^T-(D^\dagger)^T(D^TC)D^\dagger .
\]
This is real symmetric and \(AD=C\) by \(D^\dagger D=I_4\) and \(C^T D=D^TC\).
In particular \(AH=Q\) and \(ABH+2BAH=Y+2BQ=HJ_z\), which is the exact complex equation \((AB+2BA)h=zh\).
It enforces both actual graph images and every exterior projection of this equation.

The coefficient matrices forming \(Q\) from \(P\) and \(Y\) from \(H,BH,B^2H,B^3H\) have bounded norms depending only on \(R,d\).
All these matrices have a fixed number of columns.
Every row of \(C,D\) thus has norm \(O_{R,d}(\sqrt{\log N/N})\).
Moreover \(\|(D^TD)^{-1}\|\le1/c_d\), and \(\|D^TC\|=O_{R,d}(1)\).
The displayed symmetric extension has entries \(O_{R,d}(\log N/N)\) and bounded operator norm.
For large \(N\) all entries of \(\sqrt N A\) are strictly inside the uniform raw support simultaneously for these \(h,z\).
The choice of \(A\) can depend on \(h,z\).
Since the input acts in the ordinary product support, one may approximate either matrix to any positive entry precision with positive probability at each fixed dimension; this supplies no quantitative lower probability at growing \(N\).

## Concentration and covering cost with the same fixed color

For this exact root the four real coefficient vectors for a fresh interior row form the rows of \(D\) on \(I\).
The Gram lower bound on \(S\) gives a four-site minor of determinant at least \(c'_dN^{-2}\) in absolute value.
Indeed the sum of the squared four-site minors is \(\det(D_S^TD_S)\ge c_d^4\) and there are at most \(N^4\) such minors.
Every tail starting at \(i\le p=k-\lfloor k/10\rfloor\) contains those four sites.
Condition on all other independent fresh coordinates.
The remaining four uniform real scalars have joint density \((2\sqrt3)^{-4}\) on their box, and applying the selected minor bounds their image density by \(C_dN^2\).
The volume of a real four-dimensional ball now bounds each original fresh concentration function by \(C_dN^2t^4\) at radius \(t\), uniformly in its center.
Thus for any fixed \(C_1>0\) and \(s\) with \((4s-2)\cdot 9/10>C_1\), at \(t=N^{-s}\) the product over the prescribed prefix is strictly less than \(N^{-C_1 k}\) for sufficiently large \(N\).
This includes the order of parameters in [C194v35](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v35.md#statement).

A Euclidean ball of radius \(r<1/2\) centered at a unit vector meets the real unit sphere in a cap of surface measure at most \(2(Cr)^{2N-1}\) for a universal \(C\).
For example the cap projects to the tangent coordinate ball of radius \(r\) with surface Jacobian at most two, and the ratio of its unit-ball tangent volume to total sphere area is bounded by a universal constant.
A root dictionary using radius \(r=bN^{-a}\), with \(a,b>0\) fixed, therefore needs \(N^{(2a-o(1))N}\) centers to cover the preceding sphere set for this one \(B\) and one \(z\).
This bound still applies if the dictionary also uses the fixed \(z\).
At each fixed choice of the structured-class constants of [C186v29](../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v29.md#statement), the ambient sphere measure of that class itself tends to zero.
Indeed on \(\lceil\gamma N\rceil\) selected sites its \(N^{-1/2}\) covering uses at most \(N^{[1-\gamma(1-\alpha)+o(1)]N}\) centers after covering the unrestricted sites and normalizing, as in the [partial-alphabet entropy proof](../claims/Yin_C_F0194_v33.md#partial-alphabet).
The same cap bound at radius \(C N^{-1/2}\) shows that their total measure tends to zero.
Consequently the fixed-color entropy lower bound persists after requiring that the particular realized root is outside this class.

This calculation rules out a deterministic support-wide cover of all such low-product roots at fine precision by a family with an entropy exponent independent of that precision.
It uses one prescribed color with spectral atoms, and the realizing other color varies with the root.
It neither forces the event \(F_N(z)^c\), which concerns every possible near-mode of each realized pair, nor describes a set of input pairs with a lower probability independent of dimension.
Counts depending on the exterior of \(A\), estimates for typical random \(B\), and the Brown convergence question remain open to the methods indicated in the [current planar criterion](../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v29.md#statement).
