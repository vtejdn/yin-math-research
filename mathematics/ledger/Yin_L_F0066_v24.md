# Atomic coefficient tests for the repeated interior determinant

For an original real Wigner block \(U=A_{II}\) of size \(k\), write \(P_W(U)=\det(I_2\otimes U-W)\).
The [conditional determinant](../drafts/Yin_D_F0042.md#repeated-interior) supplies an exterior-measurable \(W=W(z)\) wherever the cross block has full column rank and the exterior compression has no eigenvalue at \(z\).
Fix this exterior, fix \(z\) in that domain, and allow the diagonal of \(U\) to be disclosed.
The calculations apply to every deterministic complex \(W\) of size \(2k\), without a norm premise.
All strict upper entries of \(U\) have the original normalization \(\xi_e/\sqrt N\), with distinct raw variables independent and of fixed nondegenerate real law.
The following exact coefficients and one-sided probability bounds were checked in ordinary exploration.

<a id="octet-coefficient"></a>
## A squarefree coefficient surviving a two-point law

Take \(k=8\ell\), partition the selected sites into \(\ell\) sets of eight, and split each into two labeled halves of four.
Retain all sixteen cross edges in each octet and denote their union by \(E\), with \(|E|=16\ell=2k\).
Condition also on every strict upper coordinate outside \(E\).
For any fixed complex target \(w\) the coefficient of the squarefree raw monomial \(\prod_{e\in E}\xi_e\) in \(P_W-w\) equals
\[
 c_N=576^\ell N^{-k}.
\]
Indeed the total degree is at most \(2k\) and its homogeneous part of that degree on these coordinates is the square of the determinant of the hollow matrix supported on \(E\).
The retained normalized matrix on each octet has shape \(N^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&X\\X^T&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(X\) the four-by-four raw cross array.
The contribution to the square is \(N^{-8}\det(X)^4\).
A determinant term for each of the four factors of \(\det X\) selects a permutation of four symbols.
The required squarefree term is present exactly when those four permutations, arranged as rows, give a Latin square of order four, and its contribution is the product of their signs.
Permuting the four symbols puts the first row in order without changing that product, since its sign occurs four times.
Permuting the final three rows next puts the first column in order and preserves the product.
There are \(4!\,3!\) inverse choices for each reduced square.
Labeling symbols \(0,1,2,3\), the four reduced squares have first row \(0123\) and the following remaining rows.
\[
\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1032&2301&3210& (+,+,+)\\
1032&2310&3201& (+,-,-)\\
1230&2301&3012& (-,+,-)\\
1302&2013&3210& (-,-,+).
\end{array}
\]
Here the three signs are those of the displayed permutations.
The enumeration follows by filling the second row without a fixed point, starting with symbol \(1\), and then completing the two remaining rows column by column with leading symbols \(2,3\).
The sign product is \(+1\) in all four cases, giving coefficient \(4\cdot4!\cdot3!=576\).

For the fair sign law this is the full parity coefficient on the \(2k\) selected signs after any fixed outside assignment.
Reduction modulo \(\xi_e^2=1\) cannot add any other term to the full parity character, since the degree budget is already exhausted by its \(2k\) distinct coordinates.
In particular the conditional root mean square of \(P_W-w\) is at least \(c_N\) for every \(w\).
The estimate keeps the same real entry in the two channel blocks throughout.

For any nondegenerate original law choose \(\delta,p>0\) with \(\Pr\{|\xi-\xi'|\ge\delta\}\ge p\) for independent copies.
Generate each retained raw entry by a fair choice between two independent draws, ordered by value.
The output law of each chosen draw is the original law, including when the draws coincide.
With probability at least \(p^{2k}\), all pair gaps exceed or equal \(\delta\).
Conditional on the sampled pairs, the affine substitution of each selected variable by its midpoint plus its half-gap times a fair sign gives full parity coefficient \(c_N\prod_{e\in E}|\xi_e-\xi'_e|/2^{2k}\).
Only the squarefree coefficient can contribute because of the same degree budget.
The maximum of \(|P_W-w|\) over the selector cube is at least the absolute value of that coefficient, and any given vertex has probability \(2^{-2k}\).
Consequently
\[
  \Pr\!\left\{|P_W-w|\ge 576^{k/8}N^{-k}(\delta/2)^{2k}
                 \mid W,\ (U_e)_{e\notin E}\right\}
  \ge (p/2)^{2k}.
  \tag{Octet-hit}
\]
For a block with prescribed asymptotic fraction one may take \(k=8\lfloor\lambda N/8\rfloor\) in every dimension where it is positive.
The estimate on repeated independent interior samples follows by multiplying their failure probabilities with the same conditioned exterior and fixed remaining coordinates.
For \(L\) such samples the probability that none attains the indicated modulus is at most \((1-(p/2)^{2k})^L\).
This bound does not amplify the success probability for the original single interior.

If the entire strict upper triangle is left fresh, the squarefree score can be tested on many different octet partitions simultaneously.
For \(k=8\ell\) there are
\[
  M_k=\frac{k!}{(8!)^\ell\,\ell!}\,35^\ell
\]
distinct edge sets of the indicated form, since each graph recovers its octet components and each unordered split of a component into two halves gives a different \(K_{4,4}\).
Let \(g(\xi)\) be a bounded real score with \(\mathbb Eg=0\), \(\mathbb E g^2=1\), and \(c=\mathbb E[\xi g(\xi)]>0\).
Such a score is a standardized upper-threshold indicator separating two sets of positive probability with different conditional means.
The products of \(g\) over the edges of different graphs are orthonormal.
Pairing against one of them forces every selected edge to occur and thus gives \(c^{2k}576^{k/8}N^{-k}\) by the total degree budget.
The required pairings exist under the original fourth moment because no edge occurs to power exceeding four and the score is bounded.
Consequently the root mean square is at least \(M_k^{1/2}c^{2k}576^{k/8}N^{-k}\), with the inequality also valid if that norm is infinite.
For \(k\ge\lambda N\), the factorial formula gives \(M_k^{1/2}\ge\exp(-Ck)k^{7k/16}\).
For a fixed law on two distinct points all coordinate probabilities are bounded below by a positive constant.
The [finite-product fourth moment argument](#three-support-exponential-scale) below uses only this atom bound and the fact that every polynomial monomial has at most \(2k\) active coordinates.
It therefore shows, conditional on the exterior and the diagonal, that for all sufficiently large \(k=8\ell\ge\lambda N\),
\[
  \Pr\{|P_W-w|\ge N^{-9k/16}\exp(-C_\lambda k)\}
       \ge\exp(-C_\lambda k).
  \tag{Two-point-many-octets}
\]

## A second orthogonal component for laws on at least three points

Take even \(k=2m\) with \(m\ge2\) and fix two sets of \(m\) physical coordinates.
Condition on both within-set squares, including all diagonal coordinates, and leave the full cross rectangle fresh.
Let \(G\) be the union of two disjoint perfect matchings in the bipartite rectangle.
It is a simple graph consisting of even cycles with \(2m=k\) edges altogether.
Write \(b(G)\) for its number of cycles.
The coefficient of \(\prod_{e\in G}\xi_e^2\) in the full polynomial \(P_W-w\) is
\[
 d_G=N^{-k}6^{b(G)} .
\]
To see this, keep only the supported variables at their total degree \(2k\).
The same normalization as above gives \(N^{-k}\det(X_G)^4\) on the raw \(m\)-by-\(m\) bipartite array \(X_G\).
For a cycle with \(j\) sites on either side, its bipartite determinant has the two matching monomials, with relative sign \((-1)^{j-1}\).
The fourth power contributes the desired coefficient \(\binom42=6\), since each of the two terms must occur twice.
Multiplication over disjoint cycles gives the asserted value including its sign.

Suppose \(\xi\) has finite fourth moment and its support contains at least three distinct values.
There exists a real bounded score \(g(\xi)\) satisfying \(\mathbb E g=\mathbb E[\xi g]=0\), \(\mathbb E g^2=1\), and \(\gamma=\mathbb E[\xi^2g]>0\).
For a direct construction take bounded disjoint small neighborhoods of three distinct support points, each of positive mass.
The three vectors of conditional moments of orders \(0,1,2\) on these neighborhoods are linearly independent when the neighborhoods are sufficiently small, by continuity of the three-point Vandermonde determinant.
Choose a score constant on each neighborhood and zero elsewhere, with the first two moment pairings zero and the third nonzero.
Its sign and normalization give \(\gamma>0\).

The products \(\psi_G=\prod_{e\in G}g(\xi_e)\) are orthonormal as \(G\) varies over distinct such graphs.
A raw monomial can have nonzero pairing with \(\psi_G\) only when every edge of \(G\) appears at least twice.
Its degree is at most \(2k\), so the only surviving pairing is \(\mathbb E[(P_W-w)\psi_G]=d_G\gamma^k\), still conditionally on the disclosed data.
The pairing is integrable because every single edge affects at most four rows across the two copies, so no raw upper variable occurs to power greater than four in the determinant.
The bounded scores add no moment requirement.
There are at least \((m!)^2/(3\cdot2^m)\) candidate graphs.
Indeed for each first matching there are at least \(m!/3\) derangements for a disjoint second matching, by the alternating inclusion-exclusion series for \(m\ge2\), and a graph admits at most \(2^m\) ordered decompositions by its independent cycle choices.
By orthogonality, with the root mean square understood as an extended positive quantity,
\[
 \left(\mathbb E |P_W-w|^2\right)^{1/2}
    \ge N^{-k}\gamma^k\,\frac{m!}{\sqrt{3\cdot2^m}}
\]
under the same conditioning.
If the second moment is infinite the inequality is immediate, and otherwise it follows by Bessel's inequality.
For \(k\) proportional to \(N\) this lower bound is at least \(N^{-k/2}\exp(-Ck)\).
The score with two vanishing moments exists exactly because this law admits a quadratic component outside the affine span of \(1,\xi\).
For a two-point law every function of the raw variable is affine on its support, so the squarefree octet calculation supplies the separate certificate above.

## An exponential modulus threshold from three support points

There is a stronger threshold when the raw off-diagonal law has at least three distinct support points.
For fixed \(\lambda>0\) and all sufficiently large even \(k=2m\ge\lambda N\), under the same rectangle conditioning, it gives
\[
  \Pr\{|P_W-w|\ge\exp(-C_\lambda k)\}
      \ge \exp(-C_\lambda k).
  \tag{Three-point-hit}
\]
The constants depend only on \(\lambda\) and the fixed entry law.
No boundedness assumption on the rest of the support is needed.

Choose three bounded intervals around distinct support points, pairwise separated by \(\delta>0\) and each having positive mass.
Choose \(q>0\) strictly smaller than the least of these three masses and with \(3q<1\).
Write the entry law as a mixture of each of the three interval-conditional laws with weight \(q\) and a remaining probability law with weight \(1-3q\), by subtracting these three subprobabilities.
Independently for each raw rectangle entry, draw a representative from each of the four component laws.
Conditional on all representatives, select the entry from these values with their stated weights independently across sites.
The original law is recovered after averaging.
Every conditional coordinate law has at least three separated nodes and each nonzero atom has mass at least \(p_*=\min(q,1-3q)>0\), including any coinciding fourth value.
All conditional estimates below are uniform in the representatives.

In \(L^2\) of each conditional coordinate, orthonormalize \(1,x,x^2\) in that order.
Denote the resulting real orthonormal polynomials by \(g_{e,j}\), taking \(g_{e,0}=1\) and positive leading coefficients.
For \(1\le j\le2\), write \(\gamma_{e,j}=\mathbb E[x^j g_{e,j}(x)]\), the norm of the monic orthogonal polynomial of degree \(j\).
Every real monic polynomial \(p\) of degree \(j\) has modulus at least \(\delta^j/(j+1)\) at one of any \(j+1\) of the separated nodes.
Indeed Lagrange interpolation expresses its leading coefficient \(1\) as the sum of its values at these nodes divided by products of \(j\) node differences.
It follows that \(\gamma_{e,j}\ge\sqrt{p_*}\delta^j/(j+1)\).
Fix \(0<b\le1\) smaller than both of these bounds.

Writing \(X\) for the \(m\)-by-\(m\) raw cross array, the component of highest possible total degree of \(P_W-w\) in \(X\) is \(N^{-k}\det(X)^4\).
Define \(a_\nu\) by \(\det(X)^4=\sum_\nu a_\nu X^\nu\), where \(\sum_e\nu_e=4m\) and \(0\le\nu_e\le4\).
Let \(\mathcal S\) consist of the indicated multiindices for which every \(\nu_e\le2\).
The products \(\prod_e g_{e,\nu_e}(X_e)\) are orthonormal over \(\mathcal S\).
If a monomial has nonzero pairing with one such product, every one of its site exponents must be at least \(\nu_e\), so only the matching monomial survives the total degree bound.
Bessel's inequality gives
\[
 \|P_W-w\|_2^2
 \ge N^{-2k} b^{8m}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal S}|a_\nu|^2
\]
conditionally on all representatives and the disclosed input data.

To estimate the deterministic coefficient sum use an auxiliary matrix \(G\) of independent standard circular complex Gaussian scalars, normalized by \(\mathbb E|G_{ij}|^2=1\).
Polar integration gives \(\mathbb E[G_{ij}^r\overline{G_{ij}}^{\,s}]=\mathbf1_{r=s}r!\).
Thus \(\mathbb E|\det G|^2=m!\) by expansion in permutations and
\[
 \sum_\nu |a_\nu|^2\prod_e\nu_e!
     =\mathbb E|\det G|^8
     \ge (\mathbb E|\det G|^2)^4=(m!)^4 .
\]
The inequality is Jensen's for the fourth power of \(|\det G|^2\).
Denote the full Gaussian coefficient sum by \(S_m\).
To restrict its sum to \(\mathcal S\), fix row \(i\) and let \(c=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)\) be its cofactor vector, determined by the other \(m-1\) rows.
Set \(H=\sum_j |c_j|^2\).
For the representative edge \(e=(i,j)\), write \(\det G=c_jG_{ij}+b_0\) where \(b_0=\sum_{s\ne j}c_sG_{is}\).
Conditional on the other rows, \(b_0\) is circular complex Gaussian with variance \(H-|c_j|^2\).
Since \(\mathbb E|\det G|^8=4!\,\mathbb EH^4\), polar orthogonality shows that the share of \(S_m\) carried by indices with \(\nu_e\ge3\) is
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\!\left[H^4\sum_{n=3}^4
             \binom4n T^n(1-T)^{4-n}\right]}{\mathbb EH^4},
 \qquad T=\frac{|c_j|^2}{H}.
\]
Indeed the term with \(G_{ij}^n\) has coefficient \(\binom4n c_j^n b_0^{4-n}\), whose squared norm times \(n!\) supplies exactly the displayed contribution.
Almost surely \(H>0\) and the cofactor vector spans the complex kernel of the \((m-1)\)-by-\(m\) matrix of undeleted rows.
Its squared norm \(H\) is unchanged by right unitary multiplication of these rows, by Cauchy--Binet, and their Gaussian law is invariant under that action.
Consequently the projective direction of \(c\) is uniform conditional on \(H\).
Indeed any fixed unitary acts on that line by its inverse while preserving \(H\), and averaging over the compact unitary group sends each initial line to the same uniform distribution.
The squared coordinate of the uniform line has the law of one exponential variable divided by the sum of \(m\) independent mean-one exponential variables, using a normalized complex Gaussian vector.
For \(m\ge2\) its density is \((m-1)(1-t)^{m-2}\) on \([0,1]\), as follows by changing variables to the ratio and sum of an exponential and an independent sum of \(m-1\) exponentials.
In particular \(\mathbb ET^3=6/[m(m+1)(m+2)]\).
The displayed share is at most \(5\mathbb ET^3\).
The union over \(m^2\) choices of edge thus loses at most \(30m/[(m+1)(m+2)]\) of \(S_m\).
For large \(m\), the indices in \(\mathcal S\) carry at least \(S_m/2\).
On \(\mathcal S\) one has \(\prod_e\nu_e!\le2^{4m}\).
Consequently the conditional norm is at least \(N^{-k}b^{4m}2^{-2m-1/2}(m!)^2\).
Since \(m!\ge(m/e)^m\) and \(k=2m\ge\lambda N\), this is at least \(\exp(-C_\lambda k)\).

For completeness, a fourth moment comparison on the conditional finite product converts this norm to a probability without dimension-dependent loss.
On each one-site probability space let \(T_\rho f=\mathbb Ef+\rho(f-\mathbb Ef)\).
For real \(f=m_0+g\) with \(\mathbb Eg=0\), put \(v=\mathbb Eg^2\) and use \(\|g\|_\infty\le p_*^{-1/2}\sqrt v\).
Expansion gives
\[
 \mathbb E(m_0+\rho g)^4
 \le m_0^4+(6\rho^2+2\rho^3p_*^{-1/2})m_0^2 v
             +(2\rho^3p_*^{-1/2}+\rho^4p_*^{-1})v^2.
\]
Here \(|m_0|v^{3/2}\le(m_0^2v+v^2)/2\) controls the cubic term.
Choose \(0<\rho<1\) depending only on \(p_*\) so that the two displayed coefficients are at most \(2,1\), respectively.
It follows that \(\|T_\rho f\|_4\le\|f\|_2\), also for complex \(f\), by positivity of \(T_\rho\) and application to \(|f|\).
This inequality tensorizes to independent coordinates by applying the one-coordinate estimates successively and the mixed norm inequality \(\|h\|_{L^4_y(L^2_x)}\le\|h\|_{L^2_x(L^4_y)}\), which follows by Minkowski applied to \(|h|^2\).
On the tensor product of orthogonal constants and centered one-site bases, the product operator multiplies a term supported on \(s\) coordinates by \(\rho^s\).
The polynomial \(f=P_W-w\) uses at most \(4m\) coordinates in each monomial.
Therefore its orthogonal expansion also has at most \(4m\) active coordinates per term.
Rescaling each term by \(\rho^{-s}\) gives \(f_0\) with product operator image \(f\) and \(\|f_0\|_2\le\rho^{-4m}\|f\|_2\).
Hence \(\|f\|_4\le\rho^{-4m}\|f\|_2\).
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz on \(\{|f|^2\ge\|f\|_2^2/2\}\) gives probability at least \(\rho^{16m}/4\).
The conditional norm bound and averaging over representatives prove (Three-point-hit).
The Gaussian computation evaluates fixed coefficients in a formal polynomial and introduces no perturbation of the original matrix law.

## The scale left by these tests

On the actual exterior of the conditional determinant these estimates concern the whole sampled polynomial, so they allow every candidate singular direction to depend on \(U\).
For target zero, (Octet-hit) bounds the success probability for a negative logarithm no larger than \(k\log N+O(k)\) below by \(\exp(-O(k))\).
It leaves the failure probability as large as \(1-\exp(-O(k))\), and the cycle second moment does not bound that failure probability.
With three support points, (Three-point-hit) reaches an order-\(N\) logarithmic threshold but still supplies a success probability that may decrease exponentially.
Control of the complementary lower tail on a single interior at the required original-law probability remains open for the [full-sequence planar criterion](../claims/Yin_C_F0186_v29.md#statement).
