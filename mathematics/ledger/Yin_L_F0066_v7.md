## The retained common-factor atom has a large full numerator ratio

Use the exact background and law of [L_F0066_v6](Yin_L_F0066_v6.md#allowed-background), with
\[
 a=\sqrt{2/N},\quad m=N-1,\quad
 A_0=0,\quad B_0=C_0=aI_m,\quad b=c=0,
\]
and retain every possible first-input diagonal and border:
\[
 W_1=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&w^T\\w&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \alpha\in\mathbb R,\quad w\in\mathbb R^m.
\]
The following exact-atom calculation does not require setting \(\alpha,w\) to zero or bounding them.
Put
\[
 q=x^Ty,\quad r=w^Tx,\quad t=w^Ty,\quad n_w=\|w\|^2.
\]
The product \(W_1BC\) has image contained in the span of the first coordinate and \((0,w)^T\).
A rank-two determinant expansion, valid also at \(w=0\), reduces its characteristic factor to the matrix
\[
 J=\begin{pmatrix}
  \alpha q+at&\alpha ar+rt+a^2n_w\\
  q&ar
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For example, put \(\widetilde w=(0,w)^T\) and write \(W_1BC=UV^T\) with
\[
 U=(e_1,\widetilde w),\qquad
 V^T=
 \begin{pmatrix}\alpha e_1^T+\widetilde w^T\\ e_1^T\end{pmatrix}BC;
\]
then \(V^TU=J\).
Equivalently, direct multiplication of the four blocks of \(BC\) gives the same two-by-two matrix.
Its trace and determinant yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \det(W_1BC-zI_N)&=(-z)^{N-2}p_q(z),\\
 p_q(z)&=z^2-[\alpha q+a(r+t)]z
             -qa^2n_w-(q-a^2)rt.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{Joint-probe-full-numerator}
\]

On the common-factor atom \(q=a^2\),
\[
 p_{a^2}(z)
 =z^2-[\alpha a^2+a(r+t)]z-a^4n_w.
\]
This real quadratic has two real roots, since its discriminant is
\([\alpha a^2+a(r+t)]^2+4a^4n_w\ge0\).
Hence
\[
 |p_{a^2}(z)|\ge|\operatorname{Im}z|^2.
 \tag{Joint-probe-nonreal-exclusion}
\]
This proves real spectrum of this particular rank-two product on the atom, not a real-spectrum claim for a general three-factor product.

The normalization is exactly the one required by the many-index event.
On the full-factor invertible and nonzero-adjugate branch, the identities
\(\det B\det C=s_Bs_Ca^{2m}\) and
\(\mathcal A=s_Bs_C T_i\) give
\[
 R_i
 =\frac{|\det(W_1BC-zI_N)|}{a^{2m}\|\mathcal A\|_F}.
\]
On \(q=a^2\), use the [exact nonzero denominator](Yin_L_F0066_v6.md#normalization-and-guards), with
\(\gamma_N=(-z)^{m-1}a^{-2m}\), to obtain
\[
 R_i
 =\frac{|p_{a^2}(z)|}
        {a^4\sqrt{m+2(k_xk_y-1)}}
 \ge\frac{|\operatorname{Im}z|^2}{4\sqrt3}\,N.
 \tag{Joint-probe-atom-ratio}
\]
Here \(k_x,k_y\le m\), \(a^4=4/N^2\), and
\(\sqrt{m+2(k_xk_y-1)}\le\sqrt3\,m\).
All factors \(|z|^{N-2}\) have cancelled rather than being treated as an absolute determinant scale.
Thus the simultaneous-zero graph atom is disjoint from the chosen index's tiny-ratio event for every first real border and diagonal, once the threshold is \(o(N)\).
This is a statement about that chosen index and that background, not a replacement for the general many-index argument.

## A stable conditional exponential bound on this rare background

Return now to the fixed three-point laws of L_F0066_v6, so
\[
 |\alpha|\le a,\qquad \|x\|,\|y\|,\|w\|\le\sqrt2.
\]
With \(r,t,n_w,\alpha\) unchanged, subtract the real-root quadratic just used from the general numerator:
\[
 p_q(z)-p_{a^2}(z)
 =-(q-a^2)(\alpha z+a^2n_w+rt).
\]
For \(N\ge3\) the modulus of the parenthesis is at most \(6+|z|\).
Set
\[
 \delta_z=\min\left\{1,\,
          \frac{|\operatorname{Im}z|^2}{2(6+|z|)}\right\}>0.
\]
Then \(|q-a^2|\le\delta_z\) implies
\(|p_q(z)|\ge|\operatorname{Im}z|^2/2\), uniformly in every first border and diagonal in their original support.
The full cofactor formula of L_F0066_v6 also gives
\[
 \frac{\|\mathcal A\|_F}{|\gamma_N|}
 \le |q|\|x\|\|y\|+|q|a^2\sqrt m+a^2\|x\|\|y\|
 \le8,
\]
using \(|q|\le2\), \(a^2\le1\) and \(a^2\sqrt m\le1\).
Consequently, on the branch where the actual ratio is defined,
\[
 |q-a^2|\le\delta_z
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 R_i\ge|\operatorname{Im}z|^2/16.
 \tag{Joint-probe-stable-ratio}
\]

Under the background \(\Gamma_N\) of L_F0066_v6, write
\[
 q=\frac2N\sum_{j=1}^m Z_j,\qquad
 \mathbb P(Z_j=0)=\frac34,\quad
 \mathbb P(Z_j=1)=\mathbb P(Z_j=-1)=\frac18,
\]
with independent \(Z_j\).
Their moment generating function satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E e^{\theta Z_j}
 =\frac34+\frac14\cosh\theta\le e^{\theta^2/2}.
\]
For \(N\ge4/\delta_z\), the event \(|q-a^2|>\delta_z\) forces
\(\left|\sum_jZ_j\right|>\delta_zN/4\).
The exponential Markov inequality, optimized at that threshold, therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{|q-a^2|>\delta_z\mid\Gamma_N\}
 \le2\exp(-\delta_z^2N/32).
\]
Combining this with (Joint-probe-stable-ratio) proves the concrete conditional estimate
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
  B,C\text{ invertible},\ T_i\ne0,\
  R_i\le|\operatorname{Im}z|^2/32
       \ \middle|\ \Gamma_N\right\}
 \le2e^{-\delta_z^2N/32}.
 \tag{Joint-probe-rare-background-estimate}
\]
It remains valid after fixing any allowed first border and diagonal, because the deterministic estimate was uniform in them and they are independent of \(x,y\).
No low-matching condition is needed for this bound.
It is a stronger explanation of why the retained bad-graph atom is harmless to the small-numerator event, but it concerns the exponentially rare background \(\Gamma_N\), not typical Wigner backgrounds.
