## Mechanism and missing bridge

For \(G=A*_C B\) with known torsion-free Atiyah vertex groups, investigate the \(*\)-regular closure amalgam and its \(\operatorname{Tor}_1\) obstruction from [SP25, Conjecture 7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12268v2#S7).
The [survey's infinite-edge probe](../ledger/Yin_L_A0001_v1.md#infinite-edge-probe) fixes the ring, coefficient field and unresolved implication.
The published finite-edge theorem supplies a baseline; in a torsion-free graph of groups those edges are trivial.
The [current main-route reassessment](Yin_S_A0004.md#strategic-reassessment) gives the index-two relation test priority because this route still lacks a selected edge condition addressing its Tor obstruction and subsequent analytic-closure comparison.
The available source audits do not close either obligation, so merely reformulating the Tor conjecture is not a reason to run this route in parallel.

The [KLS17 audit](../audits/literature/KLS17/Yin_U001.md#why-condition-3-has-not-been-cleared) reinforces the need to verify this exactness interface: an equality in G_0 is not an available stable module isomorphism, and induction to the affiliated algebra needs its own justification on the relevant exact sequences.
Finite-subgroup projective generation with a separately known semisimple target is usable, but supplies no replacement for the infinite-edge Tor or dimension-flatness argument.

## First discriminating step

Start with \(C\cong\mathbb Z\) and concrete embeddings into the vertex groups.
Inspect the finite-edge proof of SP25, Lemma 3.2, to isolate the argument that uses finite-dimensional edge spaces.
Compare a flatness or universal-localization replacement with a rank-metric analytic replacement.
State separately whether the target is exact Tor vanishing or only vanishing of its von Neumann dimension, and verify which conclusion would imply the required rank property.
Screen proposed examples against locally indicable, class \(\mathcal C\), virtually special and other applicable published cases.

## Decision conditions and contribution

Continue when a verifiable condition on the edge embeddings removes the identified obstruction.
A proof for an already covered example is a mechanism test; a new permanence statement needs an example or scope comparison showing what it adds.
Pause a formulation that merely restates the Tor conjecture without an approach to its vanishing or the subsequent analytic-closure comparison.
Even a successful cyclic-edge theorem would leave arbitrary torsion-free groups outside the demonstrated construction.
