## Current structural investigation

On 12 November 2026, Yin directed the work toward the full problem or its amenable case through essential algebraic and analytic structure, without exploratory tests of concrete examples.
This supersedes the earlier immediate priority on particular trinomials, cycle classes and trial pencils; their proved mathematics remains retained below.
The [structural reassessment](../ledger/Yin_L_A0030_v1.md) starts from the known amenable Atiyah/domain equivalence and distinguishes the completed analytic localization from the unresolved scalar algebra.
Its [two-term index calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0030_v1.md#why-the-existing-finite-free-complex-has-no-usable-index) shows why the free complex defined by a scalar multiplier only cancels equal unknown kernel and cokernel masses.
The [projective-trace comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_A0030_v1.md#what-the-known-trace-theorem-sees) locates the missing input in the principal annihilator, since the integer trace of finitely generated projectives is already established for this group class.
The [finite projective resolution interface](../claims/Yin_C_A0001_v2.md#the-finite-projective-resolution-interface) gives an exact sufficient homological certificate, but its existence is not derived from the two group hypotheses.
The [rank comparison and representation-uniqueness checks](../ledger/Yin_L_A0030_v1.md#why-the-available-rank-comparison-does-not-close-the-problem) rule out using the selected sources to supply that missing premise without additional work.

The next useful step must establish a group-specific implication toward such a certificate or a direct exclusion of finite multiplicative cancellation.
The [bounded homological test](../ledger/Yin_L_A0030_v1.md#finite-cohomological-dimension-does-not-supply-finite-generation) separates a finite bound \(d=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb C}G\) from the \(FP_{d-2}\) property of the particular annihilator when \(d\ge2\).
The [syzygy tail recurrence](../ledger/Yin_L_A0030_v1.md#an-infinite-coherent-resolution-can-preserve-the-fractional-tail) shows that an infinite resolution by finite projectives can keep the same fractional mass indefinitely; one needs termination, vanishing tail dimensions, or another independent certificate.
The [projective-part test](../ledger/Yin_L_A0030_v1.md#a-nonzero-projective-part-would-already-suffice) offers the alternative of constructing a single nonzero finite projective submodule, whose existence remains unproved.
Another formulation of the same zero-divisor condition, normalized finite-rank convergence alone, or an arbitrary kernel encoding does not count as that step.
No general amenable solution or new group class is claimed.

The [analytic alternative](../claims/Yin_C_A0014_v2.md#bounded-coefficient-mass-suffices-without-norm-convergence) asks for a bounded coefficient-mass subsequence of explicit approximants instead of a finite algebraic resolution.

The [principal Banach annihilator calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_A0031_v2.md#principal-annihilators-and-local-projection-constants) shows that a hypothetical nonzero kernel forces an uncomplemented principal annihilator and divergent local projection constants.

The [complete KLS17 audit](../audits/literature/KLS17/Yin_U001.md#exact-torsion-free-use-and-remaining-research-obligations) distinguishes the existing group-ring projective trace theorem from generation of K_0 of the actual affiliated division closure.
Its projective criterion requires both semisimple Artinian structure and surjectivity from finite-subgroup projectives; torsion-freeness specializes the latter source to the coefficient field but establishes neither missing property.
The [amenable induction argument](../claims/Yin_C_A0015_v1.md#scope-for-the-torsion-free-problem) reduces a single nonzero scalar pivot to establishing \([D/aD]\in\mathbb Z[D]\) independently.
Finite generation of an annihilator alone supplies no such relation, and the [general source implication](../audits/literature/KLS17/Yin_U001.md#why-condition-3-has-not-been-cleared) remains unresolved.
The structural priority remains to produce a finite projective part or resolution for the individual kernel, or another independent certificate of its dimension, rather than restating the equivalent whole-closure criterion.
Adjoining arbitrary relative inverses or kernel projections to a larger regular ring does not prove the required generation or identify that ring with the actual division closure.

## Mechanism and obstruction

The [F0001 strategy comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_A0069_v1.md#what-transfers-from-the-f-tasks) selects a direct use of the normal-generator rationality criterion on a cyclic edge and a complementary [unitary deformation of the original regular operator](Yin_S_A0012.md#route) for overlapping coefficient groups.
For the first Schur step, that deformation preserves the desired trace on an infinite set of phases while yielding full corner pivots at a specialization.
It works for [repeated denominators with compatible upper entries and lower border](../claims/Yin_C_A0123_v1.md#the-finite-inverse-layer).
For all matrix sizes, the [cyclic-edge transfer](Yin_S_A0005.md#cyclic-edge-support-through-a-regular-group-action) instead uses relative Følner sets to prove rationality of the expected graph support.

For [Yin's inverse layers](../audits/literature/Yin20/Yin_U002.md#61-definition-iv55-and-all-parts-of-remark-iv56), write \(P_j(G)\) for scalar injectivity on all nonzero members of layer \(D_j(G)\).
Under \(P_0(G)\), invertibility of a nonzero \(d-ca^{-1}b\) is the rank-two question.
If the Schur scalar has rational rank \(p/q\) strictly between zero and one, [free cyclic extension](../claims/Yin_C_A0124_v1.md#the-kernel-projection-and-its-approximants) puts its exact kernel projection in \(D_{1+p}(G*\mathbb Z)\).
A direct sum with nullity at least one [loses a coordinate](../claims/Yin_C_A0124_v1.md#removing-a-coordinate-from-the-direct-sum) at the next layer, including at equality.
For irrational rank the same construction gives positive contractions fixing the kernel with arbitrarily small error in the rank metric.
The next group-specific issue is whether the original kernel projection, now known to be a bounded rational element upstairs in the rational case, can be obtained or excluded in the original group.
The [amplified comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_A0070_v1.md#a-finite-tracial-comparison-inside-one-factor) gives full inner rank at every multiplicity and inverse depth for a scalar-injective polynomial tuple and exhibits a separate failure of descent for an atomic scalar law.
At nullity one half, an [original-group test](../claims/Yin_C_A0125_v2.md#group-ring-witnesses-and-the-exact-projection) uses a positive three-by-three group-ring matrix for each finite sum of translations.
Under center-valued trace \(\tfrac12 1\), either one test is injective or the same family has arbitrarily small strictly positive nullities.
One injective test puts the projection and [algebraic matrix units](../claims/Yin_C_A0125_v2.md#algebraic-matrix-units-from-a-single-inverse) in the second inverse layer using a single additional Gram inverse.
In the free cyclic extension that conclusion holds for every original half-dimensional Schur kernel, making indivisibility of the unit class by two in the second layer a sufficient exclusion criterion.
In the original group, [finite-index or finite-coset conditions](../ledger/Yin_L_A0071_v1.md#central-fourier-coefficients-and-finite-index) give discrete test nullities or exclude a subgroup-supported kernel.
The [faithful nonregular representation of a free group ring](../claims/Yin_C_A0126_v1.md#why-the-kernel-projection-fails-to-descend) shows that torsion-freeness, scalar injectivity and the Schur form still admit a half-dimensional kernel missing from every original layer when the trace changes.
For a fixed support of the variable group-ring coefficient, [algebraic rank strata](../claims/Yin_C_A0128_v1.md#exact-and-positive-defects-on-a-fixed-support) give a constructibility criterion for a positive gap.
Across all supports, a hypothetical half kernel supplies [explicit shift polynomials](../claims/Yin_C_A0127_v1.md#the-fixed-schur-matrix) with strictly positive nullities tending to zero for the same matrix in the regular wreath product.
The [wreath-product scalar test](../ledger/Yin_L_A0072_v1.md#the-scalar-hypothesis-under-wreath-extension) locates the additional hypothesis precisely at the finite direct powers of the original group.
For parity, [odd stabilization](../claims/Yin_C_A0129_v1.md#odd-stabilization) reduces divisibility to anticommuting involutions in an odd matrix size over the second inverse layer.
The [comparison using a different trace](../claims/Yin_C_A0130_v1.md#the-two-behaviors-of-the-fixed-schur-kernel) simultaneously realizes strictly positive small defects, an exact exchange, and an even unit in a faithful scalar-injective representation.
When all the entries lie in a two-dimensional complex space, the [field generated by one coefficient ratio](../claims/Yin_C_A0116_v1.md#rational-functions-and-schur-complements) gives exact ranks in every size.
All Schur complements and their nonzero pivot inverses in this calculation stay within one additional inverse layer.
Every two-by-two input [reduces by polynomial diagonal operations](../claims/Yin_C_A0116_v1.md#reducing-every-two-by-two-input-to-three-coefficients) to three coefficients in its original layer.
The remaining scalar is \(1+(a^{-1}c)(a^{-1}b)\) with \(a,b,c\) linearly independent.
The finite tracial comparison gives rank one half already in this exact form, leaving the additional structure of the group trace as a necessary input for this approach.
For rows supported on freely placed factors, a [commutator with a projection on reduced words](../claims/Yin_C_A0111_v1.md#argument) extracts a scalar from a single original row whenever the predicted rank fails.
For arbitrary supports, a nonzero polynomial row and column with \(uAv=0\) [force the Schur complement to have integer rank](../claims/Yin_C_A0113_v1.md#argument).
An obstruction would consequently give two freely generating algebra elements in every nonzero group-ring row combination of \(A\).
Both integer ranks can occur when this method finds no such relation.

At any fixed inverse depth the [two-graph formula for affiliated ratios](../claims/Yin_C_A0115_v1.md#two-bounded-graph-rows) gives the first Schur step on freely placed row subgroups from the scalar hypothesis at that depth.
With one independent input having no eigenvalues, [successive fractional linear substitutions](../claims/Yin_C_A0115_v1.md#reusing-one-coefficient-algebra-through-arbitrary-inverse-depths) may reuse the same coefficient subalgebra through arbitrarily many inverse gates.
An independent torsion-free abelian direct factor [preserves each individual \(P_j\)](../claims/Yin_C_A0114_v1.md#the-rank-at-a-generic-phase-for-a-finite-inverse-expression).
If two free vertex groups share an additional central cyclic direct factor, the only nontrivial singular case for their two rows has [one common central rational coefficient ratio](../claims/Yin_C_A0114_v1.md#two-rows-sharing-the-central-cyclic-subgroup).
For unrestricted coefficients, the [two scalar nullities in a wreath product](../claims/Yin_C_A0112_v1.md#the-scalar-pencil-and-its-exact-nullities) detect partial kernels without raising the inverse depth.
The dyadic construction there reduces the general induction step to an independent lower bound on the positive nullities in the specified finite spans.
Moreover [one repeated scalar denominator](../claims/Yin_C_A0117_v1.md#rewriting-the-actual-inverses) suffices throughout any finite expression at the next layer.
The corresponding polynomial matrices have a single border around disjoint upper triangular chains with that scalar on the diagonal.
One occurrence gives exactly the two-by-two test; arbitrarily long chains already occur in the depth-one construction.
No such estimate follows here from \(P_j(G)\) alone.

Use [MSY23, Theorem 5.25](../audits/literature/MSY23/Yin_U005.md#theorem-525-the-complete-equivalence): integer analytic rank for all polynomial matrices in a bounded tuple is equivalent to its actual rational closure, or equivalently its division closure, in the affiliated algebra being a division ring.
The [survey's analytic probe](../ledger/Yin_L_A0001_v1.md#relation-sensitive-analytic-probe) specifies the trace convention and the direct specialization to a tuple of regular group generators and their inverses.
This criterion already supplies the analytic-algebraic bridge; no free-field realization or algebraic freeness is required.
The remaining problem is to prove that every nonzero element of the actual rational closure is invertible, using an analytic argument that respects the group's relations.
