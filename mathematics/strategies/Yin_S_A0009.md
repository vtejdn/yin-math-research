# Characteristic transfer through the integral product table

## Task associations

| Task | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| [Yin_T_A0002](../tasks/Yin_T_A0002_v1.md#contract) | Detect a complex zero product supported on subsets of a verified product table in a torsion-free group by its geometric coefficient equations and their reductions. |

## Route

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| disposition | active |
| priority | Test the actual residual elements of a fixed modular lift for a finite commutator orbit or sufficiently slow coset expansion, and check whether a sofic residual subgroup already excludes that lift. |

The [single-prime test](../claims/Yin_C_A0093_v1.md#statement) represents the projective coefficient problem by one integer matrix.
Its rational rank decides whether the two sets contain a complex zero product.
The rank over a prime field tests existence over its algebraic closure.
For a witness nonzero in every coordinate, all occupied degree blocks must lose rank.
At odd primes, signed elimination of the pair columns bounds each remaining determinant in terms of the number of surviving bipartite components.

Use these local sizes and column norms on the actual table to compare with its witness prime.
One rationally deficient block suffices.
The [exact-support transfer in MD26](../audits/literature/MD26/Yin_U001.md#proposition-83-and-its-quantifiers) and the [fixed-point lifting test](../claims/Yin_C_A0039_v1.md#statement) retain different support and specialization requirements.
The [characteristic-two strategy](Yin_S_A0008.md) supplies possible group constructions and erasure tests.
Application here needs the complete equal-product partition, or a sufficient system of verified same-product equalities, with distinct labels on each side and verified torsion-freeness.
When the given point must retain every label, the [exact-support analysis](../ledger/Yin_L_A0059_v1.md) supplies a separate arithmetic threshold and an odd-characteristic transfer for pair-only tables.
For an abstract feasible coefficient pattern, its partial transport maps can already force torsion.
Check their finite compositions before investing in a geometric realization.
The [one-fiber classification](../claims/Yin_C_A0026_v2.md#statement) gives the exact exceptional odd primes when the characteristic-zero torus has no point.
A nonzero centered reverse product and opposite-term pairings off one output suffice for a cyclotomic lift.
The MD26 right-inverse relation supplies the former condition automatically.
The [source application](../claims/Yin_C_A0026_v2.md#the-witnesses-in-mghirbi-douglas-section-75) also proves that the original signed occurrence lifts have at least two nonzero output coefficients divisible by \(p\).
The actual pairing test therefore concerns the collected coefficients.
The [dihedral comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_A0060_v1.md) exhibits a fixed-coefficient obstruction alongside freely chosen complex solutions with identical supports.

The [fixed-factor precision test](../claims/Yin_C_A0039_v2.md#statement) uses a single MD26 tuple and a single prime throughout.
Every integral lift \(A\) of its right-invertible factor has primitive finite annihilators modulo every \(p^e\).
A witness of actual size \(s\) with \(p^e>\|A\|_2^s\) forces a finite complex annihilator for that very \(A\).
For increasing \(e\), seek a bound on the smallest primitive support or on the integer minors of the connected support components.
Separate support of the correction sum after collection from the number of occurrences in its geometric-series expression.
The [translate-count bound](../claims/Yin_C_A0039_v3.md#independent-translates-and-amenable-cosets) permits even unbounded support inside each of \(o(e)\) amenable cosets, provided the reductions give one fixed nonzero residue.
For \(D=(1-AB)/p\), positive analytic kernel of \(A\) forces eigenvalue \(1/p\) on the subgroup generated by the support of \(D\); a sofic subgroup excludes it.
A [finite orbit under commutators](../claims/Yin_C_A0039_v3.md#a-finite-adjoint-orbit-test-for-an-exact-annihilator) gives an explicit integral annihilator with the required residue.
The [source-weight calculation and obstructions](../ledger/Yin_L_A0061_v1.md) show why a modular witness alone cannot guarantee compression for a chosen integral lift.
The [support bound for trace-zero idempotents](../claims/Yin_C_A0094_v1.md#statement) independently excludes bounded support of the reverse idempotent defects at unbounded precision, for any group.
The [normality route](Yin_S_A0010.md) also tests finite commutator orbits directly in the complex algebra.
The [residual representative test](../ledger/Yin_L_A0062_v1.md) changes the integral lift of the right inverse by \(pW\) and checks \(D-AW\) by an integer linear system on a proposed support.
A one-sided height with a separately injective bottom coefficient excludes an analytic kernel of the fixed left factor.
Applying this calculation to a source witness requires its collected group labels and products.
The [signed direct-power construction](Yin_S_A0011.md) supplies a converse characteristic transfer from a hypothetical complex zero product, with an explicit change of group.
For three distinct left labels, [integral elimination on the same group](../claims/Yin_C_A0101_v1.md#statement) preserves all left labels at every finite place, and bounds the entire joint row field by the square of the allowed right support size.
One may check a surviving row by exact computation over that field and test individual primes for a decrease on the right.
For arbitrary support sizes, first pass to a pair minimal over the algebraic closure in the characteristic being tested.
The [degree and grading bound](../claims/Yin_C_A0103_v1.md#statement) requires its normalized common support subgroup to have finite abelianization of order at most \(\binom{a+b-2}{a-1}\).
For example, if this subgroup has a quotient of order \(p\) in the witness characteristic \(p\), then the same binomial coefficient must be at least \(p\).
An application to a Mghirbi–Douglas family would require verifying this quotient on the minimal supports after collection; the alphabet size alone provides no such verification.
The [socle and small-product estimates](../claims/Yin_C_A0103_v2.md#statement) lower the binomial length bound by at least \(\min(a-1,b-1)+1\) for a torsion-free realization and by one more if either size is at least four.
For the [Mian–Siddique appendix supports](../claims/Yin_C_A0105_v1.md#argument), all 896 products collide in the full table, but three short coefficient certificates exclude every possible subrectangle over every field.
The [exact two-generator calculation](../claims/Yin_C_A0105_v2.md#statement) excludes all competing pairs in balls with radius sum at most nine.
The [first possible failure of unique products](../claims/Yin_C_A0105_v3.md#statement) occurs at radius sum ten, witnessed by seventeen rows in \(B_4\) and 28 columns in \(B_6\).
Their coefficient equations still exclude all zero products over every field.
For larger supports in the same group, the [seven-generator outer-layer test](../claims/Yin_C_A0106_v1.md#statement) removes the restriction on the right radius when the top left words satisfy its terminal-letter condition.
For the [seven predecessor triples](../claims/Yin_C_A0106_v2.md#statement) the actual product graph has girth 24, so every right support with no unique product has at least 126 elements, or at least 189 if a product has three representations.
The [endpoint multiplicities of nonbacktracking paths](../claims/Yin_C_A0106_v3.md#statement) improve the two necessary sizes to 362 and 655, respectively.
Together these results exclude every left support among the seven positive generators against every right support of size at most 361, at arbitrary word radius.
In characteristics zero and two the [modular character and finite-quotient criterion](../claims/Yin_C_A0109_v1.md#statement) removes the right-support size bound for every nonzero linear combination of the seven positive generators.
Over the complex numbers it proves zero kernel on the full regular Hilbert space.
It also covers arbitrary three-point supports on which the seven-valued character takes at least two values, irrespective of type and word length.
These tests use the [separating finite quotients of the type kernel](../claims/Yin_C_A0108_v1.md#explicit-separating-quotients).
For a normalized support \(\{1,u,v\}\) inside the type kernel, the [reduced norms and adjoint determinant](../claims/Yin_C_A0110_v1.md#statement) impose three tests even when all seven-valued labels coincide.
For fixed nonidentity \(u,v\), at most one index \(j\) with three distinct support points can pass the norm test on \(\{1,u,v^{2^j}\}\).
