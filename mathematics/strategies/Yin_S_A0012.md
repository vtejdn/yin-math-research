Use unitary representation families of the actual group to preserve the canonical trace throughout a deformation.
At a singular parameter of the auxiliary polynomial matrix, its coefficients may simplify enough to give a regular-rank lower bound.
The [22 November comparison with the F-series techniques](../ledger/Yin_L_A0069_v1.md#second-priority-for-an-individual-schur-complement) selected this branch to continue beyond ordinary scalar character tests and the finite-rank separator for freely placed rows.
The auxiliary matrices must satisfy every relator at infinitely many unitary phases.
An arbitrary finite-dimensional evaluation carries no lower bound of this kind.

The [first calculation](../claims/Yin_C_A0118_v1.md#statement) gives full rank for a shared denominator involving both sides of a noncentral amalgam with finite abelianization.
For the [property (T) vertex example](../claims/Yin_C_A0121_v1.md#the-finite-quotient-detecting-the-commutator) an actual commutator supplies both invertible corner maps in a finite quotient.
The tested pencils can use coefficients generating the entire double.
The [mixed relation comparison](../claims/Yin_C_A0122_v1.md#statement) shows that a conjugator interchanging tensor factors can keep infinitely many phases on a proper quotient where every two-dimensional choice of those pivots fails.
An un-conjugated commuting relation kills the required product of the two corner maps at **any** multiplicity for the specified two weights.

For multiple inverses, [the corner rank inequality](../claims/Yin_C_A0123_v1.md#statement) applies even to affiliated vertex coefficients after clearing the common denominators on the left.
When the upper entries are from vertex one, the lower border from vertex two, and the repeated diagonal and last entry are the same mixed \(p\), every chain length is settled.
The [mixed cycle calculation](../claims/Yin_C_A0165_v1.md#two-and-three-states-with-two-vertex-contributions-on-every-arrow) gives full rank for the three-state border with \(h=s_1+s_2\), \(b'=t_1+t_2\), \(c'=s_1t_1+t_2s_2\), \(s=I+3E_{21}\) and \(t=I+3E_{32}\).
Its scalar \(p-c'p^{-1}h p^{-1}b'\) is an affiliated unit.
For a cycle of any length with one group element from each vertex per arrow, [ordered elimination](../claims/Yin_C_A0165_v1.md#eliminating-each-vertex-cycle) leaves a single translation with a finite matrix weight in each corner.
A nonidentity ordered product in both vertices suffices for full rank.

To test what a corner kernel determines, put \(q=a_1+a_2\), \(c=1-b\), \(p=q+c\), and consider the matrix with rows \((p,-q-f)\), \((-q-g,p)\) for \(f,g\in\mathbb C[b^{\pm1}]\).
Every member has the same two vertex corners: edge Laurent polynomials contribute zero, and each corner has kernel dimension \(d\), the dimension of one weight space.
The subgroup generated by \(a_1,a_2,b\) is \(F(a_1,a_2)\times\langle b\rangle\).
Indeed \(a_j\) commutes with \(b\), every nonzero power of \(a_j\) lies outside the edge, and an alternating product of these powers multiplied by an edge element remains nonidentity by amalgam normal form.
The entries commute and their determinant is
\[
                 (2c-f-g)q+c^2-fg .
\]
It vanishes identically precisely when both Laurent coefficients vanish, by independence of the fiber group words \(a_1,a_2,1\).
The two scalar equations give \(f=g=c\), since the Laurent polynomial ring is a domain.
In every other case Fourier transformation along \(\langle b\rangle\) makes the determinant a nonzero scalar in \(\mathbb C F(a_1,a_2)\) for almost every character.
It is injective by the [free-group division-ring result](../audits/literature/Lin00/Yin_U004.md#the-division-ring-and-free-field-assertions-are-separate).
The adjugate identity and right-coset replication give rank two over the double.
For \(f=g=c\) the rows are \((p,-p)\) and its negative, and \(p\) is an affiliated unit, giving rank one.

If each correction instead contains one nonedge group element with nonzero coefficient and an arbitrary edge Laurent term, the [two triangular vertex corners](../claims/Yin_C_A0170_v1.md#two-triangular-vertex-corners) give full regular rank.
One prime and one choice of graph phases detect both corrections, including the mod-five kernel elements \(I+15E_{21}\) and \(I+15E_{32}\) at prime seven.
For two distinct nonedge translations in one diagonal corner, normalize by the first invertible term.
The second then contributes a finite matrix times a single infinite-order translation, which is covered by the [circle determinant calculation](../claims/Yin_C_A0165_v1.md#eliminating-each-vertex-cycle).
With three distinct nonedge translations, the normalized corner retains two different translations.
The next question is whether their joint contribution can be made full when the other vertex is already detected.
If varying the prime and phases leaves the same unknown vertex rank, return to [cyclic edge supports](Yin_S_A0005.md#cyclic-edge-support-through-a-regular-group-action).
The general \(P_k\Rightarrow P_{k+1}\) test also allows an arbitrary final diagonal and unrestricted coefficients; no common deformation under those hypotheses is known here.
