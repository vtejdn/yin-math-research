## Original Question

Prove or rigorously refute [Yin_C_F0001](../claims/Yin_C_F0001_v1.md#yin_c_f0001), whose linked statement and conventions are incorporated into this contract.
It asserts that for every finite tuple of normal generators of a finite tracial von Neumann algebra admitting a bounded dual system, an element of the algebra belongs to the scalar division closure of the unital polynomial algebra in those generators if and only if all its commutators with the dual operators have ordinary finite rank.

The exact quantified statement has one authoritative location, Yin_C_F0001.
This goal owns its selection, scope, authorization, and completion criteria; changing Yin_C_F0001's scope also requires an explicit goal-contract change.

## Scope and Exclusions

- Cover every finite number of generators and every tuple satisfying Yin_C_F0001.
  Neither mutual commutativity nor free independence is assumed.
  The tested element is any bounded element of the generated algebra.
- Retain normality of each generator, boundedness of the dual operators, the left multiplication representation, and the prescribed rank-one commutator normalization.
- Use polynomials in the listed generators without automatically adjoining their adjoints, and division closure inside the bounded algebra M.
  Affiliated-operator closure, matrix rational closure, unbounded dual systems, and arbitrary nonnormal generating tuples are separate questions.
- The normal-generator choice is intended to include selfadjoint and Haar-unitary settings.
  Circular variables are to be considered through two selfadjoint generators; an R-diagonal model may be considered through a normal pair, such as a Haar unitary and a positive operator.
  Any such model must still satisfy the generated-algebra and bounded-dual hypotheses.
  These motivations do not add freeness to the conjecture or assert coverage of every R-diagonal distribution.
- A special case, additional-hypothesis theorem, auxiliary criterion, or failed proof method does not replace the full target.
  Novelty, publication, formalization, and classification of all admissible tuples are not completion requirements.
