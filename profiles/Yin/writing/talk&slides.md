Inherit the [author-wide writing policy](readme.md) and the applicable subfield policy under the [writing hierarchy](../../../framework/rules/preferences.md#scope-and-adoption).

## Talk Abstract Text Format

Scope: Yin's talk abstracts.

Do not use LaTeX code in talk abstract text.
Express mathematical content in ordinary prose or directly readable Unicode notation, without LaTeX commands or math delimiters.

## Default Slide Format

Scope: future mathematical presentation slides prepared for Yin.

- Prepare slides in LaTeX Beamer using the Madrid theme and a 4:3 aspect ratio.
- Use a white background, blue title bars and the theme's standard mathematical typography.
  Keep the layout focused on the mathematical content.
- Present definitions, theorems and related statements in Beamer blocks.
  Use distinct blocks for examples or questions when this helps distinguish their roles.
- Include the author, abbreviated talk title, date and frame number in the footer.
  On the title slide, give the talk title, speaker, affiliation, event or location, and date; identify joint work where applicable.
- Write mathematical slide text in English by default, following Yin's current terminology and notation choices.

## Default Talk Organization

Scope: the structure and exposition of Yin's mathematical talks.

- For introductory talks, begin with background, historical motivation and concrete examples, then introduce the main concepts and their relationships.
  In free-probability introductions, use random-matrix examples and distributions to motivate noncommutative probability and freeness.
- For research talks, introduce the motivating problem and the background needed to understand it, define the relevant objects, and then present the main results and their proof ideas.
  Adjust the amount of background to the audience.
- Use numerical plots, mathematical examples and relevant historical images to support explanations.
  Connect each visual directly to the concept or question being discussed.
- State the necessary hypotheses and conclusions clearly in result slides.
  Explain the main mechanism of a proof before presenting technical steps, with detail chosen for the allotted time.
- Close with the main conclusions and, where relevant, further questions.

## Slide Organization and Pacing

Scope: individual frames and the progression between them.

- Use short titles that identify the mathematical topic, question or result on the frame.
- Combine concise prose with the formulas needed for the explanation.
  Use side-by-side layouts when comparing examples, distributions or mathematical settings.
- Reveal material progressively when the explanation develops in steps.
  Extend a frame or repeat its title when continuing the same argument, preserving the context needed for the next step.
- Keep references close to the historical claim, definition or result they support; use compact reference text where appropriate.

## Citations on Slides

Scope: references on Yin's presentation slides.

Cite the source work itself; a slide reference need not specify a theorem, lemma, definition or page.
Leave precise locators to the manuscript, citation map or speaker notes, and keep the visible slide reference compact.
For a theorem, definition, proposition or similar environment, use a compact author--year label of the form `Mai--Speicher--Y., 23`: give the surnames, abbreviate the final surname when the author list is long, and use a two-digit year.
Do not replace this label by a bare bibliography key or by a manuscript theorem number.
Literature citations may be set in gray small type, and a frame footnote may give the standard bibliographic citation instead of a separate bibliography list.
